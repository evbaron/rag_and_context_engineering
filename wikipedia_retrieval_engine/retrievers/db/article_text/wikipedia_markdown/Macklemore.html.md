**Benjamin Hammond Haggerty** (born June 19, 1983), better known by his [stage name](/wiki/Stage_name) **Macklemore** ([/ˈmækləmɔːr/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*MAK-lə-mor*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); formerly **Professor Macklemore**), is an American rapper. A native of [Seattle](/wiki/Seattle), [Washington](/wiki/Washington_(state)), he started his career in 2000 as an independent artist releasing: *Open Your Eyes* (2000), *The Language of My World* (2005), and *The Unplanned Mixtape* (2009). He rose to international success collaborating with producer [Ryan Lewis](/wiki/Ryan_Lewis) as the duo [Macklemore & Ryan Lewis](/wiki/Macklemore_%26_Ryan_Lewis) (2009–2016).

Macklemore's and Lewis's 2012 single "[Thrift Shop](/wiki/Thrift_Shop)" (featuring [Wanz](/wiki/Wanz)) became the first self-released song to peak the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) in 11 years. However, Macklemore, in a slightly unusual [recording contract](/wiki/Recording_contract), paid a nominal percentage of sales to use [Warner Bros. Records](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records)'s radio promotion department to push his singles. Their second single, "[Can't Hold Us](/wiki/Can%27t_Hold_Us)" (featuring [Ray Dalton](/wiki/Ray_Dalton)), also peaked on chart, making Macklemore and Lewis the first duo in the chart's history to have their first two singles both reach the peak position. Their debut studio album *[The Heist](/wiki/The_Heist_(album))* (2012) peaked at number 2 on the [U.S. *Billboard* 200](/wiki/U.S._Billboard_200). At the [56th Annual Grammy Awards](/wiki/56th_Annual_Grammy_Awards), the duo won [Best New Artist](/wiki/Best_New_Artist), [Best Rap Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap_Album) (*The Heist*), [Best Rap Song](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap_Song) ("[Same Love](/wiki/Same_Love)") and [Best Rap Performance](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap_Performance) ("Thrift Shop"). The duo's second album, *[This Unruly Mess I've Made](/wiki/This_Unruly_Mess_I%27ve_Made)* (2016), followed thereafter.

In June 2017, Macklemore released the single "[Glorious](/wiki/Glorious_(Macklemore_song))" (featuring [Skylar Grey](/wiki/Skylar_Grey)), which marked his return to the music industry as well as his first major single produced without Lewis since his mainstream breakthrough. Macklemore released his second solo studio album, *[Gemini](/wiki/Gemini_(Macklemore_album))*, in September of that year, peaking at number 2 on the *Billboard* 200. On February 24, 2023, he released his third album *[Ben](/wiki/Ben_(Macklemore_album)),* which peaked at number 18.

## Early life and influences

Benjamin Hammond Haggerty was born in [Seattle](/wiki/Seattle), Washington, on June 19, 1983, one of two sons born to Bill Haggerty and Julie Schott. He was raised with his brother Tim in Seattle's [Capitol Hill](/wiki/Capitol_Hill,_Seattle) neighborhood. He has [Irish](/wiki/Irish_people) heritage and was raised [Catholic](/wiki/Catholic_Church). Haggerty was six years old when [hip hop](/wiki/Hip_hop_music) first came into his life by way of [Digital Underground](/wiki/Digital_Underground). At the age of 15, he began to write lyrics.

At the time he started to sing, Haggerty listened to "a lot of [East Coast underground hip hop](/wiki/East_Coast_hip_hop)", listing [Hieroglyphics](/wiki/Hieroglyphics_(group)), [Freestyle Fellowship](/wiki/Freestyle_Fellowship), [Aceyalone](/wiki/Aceyalone), [Living Legends](/wiki/Living_Legends_(group)), [Wu-Tang Clan](/wiki/Wu-Tang_Clan), [Mobb Deep](/wiki/Mobb_Deep), [Nas](/wiki/Nas), and [Talib Kweli](/wiki/Talib_Kweli) as his major influences. Haggerty attended [Garfield High School](/wiki/Garfield_High_School_(Seattle,_Washington)) and [Nathan Hale High School](/wiki/Nathan_Hale_High_School_(Washington)). At Hale, he developed the stage name "Professor Macklemore" for an art project involving a made-up superhero, and at Garfield, he started a hip-hop group named Elevated Elements with other students. The group released an album, *Progress*, in 2000.

Haggerty enrolled at the [College of Santa Fe](/wiki/College_of_Santa_Fe) for a year, later saying in an interview that "[i]t was a very pivotal time in a lot of ways. [Santa Fe](/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico) was the place where I got good at rapping". After not getting into the music program, he moved back to Seattle. Haggerty later enrolled at [Evergreen State College](/wiki/Evergreen_State_College) in Olympia and completed his bachelor's degree in 2009. While at Evergreen, he was interested in reaching a younger generation through his music. He became part of a program focusing on education and cultural identity called "Gateways for Incarcerated Youth", where he facilitated music workshops.

## Career

### 2000–2008: *The Language of My World*

Macklemore & Ryan Lewis at [Sasquatch! Music Festival](/wiki/Sasquatch!_Music_Festival) (2011)

In 2000, Haggerty recorded a mixtape titled *Open Your Eyes* under the name Professor Macklemore, which he distributed himself and released on October 21, 2000. Haggerty dropped "Professor" from his name and began working on his first official full-length album, called *[The Language of My World](/wiki/The_Language_of_My_World)*. The album was released on January 1, 2005, with its lead single, "Love Song", being announced the same day. "Love Song" featured singer Evan Roman, and was produced by Budo, who would later go on to produce several more tracks for Macklemore.

In 2006, Haggerty first met his future collaborator [Ryan Lewis](/wiki/Ryan_Lewis). Lewis, who would go on to release two albums with Haggerty as [Macklemore & Ryan Lewis](/wiki/Macklemore_%26_Ryan_Lewis), spent a few years working on Macklemore's promotion as a photographer. The two musicians would soon become good friends but would not formalize their collaborative efforts until 2009. In the meantime, Macklemore kept busy as a solo artist, appearing on [The Physics](/wiki/The_Physics_(music_group))'s song "Good" in 2009, as well as performing at the Seattle major arts and music festival [Bumbershoot](/wiki/Bumbershoot) in 2006, 2009, and 2011.

Macklemore's second mixtape, *[The Unplanned Mixtape](/wiki/The_Unplanned_Mixtape),* was released on September 7, 2009. It would later reach No. 7 on the [iTunes](/wiki/ITunes) Hip Hop chart. The [mixtape](/wiki/Mixtape) was accompanied by the singles "The Town" and "And We Danced", the latter of which featured singer Ziggy Stardust. "The Town" was later remixed by Sabzi of the [Blue Scholars](/wiki/Blue_Scholars).

### 2009–2017: Career with Ryan Lewis

In 2009, Macklemore and [Ryan Lewis](/wiki/Ryan_Lewis) formalized the collaboration as a duo [Macklemore & Ryan Lewis](/wiki/Macklemore_%26_Ryan_Lewis). They released the EP *The VS. EP*. They also released "Irish Celebration" in December 2009 in anticipation of the release of *The Vs. EP*. In March 2010, the duo released "Stay At Home Dad", a track that "didn't quite make" *Vs.* In October 2010, they created the *VS. Redux* EP. Macklemore used his experience with substance abuse to create the mixtape's song "Otherside", which samples the [Red Hot Chili Peppers](/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers) song of the [same title](/wiki/Otherside). On April 8, 2011, Macklemore and Ryan Lewis performed the song at the 2011 Mariners Opening Day in-front of a sold-out crowd of nearly 48,000 attendees. "[Wings](/wiki/Wings_(Macklemore_and_Ryan_Lewis_song))" was released on January 21, 2011, followed by "[Can't Hold Us](/wiki/Can%27t_Hold_Us)" featuring [Ray Dalton](/wiki/Ray_Dalton) on August 16, 2011. In February 2011, Macklemore and producer Ryan Lewis kicked off a multi-city tour in Seattle, which included three sold-out shows at the [Showbox at the Market](/wiki/Showbox_at_the_Market) music venue. That same year, the rapper appeared at many U.S. music festivals, including [Bumbershoot](/wiki/Bumbershoot), [Outside Lands](/wiki/Outside_Lands_Music_and_Arts_Festival), [Lollapalooza](/wiki/Lollapalooza), [Rock the Bells](/wiki/Rock_the_Bells), [Soundset](/wiki/Soundset_Music_Festival), [Sasquatch](/wiki/Sasquatch!_Music_Festival), and [Bonnaroo](/wiki/Bonnaroo).

Macklemore performing at The Heist Tour, in 2012

Their album *[The Heist](/wiki/The_Heist_(album))* was released in October 2012 and debuted on the U.S. [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) at number 2 of the week dated October 27, 2012, selling over 78,000 copies. "[Same Love](/wiki/Same_Love)" was released on July 18, 2012, and after "[White Walls](/wiki/White_Walls)". In January 2013, [Music Choice](/wiki/Music_Choice) featured Macklemore in the brand new series "Primed", which focuses on emerging artists. In May 2013, Haggerty was featured on [Clinton Sparks](/wiki/Clinton_Sparks)'s single "Gold Rush", along with [2 Chainz](/wiki/2_Chainz) and D.A. The Heist World Tour began in August 2012 to promote *The Heist*.

In May 2014, Macklemore was accused of [antisemitism](/wiki/Antisemitism) after wearing a costume resembling a [common Jewish caricature](/wiki/Stereotypes_of_Jews) during a private concert in [Seattle, Washington](/wiki/Seattle). The rapper released an apology and stated his intention was to disguise himself, not to impersonate any ‘type’ of person.

In January 2015, Macklemore announced via Twitter that his third studio album would be released sometime in the second half of that year. Despite this, the album was not released until February 26, 2016. On August 5, 2015, Macklemore released a song for free download titled "Growing Up (Sloane's Song)", which features [Ed Sheeran](/wiki/Ed_Sheeran). On August 27, 2015, he released a new song called "[Downtown](/wiki/Downtown_(Macklemore_%26_Ryan_Lewis_song))" which features [Foxy Shazam](/wiki/Foxy_Shazam) vocalist Eric Nally, [Kool Moe Dee](/wiki/Kool_Moe_Dee), [Melle Mel](/wiki/Melle_Mel), and [Grandmaster Caz](/wiki/Grandmaster_Caz), which he performed at the [2015 MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/2015_MTV_Video_Music_Awards) on August 30. On his first tour in two years, An Evening with Macklemore and Ryan Lewis, Macklemore announced that his new album was finished and ready for release.

On January 15, 2016, Macklemore released a teaser video on his [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) channel revealing the name of his third studio album, *[This Unruly Mess I've Made](/wiki/This_Unruly_Mess_I%27ve_Made)*, and announcing that it was scheduled to be released on February 26, 2016. On January 22, 2016, the duo released "[White Privilege II](/wiki/White_Privilege_II)", the second single on *This Unruly Mess I've Made*. On February 26, 2016, Macklemore and Ryan Lewis released the album *This Unruly Mess I've Made*. Also in 2016, Macklemore released two solo singles, "[Drug Dealer](/wiki/Drug_Dealer_(song))" and "Wednesday Morning". "Drug Dealer" features Macklemore rapping about his previous addictions and is featured in a documentary that includes clips of Macklemore discussing drug abuse with President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama). "Wednesday Morning" was released after the [2016 U.S. Election](/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election) and features Macklemore rapping about the political future of the country. The tracks were produced by [Budo](/wiki/Budo_(musician)). On June 15, 2017, Macklemore announced via his official [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram) that the duo were on hiatus.

### 2017–present: *Gemini* & *Ben*

On June 15, 2017, Macklemore released "[Glorious](/wiki/Glorious_(Macklemore_song))", featuring American singer [Skylar Grey](/wiki/Skylar_Grey), as the lead single from Macklemore's second solo studio album *[Gemini](/wiki/Gemini_(Macklemore_album))*. On July 26, 2017, Macklemore released "[Marmalade](/wiki/Marmalade_(Macklemore_song))", featuring American rapper [Lil Yachty](/wiki/Lil_Yachty), as the second single from Macklemore's upcoming album. "[Good Old Days](/wiki/Good_Old_Days_(Macklemore_song))", a collaboration with [Kesha](/wiki/Kesha), was released as a single on October 9, 2017. Macklemore released *Gemini* on September 22. The album was produced by longtime collaborator Budo. On October 1, Macklemore performed "Same Love" in a set at the opening of [the Grand Final](/wiki/2017_NRL_Grand_Final) of the [National Rugby League](/wiki/National_Rugby_League) in [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney), Australia — something that was considered by some to be controversial in the middle of a [national survey on same sex marriage](/wiki/Australian_Marriage_Law_Postal_Survey). Macklemore stated that it was one of his best performances due to the circumstances and thanked the fans in Sydney for the reception that he received throughout. On December 11, 2017, Macklemore announced an upcoming co-headlining tour with Kesha titled [The Adventures of Kesha and Macklemore](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Kesha_and_Macklemore). This was Macklemore's fifth headlining tour, promoting his album *Gemini* and Kesha's sixth headlining tour, promoting her third solo album *[Rainbow](/wiki/Rainbow_(Kesha_album))* (2017). The tour began in Phoenix on June 6, 2018, and concluded in Tampa on August 5, 2018.

On October 29, 2021, Macklemore released a new single titled "Next Year", featuring American singer [Windser](/wiki/Windser). Macklemore also worked with Ryan Lewis on the single, marking their first collaboration in three years.

On July 22, 2022, Macklemore released a new single, "[Chant](/wiki/Chant_(song))", with Australian musician [Tones and I](/wiki/Tones_and_I). On August 19, 2022, Macklemore released the single, "[Maniac](/wiki/Maniac_(Macklemore_song))", featuring American musician Windser. The third single, "[Faithful](/wiki/Faithful_(Macklemore_song))", featuring [NLE Choppa](/wiki/NLE_Choppa), was released on October 28, 2022. The fourth single, "[Heroes](/wiki/Heroes_(Macklemore_song))", featuring [DJ Premier](/wiki/DJ_Premier), was released on January 20, 2023. Macklemore's third solo studio album, *[Ben](/wiki/Ben_(Macklemore_album))*, was released on March 3, 2023. In May 2024, he released "[Hind's Hall](/wiki/Hind%27s_Hall_(song))", a [protest song](/wiki/Protest_song) in support of the [2024 pro-Palestinian protests on university campuses](/wiki/2024_pro-Palestinian_protests_on_university_campuses), with all proceeds donated towards [UNRWA](/wiki/UNRWA).

### Family

Macklemore became engaged to his girlfriend of seven years, Tricia Davis, on January 21, 2013. On January 3, 2015, he announced on Twitter that he and Davis were expecting their first child that May. After the release of "Growing Up (Sloane's Song)", which featured English singer-songwriter [Ed Sheeran](/wiki/Ed_Sheeran), the couple announced that their daughter, Sloane Ava Simone Haggerty, had been born on May 29. Following the birth of Sloane, they married on June 27, 2015.
In September 2017, the couple announced that they were expecting their second child. On April 3, 2018, Macklemore announced during a performance at the [3Arena](/wiki/3Arena_(Dublin)) that Davis had given birth to their second daughter. He did not tell the crowd her name. In 2021, they had their third child, Hugo.

In 2017, Macklemore's grandmother, Helen Schott, celebrated her [100th](/wiki/Centenarian) birthday, with the two traveling around [Modesto, California](/wiki/Modesto,_California) for the music video of his song "[Glorious](/wiki/Glorious_(Macklemore_song))."

### Health

In August 2008, Macklemore admitted himself into [drug rehabilitation](/wiki/Drug_rehabilitation) for [drug addiction](/wiki/Drug_addiction) and [alcoholism](/wiki/Alcoholism), and celebrated three years of sobriety before a brief relapse in 2011, which he describes in his song "Starting Over". He said in a 2012 documentary that he spent most of his twenties trying to combat his addictions and destructive way of life:

> I want to be someone who is respected and not just in terms of my music. I want to be respected in terms of the way that I treat people... Music is my creative outlet in terms of expressing what is important to me; what has importance, what has a value. And I want to be respected for that.

On May 14, 2016, Macklemore appeared in U.S. President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s weekly address to talk about the dangers of [addiction to opioids](/wiki/Opioid_use_disorder) and prescription [painkillers](/wiki/Analgesic). Macklemore talked about his own experiences with abuse of painkillers, stating "When you're going through it, it's hard to imagine anything being worse than addiction. But the shame and stigma associated with the disease keeps too many people from seeking the help they actually need. Addiction isn't a personal choice or a personal failure."

On September 29, 2018, Macklemore headlined Recovery Fest in [Pawtucket, Rhode Island](/wiki/Pawtucket,_Rhode_Island). Recovery Fest was formed to have a drug- and alcohol-free concert and support charities that worked in combating opioid addiction.

In the summer of 2020, during and in part due to the social isolation that took place during the [COVID-19 lockdowns](/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns), Macklemore relapsed and suffered a near-fatal overdose. He was hospitalized and given treatment; he eventually recovered from the episode. This experience, as well as the ongoing challenge of overcoming addiction while also raising children, served as an inspiration for much of the lyrical content that makes up his third solo studio album, *Ben.*

### Activism and political views

Macklemore voiced his support of [LGBT rights](/wiki/LGBT_rights) and same-sex marriage in the song "[Same Love](/wiki/Same_Love)" released in 2012, which also condemns [homophobia](/wiki/Homophobia) in mainstream hip-hop, society, and mass media. Macklemore is also an outspoken critic of [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump). In July 2016, he was featured on "Pt. 2" remix of [YG](/wiki/YG_(rapper))'s "[FDT (Fuck Donald Trump)](/wiki/FDT_(song))" alongside [G-Eazy](/wiki/G-Eazy). In January 2021, after the [presidential inauguration](/wiki/Inauguration_of_Joe_Biden) of [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden), he released "Trump's Over Freestyle". Macklemore attended the [2017 Women's March](/wiki/2017_Women%27s_March) in [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.). On May 16, 2019, Macklemore received the Stevie Ray Vaughan Award from [MusiCares](/wiki/MusiCares), in recognition of his support of MusiCares and the addiction recovery process.

Macklemore is a supporter of [Palestinian nationalism](/wiki/Palestinian_nationalism). On October 30, 2023, during the [Gaza war](/wiki/Gaza_war), he signed [Artists4Ceasefire](/wiki/Artists4Ceasefire) letter calling for a ceasefire and an end to the [Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip](/wiki/Israeli_blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip_(2023%E2%80%93present)). In November 2023, while giving a speech at a pro-Palestinian rally in Washington, D.C., he accused Israel of committing [genocide against Palestinians](/wiki/Gaza_genocide). In May 2024, he released "[Hind's Hall](/wiki/Hind%27s_Hall_(song))", a [protest song](/wiki/Protest_song) in support of the 2024 [Gaza war protests at universities](/wiki/Gaza_war_protests_at_universities), with all proceeds donated towards [UNRWA](/wiki/UNRWA). In August 2024, Macklemore cancelled his show scheduled to take place in October that year in [Dubai](/wiki/Dubai), citing the United Arab Emirates' involvement "in the [ongoing genocide and humanitarian crisis](/wiki/Humanitarian_impact_of_the_Sudanese_civil_war_(2023%E2%80%93present))" in [Sudan](/wiki/Sudan). He said he will not perform in the country until it "stops arming and funding the [RSF](/wiki/Rapid_Support_Forces)". He also mentioned having no judgement of the artists continuing to perform in the Emirates.

At a pro-Palestinian rally in September 2024, Macklemore exclaimed "fuck America", causing widespread controversy, including being dropped from the Neon City Festival in Las Vegas. The [Seattle Mariners](/wiki/Seattle_Mariners), [Seattle Kraken](/wiki/Seattle_Kraken), and [Seattle Sounders FC](/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC) all condemned his statements and stated that they would review any further involvement from Macklemore. He later apologized for the incident, saying "My thoughts and feelings are not always expressed perfectly or politely," and that at times he "slips up and gets caught in a moment." Macklemore joined with [Watermelon Pictures](/wiki/Watermelon_Pictures) and [BreakThrough News](/wiki/BreakThrough_News) to produce *[The Encampments](/wiki/The_Encampments)*, a documentary film about the [Gaza Solidarity Encampment](/wiki/Gaza_Solidarity_Encampment_(Columbia_University)) at [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University). The film features interviews with prominent protestors including [Mahmoud Khalil](/wiki/Mahmoud_Khalil_(activist)). *The Encampments* premiered at [CPH:DOX](/wiki/CPH:DOX) on March 25, 2025.

### Interests

Macklemore is a fan of the [Seattle Seahawks](/wiki/Seattle_Seahawks). He created a [12th man](/wiki/12th_man_(football)) promotional video and performed at the [2014 NFC Championship Game](/wiki/2014_NFC_Championship_Game). He remains a fan of the now-[relocated](/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics_relocation_to_Oklahoma_City) [Seattle SuperSonics](/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics). In August 2019, Macklemore joined the ownership group of [Seattle Sounders FC](/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC), the local [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) club.

He is a fan of the [Seattle Kraken](/wiki/Seattle_Kraken) of the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League); on April 18, 2022, the Kraken announced that Macklemore had joined its ownership group.

Macklemore has an eclectic taste in art, including outlandish garments spouting sequins, fringe and feathers, a [kitschy](/wiki/Kitsch) velvet painting of a [bald eagle](/wiki/Bald_eagle), an oil painting of [Drake](/wiki/Drake_(musician)) dancing and a [Dan Lacey](/wiki/Dan_Lacey) painting of a nude [Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber).

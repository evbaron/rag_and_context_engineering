**Nancy Farmer** (born 1941) is an American writer of [children's](/wiki/Children%27s_literature) and young adult books and science fiction. She has written three [Newbery Honor](/wiki/Newbery_Medal) books and won the U.S. [National Book Award for Young People's Literature](/wiki/National_Book_Award_for_Young_People%27s_Literature) for *[The House of the Scorpion](/wiki/The_House_of_the_Scorpion)*, published by [Atheneum Books for Young Readers](/wiki/Atheneum_Books_for_Young_Readers) in 2002.

## Early life

Farmer was born in [Phoenix, Arizona](/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona). She earned her B.A. at [Reed College](/wiki/Reed_College) (1963) and later studied chemistry and entomology at the University of California, Berkeley. She enlisted in the [Peace Corps](/wiki/Peace_Corps) (1963–1965), and subsequently worked in [Mozambique](/wiki/Mozambique) and [Rhodesia](/wiki/Rhodesia) (present-day [Zimbabwe](/wiki/Zimbabwe)), where she studied biological methods of controlling the [tsetse fly](/wiki/Tsetse_fly) between 1975 and 1978.

## Career

Farmer began writing in the 1980s, at the age of 40, while still living in Zimbabwe. She began writing stories in Africa. It was for one of those stories that she won the [Writers of the Future](/wiki/Writers_and_Illustrators_of_the_Future) contest, which enabled her to move back to the [United States](/wiki/United_States) and begin writing full-time. Her experiences in Africa would go on to influence her writing.

## Personal life

Farmer met her future husband, Harold Farmer, at the University of Rhodesia (now the [University of Zimbabwe](/wiki/University_of_Zimbabwe)). They married after a week-long courtship. As of 2010, Farmer lives in Arizona's [Chiricahua Mountains](/wiki/Chiricahua_Mountains) with her husband. They have one son, Daniel.

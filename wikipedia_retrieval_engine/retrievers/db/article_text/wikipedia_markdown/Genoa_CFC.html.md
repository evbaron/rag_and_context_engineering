**Genoa Cricket and Football Club** (Italian pronunciation: [[ˈdʒeːnoa]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian)) is an Italian professional [football](/wiki/Association_football) club based in [Genoa](/wiki/Genoa), [Liguria](/wiki/Liguria). The team competes in the [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A), the top division of the Italian football league system.

Established in [1893](/wiki/1893), Genoa is Italy's oldest existing football team. The club has won the [Italian Championship](/wiki/Italian_Football_Championship) nine times, with their first being Italy's inaugural national championship in [1898](/wiki/1898_Italian_Football_Championship), and their most recent coming after the [1923–24](/wiki/1923%E2%80%9324_Prima_Divisione) season. They have also won one [Coppa Italia](/wiki/Coppa_Italia) title. Overall, Genoa are the fourth most successful Italian club in terms of championships won. *Il Grifone* have played their home games at the [Stadio Luigi Ferraris](/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris) since 1911, sharing with local rivals [Sampdoria](/wiki/UC_Sampdoria). The fixture between the two teams, known as the [Derby della Lanterna](/wiki/Derby_della_Lanterna), was first played in 1946.

In 2011, Genoa was included in the "International Bureau of Cultural Capitals" (a sort of historical sporting heritage of humanity, in line with that of [UNESCO](/wiki/UNESCO)) at the request of President Xavier Tudela. The club was admitted to the "[Club of Pioneers](/wiki/Club_of_Pioneers)", an association comprising the world's oldest football clubs, in 2013; other members include [Sheffield F.C.](/wiki/Sheffield_F.C.) and [Recreativo de Huelva](/wiki/Recreativo_de_Huelva).

## History

Main article: [History of Genoa CFC](/wiki/History_of_Genoa_CFC)

Act of foundation of Genoa CFC, dated September 1893

The club was founded on 7 September 1893 as **Genoa Cricket & Athletic Club**. In its earliest years, it principally competed in [athletics](/wiki/Sport_of_athletics) and [cricket](/wiki/Cricket). [Association football](/wiki/Association_football) was only a secondary concern. Since the club was set up to represent England abroad, the original shirts worn by the organisation were white, the same colour as the [England national team](/wiki/England_national_football_team) shirt. At first Italians were not permitted to join as it was a British sporting club abroad. Genoa's activities took place in the north-west of the city in the Campasso area, at the *Piazza d'Armi*. The men who initially handled the management of the club were;

On 10 April 1897, the footballing section of the club, already in existence since 1893, became predominant thanks to [James Richardson Spensley](/wiki/James_Richardson_Spensley). It was among the oldest in [Italian football](/wiki/Football_in_Italy) at the time, only four other clubs (all in [Turin](/wiki/Turin).) had been founded. Italians were allowed to join and found a new ground in the form of *Ponte Carrega*.
The first friendly match took place at home, against a mixed team of [Internazionale Torino](/wiki/Internazionale_F.C._Torino) and [F.B.C. Torinese](/wiki/F.B.C._Torinese); Genoa lost 1–0. Not long after, Genoa recorded its first victory away against [UPS Alessandria](/wiki/U.S._Alessandria_Calcio_1912) winning 2–0. Friendly games also took place against various British sailors such as those from [HMS *Revenge*](/wiki/HMS_Revenge_(1892)).

### Championship dominance

*Genoa CAC* in 1898, the first ever Italian Championship winners

Football in Italy stepped up a level with the creation of the [Italian Football Federation](/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) and the [Italian Football Championship](/wiki/Italian_Football_Championship). Genoa competed in the first Italian Championship in [1898](/wiki/1898_Italian_Football_Championship) at [Velodrome Humbert I](/wiki/Velodrome_Humbert_I) in Turin. They defeated [Ginnastica Torino](/wiki/R.S._Ginnastica_Torino) 2–1 in their first official game on 8 May, before winning the first championship later that day by beating Internazionale Torino 3–1 after [extra-time](/wiki/Extra_time).

Genoa returned for the [following season](/wiki/1899_Italian_Football_Championship), this time with a few changes; the name of the club was altered to **Genoa Cricket & Football Club**, dropping the *Athletic* from its name. A change in shirt colour was also in order, as they changed to white and blue vertical stripes; known in Italy as *biancoblù*. Genoa won their second title in a one-day tournament which took place on 16 April 1899, by beating Internazionale Torino 3–1 for the second time. On their way to winning their third consecutive title in [1900](/wiki/1900_Italian_Football_Championship) they also beat local rivals Sampierdarenese 7–0; a winning margin which would not be bettered by any team in the league until 1910. The final was secured with a 3–1 win over [F.B.C. Torinese](/wiki/F.B.C._Torinese).

The club strip was changed again in [1901](/wiki/1901_Italian_Football_Championship), Genoa adopted its famous red-navy halves and therefore became known as the *rossoblù*; these are the colours used even to this day as with many other Italian clubs, such as Cagliari, Bologna, Crotone, Cosenza and an endless list of minor clubs. After a season of finishing runners-up to [Milan Cricket and Football Club](/wiki/AC_Milan), things were back on track in [1902](/wiki/1902_Italian_Football_Championship) with their fourth title. Juventus emerged as serious contenders to Genoa's throne from [1903](/wiki/1903_Italian_Football_Championship) onwards, when for two seasons in a row Genoa beat the *Old Lady* in the national final.

Notably Genoa became the first Italian football team to play an international match, when they visited France on 27 April 1903 to play [FVC Nice](/wiki/OGC_Nice), winning the fixture 3–0. As well as winning the Italian championship in [1904](/wiki/1904_Prima_Categoria), the year was also notable for Genoa reserves winning the first ever [II Categoria](/wiki/II_Categoria) league season; a proto-[Serie B](/wiki/Serie_B) under the top level. From [1905](/wiki/1905_Prima_Categoria) onwards when they were runners-up, Genoa lost their foothold on the Italian championship; other clubs such as Juventus, Milan and [Pro Vercelli](/wiki/F.C._Pro_Vercelli_1892) stepped up.

The fall in part during this period can be traced back to [1908](/wiki/1908_Italian_Football_Championship) when [FIGC](/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) agreed to Federal Gymnastics protests forbidding the use of foreign players. Since Genoa's birth they had always had a strong English contingent. They disagreed, as did several other prominent clubs such as Milan, [Torino](/wiki/Torino_F.C.) and Firenze; as thus they withdrew from official FIGC competitions that year. The following season the federation reversed the decision and Genoa was rebuilt with players such as [Luigi Ferraris](/wiki/Luigi_Ferraris_(footballer)) and some from [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland), such as [Daniel Hug](/wiki/Daniel_Hug) who came from [FC Basel](/wiki/FC_Basel). The rebuilding of the squad also saw the creation of a new ground in the [Marassi](/wiki/Marassi) area of Genoa, when built it had a capacity of 25,000 and was comparable to British stadiums of the time; it was officially opened on 22 January 1911.

### Garbutt revival

With the introduction of the [Italy national football team](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team), Genoa played an important part, with the likes of [Renzo De Vecchi](/wiki/Renzo_De_Vecchi); who was *azzurri* captain for some time, [Edoardo Mariani](/wiki/Edoardo_Mariani) and [Enrico Sardi](/wiki/Enrico_Sardi) earning call-ups. Englishman [William Garbutt](/wiki/William_Garbutt) was brought in as head coach to help revive the club; Garbutt was the first professional manager in Italy and was renowned for being highly charismatic, and also for constantly smoking his [tobacco pipe](/wiki/Tobacco_pipe). He was dubbed "Mister" by the players; since then Italians have referred to coaches in general by the term.

Finally by [1914–15](/wiki/1914%E2%80%9315_Prima_Categoria), Genoa had restored themselves as the top club from [Northern Italy](/wiki/Northern_Italy), winning the final round of the Northern section. However, this particular year, the national final could not be played because of the outbreak of World War I, the finals of the Southern Italian section could not be decided and thus Genoa did not have a team to play. Genoa would be awarded the title in 1919 after the end of the war, it would be their first for eleven seasons. The war took a harsh toll on Genoa as players Luigi Ferraris, Adolfo Gnecco, Carlo Marassi, Alberto Sussone and [Claudio Casanova](/wiki/Claudio_Casanova) all died while on military duty in Italy; while footballing founder [James Richardson Spensley](/wiki/James_Richardson_Spensley) was killed in Germany.

The last Genoa side to win the *Italian Football Championship*, in 1924

In the early part of the 1920s, Genoa remained strong contenders in the Northern section. Garbutt led Genoa to championship success in [1922–23](/wiki/1922%E2%80%9323_Prima_Divisione); beating [Lazio](/wiki/S.S._Lazio) 6–1 in the final, over the course of two legs. The [following season](/wiki/1923%E2%80%9324_Prima_Divisione), Genoa made their way past [Bologna](/wiki/Bologna_F.C._1909) in the Northern finals, but not without controversy; after riots in the second leg during the game in [Bologna](/wiki/Bologna), the game was called off and FIGC awarded Genoa a 2–0 victory. In the national final that season, Genoa beat [Savoia](/wiki/F.C._Savoia_1908) 4–1 over the course of two legs; this would be their ninth and to date final Italian championship.

The squad during these two championship victories included; [Giovanni De Prà](/wiki/Giovanni_De_Pra), [Ottavio Barbieri](/wiki/Ottavio_Barbieri), [Luigi Burlando](/wiki/Luigi_Burlando) and [Renzo De Vecchi](/wiki/Renzo_De_Vecchi). With Genoa's championship victory in 1923–24 came the introduction of the *scudetto* patch; which means following the season within which a club wins an Italian league championship, they are allowed to wear a [shield](/wiki/Shield) shaped patch on their shirt which features the colours of the [Italian flag](/wiki/Flag_of_Italy). For the rest of the 1920s, the club's highest finish was in second place: the [1927–28](/wiki/1927%E2%80%9328_Divisione_Nazionale) season when Genoa finished runners-up to Torino, with striker [Felice Levratto](/wiki/Felice_Levratto) scoring 20 goals in 27 games.

### Genova 1893 period

Due to the strongly British connotations attached to the name, Genoa were forced to change it by the [fascist government](/wiki/Italian_Fascism) to **Genova 1893 Circolo del Calcio** in 1928. The club competed in a proto-European Cup in the form of the [Mitropa Cup](/wiki/Mitropa_Cup), where they went out in the quarter-finals after losing heavily to [Rapid Vienna](/wiki/SK_Rapid_Wien). They followed this with a runners-up position back at home in the league, they finished behind [Ambrosiana](/wiki/F.C._Internazionale_Milano) in the [1929–30](/wiki/1929%E2%80%9330_Serie_A) season; this would be their last top level championship runners-up spot to date.

Genoa *[Coppa Italia](/wiki/Coppa_Italia)* winning side of 1937, celebrating in Florence.

The club's league form became highly erratic during the early 1930s, with varying league positions; it was during the [1933–34](/wiki/1933%E2%80%9334_Serie_A) season that Genova suffered their first ever relegation to [Serie B](/wiki/Serie_B), the second league of Italian football. Thankfully for the club, they were able to bounce back under the management of Vittorio Faroppa, winning promotion by finishing top of their group ahead of [Novara](/wiki/Novara_Calcio). In 1936, the ambitious Juan Culiolo took over as chairman of the club; in [1936–37](/wiki/1936%E2%80%9337_Serie_A) they achieved a 6th-place finish and also won the [Coppa Italia](/wiki/Coppa_Italia) by beating [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma) 1–0 with a goal from Mario Torti.

During the [following season](/wiki/1937%E2%80%9338_Serie_A) Genova finished in third place, this was a particularly tight season with winners [Ambrosiana-Inter](/wiki/F.C._Internazionale_Milano) finishing only three points ahead of the club. That summer [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) competed in the [1938 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) and won, three Genova players formed part of the triumphant squad in the form of [Sergio Bertoni](/wiki/Sergio_Bertoni), [Mario Genta](/wiki/Mario_Genta) and [Mario Perazzolo](/wiki/Mario_Perazzolo). The club finished the decade on a high, maintaining a top five foothold in the top level of the Italian football league system.

World War II affected dramatically the entire Italian football movement, but Genova did not recover as well as other clubs. In 1945, the club chose to revert their name to **Genoa Cricket and Foot-Ball Club**, the one which they had used in the very early days of the Italian championship. In the years just after the war, the club were still popular with the fans, with people previously associated with the club such as [Ottavio Barbieri](/wiki/Ottavio_Barbieri) and [William Garbutt](/wiki/William_Garbutt) returning for managerial spells. Genoa also had a new rival in the form of Sampdoria, who were founded by a merger of Associazione Calcio Andrea Doria and [Sampierdarenese](/wiki/Sampierdarenese) in 1946 and would groundshare at [Stadio Luigi Ferraris](/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris).

### Post-war period

Genoa side during 1956–57 season

After the Second World War the ability of Genoa to finish in the upper ranks of [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A) declined in a significant manner; throughout the rest of the 1940s the club were middle-table finishers. The [1948–49 season](/wiki/1948%E2%80%9349_Serie_A) saw three highly significant results, Genoa beat [Inter](/wiki/F.C._Internazionale_Milano) 4–1, the famous *[Grande Torino](/wiki/Grande_Torino)* side 3–0 and [Padova](/wiki/Calcio_Padova) 7–1. The 1950s started in poor fashion for the club, they had bought Argentine [Mario Boyé](/wiki/Mario_Boy%C3%A9) from [Boca Juniors](/wiki/Boca_Juniors) but he stayed only one season and the club [were relegated](/wiki/1950%E2%80%9351_Serie_A) after finishing bottom of the table, but after two seasons they achieved their return after winning Serie B, ahead of [Legnano](/wiki/A.C._Legnano). [Ragnar Nikolay Larsen](/wiki/Ragnar_Larsen) was a notable player for the club during this period and they sustained mid-table finishes for the rest of the decade.

Despite suffering a relegation in [1959–60](/wiki/1959%E2%80%9360_Serie_B) and then a promotion back up to Serie A in 1961–62, Genoa had a respectable amount of cup success in the first half of the 1960s. The club won the [Coppa delle Alpi](/wiki/Coppa_delle_Alpi) in 1962; it was the first time the competition had been competed between club teams instead of international ones, the final was played at home while Genoa beat French club [Grenoble Foot 38](/wiki/Grenoble_Foot_38) by 1–0 with a goal from Nizza. Genoa won the same competition again two years later, the final was held at the [Wankdorf Stadium](/wiki/Wankdorf_Stadium) in [Bern](/wiki/Bern), Switzerland; Genoa defeated [Catania](/wiki/Calcio_Catania) 2–0, with both goals from Giampaolo Piaceri to take the trophy.

1962 [Cup of the Alps](/wiki/Cup_of_the_Alps) triumph

The celebrations for the club did not last long however, as the year following their last cup success they were relegated down to Serie B again. This time their stay in the second tier of the [Italian football league system](/wiki/Italian_football_league_system) would be far longer than previous relegations, the club was unstable as it changed manager each season. Genoa even experienced their first relegation to [Serie C](/wiki/Serie_C) in 1970, financially the club fell into difficulties and had several ownership changes.

### Mixed times

Throughout the 1970s, Genoa would mostly play in the second tier. Under the management of [Arturo Silvestri](/wiki/Arturo_Silvestri) the club made its way back to Serie A for the [1973–74](/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Serie_A) season, but they were relegated straight back down. For the return of *Il Grifone* to Serie A a couple of seasons later, the squad featured the likes of [Roberto Rosato](/wiki/Roberto_Rosato), [Bruno Conti](/wiki/Bruno_Conti) and a young [Roberto Pruzzo](/wiki/Roberto_Pruzzo). This time they stuck it out in the top division for two seasons before succumbing to relegation in [1977–78](/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_Serie_A); the relegation was particularly cruel as the side above them [Fiorentina](/wiki/ACF_Fiorentina) survived on goal-difference of just a single goal, the two teams had played each other on the final day of the season ending in a 0–0 draw.

First Genoa side of the 1980s

The relegation was bad for the club in more ways than one, they lost some of their top players who could have offered them a swift return; such as Roberto Pruzzo's move to [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma) where he would go on to have great success. After a couple of middle-table finishes in Serie B, Genoa earned promotion during the [1980–81](/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_Serie_B) season under manager [Luigi Simoni](/wiki/Luigi_Simoni), the club finished as runners-up behind only [AC Milan](/wiki/A.C._Milan) who had been relegated the previous season for their part in the Totonero betting scandal.

Still with Simoni at the helm as manager, Genoa were able to survive in Serie A for their returning season, finishing just one point ahead of the relegated AC Milan. In a dramatic last day of the season, Genoa were trailing 2–1 to [Napoli](/wiki/S.S.C._Napoli) with five minutes left, until on the 85th minute Mario Faccenda scored the goal that secured the point needed by Genoa, starting an owing friendship between the two club's fans.
A couple of seasons later in [1983–84](/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_Serie_A), Genoa would not be so lucky, despite beating champions [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus_FC) on the final day of the season, the club were relegated even though they finished the season with the same number of points as surviving [Lazio](/wiki/S.S._Lazio); this was because Lazio had recorded better results in matches against Genoa.

### European experience

The club was purchased by [Calabrese](/wiki/Calabria) entrepreneur [Aldo Spinelli](/wiki/Aldo_Spinelli) in 1985 and despite no longer having Simoni as manager, Genoa were finishing in the top half of Serie B. After a slip in form during 1987–88 (failing to be promoted by a mere point in 1986–87, then having to struggle not to be retroceded the following season, being spared that fate again by a mere point), Genoa refocused their energy and were able to achieve promotion back into Serie A in 1988–89, finishing as champions ahead of [Bari](/wiki/A.S._Bari). Genoa, with an experienced trainer as [Osvaldo Bagnoli](/wiki/Osvaldo_Bagnoli) who knew how to get the best out of underdog teams (he managed to win a championship at the helm of [Hellas Verona](/wiki/Hellas_Verona) in the eighties) and with a team sporting the talents of [Carlos Aguilera](/wiki/Carlos_Aguilera_(Uruguayan_footballer)) and [Tomáš Skuhravý](/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Skuhrav%C3%BD) among others achieved highs during the [1990–91](/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_Serie_A) season where they finished fourth, remaining undefeated at home for the entire campaign, winning games against all the big sides including Juventus, [Inter](/wiki/F.C._Internazionale_Milano), Milan, [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma), Lazio, Fiorentina, Napoli, as well as their local rivals [Sampdoria](/wiki/U.C._Sampdoria) who won the title that season.

Subsequently, the club gained entry to the [UEFA Cup](/wiki/UEFA_Cup) in the [1991–92](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_UEFA_Cup) season. Genoa had a good run, making it to the semi-finals before being knocked out by [Ajax](/wiki/AFC_Ajax), that season's winners of the competition; notably Genoa did the double over [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) in the quarter-finals, becoming the first Italian side to beat the *Reds* at [Anfield](/wiki/Anfield). Unfortunately for Genoa, this success was soon followed by a 'Dark Age' following the departure of Osvaldo Bagnoli (who chose to move away from Genoa to spend more time with his daughter, whose health was rapidly declining) and the failure of the management to replace key players as they grew old or were ceded to other teams. Noted Genoa players during this period included [Gianluca Signorini](/wiki/Gianluca_Signorini), Carlos Aguilera, [Stefano Eranio](/wiki/Stefano_Eranio), [Roberto Onorati](/wiki/Roberto_Onorati) and [John van 't Schip](/wiki/John_van_%27t_Schip).
 Chairman Spinelli had a very different management approach from that of most businessmen turned football club owners. While his colleagues saw football as a marketing and [public relation](/wiki/Public_relations) investment and were quite ready to siphon funds out of their main business to keep their teams afloat and replenish their player roster Spinelli saw Genoa as another business whose main aim was that of generating revenue for its owner (namely, himself) and so was more than happy to sell esteemed players for hefty revenues of which just a minimal fraction was then re-invested in the team, often for the acquisition of lesser-valued replacements or virtual unknowns. Thus he proved all-too-eager to sell Uruguayan striker [Carlos Aguilera](/wiki/Carlos_Aguilera_(Uruguayan_footballer)) and to replace him with the markedly inferior [Kazuyoshi Miura](/wiki/Kazuyoshi_Miura) from Japanese side [Yomiuri Verdy](/wiki/Tokyo_Verdy) (a deal that especially pleased him since the Japanese sponsors were actually paying him to let Miura play in Serie A). The same season as their UEFA Cup run, they finished just one place above the relegation zone; in the seasons following Genoa remained in the lower half of the table.

During the [1994–95](/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Serie_A) season, Genoa were narrowly relegated; they finished level on points with [Padova](/wiki/Calcio_Padova) after the normal season period. This meant a relegation play-out was to be played between the two in [Florence](/wiki/Florence). The game was tied 1–1 at full-time and went to a [penalty shoot-out](/wiki/Penalty_shoot-out_(association_football)). Genoa eventually lost the shoot-out 5–4. While back down in Serie B, the club had another taste of international cup success when they became the [final winners](/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_Anglo-Italian_Cup) of the [Anglo-Italian Cup](/wiki/Anglo-Italian_Cup) by beating [Port Vale](/wiki/Port_Vale_F.C.) 5–2 with [Gennaro Ruotolo](/wiki/Gennaro_Ruotolo) scoring a [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick). Chairman Spinelli sold Genoa in 1997, moving onto other clubs ([Alessandria](/wiki/U.S._Alessandria_Calcio_1912) and, then [Livorno](/wiki/A.S._Livorno_Calcio)). The late 1990s and early 2000s would be the most trying time in the history of the club, with constant managerial changes, a poor financial situation and little hope of gaining promotion, outside of a decent 6th-place finish in [1999–00](/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_Serie_B). From 1997 until 2003, Genoa had a total of three different owners and four different chairmen, before the club was passed on to the toys and games [tycoon](/wiki/Business_magnate) from [Irpinia](/wiki/Irpinia), [Enrico Preziosi](/wiki/Enrico_Preziosi), already chairman of Como, a football club he previously owned.

### Recent times

Genoa side during 2016–17 season

Preziosi took over in 2003, when Genoa should have been relegated to C1 series after a dismal season, but was instead "saved" along with Catania and Salernitana by the football federation's controversial decision to extend [Serie B](/wiki/Serie_B) to 24 teams. Things started to look up for Genoa; they won Serie B in 2004–05. However, allegations were raised that the club had [fixed a match](/wiki/Caso_Genoa) on the last day of the season between themselves and [Venezia](/wiki/S.S.C._Venezia). The 3–2 victory in the match saw Genoa win the league, with a draw having been good enough to maintain its position in the end. The Disciplinary Committee of [FIGC](/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) saw fit to instead place Genoa bottom of the league and relegate them down to [Serie C1](/wiki/Serie_C1) with a three-point deduction on 27 July 2005.

For their season in Serie C1 for [2005–06](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Serie_C1), Genoa were hit with a six-point penalty from the previous season. After leading for much of the season, they eventually finished as runners-up and were entered into the play-offs, beating [Monza](/wiki/A.C._Monza_Brianza_1912) 2–1 on aggregate to achieve promotion back into Serie B. During the summer break [Gian Piero Gasperini](/wiki/Gian_Piero_Gasperini) was brought in as the new manager, he helped the club to gain promotion during the [2006–07](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Serie_B) season, it was ensured on the last day of the season where they drew a 0–0 with [Napoli](/wiki/S.S.C._Napoli), both clubs were happily promoted back into [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A).

The [2007–08 season](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Serie_A), the first Serie A championship played by Genoa in 12 years, saw it finishing in a respectable tenth place, right after the "big ones" of Italian football.

A careful summer market session saw chairman, Preziosi strengthening the core of the team while parting from some players on favourable economical terms (for example selling striker [Marco Borriello](/wiki/Marco_Borriello) to [AC Milan](/wiki/A.C._Milan) for a hefty sum). Genoa's aims for the [2008–09 season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Serie_A) were set on a UEFA Cup spot. This was achieved after a strong season which saw the team finish fifth in Serie A, besting traditional powerhouses like [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus_FC), [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma), and Milan, and winning both Genoa derbies against [Sampdoria](/wiki/U.C._Sampdoria), with [Diego Milito](/wiki/Diego_Milito) finishing among the top scorers of the championship. Genoa subsequently lost Milito and midfielder [Thiago Motta](/wiki/Thiago_Motta) to [Internazionale](/wiki/Inter_Milan), but were able to bring in striker [Hernán Crespo](/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Crespo). Things however did not go as planned, with the injury-plagued team eliminated in the early stages of the Europa League and Coppa Italia and falling to a ninth-place finish in Serie A in [2010](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Serie_A).

In the [2010–11 season](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Serie_A), Genoa, whose ranks had been revolutionised once again save for some long-serving players, struggled along in the mid-positions of the league; a slew of questionable results early in the season led chairman Preziosi to fire trainer [Gian Piero Gasperini](/wiki/Gian_Piero_Gasperini), who had led the team since the [2007–08 season](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Serie_A), and to select [Davide Ballardini](/wiki/Davide_Ballardini) as his successor. The newcomers, despite not securing memorable successes, kept the team steadily afloat in the "left part" of the ranking, managing to win two consecutive derby matches against rivals Sampdoria in December and May.

The [2011–12](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Serie_A) and [2012–13 seasons](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Serie_A) saw Genoa place in 17th both times, one spot away from relegation to Serie B.

In the [2014–15 season](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Serie_A), Genoa, in sixth place and set to qualify for the [UEFA Europa League](/wiki/UEFA_Europa_League) qualifying round, were denied a UEFA license because they filed paperwork late and because the [Stadio Luigi Ferraris](/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris) was not currently up to standard for UEFA competition. The spot was passed on to 7th placed [Sampdoria](/wiki/U.C._Sampdoria).

This damaged Genoa's momentum, and Genoa coasted to an eleventh-place finish in the [2015–16 season](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Serie_A). In [2016–17](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Serie_A), Genoa avoided relegation in 16th-place, and once again finished mid-table in the [2017–18 season](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Serie_A). In the [2018–19 season](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Serie_A), Genoa mathematically avoided relegation from Serie A. They were tied on 38 points with [Empoli](/wiki/Empoli_F.C.), but Empoli went down due to Genoa's superior head-to-head record.

In the [2021–22 season](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Serie_A), Genoa finished 19th in the league table to be relegated after fifteen years in top division. In the [2022–23 season](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Serie_B), the club finished second in Serie B, to promote back to Serie A after one season. In the 2023–24 season, Genoa Football Club maintains its position in Serie A and is not facing relegation. The club continues to compete in Italy's top football league, demonstrating resilience and determination to stay at the highest level of Italian football.

In the [2025–26 season](/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Serie_A), Genoa gets off to a poor start, leading to the dismissal of [Patrick Vieira](/wiki/Patrick_Vieira), who had performed well the previous year. The arrival of the new coach, former world champion [Daniele De Rossi](/wiki/Daniele_De_Rossi), greatly benefits the team, which regains some stability, allowing them to secure their place in Serie A with relative ease. In football terms, the [historic Genoa-Pisa rivalry](/wiki/Maritime_republics) is revived this year, and as tradition dictates, Genoa came out on top. In the first leg at [Marassi](/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris), the match ended in a 1-1 draw, with Colombo opening the scoring at the 15th minute and Léris equalizing for Pisa at the 38th minute. In the return match in Pisa, after Canestrelli gave Pisa an early lead at the 19th minute, Ekhator responded for Genoa at the 41st minute, and Colombo scored the winning goal at the 55th minute.
The season also saw the confirmation with good performances of [Lorenzo Colombo](/wiki/Lorenzo_Colombo), a young talent from the [AC Milan Youth Sector](/wiki/AC_Milan_Youth_Sector).

## Colours, badge and nicknames

As Genoa was a British-run club, the first ever colours were those of the [England national football team](/wiki/England_national_football_team). Not long into the club's footballing history, the kit was changed to white and blue stripes in 1899; the blue was chosen to represent the sea as Genoa is a [port city](/wiki/Port_city). In 1901 the club finally settled for their most famous red and blue halves shirt, this gained them the nickname of *rossoblù*.

One of the nicknames of Genoa is *Il Grifone* which means "the [griffin](/wiki/Griffin)"; this is derived from the [coat of arms](/wiki/Coat_of_arms) belonging to the city of Genoa. The coat of arms features two golden griffins, either side of the [Saint George's Cross](/wiki/Saint_George%27s_Cross). As well as being present on both the flag and coat of arms of the city of Genoa, the cross is evocative of the club's English founders. St. George was also the patron saint of the former [Republic of Genoa](/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa). The actual club badge of *Genoa Cricket and Football Club* is heavily derived from the city coat of arms, but also incorporated the club's red and blue colours.

## Supporters and rivalries

Main article: [Derby della Lanterna](/wiki/Derby_della_Lanterna)

Genoa CFC has the bulk of its fans in [Liguria](/wiki/Liguria), however they are also popular in [Piedmont](/wiki/Piedmont) and the [Aosta Valley](/wiki/Aosta_Valley). The seafaring traditions of the Genoese and the presence of Genoese communities in distant countries did much to spread the appeal of Genoa some further than just Italy, and immigrants founded fan clubs in [Buenos Aires](/wiki/Buenos_Aires), [Amsterdam](/wiki/Amsterdam), [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo), [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto), [New York](/wiki/New_York_City), [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco), [Barcelona](/wiki/Barcelona), [Iceland](/wiki/Iceland) and other places.

Genoa fans in June 2007 at [Piazza de Ferrari](/wiki/Piazza_De_Ferrari_(Genoa)), celebrating their return to *Serie A*.

The most significant and traditional rivalry for Genoa, is the inner-city one with the club with whom they share a ground; [Sampdoria](/wiki/U.C._Sampdoria). The two clubs compete together in the heated *[Derby della Lanterna](/wiki/Derby_della_Lanterna)* ("Derby of the Lantern"); a reference to the [Lighthouse of Genoa](/wiki/Lighthouse_of_Genoa). Genoa's supporters also have a strong distaste for [AC Milan](/wiki/A.C._Milan). A clash between opposing supporters in January 1995 resulted in the death of Genoese Vincenzo Spagnolo, who was stabbed to death by Milanese Simone Barbaglia. The assailant was a member of an informal group of [football hooligans](/wiki/Football_hooligans) dubbed "The Barbour Ones", who used to routinely carry bladed weapons to matches, a practice made possible by the relaxed security measures of the time.

Conversely, the fans of Genoa have long standing friendships with [Napoli](/wiki/S.S.C._Napoli) (which goes back to the 1982 last match of the season). On the last day of the [2006–07](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_Serie_B) season, Genoa and [Napoli](/wiki/S.S.C._Napoli) drew a practical 0–0 ensuring both were promoted back into Serie A; Genoa [ultras](/wiki/Ultras) could be seen holding up banners saying "Benvenuto fratello napoletano", meaning, "Welcome, Neapolitan brother", and the two sets of fans celebrated together in a warm and ever-co-operating manner.

On the other hand, the amicable relationship with the red-and-yellow supporters of [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma), fostered by the cession of striker [Roberto Pruzzo](/wiki/Roberto_Pruzzo) in 1979 and lasting for most of the 80's has, in recent years, cooled up a bit while another strong fraternity, which saw Genoese football fans on friendly terms with [Torino](/wiki/Torino_F.C.) (since the exchange of [Gigi Meroni](/wiki/Gigi_Meroni) between the two clubs at the end of the [1963–64](/wiki/1963%E2%80%9364_Serie_A) season and his untimely death on 15 October 1967) has perhaps broken-down for good after the Torino-Genoa match of season 2008–09.

Starved for points and risking a humiliating relegation (one of many in a troubled recent history) the Turinese fans expected a *friendly* treatment from Genoa, which, in the heat of a pitched battle with [Fiorentina](/wiki/ACF_Fiorentina) for the fourth place (which could have won a [Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League) spot for the team) did not comply, soundly beating Torino and to many effects sealing its fate. When during early August 2009 Genoa scheduled a friendly match with [Nice](/wiki/OGC_Nice) in southern [Piedmont](/wiki/Piedmont), many Turinese hooligans travelled to the match location with the precise intention of starting trouble and disorder to "get even" with Genoa and its fans.

## Ownership and structure

### 777 Partners

On 23 September 2021, it was announced that Genoa had been acquired by [777 Partners](/wiki/777_Partners), a [US](/wiki/US)-based private investment firm founded by Steven W. Pasko and Josh Wander. While terms were not publicly released, sources close to the deal revealed that the team was acquired for its enterprise value of $175 million. Despite being relegated to Serie B in their very first season under 777 Partners ownership, Genoa immediately made it back to [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A) the following year.

### Cricket

Early on, the club transformed from a multi-sport club to one exclusively focused on football. In 2007, a group of club supporters formed a section dedicated to cricket. It currently competes under the name Genoa Cricket Club 1893 in Serie A of the Italian cricket league.

# Rosaceae

Not to be confused with [Rosacea](/wiki/Rosacea_(disambiguation)).

**Rosaceae** ([/roʊˈzeɪsi.iː, -ˌaɪ, -ˌeɪ/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English)), the **rose family**, is a [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) of [flowering plants](/wiki/Flowering_plant) that includes 4,828 known species in 91 [genera](/wiki/Genera).

The name is derived from the [type genus](/wiki/Type_genus) *[Rosa](/wiki/Rose)*. The family includes herbs, shrubs, and trees. Most species are [deciduous](/wiki/Deciduous), but some are [evergreen](/wiki/Evergreen). They have a worldwide range but are most diverse in the [Northern Hemisphere](/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere).

Many economically important products come from the Rosaceae, including various edible fruits, such as [apples](/wiki/Apple), [pears](/wiki/Pear), [quinces](/wiki/Quince), [apricots](/wiki/Apricot), [plums](/wiki/Plum), [cherries](/wiki/Cherry), [peaches](/wiki/Peach), [raspberries](/wiki/Raspberries), [blackberries](/wiki/Blackberry), [loquats](/wiki/Loquat), [strawberries](/wiki/Strawberries), [rose hips](/wiki/Rose_hip), [hawthorns](/wiki/Crataegus), and [almonds](/wiki/Almonds). The family also includes popular ornamental trees and shrubs, such as [roses](/wiki/Rose), [meadowsweets](/wiki/Spiraea), [rowans](/wiki/Rowan), [firethorns](/wiki/Firethorn), and [photinias](/wiki/Photinia).

Among the most species-rich genera in the family are *[Alchemilla](/wiki/Alchemilla)* (270), *[Sorbus](/wiki/Sorbus)* (260), *[Crataegus](/wiki/Crataegus)* (260), *[Cotoneaster](/wiki/Cotoneaster)* (260), *[Rubus](/wiki/Rubus)* (250), and *[Prunus](/wiki/Prunus)* (340), which contains the [plums](/wiki/Plum), [cherries](/wiki/Cherries), [peaches](/wiki/Peach), [apricots](/wiki/Apricot), and [almonds](/wiki/Almond). However, all of these numbers should be seen as estimates—much taxonomic work remains.

## Description

Rosaceae can be woody trees, shrubs, climbers or herbaceous plants. The herbs are mostly perennials, but some annuals also exist, such as *[Aphanes arvensis](/wiki/Aphanes_arvensis)*.

### Leaves

The [leaves](/wiki/Leaves) are generally arranged [spirally](/wiki/Phyllotaxis), but have an opposite arrangement in some species. They can be simple or [pinnately](/wiki/Pinnation) compound (either odd- or even-pinnate). Compound leaves appear in around 30 genera. The leaf margin is most often serrate. Paired [stipules](/wiki/Stipule) are generally present and are considered a primitive feature within the family, though they have been independently lost in many groups of Amygdaloideae (previously called Spiraeoideae). The stipules are sometimes adnate (attached surface to surface) to the [petiole](/wiki/Petiole_(botany)). Glands or [extrafloral nectaries](/wiki/Extrafloral_nectaries) may be present on leaf margins or petioles. Spines may be present on the midrib of leaflets and the rachis of compound leaves.

### Flowers

Flowers of plants in the rose family are generally described as "showy". They are [radially symmetrical](/wiki/Actinomorphic), and almost always hermaphroditic. Rosaceae generally have five [sepals](/wiki/Sepal), [five petals](/wiki/Pentapetalae), and many spirally arranged [stamens](/wiki/Stamen). The bases of the sepals, petals, and stamens are fused together to form a characteristic cup-like structure called a [hypanthium](/wiki/Hypanthium). They can be arranged in [spikes](/wiki/Raceme), or [heads](/wiki/Head_(botany)). Solitary flowers are rare. Rosaceae have a variety of color petals, but blue is almost completely absent.

### Fruits and seeds

The [fruits](/wiki/Fruit) occur in many varieties and were once considered the main characters for the definition of subfamilies amongst Rosaceae, giving rise to a fundamentally artificial subdivision. They can be [follicles](/wiki/Follicle_(fruit)), [capsules](/wiki/Capsule_(fruit)), [nuts](/wiki/Nut_(fruit)), [achenes](/wiki/Achene), [drupes](/wiki/Drupe) (*[Prunus](/wiki/Prunus)*), and [accessory fruits](/wiki/Accessory_fruit), like the [pome](/wiki/Pome) of an apple, the [hip](/wiki/Rose_hip) of a [rose](/wiki/Rose), or the [receptacle](/wiki/Receptacle_(botany))-derived [aggregate](/wiki/Aggregate_fruit) [accessory](/wiki/Accessory_fruit) fruit of a [strawberry](/wiki/Fragaria). Many fruits of the family are edible, but their seeds often contain [amygdalin](/wiki/Amygdalin), which can release [cyanide](/wiki/Cyanide) during digestion if the seed is damaged.

## Taxonomy

### Taxonomic history

The family was traditionally divided into six subfamilies: [Rosoideae](/wiki/Rosoideae), [Spiraeoideae](/wiki/Spiraeoideae), [Maloideae](/wiki/Maloideae) (Pomoideae), [Amygdaloideae](/wiki/Amygdaloideae) (Prunoideae), Neuradoideae, and Chrysobalanoideae, and most of these were treated as families by various authors. Later (1971), [Chrysobalanoideae](/wiki/Chrysobalanaceae) was placed in [Malpighiales](/wiki/Malpighiales) and [Neuradoideae](/wiki/Neuradaceae) has been assigned to [Malvales](/wiki/Malvales). Schulze-Menz, in Engler's Syllabus edited by Melchior (1964), recognized Rosoideae, Dryadoideae, Lyonothamnoideae, Spireoideae, Amygdaloideae, and Maloideae. They were primarily diagnosed by the structure of the fruits. More recent work has identified that not all of these groups were [monophyletic](/wiki/Monophyletic). Hutchinson (1964) and Kalkman (2004)  recognized only tribes (17 and 21, respectively). Takhtajan (1997) delimited 21 tribes in 10 subfamilies: Filipenduloideae, Rosoideae, Ruboideae, Potentilloideae, Coleogynoideae, Kerroideae, Amygdaloideae (Prunoideae), Spireoideae, Maloideae (Pyroideae), Dichotomanthoideae. A more modern model comprises three subfamilies, one of which (Rosoideae) has largely remained the same.

While the boundaries of the Rosaceae are not disputed, there is no general agreement as to how many genera it contains. Areas of divergent opinion include the treatment of *[Potentilla](/wiki/Potentilla) s.l.* and *[Sorbus](/wiki/Sorbus) s.l.*. Compounding the problem is that [apomixis](/wiki/Apomixis) is common in several genera. This results in an uncertainty in the number of species contained in each of these genera, due to the difficulty of dividing apomictic complexes into species. For example, *[Cotoneaster](/wiki/Cotoneaster)* contains between 70 and 300 species, *[Rosa](/wiki/Rose)* around 100 (including the taxonomically complex [dog roses](/wiki/Dog_rose)), *[Sorbus](/wiki/Sorbus)* 100 to 200 species, *[Crataegus](/wiki/Crataegus)* between 200 and 1,000, *[Alchemilla](/wiki/Alchemilla)* around 300 species, *[Potentilla](/wiki/Potentilla)* roughly 500, and *[Rubus](/wiki/Rubus)* hundreds, or possibly even thousands of species.

### Genera

Main article: [List of Rosaceae genera](/wiki/List_of_Rosaceae_genera)

Identified clades include:

- Subfamily [Rosoideae](/wiki/Rosoideae): Traditionally composed of those genera bearing [aggregate fruits](/wiki/Fruit#Aggregate_fruit) that are made up of small achenes or [drupelets](/wiki/Drupelet), and often the fleshy part of the fruit (e.g. [strawberry](/wiki/Strawberry)) is the [receptacle](/wiki/Receptacle_(botany)) or the stalk bearing the carpels. The circumscription is now narrowed (excluding, for example, the Dryadoideae), but it still remains a diverse group containing five or six tribes and 20 or more genera, including rose, *[Rubus](/wiki/Rubus)* (blackberry, raspberry), *[Fragaria](/wiki/Fragaria)* (strawberry), *[Potentilla](/wiki/Potentilla)*, and *[Geum](/wiki/Geum)*.
- Subfamily [Amygdaloideae](/wiki/Amygdaloideae): Within this group remains an identified clade with a pome fruit, traditionally known as subfamily Maloideae (or Pyroideae) which included genera such as *[Malus](/wiki/Malus)* (apple), *[Cotoneaster](/wiki/Cotoneaster)*, and *[Crataegus](/wiki/Crataegus)* (hawthorn). To separate it at the subfamily level would leave the remaining genera as a [paraphyletic](/wiki/Paraphyletic) group, so it has been expanded to include the former Spiraeoideae and Amygdaloideae. The subfamily has sometimes been referred to by the name "Spiraeoideae", but this is not permitted by the [International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants](/wiki/International_Code_of_Nomenclature_for_algae,_fungi,_and_plants).
- Subfamily [Dryadoideae](/wiki/Dryadoideae): Fruits are achenes with hairy styles, and includes five genera (*[Dryas](/wiki/Dryas_(plant))*, *[Cercocarpus](/wiki/Cercocarpus)*, *[Chamaebatia](/wiki/Chamaebatia)*, *[Cowania](/wiki/Cowania_(plant))*, and *[Purshia](/wiki/Purshia)*), most species of which form [root nodules](/wiki/Root_nodule) which host nitrogen-fixing bacteria from the genus *[Frankia](/wiki/Frankia)*.

The sister relationship between Amygdaloideae and Dryadoideae is supported by the following shared biochemical characters not found in Rosoideae: production of [cyanogenic glycosides](/wiki/Cyanogenic_glycoside) and production of [sorbitol](/wiki/Sorbitol).

## Distribution and habitat

The Rosaceae have a cosmopolitan distribution, being found nearly everywhere except for Antarctica. They are primarily concentrated in the Northern Hemisphere in regions that are not desert or tropical rainforest.

## Uses

The rose family is considered one of the six most economically important crop plant families, and includes [apples](/wiki/Apple), [pears](/wiki/Pear), [quinces](/wiki/Quince), [medlars](/wiki/Medlar), [loquats](/wiki/Loquat), [almonds](/wiki/Almond), [peaches](/wiki/Peach), [apricots](/wiki/Apricot), [plums](/wiki/Plum), [cherries](/wiki/Cherry), [strawberries](/wiki/Strawberry), [blackberries](/wiki/Blackberry), [raspberries](/wiki/Raspberry), [sloes](/wiki/Prunus_spinosa), and [roses](/wiki/Rose).

Many genera are also highly valued ornamental plants. These include trees and shrubs (*[Cotoneaster](/wiki/Cotoneaster)*, *[Chaenomeles](/wiki/Chaenomeles)*, *[Crataegus](/wiki/Crataegus)*, *[Dasiphora](/wiki/Dasiphora)*, *[Exochorda](/wiki/Exochorda)*, *[Kerria](/wiki/Kerria_(plant))*, *[Photinia](/wiki/Photinia)*, *[Physocarpus](/wiki/Physocarpus)*, *[Prunus](/wiki/Prunus)*, *[Pyracantha](/wiki/Pyracantha)*, *[Rhodotypos](/wiki/Rhodotypos)*, *[Rosa](/wiki/Rose)*, *[Sorbus](/wiki/Sorbus)*, *[Spiraea](/wiki/Spiraea)*), herbaceous perennials (*[Alchemilla](/wiki/Alchemilla)*, *[Aruncus](/wiki/Aruncus)*, *[Filipendula](/wiki/Filipendula)*, *[Geum](/wiki/Geum)*, *[Potentilla](/wiki/Potentilla)*, *[Sanguisorba](/wiki/Sanguisorba)*), alpine plants (*[Dryas](/wiki/Dryas_(plant))*, *Geum*, *Potentilla*) and climbers (*[Rosa](/wiki/Rosa_(plant))*).

However, several genera are also introduced noxious weeds in some parts of the world, costing money to be controlled. These [invasive plants](/wiki/Invasive_plant) can have negative impacts on the diversity of local ecosystems once established. Such naturalised pests include *[Acaena](/wiki/Acaena)*, *Cotoneaster*, *Crataegus*, and *Pyracantha*.

In Bulgaria and parts of western Asia, the production of rose oil from fresh flowers such as *[Rosa damascena](/wiki/Rosa_%C3%97_damascena)*, *[Rosa gallica](/wiki/Rosa_gallica)*, and other species is an important economic industry.

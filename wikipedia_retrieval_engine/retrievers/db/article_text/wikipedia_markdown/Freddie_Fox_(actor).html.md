**Frederick Samson Robert Morice Fox** (born 5 April 1989) is an English film and stage actor. His prominent screen performances include roles as singer [Marilyn](/wiki/Marilyn_(singer)) in the BBC's [Boy George](/wiki/Boy_George) biopic *[Worried About the Boy](/wiki/Worried_About_the_Boy)* (2010), Freddie Baxter in series *[Cucumber](/wiki/Cucumber_(British_TV_series))* (2015) and *[Banana](/wiki/Banana_(TV_series))* (2015), [Jeremy Bamber](/wiki/Jeremy_Bamber) in *[White House Farm](/wiki/White_House_Farm_(TV_series))* (2020) and Spider Webb in *[Slow Horses](/wiki/Slow_Horses)* (2022–2023).

His theatre credits include starring as Simon Bliss in *[Hay Fever](/wiki/Hay_Fever_(play))* (2012) at the [Noël Coward Theatre](/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward_Theatre); as [Oscar Wilde](/wiki/Oscar_Wilde)'s young lover [Lord Alfred "Bosie" Douglas](/wiki/Lord_Alfred_Douglas) in *[The Judas Kiss](/wiki/The_Judas_Kiss_(play))* (2012–2013) at the [Hampstead Theatre](/wiki/Hampstead_Theatre), during a UK tour, and in a [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) transfer; as [Romeo](/wiki/Romeo) in [Romeo and Juliet](/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet) (2015) at the [Sheffield Crucible](/wiki/Sheffield_Crucible) and in [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh)'s 2016 production at the [Garrick Theatre](/wiki/Garrick_Theatre); as [Tristan Tzara](/wiki/Tristan_Tzara) in *[Travesties](/wiki/Travesties)* (2016–2017) at the [Menier Chocolate Factory](/wiki/Menier_Chocolate_Factory) and [Apollo Theatre](/wiki/Apollo_Theatre); as Lord Goring in *[An Ideal Husband](/wiki/An_Ideal_Husband)* (2018) at the [Vaudeville Theatre](/wiki/Vaudeville_Theatre); and as [Edmond Rostand](/wiki/Edmond_Rostand) in [Edmond de Bergerac](/wiki/Edmond_de_Bergerac) (2019) at the [Birmingham Repertory Theatre](/wiki/Birmingham_Repertory_Theatre) and during a UK tour.

Fox appeared on the big screen in *[The Three Musketeers](/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers_(2011_film))* (2011), *[The Riot Club](/wiki/The_Riot_Club)* (2014), *[Pride](/wiki/Pride_(2014_film))* (2014), *[Victor Frankenstein](/wiki/Victor_Frankenstein_(film))* (2015), *[King Arthur: Legend of the Sword](/wiki/King_Arthur:_Legend_of_the_Sword)* (2017), *[Black '47](/wiki/Black_%2747_(film))* (2018) and *[Fanny Lye Deliver'd](/wiki/Fanny_Lye_Deliver%27d)* (2019).

## Early life and education

See also: [Robin Fox family](/wiki/Robin_Fox_family)

Frederick Samson Robert Morice Fox was born on 5 April 1989 in [Hammersmith](/wiki/Hammersmith), [London](/wiki/London). He was raised between London and the family home in [Kimmeridge Bay](/wiki/Kimmeridge_Bay), [Dorset](/wiki/Dorset) and was surrounded by actors and writers from a young age.

He is the son of actress [Joanna David](/wiki/Joanna_David) (née Joanna Elizabeth Hacking) and actor [Edward Fox](/wiki/Edward_Fox_(actor)). He is the younger brother of actress [Emilia Fox](/wiki/Emilia_Fox) and the half-brother of Lucy Arabella Preston (the Viscountess Gormanston), the grandson of actress Angela Worthington Fox and theatrical agent [Robin Fox](/wiki/Robin_Fox_(theatrical_agent)), and the great-grandson of the playwright [Frederick Lonsdale](/wiki/Frederick_Lonsdale). He is the nephew of producer [Robert Fox](/wiki/Robert_Fox_(producer)) and actor [James Fox](/wiki/James_Fox), and a cousin to [Laurence](/wiki/Laurence_Fox) and [Jack](/wiki/Jack_Fox_(actor)). He was named after [Fred Zinnemann](/wiki/Fred_Zinnemann), who directed his father in *[The Day of the Jackal](/wiki/The_Day_of_the_Jackal_(film))*, and after his great-great-grandfather Victorian industrialist [Samson Fox](/wiki/Samson_Fox), who invented the corrugated boiler flue and built [Harrogate](/wiki/Harrogate)'s former Kursaal now known as the [Royal Hall](/wiki/Royal_Hall,_Harrogate). Asked if he believes in his acting gene, he said, "I used not to until my sister [Milly](/wiki/Emilia_Fox) was on the BBC's *[Who Do You Think You Are?](/wiki/Who_Do_You_Think_You_Are%3F_(British_TV_series))*" where they discovered the thespian thread goes back 100 years.

From 1994 to 2002, Fox was educated at [Arnold House School](/wiki/Arnold_House_School), a [preparatory school](/wiki/Preparatory_school_(United_Kingdom)) for boys in the [St John's Wood](/wiki/St_John%27s_Wood) area of London, where he was [head boy](/wiki/Head_boy). He was [prefect](/wiki/Prefect) during secondary school at [Bryanston School](/wiki/Bryanston_School), a boarding school in [Dorset](/wiki/Dorset) where he perfected his [elocution](/wiki/Elocution) and overcame fairly serious dyslexia. During his time there, he portrayed a headmaster in *A Risky Business*, a play written especially for him by a classics master.

He then attended the [Guildhall School of Music and Drama](/wiki/Guildhall_School_of_Music_and_Drama) in London and graduated in 2010. His drama teachers praised his talent for acting.

## Career

### Beginnings

Fox's first screen appearance was at age seven as an extra in the 1997 TV adaptation of *Rebecca*. In 2009, he appeared in *[St Trinian's 2: The Legend of Fritton's Gold](/wiki/St_Trinian%27s_2:_The_Legend_of_Fritton%27s_Gold)* as head boy and in *[Agatha Christie's Marple](/wiki/Agatha_Christie%27s_Marple)* as Tom Savage. In 2010, he starred as Jamie in the musical *[The Last Five Years](/wiki/The_Last_Five_Years)* at the Silk Street Theatre and, after a sell-out success, at the [Barbican Pit Theatre](/wiki/Barbican_Pit).

### 2010–2015: *Worried About the Boy*, *Cucumber*, *Banana*

Fox starred in his first breakthrough role as the androgynous 1980s pop star [Marilyn](/wiki/Marilyn_(singer)) in *[Worried About The Boy](/wiki/Worried_About_The_Boy)* based on the life of the singer [Boy George](/wiki/Boy_George). He appeared as Peter Scabius in *[Any Human Heart](/wiki/Any_Human_Heart_(TV_series))* and as Guy Wells in *Rosamunde Pilcher's Shades of Love*. He played cleft-palated Camille Chandebise in *[A Flea in Her Ear](/wiki/A_Flea_in_Her_Ear)* at [The Old Vic](/wiki/The_Old_Vic), Tony Davenport in the stage play revival of *[Cause Célèbre](/wiki/Cause_C%C3%A9l%C3%A8bre_(play))* at [The Old Vic](/wiki/The_Old_Vic), and Ratallack in *[The Shadow Line](/wiki/The_Shadow_Line_(TV_series))*.

His international film debut came through his role as King [Louis XIII](/wiki/Louis_XIII) of France in *[The Three Musketeers](/wiki/The_Three_Musketeers_(2011_film))* (2011). In 2012, he played the title role in *[The Mystery of Edwin Drood](/wiki/The_Mystery_of_Edwin_Drood_(miniseries))*. He appeared in *[Lewis](/wiki/Lewis_(TV_series))* as Sebastian Dromgoole and in [Tom Stoppard](/wiki/Tom_Stoppard)'s critically acclaimed period drama *[Parade's End](/wiki/Parade%27s_End_(TV_series))* as Edward Wannop. He then played Simon Bliss in *[Hay Fever](/wiki/Hay_Fever_(play))* at the [Noël Coward Theatre](/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward_Theatre) and made an early career impact as [Oscar Wilde](/wiki/Oscar_Wilde)'s young lover [Lord Alfred "Bosie" Douglas](/wiki/Lord_Alfred_Douglas) in *[The Judas Kiss](/wiki/The_Judas_Kiss_(play))* (2012–2013) at the [Hampstead Theatre](/wiki/Hampstead_Theatre), during a UK tour, and in its [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) transfer. Fox voiced [Prince Louis of Battenberg](/wiki/Prince_Louis_of_Battenberg) in Margy Kinmonth's *[Royal Paintbox](/wiki/Royal_Paintbox)* with [HRH The Prince of Wales](/wiki/HRH_The_Prince_of_Wales_(Prince_Charles)) featuring the artworks of the British royal family across centuries. He also starred as Sandy Irvine in *Words of Everest*.

In [Lone Scherfig](/wiki/Lone_Scherfig)'s *[The Riot Club](/wiki/The_Riot_Club)* (2014) Fox played James Leighton-Masters, the outgoing president of the ten-member elite Oxford University club. Based on [Laura Wade](/wiki/Laura_Wade)'s hit play *[Posh](/wiki/Posh_(play))*, it tackles class and social structure as a commentary on the [Bullingdon Club](/wiki/Bullingdon_Club). He appeared as Jeff Cole in the critically acclaimed historical comedy-drama *[Pride](/wiki/Pride_(2014_film))* and starred in [Anthony Fabian](/wiki/Anthony_Fabian)'s *Freeze-Frame* (2014) commissioned by the [Foreign and Commonwealth Office](/wiki/Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Office) to promote collaboration between China and UK's film industries.

In 2015, he played the breakout 'bisexual nympho' Freddie Baxter in the [Channel 4](/wiki/Channel_4) hit series *[Cucumber](/wiki/Cucumber_(British_TV_series))*, a role which he reprised in [E4](/wiki/E4_Series_Shinkansen)'s *[Banana](/wiki/Banana_(TV_series))*, and appeared as himself in *[Tofu](/wiki/Tofu_(web_series))*. Both *Cucumber* and *Banana* were nominated for the [GLAAD Media Award for Outstanding TV Movie or Limited Series](/wiki/GLAAD_Media_Award_for_Outstanding_TV_Movie_or_Limited_Series) in 2016. *[i-D](/wiki/I-D)* said of Fox, "you can't deny he's a connoisseur at picking the parts which have maximum impact." Fox then played the wealthy aristocrat Finnegan, the benefactor of the title character's Prometheus creation in *[Victor Frankenstein](/wiki/Victor_Frankenstein)*.

### 2015–2017: *Romeo and Juliet*, *Travesties*, *Dennis and Gnasher: Unleashed!*

In June 2016, he was announced as third prize winner at the 2015 [Ian Charleson Awards](/wiki/Ian_Charleson_Awards), having played the role of [Romeo](/wiki/Romeo) in *[Romeo and Juliet](/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet)* (2015) to great acclaim at the [Sheffield Crucible](/wiki/Sheffield_Crucible). His proficiency with the role led him to be called into service again as [Romeo](/wiki/Romeo) in [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh)'s 2016 production at the [Garrick Theatre](/wiki/Garrick_Theatre) on short notice when the preceding Romeo and his understudy were both injured. With just a 2-day rehearsal after he was cast, Fox took over the role on 26 July and performed it until the end of the play's run on 13 August, garnering rave reviews and impressing cast and crew. He then starred in *The Northleach Horror* as the maverick scientist Whitsuntide and led the cast of *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)* at [Southwark Playhouse](/wiki/Southwark_Playhouse) as Nick Bottom / Demetrius.

He performed as poet [Tristan Tzara](/wiki/Tristan_Tzara), one of the founders and central figures of the [Dada](/wiki/Dada) movement, in [Tom Stoppard](/wiki/Tom_Stoppard)'s *[Travesties](/wiki/Travesties)* from September until November 2016 at the [Menier Chocolate Factory](/wiki/Menier_Chocolate_Factory), the play being listed in [The Arts Desk](/wiki/The_Arts_Desk)'s *Best of 2016: Theatre*. The following year, it ran for a [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) revival from February to April 2017 at the [Apollo Theatre](/wiki/Apollo_Theatre) to widespread acclaim, subsequently earning Fox nominations at the [Laurence Olivier Awards](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Awards#Ceremony) for [Best Actor in a Supporting Role](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role) and the [WhatsOnStage Awards](/wiki/WhatsOnStage_Awards) for Best Supporting Actor in a Play.

He then voiced Dennis in the animated series *[Dennis & Gnasher: Unleashed!](/wiki/Dennis_%26_Gnasher:_Unleashed!)*, in its launch becoming the highest-rated animated show on [CBBC](/wiki/CBBC) for 6 to 12 year olds and one of the top 10 shows for kids across all channels. Its first season received nominations at the 2018 British Animation Awards as Best Children's Series, the 2019 Kidscreen Awards as Best Animated Series, and the 2018 [International Emmy Kids Awards](/wiki/International_Emmy_Kids_Awards) in the Animation category. He then appeared as Rubio in *[King Arthur: Legend of the Sword](/wiki/King_Arthur:_Legend_of_the_Sword)* (2017) directed by [Guy Ritchie](/wiki/Guy_Ritchie). Fox played key scenes as Mr. Sloane in *Joe Orton Laid Bare*, voiced a character/s in *[Lego Marvel Super Heroes 2](/wiki/Lego_Marvel_Super_Heroes_2)*, and voiced Corvin in *[Xenoblade Chronicles 2](/wiki/Xenoblade_Chronicles_2)*.

### 2018–2019: *The Ice King*, *An Ideal Husband*, *Hero*, *Edmond de Bergerac*, *Fanny Lye Deliver'd*

In February 2018, Fox starred as the British Commander Pope in *[Black '47](/wiki/Black_%2747_(film))*, Ireland's first historical film on the [Great Famine](/wiki/Great_Famine_(Ireland)) of 1847. Released in the same month at the [CPH:DOX](/wiki/CPH:DOX) to great acclaim was [James Erskine](/wiki/James_Erskine_(filmmaker))'s *The Ice King* narrated by Fox which profiles the life of [John Curry](/wiki/John_Curry) who revolutionised ice skating from a dated sport into an exalted art form and became the first openly gay Olympian. *[Little White Lies](/wiki/Little_White_Lies_(magazine))* remarks, "Fox affects Curry's distinctive accent with remarkable accuracy." In *[An Ideal Husband](/wiki/An_Ideal_Husband)*, which ran from May to July 2018 at the [Vaudeville Theatre](/wiki/Vaudeville_Theatre) to critical acclaim, Freddie starred with his father [Edward](/wiki/Edward_Fox_(actor)) for the first time playing fictional father and son the Earl of Caversham and Lord Goring. According to *[Financial Times](/wiki/Financial_Times)*, Freddie "effortlessly delivers that mix of insouciance and gravity" and, for *[The Telegraph](/wiki/The_Telegraph_(London))*,"embodies the Wildean spirit of modernity".

In the same year was his directorial debut through the short film *Hero* (2018), which he co-wrote and co-produced, centred on an isolated young boy and a decaying old film star brought together by their shared love of the silver screen. It premiered in August at the [Rhode Island International Film Festival](/wiki/Rhode_Island_International_Film_Festival) where he won the Grand Prize (Short) for Directorial Discovery Award. It was subsequently selected at the [Kerry Film Festival](/wiki/Kerry_Film_Festival) and the Linea d'Ombra Film Festival, and released on [Amazon UK](/wiki/Amazon_UK) and [ITunes](/wiki/ITunes) in multiple countries in November 2019.

In 2019, he headlined the English production of *[Edmond de Bergerac](/wiki/Edmond_de_Bergerac)* playing the titular role playwright [Edmond Rostand](/wiki/Edmond_Rostand) at the [Birmingham Repertory Theatre](/wiki/Birmingham_Repertory_Theatre) before touring the UK to great acclaim. *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* raved that as Edmond, Fox is "by turns a jangle of nerves, a lead weight of despondency and a blaze of success." Fox then voiced Captain Holly in *[Watership Down](/wiki/Watership_Down_(2018_TV_series))* and played the characters Jokannen, [Dorian Gray](/wiki/Dorian_Gray_(character)), and Lord Goring in *[The Importance of Being Oscar](/wiki/The_Importance_of_Being_Oscar)*. He starred as Detective Sargeant Wilbur Strauss in the highly rated Victorian comedy *[Year of the Rabbit](/wiki/Year_of_the_Rabbit_(TV_series))*, which he described as his "first real comedy show" and "a total lesson in improvised and situational comedy" that he had never had before. He also rendered dramatic performances in "The Class Ceiling" episode of *Novels That Shaped Our World*. Thomas Clay's *[Fanny Lye Deliver'd](/wiki/Fanny_Lye_Deliver%27d)* then premiered to positive reviews as a new cult classic set in 1657 Shropshire where Fox played Thomas Ashbury. *[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)* said that he "gives Thomas a flash of predatory charisma," and according to *[The Irish News](/wiki/The_Irish_News)*, "preaches 'perfect libertinism' in captivating sermons".

### 2020–present: *White House Farm*

At the start of 2020, Fox delivered a persuasive performance in his role as the convicted mass murderer [Jeremy Bamber](/wiki/Jeremy_Bamber) in the ITV crime drama *[White House Farm](/wiki/White_House_Farm_(TV_series))* based on the [White House Farm murders](/wiki/White_House_Farm_murders) in August 1985. In preparation, he created a psychology as convincing and enigmatic as the character should be "to leave the viewer asking questions". The *[London Evening Standard](/wiki/London_Evening_Standard)* praised Fox's "ambiguous, wide-eyed look that hovers between angelic and devious, that it's hard to know whether to feel sympathy or suspicion." *[The Times](/wiki/The_Times)* remarked that he managed to be both "revealing and inscrutable, which can't be easy." The series won as Best New Drama at the 2020 [TV Choice Awards](/wiki/TV_Choice_Awards) leading to its international release on [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max) in September 2020 and on [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) in February 2021. Subsequently, he appeared on his co-star [Cressida Bonas](/wiki/Cressida_Bonas)' podcast *Fear Itself*, where he expressed his fear of not reaching his full potential.

In the same year, Fox guest-starred as the alcoholic busker Miles Stevens in *[McDonald & Dodds](/wiki/McDonald_%26_Dodds)* and as King Hugo of Sweden in [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu)'s critically acclaimed comedy-drama series *[The Great](/wiki/The_Great_(TV_series))* (2020). He reprised his voice-over role as Dennis in the second season of *Dennis & Gnasher: Unleashed!* from which select episodes were condensed into the film *Dennis and Gnasher: Unleashed! On the Big Screen*. He then portrayed [Margaret Thatcher](/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher)'s son [Mark Thatcher](/wiki/Mark_Thatcher) in season 4 of the award-winning Netflix historical drama series *[The Crown](/wiki/The_Crown_(season_4))* (2020).

In 2021, Fox opened The Golden Era of Broadway episode of *The Theatre Channel*, singing an arrangement of "[Willkommen](/wiki/Willkommen)" and "[Money](/wiki/Money_(Cabaret_song))" from *[Cabaret](/wiki/Cabaret_(musical))*, and starred as Tony Kroesig, a Bullingdon club member and aristocrat banker turned politician, in *[The Pursuit of Love](/wiki/The_Pursuit_of_Love_(TV_series))*.

In April 2023, it was announced that Fox had been cast as Ser [Gwayne Hightower](/wiki/List_of_House_of_the_Dragon_characters#Gwayne_Hightower) in the second season of *[House of the Dragon](/wiki/House_of_the_Dragon)*.

In March 2025, it was announced that Fox had been cast as a villain in the [fifteenth series](/wiki/Doctor_Who_series_15) of *[Doctor Who](/wiki/Doctor_Who)*.

## Philanthropy

Fox is a patron of the [St Giles Trust](/wiki/St_Giles_Trust) and Future 5 – a campaign by Prostate Cancer Research Centre. He is also an honorary vice president of the Story of Christmas Appeal. In March 2015, he became the face of [Live Below The Line](/wiki/Live_Below_the_Line). An avid runner himself, he supported the St James's Place Foundation Triathlon and Duathlon in 2016 which raised £1.3m for [Hope and Homes for Children](/wiki/Hope_and_Homes_for_Children). He was involved with the charity when he visited orphanages in Rwanda in 2015 and in Uganda in 2018 which he wrote about in a two-part blog entry, "Love is Everything—Freddie Fox in Uganda". He is a frequent supporter and reader at fundraising activities and charity events of The Poetry Hour, [Breast Cancer Now](/wiki/Breast_Cancer_Now), the Brain Tumour Research Campaign, The Story of Christmas Appeal, and The National Brain Appeal among others. In 2020, he participated in a charity reading of [Tom Stoppard](/wiki/Tom_Stoppard)'s *[The Real Inspector Hound](/wiki/The_Real_Inspector_Hound)* that was produced by Lockdown Theatre in association with The Royal Theatrical Fund. In 2021, he performed with [Alfie Boe](/wiki/Alfie_Boe) on a global livestream concert, *Alfie Boe & Friends Live at the Savoy "Lockdown Edition"*, which sold tickets to donate to Hospitality Action and the Connection at St. Martin's.

## Public image

Fox was described by *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* as "deliciously debonair" and "at age 23, is all responsive intelligence and poised charm and is unguarded – as people happy in their own skins often are." "He is almost tyrannically polite and charming," says *[The Telegraph](/wiki/The_Telegraph_(London))*, and, "raised in a pack of upper-class thespians, his every utterance comes as a measured, um-less paragraph." Often branded as an acting royalty, he comes from a family whose decades-long performances formed a dynasty which he now fronts as the latest scion. Amidst inevitable assumptions, he clarifies that though his parents' ties may have opened doors to agents, it is "not a masonic back door to every job".

*[Another Magazine](/wiki/Another_Magazine)* remarked that at the age of 23, Fox "has surprisingly well-formed opinions on men's style." According to *The Gentleman's Journal*, whose cover he headlined on their Autumn 2014 issue, Fox "continues to impress commentators with his suave, dandy-esque style." He was featured in *[The Chap](/wiki/The_Chap)*'s Summer 2018 edition in what the magazine boasts of as their best edition, saying that "no previous issue has so comprehensively gathered so many vital strands of 'anarcho-dandyism'". Fox launched the opening of *White Shirt Week* in St James's, London in 2013. His commentary on the role his costume played in *[The Riot Club](/wiki/The_Riot_Club)* was featured in the *[Financial Times](/wiki/Financial_Times)*.

Averse to social media, he said that he keeps a boundary while talking about things he is interested in to preserve his mystery as an actor "so that there's still an element of surprise about where characters come from."

## Personal life

Fox dated [Tamzin Merchant](/wiki/Tamzin_Merchant), his co-star in *The Mystery of Edwin Drood*, for two years until September 2013. In June 2020, it was reported that he was dating *[Sex Education](/wiki/Sex_Education_(TV_series))* star [Tanya Reynolds](/wiki/Tanya_Reynolds), whom he met in 2016 during production of *Fanny Lye Deliver'd*. While promoting *[Cucumber](/wiki/Cucumber_(British_TV_series))* in 2015, Fox stated that he hopes to "fall in love with another person, as opposed to a sex" and expounded in 2020 saying that "being able to say that you have a more rounded experience as a human being, whether it be through sexuality, or whatever, is now perceived as a real advantage".

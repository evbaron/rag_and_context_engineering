All of the [United States' 50 states](/wiki/U.S._state) have a state [motto](/wiki/Motto), as do the [District of Columbia](/wiki/District_of_Columbia) and 3 of [its territories](/wiki/U.S._territories). A motto is a phrase intended to formally describe the general motivation or intention of an organization. State mottos can sometimes be found on [state seals](/wiki/Seals_of_the_U.S._states) or [state flags](/wiki/Flags_of_the_U.S._states). Some states have officially designated a state motto by an act of the [state legislature](/wiki/State_legislature_(United_States)), whereas other states have the motto only as an element of their seals. The motto of the United States itself is *[In God We Trust](/wiki/In_God_We_Trust)*, proclaimed by Congress and signed into law by President [Dwight D. Eisenhower](/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) on July 30, 1956. The motto "*[E pluribus unum](/wiki/E_pluribus_unum)*" ([Latin](/wiki/Latin) for 'out of many, one') was approved for use on the [Great Seal of the United States](/wiki/Great_Seal_of_the_United_States) in 1782, but was never adopted as the national motto through legislative action.

[South Carolina](/wiki/South_Carolina) has two official mottos, both which are in Latin. [Kentucky](/wiki/Kentucky), [North Dakota](/wiki/North_Dakota), and [Vermont](/wiki/Vermont) also have two mottos, one in Latin and the other in English. All other states and territories have only one motto, except for [Guam](/wiki/Guam) and the [Northern Mariana Islands](/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands), which do not have any mottos. English and Latin are the most-used languages for state mottos, each used by 25 states and territories. Seven states and territories use another language, of which each language is only used once. Eight states and two territories have their mottos on their [state quarter](/wiki/50_State_Quarters); thirty-eight states and four territories have their mottos on their state seals.

The dates given are, where possible, the earliest date that the motto was used in an official sense. Some state mottos are not official but are on the official state seal; in these cases the adoption date of the seal is given. The earliest use of a current motto is that of [Puerto Rico](/wiki/Puerto_Rico), *Joannes est nomen ejus*, granted to the island by the Spanish in 1511.

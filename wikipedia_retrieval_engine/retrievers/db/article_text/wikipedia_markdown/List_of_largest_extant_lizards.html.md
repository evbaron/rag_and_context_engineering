# List of largest extant lizards

Currently there are about 40 [extant](/wiki/Extant_taxon) families of [Lacertilia](/wiki/Lacertilia). These vary considerably, e.g. in shades, colours, and sizes. For example, the largest representative among [Geckos](/wiki/Geckos), the [New Caledonian giant gecko](/wiki/New_Caledonian_giant_gecko) (*Rhacodactylus leachianus*), has a length of up to 36 cm (14 in), while the largest species in the family [Varanidae](/wiki/Varanidae), [Komodo dragon](/wiki/Komodo_dragon) (*Varanus komodoensis*), has a length up to 3 metres (10 ft), and a body mass of 70 kg (154 lbs).

## By families

### Agamas (Agamidae)

See also: [Agamidae](/wiki/Agamidae)

[Makassar sailfin lizard](/wiki/Hydrosaurus_microlophus) is usually regarded as the largest representative of family [Agamidae](/wiki/Agamidae), reaching a length of 1.2 m (3.9 ft).

- The largest representatives in this group are species in the genus *[Hydrosaurus](/wiki/Hydrosaurus)*, which often exceed 1 metre (3.3 ft) in length.
  - The largest species – [Makassar sailfin lizard](/wiki/Hydrosaurus_microlophus) (*Hydrosaurus microlophus*) and [Sulawesi black sailfin lizard](/wiki/Hydrosaurus_celebensis) (*Hydrosaurus celebensis*) grow to a length of 1.2 (3.9 ft) and 1.13 m (3.7 ft) respectively.
  - [Weber's sailfin lizard](/wiki/Hydrosaurus_weberi) (*Hydrosaurus weberi*), [Philippine sailfin lizard](/wiki/Hydrosaurus_pustulatus) (*Hydrosaurus pustulatus*) and [Amboina sailfin lizard](/wiki/Hydrosaurus_amboinensis) (*Hydrosaurus amboinensis*) (previously counts the largest species in this group) have a length of 1 m (3.3 ft).
- The [Australian water dragon](/wiki/Australian_water_dragon) (*Intellagama lesueurii*) and [Chinese water dragon](/wiki/Chinese_water_dragon) (*Physignathus cocincinus*) can also exceed this length.
- The [frilled lizard](/wiki/Frilled_lizard) (*Chlamydosaurus kingii*) has length up to 85 cm (2.79 ft) and mass up to 870 g (1.92 lb), probably even 900 g (2 lb)
- *[Uromastyx aegyptia](/wiki/Uromastyx_aegyptia)* is one of heaviest representatives among agamas, with length up to 76 cm (30 in) and mass 2 kg (4.4 lbs).
- A [central bearded dragon](/wiki/Central_bearded_dragon) (*Pogona vitticeps*) and [eastern bearded dragon](/wiki/Eastern_bearded_dragon) (*Pogona barbata*) have a length of 60 cm (24 in), and are the largest in their [genus](/wiki/Genus).

### American legless lizards (Anniellidae)

See also: [Anniellidae](/wiki/Anniellidae)

- The largest American legless lizard is the [Californian legless lizard](/wiki/Anniella_pulchra) (*Anniella pulchra*) attaining 18 cm (7.1 in) long.

### Glass lizards (Anguidae)

See also: [Anguidae](/wiki/Anguidae)

[Sheltopusik](/wiki/Sheltopusik) from Southern Europe to Central Asia, is the largest [legless lizard](/wiki/Legless_lizards), reaching a length of 1.2 m (3.9 ft)

- Many representatives of this family are [legless lizards](/wiki/Legless_lizards). Among these the largest species, and also the largest legless lizard in the world, is the [sheltopusik](/wiki/Sheltopusik) (*Pseudopus apodus*), reaching a maximum length of more 135 cm (4.43 ft) and a mass in 1.1 kg (2.4 lb).
- The [eastern glass lizard](/wiki/Eastern_glass_lizard) (*Ophisaurus ventralis*) and [slender glass lizard](/wiki/Slender_glass_lizard) (*Ophisaurus attenuatus*) may reach lengths of up to 108 cm (3.54 ft) and 107 cm (3.51 ft) respectively.
- However, this family also has lizards with legs, among which the largest is the [Texas alligator lizard](/wiki/Texas_alligator_lizard) (*Gerrhonotus infernalis*) growing to from 45.7 cm (18 in) till 64 cm (25.2 in) in different sources.

### Chameleons (Chamaeleonidae)

See also: [Chamaeleonidae](/wiki/Chamaeleonidae)

- Of all [chameleons](/wiki/Chameleons), the largest is the [Parson's chameleon](/wiki/Parson%27s_chameleon) (*Calumma parsonii*), reaching a length of 68 cm (27 in). But the longest species is the [Malagasy giant chameleon](/wiki/Malagasy_giant_chameleon) (*Furcifer oustaleti*). It has a length of up to 68.5 cm (27 in).
- Other large chameleons are the [veiled chameleon](/wiki/Veiled_chameleon) (*Chameleo calyptratus*) and [Meller's chameleon](/wiki/Meller%27s_chameleon) (*Trioceros melleri*), with lengths of up to 61 cm (24 in) Exceptionally large specimens of the latter have reputedly reached over 76 cm (30 in) and mass 600 g (1.3 lb), although this is unlikely.

### Spinytail lizards (Cordylidae)

See also: [Cordylidae](/wiki/Cordylidae)

- Many species in this family are small to medium-sized lizards that range in length from 6 to 30 cm (2.4 to 11.8 inches). There are large species in the genus *[Smaug](/wiki/Smaug_(lizard))*, the largest of which is a [giant girdled lizard](/wiki/Giant_girdled_lizard) (*Smaug giganteus*), with snout-vent length up to 20.5 cm (8.07 in) and total length up to roughly 40 cm (15.74 in).

### Casquehead lizards (Corytophanidae)

See also: [Corytophanidae](/wiki/Corytophanidae)

[Basilisks](/wiki/Basiliscus_(lizard)) are quite large lizards, especially the [plumed basilisk](/wiki/Plumed_basilisk)

- Casquehead lizards are quite large lizards. In particular, the genus *[Basiliscus](/wiki/Basiliscus_(lizard))* typically measures 70–75 cm (28–30 in). But the largest species in this group is the [plumed basilisk](/wiki/Plumed_basilisk) (*Basiliscus plumifrons*), frequently growing to 91 cm (36 in).
- Another large species in this group is the [eastern casquehead iguana](/wiki/Eastern_casquehead_iguana) (*Laemanctus longipes*) reaching up to 70 cm (27.6 in) including its long, thin tail.

### Collared lizards (Crotaphytidae)

See also: [Crotaphytidae](/wiki/Crotaphytidae)

- In this [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) it is not known exactly which species is the largest, but according to current data the largest is the [reticulated collared lizard](/wiki/Reticulated_collared_lizard) (*Crotaphytus reticulatus*) with length up to 41 cm (16 in) and mass 78.77 g (2.8 oz).

### Anoles (Dactyloidae)

See also: [Dactyloidae](/wiki/Dactyloidae)

The [knight anole](/wiki/Knight_anole) is the largest anole, over half a meter in length

- Large species in this group are usually crown giant [anolis ecomorphs](/wiki/Anolis_ecomorphs) The species generally considered the largest is the [knight anole](/wiki/Knight_anole) (*Anolis equestris*), which can reach up to 51 cm (20 in) in total length, 19 cm (7.5 in) in [snout-vent length](/wiki/Snout-vent_length) and weigh 137 g (4.83 oz).
- However, in the *equester* [species complex](/wiki/Species_complex) there are also species reaching the same length and even larger. These include *[A. baracoae](/wiki/Anolis_baracoae)*, *[A. luteogularis](/wiki/Anolis_luteogularis)*, *[A. noblei](/wiki/Anolis_noblei)* and *[A. smallwoodi](/wiki/Anolis_smallwoodi)*.

### Geckos (Gekkota)

See also: [Gekkota](/wiki/Gekkota)

The [New Caledonian giant gecko](/wiki/New_Caledonian_giant_gecko) is the largest gecko, weighing up to almost 300 g (10.5 oz)

- Species of [geckos](/wiki/Geckos) belong to different groups. For example, many species of geckos are in family [Gekkonidae](/wiki/Gekkonidae), but there are very large species in the family [Diplodactylidae](/wiki/Diplodactylidae). For example, to them belongs the largest gecko species [New Caledonian giant gecko](/wiki/New_Caledonian_giant_gecko) (*Rhacodactylus leachianus*), which can be 36–43.18 cm (14–17 in) long and mass 212–279 g (7.5–9.8 oz)
- Other representatives of the same genus as the aforementioned gecko can also reach huge sizes. These include [gargoyle gecko](/wiki/Gargoyle_gecko) (*Rhacodactylus auriculatus*), [greater rough-snouted giant gecko](/wiki/Rhacodactylus_trachyrhynchus) (*R. trachyrhynchus*) and [lesser rough-snouted giant gecko](/wiki/Rhacodactylus_trachyrhynchus) (*R. trachycephalus*). As well as former species in this genus: [crested gecko](/wiki/Crested_gecko) (*Correlophus ciliatus*), [Sarasins' giant gecko](/wiki/Correlophus_sarasinorum) (*C. sarasinorum*) and [Bavay's giant gecko](/wiki/Rhacodactylus_chahoua) (*Mniarogekko chahoua*).
- An extant member of the genus *[Hoplodactylus](/wiki/Hoplodactylus)*, [Duvaucel's gecko](/wiki/Duvaucel%27s_gecko) (*Hoplodactylus duvaucelii*), in which be present a largest gecko ever lived (see [the largest extinct lizards](/wiki/The_largest_extinct_lizards)) can also reach large sizes. It can reach total length 30 cm (12 in) with a snout-vent length (SVL) up to 16 cm (6.3 in), and weigh up to 120 grams (4.2 oz).
- There are also large species in the family [Gekkonidae](/wiki/Gekkonidae). These include [tokay gecko](/wiki/Tokay_gecko) (*Gekko gekko*), [Smith's green-eyed gecko](/wiki/Gekko_smithii) (*G. smithii*) and [Andaman giant gecko](/wiki/Andaman_giant_gecko) (*G. verreauxi*), which can reach and even exceed 30 cm (12 in).
- Another genus with large geckos is *[Uroplatus](/wiki/Uroplatus)*. The largest representative in this genus is the [giant leaf-tail gecko](/wiki/Giant_leaf-tail_gecko) (*Uroplatus giganteus*), which can reach a snout–vent length of 20 cm and a total length of 32.2 cm. Another species that grows almost as large is the [common flat-tail gecko](/wiki/Common_flat-tail_gecko) (*U. fimbriatus*), which can exceed 30 cm (12 in) and more.
- The genus *[Phelsuma](/wiki/Phelsuma)* in [subfamily](/wiki/Subfamily) [Uroplatinae](/wiki/Uroplatinae) also has large species. [Standing's day gecko](/wiki/Standing%27s_day_gecko) (*Phelsuma standingi*) is one of the largest [day geckos](/wiki/Phelsuma), reaching a length of about 25.4 cm (10.0 in) and [Round Island day gecko](/wiki/Round_Island_day_gecko) (*P. guentheri*), [Madagascar giant day gecko](/wiki/Phelsuma_grandis) (*P. grandis*) and *[P. madagascariensis](/wiki/Phelsuma_madagascariensis)* can reach even more impressive lengths of 30 cm (12 in) or more.
- Outside of the families [Diplodactylidae](/wiki/Diplodactylidae) and [Gekkonidae](/wiki/Gekkonidae), there are large geckoes among the [Eublepharidae](/wiki/Eublepharidae). The [African fat-tailed gecko](/wiki/African_fat-tailed_gecko) (*Hemitheconyx caudicinctus*) reaches a length of up to 23 cm (9.1 in), and a mass of 75 g (2.6 oz). The [leopard gecko](/wiki/Leopard_gecko) (*Euplepharis macularis*) reaches even large sizes – up to 28 cm (11 in) long with a mass of 60–80 g (2.1–2.84 oz).
- The largest species of [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) [Phyllodactylidae](/wiki/Phyllodactylidae), the [giant wall gecko](/wiki/Giant_wall_gecko) (*Tarentola gigas*), reaches 30 cm (12 in) in length and 15.5 cm (6.1 in) in [SVL](/wiki/Snout-vent_length).

### Plated lizards (Gerrhosauridae)

See also: [Gerrhosauridae](/wiki/Gerrhosauridae)

- The largest species in this family is the [giant plated lizard](/wiki/Giant_plated_lizard) (*Matobosaurus validus*), with a total length of up to 69 cm (27 in) or even 75 cm (29.5 in), with a snout-vent length (SVL) of 28 cm (11 in). Another large plated lizard is the [Sudan plated lizard](/wiki/Sudan_plated_lizard) (*Broadleysaurus major*), with a length of about 51–60 cm (20–24 in).

### Beaded lizards and Gila monster (Helodermatidae)

See also: [Gila monster](/wiki/Gila_monster) and [Helodermatidae](/wiki/Helodermatidae)

The [Mexican beaded lizard](/wiki/Mexican_beaded_lizard) is the largest species in family [Helodermatidae](/wiki/Helodermatidae), reaching a length of up to 91 cm (36 in)

- This family includes large and stocky lizards, including the infamous [Gila monster](/wiki/Gila_monster) (*Heloderma suspectum*), which reaches a total length up to 60 cm (23.6 in) with a [snout-vent length](/wiki/Snout-vent_length) (SVL) of 36 cm (14.1 in) and a body mass from 1 kg (2.2 lb) to 2.2 kg (4.9 lb) depending on the source. But despite the stocky and massive body of this lizard, it is not the largest representative of the family. Greatly exceeding it in size are the [Mexican beaded lizard](/wiki/Mexican_beaded_lizard) (*Heloderma horridum*) and [Rio Fuerte beaded lizard](/wiki/Heloderma_exasperatum) (*Heloderma exasperatum*), with a total length of up to 91 cm (36 in), a SVL of up to 47 cm (18.5 in) and a mass from 2 kg (4.4 lb) to 4 kg (8.8 lb) depending on the source.

### Iguanas (Iguanidae)

See also: [Iguanidae](/wiki/Iguanidae)

The [blue iguana](/wiki/Blue_iguana) is the heaviest [iguanid](/wiki/Iguanidae) and one of the largest extant [lizards](/wiki/Lizard)

- [Iguanas](/wiki/Iguanas) are very large lizards, some of which can reach lengths of up to 2 m (6.6 ft) and mass more than 10 kg (22 lb). They are the largest lizards after some large species of [monitor lizards](/wiki/Monitor_lizards), and the largest lizards in the [New World](/wiki/New_World). Iguanas vary considerably in size and form, but even the smallest lizards in this family are still quite large. For example, the [desert iguana](/wiki/Desert_iguana) (*Dipsosaurus dorsalis*) can reach a length of up to 61 cm (24 in). The largest species of the [genus](/wiki/Genus) *[Sauromalus](/wiki/Sauromalus)* is the [San Esteban chuckwalla](/wiki/San_Esteban_chuckwalla) (*Sauromalus varius*), up to 76 cm (27 in) long and 1.4 kg (3.1 lb) in weight, while the second-largest is the [Angel Island chuckwalla](/wiki/Angel_Island_chuckwalla) (*S. hispidus*), up to 64 cm (25.2 in) long.
- Many sources describe the [green iguana](/wiki/Green_iguana) (*Iguana iguana*) as the largest [iguanid](/wiki/Iguanidae), often reaching lengths up to 1.5 metres (4.91 ft) and masses of 4 kg (8.8 lb), and with a maximum length of 2 m (6.6 ft) and a mass of 8 kg (18 lb) and in some cases even 9.1 kg (20 lb). However, the heaviest species in this family is the [blue iguana](/wiki/Blue_iguana) (*Cyclura lewisi*), with a total length of up to 1.5 m (4.91 ft), a SVL of 51–76 cm (30 in) and a mass of up to 14 kg (31 lb) It is the eighth-heaviest and largest extant lizard.

The [Galapagos land iguana](/wiki/Galapagos_land_iguana) is a very massive lizard weighing more than 10 kg (22 lb) and is one of the largest extant [lizards](/wiki/Lizard)

- Other large species in this family include the [Galapagos land iguana](/wiki/Galapagos_land_iguana) (*Conolophus subcristataus*), with a length of about 1.5 m (4.91 ft) and a mass of up to 13 kg (29 lb). It is the second-heaviest iguanid after the [blue iguana](/wiki/Blue_iguana) and the ninth-heaviest and largest lizard in the world. Another large species from the same genus is the [Santa Fe land iguana](/wiki/Santa_Fe_land_iguana) (*C. pallidus*), reaching a SVL of 56.4 cm (22.2 in) and a mass of 7.2 kg (16 lb). The [Galapagos pink land iguana](/wiki/Galapagos_pink_land_iguana) (*C. marthae*) have snout-vent length 57.5 cm (22.6 in) and the mass of 8 kg (18 lb).
- The [marine iguana](/wiki/Marine_iguana) (*Amblyrhynchus cristatus*) is the tenth-largest extant lizard in the world, and the largest reptile on [Galapagos Islands](/wiki/Galapagos_Islands) after the [Galapagos land iguana](/wiki/Galapagos_land_iguana), not including [turtles](/wiki/Turtle) reaching a maximum total length of 1.4 m (4.59 ft), a SVL of from 12 till 56 cm (from 4.72 till 22 in) and a mass of from 1 to 12 kg (2.2 to 26.5 lb) depending on islands.
- Aforementioned [genus](/wiki/Genus) *[Cyclura](/wiki/Cyclura)* includes other very large iguanas, such as [Anegada rock iguana](/wiki/Cyclura_pinguis) (*Cyclura pinguis*) reaching a [snout-vent length](/wiki/Snout-vent_length) (SVL) of 56 cm (22 in) and a mass 7.75 kg (17.1 lb). A second largest species in this genus and second longest iguanid overall, and also one of the largest [lizards](/wiki/Lizard) in the [Caribbean](/wiki/Caribbean), the [Cuban iguana](/wiki/Cyclura_nubila) (*C. nubila*) reaching a length in 1.6 m (5.2 ft) with a SVL in 74.5 cm (29.3 in).
- The [rhinoceros iguana](/wiki/Cyclura_cornuta) (*Cyclura cornuta*) has a length in 1.36 m (4.5 ft), with a SVL 56 cm (22 in) and a mass more 4.56–9 kg (10.1–19.8 lb). Previously considered a [subspecies](/wiki/Subspecies) of the aforementioned rhinoceros iguana, the [Mona ground iguana](/wiki/Mona_ground_iguana) (*C. strejnegeri*) exceeds a total length of 1.22 m (4 ft), SVL of 53.5 cm (21.1 in), mass of 6.4 kg (14 lb) and is the largest native [terrestrial](/wiki/Terrestrial_animal) [lizard](/wiki/Lizard) in [Puerto-Rico](/wiki/Puerto-Rico).
- At least two [subspecies](/wiki/Subspecies) (*[Cyclura cychlura cychlura](/wiki/Cyclura_cychlura_cychlura)* and *[Cyclura cychlura inornata](/wiki/Cyclura_cychlura_inornata)*) of the [Northern Bahamian rock iguana](/wiki/Northern_Bahamian_rock_iguana) (*Cyclura cychlura*) can be over 1.5 m (4.9 ft) in length.
- A member of the genus *[Iguana](/wiki/Iguana)*, [lesser Antillean iguana](/wiki/Lesser_Antillean_iguana) (*Iguana delicatissima*) can reach SVL 43.4 cm (17.1 in), and mass 3.5 kg (7.7 lb).
- The [black iguana](/wiki/Ctenosaura_similis) (*Ctenosaura similis*) and [western spiny-tailed iguana](/wiki/Ctenosaura_pectinata) (*C. pectinata*) are the largest species in the genus *[Ctenosaura](/wiki/Ctenosaura)*, reaching a length in 1.3 m (4.3 ft). The longest is [Mexican spiny-tailed iguana](/wiki/Ctenosaura_acanthura) (*C. acanthura*) with a length of 1.4 m (4.6 ft).

### True lizards (Lacertidae)

See also: [Lacertidae](/wiki/Lacertidae)

[Gran Canaria giant lizard](/wiki/Gran_Canaria_giant_lizard) is the largest member of a family [Lacertidae](/wiki/Lacertidae), exceeding a length of 80 cm (31 in)

- This [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) includes 300 diverse species. Among the largest is [Gran Canaria giant lizard](/wiki/Gran_Canaria_giant_lizard) (*Gallotia stehlini*), reaching a length of 80 cm (31 in), with a SVL of 26.5 cm (10.4 in) and a mass of almost 1.7 kg (3.7 lb). A subspecies in the same genus – [El Hierro giant lizard](/wiki/El_Hierro_giant_lizard) (*G. simonyi machadoi*), perhaps also is one of the largest [lacertids](/wiki/Lacertidae), and able reach a length of 60 cm (24 in). ### Monitor lizards ([Varanidae](/wiki/Varanidae))

Body size in monitor lizards shows greater variation than in any other family of animals with adult animals weighing between 23 g (0.81 oz) and 80,000 g (176.3 lb). The family includes both the largest extant lizards and the largest terrestrial lizards that have ever existed, yet about a third of the living species are dwarfs that seldom exceed 500 g (18 oz) in weight. Such massive size disparity between species makes the group ideal model animals to study the effects of gigantism on ecology and physiology, but at present little of their potential in this field has been explored. Although, species weighing around 1 kg (2.2 lb) are often found. This family includes the [Komodo dragon](/wiki/Komodo_dragon), which is the largest of all extant lizards, with a length of 3 meters (10 ft) and approximate weight of 113 kg (249 lb). Some of the largest representatives of the [Varanidae](/wiki/Varanidae) such as the [Komodo dragon](/wiki/Komodo_dragon), [crocodile monitor](/wiki/Varanus_salvadorii), and the extinct [megalania](/wiki/Megalania) can count as [apex predators](/wiki/Apex_predator). In the list of the largest lizards, [monitors](/wiki/Monitor_lizards) occupy the first seven places.

Komodo dragon ([Varanus komodoensis](/wiki/Komodo_dragon))

The [Komodo dragon](/wiki/Komodo_dragon) is the largest [extant](/wiki/Extant_species) lizard with a maximum known mass of more 80 kg (176.3 lb), also perhaps the heaviest [squamate](/wiki/Squamata)

- The [Komodo dragon](/wiki/Komodo_dragon) (*Varanus komodoensis*) is the largest living lizard in the world, with an average weight of 70 kg (150 lb) and 25 kg (55 lb) for males and females respectively. The largest males from [Komodo](/wiki/Komodo_(island)), may reach more 80 kg (176.3 lb). The largest specimen of Komodo dragon, a large male from Loh Liang National Park with length of 3.04 m (10.0 ft), a SVL of 1.54 m (5.1 ft) and a mass in 81.5 kg (178.5 lb). As stated above, females are generally much smaller than males. The largest female weighed about 42 kg (93 lb). The heaviest [specimen](/wiki/Biological_specimen) in the wild weighed about 87.4 kg (193 lb). The study noted that weights greater than 100 kg (220 lb) were possible but only after the animal had consumed a large meal. The largest accurately measured individual according to Gerard Wood for [Guinness World Records](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) was a [specimen](/wiki/Biological_specimen) put on display at the [Saint Louis Zoological Park](/wiki/Saint_Louis_Zoo), [Missouri](/wiki/Missouri) in 1937. This individual reportedly measured 3.13 m (10.27 ft) and weighed 166 kg (366 lb), although a large portion of this was likely undigested food. Because Komodo dragons can eat up to 60% their own body weight, this weight record is considered invalid. Excluding this specimen, the Komodo dragon's maximum size is surpassed by large [snakes](/wiki/Snakes) like the [green anaconda](/wiki/Green_anaconda) (up to 97.5 kg (215 lb) in the wild) and the [reticulated python](/wiki/Reticulated_python) (up to 150 kg (330 lb) in captivity), but considering that these exceptionally large specimens are reported at a much lower frequency, it is still arguable that the Komodo dragon is the largest extant squamate on Earth.

Asian water monitor ([Varanus salvator](/wiki/Asian_water_monitor))

The [Asian water monitor](/wiki/Asian_water_monitor) is the second-largest lizard with a length of more than 3 m (9.8 ft) and a weight of 25 kg (55 lb)

- The [Asian water monitor](/wiki/Asian_water_monitor) (*Varanus salvator*) is the second-biggest lizard in the world after the Komodo dragon, and also the longest lizard in the world. The largest specimens come from [Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia), particularly around the [Cameron Highlands](/wiki/Cameron_Highlands), where specimens over 2.5 m (8.2 ft) in total length are sometimes encountered. Jasmi (1988) records that wild specimens can weigh up to 25 kg (55 lb). The largest member this species from [Sri-Lanka](/wiki/Sri-Lanka) and attained in length of about 3.21 m (10.5 ft). [Thailand](/wiki/Thailand) is also home to some huge water monitors, but elsewhere the water monitor grows to smaller sizes. The biggest found in [Java](/wiki/Java) are around 2.1 m (6.9 ft) in total length, 2 m (6.6 ft) in [Sri Lanka](/wiki/Sri_Lanka), 1.7 m (5.6 ft) in [Sumatra](/wiki/Sumatra) and mainland India and less than 1.5 m (4.91 ft) on the island of [Flores](/wiki/Flores). Unverified weights of 50, 70 and even 90 kg (110, 150 and even 198 pounds) have been reported in escaped pets in [Florida](/wiki/Florida) but such enormous weights remain unverified. The 80 males slaughtered for the skin trade in [Sumatra](/wiki/Sumatra) had an average weight of only 3.42 kg (7.5 lb), with a [snout-vent length](/wiki/Snout-vent_length) of 56.6 cm (22.3 in) and a total length of 142 cm (4.66 ft); while 42 females had an average weight of 3.52 kg (7.8 lb), with a [nose-to-cloaca length](/wiki/Snout-vent_length) of 59 cm (23 in) and a total length of 149.6 cm (4.91 ft). Among these monitor lizards, some specimens weighed from 16–20 kg (35–44 lb). Another study in Sumatra by the same authors also estimates the weight of some specimens at 20 kg (44 lb) while the average adult weight in the population is estimated to be about 7.6 kg (17 lb).

Crocodile monitor ([Varanus salvadorii](/wiki/Crocodile_monitor))

The [crocodile monitor](/wiki/Crocodile_monitor) sometimes is considered the longest lizard in the world

- The [crocodile monitor](/wiki/Crocodile_monitor) (*Varanus salvadorii*) sometimes is regarded as the longest lizard in the world. The longest verified individual, a [specimen](/wiki/Zoological_specimen) at [Museum Koenig](/wiki/Museum_Koenig), is 255 cm (8.37 ft) long. Some have been claimed to be much larger, including three individuals that reportedly were 323 cm (10.60 ft), 427 cm (14.01 ft) and 475 cm (15.58 ft) long, but these sizes are unverified. The tail of the crocodile monitor is proportionally very long, typically 2–2.7 times the snout-to-vent length. A weight of up to 20 kg (44 lb) usually is reported, but in this slender and long-tailed [arboreal](/wiki/Arboreal) species healthy adults (for example, discounting obese captives) typically weigh 2.5 to 6.38 kg (5.5–14.1 lb). Some sources suggest a mass up to 90 kg (200 lb), although this is highly questionable.

Nile monitor ([Varanus niloticus](/wiki/Nile_monitor))

The [Nile monitor](/wiki/Nile_monitor) is the largest lizard native to [Africa](/wiki/Africa) with a maximum reliable length of more than 2.43 m (8.0 ft) and a mass in almost 20 kg (44 lb)

- The [Nile monitor](/wiki/Nile_monitor) (*Varanus niloticus*) is the largest lizard native to Africa alive today and fourth or fifth-largest lizard in the world after the Komodo dragon, [Asian water monitor](/wiki/Asian_water_monitor), [crocodile monitor](/wiki/Crocodile_monitor) and competes with [perentie](/wiki/Perentie). In most cases, males of [Nile monitors](/wiki/Nile_monitor) reach a total length of 150–170 cm (4.9-5.5 ft) and weigh in the region of 5–10 kg (11–22 lb), while females are about 1.34 m (4.4 ft) long and weigh about 3 kg (6.6 lb). Exceptionally large wild specimens of the Nile monitors can probably reach about 2.5 meters (8.2 feet) in length and weigh about 20 kg (44 lb), making them one of the largest lizards in the world. In 1929, a photograph of a Nile monitor from [South Africa](/wiki/South_Africa) was published, which was said to have reached a total length of 2.13 m (7.0 ft) and weighed almost 19 kg (42 lb). The same author also reports that he shot another monitor lizard approximately 2.5 m (8.2 ft) long. The largest animal this species reliably known to date reached a total length of 242 cm (7.94 ft) with a length from the muzzle to cloaca length 98 cm (39 in) and estimated at 12–15 kg (26–33 lb) According to Faust (2001), the largest representative of this species measured 2.43 m (7.97 ft). A specimen with a length of 188 cm (6.17 ft) (length from tip of muzzle to cloaca is 74 cm (29 in)) was recorded from Orange Province in South Africa. Near Lake Chad, the maximum recorded size for a male is 2.07 m (6.8 ft) and for a female is 1.55 m (5.1 ft). The largest female [Nile monitor](/wiki/Nile_monitor) for which there is reliable data reached 1.7 m (5.6 ft). Thus, it seems likely that in South Africa some specimens can reach lengths of 200 cm (6.6 ft) or more, but they are very rare.

Perentie ([Varanus giganteus](/wiki/Perentie))

The [perentie](/wiki/Perentie) is the largest [lizard](/wiki/Lizard) Australia, and also one of the biggest lizards in the world

- The [perentie](/wiki/Perentie) (*Varanus giganteus*) is by average length and weight the largest extant lizard native to Australia and fifth or fourth-biggest lizard in the world after Komodo dragon, Asian water monitor, crocodile monitor and competes with the Nile monitor. It is [endemic](/wiki/Endemic) to arid central Australia, found west of the [Great Dividing Range](/wiki/Great_Dividing_Range). The largest individual recorded by (Stokes, 1846) measuring 2438 mm (8 ft) in total length. However, based on the vagueness of this account, some authors claim Stokes' reported measurement of 2438 mm (8 ft) is exaggerated and a more likely maximum length may instead be 2.3 m (7.5 ft) (Stirling, 1912). There have been larger individuals supposedly measuring more than 2.5 m (8.2 ft) in total length and 20 kg (44 lb) in mass reported but such claims remain unverified. The maximum size of the perentie is probably not as great as many authors claim King & Green (1993) provide a useful discussion of size in this species. The longest found on [Barrow Island](/wiki/Barrow_Island_(Western_Australia)) by King et al. (1989) had a total length of 1.96 m (6.4 ft) with a SVL in 88 cm (35 in) and the heaviest weighed 11.7 kg (26 lb). Butler (1970) record s a specimen of 17 kg (37 lb) with a total length of 1.93 m (6.3 ft) from the same island. Stokes (1846) records that two specimens collected on Barrow Island in 1840 had total lengths of 2.13 m (7.0 ft). Strimple (1988) suggested that one of these animals was the type specimen used by Gray, which has a total length of only 2.02 m (6.6 ft). Many perenties do not grow to such an enormous size, and the specimens on Barrow Island may grow larger than the main [population](/wiki/Population_(biology)). The limited data available suggests that females reach a smaller maximum size than males, rarely exceeding 1.4 m (4.6 ft) TL. Bredl (1987) records males of 1.8 and 1.9 m (5 ft 11 in and 6 ft 3 in) TL, and a female of 1.3 m (4.3 ft) TL. It appears that perenties approaching 2 m (6.6 ft) in length are the exception rather than the rule, and that in general few specimens grow larger than 1.5 m (4.91 ft). If specimens of 2.4 m (7.9 ft) or more have ever existed, none seem to have survived to the present.

Black-throated monitor ([Varanus albigularis microstictus](/wiki/Black-throated_monitor))

- The [black-throated monitor](/wiki/Black-throated_monitor) (*Varanus albigularis microstictus*) is a [subspecies](/wiki/Subspecies) of [rock monitor](/wiki/Rock_monitor) (*Varanus albigularis*) and, perhaps, the heaviest [lizard](/wiki/Lizard) in [Africa](/wiki/Africa). Although, little information is available on the size of this species of [monitor lizards](/wiki/Monitor_lizard). According to many information black-throated monitor attains 2.1 m (6.89 ft) in length and 27 kg (60 lb) in mass. However, unknown whether this mass is reached in captivity or in the wild. By another – black-throated monitor has a mass of 23 kg (51 lb). However, specimens 2.13 m (7.0 ft) for this species [rock monitor](/wiki/Rock_monitor) was recorded.

Lace monitor ([Varanus varius](/wiki/Lace_monitor))

The [lace monitor](/wiki/Lace_monitor) – the second-largest [lizard](/wiki/Lizard) native to [Australia](/wiki/Australia) and seven-largest lizard in the world

- The [lace monitor](/wiki/Lace_monitor) (*Varanus varius*) is the second-largest lizard in [Australia](/wiki/Australia) after the [perentie](/wiki/Perentie) and seven-largest lizard in the world overall. The largest instances may reach a length of 2 m (6.6 ft) with a SVL of 76.5 cm (30.1 in) and a mass of 14 kg (31 lb). In 1986 [Gerard Krefft](/wiki/Gerard_Krefft) suggested that lace monitors grow as large as 250 cm (8.2 ft). None of this size exist now and specimens of 2 m (6.6 ft) TL are exceptional. A lizard from [Mallacoota](/wiki/Mallacoota), [Victoria](/wiki/Victoria_(Australia)) measured 75 cm (30 in) SVL, 192 cm (6.30 ft) TL and weighed 14 kg (31 lb). Another from [Healesville](/wiki/Healesville) was 192 cm (6.30 ft) long and weighed almost 20.5 kg (45 lb). It was found to have eaten four [foxcubs](/wiki/Fox), three young [rabbits](/wiki/Rabbit) and three large [blue-tongued skinks](/wiki/Blue-tongued_skink), and all this was taken into account when weighing, is unknown. And, accordingly, the most commonly used maximum size for this species is a length of 192 cm (6.30 ft) and a weight of 14 kg (31 lb) for the previously mentioned [specimen](/wiki/Biological_specimen). In contrast, a large male in the spring measured 1.5 m (4.91 ft) TL and weighed 4.2 kg (9.3 lb). Males grow larger than females and probably have larger home ranges.

In the genus *[Varanus](/wiki/Varanus)* has more 80 [species](/wiki/Species) of [lizards](/wiki/Lizard) and all of to 11 [subgenera](/wiki/Subgenus), members which vary considerably in sizes:

#### Subgenus [Empagusia](/wiki/Varanus_(Empagusia))

[Bengal monitor](/wiki/Bengal_monitor) is the second-largest lizard in [Asia](/wiki/Asia) after [Asian water monitor](/wiki/Asian_water_monitor)

- The largest representative of the [subgenus](/wiki/Subgenus) *[Empagusia](/wiki/Varanus_(Empagusia))* is [Bengal monitor](/wiki/Bengal_monitor) (*Varanus bengalensis*) with a length in 1.75 m (5.7 ft) and a SVL of 75 cm (30 in), a mass of 7.2 kg (16 lb), and in captivity even more – 10.2 kg (22 lb). However, typical measurements are much less – 1.5 m (4.91 ft) in a length and 2.7 kg (6.0 lb) in a mass. Even so, by some reports it grows much larger.
- Another large species of this group are the [clouded monitor](/wiki/Clouded_monitor) (*Varanus nebulosus*) with a length of 1.5 m (4.91 ft) and a SVL of 60 cm (24 in), the [roughneck monitor](/wiki/Roughneck_monitor) (*V. rudicollis*), which has a total length of 1.46 m (4.8 ft), a SVL of 59 cm (23 in) with a mass of 4 kg (8.8 lb), the [Dumeril's monitor](/wiki/Dumeril%27s_monitor) (*V. dumerilii*) (maximum size is about 135 cm (4.43 ft) TL. A breeding pair maintained in captivity measured 130 cm (4.3 ft) TL, 2,950 g (6.50 lb) ([male](/wiki/Male)) and 100 cm (3.3 ft), 2,300 g (5.1 lb) ([female](/wiki/Female)). Wild animals of 30 cm (12 in) SVL weigh about 1 kg (2.2 lb)) and the [yellow monitor](/wiki/Yellow_monitor) (*V. flavescens*) with a maximum length of 95.2 cm (3.12 ft), a SVL of 44.8 cm (17.6 in) and a mass of 1,040 g (2.29 lb).

#### Subgenus [Euprepiosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Euprepiosaurus))

- The largest members of this [subgenus](/wiki/Subgenus) are the [mangrove monitor](/wiki/Mangrove_monitor) (*Varanus indicus*) and its close relatives with a total length of 1.5 m (4.91 ft) (The largest [Mariana monitors](/wiki/Mariana_monitor) (*V. tsukamotoi*) found on [Guam](/wiki/Guam) were 58 cm (23 in) for [male](/wiki/Male) and 44 cm (17 in) for [female](/wiki/Female) in SVL with wrights of 1,900 g (4.2 lb) and 500 g (1.1 lb) in weight respectively), the [Rainer Günther's monitor](/wiki/Varanus_rainerguentheri) (*V. rainerguentheri*) with a maximum total length of 1.33 m (4.4 ft), probably even 1.5 m (4.91 ft), the [tricolor monitor](/wiki/Varanus_yuwonoi) (*V. yuwonoi*) having the maximum length of 1.46 m (4.8 ft), the [blue-tailed monitor](/wiki/Blue-tailed_monitor) (*V. doreanus*) exceeding 1.35 m (4.4 ft) long, the [Rennell Island monitor](/wiki/Rennell_Island_monitor) (*V. juxtindicus*) reaching 1.33 m (4.4 ft) in length and the [New Ireland monitor](/wiki/Varanus_douarrha) (*V. douarrha*) growing the length in 1.33 m (4.4 ft).
- Other large [lizards](/wiki/Lizard) in this group include the [quince monitor](/wiki/Quince_monitor) (*Varanus melinus*) (maximum length of 1.28 m (4.2 ft)), the [peach-throated monitor](/wiki/Peach-throated_monitor) (*V. jobiensis*) (a total length of 1.2 m (3.9 ft) and a SVL of 45 cm (18 in)), the [Mussau Island blue-tailed monitor](/wiki/Mussau_Island_blue-tailed_monitor) (*V. semotus*) (a total length of 1.2 m (3.9 ft) and a SVL of 48 cm (19 in)) and the [sago monitor](/wiki/Sago_monitor) (*V. obor*) (a total length of 1.13 m (3.7 ft) and a SVL of 44 cm (17 in)).

#### Subgenus [Hapturosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Hapturosaurus))

- The largest [tree monitor](/wiki/Varanus_(Hapturosaurus)) is the [blue tree monitor](/wiki/Varanus_macraei) (*Varanus macraei*) with a maximum total length about 1.1 m (3.6 ft) and a SVL of 36 cm (14 in). Other large [tree monitors](/wiki/Varanus_(Hapturosaurus)) are the [emerald tree monitor](/wiki/Emerald_tree_monitor) (*V. prasinus*), which can attain 1 m (3.3 ft) in total length, 30 cm (12 in) in [snout-vent length](/wiki/Snout-vent_length), the [black tree monitor](/wiki/Black_tree_monitor) (*V. beccarii*) with a maximum size of 34 cm (13 in) SVL, 94.5 cm (3.10 ft) total length and the [Bogert's monitor](/wiki/Bogert%27s_monitor) (*V. bogerti*) measuring a length of 94 cm (3.08 ft) with a SVL of 32 cm (13 in).

#### Subgenus [Odatria](/wiki/Varanus_(Odatria))

- Whilst this [subgenus](/wiki/Subgenus) has many species, they are the smallest monitor lizards. It includes [Dampier Peninsula monitor](/wiki/Dampier_Peninsula_monitor) (*Varanus sparnus*) – the smallest monitor with a length of 227 mm (8.9 in), a SVL of 116 mm (4.6 in) and a mass of 16.3 g (0.57 oz).
- The largest species in this subgenus is the [black-palmed rock monitor](/wiki/Black-palmed_rock_monitor) (*Varanus glebopalma*), reaching the maximum size of 397 mm (15.6 in) and 1.1 m (3.6 ft) for SVL and total length respectively (an average mass constitute 240 g (8.5 oz) with a SVL of 290 mm (11 in), for the max.size much higher). Another big member of this group is [Mitchell's water monitor](/wiki/Mitchell%27s_water_monitor) (*Varanus mitchelli*), which may grow to a length in 60–96 cm (1.97–3.15 ft) with a SVL of 22–32 cm (8.7–12.6 in).

#### Subgenus [Papusaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Papusaurus))

- The only species *[Papusaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Papusaurus))* is a [crocodile monitor](/wiki/Crocodile_monitor) (*Varanus salvadorii*) (see higher).

#### Subgenus [Philippinosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Philippinosaurus))

The [Gray's monitor](/wiki/Gray%27s_monitor) along with its relatives is one of heaviest [lizards](/wiki/Lizard) in the world

- These large [frugivorous](/wiki/Frugivorous) [monitor lizards](/wiki/Monitor_lizard) may be 175 cm (5.74 ft) long or more with a mass of >8 kg (18 lb). The [Northern Sierra Madre forest monitor](/wiki/Northern_Sierra_Madre_forest_monitor) (*Varanus bitatawa*) with a maximum size instead be 180 cm (5.9 ft) in length and 9 kg (20 lb) in mass, the [Gray's monitor](/wiki/Gray%27s_monitor) (*V. olivaceus*), the biggest individual of which was just 188 cm (6.17 ft) long and a mass of 8.9 kg (20 lb) and the [Panay monitor](/wiki/Panay_monitor) (*V. mabitang*) with the largest [specimen](/wiki/Biological_specimen) which had a length of 175 cm (5.74 ft) and a mass in 8 kg (18 lb), are among of the biggest [lizards](/wiki/Lizard) in the world. They measure 76.6 cm (30.2 in), 73 cm (29 in) and 70 cm (28 in) SVL respectively.

#### Subgenus [Polydaedalus](/wiki/Varanus_(Polydaedalus))

- The largest species *[Polydaedalus](/wiki/Varanus_(Polydaedalus))* are the [nile monitor](/wiki/Nile_monitor) (*Varanus niloticus*) and the [black-throated monitor](/wiki/Black-throated_monitor) (*V. albigularis microstictus*) (see higher).
- Another large member of African [monitors](/wiki/Monitor_lizard) is a [rock monitor](/wiki/Rock_monitor), and to be more precise, its [type species](/wiki/Type_species) – [white-throated monitor](/wiki/White-throated_monitor) (*Varanus albigularis albigularis*) with a maximum length of 2 m (6.6 ft) and 15–17 kg (33–37 lb) and an average size of 0.8–1.5 m (2.6–4.9 ft) in length and 3.2–5 kg (7.1–11.0 lb) for [females](/wiki/Female) and 6–8 kg (13–18 lb) for [males](/wiki/Male) in mass by some sources. But, however, according to others even mass in 7.7 kg (17 lb) is a very rarely.
- Large sizes are also reached by also other members of *[Polydaedalus](/wiki/Varanus_(Polydaedalus))*. This include [savannah monitor](/wiki/Savannah_monitor) (*Varanus exanthematicus*) and [Yemen monitor](/wiki/Yemen_monitor) (*V. yemenensis*) with a length of 100 cm (3.3 ft) (50 cm (20 in) SVL) and 115 cm (3.77 ft) (59 cm (23 in) SVL) respectively (First one can weighing don't less 1 kg (2.2 lb)) However, [Bosc's monitor](/wiki/Bosc%27s_monitor) in captivity can be extremely obese and exceed 150 cm (4.9 ft) (75 cm (30 in) SVL) long and 6 kg (13 lb)) weight.
- The [ornate monitor](/wiki/Ornate_monitor) can attains the length of 2 m (6.6 ft), however, the ornate monitor is not currently considered a separate [species](/wiki/Species), but is merely a [polymorphism](/wiki/Polymorphism_(biology)) of two different [species](/wiki/Species).

#### Subgenus [Psammosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Psammosaurus))

The Caspian monitor is a largest [lizard](/wiki/Lizard) in [Central Asia](/wiki/Central_Asia), with a maximum reliable length of 1.4 m (4.6 ft) and a mass of almost 3 kg (6.6 lb)

- The [subgenus](/wiki/Subgenus) *[Psammosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_(Psammosaurus))* includes large and medium-sized monitor lizards, growing to a length not less than 120 cm (3.9 ft). The largest member of this little group and also the largest [lizard](/wiki/Lizard) in [Central Asia](/wiki/Central_Asia) is the [desert monitor](/wiki/Desert_monitor) (*Varanus griseus*), specifically Caspian monitor (*Varanus griseus caspius*) with a max.total length of 1.4 m (4.6 ft), a SVL of 58.5 cm (23.0 in) and a mass of 2,850 g (6.28 lb) and a SVL of 46 cm (18 in) with a mass in 2,700 g (6.0 lb) for longest and heaviest [males](/wiki/Male) and [females](/wiki/Female) respectively. The [Nesterov's desert monitor](/wiki/Varanus_nesterovi) (*V. nesterovi*) is also quite large, attaining 1.2 m (3.9 ft) in length and 50 cm (20 in) in SVL.

#### Subgenus [Solomonosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_spinulosus)

- The [Solomon Island spiny monitor](/wiki/Varanus_spinulosus) (*Varanus spinulosus*) is a [species](/wiki/Species) of [subgenus](/wiki/Subgenus) *[Solomonosaurus](/wiki/Varanus_spinulosus)*, with a maximum reliable length and mass in captivity of 1 m (3.3 ft) (SVL 31 cm (12 in)) and 841 g (29.7 oz) respectively.

- Second largest after [Asian water monitor](/wiki/Asian_water_monitor) are the [marbled water monitor](/wiki/Marbled_water_monitor) (*Varanus marmoratus*) and the [Palawan water monitor](/wiki/Palawan_water_monitor) (*Varanus palawanensis*) with a total length of almost 2 m (6.6 ft) (SVL of last one measuring 78.8 cm (31.0 in)). By the data [marbled water monitor](/wiki/Marbled_water_monitor) can weigh about 7.2 kg (16 lb), although since *[V. palawanensis](/wiki/Palawan_water_monitor)* was previously considered a subspecies of *[V. marmoratus](/wiki/Marbled_water_monitor)*, which species this weight referred to is unknown.
- Even medium-sized monitors in this subgenus are quite large. For example, the [yellow-headed water monitor](/wiki/Yellow-headed_water_monitor) (*Varanus cumingi*) and the [large-scaled water monitor](/wiki/Large-scaled_water_monitor) (*V. nuchalis*) with maximum length of 1.5 m (4.9 ft) and 1.45 m (4.8 ft), a SVL of 60 cm (24 in) and 53 cm (21 in) and 2.5 kg (5.5 lb) and 2.8 kg (6.2 lb) in max. mass respectively. #### Subgenus [Varanus](/wiki/Varanus)

The [Argus monitor](/wiki/Argus_monitor) is third largest Australian lizard with mass more 7 kg (15 lb)

- The largest members of this group are the [Komodo dragon](/wiki/Komodo_dragon) (*Varanus komodoensis*), [perentie](/wiki/Perentie) (*V. giganteus*) and [lace monitor](/wiki/Lace_monitor) (*V. varius*) (see higher).
- After them is the [yellow-spotted monitor](/wiki/Yellow-spotted_monitor) or Argus monitor (*Varanus panoptes*), which also is the third biggest lizard in [Australia](/wiki/Australia), with a maximum length of 1.6 m (5.2 ft) (SVL of 74 cm (29 in)) and mass of 7 kg (15 lb), although in captivity huge individuals can weigh about 10 kg (22 lb) and even more.
- A close relative of the aforementioned species is [Gould's monitor](/wiki/Sand_goanna) (*Varanus gouldii*), also attains large sizes — exclusively large specimens measuring length of 1.55 m (5.1 ft) (SVL of 67 cm (26 in)) with a mass of 5 kg (11 lb). - The smallest representative of this genus is the [Mertens' water monitor](/wiki/Mertens%27_water_monitor) (*Varanus mertensi*) with a length of 1.3 m (4.3 ft) (SVL of 48 cm (19 in)). Though not as bulky as some other species of monitor lizards, in captivity it can weigh up to 4.9 kg (11 lb).

**Lawrence Gene David** (born July 2, 1947) is an American comedian, writer, actor, and television producer. He is known for his dry wit, portrayals of awkward social situations, and observations on everyday life. He has received two [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards) as well as nominations for three [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and six [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards). He received the [Writers Guild of America's Laurel Award](/wiki/Laurel_Award_for_TV_Writing_Achievement) in 2010.

David started his career as a stand-up comedian before transitioning into [television comedy](/wiki/Television_comedy), where he wrote and starred in [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)'s *[Fridays](/wiki/Fridays_(TV_series))* (1980–1982) and wrote briefly for *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* (1984–1985). He gained prominence and acclaim when he and [Jerry Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld) created the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) [sitcom](/wiki/Sitcom) *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* (1989–1998). He won two [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) in 1993, for [Outstanding Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Comedy_Series) and [Outstanding Writing for a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Writing_for_a_Comedy_Series). He gained further recognition for creating, writing, and starring in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) series *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)* (2000–2024).

Since 2015, David has made guest appearances on *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* impersonating 2016 and 2020 [U.S. presidential candidate](/wiki/2020_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries) [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders). He has acted in three films directed by [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen), including *[Radio Days](/wiki/Radio_Days)* (1987) and *[Whatever Works](/wiki/Whatever_Works)* (2009). He also wrote and starred in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) movie *[Clear History](/wiki/Clear_History)* (2013). On stage, he made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) debut writing and starring in the comedic play *[Fish in the Dark](/wiki/Fish_in_the_Dark)* (2015). He has written several comedic pieces for *[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* and *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*.

## Early life and education

David was born on July 2, 1947, in the [Sheepshead Bay](/wiki/Sheepshead_Bay,_Brooklyn) neighborhood of [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn), New York City. His parents are Rose (née Regina Brandes) and Mortimer Julius "Morty" David, a men's clothing manufacturer, and he has an older brother, Ken. David's family is [Jewish](/wiki/Jews). His [American Jewish](/wiki/American_Jews) father's family [moved from Germany](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Germany) to the U.S. during the 19th century. David's mother was born into a [Polish-Jewish](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) family in [Ternopil](/wiki/Ternopil), now in [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine); her mother's family name was Superfein.

David graduated from [Sheepshead Bay High School](/wiki/Sheepshead_Bay_High_School), now defunct and operating as Frank J. Macchiarola Educational Complex, in 1965. A sign with his photo is displayed in one of the complex's hallways. He then attended the [University of Maryland, College Park](/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), where he was a brother in [Tau Epsilon Phi](/wiki/Tau_Epsilon_Phi). He graduated in 1970 with a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in [history](/wiki/History). At college, he discovered that he could make people laugh simply by being himself. After college, David [joined](/wiki/Military_service) the [United States Army Reserve](/wiki/United_States_Army_Reserve) and received training as a petroleum storage specialist. To avoid the final year of his six-year enlistment, he paid a psychiatrist to write a letter declaring him unfit for duty.

## Career

### 1980–1987: Stand-up and *SNL*

While a stand-up comedian, David also worked as a store clerk, limousine driver, and historian. He lived in [Manhattan Plaza](/wiki/Manhattan_Plaza), a federally subsidized housing complex in [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan)'s [Hell's Kitchen](/wiki/Hell%27s_Kitchen,_Manhattan) neighborhood, across the hall from [Kenny Kramer](/wiki/Kenny_Kramer), the inspiration for the [Cosmo Kramer](/wiki/Cosmo_Kramer) character in *Seinfeld*. From 1980 to 1982, David became a writer and cast member for [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)'s *[Fridays](/wiki/Fridays_(TV_series))*, where he worked with [Michael Richards](/wiki/Michael_Richards), who later played Kramer on *Seinfeld*.

From 1984 to 1985, David was a writer for [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* (*SNL*), where he met cast member [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus). During his time at *SNL*, he was able to get only one sketch on the air, which aired at 12:50 am, the show's last time slot. David quit his job at *SNL* in the first season, angrily disparaging the quality of the show to producer [Dick Ebersol](/wiki/Dick_Ebersol), only to show up to work two days later as if nothing had happened. That event inspired the second-season *Seinfeld* episode "[The Revenge](/wiki/The_Revenge_(Seinfeld))". He can be heard heckling [Michael McKean](/wiki/Michael_McKean) when McKean hosted *SNL* in 1984, and can be seen in the sketch "The Run, Throw, and Catch Like a Girl Olympics" when [Howard Cosell](/wiki/Howard_Cosell) hosted the season finale in 1985. In 1987, David was a writer and performer for *Way Off Broadway*, a variety talk show on [Lifetime](/wiki/Lifetime_(TV_network)) hosted by [Joy Behar](/wiki/Joy_Behar).

### 1989–1998: Breakthrough with *Seinfeld*

Main article: [Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)

In 1989, David teamed up with comedian [Jerry Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld) to create a pilot for NBC called *[The Seinfeld Chronicles](/wiki/The_Seinfeld_Chronicles)*, which became the basis for *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)*, one of the most successful shows in history, reaching the top of *[TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide)'s* [list of the 50 greatest TV shows of all time](/wiki/TV_Guide%27s_50_Greatest_TV_Shows_of_All_Time). *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* ranked it the third-best US TV show of all time. David made occasional uncredited appearances on the show, playing such roles as Frank Costanza's cape-wearing lawyer and the voice of [George Steinbrenner](/wiki/George_Steinbrenner_(Seinfeld_character)). He was also the primary inspiration for the show's character [George Costanza](/wiki/George_Costanza). David left *Seinfeld* amicably after the show's seventh season and returned two years later to write the series finale in 1998. He also continued to voice Steinbrenner.

David wrote 62 *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* episodes, including 1992's "[The Contest](/wiki/The_Contest)", for which he won a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) and which *[TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide)* ranked as episode No. 1 on its list of "TV's [Top 100 Episodes of All Time](/wiki/Top_100_Episodes_of_All_Time)". He has also been involved in other films and television series. David wrote and directed the 1998 film *[Sour Grapes](/wiki/Sour_Grapes_(1998_film))*, about two cousins who feud over a [casino](/wiki/Casino) jackpot. It was neither a commercial nor a critical success. He has also appeared in bit roles in [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen)'s *[Radio Days](/wiki/Radio_Days)* (1987) and *[New York Stories](/wiki/New_York_Stories)* (1989).

### 1999–2024: *Curb Your Enthusiasm* and acclaim

Main article: [Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)

David in December 2009

David in 2009

The [HBO](/wiki/HBO) cable television channel aired David's one-hour special, *[Larry David: Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Larry_David:_Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, on October 17, 1999. This was followed by *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, an HBO television series whose first episode aired on October 15, 2000. The show revisits many of the themes of *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* and is improvised from a story outline only several pages long written by David (and, from the fifth season onward, additional writers).

The actors improvise their dialogue based on the outline and direction. David has said that his character in the show, a fictionalized version of himself, is what he would be like in real life if he lacked social awareness and sensitivity. The character's numerous and frequent social faux pas, misunderstandings, and ironic coincidences are the basis of much of the show's comedy and have led to the entry into the American pop culture lexicon of the expression "Larry David moment", meaning an inadvertently created socially awkward situation. *Curb Your Enthusiasm* has been described as depicting "the things nobody wants to say, but wish they could".

The show is based on David's life following the fortune he earned from *Seinfeld*; semi-retired, he strives to live a fulfilled life. Alongside David is his wife Cheryl ([Cheryl Hines](/wiki/Cheryl_Hines)), his manager and best friend Jeff ([Jeff Garlin](/wiki/Jeff_Garlin)), and Jeff's wife Susie ([Susie Essman](/wiki/Susie_Essman)). Celebrities, including comedians [Richard Lewis](/wiki/Richard_Lewis_(comedian)), [Wanda Sykes](/wiki/Wanda_Sykes), and [Bob Einstein](/wiki/Bob_Einstein), appeared on the show regularly. Actors [Ted Danson](/wiki/Ted_Danson) and [Mary Steenburgen](/wiki/Mary_Steenburgen) have had recurring roles as themselves.

The show is critically acclaimed and has been nominated for 30 Primetime Emmy Awards, with one win, as well as a Golden Globe win. In the first six seasons, [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus) and [Jason Alexander](/wiki/Jason_Alexander) appear in several episodes, and [Jerry Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld) has a cameo. In season 7, the cast of *Seinfeld*, including [Michael Richards](/wiki/Michael_Richards), return in a story arc involving David's attempt to organize a *Seinfeld* reunion special. On June 2, 2010, the series premiered on the [TV Guide Network](/wiki/TV_Guide_Network), its network television debut. TV Guide Network also produced a series of related discussions with high-profile guest stars, media pundits, and prominent social figures called "Curb: The Discussion" debating the moral implications of each episode. David is quoted as saying "Finally, thanks to the TV Guide Network, I'll get a chance to watch actual, intelligent people discuss and debate the issues addressed on 'Curb'. Now if only someone could tell me where this alleged 'Network' is, I might even watch it." The show's 12th and final season premiered in January 2024.

David played the leading role in [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen)'s 2009 comedy film *[Whatever Works](/wiki/Whatever_Works)* alongside [Evan Rachel Wood](/wiki/Evan_Rachel_Wood). He had a cameo appearance on the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) series *[Entourage](/wiki/Entourage_(U.S._TV_series))* as a client of [Ari Gold](/wiki/Ari_Gold_(Entourage)), and because his daughters were *[Hannah Montana](/wiki/Hannah_Montana)* fans, David and his daughters guest-starred as themselves in the episode "My Best Friend's Boyfriend", in which they wait for a table at a fancy restaurant. David appeared as a panelist on the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) series *[The Marriage Ref](/wiki/The_Marriage_Ref_(American_TV_series))* and also played Sister Mary-Mengele in the 2012 reboot of *[The Three Stooges](/wiki/The_Three_Stooges_(2012_film))*. He co-wrote and starred in the 2013 HBO television film *[Clear History](/wiki/Clear_History)*. David wrote and starred in the Broadway play *[Fish in the Dark](/wiki/Fish_in_the_Dark)*. Also appearing were [Rita Wilson](/wiki/Rita_Wilson), [Jayne Houdyshell](/wiki/Jayne_Houdyshell), and [Rosie Perez](/wiki/Rosie_Perez). The play centers on the death of a family patriarch. It opened on March 5, 2015. [Jason Alexander](/wiki/Jason_Alexander) took over David's role in July. The play closed in August. As of February 1, 2015, its advance sale of $13.5 million had broken records for a Broadway show.

#### Portrayal of Bernie Sanders

Since 2015, David has made multiple guest appearances portraying 2016 and 2020 United States presidential election candidate [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) on *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*; he also hosted the show on February 6, 2016, with musical guest [The 1975](/wiki/The_1975) and a cameo by Sanders himself, and on November 4, 2017, with musical guest [Miley Cyrus](/wiki/Miley_Cyrus).

In 2017, [PBS](/wiki/PBS)'s *[Finding Your Roots](/wiki/Finding_Your_Roots)* discovered through genealogical research that David and Sanders are distantly related. Sanders told David the news. "I was very happy about that," David said, according to *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))*. "I thought there must have been some connection." The comedian explained that Sanders is "a third cousin or something". He is in fact David's sixth cousin once removed.

On January 8, 2020, David joked on *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert)*, "I would say, I would beg him [Bernie] to drop out so I don't have to keep flying in from Los Angeles to do *SNL*. I thought when he had the heart attack that would be it, I wouldn't have to fly in from Los Angeles. But, you know, he's indestructible. Nothing stops this man!" He later added, "If he wins, do you know what that's going to do to my life? Do you have any idea? I mean, it will be great for the country—great for the country, terrible for me."

#### "My Dinner With Adolf" essay (2025)

Main article: [My Dinner with Adolf](/wiki/My_Dinner_with_Adolf)

On April 21, 2025, *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* published an opinion essay by David titled "My Dinner With Adolf". Framed as a fictional 1939 dinner between the narrator and [Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler), the satirical piece uses [dark humor](/wiki/Black_comedy) to explore how personal charm can dangerously obscure the true nature of monstrous people. Without directly naming any contemporary figures, the essay mirrors the language [Bill Maher](/wiki/Bill_Maher) used when describing his dinner with [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump). Despite being a longtime critic of Trump, Maher characterized Trump as "gracious and measured" in private.

### 2025–present: Return to television

In July 2025, David announced his return to television with a six-episode sketch comedy series for [HBO](/wiki/HBO), centered on overlooked figures and absurd moments in U.S. history. The series is co-written and executive produced by David and longtime collaborator [Jeff Schaffer](/wiki/Jeff_Schaffer), who will also direct. The show is produced in collaboration with [Barack](/wiki/Barack_Obama) and [Michelle Obama's](/wiki/Michelle_Obama) [Higher Ground Productions](/wiki/Higher_Ground_Productions). David, who said he was returning to TV "with a heavy heart" following the end of *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, will star alongside several familiar faces from *Curb* and guest appearances. The project is intended to coincide with the [250th anniversary of the United States](/wiki/United_States_Semiquincentennial). *Life, Larry and the Pursuit of Unhappiness: An Almost History of America* is slated to premiere in June 2026.

## Influences

David has named [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen), [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks), [Phil Silvers](/wiki/Phil_Silvers), [Abbott and Costello](/wiki/Abbott_and_Costello), [Jackie Mason](/wiki/Jackie_Mason), [Alan King](/wiki/Alan_King), [Don Rickles](/wiki/Don_Rickles), and *[Mad](/wiki/Mad_(Magazine))* magazine as influences.

## Personal life

David lives in the [Pacific Palisades](/wiki/Pacific_Palisades,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood of [Los Angeles, California](/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California). He was married to [Laurie Lennard](/wiki/Laurie_David) from 1993 to 2007. They have two daughters, [Cazzie David](/wiki/Cazzie_David) and Romy David. Larry and Laurie became contributing bloggers at *[The Huffington Post](/wiki/The_Huffington_Post)* in 2005. In 2017, David was introduced to producer Ashley Underwood at a birthday party for [Sacha Baron Cohen](/wiki/Sacha_Baron_Cohen). They married in 2020. David's niece is actress [Julie Claire](/wiki/Julie_Claire), who appears in *Seinfeld* and *Curb Your Enthusiasm*.

David is an atheist and an avid sports fan. A native New Yorker, he supports the [New York Jets](/wiki/New_York_Jets), [Yankees](/wiki/New_York_Yankees), [Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks), and [Rangers](/wiki/New_York_Rangers). David is also a supporter of the [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)). In 2010, he wrote an article for *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* criticizing the extension of the [Bush tax cuts](/wiki/Bush_tax_cuts) for the wealthy. He ended the article by sarcastically thanking then-President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) for approving the extension.

### Wealth

In 2013, [Charlie Rose](/wiki/Charlie_Rose) estimated David's [net worth](/wiki/Net_worth) at around $500 million. Two years later, two other estimates put the number between $400 million and $900 million. In 2020, *[National Review](/wiki/National_Review)* offered an estimate of about $400 million.

Most of David's wealth originates from [syndication deals](/wiki/Syndication_exclusivity) of *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* and *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, the former having netted $3.1 billion in rerun fees as of 2013. The syndication of *Seinfeld* earned David an estimated $250 million in 1998 alone. In 2008, David was reported to have grossed $55 million, mostly from *Seinfeld* syndication and work on *Curb Your Enthusiasm*.

David's net worth was parodied in a 2001 episode of *Curb Your Enthusiasm*, "[The Shrimp Incident](/wiki/List_of_Curb_Your_Enthusiasm_episodes#Episodes)", in which [HBO](/wiki/HBO) executive Allan Wasserman yells at David: "If you want shrimp, take your $475 million, go buy a shrimp boat."

In a 2015 interview with [CBS](/wiki/CBS), David confirmed that his 2007 divorce reduced his wealth by half in the [community property state](/wiki/Community_property_in_the_United_States) of California. "I have a lot of money", he said, but added that the "figures out there are crazy".

### Cryptocurrency promotion lawsuit

David was among several celebrities who appeared in television commercials for the cryptocurrency exchange [FTX](/wiki/FTX) that aired during [Super Bowl LVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI) in February 2022. In November 2022, [FTX filed for bankruptcy](/wiki/Bankruptcy_of_FTX), and David, alongside other spokespeople, was sued in a [class action lawsuit](/wiki/Class_action_lawsuit). In January 2024, David expressed some regrets over appearing in the ad. In May 2025, a federal court dismissed most claims in the lawsuit.

## Awards and nominations

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Larry David](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Larry_David)

David has received numerous awards, including two [Emmy Awards](/wiki/Emmy_Awards), three [Producers Guild of America Awards](/wiki/Producers_Guild_of_America_Awards), and three [Writers Guild of America Awards](/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_Awards). He has been nominated for three [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and six [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards). Fellow comedians and comedy insiders voted David the 23rd-greatest comedy star ever in a poll to select *The Comedian's Comedian*.

**Belcalis Marlenis Almánzar** (formerly **Cephus**; born October 11, 1992), known professionally as **Cardi B**, is an American rapper. Born and raised in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City), she is known for her energetic [rap flow](/wiki/Rap_flow) and outspoken lyrics. Since launching her music career in 2016 and releasing [her two mixtapes](/wiki/Cardi_B_discography), Cardi B has been cited for her part in helping elevate the relevance of female rappers in [popular music](/wiki/Popular_music).

Her first studio album, *[Invasion of Privacy](/wiki/Invasion_of_Privacy_(album))* (2018), debuted atop the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) with the largest female rap album streaming week of all time and became the best-selling and the highest-certified female rap album of the 21st century. Ranked by *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* as the best debut album of all time by a female rapper, it made Cardi B the first solo female artist in history to win the [Grammy Award for Best Rap Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap_Album). Its singles "[Bodak Yellow](/wiki/Bodak_Yellow)" and "[I Like It](/wiki/I_Like_It_(Cardi_B,_Bad_Bunny_and_J_Balvin_song))" both topped the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) and were certified [diamond](/wiki/RIAA_certification#RIAA_Diamond_certifications) by the [RIAA](/wiki/RIAA); the former made Cardi B the first female rapper to top the Hot 100 with a solo song in the 21st century and the first to achieve a diamond-certified song, while the latter made her the first with multiple number-one songs. Her third US number-one, the collaboration "[Girls Like You](/wiki/Girls_Like_You)" in 2018, made her the first female rapper to earn multiple RIAA diamond-certified songs.

Cardi B's second album, *[Am I the Drama?](/wiki/Am_I_the_Drama%3F)* (2025), made her the only female rapper in history to have her first two albums debut atop the *Billboard* 200. It became the female rap album with the highest certification of the 2020s, the most weeks inside the top ten of the *Billboard* 200 in the 2020s, and the most charting songs (18) in *Billboard* Hot 100 history. Including the US top-ten song "[Outside](/wiki/Outside_(Cardi_B_song))" and US number-ones "[WAP](/wiki/WAP_(song))" and "[Up](/wiki/Up_(Cardi_B_song))", it tied with her own *Invasion of Privacy* as the female rap album with the most Hot 100 number-ones (two each). "WAP" is the only female rap collaboration to debut atop the Hot 100 and had a large cultural impact.

Cardi B is [the female rapper with the most number-one singles](/wiki/List_of_Billboard_Hot_100_chart_achievements_and_milestones) (five) on the *Billboard* Hot 100, the only female rapper to achieve multiple solo number-ones, and the only to earn number-one singles in two decades (2010s and 2020s). She further is [the highest-certified female rapper of all time](/wiki/List_of_highest-certified_music_artists_in_the_United_States) in US digital single sales (69 million), has three diamond-certified songs—with "Bodak Yellow" being [the highest-certified female rap song of all time](/wiki/List_of_highest-certified_digital_singles_in_the_United_States)—and achieved an overall total of 100 million RIAA-certified [units](/wiki/Album-equivalent_unit) sold in the US. Additionally, *Invasion of Privacy* is the most-streamed female rap album on [Apple Music](/wiki/Apple_Music) and [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify), and was the first album in history to have all of its tracks certified platinum or higher. Cardi B [has won](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Cardi_B) a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award), the most [*Billboard* Music Awards](/wiki/Billboard_Music_Awards) (8), *[Guinness World Records](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records)* (8), [BET Hip Hop Awards](/wiki/BET_Hip_Hop_Awards) (14), and [ASCAP](/wiki/ASCAP) [Songwriter of the Year](/wiki/American_Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Publishers#Awards) awards (2) among female rappers, six [American Music Awards](/wiki/American_Music_Awards) and four [MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards). *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* listed her as one of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100) in 2018, while [*Billboard*](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine)) named her [Woman of the Year](/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music#Woman_of_the_Year_Award) in 2020 and ranked *Invasion of Privacy* as the top female rap album of the 2010s.

## Early life

Cardi B was born and raised in the southern part of the [South Bronx](/wiki/South_Bronx).

Belcalis Marlenis Almánzar was born on October 11, 1992, in the [Washington Heights](/wiki/Washington_Heights,_Manhattan) neighborhood of [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan), New York City. Her father Carlos is [Dominican](/wiki/People_of_the_Dominican_Republic) and her mother Clara is a [Trinidadian](/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago) of [mixed](/wiki/Multiracial_people) descent; Almánzar identifies as an [Afro-Caribbean](/wiki/Black_Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans). Almánzar was raised in the [Highbridge](/wiki/Highbridge,_Bronx) neighborhood of the [South Bronx](/wiki/South_Bronx), and spent much time at her paternal grandmother's home in Washington Heights, which she credits with giving her "such a thick accent." Almánzar developed the stage name "Cardi B" as a derivation of [Bacardi](/wiki/Bacardi), a [rum](/wiki/Rum) brand that was formerly her nickname. She has a younger sister, [Hennessy Carolina](/wiki/Hennessy_Carolina), who was born in 1995. She has said she was a gang member with the [Bloods](/wiki/United_Blood_Nation) in her youth, since age 16, but stated she would not encourage joining a gang. She attended Renaissance High School for Musical Theater & Technology, a [vocational high school](/wiki/Vocational_high_school) on the [Herbert H. Lehman High School](/wiki/Herbert_H._Lehman_High_School) campus. Belcalis speaks [Dominican Spanish](/wiki/Dominican_Spanish), English, and a small amount of [Russian](/wiki/Russian_language) and French.

During her teens, Cardi B was employed at a grocery store in [Tribeca](/wiki/Tribeca). She was fired and became a dancer at a strip club across the street. Cardi B has said that becoming a stripper was positive for her life in many ways: "It really saved me from a lot of things. When I started stripping I went back to school." She stated that she became a stripper to escape poverty and [domestic violence](/wiki/Domestic_violence), having been in an abusive relationship at the time after being kicked out of her mother's house, and that stripping was her only way to earn enough money to escape the situation and get an education. She attended [Borough of Manhattan Community College](/wiki/Borough_of_Manhattan_Community_College) before eventually dropping out. While stripping, Cardi B lied to her mother by telling her she was making money by [babysitting](/wiki/Babysitting).

In 2013, she began to gain publicity due to several of her videos spreading on social media, on [Vine](/wiki/Vine_(service)) and her [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram) page.

## Career

### 2015–2016: Career beginnings

Cardi B interviewing on *[Street Soldiers with Lisa Evers](/wiki/Lisa_Evers)* in January 2016

In 2015, Cardi B joined the cast of the [VH1](/wiki/VH1) reality television series *[Love & Hip Hop: New York](/wiki/Love_%26_Hip_Hop:_New_York)*, debuting in season six. *[Jezebel](/wiki/Jezebel_(website))* considered her the breakout star of the show's sixth season. *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* wrote that she garnered popularity with "her ability to rattle off one-liners". The sixth and seventh seasons chronicle her rise to stardom and her turbulent relationship with her incarcerated fiancé. On December 30, 2016, after two seasons, she announced that she would be leaving the show to further pursue a career in music.

In November 2015, Cardi B made her musical debut on Jamaican [reggae fusion](/wiki/Reggae_fusion) singer [Shaggy](/wiki/Shaggy_(musician))'s remix to his single "Boom Boom", alongside fellow Jamaican [dancehall](/wiki/Dancehall) singer [Popcaan](/wiki/Popcaan). She made her music video debut on December 15, 2015, with the song "Cheap Ass Weave", her rendition of British [rapper](/wiki/Rapper) [Lady Leshurr](/wiki/Lady_Leshurr)'s "Queen's Speech 4". On March 7, 2016, Cardi B released her first full-length project, a [mixtape](/wiki/Mixtape) titled *[Gangsta Bitch Music, Vol. 1](/wiki/Gangsta_Bitch_Music,_Vol._1)*. In November 2016, she was featured on the digital cover of *[Vibe](/wiki/Vibe_(magazine))* magazine's "Viva" issue.

On September 12, 2016, KSR Group released the compilation *Underestimated: The Album*, a collaboration between Cardi B and other KSR Group artists [HoodCelebrityy](/wiki/HoodCelebrityy), SwiftOnDemand, Cashflow Harlem, and [Josh X](/wiki/Josh_X). It was previously released only to attendees of their U.S. tour. As the label's flagship artist, Cardi B said: "I wanted to make a song that would make girls dance, [twerk](/wiki/Twerk) and at the same time encourage them to go get that Shmoney," in regard to the compilation's single "What a Girl Likes".

She appeared on the December 9, 2015, episode of *[Uncommon Sense with Charlamagne](/wiki/Uncommon_Sense_with_Charlamagne)*. On April 6, 2016, she was on the twelfth episode of [Khloé Kardashian](/wiki/Khlo%C3%A9_Kardashian)'s *[Kocktails with Khloé](/wiki/Kocktails_with_Khlo%C3%A9)*: in this episode, she revealed how she told her mother that she was a stripper. In November 2016, it was announced that she would be joining the cast of the [BET](/wiki/BET) series *[Being Mary Jane](/wiki/Being_Mary_Jane)*. [TVLine](/wiki/TVLine) describes her character, Mercedes, as a "round-the-way beauty with a big weave, big boobs and a big booty to match her oversize, ratchet personality."

In 2016, Cardi B was featured in her first endorsement deal with [Romantic Depot](/wiki/Romantic_Depot), a large New York chain of lingerie stores that sell sexual health and wellness products. The ad campaign was featured on radio and cable TV.

### 2017–2018: Breakthrough with *Invasion of Privacy*

Cardi B at the [2018 MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/2018_MTV_Video_Music_Awards)

On January 20, 2017, Cardi B released her second mixtape, *[Gangsta Bitch Music, Vol. 2](/wiki/Gangsta_Bitch_Music,_Vol._2)*. In February 2017, Cardi B partnered with [MAC Cosmetics](/wiki/MAC_Cosmetics) and Rio Uribe's [Gypsy Sport](/wiki/Gypsy_Sport) for an event for [New York Fashion Week](/wiki/New_York_Fashion_Week). In late February, Cardi B signed her first major record label recording contract with [Atlantic Records](/wiki/Atlantic_Records). On February 25, 2017, Cardi B was the [opening act](/wiki/Opening_act) for [East Coast hip hop](/wiki/East_Coast_hip_hop) group [The Lox](/wiki/The_Lox)'s [Filthy America... It's Beautiful](/wiki/Filthy_America..._It%27s_Beautiful) Tour, alongside fellow New York City-based rappers [Lil' Kim](/wiki/Lil%27_Kim) and [Remy Ma](/wiki/Remy_Ma). In April 2017, she was featured in *[i-D](/wiki/I-D)*'s "A-Z of Music" video sponsored by [Marc Jacobs](/wiki/Marc_Jacobs). Cardi guest-starred on the celebrity [panel show](/wiki/Panel_show) *[Hip Hop Squares](/wiki/Hip_Hop_Squares)*, appearing on the March 13 and April 3, 2017, episodes. She also released the [freestyle](/wiki/Freestyle_rap) "Red Barz".

In May 2017, the nominees for the [2017 BET Awards](/wiki/2017_BET_Awards) were announced, revealing that Cardi B had been nominated for [Best New Artist](/wiki/BET_Award_for_Best_New_Artist) and [Best Female Hip-Hop Artist](/wiki/BET_Award_for_Best_Female_Hip-Hop_Artist). Although [Chance the Rapper](/wiki/Chance_the_Rapper) and Remy Ma won those categories, respectively, Cardi B performed at the [BET Awards](/wiki/BET_Awards) [Afterparty](/wiki/Party#After-party) show. On June 11, 2017, during [Hot 97](/wiki/Hot_97)'s annual [Summer Jam](/wiki/Summer_Jam_(festival)) music festival, Remy Ma brought out Cardi B, along with [The Lady of Rage](/wiki/The_Lady_of_Rage), [MC Lyte](/wiki/MC_Lyte), [Young M.A](/wiki/Young_M.A), [Monie Love](/wiki/Monie_Love), Lil' Kim, and [Queen Latifah](/wiki/Queen_Latifah), to celebrate female rappers and perform Latifah's 1993 hit single "[U.N.I.T.Y.](/wiki/U.N.I.T.Y.)" about [female empowerment](/wiki/Female_empowerment). In June 2017, it was revealed that Cardi B would be on the cover of *[The Fader](/wiki/The_Fader)*'s Summer Music issue for July/August 2017. She performed at [MoMA PS1](/wiki/MoMA_PS1) on August 19 to a crowd of 4,000.

On June 16, 2017, Atlantic Records released Cardi B's commercial debut single, "[Bodak Yellow](/wiki/Bodak_Yellow)", via [digital distribution](/wiki/Digital_distribution). She performed the single on *[The Wendy Williams Show](/wiki/The_Wendy_Williams_Show)* and *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)* The song climbed the charts for several months, and, on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) chart dated September 25, 2017, "Bodak Yellow" reached the number one spot, making Cardi B the first female rapper to do so with a solo single since [Lauryn Hill](/wiki/Lauryn_Hill)'s "[Doo Wop (That Thing)](/wiki/Doo_Wop_(That_Thing))" debuted atop the chart in 1998. The song stayed atop the charts for three consecutive weeks, tying with American pop singer [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift)'s "[Look What You Made Me Do](/wiki/Look_What_You_Made_Me_Do)" as the longest running female at the number one spot in 2017.

Cardi B became the first person of Dominican descent to reach number one in the history of the Hot 100 since it was launched in 1958. An editor of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* called it "the rap anthem of the summer". Selected by *[The Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* and *[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website))* music critics as the best song of 2017, "Bodak Yellow" was eventually certified [Diamond](/wiki/RIAA_certification) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA). The song received nominations for [Best Rap Performance](/wiki/Best_Rap_Performance) and [Best Rap Song](/wiki/Best_Rap_Song) at the [60th Grammy Awards](/wiki/60th_Grammy_Awards). It won Single of the Year at the [2017 BET Hip Hop Awards](/wiki/2017_BET_Hip_Hop_Awards). With her collaborations "[No Limit](/wiki/No_Limit_(G-Eazy_song))" and "[MotorSport](/wiki/MotorSport)", she became the first female rapper to land her first three entries in the top 10 of the Hot 100, and the first female artist to achieve the same on the [Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs](/wiki/Hot_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Songs) chart. In October 2017, Cardi B headlined [Power 105.1](/wiki/Power_105.1)'s annual Powerhouse music celebration, alongside [the Weeknd](/wiki/The_Weeknd), [Migos](/wiki/Migos), and [Lil Uzi Vert](/wiki/Lil_Uzi_Vert), at the [Barclays Center](/wiki/Barclays_Center) in Brooklyn, New York. In December, she released two songs: a collaboration with Puerto Rican singer [Ozuna](/wiki/Ozuna) titled "[La Modelo](/wiki/La_Modelo_(Ozuna_song))", and "[Bartier Cardi](/wiki/Bartier_Cardi)", the second single from her [debut album.](/wiki/Invasion_of_Privacy_(album))

Cardi B backstage at the 2018 [Jingle Ball](/wiki/Jingle_Ball)

On January 3, 2018, Cardi B was featured on the remix of [Bruno Mars](/wiki/Bruno_Mars)' single "[Finesse](/wiki/Finesse_(Bruno_Mars_song))", and appeared in the 90s inspired video. It reached the top three on the Hot 100, Canada and New Zealand. On January 18, 2018, Cardi B became the first female rapper to have five top 10 singles simultaneously on the *Billboard* Hot R&B/Hip-Hop Songs chart. She released another single, "[Be Careful](/wiki/Be_Careful_(Cardi_B_song))", on March 30, 2018, a week before her album's release.

Her debut studio album, *[Invasion of Privacy](/wiki/Invasion_of_Privacy_(album))*, was released on April 6, 2018, to universal acclaim from music critics. Editors from *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* and *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* called it "one of the most powerful debuts of this millennium" and "a hip-hop album that doesn't sound like any of its temporal peers," respectively. The album entered at number one in the United States, while she became the first female artist to chart 13 entries simultaneously on the *Billboard* Hot 100, on the chart issue dated April 21. It became the most [streamed](/wiki/Streamed) album by a female artist in a single week in [Apple Music](/wiki/Apple_Music), and the largest on-demand audio streaming week ever for an album by a woman. Cardi held the latter record [until 2019](/wiki/Thank_U,_Next). The album's title reflects Cardi B's feeling that as she gained popularity her privacy was being invaded in a variety of ways. Following the album's release, during a performance on *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*, Cardi B officially announced her pregnancy, after much media speculation. She also co-hosted an episode of *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)*.

After the release of "[Girls](/wiki/Girls_(Rita_Ora_song))" in May 2018, a collaboration where she had a featured verse, Cardi B responded to accusations of the song trivializing [LGBT](/wiki/LGBT) relationships; she tweeted, "We never try to cause harm or had bad intentions with the song." She went on to add, "I personally myself had experiences with other women." Several months later, in July 2018, *Invasion*'s fourth single, "[I Like It](/wiki/I_Like_It_(Cardi_B,_Bad_Bunny_and_J_Balvin_song))", which features vocals from [Bad Bunny](/wiki/Bad_Bunny) and [J Balvin](/wiki/J_Balvin), reached number one on the [Hot 100](/wiki/Hot_100); this marked her second number one on the chart and made her the first female rapper to achieve multiple chart-toppers. It received critical acclaim, with *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* naming it "the [best summer song](/wiki/Song_of_the_summer) of all time" in 2020. Her collaboration with [Maroon 5](/wiki/Maroon_5), "[Girls Like You](/wiki/Girls_Like_You)", also reached number one on the Hot 100 chart, extending her record among female rappers and also making her the sixth female artist to achieve three number-one singles on the chart during the 2010s. The song's music video has received more than 3.8 billion views on [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) and was the [fifth-best selling song](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles) of the year globally.

With "Girls Like You" following "I Like It" at the top of the *Billboard* [Radio Songs](/wiki/Hot_100_Airplay_(Radio_Songs)) chart, Cardi B became the [first female rapper](/wiki/Hot_100_Airplay_(Radio_Songs)#Self-replacement_at_number_one) to replace herself at number one on the chart. The single spent seven weeks atop the Hot 100, making Cardi the female rapper with the most cumulative weeks atop the chart, with eleven weeks. It spent 33 weeks in the top 10, tying both [Ed Sheeran](/wiki/Ed_Sheeran)'s "[Shape of You](/wiki/Shape_of_You)" and [Post Malone](/wiki/Post_Malone) and [Swae Lee](/wiki/Swae_Lee)'s "[Sunflower](/wiki/Sunflower_(Post_Malone_and_Swae_Lee_song))" for the longest top 10 run in the chart's archives at the time. In September 2025, *Invasion of Privacy* was certified [six-times platinum](/wiki/RIAA_Certification) by the RIAA. With the thirteen tracks, it became the first album in history to have all of its songs certified platinum or higher in the US.

Cardi B received the most nominations for the [2018 MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/2018_MTV_Video_Music_Awards) with 12 mentions—including for [Video of the Year](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Video_of_the_Year), winning three awards. She tied with [Drake](/wiki/Drake_(musician)) for the most nominations at the [2018 American Music Awards](/wiki/2018_American_Music_Awards). She won three AMAs and performed at the ceremony. Her single "[Money](/wiki/Money_(Cardi_B_song))" earned her a fourth Video Music Award, with visuals that feature Cardi playing characters in different locations, including in an art museum, a bank and a strip club. Her collaboration with [DJ Snake](/wiki/DJ_Snake) "[Taki Taki](/wiki/Taki_Taki)" topped the charts in a number of Hispanic countries, made Cardi B the first female rapper to top the Spotify [Global 50](/wiki/List_of_most-streamed_songs_on_Spotify#Weekly_number-one_songs) chart, and has garnered more than 2 billion views. Both singles were certified multiple-platinum by the RIAA. *[People en Español](/wiki/People_en_Espa%C3%B1ol)* named her Star of the Year, and *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* deemed her "a pop culture phenomenon", as she was named one of "2018 Entertainers of the Year".

On November 30, 2018, Cardi B was honored at *[Ebony](/wiki/Ebony_Magazine)*'s annual Power 100 Gala. Cardi ranked fifth on the 2018 [*Billboard* Year-End](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End) Top Artists chart, while *Invasion of Privacy* ranked sixth. She achieved the most-streamed album of the year by a female artist globally in [Apple Music](/wiki/Apple_Music), and ranked as the most streamed female artist of the year in the United States in [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify). Editorial staff from Apple Music and *Billboard* named "I Like It" the best song of 2018, while [*Time* magazine](/wiki/Time_(magazine)) and *Rolling Stone* named *Invasion of Privacy* the best album of the year. Also in 2018, *Time* included her on their [annual list](/wiki/Time_100) of the 100 most influential people in the world. In its decade-end review article, *[NME](/wiki/NME)* stated that the era secured "her crown as the new [Queen of Rap](/wiki/Queen_of_Rap)."

### 2019–2020: *Hustlers*, *Rhythm + Flow* and "WAP"

Cardi B performing at the [Openair Frauenfeld](/wiki/Openair_Frauenfeld) in July 2019

Cardi B received five nominations at the [61st Grammy Awards](/wiki/61st_Grammy_Awards), including for [Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year), [Best Rap Album](/wiki/Best_Rap_Album) and [Record of the Year](/wiki/Record_of_the_Year) ("I Like It"). She became the third female rapper to be nominated for Album of the Year, following [Lauryn Hill](/wiki/Lauryn_Hill) (1999) and [Missy Elliott](/wiki/Missy_Elliott) (2004). On February 10, 2019, she then performed at the award ceremony, where she wore three vintage [Thierry Mugler](/wiki/Thierry_Mugler) couture looks during the telecast and became the first female rapper to win [Best Rap Album](/wiki/Best_Rap_Album) as a solo artist. Cardi B led the [2019 Billboard Music Awards](/wiki/2019_Billboard_Music_Awards) nominations, with 21, earning the most nominations in a single year by a female artist and the third most nominations in a year (behind Drake and The Chainsmokers, who both had 22 in a year). She ended up winning six awards, including for [Top Hot 100 Song](/wiki/Billboard_Music_Award_for_Top_Hot_100_Song), bringing her career total wins to seven—the most of any female rapper in history. An article by *[Omaha World-Herald](/wiki/Omaha_World-Herald)* called her "the biggest rapper in the world."

On February 15, 2019, Cardi B released "[Please Me](/wiki/Please_Me)", a collaboration with Bruno Mars, which became her seventh top-ten song on the Hot 100, reaching number three. The song marked Cardi and Bruno's second collaboration, following "[Finesse](/wiki/Finesse_(Bruno_Mars_song))" in 2018. The official music video was released two weeks later. On March 1, Cardi set a new attendance record at the [Houston Livestock Show and Rodeo](/wiki/Houston_Livestock_Show_and_Rodeo), with 75,580 fans in the audience. With "[Backin' It Up](/wiki/Backin%27_It_Up)", "[Twerk](/wiki/Twerk_(song))" and "[Money](/wiki/Money_(Cardi_B_song))", Cardi became the first female artist to occupy the top three on the *Billboard* Mainstream R&B/Hip-Hop airplay chart.

Her following single titled "[Press](/wiki/Press_(Cardi_B_song))" was released on May 31, 2019. The [parental-advisory](/wiki/Parental_Advisory) labeled music video marked her directorial debut—being credited as co-director, and was released on June 26, 2019. It had its debut performance at the [2019 BET Awards](/wiki/2019_BET_Awards), where she received the most nominations with seven, and won [Album of the Year](/wiki/BET_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year). During the summer of 2019, she embarked on an arena tour. In September, she led the [BET Hip Hop Awards](/wiki/2019_BET_Hip_Hop_Awards) nominations with ten.

Cardi B made her film debut in *[Hustlers](/wiki/Hustlers_(film))* directed by [Lorene Scafaria](/wiki/Lorene_Scafaria), opposite [Jennifer Lopez](/wiki/Jennifer_Lopez), [Constance Wu](/wiki/Constance_Wu), and [Lili Reinhart](/wiki/Lili_Reinhart). The film was released on September 13, 2019. Cardi B, along with [Chance the Rapper](/wiki/Chance_the_Rapper) and [T.I.](/wiki/T.I.), were confirmed as judges for the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) series *[Rhythm + Flow](/wiki/Rhythm_%2B_Flow)*, a ten-part hip-hop talent search that premiered on October 9, 2019, which she also [executive produced](/wiki/Executive_produced). In December 2019, Cardi B embarked on her first tour of Africa, performing in Nigeria and Ghana. Her collaboration "[Clout](/wiki/Clout_(Offset_song))" was nominated for a [Grammy Award for Best Rap Performance](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap_Performance). She was the most streamed female rapper of 2019 in the US, according to Spotify. *[Consequence of Sound](/wiki/Consequence_of_Sound)* deemed her "one of the most formidable hip-hop artists of the decade." In March 2020, Cardi B created a reaction video about the [coronavirus pandemic](/wiki/Coronavirus_pandemic). DJ iMarkkeyz, a Brooklyn DJ known for turning memes and online moments into full-length songs, created a track, based on her reaction titled "Coronavirus", which became an [internet meme](/wiki/Internet_meme) and was released to music platforms. Netflix announced the return of *Rhythm + Flow* for 2021.

Cardi B released the single "[WAP](/wiki/WAP_(song))" featuring American rapper [Megan Thee Stallion](/wiki/Megan_Thee_Stallion) on August 7, 2020, as the lead single off her forthcoming second studio album. The song received critical acclaim and was praised for its [sex positive](/wiki/Sex_positive) messages. The [Colin Tilley](/wiki/Colin_Tilley)-directed music video accompanied the song itself, and broke the record for the biggest 24-hour debut for an all-female collaboration on YouTube. She became the only female rap artist to top the Global Spotify chart multiple times. "WAP" debuted at number-one on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) chart, garnering Cardi B her fourth chart-topper in the US, extending her record as the female rapper with the most number-one singles, and also making her the first female rapper to achieve Hot 100 number one singles in two different decades (2010s and 2020s).

With 93 million streaming units, it became the largest first-week streams for a song, breaking the all-time record held by [Ariana Grande](/wiki/Ariana_Grande)'s "[7 Rings](/wiki/7_Rings)". It has spent four weeks atop the Hot 100. The single has also spent multiple weeks at number one in seven other countries, including Australia and the United Kingdom. Neil Shah of *[The Wall Street Journal](/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)* deemed it "a big moment for female rappers" and "a historic sign that women artists are making their mark on hip-hop like never before". "WAP" became the first number one single on the inaugural [*Billboard* Global 200](/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) chart.

It became critics' best song of the year according to a compilation of rankings made by the [BBC](/wiki/BBC), with publications such as *[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website))* and *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* placing it at number one. Cardi B won the [*Billboard* Music Award for Top Rap Female Artist](/wiki/Billboard_Music_Award_for_Top_Rap_Female_Artist) for the third time at the [2020 ceremony](/wiki/2020_Billboard_Music_Awards). In December 2020, Cardi B became the first female rapper to be named [Woman of the Year](/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music#Woman_of_the_Year_Award) at the [*Billboard* Women in Music](/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music) Awards. With her win for "WAP" at the [American Music Awards](/wiki/American_Music_Awards), she became the first artist to win the [American Music Award for Favorite Rap/Hip-Hop Song](/wiki/American_Music_Award_for_Favorite_Rap/Hip-Hop_Song) multiple times, following her win for "Bodak Yellow" in 2018.

On February 7, 2025, Cardi and [Pardison Fontaine](/wiki/Pardison_Fontaine) released "Toot It Up". Cardi's second studio album *[Am I the Drama?](/wiki/Am_I_the_Drama%3F)*, was released on September 19, 2025, over seven years after her debut album. The project was delayed numerous times over multiple years after the release of *Invasion of Privacy*, citing perfectionism and pressure to live up to expectations. Recorded over a six-year period, the album features 23 tracks, including her previous singles "WAP" and "Up".

It was preceded by the singles "[Outside](/wiki/Outside_(Cardi_B_song))" on June 20 and "[Imaginary Playerz](/wiki/Imaginary_Playerz)" on August 15. The former debuted at number ten on the *Billboard* Hot 100 and made Cardi the female rapper with the most top-ten debuts on the chart (3), while the latter samples [Jay-Z](/wiki/Jay-Z)'s 1997 track "Imaginary Players". "[Safe](/wiki/Safe_(Cardi_B_song))", featuring [Kehlani](/wiki/Kehlani), was released as the third single in tandem with the album; it became Cardi's tenth number-one on the *Billboard* [Rhythmic Airplay](/wiki/Rhythmic_Airplay) chart. It debuted atop the *Billboard* 200 with 200,000 album-equivalent units, marking Cardi's second consecutive number-one album and achieving 2025's biggest sales week for an R&B/hip-hop album by a woman. She became the only female rapper in history to have her first two albums debut atop the *Billboard* 200. The record earned the most entries for a female rap album in Hot 100 history (18 songs), and became the female rap album with the most weeks in the top-ten of the *Billboard* 200 in the 2020s decade (eight). It was certified triple platinum by the RIAA, becoming the highest-certified female rap album of the 2020s decade and the fastest female album in history to achieve the feat. It was also the fastest album to be certified double platinum in history. On September 27, Cardi co-headlined the Global Citizen Festival in New York City. On December 13, she headlined the final night of the 2025 Soundstorm Festival in [Riyadh, Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Riyadh,_Saudi_Arabia). On January 31, 2026, Cardi appeared as the musical guest on the [1,000th episode](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_season_51#ep999) of *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*, hosted by [Alexander Skarsgård](/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd). In February, she won three awards at the [NAACP Image Awards](/wiki/57th_NAACP_Image_Awards): [Outstanding Album](/wiki/NAACP_Image_Award_for_Outstanding_Album) for *Am I The Drama?*, Outstanding Hip-Hop/Rap Song for "ErrTime", and further became the first Hip-Hop artist to win [Outstanding Female Artist](/wiki/NAACP_Image_Award_for_Outstanding_Female_Artist) since [Lauryn Hill](/wiki/Lauryn_Hill) in 1999. *Am I The Drama?* is only the third hip-hop album to win Outstanding Album, following Hill's *[The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill](/wiki/The_Miseducation_of_Lauryn_Hill)* (1999) and [Kendrick Lamar](/wiki/Kendrick_Lamar)'s *[Damn](/wiki/Damn_(album))* (2017). In support of *Am I the Drama?*, Cardi B headlined the [Little Miss Drama Tour](/wiki/Little_Miss_Drama_Tour), spanning 35 arena shows across the US and Canada, from February to April 2026. The tour was met with acclaim from critics, who praised her performance, the production, wardrobe, and visual representation. Paula Mejía of *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* described it as an "ambitious spectacle" that successfully translated Cardi's "online-era charisma into a physical arena powerhouse". The Little Miss Drama Tour sold 453,000 tickets and grossed $70 million. Cardi featured on the track "Pocket" off [Kehlani](/wiki/Kehlani)'s [eponymous album](/wiki/Kehlani_(album)), released on April 24, 2026, marking their third collaboration.

## Artistry

### Influences

In *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))*'s "You Should Know" series, Cardi B said the first albums she ever purchased were by American entertainers [Missy Elliott](/wiki/Missy_Elliott) and [Tweet](/wiki/Tweet_(singer)), respectively. She has credited Puerto Rican rapper [Ivy Queen](/wiki/Ivy_Queen) and Jamaican dancehall artist [Spice](/wiki/Spice_(musician)) as influences, as well as [Beyoncé](/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9), [Lady Gaga](/wiki/Lady_Gaga), [Lil' Kim](/wiki/Lil%27_Kim), [Madonna](/wiki/Madonna), and [Selena](/wiki/Selena). She has mentioned [Chicago drill music](/wiki/Drill_music) as an important influence. When asked about the initial direction for her music, Cardi B said in an interview,

> "When I first started rapping [...] I liked certain songs from [Khia](/wiki/Khia) and [Trina](/wiki/Trina), and they [were] fighting songs. I haven't heard fighting songs for a very long time," crediting the two female rappers for her aggressive rap style. She continued, saying "a lot of girls they cannot afford [red bottoms](/wiki/Christian_Louboutin), a lot of girls they cannot afford foreign cars [...] but I know that every girl has beef with a girl [...] I know that every bitch don't like some bitch, and it's like 'that's what I wanna rap about.'"

She credits her experiences growing up in the South Bronx for her success, stating: "I wouldn't be able to rap about the things that I rap about now [if I hadn't grown up there]."

### Musical style

Her debut studio album, *[Invasion of Privacy](/wiki/Invasion_of_Privacy_(album))*, is primarily a [hip hop](/wiki/Hip_hop) record, which comprises elements of [trap](/wiki/Trap_music_(hip_hop)), [Latin music](/wiki/Latin_music), and [R&B](/wiki/Contemporary_R%26B). Her [flow](/wiki/Flow_(rapping)) was described as "acrobatic and nimble" by *[Consequence of Sound](/wiki/Consequence_of_Sound)*, and "aggressive" by *[Newsweek](/wiki/Newsweek)* and [AllMusic](/wiki/AllMusic). [Stereogum](/wiki/Stereogum) called her vocals an "unabashedly loud and sexual fuck-you New York honk" and "a full-bodied New Yawk nasal bleat, the sort of thing that you've heard if someone has ever told you that you stupid for taking too long at swiping your [MetroCard](/wiki/MetroCard)." *Pitchfork* wrote that her vocal performance in "WAP" is in "her classic throaty style", while *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* characterized it as a "staccato bark".

*NME* described her album as "sexually free" and performed with "rapid" flow. [*Pitchfork*](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website)) and *[The Source](/wiki/The_Source_(magazine))* highlighted the album's punchlines as clever and quotable, and *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* called them "classically comedic". In a 2017 *[Complex](/wiki/Complex_(magazine))* article, the editor wrote: "unapologetic does not begin to describe the totally unfiltered and sheer Cardi B-ness of Cardi B's personality. She's a hood chick who's not afraid to be hood no matter the setting ... this is why she resonates with people, and that same energy comes out in her music." She possesses a [New York-Dominican](/wiki/Dominican_Americans#New_York_City) accent.

Cardi B has defended her musical content featuring sexually charged lyrics—like most contemporary female rappers; she stated that the content "seems like that's what people want to hear", since she faced negative reactions after releasing her more emotional song, "[Be Careful](/wiki/Be_Careful_(Cardi_B_song))". She has declared, "[Drill music] is the type of artist I always wanted to be: I like to rap about the streets, and I like to rap about my pussy. I don't give a fuck about it." She has stated that writing and performing songs about her personal life and relationships initially caused her a "weird and uncomfortable" feeling and shyness.

### Grow Good Beauty

In February 2026, Cardi B announced the launch of her haircare brand, Grow Good Beauty, her first venture with the Revolve Group. The venture follows years of the artist sharing homemade hair mask recipes and DIY hair care routines on [social media](/wiki/Social_media). Industry analysts noted that the transition into the beauty space was a "natural evolution" for the rapper, given her long-standing transparency regarding her natural hair journey and her advocacy for hair health within the [Afro-Latino](/wiki/Afro-Latino) community.

According to *[Women's Wear Daily](/wiki/Women%27s_Wear_Daily)* (WWD), the brand aims to provide "accessible luxury" in the textured hair care market, focusing on hydration, growth, and scalp health. The product lineup is formulated to cater to different hair textures, specifically addressing the needs of those with natural and chemically treated hair. Grow Good Beauty debuted on April 15, 2026, releasing a range of six products—two shampoos, two conditioners, a hair mask, and a serum—on its webstore. The range sold out within the first 45 minutes since launch. Financial experts noted that Cardi's significant digital footprint and "authentic connection" to the subject matter positioned the brand to compete with established celebrity-led beauty brands.

### Philanthropy

In 2022, Cardi B donated $100,000 to the elementary school she attended, I.S. 232 in [The Bronx](/wiki/The_Bronx). In the same year, Cardi B pledged to cover the funeral expenses of all 17 victims of the [2022 Bronx apartment fire](/wiki/2022_Bronx_apartment_fire), as well as repatriation costs for a number of the victims who are to be buried in [Gambia](/wiki/Gambia).

## Public image

### Political statements

Cardi B at the [Weho Pride](/wiki/Pride_parade) in [West Hollywood, California](/wiki/West_Hollywood,_California), in 2022

Cardi B identifies as a [feminist](/wiki/Feminist). The rapper has been called "unabashedly, directly political" and often uses social media to advocate for causes she believes in, such as [gun control](/wiki/Gun_control). During the [2016 presidential primaries](/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), she warned her fans of [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump)'s immigration policies and encouraged them to vote for Senator [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders). At the [Grammy Awards](/wiki/60th_Annual_Grammy_Awards) in 2018, she appeared in a video along with [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) to narrate a portion of *[Fire and Fury](/wiki/Fire_and_Fury)*, [Michael Wolff](/wiki/Michael_Wolff_(journalist))'s insider's account of Trump's administration, and stated "Why am I even reading this shit? I can't believe this. I can't believe—this is how he really lives his life?"

Early in 2018, she used her social media to demand transparency on [tax policy](/wiki/Tax_policy), asking for detailed information on how her taxpayer dollars are being spent in New York state and criticizing the maintenance of its streets, prisons, and [public transportation](/wiki/Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority). Also in 2018, she praised President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) for advocating for the [Social Security](/wiki/Social_Security_(United_States)) program and the [New Deal](/wiki/New_Deal) project. She said of Roosevelt, "he helped us get over the [Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression), all while he was in a wheelchair. Like, this man was suffering from [polio](/wiki/Polio) at the time of his presidency, and yet all he was worried about was trying to make America great—make America great again for real. He's the real 'Make America Great Again,' because if it wasn't for him, old people wouldn't even get Social Security." Sanders has praised her for her "leading role" in calling attention to Social Security.

Cardi B endorsed Sanders once again in his [second bid](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders_2020_presidential_campaign) for the presidency in the [2020 United States presidential election](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election), while praising U.S. Representative [Tim Ryan](/wiki/Tim_Ryan_(Ohio_politician)). She stated that one of the reasons for her endorsement is Sanders' long-time involvement in supporting underprivileged minorities and "people getting [Medicare](/wiki/Medicare_(United_States)) because he knows they can't afford it," while [Politico](/wiki/Politico) argued that she "might be one of Bernie's most powerful 2020 allies."

In 2020, Cardi B used her social pages to raise awareness for victims of [police brutality](/wiki/Police_brutality), and has encouraged people to vote for mayors, judges and [district attorneys](/wiki/District_attorney) in local elections. In a conversation with Democratic candidate [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) for *[Elle](/wiki/Elle_(magazine))*, they discussed Medicare, [free college tuition](/wiki/Free_college_tuition), and racial equality. According to a study published by *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*, Cardi B ranked as the fifth most influential celebrity, and fourth among [Generation Z](/wiki/Generation_Z), for the 2020 presidential election.

In late 2023, Cardi B stated she no longer supports Joe Biden for president, saying that she would "not [be] endorsing no fucking presidents no more" as a result of Biden's "spending on wars". In 2024, she endorsed [Kamala Harris](/wiki/Kamala_Harris_2024_presidential_campaign). She endorsed [Jasmine Crockett](/wiki/Jasmine_Crockett) in the [2026 United States Senate election in Texas](/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Texas).

### Fashion

Cardi has a noted affinity for [Christian Louboutin](/wiki/Christian_Louboutin) heels, a running theme in her song "Bodak Yellow". She has also mentioned her affinity for cheap, [fast fashion](/wiki/Fast_fashion) brands stating "I don't care if it cost $20 or $15. If it looks good on me, it looks good on me". During an interview in early 2017, Cardi B spoke on being rejected by some fashion designers. Cardi wore vintage [Thierry Mugler](/wiki/Thierry_Mugler) to the [2019 Grammy Awards](/wiki/2019_Grammy_Awards), with an *[i-D](/wiki/I-D)* article stating that the fashion house's "resurgence onto the fashion scene can almost single-handedly be attributed" to the [Swarovski crystal](/wiki/Swarovski_crystal)-embroidered crinoline sheath gown she wore at the ceremony.

Mugler's collaboration with Cardi B marked the second time in 25 years that the Paris house opened its archives to dress a celebrity, the first being [Beyoncé](/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9) for her 2009 concert tour. [*W*](/wiki/W_(magazine)) magazine credited the "WAP" music video for popularizing the Mugler bodysuit in the mainstream. Cardi has acknowledged Mugler as one of the first designers to "take a major chance on [her]" for their fashion collaborations.

*[Vogue](/wiki/Vogue_(magazine))*, *[The Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)*, *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))*, and *[Vibe](/wiki/Vibe_(magazine))* have referred to her as a [fashion icon](/wiki/Fashion_icon). An article from *Vogue* noted she "is famous for her statement getups—whether she's rocking archival Mugler on the red carpet, or dripping in Chanel while sitting courtside at a basketball game." Her over-the-top manicures, designed by [nail artist](/wiki/Nail_artist) [Jenny Bui](/wiki/Jenny_Bui) and studded with [Swarovski crystals](/wiki/Swarovski_crystals), has become a part of her signature look. Editor [Christian Allaire](/wiki/Christian_Allaire) from the magazine in 2021 commented that her signature "bold" ensembles "create a spectacle" during [fashion weeks](/wiki/Fashion_week).

In 2018, she became the first female rapper in the U.S. to appear on the cover of *Vogue* magazine. Photographed by [Annie Leibovitz](/wiki/Annie_Leibovitz), the cover, one of four for the January 2019 issue that included [Stella McCartney](/wiki/Stella_McCartney), features her in a red and white [Michael Kors](/wiki/Michael_Kors) dress and matching red [Jimmy Choo](/wiki/Jimmy_Choo) shoes, while holding her daughter, Kulture.

In 2019, the [Council of Fashion Designers of America](/wiki/Council_of_Fashion_Designers_of_America) included her on their list of "28 Black Fashion Forces". *Vogue* editor-in-chief [Anna Wintour](/wiki/Anna_Wintour) commended her fashion sense, declaring that she "completely rethought [her] opinion of Cardi B's style" after the 2019 [Met Gala](/wiki/Met_Gala), where the rapper wore a [Thom Browne](/wiki/Thom_Browne)-designed burgundy gown that extends outward in concentric circles for about ten feet and was inspired by the [female form](/wiki/Female_form).

Cardi B became the face of [Balenciaga](/wiki/Balenciaga)'s ad campaign for the winter 2020 season. The campaign includes billboards in several international locations, such as the [Louvre](/wiki/Louvre) museum. *Vogue*'s Brooke Bobb commented, "This is Cardi's first campaign for a luxury fashion house, though she's definitely no stranger to the Parisian style scene", citing her floral printed [Richard Quinn](/wiki/Richard_Quinn_(designer)) ensemble "that literally covered her from head to toe" and her being "a front row fixture" at [high fashion](/wiki/High_fashion) shows, adding, "She and her stylist Kollin Carter have been wildly successful in carving out a much-needed space for Cardi within the fashion industry, and they've cultivated a personal style that is all her own while being inspiring to all".

In 2020, Cardi B became the first female rapper to be awarded by the [FN Achievement Awards](/wiki/Footwear_News) when she won the Style Influencer of the Year award, which was presented to her by [Christian Louboutin](/wiki/Christian_Louboutin). In a press release for the awards show, she was called an "influence just about everything in pop culture—from music, fashion and style to social media, politics and even public service". In 2022, *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* ranked her as the second most-stylish woman in music, behind [Lady Gaga](/wiki/Lady_Gaga), and *[GQ](/wiki/GQ)* considered her "one of fashion's preeminent risk-takers."

## Impact

Cardi B has been referred to as the "[Reigning Queen of Hip Hop](/wiki/Honorific_nicknames_in_popular_music)" by multiple publications, including *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))*, *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*, *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)*, *[Omaha World-Herald](/wiki/Omaha_World-Herald)*, *[Black Enterprise](/wiki/Black_Enterprise)*, *[Newsweek](/wiki/Newsweek)*, and *[The A.V. Club](/wiki/The_A.V._Club)*, and as the "Queen of Rap" by *[NME](/wiki/NME)*, *[Essence](/wiki/Essence_(magazine))*, *[Harper's Bazaar Malaysia](/wiki/Harper%27s_Bazaar)*, *[The Jakarta Post](/wiki/The_Jakarta_Post)*, [Uproxx](/wiki/Uproxx), [iHeartRadio](/wiki/IHeartRadio), [Geo TV](/wiki/Jang_Media_Group), *[Vanity Fair](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine))*, *[Joe](/wiki/Joe_(website))*, *[Boston Herald](/wiki/Boston_Herald)*, [Refinery 29](/wiki/Refinery_29), [France 24](/wiki/France_24), and Nigerian media *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian_(Nigeria))*, [BBC News](/wiki/BBC_News), and *[Daily Trust](/wiki/Daily_Trust)*.

*[Spin](/wiki/Spin_(magazine))* staff credited her for opening "the table to a new generation of pop artists remaking American music in their own image and accents," as Cardi B "recognized that [POC](/wiki/Person_of_color) artists no longer need to pander or soften themselves in order to become household names." *Billboard* editors stated that with "Bodak Yellow"'s commercial success, "she left an indelible mark on the summer of 2017, not only because she rewrote history, but she gave hope to the have nots...". Several publications have credited "I Like It", the first [Latin trap](/wiki/Latin_trap) song to reach number one on the [Hot 100](/wiki/Hot_100), for introducing the "musical movement" to a mainstream, massive audience. *Billboard*'s Carl Lamarre considered "WAP"'s achievements "a clever Trojan horse for the myriad ways Cardi influences the culture with every move she makes." *Billboard* noted that from the late 2010s to the early 2020s, "Cardi kept cranking out hits that either crossed over to pop radio (like "Girls Like You" with Maroon 5) or exploded across hip-hop culture (like "WAP" with Megan Thee Stallion), while also dabbling in Hollywood projects and brand deals".

*[The Wall Street Journal](/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)*'s Neil Shah stated in 2020 that her breakthrough and success influenced "today's female-rap renaissance," while [Genius](/wiki/Genius_(website)) staff credited her for "helping jumpstart a new wave of female hip-hop signings and promotion at labels," and [NPR Music](/wiki/NPR_Music) commented that the "renaissance" of the dynamism of women in rap grew "in enthusiasm and breadth" since Cardi's "first historic run" in 2017. Similarly, [Clover Hope](/wiki/Clover_Hope)'s book *The Motherlode* (2021) stated that *Invasion of Privacy* "jump-started a new era for women rappers in which success felt much more tangible" as Cardi B "multiplied the wealth of talent and resurrected the idea that numerous women who controlled their own stories could dominate rap at once."

*[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* has credited her for "changing a genre that has rarely allowed for more than one female superstar at a time." *[Uproxx](/wiki/Uproxx)* noted Cardi B for promoting up-and-coming female rappers; "[she is] choosing to use her position at the height of stardom to open doors for other women to flourish in hip-hop at a greater level than any since the [Golden Era](/wiki/Golden_age_hip_hop) and '[Ladies First](/wiki/All_Hail_the_Queen)'," considering it "something of a departure from tradition; for the decade previous to Cardi's precipitous come-up, it seemed hip-hop had an unspoken, [Highlander](/wiki/Highlander_(franchise))-esque rule in place regarding women." *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* deemed her a "hip-hop icon", and *[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)* called her "the people's pop culture icon", writing that she "has become one of the most recognisable cultural figures of the past 10 years". In 2024, *Billboard* included Cardi B among the honorable mentions of its "[25 Greatest Pop Stars of the 21st Century](/wiki/Billboard%27s_Greatest_Pop_Stars_of_the_21st_Century)" listicle. The magazine called her one of the "most influential female rappers ever, and one of the most bankable hit-makers across all of hip-hop".

[NPR](/wiki/NPR) defined "Cardi B effect" as "a [branding power](/wiki/Personal_branding) rooted in specific authenticity, created and permeated by rapper Cardi B" and noticed that with her breakthrough, "brands finally started to become hip to [her] effect, noticing the cultural markers outside of the rap world that were proving it wasn't limited to clubs, concerts and radio." Business magazine *[Inc.](/wiki/Inc._(magazine))* stated that her success "shows how social media changed everything we knew about [traditional marketing](/wiki/Promotional_mix) and media", which no longer relies on a "well-thought marketing scheme or millions of dollars in advertising." In 2019, a life-sized sculpture of her was on display at the [Brooklyn Museum](/wiki/Brooklyn_Museum), as part of [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify)'s RapCaviar "Pantheon".

[Bloomberg News](/wiki/Bloomberg_News) reported that her data bill helped to boost Ghana's GDP growth in 2019, after it was part of a concert tour. She inspired the creation of the sitcom *Partners in Rhyme*, executively produced by [MC Lyte](/wiki/MC_Lyte) about a young woman in high school who "aspires to be the next Cardi B." *[P-Valley](/wiki/P-Valley)* creator and executive producer [Katori Hall](/wiki/Katori_Hall) cited her an inspiration for the TV series, and credited her for "helping prepare the public" for its storyline.

Several artists have cited Cardi B's work as an inspiration, including [Rosalía](/wiki/Rosal%C3%ADa), [Olivia Rodrigo](/wiki/Olivia_Rodrigo), [Jazmine Sullivan](/wiki/Jazmine_Sullivan), [Selena Gomez](/wiki/Selena_Gomez), [Blackpink](/wiki/Blackpink), [Spice](/wiki/Spice_(musician)), [Greta Gerwig](/wiki/Greta_Gerwig), [Nathy Peluso](/wiki/Nathy_Peluso), [Rubi Rose](/wiki/Rubi_Rose), [María Becerra](/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Becerra), and [Abigail Asante](/wiki/Abigail_Asante). Cardi B has been credited for supporting and uniting female rappers in the industry, with a writer from *Uproxx* considering her co-sign "the new [Drake](/wiki/Drake_(musician)) effect" for women in hip hop.

## Achievements

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Cardi B](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Cardi_B)

Cardi B is the recipient of numerous accolades, including a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award), eight [*Billboard* Music Awards](/wiki/Billboard_Music_Awards) (including three consecutive [Top Rap Female Artist](/wiki/Billboard_Music_Award_for_Top_Rap_Female_Artist) wins), eight [Guinness World Records](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records), six [American Music Awards](/wiki/American_Music_Awards), four [MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards), six [BET Awards](/wiki/BET_Awards) (including [Album of the Year](/wiki/BET_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year)), and fourteen [BET Hip Hop Awards](/wiki/BET_Hip_Hop_Awards). *Invasion of Privacy*—which made her the first female rapper to win the [Grammy Award for Best Rap Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap_Album) as a solo artist—became the first female rap album [in fifteen years](/wiki/Under_Construction_(Missy_Elliott_album)) to be nominated for a [Grammy Award for Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year).

*[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* included her on their [annual list](/wiki/Time_100) of the 100 most influential people in the world in 2018. She received the [ASCAP](/wiki/ASCAP) award for [Songwriter of the Year](/wiki/American_Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Publishers#Awards) in 2019, becoming the first female rapper to win the award. She received the honor for the second time in 2020, making her the first female songwriter to win the award twice. In 2020, Cardi B became the first female rapper to be named [Woman of the Year](/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music#Woman_of_the_Year_Award) at the [*Billboard* Women in Music](/wiki/Billboard_Women_in_Music) Awards.

Cardi B is the female rapper with the most [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) number one singles (5) and the one with the most total weeks on the top position (16). "I Like It" became the first song led by a female rapper to surpass [a billion streams](/wiki/List_of_most-streamed_songs_on_Spotify) on [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify), making her the first woman in hip hop with multiple billion-streamers on the service, and the female rapper with the second-most, with a total of four so far. With the singles "Taki Taki" and "WAP" she became the only female rapper to top the global Spotify chart multiple times. Since August 2020, "WAP" holds the record of the biggest first-week streams for a song in the United States. *Invasion of Privacy* was the top female rap album of the 2010s, according to the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) decade-end chart. It became the longest-charting album by a female rapper on the *Billboard* 200, and the most-streamed female rap album on Spotify.

Cardi is the first female rapper with at least three [Diamond-certified](/wiki/RIAA_certification#RIAA_Diamond_certifications) songs by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA): "Bodak Yellow", which made her the first female rapper to have a song certified Diamond; "[Girls Like You](/wiki/Girls_Like_You)", which made her the only female rapper to achieve multiple Diamond-certified songs; and "I Like It", a tie for the second-most among women artists. Cardi B has topped twice *[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website))*'s [annual list of best songs of the year](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website)#Pitchfork_awards) (2017 and 2020). *Billboard* staff and *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* ranked her debut album number 13 and 34 on their critics' lists of the best albums of the 2010s respectively, both the highest rank for a female rapper for the decade. Cardi B has become the highest-certified female rapper of all time on the [RIAA's Top Artists (Digital Singles)](/wiki/List_of_highest-certified_music_artists_in_the_United_States) ranking, with 69 million certified units, also being among the highest-certified female artists overall. In October 2022, five years after her major-label debut, she reached 100 million units sold in RIAA certifications, across her album, singles and guest appearances.

In the US, Cardi has achieved three times the best-performing song of the year by a female artist—the first act to do so this century—in 2017, 2018, and 2020. As of 2021, "I Like It" is the most-streamed song by a female rapper in the United Kingdom. In August 2021, "Bodak Yellow" made Cardi B the first female rapper to have two videos on her [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) channel with more than 1 billion views, joining "I Like It", and became the fastest solo female rap song to reach that mark on the platform. She has the most-viewed music videos on YouTube, both as a solo act and feature, among female rappers ("Bodak Yellow"; "Girls Like You"). As of September 2024, she has one music video with over 3 billion views ("Girls Like You"); one video with over 2 billion views ("Taki Taki"); and three videos with over a billion views ("I Like It", "Bodak Yellow", "Girls Like You (Volume 2)") on YouTube. Cardi is the only female rapper ranked on *Billboard*'s Greatest Hot 100 Hits of All Time, with "Girls Like You" at number 30.

Cardi B's sophomore studio album, *[Am I the Drama?](/wiki/Am_I_the_Drama%3F)* (2025), achieved significant commercial and critical success upon its release. The album debuted at number one on the US *[Billboard 200](/wiki/Billboard_200)*, earning 200,000 [album-equivalent units](/wiki/Album-equivalent_units) in its first week. This milestone made her the only female rapper in history to have her first two studio albums debut at the top of the chart. The project also set a record for the most charting songs by a female rapper in a single week on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100), with 18 tracks entering the tally simultaneously.

The album was certified [platinum](/wiki/RIAA_certification) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA) on its day of release, a feat attributed to the pre-release success of the singles "[WAP](/wiki/WAP_(song))" and "[Up](/wiki/Up_(Cardi_B_song))". By February 2026, the album had been certified double platinum, making it the highest-certified female rap album of the 2020s decade thus far.

## Personal life

Cardi B defined herself as a practicing [Catholic](/wiki/Catholic).

In 2016, Cardi B said that when working as a stripper, she used to drug and rob men for their money. Following controversies on the matter, sparked in 2019, she defended herself, saying, "whether or not they were poor choices at the time, I did what I had to do to survive." She explains: "I never glorified the things I brought up in that live [video], I never even put those things in my music because I'm not proud of it and feel a responsibility not to glorify it. I made the choices that I did at the time because I had very limited options." In 2024, Cardi B spoke on the matter again, saying "Why do y'all keep writing 'Cardi drugged, whatever, robbed men,' other crap. So what? I'll do it again bitch. I don't give a fuck, I don't feel bad. I don't feel bad for no niggas. I'll do it the fuck again. And y'all think y'all gagging. I talk about that shit on my album too. I don't give a fuck."

She identifies as [bisexual](/wiki/Bisexual). In connection with the [MeToo movement](/wiki/MeToo_movement), she has discussed being sexually assaulted in the past while working as a stripper.

In a 2018 interview, Cardi B talked about being [Afro-Latina](/wiki/Afro-Latin_Americans) and [Afro-Caribbean](/wiki/Afro-Caribbean):

> We are Caribbean people. [...] Some people want to decide if you're black or not, depending on your skin complexion, because they don't understand Caribbean people or our culture. [...] I don't got to tell you that I'm black. I expect you to know about it. When my father taught me about Caribbean countries, he told me that Europeans took over our lands. That's why we all speak different languages. [...] Just like everybody else, we came over here the same way. I hate when people try to take my roots from me. Because we know that there's African roots inside of us.

Rapper [GloRilla](/wiki/GloRilla) is Cardi's biological first cousin (once removed) due to Glorilla's father being a half-sibling to one of Cardi's parents, but referred to her as her “half-niece”. Glorilla also confirmed they are cousins. Cardi B has also been involved in [a widely publicized feud](/wiki/Nicki_Minaj%E2%80%93Cardi_B_feud) with rapper [Nicki Minaj](/wiki/Nicki_Minaj) since around 2017.

### Relationships

In early 2017, Cardi B began dating fellow rapper [Offset](/wiki/Offset_(rapper)). They became engaged on October 27, 2017. On April 7, 2018, during her second performance on *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*, Cardi B revealed her pregnancy; she was about six months (24 weeks) pregnant at the time. On June 25, 2018, [TMZ](/wiki/TMZ) found a marriage license revealing Cardi B and Offset had actually secretly married in September 2017, one month before the public proposal. She later confirmed this in a social media post.

Cardi B gave birth to a daughter on July 10, 2018. In December 2018, she announced on Instagram that she and Offset had separated, though the pair later reunited. In February 2019, the couple made a public appearance for the Grammys. He accompanied her onstage during her acceptance speech for [Best Rap Album](/wiki/Best_Rap_Album).

In September 2020, it was reported that Cardi B had filed for divorce from Offset, but the following month it was revealed they were back together. In June 2021, Cardi B revealed she was pregnant with her second child, a boy. She gave birth to a son on September 4, 2021. On the fifth anniversary of her marriage, Cardi B announced she was planning her official wedding. In December 2023, Cardi shared via Instagram that she had been "single for a minute," confirming that she and Offset were no longer together. However, as of March 2024, the current status of their marriage became unclear as they were still working together, with Offset having directed a music video for Cardi B. In July 2024, Cardi officially filed for divorce from Offset. She announced her third pregnancy the day after. She gave birth to her third child, a daughter, on September 7, 2024.

In October 2024, Cardi was rumored to be in a relationship with NFL player [Stefon Diggs](/wiki/Stefon_Diggs). On June 1, 2025, she publicly confirmed her relationship with Diggs in an Instagram post with photos with them together. On September 17, 2025, in an interview with [Gayle King](/wiki/Gayle_King), Cardi confirmed she was pregnant with her fourth child, also her first with Diggs. She gave birth to a son in November of that year. Cardi and Diggs split in 2026.

### Legal issues

On October 1, 2018, Cardi B was accused of an alleged assault of two female bartenders. The victims claimed that Cardi B and her entourage "threw bottles and alcohol at them." She denied involvement. She was charged with two [misdemeanors](/wiki/Misdemeanor): assault and reckless endangerment. Cardi B appeared in court for her arraignment on December 7, 2018, after she did not show up for the originally scheduled date due to a scheduling conflict, according to her attorney. On June 21, 2019, a jury indicted Cardi B on 14 charges, including two counts of felony assault with intent to cause serious physical injury, stemming from the incident. She was arraigned on June 25, 2019, and pleaded not guilty on all charges. On September 15, 2022, she pleaded guilty to third-degree assault and second-degree reckless endangerment, resulting in a sentence of 15 days of community service.

On January 24, 2022, Cardi won a million-dollar defamation verdict against YouTuber Latasha Kebe ([Tasha K](/wiki/Tasha_K)) for waging a "malicious campaign" to hurt her reputation by posting false rumors. She stated that the lies had led her to contemplate suicide in 2019. The jury issued a verdict that Cardi B had been defamed and awarded $1.25 million in damages. Further proceedings brought the total fine against Kebe to $3.82 million.

On March 25, 2022, Cardi, her sister Hennessy, and Hennessy's girlfriend won a defamation lawsuit filed against them by three men over an incident at a Suffolk County beach.

In July 2023, Cardi B threw her microphone into the crowd at a [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas) concert of hers after a fan threw a liquid at her. Subsequently, one fan attempted to press charges but the [Clark County](/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada) Police Department following an investigation decided not to move forward with a case.

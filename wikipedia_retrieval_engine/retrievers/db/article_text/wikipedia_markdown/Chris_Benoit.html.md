**Christopher Michael Benoit** ([/bəˈnwɑː/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*bə-NWAH*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); May 21, 1967 – June 24, 2007) was a Canadian [professional wrestler](/wiki/Professional_wrestler) who worked for various [promotions](/wiki/List_of_professional_wrestling_promotions) during his 22-year career. Despite his accomplishments, he is more generally known for [murdering his wife and youngest son](/wiki/Chris_Benoit_double-murder_and_suicide) before [committing suicide](/wiki/Suicide).

Bearing the nicknames **The (Canadian) Crippler** alongside **The Rabid Wolverine** throughout his career, Benoit held 30 [championships](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_championship) between the [World Wrestling Federation/World Wrestling Entertainment](/wiki/World_Wrestling_Federation/World_Wrestling_Entertainment) (WWF/WWE), [World Championship Wrestling](/wiki/World_Championship_Wrestling) (WCW), [Extreme Championship Wrestling](/wiki/Extreme_Championship_Wrestling) (ECW – all United States), [New Japan Pro-Wrestling](/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling) (NJPW – Japan), and [Stampede Wrestling](/wiki/Stampede_Wrestling) (Canada). Benoit was a two-time [world champion](/wiki/World_champion_(professional_wrestling)), having reigned as a one-time [WCW World Heavyweight Champion](/wiki/WCW_World_Heavyweight_Champion) and a one-time [World Heavyweight Champion](/wiki/World_Heavyweight_Championship_(WWE,_2002%E2%80%932013)) in WWE; he was [booked](/wiki/Booker_(professional_wrestling)) to win the [ECW World Championship](/wiki/ECW_World_Championship) at [Vengeance: Night of Champions](/wiki/Vengeance:_Night_of_Champions), which was held the night of his death. Benoit was the twelfth [WWE Triple Crown Champion](/wiki/WWE_Triple_Crown_Champion) and the seventh [WCW Triple Crown Champion](/wiki/WCW_Triple_Crown_Champion), and the second of four men in history to achieve both the WWE and the WCW Triple Crown Championships. He was also the [2004 Royal Rumble](/wiki/2004_Royal_Rumble) winner, joining [Shawn Michaels](/wiki/Shawn_Michaels) and preceding [Edge](/wiki/Edge_(wrestler)) as one of the three men to win the [Royal Rumble match](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_match) as the number one entrant. Benoit headlined multiple [pay-per-views](/wiki/Pay-per-view) for WWF/WWE, including a victory in the World Heavyweight Championship [main event](/wiki/Main_event) of [WrestleMania XX](/wiki/WrestleMania_XX) in March 2004.

In a three-day double-murder and suicide, Benoit murdered [his wife](/wiki/Nancy_Benoit) in their residence on June 22, 2007, and his 7-year-old son the next day, before killing himself on June 24. The incident profoundly shocked and changed the professional wrestling industry and drew intense media criticism regarding brain injuries, substance abuse, and the long-term health of athletes in contact sports. Subsequent research undertaken by the Sports Legacy Institute (now the [Concussion Legacy Foundation](/wiki/Concussion_Legacy_Foundation)) suggested that [depression](/wiki/Depression_(mood)) and advanced [chronic traumatic encephalopathy](/wiki/Chronic_traumatic_encephalopathy) (CTE), a condition of [brain damage](/wiki/Brain_damage), from repeated legitimate strikes to the head that Benoit had sustained throughout his pro-wrestling career were likely contributing factors of the crimes.

Due to his murders, Benoit's legacy in the professional wrestling industry is widely controversial and heavily debated. Benoit has been renowned by many for his exceptional [technical wrestling](/wiki/Technical_wrestling) ability. Prominent combat sports journalist [Dave Meltzer](/wiki/Dave_Meltzer) considers Benoit "one of the top 10, maybe even [in] the top five, all-time greats" in professional wrestling history. Benoit was inducted into the [Stampede Wrestling Hall of Fame](/wiki/Stampede_Wrestling_Hall_of_Fame) in 1995 and the [Wrestling Observer Newsletter Hall of Fame](/wiki/Wrestling_Observer_Newsletter_Hall_of_Fame) in 2003. His WON induction was put to a re-vote in 2008 to determine if Benoit should remain a member of their Hall of Fame. The threshold percentage of votes required to remove Benoit was not met. While arguments have been made by a number of pro-wrestling fans and industry alumni for Benoit to one day enter the [WWE Hall of Fame](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame) on account of his in-ring work, the overwhelming opinion from the majority of industry veterans is that the nature of Benoit's death disqualifies him from a [posthumous induction](/wiki/Posthumous_award).

## Early life

Benoit was born in [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), Quebec, the son of Michael and Margaret Benoit. He grew up in [Edmonton](/wiki/Edmonton), Alberta, from where he was billed throughout the bulk of his career. He had a sister who lived near Edmonton.

During his childhood and early adolescence in Edmonton, Benoit idolized [Tom "Dynamite Kid" Billington](/wiki/Dynamite_Kid) and [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart); at twelve years old, he attended a local wrestling event at which the two performers "stood out above everyone else". Benoit trained to become a professional wrestler in the [Hart family](/wiki/Hart_family) "[Dungeon](/wiki/Hart_Dungeon)", receiving education from family patriarch [Stu Hart](/wiki/Stu_Hart). In-ring, Benoit emulated both Billington and Bret Hart, cultivating a high-risk style and physical appearance more reminiscent of the former (years later, he adopted Hart's own "[Sharpshooter](/wiki/Sharpshooter_(professional_wrestling))" hold as a finishing move).

## Professional wrestling career

### Stampede Wrestling (1985–1989)

Benoit began his career in 1985, in Stu Hart's [Stampede Wrestling](/wiki/Stampede_Wrestling) [promotion](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_promotion). From the beginning, similarities between Benoit and Billington were apparent, as Benoit adopted many of his moves such as the [diving headbutt](/wiki/Diving_headbutt) and the [snap suplex](/wiki/Snap_suplex); the homage was complete with his initial billing as "Dynamite" Chris Benoit. According to Benoit, in his first match, he attempted the diving headbutt before learning how to land correctly, and had the wind knocked out of him; he said he would never do the move again at that point. His debut match was a tag team match on November 22, 1985, in [Calgary, Alberta](/wiki/Calgary,_Alberta), where he teamed with "The Remarkable" Rick Patterson against Butch Moffat and Mike Hammer, which Benoit's team won the match after Benoit pinned Moffat with a [sunset flip](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_aerial_techniques#Sunset_flip). The first title Benoit ever won was the [Stampede British Commonwealth Mid-Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/Stampede_British_Commonwealth_Mid-Heavyweight_Championship) on March 18, 1988, against [Gama Singh](/wiki/Gama_Singh). During his tenure in Stampede, he won four International Tag Team and three more British Commonwealth titles, and had a lengthy [feud](/wiki/Feud_(professional_wrestling)) with [Johnny Smith](/wiki/John_Hindley) that lasted for over a year, which both men traded back-and-forth the British Commonwealth title. In 1989, Stampede closed its doors, and with a recommendation from [Bad News Allen](/wiki/Bad_News_Allen), Benoit departed for [New Japan Pro-Wrestling](/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling).

### New Japan Pro-Wrestling (1986–1999)

Upon arriving to [New Japan Pro-Wrestling](/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling) (NJPW), Benoit spent about a year training in their "New Japan Dojo" with the younger wrestlers to improve his abilities. While in the dojo, he spent months doing strenuous activities like push-ups and floor sweeping before stepping into the ring. He made his Japanese debut in 1986 under his real name. In 1989, he started wearing a mask and assuming the [name](/wiki/Ring_name) The Pegasus Kid. Benoit said numerous times that he originally hated the mask, but it eventually became a part of him. While with NJPW, he came into his own as a performer in matches with luminaries like [Jushin Thunder Liger](/wiki/Jushin_Thunder_Liger), [Shinjiro Otani](/wiki/Shinjiro_Otani), [Black Tiger](/wiki/Eddie_Guerrero), and [El Samurai](/wiki/El_Samurai) in their junior heavyweight division.

In August 1990, he won his first major championship, the [IWGP Junior Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/IWGP_Junior_Heavyweight_Championship), from Jushin Thunder Liger. He eventually lost the title in November 1990 (and in July 1991 in Japan and in November 1991 in Mexico, his [mask](/wiki/Luchas_de_Apuestas)) back to Liger, forcing him to reinvent himself as Wild Pegasus. Benoit spent the next couple years in Japan, winning the [Best of the Super Juniors](/wiki/Best_of_the_Super_Juniors) tournament twice in [1993](/wiki/Best_of_the_Super_Juniors#1993) and [1995](/wiki/Best_of_the_Super_Juniors#1995). He went on to win the [inaugural](/wiki/Super_J-Cup_(1994)) [Super J-Cup tournament](/wiki/Super_J-Cup) in 1994, defeating Black Tiger, [Gedo](/wiki/Gedo_(wrestler)), and [The Great Sasuke](/wiki/The_Great_Sasuke) in the finals. He wrestled outside New Japan occasionally to compete in Mexico and Europe, where he won a few regional championships, including the [UWA Light Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/WWF_Light_Heavyweight_Championship). He held that title for over a year, having many forty-plus minute matches with [Villano III](/wiki/Villano_III).

### World Championship Wrestling (1992–1993)

Benoit first came to [World Championship Wrestling](/wiki/World_Championship_Wrestling) (WCW) in June 1992, teaming up with fellow Canadian wrestler [Biff Wellington](/wiki/Biff_Wellington) for the [NWA World Tag Team Championship](/wiki/NWA_World_Tag_Team_Championship) tournament; they were defeated by [Brian Pillman](/wiki/Brian_Pillman) and Jushin Thunder Liger in the first round at [Clash of the Champions XIX](/wiki/Clash_of_the_Champions_XIX).

He did not return to WCW until January 1993 at *[Clash of the Champions XXII](/wiki/Clash_of_the_Champions_XXII)*, defeating [Brad Armstrong](/wiki/Brad_Armstrong_(wrestler)). A month later, at [SuperBrawl III](/wiki/SuperBrawl_III), he lost to [2 Cold Scorpio](/wiki/2_Cold_Scorpio), getting pinned with only three seconds left in the 20-minute time limit. At the same time, he formed a tag team with [Bobby Eaton](/wiki/Bobby_Eaton). After he and Eaton lost to Scorpio and [Marcus Bagwell](/wiki/Marcus_Bagwell) at [Slamboree](/wiki/Slamboree_(1993)), Benoit headed back to Japan.

### Various promotions (1993–1994)

After WCW, Benoit worked in [Australia](/wiki/Australia), and [CMLL](/wiki/CMLL) in [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico). In early 1994, he worked for NWA New Jersey where he defeated [Jerry Lawler](/wiki/Jerry_Lawler). A month later he fought [Terry Funk](/wiki/Terry_Funk) to a double count out.

### Extreme Championship Wrestling (1994–1995)

In August 1994, Benoit began working with [Extreme Championship Wrestling](/wiki/Extreme_Championship_Wrestling) (ECW) in between tours of Japan. He was booked as a dominant wrestler there, gaining notoriety as the "Crippler" after he put [Rocco Rock](/wiki/Rocco_Rock) out. In his first appearance, Benoit competed in a [one-night eight-man tournament](/wiki/NWA_World_Title_Tournament) for the vacant [NWA World Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/NWA_World_Heavyweight_Championship), losing to [2 Cold Scorpio](/wiki/2_Cold_Scorpio) in the quarter-finals match.

At [November to Remember](/wiki/November_to_Remember_(1994)), Benoit accidentally broke [Sabu](/wiki/Sabu_(wrestler))'s neck within the opening seconds of the match. The injury came when Benoit threw Sabu with the intention that he take a face-first "pancake" [bump](/wiki/Bump_(professional_wrestling)), but Sabu attempted to turn mid-air and take a backdrop bump instead. He did not achieve full rotation and landed almost directly on his neck.

After this match Benoit returned to the locker room and broke down over the possibility that he might have paralysed someone. [Paul Heyman](/wiki/Paul_Heyman), the head booker of ECW at the time, came up with the idea of continuing the "Crippler" moniker for Benoit. From that point until his departure from ECW, he was known as "Crippler Benoit". When he returned to WCW in October 1995, WCW modified his ring name to "Canadian Crippler Chris Benoit". In *The Rise and Fall of ECW* book, Heyman commented that he planned on using Benoit as a dominant heel for quite some time, before putting the company's main title, the [ECW World Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/ECW_World_Heavyweight_Championship), on him to be the long-term champion of the company.

Benoit and [Dean Malenko](/wiki/Dean_Malenko) won the [ECW World Tag Team Championship](/wiki/ECW_World_Tag_Team_Championship) – Benoit's first American title – from Sabu and [The Tazmaniac](/wiki/The_Tazmaniac) in February 1995 at [Return of the Funker](/wiki/Return_of_the_Funker). After winning, they were initiated into the [Triple Threat](/wiki/The_Triple_Threat) [stable](/wiki/List_of_professional_wrestling_terms#S), led by ECW World Heavyweight Champion, [Shane Douglas](/wiki/Shane_Douglas), as Douglas's attempt to recreate the [Four Horsemen](/wiki/Four_Horsemen_(professional_wrestling)), as the three-man contingency held all three of the ECW championships at the time (Malenko also held the [ECW World Television Championship](/wiki/ECW_World_Television_Championship) at the time). The team lost the championship to [The Public Enemy](/wiki/The_Public_Enemy_(professional_wrestling)) that April at [Three Way Dance](/wiki/Three_Way_Dance). Benoit spent some time in ECW feuding with [The Steiner Brothers](/wiki/The_Steiner_Brothers) and rekindling the feud with 2 Cold Scorpio. He was forced to leave ECW after his work visa expired; Heyman was supposed to renew it, but he failed to make it on time, so Benoit left ECW in August 1995 as a matter of job security and the ability to enter the United States. He toured Japan until WCW called.

### World Wrestling Federation (1995)

In June 1995, while under contract with ECW, Benoit worked in three [dark matches](/wiki/Dark_match), losing to [Bob Holly](/wiki/Bob_Holly), [Adam Bomb](/wiki/Bryan_Clark) and [Owen Hart](/wiki/Owen_Hart).

### Return to WCW (1995–2000)

#### The Four Horsemen (1995–1999)

[New Japan Pro-Wrestling](/wiki/New_Japan_Pro-Wrestling) (NJPW) and [World Championship Wrestling](/wiki/World_Championship_Wrestling) (WCW) had a working relationship, and because of their "talent exchange" program, Benoit signed with WCW in late 1995 along with a number of talent working in New Japan to be a part of the [angle](/wiki/List_of_professional_wrestling_terms#A). Like the majority of those who came to WCW in the exchange, he started out in as a member of the [cruiserweight](/wiki/Cruiserweight_(professional_wrestling)) division, having lengthy matches against many of his former rivals in Japan on almost every single broadcast. At the end of 1995, Benoit went back to Japan as a part of the "talent exchange" to wrestle as a representative for New Japan in the [Super J-Cup: 2nd Stage](/wiki/Super_J-Cup#1995), defeating [Lionheart](/wiki/Chris_Jericho) in the quarterfinals (he received a bye to the quarterfinals for his work in 1995, similar to the way he advanced in the 1994 edition) and losing to [Gedo](/wiki/Keiji_Takayama) in the semifinals.

Benoit with a fan during his time in WCW

After impressing higher-ups with his work, he was approached by [Ric Flair](/wiki/Ric_Flair) and the WCW booking staff to become a member of the reformed [Four Horsemen](/wiki/Four_Horsemen_(professional_wrestling)) in 1995, alongside Flair, [Arn Anderson](/wiki/Arn_Anderson), and [Brian Pillman](/wiki/Brian_Pillman); he was introduced by Pillman as a gruff, no-nonsense heel similar to his ECW persona, "The Crippler". He was brought in to add a new dynamic for Anderson and Flair's tormenting of [Hulk Hogan](/wiki/Hulk_Hogan) and [Randy Savage](/wiki/Randy_Savage) in their "Alliance to End Hulkamania", which saw the Horsemen team up with [The Dungeon of Doom](/wiki/The_Dungeon_of_Doom), but that alliance ended with Dungeon leader and WCW [booker](/wiki/List_of_professional_wrestling_terms#B), [Kevin Sullivan](/wiki/Kevin_Sullivan_(wrestler)) feuding with Pillman. When Pillman abruptly left the company for the WWF, Benoit was placed into his ongoing feud with Sullivan. This came to fruition through a dissension between the two in a tag team match with the two reluctantly teaming with each other against [The Public Enemy](/wiki/The_Public_Enemy_(professional_wrestling)), and Benoit being attacked by Sullivan at [Slamboree](/wiki/Slamboree_1996). This led to the two having violent confrontations at [pay-per-views](/wiki/Pay-per-view), which led to Sullivan booking a feud in which Benoit was having an [affair](/wiki/Affair) with Sullivan's real-life wife and [onscreen](/wiki/Kayfabe) [valet](/wiki/List_of_professional_wrestling_terms#V), [Nancy](/wiki/Nancy_Benoit) (also known as Woman). Benoit and Nancy were forced to spend time together to make the affair look real, (hold hands in public, share hotel rooms, etc.).

This onscreen relationship developed into a real-life affair offscreen. As a result, Sullivan and Benoit had a contentious backstage relationship at best, and an undying hatred for each other at worst. Benoit did, however, admit having a certain amount of respect for Sullivan, saying on the DVD *[Hard Knocks: The Chris Benoit Story](/wiki/Hard_Knocks:_The_Chris_Benoit_Story)* that Sullivan never took undue liberties in the ring during their feud, even though he blamed Benoit for breaking up his marriage. This continued for over the course of a year with Sullivan having his [enforcers](/wiki/Enforcer_(professional_wrestling)) apprehend Benoit in a multitude of matches. This culminated in a [retirement match](/wiki/Retirement_match) at the [Bash at the Beach](/wiki/Bash_at_the_Beach_1997), where Benoit defeated Sullivan; this was used to explain Sullivan going to a behind-the-scenes role, where he could focus on his initial job of booking.

Benoit in 1999

In 1998, Benoit had a long feud with [Booker T](/wiki/Booker_T_(wrestler)). They fought over the [WCW World Television Championship](/wiki/WCW_World_Television_Championship) until Booker lost the title to [Fit Finlay](/wiki/Fit_Finlay). Booker won a "Best-of-Seven" series which was held between the two to determine a number one contender. Benoit went up 3 to 1 before Booker caught up, forcing the 7th and final match on [*Monday Nitro*](/wiki/WCW_Monday_Nitro). During the match, [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) interjected himself, interfering on behalf of Benoit in an attempt to get him to join the [New World Order](/wiki/New_World_Order_(professional_wrestling)). Benoit refused to win that way and told the referee what happened, getting himself disqualified. Booker refused that victory, instead opting for an eighth match at the [Great American Bash](/wiki/Great_American_Bash_1998) to see who would fight Finlay later that night. Booker won the final match and went on to beat Finlay for the title. This feud significantly elevated both men's careers as singles competitors, and both remained at the top of the midcard afterward.

In 1999, Benoit teamed with Dean Malenko once again and defeated [Curt Hennig](/wiki/Curt_Hennig) and [Barry Windham](/wiki/Barry_Windham) to win the [WCW World Tag Team Championship](/wiki/WCW_World_Tag_Team_Championship). This led to a reformation of the Four Horsemen with the tag team champions, Anderson, and [Steve "Mongo" McMichael](/wiki/Steve_%22Mongo%22_McMichael). The two hunted after the tag team championship for several months, feuding with teams like [Raven](/wiki/Raven_(wrestler)) and [Perry Saturn](/wiki/Perry_Saturn) or [Billy Kidman](/wiki/Billy_Kidman) and [Rey Mysterio Jr.](/wiki/Rey_Mysterio_Jr.)

#### The Revolution and World Heavyweight Champion (1999–2000)

After a falling out with Anderson and McMichael, Benoit and Malenko left the Horsemen; he won the [WCW United States Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/WCW_United_States_Heavyweight_Championship) before bringing together Malenko, Perry Saturn, and [Shane Douglas](/wiki/Shane_Douglas) to form "[the Revolution](/wiki/Revolution_(WCW))".

The Revolution was a heel stable of younger wrestlers who felt slighted (both kayfabe and legitimate) by WCW management, believing they never gave them the chance to be stars, [pushing](/wiki/Push_(professional_wrestling)) older, more established wrestlers instead, despite their then-current questionable worthiness of their pushes. This led to the Revolution seceding from WCW, and forming their own nation, complete with a flag. This led to some friction being created between Benoit and leader, Douglas, who called into question Benoit's heart in the group, causing Benoit to quit the group, thus turning face, and having his own crusade against the top stars, winning the Television title one more time and the United States title from [Jeff Jarrett](/wiki/Jeff_Jarrett) in a [ladder match](/wiki/Ladder_match). In October 1999 on *Nitro* in [Kansas City, Missouri](/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), Benoit wrestled Bret Hart as a tribute to Bret's brother [Owen Hart](/wiki/Owen_Hart), who had recently died due to an [equipment malfunction](/wiki/Over_the_Edge_(1999)#Owen_Hart_accident). Hart defeated Benoit by submission, and the two received a standing ovation, and an embrace from guest ring announcer, [Harley Race](/wiki/Harley_Race).

Benoit was unhappy working for WCW. One last attempt in January 2000 was made to try to keep him with WCW, by putting the vacant [WCW World Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/WCW_World_Heavyweight_Championship) on him by defeating [Sid Vicious](/wiki/Sid_Eudy) at [Souled Out](/wiki/Souled_Out_2000). However, due to disagreements with management and to protest the promotion of Kevin Sullivan to head booker, Benoit left WCW the next day alongside his friends [Eddie Guerrero](/wiki/Eddie_Guerrero), Dean Malenko, and Perry Saturn, forfeiting his title in the process. WCW then refused to acknowledge Benoit's victory as an official title reign, and Benoit's title reign was not listed in the title lineage at WCW.com. However, the WWF recognized Benoit's title win, and Benoit's title reign is still listed in the title lineage at WWE.com. Benoit spent the next few weeks in Japan before heading to the WWF, who acknowledged his WCW World Heavyweight Championship win and presented him as a former world champion.

### World Wrestling Federation/Entertainment (2000–2007)

#### The Radicalz (2000–2001)

Main article: [The Radicalz](/wiki/The_Radicalz)

Benoit was disqualified from the [2000 King of the Ring](/wiki/King_of_the_Ring_(2000)) for using a chair against [Rikishi](/wiki/Rikishi_(wrestler)).

Benoit joined the [World Wrestling Federation](/wiki/World_Wrestling_Federation) (WWF) near the end of its [Attitude Era](/wiki/Attitude_Era). Along with Guerrero, Saturn and Malenko, Benoit debuted in a stable that became known as [the Radicalz](/wiki/The_Radicalz). After losing their "tryout matches" upon entry, The Radicalz aligned themselves with [WWF Champion](/wiki/WWF_Champion) [Triple H](/wiki/Triple_H) and became a [heel](/wiki/Heel_(professional_wrestling)) [faction](/wiki/Glossary_of_professional_wrestling_terms#Faction). At [WrestleMania 2000](/wiki/WrestleMania_2000) on April 2, Benoit pinned [Chris Jericho](/wiki/Chris_Jericho) in a triple threat match to win [Kurt Angle](/wiki/Kurt_Angle)'s [Intercontinental Championship](/wiki/WWE_Intercontinental_Championship). In Benoit's first WWF pay-per-view main events, he challenged [The Rock](/wiki/Dwayne_Johnson) for the WWF Championship at [Fully Loaded](/wiki/Fully_Loaded_(2000)) on July 23 and in a fatal four-way match at [Unforgiven](/wiki/Unforgiven_(2000)) on September 24. On both occasions Benoit appeared to have won the title, only to have the decision reversed by then-WWF [commissioner](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_authority_figures) [Mick Foley](/wiki/Mick_Foley) due to cheating on Benoit's part. Benoit simultaneously entered into a lengthy feud over the Intercontinental title with Jericho, who he defeated at [Backlash](/wiki/Backlash_(2000)) on April 30, [Judgment Day](/wiki/WWF_Judgment_Day_(2000)) on May 21 and [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam_(2000)) on August 27. The feud culminated in Jericho defeating Benoit in a [ladder match](/wiki/Ladder_match) at [Royal Rumble](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_(2001)) on January 21, 2001. Benoit won the title three times between April 2000 and January 2001.

In early 2001, Benoit broke away from The Radicalz (who had recently reformed three months earlier) and turned face, feuding first with his former stablemates and then with Angle. Benoit lost to Angle at [WrestleMania X-Seven](/wiki/WrestleMania_X-Seven) on April 1, but defeated him in an "Ultimate Submission" match at [Backlash](/wiki/Backlash_(2001)) on April 29. The feud continued after Benoit stole Angle's cherished [Olympic Gold Medal](/wiki/Olympic_Gold_Medal), with Angle defeating Benoit at [Judgment Day](/wiki/WWF_Judgment_Day_(2001)) on May 20 in a [two-out-of-three falls match](/wiki/Two-out-of-three_falls_match) with the help of [Edge and Christian](/wiki/Edge_and_Christian). In response, Benoit teamed up with his former rival Jericho to defeat Edge and Christian in that night's [Tag Team Turmoil match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_tag_team_match_types#Tag_Team_Turmoil) to become the number one contenders to the [WWF Tag Team Championship](/wiki/WWF_Tag_Team_Championship).

The next night on *[Raw Is War](/wiki/Raw_Is_War)*, Benoit and Jericho won the titles from [Stone Cold Steve Austin](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin) and Triple H. On the May 24 episode of *[SmackDown!](/wiki/SmackDown!)*, Benoit suffered a legitimate neck injury in a four-way [TLC match](/wiki/TLC_match). Benoit challenged Austin for the WWF Championship on two occasions, first losing in a manner similar to the [Montreal Screwjob](/wiki/Montreal_Screwjob) in Calgary on the May 28 episode of *Raw is War* and then losing in a close match in Benoit's hometown of Edmonton on the May 31 episode of *SmackDown!*. Despite the neck injury, Benoit continued to wrestle until the [King of the Ring](/wiki/King_of_the_Ring_(2001)) on June 24, where he was pinned by Austin in a triple threat match for the WWF Championship also involving Jericho. Benoit missed the next year due to his neck injury, missing the entire [Invasion storyline](/wiki/The_Invasion_(professional_wrestling)).

#### Championship pursuits and reigns (2002–2003)

Benoit at the *[Tribute to the Troops](/wiki/WWE_Tribute_to_the_Troops)* in [2003](/wiki/WWE_Tribute_to_the_Troops_results#2022)

During the first [WWF draft](/wiki/WWE_brand_extension), Benoit was the third wrestler picked by Vince McMahon to be part of the new [SmackDown!](/wiki/SmackDown_(WWE_brand)) roster, although still on the injured list. However, when Benoit returned, he did so as a member of the [Raw](/wiki/Raw_(WWE_brand)) roster, turning heel by aligning himself with Eddie Guerrero. Benoit and Guerrero lost to the [Dudley Boyz](/wiki/Dudley_Boyz) ([Bubba Ray](/wiki/Bubba_Ray) and [Spike](/wiki/Spike_Dudley)) in an elimination tables match at [Vengeance](/wiki/Vengeance_(2002)) on July 21. The following week on *Raw*, Benoit defeated [Rob Van Dam](/wiki/Rob_Van_Dam) to become Intercontinental Champion for the fourth and final time. Benoit and Guerrero were then moved to SmackDown! during a storyline "open season" on wrestler contracts, with Benoit taking the Intercontinental Championship to *SmackDown!*. Van Dam defeated Benoit at [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam_(2002)) on August 25 and returned the title to *Raw*.

Benoit defeated Kurt Angle at [Unforgiven](/wiki/Unforgiven_(2002)) on September 22. Benoit was then paired with Angle in a [tournament](/wiki/WWE_tournaments#WWE_Tag_Team_Championship_Tournament) to crown the first-ever [WWE Tag Team Champions](/wiki/WWE_Raw_Tag_Team_Championship), defeating [Edge](/wiki/Edge_(wrestler)) and [Rey Mysterio](/wiki/Rey_Mysterio) in the finals at [No Mercy](/wiki/No_Mercy_(2002)) on October 20. Benoit and Angle made their first successful defense against [Los Guerreros](/wiki/Los_Guerreros#World_Wrestling_Entertainment_(2002–2004)) (Eddie and [Chavo](/wiki/Chavo_Guerrero_Jr.)) at [Rebellion](/wiki/Rebellion_(2002)) on October 26. Benoit and Angle lost the WWE Tag Team Championship to Edge and Mysterio on the November 7 episode of *SmackDown!* in a two-out-of-three falls match. At [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(2002)) on November 17, Benoit and Angle were the first team eliminated in a [triple threat elimination match](/wiki/Triple_threat_elimination_match) for the titles against Edge and Mysterio and Los Guerreros. Shortly after, the team split when Angle became the number one contender to the WWE Championship, turning Benoit face. Benoit defeated Eddie Guerrero on December 15 at [Armageddon](/wiki/Armageddon_(2002)).

After Angle won the WWE Championship at Armageddon, Benoit feuded with Angle and his [Team Angle](/wiki/The_World%27s_Greatest_Tag_Team) stablemates [Charlie Haas](/wiki/Charlie_Haas) and [Shelton Benjamin](/wiki/Shelton_Benjamin). On January 19, 2003, at the [Royal Rumble](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_(2003)), Benoit failed to win the title from Angle. At [No Way Out](/wiki/No_Way_Out_(2003)) on February 23, Benoit and [Brock Lesnar](/wiki/Brock_Lesnar) defeated Team Angle in a handicap match. At [WrestleMania XIX](/wiki/WrestleMania_XIX) on March 30, Benoit and [Rhyno](/wiki/Rhyno) failed to win the WWE Tag Team Championship from Team Angle in a triple threat tag team match also involving Los Guerreros. They teamed with [Spanky](/wiki/Brian_Kendrick) in a loss to [John Cena](/wiki/John_Cena) and [The Full Blooded Italians](/wiki/The_Full_Blooded_Italians) at [Judgment Day](/wiki/WWE_Judgment_Day_(2003)) on May 18.

In June, the [WCW United States Championship](/wiki/WCW_United_States_Championship) was reactivated and renamed the WWE United States Championship, and Benoit participated in a tournament for the title. Benoit lost in the finals to Eddie Guerrero at [Vengeance](/wiki/Vengeance_(2003)) on July 27 after Rhyno turned on Benoit. Benoit failed to win the title in a fatal four-way match at [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam_(2003)) on August 24 and defeated [A-Train](/wiki/Matt_Bloom) at [No Mercy](/wiki/No_Mercy_(2003)) on October 19. At [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(2003)) on November 16, Benoit eliminated Lesnar as part of a [Survivor Series elimination tag team match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_tag_team_match_types#Elimination_tag_team_elimination) between Team Angle against Team Lesnar. As a result, Benoit challenged Lesnar for the WWE Championship on the December 4 episode of *SmackDown!*, but lost after passing out to Lesnar's debuting [*Brock Lock*](/wiki/Boston_crab#Over-the-shoulder_single_leg_Boston_crab) [submission hold](/wiki/Submission_hold).

#### World Heavyweight Champion (2004–2005)

On the January 1, 2004 episode of *SmackDown!*, Benoit and Cena defeated FBI in a handicap match to qualify for the [Royal Rumble match](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_match); SmackDown! General Manager [Paul Heyman](/wiki/Paul_Heyman) named Benoit as the number one entrant. At the [Royal Rumble](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_(2004)) on January 25, Benoit won the Royal Rumble match by last eliminating [Big Show](/wiki/Big_Show), earning a world title shot at [WrestleMania XX](/wiki/WrestleMania_XX) and becoming the second person to win the Royal Rumble as the number one entrant after [Shawn Michaels](/wiki/Shawn_Michaels). Instead of challenging for SmackDown!'s WWE Championship, Benoit exploited a "loophole" in the rules and moved to the Raw brand to challenge [World Heavyweight Champion](/wiki/World_Heavyweight_Championship_(WWE,_2002%E2%80%932013)) [Triple H](/wiki/Triple_H) at WrestleMania. Michaels, whose [Last Man Standing match](/wiki/Last_Man_Standing_match) for the title against Triple H at Royal Rumble ended in a draw, thought that he deserved to be in the main event. Before Benoit could sign the contract putting himself in the main event, Michaels [superkicked](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_attacks#superkick) him and signed his name on the contract, resulting in a triple threat match between Michaels, Benoit, and the champion Triple H.

Benoit with his [real-life](/wiki/Real_life) best friend [Eddie Guerrero](/wiki/Eddie_Guerrero), celebrating their respective world championship victories at [WrestleMania XX](/wiki/WrestleMania_XX)

At WrestleMania XX on March 14, Benoit won the World Heavyweight Championship after Triple H submitted to his signature submission move, the *Crippler Crossface*, marking the first time the main event of a WrestleMania ended in submission. After the match, Benoit celebrated his win with then-reigning WWE Champion Eddie Guerrero. At [Backlash](/wiki/Backlash_(2004)) on April 18, in Benoit's hometown of Edmonton, he successfully defended the title in a rematch after Michaels submitted to Benoit's Sharpshooter. The next night in Calgary on *Raw*, Benoit and Edge won the World Tag Team Championship from [Batista](/wiki/Dave_Bautista) and [Ric Flair](/wiki/Ric_Flair), making Benoit a double champion. Benoit simultaneously feuded with [Kane](/wiki/Kane_(wrestler)), and Benoit and Edge lost the titles to [La Résistance](/wiki/La_R%C3%A9sistance_(professional_wrestling)) on the May 31 episode of *Raw*. On June 13, at [Bad Blood](/wiki/WWE_Bad_Blood_(2004)), Benoit and Edge failed to regain the World Tag Team Championship (winning by disqualification when Kane interfered), while Benoit successfully defended the World Heavyweight Championship against Kane later that night. Benoit again defeated Kane to retain the title on the June 28 episode of *Raw*.

At [Vengeance](/wiki/Vengeance_(2004)) on July 11, Benoit successfully defended the title against Triple H. At [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam_(2004)) on August 15, Benoit lost the World Heavyweight Championship to [Randy Orton](/wiki/Randy_Orton), ending his reign at 154 days. Benoit failed to regain the title the next night on *Raw*. Benoit and [William Regal](/wiki/William_Regal) defeated Batista and Flair at [Unforgiven](/wiki/Unforgiven_(2004)) on September 12.

Benoit then feuded with Edge, who had turned into an arrogant and conceited heel. At [Taboo Tuesday](/wiki/Taboo_Tuesday_(2004)) on October 19, Benoit, Edge and Michaels were all put into a poll to see who would face Triple H for the World Heavyweight Championship that night. Michaels received the most votes, and Edge and Benoit were forced to face World Tag Team Champions La Résistance in the same night. Despite Edge deserting Benoit during the match, they regained the World Tag Team Championship, before losing the titles in a rematch on the November 1 episode of *Raw*. Benoit was a part of Orton's team at [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(2004)) on November 14, while Edge was on Triple H's team. Despite Benoit being pinned after a *Pedigree* from Triple H, Orton's team won. At [New Year's Revolution](/wiki/New_Year%27s_Revolution_(2005)) on January 9, 2005, Benoit and Edge competed in the [Elimination Chamber](/wiki/Elimination_Chamber) match for the vacant World Heavyweight Championship, but both lost. Benoit entered the [Royal Rumble](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_(2005)) as the second entrant on January 30, lasting longer than any competitor before being eliminated by Flair. Benoit competed in the [Money in the Bank ladder match](/wiki/Money_in_the_Bank_ladder_match) at [WrestleMania 21](/wiki/WrestleMania_21) on April 3, which was won by Edge after he knocked Benoit off the ladder by smashing his arm with a chair. Their feud ended in a Last Man Standing match at [Backlash](/wiki/Backlash_(2005)) on May 1, which Edge won with a brick shot to the back of Benoit's head.

#### United States Champion (2005–2007)

On June 9, Benoit was drafted to the SmackDown! brand after being the first man selected by *SmackDown!* in the [2005 Draft Lottery](/wiki/2005_WWE_draft_lottery) and participated in an ECW-style revolution against the *SmackDown!* heels. Benoit appeared at [ECW One Night Stand](/wiki/ECW_One_Night_Stand_(2005)) on June 12, defeating Eddie Guerrero.

Benoit as [WWE United States Champion](/wiki/WWE_United_States_Championship) in September 2005

On July 24, at [The Great American Bash](/wiki/The_Great_American_Bash_(2005)), Benoit failed to win the United States Championship from [Orlando Jordan](/wiki/Orlando_Jordan), but won it in a rematch at [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam_(2005)) on August 21 in 25 seconds. Benoit then won three consecutive matches against Jordan in less than a minute. At [No Mercy](/wiki/No_Mercy_(2005)) on October 9, Benoit successfully defended the title against [Booker T](/wiki/Booker_T_(wrestler)), [Christian](/wiki/Christian_Cage) and Jordan in a fatal-four way match. However, Booker T and his wife, [Sharmell](/wiki/Sharmell), cheated Benoit out of the title on the October 21 episode of *SmackDown!*.

On November 13, Eddie Guerrero was found dead in his hotel room. The following night, *Raw* held a Guerrero tribute show hosted by both Raw and SmackDown! wrestlers. Benoit was devastated at Guerrero's death and was very emotional during a series of video testimonials, breaking down on camera. The same week on *SmackDown!* (taped on the same night as *Raw*), Benoit defeated Triple H in a tribute match to Guerrero, after which they and Dean Malenko assembled in the ring and pointed to the sky in salute of Guerrero.

After controversy surrounding a United States Championship match against Booker T on the November 25 episode of *SmackDown!*, [Theodore Long](/wiki/Theodore_Long) set up a "Best of Seven" series between the two. Booker T won three times in a row at [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(2005)) on November 27, the November 29 *SmackDown! Special* and the December 9 episode of *SmackDown!*, due largely to Sharmell's interference, and Benoit faced elimination in the series. Benoit won the fourth match to stay alive at [Armageddon](/wiki/Armageddon_(2005)) on December 18, but after the match, Booker T suffered a legitimate groin injury, and Randy Orton was chosen as a stand-in. Benoit defeated Orton twice by disqualification on the December 30 and January 6, 2006 episodes of *SmackDown!*. However, in the seventh and final match, Orton defeated Benoit with the help of Booker T, Sharmell and Jordan, awarding Booker T the United States Championship. At [No Way Out](/wiki/No_Way_Out_(2006)) on February 19, Benoit won the title after making Booker T submit to the *Crippler Crossface*, ending the feud.

The next week on *SmackDown!*, Benoit ([kayfabe](/wiki/Kayfabe)) broke [John "Bradshaw" Layfield](/wiki/John_%22Bradshaw%22_Layfield) (JBL)'s hand (JBL actually needed surgery to remove a cyst). At [WrestleMania 22](/wiki/WrestleMania_22) on April 2, Benoit lost the title to JBL, who used an illegal cradle to win. Benoit used his rematch clause two weeks later in a steel cage match on *SmackDown!*, but JBL again won with illegal tactics. Benoit entered the [King of the Ring](/wiki/King_of_the_Ring_2006) tournament, but lost to [Finlay](/wiki/Dave_Finlay) in the first round after Finlay struck Benoit's neck with a chair and delivered a *Celtic Cross*. At [Judgment Day](/wiki/WWE_Judgment_Day_(2006)) on May 21, Benoit defeated Finlay. On the following episode of *SmackDown!*, [Mark Henry](/wiki/Mark_Henry) brutalized Benoit during their match, giving him (kayfabe) back and rib injuries and causing him to bleed from his mouth. Benoit then took a sabbatical to heal nagging shoulder injuries.

On October 8, Benoit made his surprise return at [No Mercy](/wiki/No_Mercy_(2006)), defeating William Regal. Later that week, Benoit defeated [Mr. Kennedy](/wiki/Mr._Kennedy) to win his fifth and final United States Championship. Benoit engaged in a feud with [Chavo](/wiki/Chavo_Guerrero_Jr.) and [Vickie Guerrero](/wiki/Vickie_Guerrero), seeking answers from them for their rash behaviour towards [Rey Mysterio](/wiki/Rey_Mysterio), but was avoided by the two and eventually assaulted. Benoit successfully defended the title against Chavo at [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(2006)) on November 26, [Armageddon](/wiki/Armageddon_(2006)) on December 17, and in a no disqualification match on the January 19, 2007 episode of *SmackDown!*. Benoit's next feud was with [Montel Vontavious Porter](/wiki/Montel_Vontavious_Porter) (MVP), teaming with the [Hardy Boys](/wiki/Hardy_Boyz) ([Jeff](/wiki/Jeff_Hardy) and [Matt](/wiki/Matt_Hardy)) to defeat MVP and [MNM](/wiki/MNM_(professional_wrestling)) ([Joey Mercury](/wiki/Joey_Mercury) and [Johnny Nitro](/wiki/John_Morrison_(wrestler))) at [No Way Out](/wiki/No_Way_Out_(2007)) on February 18. Benoit successfully defended the title against MVP at [WrestleMania 23](/wiki/WrestleMania_23) on April 1 and [Backlash](/wiki/Backlash_(2007)) on April 29, before losing it to MVP in a two-out-of-three falls match at [Judgment Day](/wiki/WWE_Judgment_Day_(2007)) on May 20.

#### ECW (2007)

On the June 11 episode of *Raw*, Benoit was drafted to the [ECW](/wiki/ECW_(WWE_brand)) brand as part of the [2007 WWE draft](/wiki/2007_WWE_draft) after losing to [ECW World Champion](/wiki/ECW_World_Champion) [Bobby Lashley](/wiki/Bobby_Lashley). In his debut on the ECW brand, Benoit teamed with [CM Punk](/wiki/CM_Punk) to defeat [Elijah Burke](/wiki/Elijah_Burke) and [Marcus Cor Von](/wiki/Marcus_Cor_Von). On the June 19 episode of *[ECW](/wiki/WWE_ECW)*, Benoit wrestled his final match, defeating Burke in a match to determine who would compete for the vacated [ECW World Championship](/wiki/ECW_World_Championship) at [Vengeance](/wiki/Vengeance:_Night_of_Champions) on June 24. Since Lashley was drafted to Raw, he had vacated the title.

The diving headbutt (seen here at [WrestleMania 23](/wiki/WrestleMania_23) in April 2007) was blamed as one of the primary causes of Benoit's death.

Benoit missed the weekend [house shows](/wiki/House_show), informing close colleagues that his wife and son were vomiting blood due to [food poisoning](/wiki/Food_poisoning). When he failed to show up for Vengeance, viewers were informed that he was unable to compete due to a "family emergency" and he was replaced in the title match by [Johnny Nitro](/wiki/John_Morrison_(wrestler)), who defeated Punk to become ECW World Champion. The crowd spent the majority of the match chanting for Benoit. It would be revealed in the following days that Benoit [had murdered his wife Nancy and son Daniel before committing suicide](/wiki/Chris_Benoit_double-murder_and_suicide).

WWE executive [Stephanie McMahon](/wiki/Stephanie_McMahon) later indicated that Benoit would have defeated [CM Punk](/wiki/CM_Punk) for the ECW World Championship had he been present for Vengeance. Professional wrestler and MMA fighter [Bob Sapp](/wiki/Bob_Sapp), whom WWE had tried to sign up before a contract dispute with [K-1](/wiki/K-1) rendered it impossible, reported he would have been put into an oncoming [angle](/wiki/Angle_(professional_wrestling)) with Benoit in case he would have been able to debut.

## Professional wrestling style

Benoit, known for his high-impact technical style, included a wide array of submission holds in his move-set and used a [crossface](/wiki/Crossface), dubbed the *Crippler Crossface*, and a [sharpshooter](/wiki/Sharpshooter_(professional_wrestling)) as finishers. He also used a [diving headbutt](/wiki/Diving_headbutt), which saw him leap off the top rope and land head first on his opponent; this move was partially blamed for the head trauma that caused Benoit to commit his crimes. Another of Benoit's trademark moves was three rolling [German suplexes](/wiki/Suplex_(professional_wrestling)). This move would later be mimicked by multiple other wrestlers, including [Brock Lesnar](/wiki/Brock_Lesnar) who uses it as *Suplex City*.

Former WWE rival [Kurt Angle](/wiki/Kurt_Angle) said in a 2017 interview that "he has got to be in the top three of all time."

## Personal life

Benoit spoke both English and French fluently. He married twice, and had two children (David and Megan) with his first wife, Martina. By 1997, that marriage had broken down, and Benoit was living with [Nancy Sullivan](/wiki/Nancy_Benoit), the wife of the WCW booker and frequent opponent [Kevin Sullivan](/wiki/Kevin_Sullivan_(wrestler)). On February 25, 2000, Chris and Nancy's son Daniel was born; on November 23, 2000, Chris and Nancy married. It was Nancy's third marriage. In 2003, Nancy filed for divorce from Benoit, citing the marriage as "irrevocably broken" and alleging "cruel treatment". She claimed that he would break and throw furniture around. She later dropped the suit as well as the [restraining order](/wiki/Restraining_order) she had filed.

Benoit was good friends with fellow pro-wrestlers [Eddie Guerrero](/wiki/Eddie_Guerrero) and [Dean Malenko](/wiki/Dean_Malenko); the trio travelled from promotion to promotion together putting on matches, eventually being dubbed the "Three Amigos" by commentators. According to Benoit, the [Crippler Crossface](/wiki/Crippler_Crossface) was borrowed from Malenko and eventually caught on as Benoit's finishing hold. After [Guerrero died in November 2005](/wiki/Eddie_Guerrero#Death), Benoit acquired a [diary](/wiki/Diary) in which he wrote [grief-stricken](/wiki/Grief) personal entries to a deceased Guerrero as a [coping mechanism](/wiki/Coping_mechanism).

Benoit's lost tooth, his top-right lateral incisor, was commonly misattributed to training or an accident early on in his wrestling career. It actually resulted from an accident involving his pet [Rottweiler](/wiki/Rottweiler): one day while playing with the dog, the animal's head struck Benoit's chin, and his tooth "popped out".

## Death

Main article: [Chris Benoit double-murder and suicide](/wiki/Chris_Benoit_double-murder_and_suicide)

On June 25, 2007, police entered Benoit's home in [Fayetteville, Georgia](/wiki/Fayetteville,_Georgia), when WWE, Benoit's employers, requested a "welfare check" after Benoit missed weekend events without notice, leading to concerns. The officers discovered the bodies of Benoit, his wife [Nancy](/wiki/Nancy_Benoit), and their 7-year-old son Daniel at around 2:30 p.m. [EDT](/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone). Upon investigating, no additional suspects were sought by authorities. It was determined that Benoit had committed the murders. Over a three-day period, Benoit had killed his wife and son before committing [suicide](/wiki/Suicide). His wife was bound before the killing. Benoit's son was drugged with [Xanax](/wiki/Xanax) and likely unconscious before Benoit strangled him. Benoit then committed suicide by hanging himself on his lat pulldown machine.

WWE cancelled the scheduled three-hour-long live *[Raw](/wiki/WWE_Raw)* show on June 25 and replaced the broadcast version with a three-hour tribute to Benoit's life and career, featuring his past matches, segments from the *Hard Knocks: The Chris Benoit Story* DVD, and comments from wrestlers and announcers. Once the details of Benoit's actions became apparent, WWE made the decision to [remove nearly all mentions of Chris Benoit from their website, future broadcasts, and all publications](/wiki/Blacklisting).

[Toxicology](/wiki/Toxicology) reports released on July 17, 2007, revealed that at their time of death, Nancy had three different drugs in her system: Xanax, [hydrocodone](/wiki/Hydrocodone), and [hydromorphone](/wiki/Hydromorphone), all of which were found at the therapeutic rather than toxic levels. Daniel was found to have Xanax in his system, which led the chief medical examiner to believe that he was sedated before he was murdered. Benoit was found to have Xanax, hydrocodone, and an elevated level of [testosterone](/wiki/Testosterone_(medication)), caused by a synthetic form of the hormone, in his system. The chief medical examiner attributed the testosterone level to Benoit possibly being treated for a deficiency caused by previous [steroid](/wiki/Steroid) abuse or testicular insufficiency. There was no indication that anything in Benoit's body contributed to his violent behaviour that led to the murder-suicide, concluding that there was no "[roid-rage](/wiki/Anabolic_steroid#Aggression_and_hypomania)" involved. Prior to the murder-suicide, Benoit had illegally been given medications not in compliance with [WWE's Talent Wellness Program](/wiki/WWE#Drug_testing_and_wellness_program) in February 2006, including [nandrolone](/wiki/Nandrolone), an [anabolic steroid](/wiki/Anabolic_steroid), and [anastrozole](/wiki/Anastrozole), a [breast cancer](/wiki/Breast_cancer) medication which is used by [bodybuilders](/wiki/Bodybuilders) for its powerful [antiestrogenic](/wiki/Antiestrogen) effects. During the investigation into [steroid](/wiki/Steroid) abuse, it was revealed that other wrestlers had also been given steroids.

After the double-murder suicide, neuroscientist and retired professional wrestler [Christopher Nowinski](/wiki/Christopher_Nowinski) contacted Michael Benoit, Chris's father, suggesting that years of [trauma](/wiki/Traumatic_brain_injury) to his son's brain may have led to his actions. Tests were conducted on Benoit's brain by Julian Bailes, the head of neurosurgery at [West Virginia University](/wiki/West_Virginia_University), and results showed that "Benoit's brain was so severely damaged it resembled the brain of an 85-year-old [Alzheimer's](/wiki/Alzheimer%27s) patient." He was reported to have had an advanced form of dementia, similar to the brains of four retired NFL players who had multiple concussions, sank into depression, and harmed themselves or others. Bailes and his colleagues concluded that repeated concussions can lead to [dementia](/wiki/Dementia), which can contribute to severe behavioural problems. Benoit's father suggests that brain damage may have been the leading cause.

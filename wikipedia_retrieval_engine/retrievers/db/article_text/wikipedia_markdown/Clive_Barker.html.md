This article is about the artist, author and filmmaker. For other uses, see [Clive Barker (disambiguation)](/wiki/Clive_Barker_(disambiguation)).

**Clive Barker** (born 5 October 1952) is a British writer, filmmaker, and visual artist. He came to prominence in the 1980s with a series of short stories collectively named the *[Books of Blood](/wiki/Books_of_Blood)*, which established him as a leading horror author. His work has been adapted into films, notably the *[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser_(franchise))* series (the first installment of which he also wrote and directed) and the *[Candyman](/wiki/Candyman_(film_series))* series.

Barker's paintings and illustrations have been shown in galleries in the United States, and have appeared in his books. He has also created characters and series for comic books, and some of his more popular horror stories have been featured in ongoing comics series.

## Early life

Barker was born in [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool) on 5 October 1952. His mother, Joan Ruby (née Revill), was a painter and school welfare officer; his father, Leonard Barker, worked as the personnel director for an industrial relations firm. He was educated at [Dovedale Primary School](/wiki/Dovedale_Primary_School) and [Quarry Bank High School](/wiki/Calderstones_School) in Liverpool before joining the [University of Liverpool](/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), where he studied English and philosophy. At the age of three, he witnessed the infamous death of French skydiver [Léo Valentin](/wiki/L%C3%A9o_Valentin), who plummeted to the ground during a performance at an air show in Liverpool. He would later allude to Valentin in many of his stories.

## Theatrical work

Barker's involvement in live theatre began while still in school with productions of *Voodoo* and *Inferno* in 1967. He collaborated on six plays with *Theatre of the Imagination* in 1974 and two more that he was the sole writer of, *A Clowns' Sodom* and *Day of the Dog,* for *The Mute Pantomime Theatre* in 1976 and 1977.

Barker co-founded the avant-garde theatrical troupe The Dog Company in 1978 with former schoolmates and up-and-coming actors, many of whom would go on to become key collaborators in his film work; [Doug Bradley](/wiki/Doug_Bradley), his long-time friend and former classmate at [Quarry Bank High School](/wiki/Calderstones_School) in Liverpool, took on the now-iconic role of [Pinhead](/wiki/Pinhead_(Hellraiser)) in the *[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser_(franchise))* series while Peter Atkins wrote the scripts for the first three *Hellraiser* sequels. Over the next five years Barker wrote nine plays, often serving as director, including some of his best-known stage productions, *The History of The Devil*, *Frankenstein in Love*, and *The Secret Life of Cartoons*.

From 1982 to 1983, he wrote *Crazyface*, *Subtle Bodies*, and *Colossus* for the Cockpit Youth Theatre.

His theatrical work came to a close as he shifted focus to writing the *[Books of Blood](/wiki/Books_of_Blood)*.

## Writing career

Barker is an author of horror and [fantasy](/wiki/Fantasy_fiction), although he has said that he thinks of his writing less and less as horror. He began writing early in his career, mostly in the form of short stories (collected in *[Books of Blood](/wiki/Books_of_Blood)* 1–6) and the [Faustian](/wiki/Faustian) novel *[The Damnation Game](/wiki/The_Damnation_Game_(novel))* (1985). Later he moved toward modern-day fantasy and [urban fantasy](/wiki/Urban_fantasy) with horror elements in *[Weaveworld](/wiki/Weaveworld)* (1987), *[The Great and Secret Show](/wiki/The_Great_and_Secret_Show)* (1989), the world-spanning *[Imajica](/wiki/Imajica)* (1991), and *[Sacrament](/wiki/Sacrament_(novel))* (1996).

When *Books of Blood* was first published in the United States in paperback, [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King) was quoted on the book covers: "I have seen the future of horror and his name is Clive Barker." As influences on his writing, Barker lists [Herman Melville](/wiki/Herman_Melville), [Edgar Allan Poe](/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe), [Ray Bradbury](/wiki/Ray_Bradbury), [William S. Burroughs](/wiki/William_S._Burroughs), [William Blake](/wiki/William_Blake), and [Jean Cocteau](/wiki/Jean_Cocteau), among others.

He is the writer of the best-selling *[Abarat](/wiki/Abarat)* series.

In early 2024, he announced he would stop attending conventions and public events so he could focus more on his writing, as he was working on the manuscripts for 31 different projects, some closer to completion than others.

## Personal life

Barker stated on *[Loveline](/wiki/Loveline)* in 1996 that he had several relationships with older women during his teenage years, but realised he was gay when he was around 18 or 19 years old. He dated John Gregson from 1975 to 1986, and was later in a relationship from 1996 to 2009 with photographer David Armstrong, who was described as his husband in the introduction to *[Coldheart Canyon](/wiki/Coldheart_Canyon)*.

During his early years as a writer, Barker occasionally worked as an [escort](/wiki/Escort_agency) when his writing did not provide sufficient income. He has been open about his experiences with [sadomasochism](/wiki/Sadomasochism), calling himself a "six" on its "sliding scale".

In 2003, Barker received the Davidson/Valentini Award at the [15th GLAAD Media Awards](/wiki/15th_GLAAD_Media_Awards).

Barker is critical of organised religion, but has said that the Bible influences his work and spirituality. In 2017, he clarified on [Facebook](/wiki/Facebook) that he did not identify as a Christian.

Barker said in a December 2008 online interview (published in March 2009) that he had throat [polyps](/wiki/Polyp_(medicine)) which were so severe that a doctor told him he was taking in only 10% of the air he was supposed to. He has had two surgical procedures to remove them and believes his voice has improved as a result. He said he did not have cancer and has given up cigars.

In 2012, Barker fell into a coma for several days after contracting [toxic shock syndrome](/wiki/Toxic_shock_syndrome), triggered by a visit to a dentist where a spillage of poisonous bacteria entered his bloodstream and almost killed him. Realising he might have just a short time to live, he decided to put his personal concerns about the world and society into the novel *Deep Hill*, which he thought could be his final book.

As of 2015, Barker is a member of the board of advisers for the Hollywood Horror Museum.

## Film work

Barker wrote the screenplays for *[Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_(1985_film))* (1985) and *[Rawhead Rex](/wiki/Rawhead_Rex_(film))* (1986), both directed by [George Pavlou](/wiki/George_Pavlou). Displeased by how his material was handled, he moved to directing with *[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser)* (1987), based on his novella *[The Hellbound Heart](/wiki/The_Hellbound_Heart)*. After his film *[Nightbreed](/wiki/Nightbreed)* (1990) flopped, Barker returned to write and direct *[Lord of Illusions](/wiki/Lord_of_Illusions)* (1995). The short story "[The Forbidden](/wiki/The_Forbidden_(short_story))", from Barker's *Books of Blood*, provided the basis for the 1992 film *[Candyman](/wiki/Candyman_(1992_film))* and its three sequels. He had been working on a series of film adaptations of his *[The Abarat Quintet](/wiki/The_Books_of_Abarat)* books under [The Walt Disney Company](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_(division))'s management, but due to creative differences, the project was cancelled.

He served as an executive producer for the 1998 film *[Gods and Monsters](/wiki/Gods_and_Monsters_(film))*, a semi-fictional tale of *[Frankenstein](/wiki/Frankenstein_(1931_film))* director [James Whale](/wiki/James_Whale)'s later years, which won an Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay. Barker said of his interest in the project: "Whale was gay, I'm gay; Whale was English, I'm English…Whale made some horror movies, and I've made some horror movies. It seemed as if I should be helping to tell this story." Barker also provided the foreword on the published shooting script.

In 2005, Barker and horror film producer [Jorge Saralegui](/wiki/Jorge_Saralegui) created the film production company Midnight Picture Show with the intent of producing two horror films per year.

In October 2006, Barker announced through his website that he will be writing the script to a forthcoming remake of the original *Hellraiser* film. He was developing a film based on his *[Tortured Souls](/wiki/Tortured_Souls)* line of toys from [McFarlane Toys](/wiki/McFarlane_Toys). In 2020, Barker regained control of the *Hellraiser* franchise, and served as executive producer on a [2022 reboot](/wiki/Hellraiser_(2022_film)) film for the streaming service [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu).

## Television work

In May 2015, [*Variety*](/wiki/Variety_(magazine)) reported that Clive Barker was developing a television series adaptation of various [creepypastas](/wiki/Creepypasta) in partnership with [Warner Brothers](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures), to be called *Clive Barker's Creepypastas*, a feature arc based on *[Slender Man](/wiki/Slender_Man)* and *[Ben Drowned](/wiki/Ben_Drowned)*. Barker was involved in a streaming service [film adaptation](/wiki/Books_of_Blood_(film)) of *The Books of Blood* in 2020, and is developing a [*Nightbreed* television series](/wiki/Nightbreed_(TV_series)) directed by [Michael Dougherty](/wiki/Michael_Dougherty) and written by [Josh Stolberg](/wiki/Josh_Stolberg) for [SyFy](/wiki/SyFy). In April 2020, [HBO](/wiki/HBO) was announced to be developing a *Hellraiser* television series that would serve as "an elevated continuation and expansion" of its mythology with [Mark Verheiden](/wiki/Mark_Verheiden) and [Michael Dougherty](/wiki/Michael_Dougherty) writing the series and [David Gordon Green](/wiki/David_Gordon_Green) directing several episodes. Verheiden, Dougherty and Green will also be executive producing the series with [Danny McBride](/wiki/Danny_McBride), [Jody Hill](/wiki/Jody_Hill), [Brandon James](/wiki/Brandon_James) and [Roy Lee](/wiki/Roy_Lee) of [Vertigo Entertainment](/wiki/Vertigo_Entertainment).

## Visual art

Barker is a prolific visual artist, often illustrating his own books. His paintings have been seen first on the covers of his official fan club magazine, *Dread*, published by [Fantaco](/wiki/Fantaco) in the early 1990s; on the covers of the collections of his plays, *Incarnations* (1995) and *Forms of Heaven* (1996); and on the second printing of the original British publications of his *Books of Blood* series. Barker also provided the artwork for his young adult novel *[The Thief of Always](/wiki/The_Thief_of_Always)* and for the *Abarat* series. His artwork has been exhibited at Bert Green Fine Art in Los Angeles and Chicago, at the Bess Cutler Gallery in New York and La Luz De Jesus in Los Angeles. Many of his sketches and paintings can be found in the collection *[Clive Barker, Illustrator](/wiki/Clive_Barker,_Illustrator)*, published in 1990 by Arcane/Eclipse Books, and in *Visions of Heaven and Hell*, published in 2005 by Rizzoli Books.

He worked on the horror video game *[Clive Barker's Undying](/wiki/Clive_Barker%27s_Undying)*, providing the voice for the character Ambrose. *Undying* was developed by [DreamWorks Interactive](/wiki/DreamWorks_Interactive) and released in 2001. He worked on *[Clive Barker's Jericho](/wiki/Clive_Barker%27s_Jericho)* for [Codemasters](/wiki/Codemasters), which was released in late 2007.

Barker created [Halloween](/wiki/Halloween) costume designs for Disguise Costumes.

Around 150 art works by Barker were used in the set of the Academy of the Unseen Arts for the Netflix TV series [*Chilling Adventures of Sabrina*](/wiki/Chilling_Adventures_of_Sabrina_(TV_series)).

## Comic books

Barker published his [Razorline](/wiki/Razorline) imprint via [Marvel Comics](/wiki/Marvel_Comics) in 1993.

Barker horror adaptations and spin-offs in comics include the Marvel/[Epic Comics](/wiki/Epic_Comics) series *[Hellraiser](/wiki/Hellraiser_(comics))*, *[Nightbreed](/wiki/Nightbreed_(comics))*, *[Pinhead](/wiki/Pinhead_(comics))*, *The Harrowers*, *Book of the Damned*, and *Jihad*; [Eclipse Books](/wiki/Eclipse_Comics)' series and [graphic novels](/wiki/Graphic_novels) *Tapping The Vein*, *Dread*, *Son of Celluloid*, *Revelations*, *The Life of Death*, *Rawhead Rex* and *The Yattering and Jack*, and [Dark Horse Comics](/wiki/Dark_Horse_Comics)' *Primal*, among others. Barker served as a consultant and wrote issues of the *Hellraiser* anthology comic book.

In 2005, IDW published a three-issue adaptation of Barker's children's fantasy novel *[The Thief of Always](/wiki/The_Thief_of_Always_(comics))*, written and painted by Kris Oprisko and Gabriel Hernandez. IDW is publishing a 12 issue adaptation of Barker's novel *[The Great and Secret Show](/wiki/The_Great_and_Secret_Show_(comics))*.

In December 2007, Chris Ryall and Clive Barker announced an upcoming collaboration of an original comic book series, *Torakator*, to be published by IDW.

In 2008, Barker authored a foreword for the first volume of the *DEMONICSEX* comic series by Chuck Conner and Sean Platter.

In October 2009, IDW published *Seduth*, co-written by Barker. The work was released with three variant covers.

In 2011, [Boom! Studios](/wiki/Boom_studios) began publishing an original *Hellraiser* comic book series.

In 2013, Boom! Studios announced *Next Testament*, the first original story by Barker to be published in comic book format.

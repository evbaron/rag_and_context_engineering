**Andhra Pradesh**, retrospectively referred to as **United Andhra Pradesh** or **Undivided Andhra Pradesh**, was a state in [India](/wiki/India) formed by [States Reorganisation Act, 1956](/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act,_1956) with [Hyderabad](/wiki/Hyderabad) as its capital and was reorganised by [Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Reorganisation_Act,_2014). The state was made up of three distinct cultural regions of [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana), [Rayalaseema](/wiki/Rayalaseema), and [Coastal Andhra](/wiki/Coastal_Andhra). Before the 1956 reorganisation, Telangana had been part of [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State_(1948%E2%80%931956)), whereas Rayalaseema and Coastal Andhra had been part of [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State), formerly a part of [Madras Presidency](/wiki/Madras_Presidency) of [British India](/wiki/British_Raj).

## Creation of United Andhra Pradesh

[Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) (yellow), which merged with [Hyderabad state](/wiki/Hyderabad_State_(1948%E2%80%931956)) (white) to form the State of Andhra Pradesh in 1956

Map of [Southern India](/wiki/Southern_India) (1953–1956) before the [States Reorganisation Act](/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act) of 1956

To gain an independent state based on linguistic identity, and to protect the interests of the [Telugu people](/wiki/Telugu_people) of [Madras State](/wiki/Madras_State), [Potti Sreeramulu](/wiki/Potti_Sreeramulu) fasted to death in 1952. As Madras became a bone of contention, in 1949 a JVP committee report stated: "Andhra Province could be formed provided the Andhras give up their claim on the city of Madras (now Chennai)". After Potti Sreeramulu's death, the Telugu-speaking area of [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) was carved out of Madras State on 30 November 1953, with [Kurnool](/wiki/Kurnool) as its capital city. On the basis of the [gentlemen's agreement](/wiki/Gentlemen%27s_Agreement_of_1956) of 1 November 1956, the [States Reorganisation Act](/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act) formed [Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh) by merging Andhra State with the Telugu-speaking areas of the then-existing [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State_(1948%E2%80%931956)). [Hyderabad](/wiki/Hyderabad) was made the capital of the new state. The [Marathi-speaking areas](/wiki/Marathwada) of Hyderabad State merged with [Bombay State](/wiki/Bombay_State) which later bifurcated in [Gujarat](/wiki/Gujarat) and [Maharashtra](/wiki/Maharashtra) and the [Kannada-speaking areas](/wiki/Kalyana-Karnataka) were merged with [Mysore State](/wiki/Mysore_State) which was later renamed as [Karnataka](/wiki/Karnataka).

In February 2014, the [Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Reorganisation_Act,_2014) bill was passed by the [Parliament of India](/wiki/Parliament_of_India) for the formation of the [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana) state comprising [ten districts](/wiki/List_of_districts_in_Telangana). [Hyderabad](/wiki/Hyderabad) will remain as a joint capital for not exceeding ten years. The new state of Telangana came into existence on 2 June 2014 after approval from the [President of India](/wiki/President_of_India). Number of petitions questioning the validity of Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014 have long been pending for the verdict since April 2014 before the [supreme court](/wiki/Supreme_court_of_India) constitutional bench.

The Visalandhra, Vishalandhra or Vishala Andhra was a movement in post-independence India for a united state for all Telugu speakers, a Greater Andhra (Telugu: విశాలాంధ్ర Viśālāndhra). This movement was led by the Communist Party of India under the banner of Andhra Mahasabha with a demand to merge all the Telugu-speaking areas into one state.  (The Communist Party of India demanded the formation of similar linguistic states across India.) The movement succeeded and a separate state of Andhra Pradesh was formed by merging Telugu-speaking areas of Hyderabad State (Telangana) with Andhra State on 1 November 1956 as part of the States Reorganisation Act. (Andhra State had been previously carved out of Madras State on 1 October 1953.) However, on 2 June 2014, Telangana State separated again from Andhra Pradesh and the Vishalandhra movement came to an end. The residual Andhra Pradesh now has approximately the same borders but lost Bhadrachalam Town,Nuguru Taluk: Now known as the Venkatapuram and Wazeedu areas which are initially from East Godavari district of Andhra Pradesh

### History

To gain an independent state based on linguistic identity and to protect the interests of the Telugu-speaking people of [Madras State](/wiki/Madras_State), [Potti Sreeramulu](/wiki/Potti_Sreeramulu) fasted to death in 1952. As the city of Madras became a bone of contention, in 1949 a committee with [Jawaharlal Nehru](/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), [Vallabhbhai Patel](/wiki/Vallabhbhai_Patel), and [Pattabhi Sitaramayya](/wiki/Bhogaraju_Pattabhi_Sitaramayya) was constituted. The committee recommended that Andhra State could be formed provided the Andhras gave up their claim on the city of Madras (now [Chennai](/wiki/Chennai)). After Potti Sreeramulu's death, the Telugu-speaking area of [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) was carved out of [Madras State](/wiki/Madras_State) on 1 October 1953, with [Kurnool](/wiki/Kurnool) as its capital city. [Tanguturi Prakasam](/wiki/Tanguturi_Prakasam) became the [first chief minister](/wiki/Chief_minister_(India)). Based on the [Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956](/wiki/Gentlemen%27s_Agreement_of_1956),and as per the request of Hyderabad state leaders,the [States Reorganisation Act](/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act,_1956) created Andhra Pradesh by merging the neighbouring Telugu-speaking areas of the [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State) with Hyderabad as the capital on 1 November 1956.

The [Indian National Congress](/wiki/Indian_National_Congress) (INC) ruled the state from 1956 to 1982. [Neelam Sanjiva Reddy](/wiki/Neelam_Sanjiva_Reddy) became the first [chief minister](/wiki/List_of_chief_ministers_of_Andhra_Pradesh). Among other chief ministers, [P. V. Narasimha Rao](/wiki/P._V._Narasimha_Rao) is known for implementing land reforms and land ceiling acts and securing a reservation for lower castes in politics. [Nagarjuna Sagar Dam](/wiki/Nagarjuna_Sagar_Dam), completed in 1967, and [Srisailam Dam](/wiki/Srisailam_Dam), completed in 1981, are some of the irrigation projects that helped increase the production of paddy in the state.

In 1983, the [Telugu Desam Party](/wiki/Telugu_Desam_Party) (TDP) won the state elections, and [N. T. Rama Rao](/wiki/N._T._Rama_Rao) became the chief minister of the state for the first time after launching his party just nine months earlier. This broke the long-time single-party monopoly enjoyed by the INC. He transformed the sub-district administration by forming mandals in place of earlier taluks, removing hereditary village heads, and appointing non-hereditary village revenue assistants. The [1989 elections](/wiki/1989_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election) ended the rule of Rao, with the INC returning to power with [Marri Chenna Reddy](/wiki/Marri_Chenna_Reddy) at the helm. In [1994](/wiki/1994_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election), Andhra Pradesh gave a mandate to the Telugu Desam Party again, and Rao became the chief minister again. [Nara Chandrababu Naidu](/wiki/N._Chandrababu_Naidu), Rao's son-in-law, came to power in 1995 with the backing of a majority of the [MLAs](/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_(India)). The Telugu Desam Party won both the [assembly](/wiki/1999_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election) and [Lok Sabha](/wiki/Lok_Sabha) [elections](/wiki/1999_Indian_general_election) in 1999 under the leadership of Chandrababu Naidu. Thus, Naidu held the record for the longest-serving chief minister (1995–2004) of the united Andhra Pradesh. He introduced [e-governance](/wiki/E-governance) by launching *[e-Seva](/wiki/Mee_Seva_(Andhra_Pradesh))* centres in 2001 for paperless and speedy delivery of government services. He is credited with transforming Hyderabad into an IT hub by providing incentives for tech companies to set up centres.

In [2004](/wiki/2004_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election), Congress returned to power with a new chief ministerial face, [YS Rajashekara Reddy](/wiki/Y._S._Rajasekhara_Reddy), better known as YSR. The main emphasis during Reddy's tenure was on social welfare schemes such as free electricity for farmers, health insurance, tuition fee reimbursement for the poor, and the national rural employment guarantee scheme. He took over the free emergency ambulance service initiated by a corporation and ran it as a government project. INC won the [2009 elections](/wiki/2009_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election) under the leadership of YSR in April. He was elected chief minister again but was killed in a [helicopter crash](/wiki/2009_Andhra_Pradesh_Chief_Minister_helicopter_crash) that occurred in September 2009. He was succeeded by Congressmen [Konijeti Rosaiah](/wiki/Konijeti_Rosaiah) and [Nallari Kiran Kumar Reddy](/wiki/Nallari_Kiran_Kumar_Reddy); the latter resigned over the impending division of the state to form [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana).

During its 58 years as a unified state, the state weathered separatist [movements from Telangana](/wiki/Telangana_movement) (1969) and [Andhra](/wiki/1972_Jai_Andhra_movement) (1972) successfully. A new party called [Telangana Rashtra Samithi](/wiki/Bharat_Rashtra_Samithi), formed in April 2001 by [Kalvakuntla Chandrashekar Rao](/wiki/K._Chandrashekar_Rao) (KCR), reignited the Telangana movement. A joint action committee formed with political parties, government employees, and the general public spearheaded the agitation. When KCR's health deteriorated due to his fast-unto-death undertaking, the central government decided to initiate the process of forming an independent Telangana in December 2009. This triggered the [Samaikyandhra movement](/wiki/Samaikyandhra_Movement) to keep the state united. The [Srikrishna committee](/wiki/Srikrishna_Committee) was formed to give recommendations on how to deal with the situation. It gave its report in December 2010. The agitations continued for nearly 5 years, with the Telangana side harping on the marginalisation of food culture, language, and unequal economic development and the Samaikyandhra movement focusing on the shared culture, language, customs, and historical unity of Telugu-speaking regions. The [Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Reorganisation_Act,_2014) bill was passed by the [parliament of India](/wiki/Parliament_of_India) for the formation of the Telangana state, comprising [ten districts](/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Telangana), despite opposition by the state legislature. The bill included the provision to retain [Hyderabad](/wiki/Hyderabad) as the capital for up to ten years and the provision to ensure access to educational institutions for the same period. The bill received the assent of the president and was published in the gazette on 1 March 2014. The new state of Telangana came into existence on 2 June 2014 after approval from the [president of India](/wiki/President_of_India), with the residual state continuing as Andhra Pradesh. The present form of Andhra Pradesh is the same as that of [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State), except for [Bhadrachalam](/wiki/Bhadrachalam) town, which continues in Telangana. A number of petitions questioning the validity of the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act have been pending before the [Supreme Court](/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India) constitutional bench since April 2014.

In the final elections held in the unified state in [2014](/wiki/2014_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election), the TDP got a mandate in its favour, defeating its nearest rival, the [YSR Congress Party](/wiki/YSR_Congress_Party), a breakaway faction of the Congress founded by [Y. S. Jagan Mohan Reddy](/wiki/Y._S._Jagan_Mohan_Reddy), son of former Chief Minister Y. S. Rajasekhara Reddy. [N. Chandrababu Naidu](/wiki/N._Chandrababu_Naidu), the chief of the TDP, became the chief minister on 8 June 2014. In 2017, [the government of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Government_of_Andhra_Pradesh) began operating from its new greenfield capital, [Amaravati](/wiki/Amaravati), for which 33,000 acres were acquired from farmers through an innovative land pooling scheme. Interstate issues with Telangana relating to the division of assets of public sector institutions and organisations of the united state and the division of river waters are not yet resolved.

## List of chief ministers

On 1 November 1956, [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State_(1948%E2%80%931956)) ceased to exist; its [Gulbarga](/wiki/Gulbarga_division) and [Aurangabad divisions](/wiki/Aurangabad_division) were merged into [Mysore State](/wiki/Mysore_State) and [Bombay State](/wiki/Bombay_State) respectively. Its remaining [Telugu](/wiki/Telugu_language)-speaking portion, [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana), was merged with [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) to form the new state of [United Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/United_Andhra_Pradesh). Sanjiva Reddy was the first chief minister.

[Neelam Sanjiva Reddy](/wiki/Neelam_Sanjiva_Reddy) of [Indian National Congress](/wiki/Indian_National_Congress) was the first chief minister of United Andhra Pradesh, [N. Chandrababu Naidu](/wiki/N._Chandrababu_Naidu) of [Telugu Desam Party](/wiki/Telugu_Desam_Party) was the longest served chief minister and Bhaskar Rao was the shortest serving both belonging to TDP. [Kiran Kumar Reddy](/wiki/Kiran_Kumar_Reddy) of the [Indian National Congress](/wiki/Indian_National_Congress) was the last chief minister of United Andhra Pradesh.

## Creation of Telangana

See also: [Telangana movement](/wiki/Telangana_movement); [Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Reorganisation_Act,_2014); and [Samaikyandhra Movement](/wiki/Samaikyandhra_Movement)

After decades of protests and agitations, the central government, under the [United Progressive Alliance](/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance), decided to bifurcate the existing Andhra Pradesh state and on 2 June 2014, the Union Cabinet unilaterally cleared the bill for the creation of Telangana. Lasting for almost 5 decades, it was one of the longest lasting movements for statehood in South India. On 18 February 2014, the [Lok Sabha](/wiki/Lok_Sabha) passed the bill with a [voice vote](/wiki/Voice_vote). Subsequently, the bill was passed by the [Rajya Sabha](/wiki/Rajya_Sabha) two days later, on 20 February. As per the bill, Hyderabad would be the capital of Telangana, while the city would also remain the capital of the residual state of Andhra Pradesh for no more than ten years. Hyderabad was the de jure joint capital. On 2 June 2014, [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana) was created with Hyderabad as its capital

The [Telangana movement](/wiki/Telangana_movement) refers to a movement for the creation of a state, Telangana, from the pre-existing state of Andhra Pradesh in India. The new state corresponds to the Telugu-speaking portions of the erstwhile princely state of Hyderabad.

# Andy Roddick career statistics

This is a list of the main career statistics of retired professional American tennis player, **[Andy Roddick](/wiki/Andy_Roddick)**. Throughout his career, Roddick won thirty-two [ATP](/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals) singles titles including one [grand slam](/wiki/Grand_slam_(tennis)) singles title and five [ATP Masters 1000](/wiki/ATP_Masters_1000) singles titles. He was also the runner-up at the [Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/Wimbledon_Championships) in [2004](/wiki/2004_Wimbledon_Championships), [2005](/wiki/2005_Wimbledon_Championships) and [2009](/wiki/2009_Wimbledon_Championships) and the [US Open](/wiki/US_Open_(tennis)) in [2006](/wiki/2006_US_Open_(tennis)), losing on all four occasions to [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer). Roddick was also a four-time semifinalist at the [Australian Open](/wiki/Australian_Open) and a three-time semifinalist at the year-ending [ATP World Tour Finals](/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_Finals). On November 3, 2003, Roddick became the [World No. 1](/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players) for the first time in his career.

## Career achievements

Roddick won thirty-two singles titles throughout his career including one [grand slam](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)) singles title at the [2003 US Open](/wiki/2003_US_Open_(tennis)).

Roddick reached his first career Grand Slam singles quarterfinal at the [2001 US Open](/wiki/2001_US_Open_(tennis)), where he lost to fourth-seeded Australian and eventual champion [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt) in a five-set thriller 7–6, 3–6, 4–6, 6–3, 4–6. A year later, he reached his first Masters Series singles final at the [2002 Rogers Cup](/wiki/2002_Canada_Masters), losing in straight sets to Argentine [Guillermo Cañas](/wiki/Guillermo_Ca%C3%B1as). The following year, Roddick reached his first grand slam semifinal at the [2003 Australian Open](/wiki/2003_Australian_Open), where he lost to thirty-first seed [Rainer Schüttler](/wiki/Rainer_Sch%C3%BCttler) in four sets, 5–7, 6–2, 3–6, 3–6. In August, after beating [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) for the first time in 5 meetings, Roddick would advance to the final of the [2003 Rogers Cup](/wiki/2003_Canada_Masters). Playing an Argentine in the Rogers Cup final for the second consecutive year, he would defeat [David Nalbandian](/wiki/David_Nalbandian) in straight sets to win his first [Big title](/wiki/List_of_ATP_Big_Titles_singles_champions) in singles at the Rogers Cup. The following week, Roddick would win his first five matches at the [2003 Cincinnati Masters](/wiki/2003_Western_%26_Southern_Financial_Group_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles) to reach his third Masters Series final where he would face his childhood friend, [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish). After nearly 2+1⁄2 hours, Roddick would defeat Fish 4–6, 7–6(7-3), 7-6(7-4) his second consecutive Masters Series title. Two weeks later, Roddick rallied from two sets, and a match point, down to defeat Nalbandian in five sets to reach his first Grand Slam singles final at the [2003 US Open](/wiki/2003_US_Open_(tennis)). Roddick defeated No. 4-seed Spaniard [Juan Carlos Ferrero](/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero) in the final, 6–3, 7–6, 6–3, to win his first and only Grand Slam singles title of his career (he would make four more grand slam finals, but lost all four to [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer). By virtue of his Canada Masters, Cincinnati Masters, and US Open titles in the same season, Roddick became just the 2nd man in the Open Era, joining [Patrick Rafter](/wiki/Patrick_Rafter), to complete the [Summer Slam](/wiki/List_of_ATP_Big_Titles_singles_champions#Calendar_sweeps) ([Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) would join them in 2013). Roddick's six Tour titles throughout the year allowed him to qualify for the year-ending [2003 ATP World Tour Finals](/wiki/2003_Tennis_Masters_Cup) for the first time in his career. He advanced to the semifinals of the event after victories over [Guillermo Coria](/wiki/Guillermo_Coria) and [Carlos Moyá](/wiki/Carlos_Moy%C3%A1) in the round robin stage but lost in straight sets to then world No. 2 Roger Federer. Despite the loss, Roddick finished the year as the [Year-end No. 1](/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players#Year-end_No._1_players) for the first (and only) time in his career, becoming the first American player to finish as Year-end No. 1 since [Andre Agassi](/wiki/Andre_Agassi) in 1999.

In July 2004, Roddick reached his first [Wimbledon](/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon) final but lost in four sets to then World No. 1, Roger Federer. He reached the final of the event again the following year but once again lost to Federer, this time in straight sets. The following year, Roddick reached his fourth grand slam singles final but once again lost to Federer, this time in the final of the [US Open](/wiki/2006_US_Open_(tennis)). In 2007, Roddick reached the semifinals of the year-ending [ATP World Tour Finals](/wiki/2007_Tennis_Masters_Cup) for the third and final time in his career, losing in straight sets to Spaniard, [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer).

Roddick enjoyed a resurgent year in 2009, during which he reached the semifinals of the [Australian Open](/wiki/2009_Australian_Open) for the fourth and final time in his career and the fourth round of the [French Open](/wiki/2009_French_Open) for the first and only time in his career. The highlight of Roddick's year came at the [2009 Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2009_Wimbledon_Championships) where he reached his third final at the event and fifth and final grand slam singles final. There, he lost to Federer 7–5, 6–7(6-8), 6–7(5-7), 6–3, 14–16 in 4 hours 16 minutes. On July 22, 2012, Roddick won his thirty-second and final career singles title at the [Atlanta Tennis Championships](/wiki/2012_Atlanta_Tennis_Championships), defeating [Gilles Müller](/wiki/Gilles_M%C3%BCller) of [Luxembourg](/wiki/Luxembourg) in the final in three sets; Roddick won his first career singles title at the same event eleven years prior.

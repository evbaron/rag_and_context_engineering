**Michael John Keane** (born May 29, 1967) is a [Canadian](/wiki/Canadians) former professional [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) [winger](/wiki/Winger_(ice_hockey)). Undrafted, Keane played over 1,100 games in the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) from 1988 until 2004. He then played five seasons for his hometown [Manitoba Moose](/wiki/Manitoba_Moose) of the [American Hockey League](/wiki/American_Hockey_League) until he retired in 2010. Keane is a three-time [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup) champion, having won with the [Montreal Canadiens](/wiki/Montreal_Canadiens) in [1993](/wiki/1993_Stanley_Cup_Playoffs), [Colorado Avalanche](/wiki/Colorado_Avalanche) in [1996](/wiki/1996_Stanley_Cup_Playoffs), and the [Dallas Stars](/wiki/Dallas_Stars) in [1999](/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_Playoffs). On September 3, 2013, the [Winnipeg Jets](/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) announced the hiring of Keane as Assistant of Player Development.

## Playing career

Keane began his [junior hockey](/wiki/Junior_hockey) career in the Junior A [Manitoba Junior Hockey League](/wiki/Manitoba_Junior_Hockey_League) (MJHL) with the [Winnipeg South Blues](/wiki/Winnipeg_South_Blues). He then joined the [major junior](/wiki/Junior_hockey#Major_junior) ranks in the [Western Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Hockey_League) (WHL) with the [Moose Jaw Warriors](/wiki/Moose_Jaw_Warriors).

Undrafted out of junior hockey, Keane signed as a [free agent](/wiki/Free_agent) on September 25, 1985, with the [Montreal Canadiens](/wiki/Montreal_Canadiens) and started his NHL career in [1988](/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_NHL_season). He helped the Canadiens to a [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup) championship in [1993](/wiki/1993_Stanley_Cup_Playoffs), his fifth NHL season. He spent eight seasons in Montreal and was [captain](/wiki/Captain_(hockey)) of the team from April 1995 to December 1995. After [Kirk Muller](/wiki/Kirk_Muller) was traded near the end of the [1994–95 season](/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Montreal_Canadiens_season), Keane was named as the 24th captain of the Montreal Canadiens. Keane was subject to media scrutiny after speaking to Mathias Brunet of [*La Presse*](/wiki/La_Presse_(Canadian_newspaper)) (a French language newspaper). Keane declared that he had no intention of learning French. He drew controversy in the French media after saying he did not believe it was necessary to speak French, since players on the team spoke predominantly English.

During the [1995–96 NHL season](/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_NHL_season), Keane was traded along with [Patrick Roy](/wiki/Patrick_Roy) to the [Colorado Avalanche](/wiki/Colorado_Avalanche) in exchange for [Jocelyn Thibault](/wiki/Jocelyn_Thibault), [Andrei Kovalenko](/wiki/Andrei_Kovalenko), and [Martin Ručinský](/wiki/Martin_Ru%C4%8Dinsk%C3%BD). He won his second Stanley Cup that season, helping the Avalanche defeat the [Florida Panthers](/wiki/Florida_Panthers) in four games in the [Finals](/wiki/1996_Stanley_Cup_Finals). Keane spent another season with the Avalanche, before becoming a free agent in the summer of 1997. He signed with the [New York Rangers](/wiki/New_York_Rangers), where he played for half a season before the Rangers traded Keane, [Brian Skrudland](/wiki/Brian_Skrudland), and the Rangers' 1998 6th round draft choice ([Pavel Patera](/wiki/Pavel_Patera)) to the [Dallas Stars](/wiki/Dallas_Stars) in exchange for [Todd Harvey](/wiki/Todd_Harvey), [Bob Errey](/wiki/Bob_Errey), and the Stars' 1998 4th round draft choice ([Boyd Kane](/wiki/Boyd_Kane)). Keane played in Dallas until [2000–01](/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_NHL_season), helping the Stars win the Stanley Cup in [1999](/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) and returning to the finals the next year. He started the [2001–02 NHL season](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_NHL_season) with the [St. Louis Blues](/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) but finished that season back with Colorado. Keane stayed in Colorado until he left for the [Vancouver Canucks](/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) at the start of the [2003–04 NHL season](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_NHL_season).

After the [2004–05 lockout](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout), Keane signed with his hometown team, the [Manitoba Moose](/wiki/Manitoba_Moose), for the [2005–06 AHL season](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_AHL_season). Keane played five seasons for the Moose. He was named captain in 2005 and remained Manitoba's captain until July 20, 2010, when his contract was not renewed. Keane was also captain of the AHL All-Star Team Canada in 2007. Keane's number 12 jersey was retired February 12, 2011, when the Manitoba Moose took on the [San Antonio Rampage](/wiki/San_Antonio_Rampage) at [Bell MTS Place](/wiki/Bell_MTS_Place). Keane's number was the first number retired in Manitoba Moose history.

## International play

Keane competed for [Team Canada](/wiki/Canada_men%27s_national_junior_ice_hockey_team) at the [1987 World Junior Championships](/wiki/1987_World_Junior_Ice_Hockey_Championships) in [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia). While playing for the gold medal against the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_ice_hockey_team), both teams were disqualified as a result of the infamous [Punch-up in Piestany](/wiki/Punch-up_in_Piestany).

# Literary and Philosophical Society of Newcastle upon Tyne

## Infobox

- **Location:** Tyne and Wear, England, UK
- **Established:** 1793

The **Literary and Philosophical Society of Newcastle upon Tyne** (or the *Lit & Phil* as it is popularly known) is a historical library in [Newcastle upon Tyne](/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne), [England](/wiki/England), and the largest [independent library](/wiki/Subscription_library) outside [London](/wiki/London). The library is still available for both [lending](/wiki/Lending_library) (to members) and as a free [reference library](/wiki/Reference_library). The society is a [registered charity](/wiki/Charitable_organization).

## Founding

Founded in 1793 as a "conversation club" by the [Reverend William Turner](/wiki/William_Turner_(Unitarian_minister)) and others – more than fifty years before the [London Library](/wiki/London_Library) – the annual subscription was originally one [guinea](/wiki/Guinea_(British_coin)). The Lit and Phil library contained works in French, Spanish, German and Latin; its contacts were international, and its members debated a wide range of issues, but religion and politics were prohibited. Women were first admitted to the library in 1804.
In February 2011, actor and comedian [Alexander Armstrong](/wiki/Alexander_Armstrong_(comedian)) became President of the Lit & Phil. He launched their funding appeal at a special gala event.
At the start of 2012, membership of the Library reached 2,000, the highest number since 1952.

## History

Library interior

During the 19th and 20th centuries, the Lit & Phil was host to a long list of the intelligentsia of the era. Engineer and inventor [George Stephenson](/wiki/George_Stephenson) showed his miner's lamp there, and in 1879, when [Joseph Swan](/wiki/Joseph_Swan) demonstrated his electric light bulbs, the Lit and Phil building became the first public building to be so illuminated.

The Society received in 1800 the country's first specimens of the [wombat](/wiki/Wombat) and the [duck-billed platypus](/wiki/Platypus) from [John Hunter](/wiki/John_Hunter_(Royal_Navy_officer)), Governor of New South Wales and honorary member of the Lit and Phil.

Between 1822 and 1825, a new building was created for the Society on Westgate Road, designed by John Green. The building is still in use today, with many original features including iron-work second-floor galleries.

**Isadore Elliot Handler** (April 9, 1916 – July 21, 2011) was an American inventor, business magnate, and the co-founder of [Mattel](/wiki/Mattel). With his wife, [Ruth Handler](/wiki/Ruth_Handler), he developed some of the biggest-selling toys in American history, including [Barbie](/wiki/Barbie), [Chatty Cathy](/wiki/Chatty_Cathy), [Creepy Crawlers](/wiki/Creepy_Crawlers), and [Hot Wheels](/wiki/Hot_Wheels).

## Early life

Isadore "Izzy" Elliot Handler was born to a [Jewish](/wiki/American_Jews) family in [Chicago, Illinois](/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois), on April 9, 1916. Handler's parents were [Ukrainian](/wiki/Ukrainian_language) Jews who largely spoke [Yiddish](/wiki/Yiddish) within their household. The family moved out of Chicago, and Handler grew up on the west side of [Denver, Colorado](/wiki/Denver,_Colorado). He studied [industrial design](/wiki/Industrial_design) at the [Art Center College of Design](/wiki/Art_Center_College_of_Design) in [Pasadena, California](/wiki/Pasadena,_California). In 1929, he met his future wife [Ruth Mosko](/wiki/Ruth_Handler) at a [B'nai B'rith](/wiki/B%27nai_B%27rith) dance for teenagers. They dated on and off for years, and were married in June 1938. Shortly after their marriage, his new wife Ruth requested he change his name: partially due to her preferring his middle name "Elliot" over "Izzy", and partially from her fears of [American antisemitism](/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_United_States) toward a name frequently associated with being Jewish like Isadore. He complied. The couple had two children: Barbara, who was the namesake of [Barbie](/wiki/Barbie) dolls; and [Kenneth](/wiki/Kenneth_Handler), the namesake of [Ken](/wiki/Ken_(doll)) dolls. While a struggling art student and designer of light fixtures, Handler partnered with Harold Matson to design a realistic-looking miniature piano that received roughly 300,000 orders; however, they mispriced the product and lost a dime on each one produced.

## Mattel

[Mattel](/wiki/Mattel) received its name from business partners [Harold "Matt" Matson](/wiki/Harold_Matson_(businessman)) and Elliot Handler in 1945. Elliot's wife, Ruth, took Matson's role when the Handlers bought his share in the late 1940s.

Handler holds credit for developing the first talking doll [Chatty Cathy](/wiki/Chatty_Cathy), using a [pull string](/wiki/Pull_string) talking mechanism, revolutionizing the toy industry. Mattel continued to develop a number of talking toys, including Chatty Baby, Tiny Chatty Baby, and [Charmin' Chatty](/wiki/Charmin%27_Chatty). Toys were made for cartoon favorites such as [Bugs Bunny](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny) and [Porky Pig](/wiki/Porky_Pig), and for television characters such as [Herman Munster](/wiki/Herman_Munster) and [Mr. Ed](/wiki/Mr._Ed).

When Handler's daughter Barbara married Allan Segal, they created Allan, Ken's friend. The 1965 talking doll Baby Cheryl was named after the Handlers' first grandchild, and the Todd doll in the Barbie line was named after their grandson.

Handler was primarily responsible for two additional Mattel product lines. In 1966, Mattel introduced smaller dolls called [Liddle Kiddles](/wiki/Liddle_Kiddles). Handler claimed he wanted them to resemble little children in neighborhoods across America. They were sculpted by doll artist Martha Armstrong-Hand. Kiddles were a great success and continued to be produced in different versions until the early 1970s. Another product line was [Hot Wheels](/wiki/Hot_Wheels), introduced in 1968, which gave rise to 10,000 different models.

First called Mattel Creations, the company has become the largest toy maker in the world in terms of revenue. In April 2006, Mattel honored Handler with a 90th birthday party at its headquarters in [El Segundo, California](/wiki/El_Segundo,_California). Guests included his daughter Barbara Segal, the namesake of the Barbie doll.

## Personal life

Handler died of [heart failure](/wiki/Heart_failure) at home in [Century City](/wiki/Century_City), a district of [Los Angeles, California](/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California), at age 95 on July 21, 2011. Ruth Handler, Elliot's wife, died in 2002; their son Ken died in 1994.

**The Game Awards 2020** was an award show that honored the best [video games of 2020](/wiki/Video_games_of_2020). It was produced and hosted by [Geoff Keighley](/wiki/Geoff_Keighley), and took place on December 10, 2020. The preshow ceremony was hosted by Sydnee Goodman. Unlike previous [Game Awards](/wiki/Game_Awards), the show was broadcast virtually due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic); Keighley presented at a soundstage in [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles), while musical performances took place virtually at stages in [London](/wiki/London) and [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo). The show introduced the award's first Future Class, a list of individuals from the video game industry who best represent the future of video games, Innovation in Accessibility award, an award for games that featured notable [accessibility options](/wiki/Game_accessibility). The show was [live streamed](/wiki/Live_stream) across 45 different platforms. It featured musical performances from the [London Philharmonic Orchestra](/wiki/London_Philharmonic_Orchestra) and [Eddie Vedder](/wiki/Eddie_Vedder), and presentations from celebrity guests, including [Reggie Fils-Aimé](/wiki/Reggie_Fils-Aim%C3%A9), [Gal Gadot](/wiki/Gal_Gadot), [Brie Larson](/wiki/Brie_Larson), and [Keanu Reeves](/wiki/Keanu_Reeves).

*[The Last of Us Part II](/wiki/The_Last_of_Us_Part_II)* received eleven nominations and seven wins—the most in the show's history to date—and was awarded [Game of the Year](/wiki/The_Game_Award_for_Game_of_the_Year). [Neil Druckmann](/wiki/Neil_Druckmann) and [Halley Gross](/wiki/Halley_Gross) won Best Narrative for their work on the game, while [Laura Bailey](/wiki/Laura_Bailey) was awarded Best Performance for her role as [Abby](/wiki/Abby_(The_Last_of_Us)). Several new games were announced, including *[Ark II](/wiki/Ark_II_(video_game))*, *[Perfect Dark](/wiki/Perfect_Dark_(cancelled_video_game))*, and [an untitled *Mass Effect* game](/wiki/Untitled_Mass_Effect_game). The show was the most expensive ceremony to date. It was viewed by over 83 million streams, the most in its history to date, with 8.3 million concurrent viewers at its peak. It received a mixed reception from media publications, with praise directed at new game announcements, and criticism for not allowing developers more time to speak. Some critics and viewers shared concerns over the success of *The Last of Us Part II* due to its developer's [crunch](/wiki/Crunch_(video_games)) practices.

## Background

As with [the previous iteration](/wiki/The_Game_Awards_2019) of [The Game Awards](/wiki/The_Game_Awards), [Geoff Keighley](/wiki/Geoff_Keighley) (left) hosted the main show while Sydnee Goodman (right) hosted the preshow.

As with previous iterations of [The Game Awards](/wiki/The_Game_Awards), the 2020 show was hosted and produced by Canadian games journalist [Geoff Keighley](/wiki/Geoff_Keighley). He returned as an executive producer alongside Kimmie Kim, and Richard Preuss and LeRoy Bennett returned as director and creative director, respectively. Sydnee Goodman returned as host of the preshow. Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), Keighley did not want to host a normal ceremony. Not wanting to take a hiatus and inspired by the success of [Summer Game Fest](/wiki/Summer_Game_Fest), he considered hosting from his home but his board urged him to attempt a larger show on par with previous years. In case of a significant surge of [COVID-19 cases in California](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_California), the crew had several back-up plans, including broadcasting from Keighley's house. He worked with his partners to develop a virtual show; he and his team took inspiration from other shows throughout the year, including the [Democratic National Convention](/wiki/2020_Democratic_National_Convention), in which the "audience" was featured on virtual screens, as well as the [72nd Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/72nd_Primetime_Emmy_Awards), wherein the hosts were isolated on stage and the winners accepted via [video call](/wiki/Video_call).

The presentation used three soundstages in Los Angeles, London, and Tokyo; each had minimal attendees, mostly related to production crew and presenters. Keighley said this allowed them to include additional presentation events as with past shows, as well as explore taking future shows to different venues. The 2020 show—featuring a production of more than 400 people, six of whom are full-time employees—had a budget of under US$10 million and was the most expensive to date, partly due to the [COVID-19 tests](/wiki/COVID-19_tests) required for the crew and the worldwide remote camera set-ups. It remained profitable due to revenue from advertisers and sponsors, as well as minor earnings from streaming services. The show's theme was strength and comfort due to the impact of the pandemic. Keighley wanted to implore the theme of unity, given the release of the [PlayStation 5](/wiki/PlayStation_5) and [Xbox Series X/S](/wiki/Xbox_Series_X/S) in November 2020; he cited [The Game Awards 2018](/wiki/The_Game_Awards_2018) as an example of this theme, which had led with [Nintendo](/wiki/Nintendo)'s [Reggie Fils-Aimé](/wiki/Reggie_Fils-Aim%C3%A9), Microsoft's [Phil Spencer](/wiki/Phil_Spencer_(business_executive)), and Sony's [Shawn Layden](/wiki/Shawn_Layden) sharing the stage. Keighley felt the inclusion of film and television stars was an interesting way to show a wider appreciation for the industry. His team wanted to include [Henry Cavill](/wiki/Henry_Cavill) in the show, but he was busy working on *[The Witcher](/wiki/The_Witcher_(TV_series))*.

While developing the show, Keighley spoke to hundreds of viewers via [Zoom](/wiki/Zoom_(software)) to discuss their own interests, often alongside industry figures like [Valve Corporation](/wiki/Valve_Corporation) president [Gabe Newell](/wiki/Gabe_Newell) and [Epic Games](/wiki/Epic_Games) creative director [Donald Mustard](/wiki/Donald_Mustard). As with the previous show, the presentation ran alongside the Game Festival, consisting of playable demos and additional in-game content. The show introduced the award's first Future Class, a list of individuals from across the video game industry who best represent the future of video games. The inductees included industry professionals such as [Kinda Funny](/wiki/Kinda_Funny)'s Blessing Adeoye Jr., [Naughty Dog](/wiki/Naughty_Dog)'s [Halley Gross](/wiki/Halley_Gross), and *[GameSpot](/wiki/GameSpot)*'s Kallie Plagge. The presentation was aired on December 10, 2020, [live streamed](/wiki/Live_stream) across more than 45 online platforms. It aired on more than ten networks in China, including [Bilibili](/wiki/Bilibili), [Douyin](/wiki/Douyin), and [Huya Live](/wiki/Huya_Live), and on several networks in India including [Disney+ Hotstar](/wiki/Disney%2B_Hotstar), [JioTV](/wiki/JioTV), and [MX Player](/wiki/MX_Player).

## Winners and nominees

[Neil Druckmann](/wiki/Neil_Druckmann) and [Halley Gross](/wiki/Halley_Gross) were awarded Best Narrative. Druckmann accepted the awards for [Game of the Year](/wiki/The_Game_Award_for_Game_of_the_Year) and Best Game Direction, and Gross was inducted in Future Class.

[Nobuo Uematsu](/wiki/Nobuo_Uematsu) and [Masashi Hamauzu](/wiki/Masashi_Hamauzu) won Best Score and Music alongside Mitsuto Suzuki *(not pictured)*.

[Laura Bailey](/wiki/Laura_Bailey) won Best Performance for her role as [Abby](/wiki/Abby_(The_Last_of_Us)) in *[The Last of Us Part II](/wiki/The_Last_of_Us_Part_II)*.

Lead director Sean Murray accepted Best Ongoing Game for *[No Man's Sky](/wiki/No_Man%27s_Sky)*.

[Emilia Schatz](/wiki/Emilia_Schatz) and Matthew Gallant accepted Innovation in Accessibility for *[The Last of Us Part II](/wiki/The_Last_of_Us_Part_II)*.

*[The Last of Us Part II](/wiki/The_Last_of_Us_Part_II)* co-game director Kurt Margenau accepted Best Action/Adventure Game.

The nominees for The Game Awards 2020 were announced on November 18, 2020. Any game released on or before November 20, 2020 was eligible for consideration. The nominees were compiled by a jury panel with members from 96 media outlets globally; ballots were sent to outlets on October 29 and due back on November 6, though they had until November 13 to submit updated ballots. Outlets were required to submit three games for each category to determine the nominees. Winners were determined between the jury (90%) and public votes (10%); the latter was held via the official website and on social media platforms such as [Facebook](/wiki/Facebook) and [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter), and closed on December 9. The two exceptions were the Most Anticipated Game and Player's Voice awards, which were fully nominated and voted-on by the public; the former was determined exclusively on Twitter and announced during the show, and the latter was announced on December 8 after several rounds of voting. A new Innovation in Accessibility award was added for games that featured notable [accessibility options](/wiki/Game_accessibility). Around 18.3 million people participated in the public vote, doubling from the previous show.

## Ratings and reception

### Nominees

*[Inverse](/wiki/Inverse_(website))*'s Corey Plante felt the cutoff date led to several games getting snubbed, including *[Demon's Souls](/wiki/Demon%27s_Souls_(2020_video_game))* and *[Marvel's Spider-Man: Miles Morales](/wiki/Marvel%27s_Spider-Man:_Miles_Morales)*, as well as [*Pokémon Sword* and *Shield*](/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Sword_and_Shield), which was eligible for both the [2019](/wiki/The_Game_Awards_2019) and 2020 awards but was unrecognized in both. He felt *Ghost of Tsushima* was more deserving of a Best Score and Music nomination than *Doom Eternal*. *[Den of Geek](/wiki/Den_of_Geek)*'s Matthew Byrd similarly lamented the lack of recognition for *Demon's Souls* and *Marvel's Spider-Man: Miles Morales*. Kat Bailey of *[USgamer](/wiki/USgamer)* questioned *Doom Eternal*'s nomination for Game of the Year, describing it as "messy, unfocused, and, well, just not as good" as its predecessor. *[Kotaku Australia](/wiki/Kotaku_Australia)*'s Alex Walker similarly found the nomination misplaced and considered games like *[Half-Life: Alyx](/wiki/Half-Life:_Alyx)* and *[Microsoft Flight Simulator](/wiki/Microsoft_Flight_Simulator_(2020_video_game))* more deserving.

### Ceremony

The show received a mixed reception from media publications. *[VentureBeat](/wiki/VentureBeat)*'s [Dean Takahashi](/wiki/Dean_Takahashi) praised the ceremony, particularly applauding its celebration of diverse games such as *The Last of Us Part II* and *[Tell Me Why](/wiki/Tell_Me_Why_(video_game))* as well as the varied and interesting new game announcements. Todd Martens of *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* felt the show should have allowed more time for the developers to speak and discuss their artistic visions behind the games, noting the presentation does little to demonstrate [video games as art](/wiki/Video_games_as_art). *[Eurogamer](/wiki/Eurogamer)*'s Martin Robinson said the show was understandably "low-key" but called it a "three-hour long advert". *Inverse*'s Ana Diaz criticized the rapid announcement of winners between premieres and during the preshow, preventing developers from accepting the awards, and its focus on [Hollywood](/wiki/Cinema_of_the_United_States) actors over game creators.

Similar to concerns over *[Death Stranding](/wiki/Death_Stranding)*'s predominance in the nominations and ceremony for the 2019 awards due to its creator [Hideo Kojima](/wiki/Hideo_Kojima)'s friendship with Keighley, some viewers shared concerns related to *The Last of Us Part II* at the 2020 awards, both for its awards success and due to the developer's [crunch](/wiki/Crunch_(video_games)) practices. *The Last of Us Part II* was well-received at release but narrative elements polarized some critics and players, and the game had been subject to [review bombing](/wiki/Review_bomb); Keighley clarified the awards were not rigged in the manner some viewers had suggested and there was no influence of Naughty Dog or its staff on the award selection, citing the game's popularity among players and media alike as proven by its runner-up placement in the Player's Voice award. *[Kotaku](/wiki/Kotaku)*'s Ian Walker criticized the game's Best Game Direction win, noting *Hades* should have won due to developer Supergiant Games's less demanding work culture. *TheGamer*'s Peter Glagowski similarly felt the game's awards were a message excusing crunch culture in game development. Keighley felt it would be difficult to incorporate criteria related to games developed under poor industry practices like crunch into the awards selection process without becoming a [slippery slope](/wiki/Slippery_slope), but believes discussions of these practices should be a conversation held by the larger community.

### Viewership

Over 83 million streams were used to view the ceremony, the most in the show's history to date, with 8.3 million concurrent viewers at its peak. On [Twitch](/wiki/Twitch_(service)), the show had over 2.63 million concurrent viewers, more than double the previous year, with 9,000 creators co-streaming the ceremony. On [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube), live viewership increased 84 percent over the previous year. In total, live hours viewed increased by over 129 percent across [Facebook Gaming](/wiki/Facebook_Gaming), Twitch, and YouTube. The show [trended worldwide on Twitter](/wiki/Twitter_trends), with a 31 percent increase in conversation from the previous year, while usage of the [hashtag](/wiki/Hashtag) #TheGameAwards increased 107 percent. Keighley expressed his surprise by the consistent growth of the show over the years, but confessed it has led him to fear "that year where it doesn't grow ... There's going to be a year where we don't have the same viewers".

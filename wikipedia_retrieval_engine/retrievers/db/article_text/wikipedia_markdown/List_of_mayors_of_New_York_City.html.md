# List of mayors of New York City

For a list of the Dutch directors-general who governed [New Amsterdam](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) as part of [New Netherland](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) between 1624 and 1664, see [Director-General of New Netherland](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_New_Netherland).

[Gracie Mansion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracie_Mansion), the official residence of the mayor of New York City since 1942

The [mayor of New York City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_New_York_City) is the [chief executive](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive) of the [Government of New York City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_City), as stipulated by New York City's charter. The current officeholder, the 112th in the sequence of regular mayors, is [Zohran Mamdani](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohran_Mamdani), a member of the [Democratic Party](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)).

During the Dutch colonial period from 1624 to 1664, [New Amsterdam](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) was governed by the [Director of New Netherland](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_New_Netherland). Following the 1664 creation of the British [Province of New York](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York), newly renamed New York City was run by the British military governor, [Richard Nicolls](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nicolls). The office of Mayor of New York City was established in 1665. Holders were appointed by colonial governors, beginning with [Thomas Willett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Willett). The position remained appointed until 1777. That year, during the [American Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), a [Council of Appointment](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Appointment) was formed by the [State](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_(state)) of [New York](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state)). In 1821 the [New York City Council](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council) – then known as the Common Council – began appointing mayors. Since 1834, [mayors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor#United_States) have been elected by direct popular vote.

The city included little beyond the island of Manhattan before 1874, when it annexed [the western part of the Bronx](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bronx), to be followed in 1895 by the rest of the Bronx. The [1898 consolidation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_New_York) created the city as it is today with [five boroughs](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_New_York_City): [Queens](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), [Brooklyn](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [Manhattan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan), [Staten Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staten_Island) and [the Bronx](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx). The first mayor of the expanded city was [Robert Anderson Van Wyck](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Anderson_Van_Wyck).

The longest-serving mayors have been [Fiorello H. La Guardia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_H._La_Guardia) (1934–1945), [Robert F. Wagner Jr.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Wagner_Jr.) (1954–1965), [Ed Koch](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Koch) (1978–1989) and [Michael Bloomberg](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg) (2002–2013), each of whom was in office for twelve years (three successive four-year terms). The shortest terms in office since 1834 have been those of acting mayors: [William T. Collins](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_T._Collins) served a single day on December 31, 1925, [Samuel B. H. Vance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_B._H._Vance) served one month (from November 30 to December 31, 1874), and [Thomas Coman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Coman) served five weeks (from Monday, November 30, 1868, to Monday, January 4, 1869).

## Pre-consolidation mayors (1784–1897)

The mayor continued to be selected by the [Government of New York's](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_(state)) [Council of Appointment](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Appointment) until 1821, when [Stephen Allen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Allen_(American_politician)) became the first mayor appointed by a local [Common Council](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council#History). Under the Charter of 1834, mayors were elected annually by direct popular vote. Starting in 1849, mayors were elected to serve two-year terms.

**Notes**

1. As a result of a conflict between the [Republican](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States))-dominated [New York State Legislature](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Legislature) and the [Tammany Hall](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammany_Hall) political machine, [Fernando Wood's](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Wood) second consecutive term as mayor was shortened by a year through a vote passed by both chambers of legislature to revise the [New York City Charter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Charter). A [mayoral election](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1857_New_York_City_mayoral_election) for a standard-length term was held later that year, resulting in the incumbent Wood's loss to [independent](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_politician) candidate [Daniel F. Tiemann](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_F._Tiemann).
2. [John T. Hoffman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_T._Hoffman) resigned after his election as [governor of the State of New York](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_York) but before the end of his mayoral term. [Thomas Coman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Coman), president of the [New York City Board of Aldermen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Board_of_Aldermen), completed Hoffman's term as acting mayor until his elected successor, [A. Oakey Hall](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Oakey_Hall), took office.
3. When Hall temporarily retired from office during the Tweed investigation in 1872, the acting mayor was Board of Aldermen president [John Cochrane](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cochrane_(politician)).
4. [William F. Havemeyer](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Frederick_Havemeyer) died during his last term in office. [Samuel B. H. Vance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_B._H._Vance), president of the Board of Aldermen, completed Havemeyer's term as acting mayor until his elected successor, [William H. Wickham](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Wickham), took office.
5. [William Lafayette Strong](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lafayette_Strong) served an additional year in office because New York City mayoral elections were changed to be held in odd-numbered years due to the impending [consolidation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_New_York) of New York City.

† *died in office*

## Post-consolidation mayors (since 1897)

See also: [New York City mayoral elections](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_mayoral_elections)

The 1898–1901 term was for four years. The City Charter was changed to make the mayor's term a two-year one beginning in 1902, but after two such terms was changed back to resume four-year terms in 1906. [George B. McClellan Jr.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._McClellan_Jr.) thus served one two-year term from 1904 to 1905, during which he was elected to a four-year term from 1906 to 1909. Since then, mayors have had to be elected with the support of all five boroughs: Queens, Brooklyn, Manhattan, Staten Island, and the Bronx.

The party of the mayor reflects party registration, as opposed to the party lines run under during the general election.

## Appendices

### Official misnumbering

In 1937, [Charles Lodwick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lodwik), who served from as mayor from 1694 to 1695, was inserted as the city's 21st mayor, increasing the official numbering of all subsequent mayors by one.

In December 2025, another official numbering issue affecting all mayors after [John Lawrence's](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lawrence_(New_York_politician)) first term in 1673 was widely reported. Similar to American presidents, mayors are counted twice in the official numbering if they served non-consecutive terms. [Matthias Nicoll](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Nicoll), who was the sixth mayor from 1672–73, was appointed to a non-consecutive term by governor [Edmund Andros](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Andros) in 1674 as the city's eighth mayor after the city was returned to English control following the [Dutch reconquest](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_reconquest_of_New_York_City) during [Third Anglo-Dutch War](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Dutch_War) as part of the [Treaty of Westminster](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Westminster_(1674)), but his second term was erroneously omitted in official records. The error appeared in official numberings from as early as an 1841 Manual of the Corporation of the City of New York. The [New York City Department of Records and Information Services](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Records_and_Information_Services) located original court documents corroborating Nicoll's second term after reporting by *[Gothamist](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothamist)* the same month. The error had previously been noted by various researchers since as early as 1935.

The outgoing administration of [Eric Adams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Adams) declined to address the issue in its final weeks. On January 1, 2026, as [Zohran Mamdani](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohran_Mamdani) was sworn into office his official website called him the 112th mayor. In his inauguration speech, Mamdani joked about the misnumbering, saying "I stand before you ... honored to serve as either your 111th or 112th Mayor of New York City."

### Mayoral terms and term limits in New York City since 1834

Direct elections to the mayoralty of the unconsolidated City of New York began in 1834 for a term of one year, extended to two years after 1849. The 1897 Charter of the consolidated City stipulated that the mayor was to be elected for a single four-year term. In 1901, the term halved to two years, with no restrictions on reelection. In 1905, the term was extended to four years once again. (Mayors [Fiorello La Guardia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_La_Guardia), [Robert F. Wagner Jr.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Wagner_Jr.), [Ed Koch](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Koch) and [Michael Bloomberg](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bloomberg) were later able to serve for twelve years each.) In 1993, the voters approved a two-term (eight-year) limit, and reconfirmed this limit when the issue was submitted to referendum in 1996. In 2008, the [New York City Council](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Council) voted to change the two-term limit to three terms (without submitting the issue to the voters). Legal challenges to the Council's action were rejected by Federal courts in January and April 2009. However, in 2010, yet another referendum, reverting the limit to two terms, passed overwhelmingly.

# Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor

## Infobox

- **Awarded for:** Best Performance by an Actor in a Supporting Role
- **Country:** United States
- **Presented by:** Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS)
- **First award:** March 4, 1937; 89 years ago ( 1937-03-04 ) (for films released in 1936 )
- **Most recent winner:** Sean Penn, One Battle After Another ( 2025 )
- **Most awards:** Walter Brennan (3)
- **Most nominations:** Walter Brennan, Jeff Bridges, Robert Duvall, Arthur Kennedy, Jack Nicholson, Al Pacino, Claude Rains, and Mark Ruffalo (4)
- **Website:** oscars.org

The **Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor** is an award presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) (AMPAS). It has been awarded since the [9th Academy Awards](/wiki/9th_Academy_Awards) to an actor who has delivered an outstanding performance in a [supporting role](/wiki/Supporting_character) in a film released that year. The award is traditionally presented by the previous year's [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress) winner. However, in recent years, it has shifted towards being presented by previous years' Best Supporting Actor winners instead. In lieu of the traditional [Oscar statuette](/wiki/Academy_Awards#Oscar_statuette), supporting acting recipients were given plaques up until the [16th Academy Awards](/wiki/16th_Academy_Awards), when statuettes were awarded to each category instead.

The Best Supporting Actor award has been presented a total of 90 times, to 81 actors. The first winner was [Walter Brennan](/wiki/Walter_Brennan) for his role in *[Come and Get It](/wiki/Come_and_Get_It_(1936_film))* (1936). The most recent winner is [Sean Penn](/wiki/Sean_Penn) for *[One Battle After Another](/wiki/One_Battle_After_Another)* (2025). The record for [most wins](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_two_or_more_Academy_Awards_in_acting_categories) is three, held by Brennan–who won every other year within a succession of the first five years. Seven other actors have won twice. Brennan is also tied for receiving the [most nominations](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_more_than_one_Academy_Award_nomination_in_the_acting_categories) in the category (with four altogether) along with [Jeff Bridges](/wiki/Jeff_Bridges), [Robert Duvall](/wiki/Robert_Duvall), [Arthur Kennedy](/wiki/Arthur_Kennedy), [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson), [Al Pacino](/wiki/Al_Pacino), [Claude Rains](/wiki/Claude_Rains), and [Mark Ruffalo](/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo). For his performance in *[The Dark Knight](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight)* (2008), [Heath Ledger](/wiki/Heath_Ledger) became the first actor to [win posthumously in this category](/wiki/List_of_posthumous_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees)—and second overall. [Christopher Plummer](/wiki/Christopher_Plummer) is the oldest actor to [receive a nomination in any category](/wiki/List_of_oldest_and_youngest_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Oldest_nominees_4) at age 88, for *[All the Money in the World](/wiki/All_the_Money_in_the_World)* (2017).

## Nominations process

Nominees are currently determined by [single transferable vote](/wiki/Single_transferable_vote) within the actors branch of AMPAS; winners are selected by a [plurality vote](/wiki/Plurality_(voting)) from the entire [eligible voting members](/wiki/Film_industry) of the Academy.

## Winners and nominees

In the following table, the years are listed as per Academy convention, and generally correspond to the year of film release in [Los Angeles County](/wiki/Los_Angeles_County,_California); the ceremonies are always held the following year. For the first five ceremonies, the eligibility period spanned twelve months, from August 1 to July 31. For the 6th ceremony held in 1934, the eligibility period lasted from August 1, 1932, to December 31, 1933. Since the 7th ceremony held in 1935, the period of eligibility became the full previous calendar year from January 1 to December 31.

## Films with multiple Supporting Actor nominations

There have been 23 instances in which films have produced more than one nominee within this category. All resulted in two nominations, with the exceptions of *On the Waterfront* (1954), *The Godfather* (1972), and *The Godfather Part II* (1974), which each obtained three.

Winners are in **bold**.

- *[Mr. Smith Goes to Washington](/wiki/Mr._Smith_Goes_to_Washington)* (1939) – [Harry Carey Sr.](/wiki/Harry_Carey_(actor)) and [Claude Rains](/wiki/Claude_Rains)
- *[Quo Vadis](/wiki/Quo_Vadis_(1951_film))* (1951) – [Leo Genn](/wiki/Leo_Genn) and [Peter Ustinov](/wiki/Peter_Ustinov)
- *[Shane](/wiki/Shane_(film))* (1953) – [Brandon deWilde](/wiki/Brandon_deWilde) and [Jack Palance](/wiki/Jack_Palance)
- *[On the Waterfront](/wiki/On_the_Waterfront)* (1954) – [Lee J. Cobb](/wiki/Lee_J._Cobb), [Karl Malden](/wiki/Karl_Malden), and [Rod Steiger](/wiki/Rod_Steiger)
- *[Peyton Place](/wiki/Peyton_Place_(film))* (1957) – [Arthur Kennedy](/wiki/Arthur_Kennedy) and [Russ Tamblyn](/wiki/Russ_Tamblyn)
- *[Anatomy of a Murder](/wiki/Anatomy_of_a_Murder)* (1959) – [Arthur O'Connell](/wiki/Arthur_O%27Connell) and [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott)
- *[The Hustler](/wiki/The_Hustler)* (1961) – [Jackie Gleason](/wiki/Jackie_Gleason) and George C. Scott

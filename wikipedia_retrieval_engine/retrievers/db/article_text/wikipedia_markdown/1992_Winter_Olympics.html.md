The **1992 Winter Olympics**, officially known as the **XVI Olympic Winter Games** ([French](/wiki/French_language): *XVIes Jeux Olympiques d'hiver*) and commonly known as **Albertville '92** ([Arpitan](/wiki/Franco-Proven%C3%A7al): *Arbèrtvile '92*), were a winter [multi-sport event](/wiki/Multi-sport_event) held from 8 to 23 February 1992 in and around [Albertville](/wiki/Albertville), France. Albertville won the bid to host the Winter Olympics in 1986, beating [Sofia](/wiki/Sofia), [Falun](/wiki/Falun), [Lillehammer](/wiki/Lillehammer), [Cortina d'Ampezzo](/wiki/Cortina_d%27Ampezzo), [Anchorage](/wiki/Anchorage,_Alaska), and [Berchtesgaden](/wiki/Berchtesgaden). The 1992 [Winter Olympics](/wiki/Winter_Olympics) were the last winter games held in the same year as the [Summer Olympics](/wiki/Summer_Olympics). The next Winter Olympics were held two years after this one instead of the usual four, which allowed the Olympic Games to take place on every even-numbered year instead of every four years. The Games were the fifth Olympic Games held in France and the country's third Winter Olympics, after the [1924 Winter Games](/wiki/1924_Winter_Olympics) in [Chamonix](/wiki/Chamonix) and the [1968 Winter Games](/wiki/1968_Winter_Olympics) in [Grenoble](/wiki/Grenoble). This games was the first of two consecutive Olympic games to be held in Western Europe, preceding the [1992 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics) in Barcelona, Spain.

18 events in [Figure skating](/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), [short track speed skating](/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), [speed skating](/wiki/Speed_skating_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), and the opening and closing ceremonies took place in Albertville. The 39 other events were held in the nearby 9 villages and resorts around [Savoie](/wiki/Savoie): [Courchevel](/wiki/Courchevel), [La Plagne](/wiki/La_Plagne), [Les Arcs](/wiki/Les_Arcs), [Les Menuires](/wiki/Les_Menuires), [Les Saisies](/wiki/Les_Saisies), [Méribel](/wiki/M%C3%A9ribel), [Pralognan-la-Vanoise](/wiki/Pralognan-la-Vanoise), [Tignes](/wiki/Tignes), and [Val d'Isère](/wiki/Val_d%27Is%C3%A8re). Sixty-four National Olympic Committees and 1,801 athletes participated in six [sports](/wiki/Olympic_sports) and fifty-seven [events](/wiki/Olympic_events). This included both the [Unified Team](/wiki/Unified_Team_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), representing the non-Baltic former [Soviet republics](/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union), and [Germany](/wiki/Germany_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), newly consolidated again as a team following the [reunification](/wiki/German_reunification) of the former [East](/wiki/East_Germany) and [West Germany](/wiki/West_Germany) in 1990. The event also saw the debut of eight nations in the Winter Olympics. The Winter Olympic program has grown this time with the addition of 11 new events. While sports that were already on the program received 5 new events (2 new events in cross-country skiing, at the same time women were allowed to compete in biathlon for the first time and won 3 events exclusive to them.) Another 6 events were added with the implementation of 2 sports that were demonstrated 4 years earlier (freestyle skiing and short track speed skating). These were the last Winter Olympics to include [demonstration sports](/wiki/Demonstration_sport), consisting of [curling](/wiki/Curling_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), [aerials and ski ballet](/wiki/Freestyle_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics), and [speed skiing](/wiki/Speed_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics). Due to a rule change implemented in 1986, this was the last edition in the history of the Winter Olympic Games in which all speed skating events were held in an open-air venue.

## Highlights

[Bjørn Dæhlie](/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rn_D%C3%A6hlie) and [Vegard Ulvang](/wiki/Vegard_Ulvang) dominated the men's [cross-country skiing](/wiki/Cross-country_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics) races, both taking home three gold medals with [Norway](/wiki/Norway) taking a medal sweep in the event. 16-year-old [Ski jumper](/wiki/Ski_jumping_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics) [Toni Nieminen](/wiki/Toni_Nieminen) became the youngest male gold medalist in a Winter Olympic event until [2002](/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics). [Petra Kronberger](/wiki/Petra_Kronberger) won both the [combined event](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_combined) and the [slalom](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_slalom) of [alpine skiing](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_Winter_Olympics), while [Bonnie Blair](/wiki/Bonnie_Blair) won both the [500 m](/wiki/Speed_skating_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_500_metres) and [1000 m](/wiki/Speed_skating_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_1000_metres) speed skating events, and [Gunda Niemann](/wiki/Gunda_Niemann) took both of the longest races.

Three National Olympic Committees in [Asia-Pacific](/wiki/Asia-Pacific) region won their first medals at the Winter Olympics, one in a sport making its debut at the Games ([short track speed skating](/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating).) [Kim Ki-hoon](/wiki/Kim_Ki-hoon)'s gold medal in [1000 m short track speed skating](/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_1000_metres) signified [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea)'s first medal in the Winter Olympics, while [Ye Qiaobo](/wiki/Ye_Qiaobo)'s silver medal in women's 500 m speed skating represented [China](/wiki/China)'s first Winter Olympics medal. [Annelise Coberger](/wiki/Annelise_Coberger) from [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics) became the first [Oceanian](/wiki/Oceania) athlete to win a medal in [women's alpine skiing slalom](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_slalom), making her the first athlete from the southern hemisphere to mount the podium at the Winter Games.

Swiss speed skier [Nicolas Bochatay](/wiki/Nicolas_Bochatay) died on the penultimate day of the Games, when he crashed into a snow-grooming vehicle during a training run.

## Legacy

The 1992 Olympic Winter Games marked the last time both the Winter and Summer games were held in the same year. The 1992 Winter Olympics also marked the last time [France](/wiki/France) hosted the Olympics until [2024](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics), when [Paris](/wiki/Paris) became the second city to host the Summer Olympics three times. France is set to host the Winter Games in [2030](/wiki/2030_Winter_Olympics) in the [French Alps](/wiki/French_Alps), 38 years after Albertville played host and will host it again in 2030.

### Cost and cost overrun

*The Oxford Olympics Study* established the outturn cost of the Albertville 1992 Winter Olympics at US$2.0 billion (in 2015-dollars) and cost overrun at 137% in real terms. This includes sports-related costs only, that is: (i) *operational costs* incurred by the organizing committee to stage the Games, e.g., expenditures for technology, transportation, workforce, administration, security, catering, ceremonies, and medical services; and (ii) *direct capital costs* incurred by the host city and country or private investors to build, e.g., the competition venues, the Olympic village, international broadcast center, and media and press center, which are required to host the Games. Indirect capital costs were not included, e.g. road, rail, or airport infrastructure, or hotel upgrades or other business investment incurred in preparation for the Games but not directly related to their staging. In comparison, the cost and cost overrun of the [2010 Vancouver Winter Olympics](/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) were US$2.5 billion and 13%, respectively, while the [2014 Sochi Winter Olympics](/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics) (the most costly Olympics to date) had costs and cost overrun at US$51 billion and 289%, respectively. The average cost for the Winter Games since 1960 is US$3.1 billion, while the average cost overrun is 142%.

## Mascot

Main article: [Magique (mascot)](/wiki/Magique_(mascot))

The 1992 Winter Games [mascot](/wiki/List_of_Olympic_mascots), [Magique](/wiki/Magique_(mascot)) (Magic), was a small imp in the shape of a star and a cube. The mascot was created by Philippe Mairesse and replaced the bid mascot, which was a mountain goat. The star shape symbolized dreams and imagination, while the mascot's red and blue colors originated from the [French flag](/wiki/French_flag).

### Demonstration sports

This was the last time demonstration events were included in the Winter Olympics program. Of the 8 events that were under evaluation, 4 received the endorsement to be included in an official form in future editions of the Games ([Curling](/wiki/Curling) tournaments and the [aerials](/wiki/Aerial_skiing) events on the [freestyle skiing](/wiki/Freestyle_skiing)). The other four events (speed skiing and skiing ballet events on the freestyle skiing) were rejected and have not since returned.

- [Curling](/wiki/Curling_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics) – Was an official sport in the Olympic program in [1924](/wiki/Curling_at_the_1924_Winter_Olympics), after which it was a demonstration sport twice, in [1932](/wiki/Curling_at_the_1932_Winter_Olympics) and [1988](/wiki/Curling_at_the_1988_Winter_Olympics). There was a possibility of re-inclusion in [Lillehammer 1994](/wiki/1994_Winter_Olympics), but in 1990 the IOC Executive Committee rejected its return as an official sport. The IOC accepted its return in [Nagano 1998](/wiki/Curling_at_the_1998_Winter_Olympics).
- [Freestyle skiing](/wiki/Freestyle_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics) – Like curling, it was a demonstration sport [four years previously](/wiki/Freestyle_skiing_at_the_1988_Winter_Olympics) before becoming part of the official program. Only [moguls skiing](/wiki/Moguls_skiing) received this status, while aerials and ballet remained demonstration events. [Aerials became an official event two years later](/wiki/Freestyle_skiing_at_the_1994_Winter_Olympics), while ballet skiing appeared in the games for the last time, going into a progressive decline and losing its status as a competitive discipline by the [International Ski Federation](/wiki/International_Ski_Federation) (FIS) in 2000.
- [Speed skiing](/wiki/Speed_skiing_at_the_1992_Winter_Olympics) – Considered one of the most dangerous events in the sporting world, the discipline won a chance to be evaluated by the International Olympic Committee and the FIS for possible inclusion in a future program. The event, hosted at Les Arcs, was a success, pushing the world record to 229.299 km/h (142mph). Tragically, after qualifying for the final, Swiss skier [Nicolas Bochatay](/wiki/Nicolas_Bochatay) ran into a snow-grooming vehicle while skiing on an adjacent public slope with a teammate, dying instantly. According to reports, Bochatay crested a small hill and crashed into the machine which he had not seen. The discipline has not been featured on an Olympic Games program since then.

## Participating nations

Sixty-four nations sent competitors to the 1992 Olympics, including seven nations making their first appearance at a Winter Olympics. Following the [collapse of the Soviet Union](/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_(1985%E2%80%931991)) in 1991, six former-Soviet bloc nations chose to form a [Unified Team](/wiki/Unified_Team_at_the_Olympics), while the [Baltic states](/wiki/Baltic_states) of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania competed as independent nations for the first time since 1936. Czechoslovakia made its last appearance in the Winter Olympics before [its dissolution](/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia) at the end of 1992. [United Nations Security Council Resolution 757](/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_757) took effect on 30 May 1992 (97 days after the closing ceremonies), and Yugoslav athletes were able to participate under their country's national symbols. It also suspended the activities of the [Yugoslav Olympic Committee](/wiki/Yugoslav_Olympic_Committee), making the country's athletes ineligible to compete on the [1992 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics). Despite this, some of their athletes classified in individual sports and gained authorization to compete as [Independent Olympic Participants](/wiki/Independent_Olympic_Participants_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics) (which also happened at the [1992 Summer Paralympics](/wiki/Independent_Paralympic_Participants_at_the_1992_Summer_Paralympics)). Yugoslav athletes [returned to the Olympic Games in the 1996 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Yugoslavia_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics), when only Serbia, Montenegro and Kosovo were still part of the country. The 1992 Winter Olympics were the first time since the [1964 Summer Olympics](/wiki/United_Team_of_Germany_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) that Germany competed with a unified team. Seven [National Olympic Committees](/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) sent their first delegations to the Winter Olympics: Algeria, Bermuda, Brazil, Honduras, Ireland, Swaziland, Croatia, and Slovenia (the last two making their first appearances at any Olympics, just a few months after their respective declarations of independence from [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia)). Through the [2022 Winter Olympics](/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics), this has been the only participation of Swaziland (now Eswatini) and Honduras in an edition of the Winter Olympics.

- [Les Saisies](/wiki/Les_Saisies) – Biathlon, cross-country skiing
- [Tignes](/wiki/Tignes) – Freestyle skiing

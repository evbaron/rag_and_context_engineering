# Contiguous United States

A map showing the contiguous [United States](/wiki/United_States)

The **contiguous United States**, also known as the **U.S. mainland**, officially referred to as the **conterminous United States**, consists of the 48 adjoining [U.S. states](/wiki/U.S._state) and the [District of Columbia](/wiki/District_of_Columbia) of the [United States](/wiki/United_States) in central [North America](/wiki/North_America). The term excludes the only two non-[contiguous](/wiki/Geographic_contiguity) states and the last two to be [admitted to the Union](/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union), which are [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska) and [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii), and all other offshore [insular areas](/wiki/Insular_area), such as the [U.S. territories](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) of [American Samoa](/wiki/American_Samoa), [Guam](/wiki/Guam), the [Northern Mariana Islands](/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands), [Puerto Rico](/wiki/Puerto_Rico), and the [U.S. Virgin Islands](/wiki/U.S._Virgin_Islands). The colloquial term ***Lower** **48*** is also used, especially in relation to Alaska. The term **The Mainland** is used in [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii). The related but distinct term ***continental United States*** includes [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska), which is also in North America, but separated from the 48 states by [British Columbia](/wiki/British_Columbia) in Canada, but excludes [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii) and all the insular areas in the [Caribbean](/wiki/Caribbean) and the [Pacific](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean).

Map highlighting [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska) and [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii)'s geographical relationship to the contiguous United States. Alaska, in red, is in the upper part of the map, while Hawaii is the islands also in red to the far left. Contiguous US is near the center in pale

The greatest distance on a [great-circle](/wiki/Great-circle_distance) route entirely within the contiguous U.S. is 2,802 miles (4,509 km), coast-to-coast between [Florida](/wiki/Florida) and [Washington state](/wiki/Washington_(state)); the greatest north–south line is 1,650 miles (2,660 km). The contiguous United States occupies an area of 3,119,884.69 square miles (8,080,464.3 km2). Of this area, 2,959,064.44 square miles (7,663,941.7 km2) is actual land, composing 83.65 percent of the country's total land area, and is comparable in size to the area of Australia. Officially, 160,820.25 square miles (416,522.5 km2) of the contiguous United States is water area, composing 62.66 percent of the nation's total water area.

If just the contiguous United States were a country, it would be fifth on the [list of countries and dependencies by area](/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area), behind [Russia](/wiki/Russia), [Canada](/wiki/Canada), [China](/wiki/China), and [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil). However, the total area of the United States, including Alaska and Hawaii, ranks third or fourth. Brazil is 166,000 square miles (431,000 km2) larger than the contiguous United States, but smaller than the entire United States, including Alaska, Hawaii, and overseas territories. The [2020 U.S. census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census) population of the area was 328,571,074, comprising 99.13 percent of the nation's total population, and a density of 111.04 inhabitants/sq mi (42.872/km2), compared to 93.844/sq mi (36.233/km2) for the nation as a whole.

## Other terms

While *conterminous U.S.* has the precise meaning of *contiguous U.S.* (both adjectives meaning "sharing a common boundary"), other terms commonly used to describe the 48 contiguous states have a greater degree of ambiguity.

### Continental and mainland United States

"Continental United States" redirects here. For the continental shelf, see [Continental shelf of the United States](/wiki/Continental_shelf_of_the_United_States).

Because [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska) is also a part of [North America](/wiki/North_America), the term ***continental United States*** also includes that state, so the term is qualified with the explicit inclusion of Alaska to resolve any ambiguity. On May 14, 1959, the [United States Board on Geographic Names](/wiki/United_States_Board_on_Geographic_Names) issued the following definitions based partially on the reference in the Alaska Omnibus Bill, which defined the continental United States as "the 49 States on the North American Continent and the District of Columbia..." The Board reaffirmed these definitions on May 13, 1999. However, even before Alaska became a state, it was properly included within the continental U.S. due to being an [incorporated territory](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States#Incorporated_vs._unincorporated_territories).

The term ***mainland United States*** is sometimes used synonymously with *continental United States*, but technically refers only to those parts of states connected to the landmass of North America, thereby excluding not only [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii) and overseas [insular areas](/wiki/Insular_area), but also islands which are part of continental states but separated from the mainland, such as the [Aleutian Islands](/wiki/Aleutian_Islands) ([Alaska](/wiki/Alaska)), [San Juan Islands](/wiki/San_Juan_Islands) ([Washington](/wiki/Washington_(state))), the [Channel Islands](/wiki/Channel_Islands_(California)) ([California](/wiki/California)), [the Keys](/wiki/Florida_Keys) ([Florida](/wiki/Florida)), the [barrier islands](/wiki/Barrier_island) ([Gulf](/wiki/Gulf_Coast_of_the_United_States) and [East Coast](/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States) states), and [Long Island](/wiki/Long_Island) (New York).

### CONUS and OCONUS

"CONUS" redirects here. For the sea snail, see [*Conus*](/wiki/Conus). For other uses, see [Conus (disambiguation)](/wiki/Conus_(disambiguation)).

*CONUS*, a technical term used by the [U.S. Department of Defense](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense), [General Services Administration](/wiki/General_Services_Administration), [NOAA/National Weather Service](/wiki/National_Oceanic_and_Atmospheric_Administration), and others, has been defined both as the continental United States and as the 48 contiguous states. The District of Columbia is not always specifically mentioned as being part of *CONUS*.

*OCONUS* is derived from CONUS with *O* for outside added, thus referring to Outside of Continental United States.

### The lower 48

The term *lower 48* is also used to refer to the conterminous United States. The *[National Geographic](/wiki/National_Geographic_(magazine))* style guide recommends the use of *contiguous* or *conterminous United States* instead of *lower 48* when the 48 states are meant, unless used in the context of Alaska. Almost all of Hawaii is south of the southernmost point of the conterminous United States in Florida.

### Zone of the Interior

During [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), the first four [numbered Air Forces](/wiki/Numbered_Air_Force) of the [United States Army Air Forces](/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) (USAAF) were said to be assigned to the *[Zone of the Interior](/wiki/USAAF#Emblems)* by the American military organizations of the time—the future states of [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska) and [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii), then each only [organized incorporated territories](/wiki/Organized_incorporated_territories_of_the_United_States) of the Union, were respectively covered by the [Eleventh Air Force](/wiki/Eleventh_Air_Force#Origins) and [Seventh Air Force](/wiki/Seventh_Air_Force#Origins) during the war.

## Terms used in the non-contiguous U.S. jurisdictions

Residents of Alaska, Hawaii, and offshore [U.S. territories](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) have unique labels for the contiguous United States because of their own locations relative to it.

### Alaska

The vast territory of [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska) became the 49th state of the United States on January 3, 1959. Alaska is the northwest extremity of the North American continent, separated from the [U.S. West Coast](/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) by the [Canadian province](/wiki/Provinces_and_territories_of_Canada) of [British Columbia](/wiki/British_Columbia). The term *Lower 48* has, for many years, been a common Alaskan equivalent for "contiguous United States"; some Alaskans may use the term *[Outside](/wiki/Outside_(Alaska))* for those states, though some may use *Outside* to refer to any location not within Alaska.

### Hawaii

The territory of [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii), consisting of the entire [Hawaiian Islands](/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands) archipelago except for [Midway Atoll](/wiki/Midway_Atoll), became the 50th state of the United States on August 21, 1959. It is the southernmost U.S. state, and the latest one to join the Union. Not part of any continent, Hawaii is located in the [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean), about 2,200 miles (3,541 km) from North America and almost halfway between North America and [Asia](/wiki/Asia). In Hawaii and [overseas American territories](/wiki/Insular_area), for instance, the terms *the Mainland* or *U.S. Mainland* are often used to refer to the 49 states in North America.

### Puerto Rico

Puerto Rico is an [unincorporated territory](/wiki/Unincorporated_territories_of_the_United_States) of the United States located in the northeast [Caribbean Sea](/wiki/Caribbean_Sea), approximately 1,000 miles (1,609 km) southeast of [Miami](/wiki/Miami), [Florida](/wiki/Florida). Puerto Ricans born in Puerto Rico are U.S. citizens and are free to move to the mainland United States. The term *[Stateside Puerto Rican](/wiki/Stateside_Puerto_Rican)* refers to residents of any U.S. state or the District of Columbia who were born in, or can trace their family ancestry to, Puerto Rico.

### U.S. Virgin Islands

The [U.S. Virgin Islands](/wiki/U.S._Virgin_Islands) is a [U.S. territory](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) located directly to the east of Puerto Rico in the Caribbean Sea. The term *stateside* is used to refer to the mainland, in relation to the U.S. Virgin Islands (see [Stateside Virgin Islands Americans](/wiki/Stateside_Virgin_Islands_Americans)).

### American Samoa

Further information: [Samoan Americans](/wiki/Samoan_Americans)

[American Samoa](/wiki/American_Samoa) is a [U.S. territory](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States) located in the [South Pacific Ocean](/wiki/South_Pacific_Ocean) in [Polynesia](/wiki/Polynesia), south of the equator — it is 2,200 miles (3,500 km) southwest of [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii). In American Samoa, the contiguous United States is called the "mainland United States" or "the states"; those not from American Samoa are called *palagi* (outsiders).

## Non-contiguous areas within the contiguous United States

See also: [Canada–United States border § Practical exclaves of the United States](/wiki/Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border#Practical_exclaves_of_the_United_States)

Apart from offshore [U.S. islands](/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_United_States_by_area), a few continental portions of the contiguous United States are accessible by road only by traveling through Canada. [Point Roberts, Washington](/wiki/Point_Roberts,_Washington); [Elm Point, Minnesota](/wiki/Elm_Point,_Minnesota), and two nearby points; the [Northwest Angle](/wiki/Northwest_Angle) in [Minnesota](/wiki/Minnesota); a peninsula in Osthus Lake in [North Dakota](/wiki/North_Dakota)'s [Rolette County](/wiki/Rolette_County,_North_Dakota) and a slice of land on the edge of [Lake Metigoshe](/wiki/Lake_Metigoshe) in [Bottineau County](/wiki/Bottineau_County,_North_Dakota) bordering [Winchester, Canada](/wiki/Municipality_of_Deloraine_%E2%80%93_Winchester), are seven such places. [Alburgh, Vermont](/wiki/Alburgh_(town),_Vermont) is not directly connected by land to the rest of the contiguous US, but is accessible by road via bridges from within Vermont and from New York, and nearby [Province Point](/wiki/Province_Point) is accessible over land only from Canada, though no roads go there. In contrast, [Hyder, Alaska](/wiki/Hyder,_Alaska), is physically part of contiguous Alaska and is its easternmost town, but the only practical overland access is by road through Canada.

**Stamatios (Tom) Mike Krimigis** ([Greek](/wiki/Greek_language): Σταμάτιος Κριμιζής; born September 10, 1938) is a Greek-American [space physicist](/wiki/Space_physics), who contributed to multiple space probe missions. He has contributed to exploration missions to almost every planet of the Solar System. In 1999, the [International Astronomical Union](/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) named the asteroid [8323 Krimigis](/wiki/8323_Krimigis) (previously 1979 UH) in his honor.

## Biography

Stamatios Krimigis was born September 10, 1938 in [Vrontados](/wiki/Vrontados), [Chios](/wiki/Chios), [Greece](/wiki/Greece), where he completed his early education. He then moved to the United States to further his studies. Krimigis studied physics at the [University of Minnesota](/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) (BSc 1961) and at the [University of Iowa](/wiki/University_of_Iowa) (MSc 1963, [Ph.D.](/wiki/Ph.D.) 1965). His PhD advisor was renowned space scientist [James Van Allen](/wiki/James_Van_Allen).

He is Head [Emeritus](/wiki/Emeritus) of the Space Department at the [Johns Hopkins](/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) [Applied Physics Laboratory](/wiki/Applied_Physics_Laboratory) in [Laurel, Maryland](/wiki/Laurel,_Maryland). He holds the Chair of Science of Space at the [Academy of Athens](/wiki/Academy_of_Athens_(modern)), [Greece](/wiki/Greece), and serves as the President of the Greek National Council for Research and Technology.

Krimigis in c. 1980

Krimigis led or participated in space physics experiments on space probe mission to all nine classical planets. He worked on 21 instruments for various NASA and [European Space Agency](/wiki/European_Space_Agency) missions. His Low Energy Charged Particle Experiment (LECP) instrument flies aboard both Voyager spacecraft; on Voyager 1, LECP data was essential to determining that a spacecraft had left the solar system for the first time in 2012. Krimigis also participated in establishing NASA's [Discovery Program](/wiki/Discovery_Program) of low-cost planetary missions, as well as the [New Frontiers](/wiki/New_Frontiers_program) program, for which the APL-built New Horizons to Pluto was the first mission. He is also a co-investigator on the [Parker Solar Probe](/wiki/Parker_Solar_Probe), launched in 2018.

He is co-investigator for LAN/HI-SCALE on [Ulysses](/wiki/Ulysses_(spacecraft)) solar polar orbiter, EPIC on [GEOTAIL](/wiki/GEOTAIL), EDP for [Galileo](/wiki/Galileo_(spacecraft)) mission, TRD on [Mariner 3](/wiki/Mariner_3), and for the LECR on [Mariner 4](/wiki/Mariner_4). Krimigis has also worked on the [Cassini](/wiki/Cassini-Huygens), [Advanced Composition Explorer](/wiki/Advanced_Composition_Explorer), the [Mariner 5](/wiki/Mariner_5), [MESSENGER](/wiki/MESSENGER) and [New Horizons](/wiki/New_Horizons) programs.

In 1986, Krimigis briefed [Ronald Reagan](/wiki/Ronald_Reagan) on the [AMPTE](/wiki/AMPTE-CCE) mission. He also met with [Mikhail Gorbachev](/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev) in 1987 and [George W. Bush](/wiki/George_W._Bush) in 1990 to discuss [space exploration](/wiki/Space_exploration).

In 2016, Krimigis received NASA's highest service honor, the [NASA Distinguished Public Service Medal](/wiki/NASA_Distinguished_Public_Service_Medal), for his lifelong efforts to advance space exploration and science.

In 2017, Krimigis's contributions to the Voyager program were highlighted in the documentary film "[The Farthest](/wiki/The_Farthest)."

## Research contributions

Krimigis's research has focused on the study of the [magnetospheres](/wiki/Magnetospheres) of planets and the [heliosphere](/wiki/Heliosphere).

Krimigis's publication record spans from "Interplanetary [diffusion model](/wiki/Cosmic_ray#Solar_modulation) for [time behavior of intensity](/wiki/Solar_energetic_particles) in a solar cosmic ray event," published in the [Journal of Geophysical Research](/wiki/Journal_of_Geophysical_Research) in 1965, to "Search for the exit: Voyager 1 at heliosphere's border with the galaxy," published in [Science](/wiki/Science_(journal)) in 2013. Krimigis has published nearly 600 articles in scientific journals and books, on solar observations, planetary magnetospheres, and the charged particles in the interplanetary space. Krimigis has over 21,000 citations.

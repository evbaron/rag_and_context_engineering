"**The Twist**" is an American song written and originally released in 1958 by [Hank Ballard and the Midnighters](/wiki/Hank_Ballard) as a [B-side](/wiki/A-side_and_B-side) to "Teardrops on Your Letter". It was inspired by the [twist](/wiki/Twist_(dance)) [dance craze](/wiki/Dance_craze). Ballard's version was a moderate hit, peaking at number 28 on the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) in 1960. On the US *Billboard* [Hot R&B Sides](/wiki/Hot_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Songs) chart, the original version of "The Twist" first peaked at number 16 in 1959 and at number six in 1960. By 1962, the record sold in excess of one million copies, becoming Ballard's fourth million seller.

[Chubby Checker](/wiki/Chubby_Checker)'s 1960 [cover version](/wiki/Cover_version) of the song reached number one on the *Billboard* Hot 100 on September 19, 1960, where it stayed for one week and then went on to set a record at the time as the only song to reach number one during two different chart runs when it resurfaced and topped the Hot 100 again for two weeks starting on January 13, 1962. This would not happen for another song for nearly 59 years until December 2020, when [Mariah Carey](/wiki/Mariah_Carey)'s "[All I Want for Christmas Is You](/wiki/All_I_Want_for_Christmas_Is_You)" reached the summit after previously topping in another separate chart run in December 2019.

On *[Billboard's](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* list of all-time No. 1 Hot 100 singles, which considers total weeks on the chart and chart position each week, "The Twist" held the top position from 2008 until September 2020, when "[Blinding Lights](/wiki/Blinding_Lights)" by [the Weeknd](/wiki/The_Weeknd) took over the top position. In 2014, *Billboard* magazine declared the song the "biggest hit" of the 1960s. On Canada's [CHUM Charts](/wiki/CHUM_Chart), the songs were co-charted, reaching number two on August 22, 1960.

## History

Hank Ballard wrote "The Twist" after seeing teenagers in Tampa, Florida doing the titular dance. In a 2014 interview with Tom Meros, Midnighters member Lawson Smith claimed that The Gospel Consolaters' Nathaniel Bills wrote the song and initially asked [The Spaniels](/wiki/The_Spaniels) to record it, and that Ballard "stole" the song, falsely claiming authorship. Green and Ballard already had written a song together called "Is Your Love for Real", which was based on [Clyde McPhatter](/wiki/Clyde_McPhatter) and [The Drifters](/wiki/The_Drifters)' 1955 song "[What'cha Gonna Do](/wiki/What%27cha_Gonna_Do_(The_Drifters_song))", so they created an entirely new song by simply putting the new Twist words to the older melody. They originally recorded a loose version of the song in a Florida studio for [Vee-Jay Records](/wiki/Vee-Jay_Records) in early 1958, with slightly different lyrics, featuring Green on guitar playing like [Jimmy Reed](/wiki/Jimmy_Reed). This version appeared on the box set "The Vee-Jay Story" in 1993, but it went unreleased at the time. They did not get around to recording the released version until November 11, 1958, when the Midnighters were in Cincinnati.

Ballard thought "The Twist" was the hit side, but [King Records](/wiki/King_Records_(United_States)) producer [Henry Glover](/wiki/Henry_Glover) preferred the ballad "Teardrops on Your Letter", which he had written himself. The song ultimately became the [B-side](/wiki/A-side_and_B-side) of Hank Ballard and The Midnighters' 1959 single "Teardrops on Your Letter".

## Chubby Checker version

The song became popular on a [Baltimore](/wiki/Baltimore) television dance show hosted by local DJ [Buddy Deane](/wiki/Buddy_Deane); Deane recommended the song to [Dick Clark](/wiki/Dick_Clark), host of the national *[American Bandstand](/wiki/American_Bandstand)*, then based in [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia). When the song proved popular with his audience, Clark attempted to book Ballard to perform on the show. Ballard was unavailable, and Clark searched for a local artist to record the song. He settled on Checker, whose voice was very similar to Ballard's. Checker's version featured Buddy Savitt on saxophone and Ellis Tollin on drums, with backing vocals by [the Dreamlovers](/wiki/The_Dreamlovers). Exposure for the song on *American Bandstand* and on *The Dick Clark Saturday Night Show* helped propel the song to the top of the American charts.

In July 1960, Checker performed "The Twist" for the first time in front of a live audience at the Rainbow Club in [Wildwood, New Jersey](/wiki/Wildwood,_New_Jersey), and just weeks later, on August 6, 1960, the song became a national sensation after Checker performed it on Dick Clark's *American Bandstand*. In Canada, his version was co-charted with the Hank Ballard version.

In late 1961 and early 1962, the twist craze belatedly caught on in high society. Sightings of celebrities doing the dance made the song a hit with adults, particularly after a report in the [Cholly Knickerbocker](/wiki/Igor_Cassini) gossip column. Soon there were long lines at the [Peppermint Lounge](/wiki/Peppermint_Lounge) nightclub in New York, the most popular celebrity twisting spot. This new interest made "The Twist" the first recording to hit number one on the United States charts during two separate chart runs, and marked a major turning point for adult acceptance of rock and roll music.

Checker re-recorded the song numerous times. An updated 1982 recording (from his album *The Change Has Come*) was retitled "T-82", and in the 1990s, he recorded a [country](/wiki/Country_music) version. In the late 1970s, he recorded a new version that, except for the sound mix and some minor arrangement changes, was identical to the 1960 original; as a result this later version is often misidentified on compilations as the original recording. In 1988, he joined [The Fat Boys](/wiki/The_Fat_Boys) on a rap version of the song, which hit number 2 in the UK, number 16 in the US, and number 1 in Germany and Switzerland. Checker also joined the group to perform the song that summer at [a London tribute concert](/wiki/Nelson_Mandela_70th_Birthday_Tribute) for [Nelson Mandela](/wiki/Nelson_Mandela). In addition, he recorded variations on the theme, such as "[Let's Twist Again](/wiki/Let%27s_Twist_Again)" to keep the craze alive ("Let's Twist Again" was and has remained more popular than "The Twist" itself in the United Kingdom). [Joey Dee and the Starliters](/wiki/Joey_Dee_and_the_Starliters), the Peppermint Lounge house band, scored a hit with "[Peppermint Twist](/wiki/Peppermint_Twist)", while other artists, including [Sam Cooke](/wiki/Sam_Cooke) scored with other "Twist"-themed songs. In Europe, [Petula Clark](/wiki/Petula_Clark) scored hits in several countries with "Twist"-themed records, while [Bill Haley and His Comets](/wiki/Bill_Haley_and_His_Comets) recorded several albums of Twist songs in [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico) for the [Orfeon Records](/wiki/Orfe%C3%B3n_(Mexican_record_label)) label. In 1997, the song was featured in a Teledyne Waterpik commercial, and a commercial for [Denny's](/wiki/Denny%27s) in 1998, to promote the New Slams.

In the sixth episode of the second season of the TV series *[Quantum Leap](/wiki/Quantum_Leap_(1989_TV_series))*, entitled "Good Morning, Peoria" (set on September 9, 1959), Dr. Sam Beckett ([Scott Bakula](/wiki/Scott_Bakula)) and Al Calavicci ([Dean Stockwell](/wiki/Dean_Stockwell)) meet Chubby Checker (played by himself) in a radio station, where they sing and dance "The Twist". An impressed Checker asks, "Can I use that move?" Sam responds, "Yah, but I got it from you!". The song was used in the 2007 film *[Spider-Man 3](/wiki/Spider-Man_3)* and appears on the film's [official soundtrack album](/wiki/Spider-Man_3_(soundtrack)).

In 1988, American hip-hop trio [Fat Boys](/wiki/Fat_Boys) covered "The Twist" with featured lyrics from Checker and additional rap lyrics written by Jim Glenn and [Damon Wimbley](/wiki/Damon_Wimbley). Produced by Albert Cabrera and [Tony Moran](/wiki/Tony_Moran_(DJ)) (the Latin Rascals), this version reached number two in the United Kingdom and number one in [West Germany](/wiki/West_Germany), as well as number 16 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. The song's music video was produced by Ken Walz and shows the Fat Boys doing the twist with Checker at a [sweet sixteen](/wiki/Sweet_sixteen_(birthday)) party. Checker initially missed the filming in May 1988 due to being hospitalized with [spinal meningitis](/wiki/Spinal_meningitis).

## Accolades

Chubby Checker's "The Twist" held the honor of being the most successful single in *Billboard* history on its various "Greatest Hot 100 Songs of All Time" charts over the decades. It held the title until 2021, when it was dethroned by the Weeknd's "[Blinding Lights](/wiki/Blinding_Lights)".

The song was ranked number 451 on the *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* magazine's list of [The 500 Greatest Songs of All Time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Songs_of_All_Time) in 2004, and number 457 on the revised list in 2010. [Jim Dawson](/wiki/Jim_Dawson) wrote a 1995 book about the song and the Twist phenomenon called *The Twist: The Story of the Song and Dance That Changed the World* for [Faber and Faber](/wiki/Faber_and_Faber).

In 2000, the 1960 recording by [Chubby Checker](/wiki/Chubby_Checker) on [Parkway Records](/wiki/Parkway_Records) was inducted into the [Grammy Hall of Fame](/wiki/Grammy_Hall_of_Fame).

The song has been added to the [National Recording Registry](/wiki/National_Recording_Registry) in the [Library of Congress](/wiki/Library_of_Congress) on March 21, 2013, for long-term preservation. In 2014, *Billboard* magazine declared the song the "biggest hit" of the 1960s.

In 2018, Checker's version was one of six singles inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame) in a new category honoring singles by artists who have not been elected to the Hall.

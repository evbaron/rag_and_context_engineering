# Hot Rock & Alternative Songs

"Rock Songs" redirects here. For information about rock songs, see [Rock music](/wiki/Rock_music).

**Hot Rock & Alternative Songs** (formerly known as **Rock Songs** and **Hot Rock Songs**) is a [record chart](/wiki/Record_chart) published by *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* magazine. From its debut on June 20, 2009, through October 13, 2012, the chart ranked the airplay of songs across [alternative](/wiki/Alternative_rock), [mainstream rock](/wiki/Mainstream_rock), and [triple A](/wiki/Adult_album_alternative) radio stations in the United States. Beginning with the chart dated October 20, 2012, the chart has followed the methodology of the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) by incorporating [digital download](/wiki/Music_download) sales, streaming data, and radio airplay of rock songs over all formats. From that time until mid-2020, only the performance of core rock songs, including those with an "alternative bent", were tabulated and ranked for the chart. With the chart dated June 13, 2020, *Billboard* revamped the chart to permit a broader selection of songs considered alternative "hybrids" with other genres and renamed it to Hot Rock & Alternative Songs.

## Number ones

[Linkin Park](/wiki/Linkin_Park) spent 28 weeks atop the chart with three number-one songs. The band also holds the record for most songs to chart in a single week with 23

[The Black Keys](/wiki/The_Black_Keys)' "[Lonely Boy](/wiki/Lonely_Boy_(The_Black_Keys_song))" spent 14 weeks atop the chart in 2011, and was later ranked as the year-end number-one song

[Gotye](/wiki/Gotye) became the first solo artist to reach the number-one position, with the single "[Somebody That I Used to Know](/wiki/Somebody_That_I_Used_to_Know)" in 2012

[Prince](/wiki/Prince_(musician))'s single "[Purple Rain](/wiki/Purple_Rain_(song))" is the first posthumous number-one single

[Panic! at the Disco](/wiki/Panic!_at_the_Disco)'s single "[High Hopes](/wiki/High_Hopes_(Panic!_at_the_Disco_song))" is the longest running number-one single

[Billie Eilish](/wiki/Billie_Eilish) has spent the most weeks atop the chart for a solo artist and is tied with [Imagine Dragons](/wiki/Imagine_Dragons) with the most number-one songs by an artist with five each. Her 2024 single, "[Birds of a Feather](/wiki/Birds_of_a_Feather_(Billie_Eilish_song))", was the second song, after [Panic! at the Disco](/wiki/Panic!_at_the_Disco)'s "[High Hopes](/wiki/High_Hopes_(Panic!_at_the_Disco_song))", to spend more than a year at number one

The first number-one track on the chart was [Green Day](/wiki/Green_Day)'s "[Know Your Enemy](/wiki/Know_Your_Enemy_(Green_Day_song))". Since its introduction, 79 songs have reached the number-one position on chart. [Imagine Dragons](/wiki/Imagine_Dragons) and [Billie Eilish](/wiki/Billie_Eilish) are tied with the most with five each, with Imagine Dragons having a total of 104 weeks at number one on the chart. [Panic! at the Disco](/wiki/Panic!_at_the_Disco)'s "[High Hopes](/wiki/High_Hopes_(Panic!_at_the_Disco_song))" spent a record 65 non-consecutive weeks atop the chart, and along with their song "[Hey Look Ma, I Made It](/wiki/Hey_Look_Ma,_I_Made_It)", the band claimed the number-one spot on the chart for the entirety of 2019.

# Andrew Lesnie

## Infobox

- **Born:** 1 January 1956 Sydney, New South Wales, Australia
- **Died:** 27 April 2015 (2015-04-27) (aged 59) Sydney, New South Wales, Australia
- **Years active:** 1978–2014

**Andrew Lesnie** [ACS](/wiki/Australian_Cinematographers_Society) [ASC](/wiki/American_Society_of_Cinematographers) (1 January 1956 – 27 April 2015) was an Australian cinematographer.

He was best known for his collaboration with director [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson) on [*The Hobbit* trilogy](/wiki/The_Hobbit_(film_series)) (2012–2014) and [*The Lord of the Rings* trilogy](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_(film_series)) (2001–2003), with *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* earning him the [Academy Award for Best Cinematography](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Cinematography).

## Early life

Lesnie was born in Sydney, New South Wales, on 1 January 1956, the son of Shirley (Lithgow) and Allan Lesnie, who worked for the family's company, butcher suppliers Harry Lesnie Pty Ltd.

He was educated at [Sydney Grammar School](/wiki/Sydney_Grammar_School). Lesnie finished 6th form and his [Higher School Certificate](/wiki/Higher_School_Certificate_(New_South_Wales)) in 1974.

## Career

Lesnie started his career as an assistant camera operator on the film [*Patrick*](/wiki/Patrick_(1978_film)) (1978) while still studying at the [Australian Film, Television and Radio School](/wiki/Australian_Film,_Television_and_Radio_School) (AFTRS).

His first job after graduation in 1979 was as a cameraman on the [Logie Award](/wiki/Logie_Award)-winning Australian magazine-style afternoon TV show *[Simon Townsend's Wonder World](/wiki/Simon_Townsend%27s_Wonder_World)*. Simon Townsend gave Lesnie almost daily opportunities to develop his craft with little restriction over a wide variety of stories and situations, and to experiment with camera and lighting techniques in hundreds of locations and situations. After two years of working on the show, Lesnie moved on to numerous Australian film and television productions, including the mini-series *[Bodyline](/wiki/Bodyline_(miniseries))*.

He later worked as a second camera assistant on the film *[The Killing of Angel Street](/wiki/The_Killing_of_Angel_Street)* (1981).

Lesnie would then go on to develop his craft as he photographed films like *Stations* (1983), [*The Delinquents*](/wiki/The_Delinquents_(1989_film)) (1989), [*Temptation of the Monk*](/wiki/Temptation_of_a_Monk) (1993), and *[Spider and Rose](/wiki/Spider_and_Rose)* (1994).

### *The Lord of the Rings* trilogy (2001–2003)

Lesnie used motion picture camera company [Arri](/wiki/Arri)'s [Arriflex 435](/wiki/Arriflex_435), [Arriflex 535](/wiki/Arriflex_535), and [ArriCam Studio](/wiki/Arricam) [35mm](/wiki/35mm_movie_film) film cameras for the trilogy. He used [Carl Zeiss](/wiki/Carl_Zeiss_AG) Ultra Prime Lenses and [Kodak](/wiki/Eastman_Kodak)'s 5279 (tungsten-balanced) film stock to photograph the films.

He planned far ahead into the production with Jackson with previsualisation programs to help establish frame sizes and angles, as well as construction of sets. During filming, Lesnie emphasised earthy colours in the makeup and wardrobe of the cast and extras.

His work on *[The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* earned him the [Academy Award for Best Cinematography](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Cinematography).

At the acceptance speech, Lesnie dedicated to chief lighting technician [Brian Bansgrove](/wiki/Brian_Bansgrove), who he described as a major contributor to the quality of the film's cinematography.

### *The Hobbit* trilogy (2012–2014)

For production, Lesnie used [Red Digital Cinema](/wiki/Red_Digital_Cinema_Camera_Company)'s Epic cameras as well as [Carl Zeiss](/wiki/Carl_Zeiss_AG) Ultra Prime Lenses to photograph the film. Jackson and Lesnie decided to shoot the film in 3D with as many as 15 [stereoscopic](/wiki/Stereoscopic) camera rigs (2 cameras each) with [3ality](/wiki/3ality_Technica). They also decided to shoot the film in an uncommon frame rate of 48 frames per second versus the industry standard of 24 frames per second. This would make Lesnie the first cinematographer to employ such a method that claims to induce more clarity, reduce motion blur, and make 3D easier to watch.

## Personal life and death

Lesnie lived on Sydney's north coast.

He was a member of both the [Australian Cinematographers Society](/wiki/Australian_Cinematographers_Society) and the [American Society of Cinematographers](/wiki/American_Society_of_Cinematographers).

Lesnie died of a heart attack in his Sydney home on 27 April 2015, after having suffered from a heart condition for half a year.

His final film, *[The Water Diviner](/wiki/The_Water_Diviner)*, was released in Australia in December 2014 and in America in April 2015, one week before his death.

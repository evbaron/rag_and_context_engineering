"**...Baby One More Time**" is a song by American singer [Britney Spears](/wiki/Britney_Spears) from her [debut studio album of the same name](/wiki/...Baby_One_More_Time_(album)) (1999). It was written by [Max Martin](/wiki/Max_Martin) and produced by Martin and [Rami Yacoub](/wiki/Rami_Yacoub). Released as Spears' debut single on September 29, 1998, by [Jive Records](/wiki/Jive_Records), the song became a worldwide success, topping the charts in over 20 countries, including the United States and the United Kingdom, where it earned quintuple and triple-platinum certifications from the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA) and the [British Phonographic Industry](/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry) (BPI), respectively, and was the latter's best-selling single of 1999. A [teen pop](/wiki/Teen_pop) and [dance-pop](/wiki/Dance-pop) song about longing for the return of an ex-boyfriend, "...Baby One More Time" is one of the [best-selling singles of all time](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles), with over 10 million copies sold.

An accompanying music video, directed by [Nigel Dick](/wiki/Nigel_Dick), features Spears as a high-school student who starts to sing and dance around the school, while watching her love interest from afar. In 2010, the music video for "...Baby One More Time" was voted the third most influential video in the history of [pop music](/wiki/Pop_music), in a poll held by [Jam!](/wiki/Jam!). In 2011, "...Baby One More Time" was voted by *Billboard* to be the best music video of the 1990s. It has been featured on all of her greatest hits and other compilation albums. In 2020, *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* named "...Baby One More Time" as the greatest debut single of all time. In 2021, the song was ranked at number 205 on the list of [*Rolling Stone*'s 500 Greatest Songs of All Time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Songs_of_All_Time).

Spears has performed "...Baby One More Time" in a number of live appearances and during all of her concert tours. The song was nominated for [Best Female Pop Vocal Performance](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Pop_Vocal_Performance) at the [42nd Annual Grammy Awards](/wiki/42nd_Annual_Grammy_Awards) (2000), and has been included in lists by *[Blender](/wiki/Blender_(magazine))*, *Rolling Stone* and [VH1](/wiki/VH1). It has been noted for redefining the sound of late [1990s music](/wiki/1990s_in_music). Spears has named "...Baby One More Time" as one of her favorite songs from her catalog. It was also the final song to be played on the [BBC](/wiki/BBC)'s music programme *[Top of the Pops](/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops)* in the 1990s. A cover of the song by Windy Wagner was featured in the 2011 dance video game by [Ubisoft](/wiki/Ubisoft), *[Just Dance 3](/wiki/Just_Dance_3)*. In 2018, readers of German teen magazine *[Bravo](/wiki/Bravo_(magazine))* voted "...Baby One More Time" to be the biggest hit since its first music compilation was released in 1992.

## Background

In June 1997, Britney Spears was in talks with manager [Lou Pearlman](/wiki/Lou_Pearlman) to join female [pop](/wiki/Pop_music) group [Innosense](/wiki/Innosense). Her mother [Lynne Spears](/wiki/Lynne_Spears) asked family friend and entertainment lawyer [Larry Rudolph](/wiki/Larry_Rudolph) for his opinion and submitted a tape of Spears singing over a [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston) karaoke song along with some pictures. Rudolph decided he wanted to pitch her to record labels, therefore she needed a professional demo. He sent Spears an unused song from [Toni Braxton](/wiki/Toni_Braxton); she rehearsed for a week and recorded her vocals in a studio with a [sound engineer](/wiki/Audio_engineering). Spears traveled to New York with the demo and met with executives from four labels, returning to her hometown of [Kentwood, Louisiana](/wiki/Kentwood,_Louisiana) the same day. Three of the labels rejected her, arguing audiences wanted pop bands such as the [Backstreet Boys](/wiki/Backstreet_Boys) and the [Spice Girls](/wiki/Spice_Girls), and "there wasn't going to be another [Madonna](/wiki/Madonna), another [Debbie Gibson](/wiki/Debbie_Gibson), or another [Tiffany](/wiki/Tiffany_Darwish)." Two weeks later, executives from [Jive Records](/wiki/Jive_Records) returned calls to Rudolph. Senior vice president of [A&R](/wiki/Artists_and_repertoire) Jeff Fenster stated about Spears' audition that "It's very rare to hear someone that age who can deliver emotional content and commercial appeal. [...] For any artist, the motivation—the 'eye of the tiger'—is extremely important. And Britney had that." They appointed her to work with producer [Eric Foster White](/wiki/Eric_Foster_White) for a month, who reportedly shaped her voice from "lower and less poppy" delivery to "distinctively, unmistakably Britney." After hearing the recorded material, president [Clive Calder](/wiki/Clive_Calder) ordered a full album. Spears had originally envisioned "[Sheryl Crow](/wiki/Sheryl_Crow) music, but younger more [adult contemporary](/wiki/Adult_contemporary_music)" but felt all right with her label's appointment of producers, since "It made more sense to go pop, because I can dance to it—it's more me."

> I had been in studio for about six months listening and recording material, but I hadn't really heard a hit yet. When I started working with [Max Martin](/wiki/Max_Martin) in Sweden, he played the demo for 'Baby One More Time' for me, and I knew from the start it [was one] of those songs you want to hear again and again. It just felt really right. I went into the studio and did my own thing with it, trying to give it a little more attitude than the demo. In 10 days, I never even saw Sweden. We were so busy.

— Britney Spears, talking to Chuck Taylor, *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))*

Fenster asked producer Max Martin to meet Spears in New York, after which he returned to Sweden to write her a handful of songs with long-term collaborator [Denniz Pop](/wiki/Denniz_Pop). Pop was ill, so Martin asked producer [Rami Yacoub](/wiki/Rami_Yacoub) to help. When six songs were ready, Spears flew to [Cheiron Studios](/wiki/Cheiron_Studios) in [Stockholm](/wiki/Stockholm), Sweden, where half of the album was recorded in May 1998, nominally produced by Martin, Pop and Yacoub. Pop, however, was too ill to attend any of the recording sessions, and Spears never met him. In his place, Martin was the acting producer.

Martin showed Spears and her management a track titled "Hit Me Baby One More Time", which was originally written for American [boy band](/wiki/Boy_band) Backstreet Boys and the [R&B](/wiki/Contemporary_R%26B) [girl group](/wiki/Girl_group) [TLC](/wiki/TLC_(group)), but they both rejected it. The label thought the song would work for the English group [Five](/wiki/Five_(band)), but they also passed on it. Spears later said that she felt excited when she heard it and knew it was going to be a hit record. Jive A&R man Steve Lunt recalled, "We at Jive said, 'This is a fuckin' smash'"; but other executives were concerned that the line "Hit Me" would condone [domestic violence](/wiki/Domestic_violence). The title was revised to "...Baby One More Time".

Spears recorded her vocals for the song in May 1998 at Cheiron Studios. She stayed up late the night before listening to [Soft Cell](/wiki/Soft_Cell)'s "[Tainted Love](/wiki/Tainted_Love)" ("What a sexy song") to get the growl she wanted: "I wanted my voice to be kind of rusty." Spears revealed that she "didn't do well at all the first day in the studio [recording the song], I was just too nervous. So I went out that night and had some fun. The next day I was completely relaxed and nailed it. You gotta be relaxed singing '... Baby One More Time'." The song was produced by Martin and Rami, and was also [mixed](/wiki/Audio_mixing_(recorded_music)) by Martin at Cheiron Studios. Thomas Lindberg played the guitar, while Johan Carlberg played the bass guitar. [Background vocals](/wiki/Background_vocals) were provided by Spears, Martin and [Nana Hedin](/wiki/Nana_Hedin). Denniz Pop was credited as producer even though he was not present for the recording or mixing. Spears also recorded a track called "Autumn Goodbye", written and produced by Eric Foster White, that was released as a [B-side](/wiki/B-side) to "...Baby One More Time". "Autumn Goodbye" was recorded in 1998 at 4MW East Studios in [New Jersey](/wiki/New_Jersey).

"...Baby One More Time" was released by Jive as Spears' debut single on September 29, 1998, when she was only 16 years old. Spears said "...Baby One More Time" was one of her favorite songs in her entire catalog, naming "[Toxic](/wiki/Toxic_(song))" and "He About to Lose Me" as the other two.

## Music and lyrics

"...Baby One More Time" is a [teen pop](/wiki/Teen_pop) and [dance-pop](/wiki/Dance-pop) song that lasts three minutes and thirty seconds. The song is composed in the [key](/wiki/Key_(music)) of [C minor](/wiki/C_minor) and is set in the [time signature](/wiki/Time_signature) of 4/4 [common time](/wiki/Common_time) with a moderate [tempo](/wiki/Tempo) of 93 [beats per minute](/wiki/Beats_per_minute). Songwriting and production is largely based on previous Cheiron productions, most notably [Robyn](/wiki/Robyn)'s "[Show Me Love](/wiki/Show_Me_Love_(Robyn_song))", which shows similar song scheme, drum patterns, wah guitars and piano hits. Spears' vocal range spans over one octave from E♭3 to C5. The song begins with a three-note motif in the bass range of the piano, an opening that has been compared to many other songs, such as "[We Will Rock You](/wiki/We_Will_Rock_You)" (1977), "[Start Me Up](/wiki/Start_Me_Up)" (1981), and the theme song of the 1975 film *[Jaws](/wiki/Jaws_(film))* due to the fact the track "makes its presence known in exactly one second". According to magazine *[Blender](/wiki/Blender_(magazine))*, "...Baby One More Time" is composed by "[wah-wah](/wiki/Wah-wah_(music)) guitar lines and [EKG](/wiki/Electrocardiography)-machine bass-slaps".

Claudia Mitchell and Jacqueline Reid-Walsh, authors of *Girl Culture: Studying girl culture: a readers' guide* (2008), noted the lyrics of the song "gesture toward [Spears] longing for the return of an ex-boyfriend." Spears said "...Baby One More Time" is a song "every girl can relate to. She regrets it. She wants him back." The lyrics, however, caused controversy in the United States, because the line "Hit me baby one more time" supposedly has [sadomasochistic](/wiki/Sadomasochism) connotations. As a response, the singer said the line "doesn't mean physically hit me. [...] It means just give me a sign, basically. I think it's kind of funny that people would actually think that's what it meant." Music journalist John Seabrook has said "Everybody thought it was some sort of weird allusion to domestic violence or something. But what it really was, was the Swedes using English not exactly correctly. What they really wanted to say was, "hit me up on the phone one more time" or something. But at that point, Max's English wasn't that great. So it came out sounding a little bit weird in English."

## Critical reception

Spears and some of her dancers performing "...Baby One More Time" during [The Circus Starring Britney Spears](/wiki/The_Circus_Starring_Britney_Spears) in 2009

Marc Oxoby, author of *The 1990s* (2003), noted the song "was derided as vapid by some critics, yet tapped into the same kind of audience to whom the Spice Girls music appealed, young teens and pre-teens." Amanda Murray of Sputnikmusic commented, "[" ... Baby One More Time" is] well-composed, tightly arranged, and even with Spears' vocal limitations it goes straight for the proverbial pop jugular." She also said that the song was a highlight in the pop music genre and added, "There is little doubt that '...Baby One More Time' will be long remembered as one of the cornerstones of pop music in general, and it is a strong front-runner as the prototype for the late 90s pop resurgence." Bill Lamb of [About.com](/wiki/About.com) considered "...Baby One More Time" as Spears' best song, saying, "the song is full of hooks and a big mainstream pop sound. The accompanying schoolgirl video caused a sensation, and, when the single hit No. 1, Britney was assured of stardom." In a list compiled by Sara Anderson of [AOL Radio](/wiki/AOL_Radio), "...Baby One More Time" was ranked sixth in a list of Spears' best songs. She noted the singer "somehow made the school girl outfit and pink pom-pom hair-ties trendy again, worn by every tween in the succeeding years."

[Larry Flick](/wiki/Larry_Flick) of *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* wrote, "Produced by famed [Euro-pop](/wiki/Europop)sters Max Martin and Eric Foster, "Baby, One More Time" chugs with an insinuating faux-[funk](/wiki/Funk) beat and super-shiny synths. Spears has a charming [kewpie](/wiki/Kewpie)-doll voice that has a [soulful](/wiki/Soul_music) quality that leaves the listener intrigued and wondering where she'll go with time and experience." Beth Johnson of *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* called it a "candy-pop-with-a-funky-edge smash", while [Stephen Thomas Erlewine](/wiki/Stephen_Thomas_Erlewine) of [AllMusic](/wiki/AllMusic) said the song was "ingenious", Brian Raftery of *[Blender](/wiki/Blender_(magazine))* called it "a perfectly fine, slickly conceived pop tune. [..] At the time, teen-pop was still a boys' club, but while the guys were crooning about crushes, Spears was already planning the sleep-over party". In 2009, [Jody Rosen](/wiki/Jody_Rosen) of *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* called it "some of the best radio pop of the past decade-plus". *[NME](/wiki/NME)* considered "...Baby One More Time" "incredible", commenting that "it's a symphony of teenage lust as fully realised as anything [Brian Wilson](/wiki/Brian_Wilson) ever wrote—a truly grand pop song that overwhelms any lingering undercurrent of *[Lolita](/wiki/Lolita)* paedo-creepiness through the sheer fanatical earnestness of its delivery." "...Baby One More Time" won a [Teen Choice Award for Single of the Year](/wiki/Teen_Choice_Award_for_Choice_Music_%E2%80%93_Single) at the [1999 Teen Choice Awards](/wiki/1999_Teen_Choice_Awards) and an [MTV Europe Music Award for Best Song](/wiki/MTV_Europe_Music_Award_for_Best_Song) at the [1999 MTV Europe Music Awards](/wiki/1999_MTV_Europe_Music_Awards).

## Commercial performance

The encore performance of "...Baby One More Time" during the [Dream Within a Dream Tour](/wiki/Dream_Within_a_Dream_Tour), with Spears standing in the water screen, 2001

The song was officially sent to [contemporary hit](/wiki/Contemporary_hit_radio) and [rhythmic contemporary](/wiki/Rhythmic_contemporary) radio on September 29, 1998. On November 21, 1998, "...Baby One More Time" debuted on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) at number 17 and topped the chart two and a half months later for two consecutive weeks, replacing R&B singer [Brandy](/wiki/Brandy_Norwood)'s "[Have You Ever?](/wiki/Have_You_Ever%3F)". Simultaneously, it climbed to number one on the [Canadian Singles Chart](/wiki/Canadian_Singles_Chart). The song reached the top spot of the [Hot 100 Singles Sales](/wiki/Hot_100_Singles_Sales) and stayed there for four consecutive weeks. This eventually propelled the single to a [platinum certification](/wiki/Platinum_single) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America). Though not as strong as its sales tallies, "...Baby One More Time" also experienced considerable airplay, becoming her first top ten hit on the [Hot 100 Airplay](/wiki/Hot_100_Airplay), peaking at number eight. The single also became an all-around hit on [Top 40 radio](/wiki/Top_40_radio), going top ten on both the [Top 40 Tracks](/wiki/Top_40_Tracks) and [Rhythmic Top 40](/wiki/Rhythmic_Top_40), and to number one for five weeks on the [Mainstream Top 40](/wiki/Mainstream_Top_40). It spent 32 weeks on the Hot 100 and ended up at number five on *Billboard*'s year-end chart. As of June 2012, "...Baby One More Time" has sold 1,412,000 physical singles, with 511,000 paid digital downloads in the United States. It is Spears' best-selling physical single in the country. "...Baby One More Time" debuted on January 31, 1999, at number 20 on the [Australian Singles Chart](/wiki/ARIA_Charts), a month later reached number one and stayed there for nine consecutive weeks. The song eventually became the second [highest-selling single of the year](/wiki/List_of_Top_25_singles_for_1999_in_Australia), only behind [Lou Bega](/wiki/Lou_Bega)'s "[Mambo No. 5](/wiki/Mambo_No._5)", and was certified three-times platinum by the [Australian Recording Industry Association](/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association) for selling over 210,000 copies. In New Zealand, the single spent four non-consecutive weeks at the top of the charts and after shipping over 15,000 units to retailers the [Recording Industry Association of New Zealand](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_New_Zealand) certified it platinum.

The track reached the top spot in the majority of countries in which it charted. "...Baby One More Time" spent two consecutive weeks at number-one on the [French Singles Chart](/wiki/French_Singles_Chart) and was certified platinum by the [Syndicat National de l'Édition Phonographique](/wiki/Syndicat_National_de_l%27%C3%89dition_Phonographique) after selling over 500,000 units in the country. Additionally, the song topped the [German Singles Chart](/wiki/Media_Control_Charts) for six consecutive weeks and sold over 750,000 copies, resulting in a three-times gold certification by the [International Federation of the Phonographic Industry](/wiki/International_Federation_of_the_Phonographic_Industry). In the United Kingdom, according to Jive Records, "...Baby One More Time" sold more than 250,000 copies in a mere three days after its February 1999 release. Spears broke a first-week sales record for a female act in the UK at the time when "...Baby One More Time" sold a total of 460,000 copies. Eventually, the [British Phonographic Industry](/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry) certified it two-times platinum on March 26, 1999. The single went on to sell over 1,445,000 units by the end of 1999, making it the [highest-selling single of that year](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles_by_year_in_the_United_Kingdom) and the [8th biggest song of the 1990s](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles_of_the_1990s_in_the_United_Kingdom). As of 2018, it is the [32nd best-seller of all time in the UK](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles_in_the_United_Kingdom). The song has sold two million units in the country as of August 2022. Additionally, "...Baby One More Time" is the fifth best-selling single by a female artist in the country, behind [Cher](/wiki/Cher)'s "[Believe](/wiki/Believe_(Cher_song))", Whitney Houston's "[I Will Always Love You](/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You#Whitney_Houston_version)", [Adele](/wiki/Adele)'s "[Someone like You](/wiki/Someone_like_You_(Adele_song))" and [Céline Dion](/wiki/C%C3%A9line_Dion)'s "[My Heart Will Go On](/wiki/My_Heart_Will_Go_On)". "...Baby One More Time" is one of the [best-selling singles of all time](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles), with over 10 million copies sold worldwide. As of May 2020, "...Baby One More Time" has generated over 285 million streams in the US.

## Music video

### Background

The music video was directed by [Nigel Dick](/wiki/Nigel_Dick). After being chosen, Dick received criticism from his colleagues about wanting to work with Spears. He responded saying, "It's a great song. I don't know anything about Britney. I never watched *[The Mickey Mouse Club](/wiki/The_Mickey_Mouse_Club#1990s_revival,_The_All-New_Mickey_Mouse_Club_(MMC))*. She seems like a great kid and she's very enthusiastic, but I just love the song. It's just a great song". The video's original setup was very different from what eventually became the final product. The plan was to have the video in a [cartoon-like](/wiki/Cartoon) environment, a likely attempt to attract an audience of younger children. Spears was unhappy with this, and argued that she wanted her video to reflect the lives of her fans and wanted to set the video in a school. Spears pitched this idea to Dick, and explained she wanted the video to have dance scenes. The original setting was scrapped and replaced with Spears' concept. Dick's first idea for the wardrobe was jeans and a T-shirt, but during the wardrobe fitting Spears asked for a [schoolgirl](/wiki/Student) outfit. Dick said that "Every piece of wardrobe in the video came from [Kmart](/wiki/Kmart_(United_States)), and I was told at the time not one piece of clothing in the video cost more than $17. On that level, it's real. That probably, in retrospect, is a part of its charm." The knotted shirt was Spears' idea, she recollects, saying, "The outfits looked kind of dorky, so I was like, 'Let's tie up our shirts and be cute'". About the experience of shooting her first music video, Spears said, "It was a wonderful experience. All these people there, working for you. I had my own trailer. It was an amazing experience". The music video was shot on August 7 and 8, 1998, at [Venice High School](/wiki/Venice_High_School_(Los_Angeles)), the same school used to shoot the 1978 film *[Grease](/wiki/Grease_(film))*. The video premiered on [MTV](/wiki/MTV) and other video stations on November 26, 1998.

### Synopsis

Spears wearing the schoolgirl outfit in the song's music video

The video begins with Spears appearing bored in class at high school. Her assistant Felicia Culotta played the role of Spears' teacher. When the bell rings, Spears runs out into the hall and begins a choreographed dance in the corridor. After this, Spears is outside, now adorned in a pink athletic outfit, and seen in a car. Along with a couple of other students, she performs a number of [gymnastic](/wiki/Gymnastic) moves before heading back inside. She is then sitting on the [bleachers](/wiki/Bleacher) in the gymnasium watching a basketball game, and she dances in the gymnasium. Her love interest is revealed sitting close to her, played by her real-life cousin Chad.

### Reception

The schoolgirl outfit is considered to be one of Spears' most iconic looks as well as amongst the hallmarks of [pop culture](/wiki/Popular_culture). It is on display at the [Hard Rock Hotel and Casino](/wiki/Hard_Rock_Hotel_and_Casino_(Las_Vegas)) in [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley), [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada). The ensemble caused controversy among parents associations for showing the [midriff](/wiki/Midriff) of a sixteen-year-old. Spears faced the criticism saying, "Me showing my belly? I'm from [the South](/wiki/Southern_United_States); you're stupid if you don't wear a sports bra [when you] go to dance class, you're going to be sweating your butt off." In 1999, "...Baby One More Time" earned Spears her first three [MTV Video Music Award](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Awards) nominations, in the categories of [Best Pop Video](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Pop_Video), [Best Choreography](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Choreography), and [Best Female Video](/wiki/MTV_Video_Music_Award_for_Best_Female_Video) at the [1999 MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/1999_MTV_Video_Music_Awards). In a list compiled by [VH1](/wiki/VH1) in 2001, it was listed at number ninety in the best videos of all time. The video was the first of fourteen of her videos to retire on MTV's television series *[Total Request Live](/wiki/Total_Request_Live)* (TRL). On its final episode, a three-hour special aired on November 16, 2008, "...Baby One More Time" was number one in their final countdown as the most iconic music video of all time and was the last video to be played on the show. [Wesley Yang](/wiki/Wesley_Yang) in his essay "Inside the Box" in *[n+1](/wiki/N%2B1)*, compared the music video to [Britny Fox](/wiki/Britny_Fox)'s "Girlschool" because it featured "a classroom full of Catholic schoolgirls gyrating to the beat in defiance of a stern teacher. [..] But that was a sexist video by a horrible [hair metal](/wiki/Glam_metal) band that exploited women. Britney Spears was something else—an inflection point in the culture". The music video is also referenced in the video for Spears' 2009 single, "[If U Seek Amy](/wiki/If_U_Seek_Amy)". After Spears comes out of her house dressed as a housewife, her daughter is dressed with a similar schoolgirl outfit while wearing pink ribbons in her hair. The video was ranked at number four on a list of the ten most controversial music videos in pop by [AOL](/wiki/AOL) on September 29, 2011. *Rolling Stone* placed "...Baby One More Time" at number 30 on [its list of the 100 greatest music videos of all time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Music_Videos_of_All_Time).

## Live performances

Spears and some of her dancers performing "...Baby One More Time" during the [Femme Fatale Tour](/wiki/Femme_Fatale_Tour), 2011

The first live performance of the song was at the Singapore Jazz Festival in Singapore on May 16, 1998. That day, Spears also performed the song "[Sometimes](/wiki/Sometimes_(Britney_Spears_song))" for the first time. Spears performed "...Baby One More Time" on several occasions. She performed the song on July 6, 1999, during her appearance in [Bethel, New York](/wiki/Bethel,_New_York) at the original site of [Woodstock](/wiki/Woodstock). [Neil Strauss](/wiki/Neil_Strauss), from *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, noted that "all the backing music was on tape, and most of the vocals were recorded, with Ms. Spears just reinforcing selected words in choruses and singing an occasional snippet of a verse". It was also performed at the 1999 MTV Video Music Awards; after a classroom [roll call](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/roll_call) ended, Spears appeared on the stage and began performing the song. Halfway through, she was joined by [Justin Timberlake](/wiki/Justin_Timberlake) and the members of [NSYNC](/wiki/NSYNC) for a dance routine. Afterwards, the band performed their hit "[Tearin' Up My Heart](/wiki/Tearin%27_Up_My_Heart)". The song was also performed at the 1999 MTV Europe Music Awards, along with "[(You Drive Me) Crazy](/wiki/(You_Drive_Me)_Crazy)", the [1999 *Billboard* Music Awards](/wiki/1999_Billboard_Music_Awards), the 1999 [Smash Hits Poll Winners Party](/wiki/Smash_Hits_Poll_Winners_Party), the Christmas Day edition of *[Top of the Pops](/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops)* and the Greenwich Millennium concert on December 31, 1999. She also performed it with broadcasters [David Dimbleby](/wiki/David_Dimbleby) and [Michael Buerk](/wiki/Michael_Buerk) on *[2000 Today](/wiki/2000_Today)*. Spears performed the song in a medley with "[From the Bottom of My Broken Heart](/wiki/From_the_Bottom_of_My_Broken_Heart)" at the [42nd Grammy Awards](/wiki/42nd_Grammy_Awards). Spears was wearing a turtleneck and a full tulle skirt at the beginning of the performance, while dancers surrounded her with enormous [hand fans](/wiki/Hand_fan). After singing a shortened version of the song, she then took a few moments to shuffle into a form-fitting red rhinestone outfit (with side cutouts) and emerged onto a stage to perform "...Baby One More Time." Spears was also criticized of lipsynching the song during her performance. Later, in 2003, Spears performed the song in a remixed form at *[Britney Spears: In the Zone](/wiki/Britney_Spears:_In_the_Zone)*, a concert special that aired in [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) on November 17, 2003. "...Baby One More Time" was also performed at the [2003 NFL Kickoff Live](/wiki/National_Football_League_Kickoff_game) on September 4, 2003, at the [National Mall](/wiki/National_Mall), in a medley with "[I'm a Slave 4 U](/wiki/I%27m_a_Slave_4_U)" (2001), which included [pyrotechnics](/wiki/Pyrotechnics). She sported shoulder-length blond hair and was dressed in black football pants, a black-and-white referee halter top and boots from [Reebok](/wiki/Reebok). Her outfit was later auctioned off to benefit the Britney Spears Foundation.

"...Baby One More Time" has been performed in seven of Spears' concert tours since its release. On 1999's [...Baby One More Time Tour](/wiki/...Baby_One_More_Time_Tour), the encore consisted of a performance of the song, in which Spears wore a black bra under pink halter, a pink sequined plaid mini-skirt, and black [thigh-high stockings](/wiki/Thigh-high_stockings). On 2000's [Oops!... I Did It Again Tour](/wiki/Oops!..._I_Did_It_Again_Tour), "...Baby One More Time" was performed after a dance interlude in which the dancers showed their individual moves while their names appeared on the screens. Spears took the stage in a conservative schoolgirl outfit to perform the song. She ripped it off halfway through the song to reveal a [cheerleader](/wiki/Cheerleader) ensemble. The song was also the encore of 2001's [Dream Within a Dream Tour](/wiki/Dream_Within_a_Dream_Tour). It began with a giant projection of a hologram of Spears onto a water screen. The projection gradually shrank until Spears rose from the stage while wearing a plastic cowboy hat, blue hip-huggers, and a matching bra top. She began performing "...Baby One More Time" in a ballad version until reaching the end of the runway. Pyrotechnics surrounded the stage while the song changed to a more uptempo version with elements of [techno](/wiki/Techno).

On [The Onyx Hotel Tour](/wiki/The_Onyx_Hotel_Tour), after performing "[Showdown](/wiki/In_the_Zone)", a video interlude followed featuring Spears and her friends outside a club. While she was leaving, she noticed a woman dressed in [1930s fashion](/wiki/1930%E2%80%9345_in_fashion). She followed her and the woman asked Spears to enter the "Mystic Lounge". Spears reappeared wearing a corset to perform "...Baby One More Time" along with "[Oops!...I Did It Again](/wiki/Oops!...I_Did_It_Again_(song))" and "(You Drive Me) Crazy". All of the three were reworked for the show with elements of [jazz](/wiki/Jazz) and [blues](/wiki/Blues). "...Baby One More Time" was also performed on the promotional tour made on some [House of Blues](/wiki/House_of_Blues) locations, called [The M+M's Tour](/wiki/The_M%2BM%27s_Tour). The show started with Spears singing a short version of the song dressed in a white [go-go boots](/wiki/Go-go_boot), a white [miniskirt](/wiki/Miniskirt) and a sparkling pink [bikini](/wiki/Bikini) top. On [The Circus Starring Britney Spears](/wiki/The_Circus_Starring_Britney_Spears), the song made into the Electro Circ act. It was the final song of the act, performed after "[Toxic](/wiki/Toxic_(song))". The performance consisted on Spears and her dancers performing a remix of the song. On 2011's [Femme Fatale Tour](/wiki/Femme_Fatale_Tour), "...Baby One More Time" was performed in a medley with the remix of [Rihanna](/wiki/Rihanna)'s "[S&M](/wiki/S%26M_(song))" (2010). At Spears' residency show [Britney: Piece of Me](/wiki/Britney:_Piece_of_Me) in Las Vegas, the song was included on its setlist.

## Cover versions, samples and usage in media

[Lea Michele](/wiki/Lea_Michele) from the cast of *[Glee](/wiki/Glee_(TV_series))* covered the song in the episode "[Britney/Brittany](/wiki/Britney/Brittany)". The episode featured an appearance made by Spears herself.

"...Baby One More Time" has been covered on numerous occasions. One of the earliest live covers of the song was by the Scottish band [Travis](/wiki/Travis_(band)), recorded during one of their concerts in [Robin Hood's Bay](/wiki/Robin_Hood%27s_Bay), [North Yorkshire](/wiki/North_Yorkshire), England. The song was later included in the release of their 1999 single, "[Turn](/wiki/Turn_(Travis_song))". Lead singer Francis Healey said, "We did it for a laugh the first time. [..] And as we played it, the irony slipped from my smile. It's a very well-crafted song. It [has] that magic thing." *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* said this cover showed a new and more "dark" side of the band, commenting "slowed down to a mournful crawl, it was amazing how ominous the couplet "This loneliness is killing me / Hit me, baby, one more time" sounded". PopWreckoning.com called it "perhaps the most well done cover of Britney's catalyst to eternal fame". Spears heard their version while shopping in a mall and said, "It was so weird. I liked it though, I thought it was cool. It was a very different vibe from what I did".
In July 2005, [the Dresden Dolls](/wiki/The_Dresden_Dolls) performed a cover during their summer concerts while opening for [Panic! at the Disco](/wiki/Panic!_at_the_Disco). On July 18, 2006, frontman [Brendon Urie](/wiki/Brendon_Urie) joined the band to perform the song in [Pittsburgh](/wiki/Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania. PopWreckoning.com said the cover was "a strange twist to this pop ditty. It's obviously darker and actually tortured as opposed to Britney's school girl despair".

On July 13, 2009, [Tori Amos](/wiki/Tori_Amos) covered the song live during her [Sinful Attraction Tour](/wiki/Sinful_Attraction_Tour) at the [Paramount Theatre](/wiki/Paramount_Theatre_(Oakland,_California)) in [Oakland, California](/wiki/Oakland,_California). On October 15, that year, [Kris Allen](/wiki/Kris_Allen) covered the song for the first time at a concert in [Seton High School](/wiki/Seton_High_School_(Cincinnati,_Ohio)) in [Cincinnati](/wiki/Cincinnati), Ohio. His rendition received positive reviews. The song is heard, prominently but briefly, during the climactic fight-scene in the 2005 animated film *[Robots](/wiki/Robots_(2005_film))*. Fender Pinwheeler (voiced by [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams)), while wearing a skirt, was busy fighting to the beat of the song. An excerpt was used in the [comedy film](/wiki/Comedy_film), but was not included on the [soundtrack album](/wiki/Robots_(2005_soundtrack)). In 2000, British [death metal](/wiki/Death_metal) cover band Ten Masked Men included a rendition of the song on their *Return of the Ten Masked Men* album. A cover by [Ahmet](/wiki/Ahmet_Zappa) and [Dweezil Zappa](/wiki/Dweezil_Zappa) was featured in the soundtrack of the 2000 film *[Ready to Rumble](/wiki/Ready_to_Rumble)*. The song was covered twice in [Fearless Records](/wiki/Fearless_Records)' *[Punk Goes...](/wiki/Punk_Goes...)* compilation album series, first by [pop punk](/wiki/Pop-punk) band Nicotine for 2002's *[Punk Goes Pop](/wiki/Punk_Goes_Pop)* and then by [metalcore](/wiki/Metalcore) band [August Burns Red](/wiki/August_Burns_Red) for its 2009 successor, *[Punk Goes Pop Volume Two](/wiki/Punk_Goes_Pop_Volume_Two)*. In 2003, the song was covered by American pop punk band [Bowling for Soup](/wiki/Bowling_for_Soup) for the [soundtrack](/wiki/Freaky_Friday_(soundtrack)) of the film *[Freaky Friday](/wiki/Freaky_Friday_(2003_film))* and commented that their version was "really, really, dark and really [rock](/wiki/Rock_music), [..] not the kind of 'pop'-py stuff that we usually do." In 2005, [power pop](/wiki/Power_pop) band [Fountains of Wayne](/wiki/Fountains_of_Wayne) covered the song for their compilation album *[Out-of-State Plates](/wiki/Out-of-State_Plates)*. [Robert Christgau](/wiki/Robert_Christgau) of *[The Village Voice](/wiki/The_Village_Voice)* highlighted their rendition saying the song is "as redolent and fetching as any of [Fountains of Wayne]'s peaks".

In the 2010 *[Glee](/wiki/Glee_(TV_series))* [season 2](/wiki/Glee_season_2) episode "[Britney/Brittany](/wiki/Britney/Brittany)", the character of [Rachel Berry](/wiki/Rachel_Berry), played by [Lea Michele](/wiki/Lea_Michele), covered the song using similar outfits to the ones of the music video. Spears also made a cameo, taking the teacher's role, previously played by Cullota.
In the 2010 [Nickelodeon](/wiki/Nickelodeon) film, *[The Boy Who Cried Werewolf](/wiki/The_Boy_Who_Cried_Werewolf_(2010_film))*, the song was featured in the end credits performed by the actors as their respective characters. [Darren Criss](/wiki/Darren_Criss) also of *Glee* performed a mash-up of "...Baby One More Time" with "[Für Elise](/wiki/F%C3%BCr_Elise)" on *Sing Out, Raise Hope* for [The Trevor Project](/wiki/The_Trevor_Project) and the [Elizabeth Glaser Pediatric AIDS Foundation](/wiki/Elizabeth_Glaser_Pediatric_AIDS_Foundation) in December 2011. In 2012, British singer [Ed Sheeran](/wiki/Ed_Sheeran) performed an acoustic version of the song on [NOW 100.5 FM](/wiki/KZZO). A year later he covered it on [Z100](/wiki/WHTZ)'s *[Elvis Duran and the Morning Show](/wiki/Elvis_Duran_and_the_Morning_Show)* and added a [rap](/wiki/Hip-hop) verse. The song is sung by actresses [Selena Gomez](/wiki/Selena_Gomez), [Vanessa Hudgens](/wiki/Vanessa_Hudgens), [Ashley Benson](/wiki/Ashley_Benson) and [Rachel Korine](/wiki/Rachel_Korine) in [Harmony Korine](/wiki/Harmony_Korine)'s film *[Spring Breakers](/wiki/Spring_Breakers)*. Swedish singer [Tove Styrke](/wiki/Tove_Styrke) released a cover of the song on July 24, 2015. [Charli XCX](/wiki/Charli_XCX) and [Troye Sivan](/wiki/Troye_Sivan) referenced the song on their single "[1999](/wiki/1999_(Charli_XCX_and_Troye_Sivan_song))" on XCX's album *[Charli](/wiki/Charli_(album))*. [Alternative rock](/wiki/Alternative_rock) band [Slothrust](/wiki/Slothrust) recorded a cover of the song as part of their 2017 EP *Show Me How You Want It to Be.* [Anne Marie](/wiki/Anne-Marie_(singer)) also references the song on her single "[2002](/wiki/2002_(song))". The music video also pays homage to Spears. Both singles were released in 2018. In 2020, Italian [symphonic death metal](/wiki/Symphonic_metal) band [Fleshgod Apocalypse](/wiki/Fleshgod_Apocalypse) paid homage to the song in their single "No", slightly interpolating the chorus lines and melody near the end of the track with altered lyrics.

In 2018, the sitcom *[Superstore](/wiki/Superstore_(TV_series))* also used "Baby, One More Time" in [season 4](/wiki/Superstore_season_4), episode 2, "Baby Shower", during a scene of Amy Sosa ([America Ferrera](/wiki/America_Ferrera))'s baby shower. In addition, the song was also used in the trailer of *[Hitman's Wife's Bodyguard](/wiki/Hitman%27s_Wife%27s_Bodyguard)* in 2021. In 2022, the song was re-versioned for the Mexican [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) television series *[Rebelde](/wiki/Rebelde_(2022_TV_series))*. It was performed by Brazilian actress Giovanna Grigio and Mexican actor [Alejandro Puente](/wiki/Alejandro_Puente) and was included in the soundtrack of the series released on January 5, 2022, by [Sony Music Mexico](/wiki/Sony_Music_Mexico).

The song appears in the 2019 Max Martin [jukebox musical](/wiki/Jukebox_musical) *[& Juliet](/wiki/%26_Juliet)* where it was performed by [Juliet Capulet](/wiki/Juliet) in Act 1.

A [rock](/wiki/Rock_music) cover of the song by [Tenacious D](/wiki/Tenacious_D) is featured in the end credits of the 2024 animated film *[Kung Fu Panda 4](/wiki/Kung_Fu_Panda_4)*, which stars Tenacious D frontman [Jack Black](/wiki/Jack_Black) in the titular role of [Po](/wiki/Po_(Kung_Fu_Panda)).

In 2025, South Korean singer [Seulgi](/wiki/Seulgi) sampled the guitar riffs from the song, for her comeback single "Baby, Not Baby".

## Legacy

> "One of those pop manifestos that announces a new sound, a new era, a new century. But most of all, a new star. [...] '...Baby One More Time' is an apocalyptic thunder-clap of a song, with Max Martin's mega-boom production [...]. In the great tradition of debut singles, it was a divisive statement that drew a line between past and future. [...] With '...Baby One More Time,' [Spears] changed the sound of pop forever."

— *Rolling Stone*'s Rob Sheffield commenting on the song's impact.

In 2020, *Rolling Stone* ranked the song at number one on a list of the 100 Greatest Debut Singles of All Time. In 2021, the same magazine ranked the song at number 205 on their updated list of [500 Greatest Songs of All Time.](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Songs_of_All_Time) *Billboard*'s Robert Kelly observed that Spears' "sexy and coy" vocals on the track "kicked off a new era of pop vocal stylings that would influence countless artists to come." "...Baby One More Time" was listed at number twenty five in the greatest pop songs since 1963, in a list compiled by *Rolling Stone* and MTV in 2000. *[Blender](/wiki/Blender_(magazine))* listed it at number nine in the 500 Greatest Songs Since You Were Born. The song was also listed as the 2nd best song of the 1990s by VH1 and in a listing compiled in 2003, ranked at number one in 100 Best Songs of the Past 25 Years. Bill Lamb of About.com ranked "...Baby One More Time" at number one on a compiled list with the Top 40 Pop Songs Of All Time. The music video was voted the third most influential promo in the history of pop music on a poll held by [Jam!](/wiki/Jam!). "...Baby One More Time" is also one of the [best-selling singles of all time](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles), with over 9 million copies sold, and also earned Spears' first nomination for a [Grammy Award for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Pop_Vocal_Performance). In April 2005, the British TV network [ITV](/wiki/ITV_(TV_network)) aired a short series called *[Hit Me, Baby, One More Time](/wiki/Hit_Me,_Baby,_One_More_Time_(TV_series))* hosted by [Vernon Kay](/wiki/Vernon_Kay). The show pitted [one-hit wonders](/wiki/One-hit_wonder) who generally had their moments of fame in the 1980s against each other to play their own hits and a currently popular cover song. The favorites were chosen by audience voting. The American version of the show also aired on [NBC](/wiki/NBC) later in the year, and it was also hosted by Kay. In the 2012 poll created by [The Official Charts Company](/wiki/The_Official_Charts_Company) and [ITV](/wiki/ITV_(TV_network)) to discover [The Nation's Favourite Number 1 Single](/wiki/The_Nation%27s_Favourite) of all time, "...Baby One More Time" was listed as the seventh favorite song by [the United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom). In 2018, readers of German teen magazine [Bravo](/wiki/Bravo_(magazine)) voted "...Baby One More Time" to be the biggest hit since its first music compilation was released in 1992. In 2021, *Billboard* and *[American Songwriter](/wiki/American_Songwriter)* both ranked the song number two on their lists of the greatest Britney Spears songs.

Spears led the teen pop pack of [Christina Aguilera](/wiki/Christina_Aguilera), [Jessica Simpson](/wiki/Jessica_Simpson) and [Mandy Moore](/wiki/Mandy_Moore), who were all seen as "pop princesses" gaining chart success in 1999. These four performers had each been developing material in 1998, but "...Baby One More Time" changed the market in December, opening the door for the others. *Rolling Stone* wrote that Spears "spearheaded the rise of post-millennial teen pop ... Spears early on cultivated a mixture of innocence and experience that generated lots of cash". Barbara Ellen of *[The Observer](/wiki/The_Observer)* has reported: "Spears is famously one of the 'oldest' teenagers pop has ever produced, almost middle aged in terms of focus and determination. Many 19-year-olds haven't even started working by that age, whereas Britney, a former Mouseketeer, was that most unusual and volatile of American phenomena—a child with a full-time career. While other little girls were putting posters on their walls, Britney was wanting to be the poster on the wall. Whereas other children develop at their own pace, Britney was developing at a pace set by the ferociously competitive American entertainment industry".

Scott Plagenhoef of *[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website))* noted: "songs like [Nirvana](/wiki/Nirvana_(band))'s "[Smells Like Teen Spirit](/wiki/Smells_Like_Teen_Spirit)", [Dr. Dre](/wiki/Dr._Dre)'s "[Nuthin' But a 'G' Thang](/wiki/Nuthin%27_but_a_%27G%27_Thang)", and Britney Spears' " ... Baby One More Time" altered the landscape of pop culture so quickly in large part because they were delivered to all corners of the U.S. simultaneously by MTV. ... MTV's ability to place a song and musician into the pop music conversation was unparalleled at the time, and by the end of the decade that meant absurd levels of both financial and creative commitment to music videos." *[PopMatters](/wiki/PopMatters)*'s writer Evan Sawdey commented that Spears' concept for the song's music video was the one responsible for her immediate success, saying that, as a result, the singer "scored a massive No. 1 single, inadvertently started the late '90s teen pop boom, and created a public persona for herself that was simultaneously kid-friendly and pure male fantasy. Her videos got played on both MTV and the [Disney Channel](/wiki/Disney_Channel) at the same time, showing just how well Spears (and her armies of PR handlers) managed to walk that fine line between family-friendly pop idol and unabashed sex object."

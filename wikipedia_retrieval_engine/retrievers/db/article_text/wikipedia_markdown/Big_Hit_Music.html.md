**Big Hit Music** ([Korean](/wiki/Korean_language): 빅히트 뮤직, stylized in [all caps](/wiki/All_caps)), formerly **Big Hit Entertainment** ([Korean](/wiki/Korean_language): 빅히트 엔터테인먼트), is a South Korean [record label](/wiki/Record_label) established in 2005 by [Bang Si-hyuk](/wiki/Bang_Si-hyuk). In March 2021, it was re-branded and re-structured into an independent label under [Hybe Corporation](/wiki/Hybe_Corporation) (formerly Big Hit Entertainment Co., Ltd.). As of date, the label is home to soloist [Lee Hyun](/wiki/Lee_Hyun), and boy groups [BTS](/wiki/BTS), [Tomorrow X Together](/wiki/Tomorrow_X_Together), and [Cortis](/wiki/Cortis). It previously managed soloist [Lim Jeong-hee](/wiki/Lim_Jeong-hee), and groups [8Eight](/wiki/8Eight), [2AM](/wiki/2AM_(band)), and co-managed [Glam](/wiki/Glam_(group)).

## History

### 2005–2021: Big Hit Entertainment

Big Hit Entertainment's logo from 2005 to 2021

Big Hit Entertainment was founded on February 1, 2005, and signed the trio [8Eight](/wiki/8Eight) in 2007. In 2010, the company signed a joint management contract with [JYP Entertainment](/wiki/JYP_Entertainment) over the boy group [2AM](/wiki/2AM_(band)). That year, Bang Si-hyuk signed [RM](/wiki/RM_(rapper)) as the first member of [BTS](/wiki/BTS) and launched nationwide auditions to recruit other members of the group—BTS made their debut under Big Hit on June 13, 2013.

In 2012, the company signed [Lim Jeong-hee](/wiki/Lim_Jeong-hee), and formed the girl group [Glam](/wiki/Glam_(group)) as a collaboration with Source Music. The group was active until 2014 when it was disbanded due to a controversy involving one of its members, Kim Da-hee—Kim was sentenced to prison after being found guilty of blackmailing actor [Lee Byung-hun](/wiki/Lee_Byung-hun).

Following the end of the joint contract between Big Hit and JYP in April 2014, three members of 2AM returned to JYP, while [Lee Chang-min](/wiki/Lee_Chang-min_(singer)) remained with Big Hit in order to continue with his solo career and as part of the duo [Homme](/wiki/Homme_(duo)). The year also saw the disbandment of 8Eight after Baek Chan and Joo Hee's contracts with Big Hit ended. In May 2015, Lim Jeong-hee parted ways with the agency, following the expiration of her three-year contract.

In February 2018, Homme disbanded after member Changmin's contract came to an end. He left the company to start his own agency, while Lee Hyun continued on as a solo artist. In October, BTS renewed and extended their contract with the agency for seven more years. Big Hit debuted its second male group, [Tomorrow X Together](/wiki/Tomorrow_X_Together) (TXT), in March 2019.

In February 2021, Big Hit announced a partnership with [Universal Music Group](/wiki/Universal_Music_Group), which would include a joint venture between Big Hit America and [Geffen Records](/wiki/Geffen_Records) on a [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles)-based label and to develop a pop group under a K-pop-influenced model, and UMG exploring further use of Big Hit's social platform [Weverse](/wiki/Weverse). As a consequence of this partnership, Big Hit reached an agreement in October for BTS to move its distribution in the U.S. and several other countries from [Sony Music](/wiki/Sony_Music)'s [Columbia Records](/wiki/Columbia_Records) and [The Orchard](/wiki/The_Orchard_(company)) to Universal.

### 2021–present: Big Hit Music

On March 19, 2021, Big Hit Entertainment announced that it had been renamed [Hybe Corporation](/wiki/Hybe_Corporation) to emphasize its wider array of business units and ventures. With the change, the Big Hit record label was renamed Big Hit Music . On April 1, 2021, Hybe announced through a board resolution that it would separate the label business (Big Hit Music) from Hybe and establish a new company with 100% of the stake held by Hybe. On July 1, 2021, Big Hit Music became a subsidiary of Hybe.

## Philanthropy

In January 2017, Big Hit Entertainment donated ₩30 million to the 4/16 Sewol Families for Truth and A Safer Society, an organization connected to the families of the [sinking of MV *Sewol*](/wiki/Sinking_of_MV_Sewol). In June 2020, Big Hit, together with BTS, donated $1 million in support of the [Black Lives Matter](/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) movement, during the [George Floyd protests](/wiki/George_Floyd_protests), and another $1 million to [Live Nation](/wiki/Live_Nation_Entertainment)'s Crew Nation campaign to help support live music personnel during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).

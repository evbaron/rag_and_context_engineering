Australian [rock](/wiki/Rock_music) band [AC/DC](/wiki/AC/DC) have released 17 [studio albums](/wiki/Studio_album), two [soundtrack albums](/wiki/Soundtrack_album), three [live albums](/wiki/Live_album), one [extended play](/wiki/Extended_play), 57 [singles](/wiki/Single_(music)), 11 [video albums](/wiki/Video_album), 52 [music videos](/wiki/Music_video) and two [box sets](/wiki/Box_set). Although many AC/DC singles have been released, the band refused to issue any [greatest hits](/wiki/Greatest_hits) albums. *[Who Made Who](/wiki/Who_Made_Who)*, which served as the soundtrack to Stephen King's film *[Maximum Overdrive](/wiki/Maximum_Overdrive)*, *[Iron Man 2](/wiki/Iron_Man_2_(soundtrack))* and the band's various live recordings are the closest they have issued to such a compilation.

Brothers [Angus](/wiki/Angus_Young) (lead guitar) and [Malcolm Young](/wiki/Malcolm_Young) (rhythm guitar) formed AC/DC in 1973. The band released two albums in [Australasia](/wiki/Australasia) before issuing their first international album, *[High Voltage](/wiki/High_Voltage_(1976_album))* in 1976; the Youngs had been joined by vocalist [Bon Scott](/wiki/Bon_Scott), bass guitarist [Mark Evans](/wiki/Mark_Evans_(musician)) and drummer [Phil Rudd](/wiki/Phil_Rudd). In 1980, less than a year after the appearance of the successful *[Highway to Hell](/wiki/Highway_to_Hell)*, Scott died and was replaced by English singer [Brian Johnson](/wiki/Brian_Johnson), with whom AC/DC released their best-selling album, *[Back in Black](/wiki/Back_in_Black)*. The album *[Black Ice](/wiki/Black_Ice_(album))*, from 2008, reached number one in 29 countries. In 50 years of their career, AC/DC have sold over 200 million albums worldwide, and 83 million in the United States. *Back in Black* sold 50 million copies worldwide, and became the [second best-selling album in history](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums).

[Verizon](/wiki/Verizon_(mobile_network)) made AC/DC albums available for [digital download](/wiki/Music_download) in 2007; for five years the band refused to release their albums via [iTunes](/wiki/ITunes), as that company allows downloading of individual tracks. Angus Young observed, "We honestly believe the songs on any of our albums belong together. If we were on iTunes, we know a certain percentage of people would only download two or three songs from the album – and we don't think that represents us musically." However, in November 2012, the band relented and released their entire discography via iTunes. AC/DC’s consistent hard rock sound across decades has been both praised and criticized by music critics. Many reviewers note that the band’s focus on straightforward riffs and high-energy performances helped solidify hard rock’s commercial appeal in the late 1970s and 1980s. According to *Rolling Stone*, the band’s “unapologetically simple approach created one of the most recognizable sounds in rock history.”  On 30 June 2015, their catalogue appeared on [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify) for the first time, which made their works available for [streaming](/wiki/Media_streaming). Their most certified singles in the US are "[Thunderstruck](/wiki/Thunderstruck_(song))" (1990), "[Back in Black](/wiki/Back_in_Black_(song))" (1980), "[Highway to Hell](/wiki/Highway_to_Hell_(song))" (1979), and "[You Shook Me All Night Long](/wiki/You_Shook_Me_All_Night_Long)" (1980) – each have received Diamond, 7× Platinum, and 6× Platinum and from the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA) in 2024, respectively. In Australia, "Thunderstruck" (1990) was accredited 10× Platinum by the [Australian Recording Industry Association](/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association) (ARIA) in 2022.

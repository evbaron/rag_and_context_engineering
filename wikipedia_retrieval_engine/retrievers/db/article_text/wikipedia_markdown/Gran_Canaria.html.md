**Gran Canaria** ([UK](/wiki/British_English): [/ˌɡræn kəˈnɛəriə, -ˈnɑːr-/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English), [US](/wiki/American_English): [/ˌɡrɑːn kəˈnɑːriə, -ˈnɛər-/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English); Spanish: [[ɡɾaŋ kaˈnaɾja]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:Pronunciation_of_Gran_Canaria_in_Spanish.ogg)), also **Grand Canary Island**, is the third-largest and second-most-populous island of the [Canary Islands](/wiki/Canary_Islands), a [Spanish](/wiki/Spain) archipelago off the Atlantic coast of [Northwest Africa](/wiki/Northwest_Africa). As of 2023 the island had a population of 862,893 that constitutes approximately 40% of the population of the archipelago. [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria), the capital of the island, is the largest city of the Canary Islands and the ninth-largest city of Spain.

Gran Canaria is located in the Atlantic Ocean in a region known as [Macaronesia](/wiki/Macaronesia) about 150 kilometres (93 mi) off the northwestern coast of Africa and about 1,350 km (840 mi) from Europe. With an area of 1,560 km2 (600 sq mi) and an altitude of 1,956 m (6,417 ft) at [Morro de la Agujereada](/wiki/Morro_de_la_Agujereada), Gran Canaria is the third largest island of the archipelago in both area and altitude. Gran Canaria is also the third most populated island in Spain.

## History

In [antiquity](/wiki/Classical_antiquity), Gran Canaria was populated by the North African [Canarii](/wiki/Guanches), who may have arrived as early as 500 BC.

In the [medieval period](/wiki/Middle_Ages), after over a century of European incursions and attempts at conquest, the island was conquered on 29 April 1483, by the [Crown of Castile](/wiki/Crown_of_Castile), under [Queen Isabella I](/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile). The Canarian queen [Abenchara](/wiki/Abenchara) was captured and imprisoned by the Spanish during the conquest. The conquest succeeded after a war that lasted five years, and it was an important step towards the expansion of the unified Spain.

The capital city of [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) was founded on 24 June 1478, under the name "Real de Las Palmas", by [Juan Rejón](/wiki/Juan_Rej%C3%B3n), head of the invading [Castilian](/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) army. In 1492, [Christopher Columbus](/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) anchored in the [Port of Las Palmas](/wiki/Port_of_Las_Palmas) (and spent some time on the island) on his first trip to the Americas. Las Palmas de Gran Canaria is, jointly with [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife), the capital of the [autonomous community](/wiki/Autonomous_communities_of_Spain) of the Canary Islands. Much of Iberian migration to the island consisted of [conversos](/wiki/Converso) of Jewish origin.

On 27 March 1977, a bomb went off in Gran Canaria Airport, the main airport on the island. Flights were diverted to the nearby Tenerife Airport: this led to the [Tenerife airport disaster](/wiki/Tenerife_airport_disaster).

## Etymology

[Pliny](/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) claimed that the island was named for the dogs on the island, of which two were presented as a gift to [Juba](/wiki/Juba_II), King of [Mauretania](/wiki/Mauretania). This opinion is disputed as when the Europeans arrived on the island, no dogs were found. Another source suggested the way the natives ate fish was doglike and this was the reason for its name. A more plausible assumption is that the island's name derives from the people Canario or Canarii living near the [Atlas mountain range](/wiki/Atlas_Mountains). [Jean de Béthencourt](/wiki/Jean_de_B%C3%A9thencourt) named the island **Gran** Canaria as the people on the island were valorous opponents to his [conquest](/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Canary_Islands#The_Conquista_Betancuriana).

## Geography

Topography of Gran Canaria

Gran Canaria is located in the [Canary Islands](/wiki/Canary_Islands) archipelago southeast of [Tenerife](/wiki/Tenerife) and west of [Fuerteventura](/wiki/Fuerteventura). The island is of [volcanic](/wiki/Volcano) origin, mostly made of [fissure vents](/wiki/Fissure_vent). It has a round shape, with a diameter of approximately 50 km (31 mi) and a surface area of 1,560 km2 (600 sq mi). Gran Canaria's maximum elevation is 1,956 metres (6,417 ft) at [Morro de la Agujereada](/wiki/Morro_de_la_Agujereada), although the nearby [Pico de las Nieves](/wiki/Pico_de_las_Nieves) has traditionally been considered the island's tallest peak. The coastline measures 236 km.

### Geology

About 80% of the volume of the island was formed during the [Miocene](/wiki/Miocene) period eruptions, between 14 and 9 million years ago. This is called the "Old Cycle" and is estimated to have lasted some 200,000 years and have emitted about 1,000 km3 (240 cu mi), mostly of fissural [alkali basalt](/wiki/Alkali_basalt). This cycle continued with the emission of [trachytes](/wiki/Trachyte), [phonolites](/wiki/Phonolite) and [peralkaline](/wiki/Peralkaline) rocks. This period was followed by one of erosion, which lasted some 4 million years.

A second cycle of volcanic eruptions, known as the "Roque Nublo cycle", took place between 4.5 and 3.4 million years ago. This shorter cycle emitted about 100 km3 (24 cu mi). Most of the inland peaks were formed by erosion from these materials. This period also started with fissural basalts, but ended with violent eruptions of [pyroclastic flows](/wiki/Pyroclastic_flows). Some phonolitic features, like the Risco Blanco, were also formed in its last stages.

The third or recent cycle is held to have started some 2.8 million years ago and is considered to be still active. The last eruptions are held to have occurred some 2000 years ago.

The changes in volume and, therefore, weight of the island have also caused the island to rise above the previous sea level during erosive periods and to sink during eruptive periods. Some of these "fossil beaches" can be seen in the cliff faces of the more eroded northern coast.

### Climate

According to the [Köppen climate classification](/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Gran Canaria is considered to have a [hot desert climate (BWh)](/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) due to its severe lack of precipitation. Gran Canaria has consistent warm temperatures in spring, summer and autumn, and mild winters. Gran Canaria is noted for its rich variety of [microclimates](/wiki/Microclimate). Generally speaking though, the average daytime high ranges from 20 °C (68 °F) in winter to 26 °C (79 °F) in summer. Some cool nights occur in winter, but lows below 10 °C (50 °F) are unknown near the coast. Inland the climate is still mild but mountainous areas see the occasional frost or snow. Annual rainfall averages 228 mm (9.0 in), most of this falling in the cooler months, with July, August and September normally rainless. Rainfall is unevenly distributed through the island with some areas being much drier than others.
Cloud cover and sunshine is often quite variable during the cooler months, and there can be several rather cloudy days at times in winter. Summers are generally quite sunny however, with the south of the island being most favoured.

## Vegetation

Until the conquest, Gran Canaria had extensive forests, but then suffered extensive [deforestation](/wiki/Deforestation) as a result of continuous logging, land divisions and other intensive uses. This reduced the forest cover to just 56,000 hectares (140,000 acres), making the island the most deforested of the Canary Islands. However, in the twentieth century reforestation of the ridge of the island was begun, recovering some of the lost forest mass. Much of the summit of the island is forested mostly due to [reforestation](/wiki/Reforestation).

Municipalities of Gran Canaria

## Transportation

### Roads

*Autopistas* in Gran Canaria provide rapid road transport around the coast

Gran Canaria Airport

The Tenerife catamaran ferry

Gran Canaria has roads encircling the whole island and extending into the mountain areas. In the late 20th century, its dual carriageway, among the first in the Canary Islands, were opened and run around Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, and were later extended to the north coast and the airport and subsequently to the south coast in response to increased tourist traffic. The only highway of Gran Canaria are [GC1](/wiki/GC1_(Gran_Canaria)). Dual carriageway is [GC2](/wiki/GC2_(Gran_Canaria)), and GC31, GC4 and GC5. The western and the north-western parts, with the fewest inhabitants, are linked only with main roads.

### Buses

Public transport around Gran Canaria is provided by an extensive bus network, known in the local dialect as [*guaguas*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/guagua). The *[Autoridad Única del Transporte de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Autoridad_%C3%9Anica_del_Transporte_de_Gran_Canaria)* (Gran Canaria Transport Authority, TGC) manages the network and operates a number of bus stations across the island, including San Telmo and Santa Catalina bus stations in Las Palmas, Maspalomas and Galdar. Bus tickets may be purchased with cash, and AUTGC also operates a [contactless](/wiki/Contactless_smart_card) [electronic ticket](/wiki/Electronic_ticket) called the TransGC Card, which is valid across the whole network.

Inter-urban bus services across the island are operated by the Global bus company. Global was created in 2000 after the merger of two bus companies, Utinsa (which operated in the north of the island) and Salcai (the bus operator for the south). Local bus services in Las Palmas are run by the municipal bus company, *Guaguas Municipales de Las Palmas de Gran Canaria*.

### Airport

[Gran Canaria Airport](/wiki/Gran_Canaria_Airport) ([IATA](/wiki/IATA): LPA) is the only commercial airport on the island. The large number of aircraft and passengers passing through it each year make it one of the busiest in Spain. Gran Canaria is also responsible for all air traffic control in the Canaries. By destination island, Gran Canaria is the second island that congregates the largest number of passengers in the Canary Islands. Car rental companies are located at the airport.

### Sea ports

The most important ports in the island are the Port of Las Palmas *([Puerto de la Luz](/wiki/Puerto_de_Las_Palmas)),* in the city of [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria); [Arguineguín](/wiki/Arguinegu%C3%ADn), which exports [cement](/wiki/Cement) from a large factory; and [Arinaga](/wiki/Arinaga), located in the main industrial zone of Canaries and one of the major ones of Spain.

The main passenger ports are the Port of La Luz, where [Trasmediterránea](/wiki/Trasmediterr%C3%A1nea) operates a weekly ferry route to [Cádiz](/wiki/C%C3%A1diz) on the Spanish mainland, and the [Port of Las Nieves](/wiki/Puerto_de_las_Nieves), located in [Agaete](/wiki/Agaete) on the west side of the island, where [Fred Olsen Express](/wiki/Fred_Olsen_Express) operates a [catamaran](/wiki/Catamaran) ferry service to [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife).

Gran Canaria is connected by regular ferry services to mainland Spain and other Canary Islands, including routes from Cádiz and Tenerife.

### Rail

Plans for a [Tren de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Tren_de_Gran_Canaria) railway network linking the capital with the south have been approved by both the [Gran Canaria Cabildo](/wiki/Cabildo_insular) and the autonomous Canary Islands' Government, though the discussion with the central Spanish Government hinges now on budget. The planned 57 km (35 mi) railway line would run between Las Palmas and Meloneras, with the section in the capital running entirely underground as far as the suburb of Jinámar. The line is planned to have 11 stations, including an underground station at Gran Canaria Airport. The scheme was first announced in 2009, with a planned operational date in 2015. A public company called *Ferrocarriles de Gran Canaria* has been formed by the Cabildo's Gran Canaria Transport Authority. Plans were still being discussed in 2018. As of late 2025 some progress had been made in making the railway project a reality.

## Agriculture

Gran Canaria agriculture is unique among the Canaries islands in that it was traditionally dominated by plantations, with much of these being grains as well as [sugarcane](/wiki/Sugarcane), rather than by stock-breeding. The [caves of Valerón](/wiki/Caves_of_Valer%C3%B3n) (a [property of cultural interest](/wiki/Property_of_cultural_interest) in the "archaeological site" category) in the municipality of [Santa María de Guía](/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_Gu%C3%ADa_de_Gran_Canaria) bears testimony of it by being the largest pre-Hispanic collective granary of the Canaries.

## Tourism

[Puerto de Mogán](/wiki/Puerto_de_Mog%C3%A1n)

[Maspalomas Lighthouse](/wiki/Maspalomas_Lighthouse) at the southern end of the island

[Roque Nublo](/wiki/Roque_Nublo) in the interior of the island

This island is called a "miniature continent" due to the different climates and variety of landscapes found, with long beaches and dunes of white sand, contrasting with green ravines and picturesque villages. A third of the island is under protection as a [Biosphere Reserve](/wiki/Man_and_the_Biosphere_Programme) by [UNESCO](/wiki/UNESCO).

The number of annual visitors was 3.6 million in 2014 (of which 450.000 Spaniards). It is the second island in the archipelago in number of annual tourists, after Tenerife. Most of the tourists visit the southern part of the island. The north tends to be cooler, while the south is warmer and sunny. The east coast of the island is flat, dotted with beaches, while the western coast is rockier and mountainous.

The island possesses 32 Natural Protected Spaces, notably the Rural Park of Nublo, The Doramas Jungle, the Azuaje Ravine, Tamadaba, Pino Santo, etc.

In the south there is a large bird park, [Palmitos Park](/wiki/Palmitos_Park), as well as many beach resort communities. Resorts are concentrated in the central eastern part of the southern coast in the [Maspalomas](/wiki/Maspalomas) area, which includes the towns of [San Agustín](/wiki/San_Agustin,_Las_Palmas), [Playa del Inglés](/wiki/Playa_del_Ingl%C3%A9s) and [Meloneras](/wiki/Meloneras). The [Maspalomas Dunes](/wiki/Maspalomas_Dunes) are located between Playa del Inglés ("The Englishman's Beach") and the distinctive 19th century [Maspalomas lighthouse](/wiki/Maspalomas_Lighthouse). Playa del Ingles is home to the Yumbo Centre, which was opened in 1982 and has almost 200 shops, including bars, restaurants, cafes, fashion boutiques, electronic outlets and jewellery stores.

In Tarajalillo, an Aeroclub exists from where tourist flights can be taken over the island.

Still further to the west along the southern shore, in the Municipality of Mogán, are the communities of [Puerto Rico](/wiki/Puerto_Rico,_Las_Palmas) and [Puerto de Mogán](/wiki/Puerto_de_Mog%C3%A1n), a village referred to as "Little Venice" on account of its many canals.

Other attractions include Cocodrilos Park, [Roque Nublo](/wiki/Roque_Nublo) (an 80 m monolith), [Cenobio de Valerón](/wiki/Cenobio_de_Valer%C3%B3n) with more than 350 storage cavities, [Painted cave of Galdar](/wiki/Painted_Cave,_Galdar) the most important archaeological park in Canary Islands, or the botanical gardens [*Jardín Canario*](/wiki/Jard%C3%ADn_Bot%C3%A1nico_Canario_Viera_y_Clavijo) (in Tafira Alta) and *Cactualdea* (in [La Aldea de San Nicolás](/wiki/La_Aldea_de_San_Nicol%C3%A1s)).

[El Dedo de Dios](/wiki/El_Dedo_de_Dios), or "God's Finger", was a rocky spire jutting from the sea in [Puerto de las Nieves](/wiki/Puerto_de_las_Nieves), and was previously the signature attraction of the Canary Islands until it was destroyed by [tropical storm Delta](/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Delta_(2005)) that crossed the archipelago in November 2005.

Other well-known rock formations are El Cura (also known as El Fraile), The Frog (La Rana), [Roque Bentayga](/wiki/Roque_Bentayga), the Roque de Gando, and the Peñón Bermejo. Traditionally, the highest peak of the island has been considered to be the [Pico de las Nieves](/wiki/Pico_de_las_Nieves), at 1,949 metres (6,394 ft); however, [Morro de la Agujereada](/wiki/Morro_de_la_Agujereada) is taller, at 1,956 metres (6,417 ft).

The capital city is [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria). [*Las Canteras* beach](/wiki/Las_Canteras_beach), a protected area and diving zone, lies in the heart of the city. Las Palmas de Gran Canaria is also known for its annual [*carnaval*](/wiki/Carnival). It was the first stop of Christopher Columbus' expedition on his way back from the Americas, a commemoration of which is the Hermitage of San Antonio Abad, where the navigator prayed, and the *Casa de Colón*. Other attractions in the capital city include the *Museo Canario* (the most important archaeology museum in the archipelago), the cathedral and the *Plaza del Espíritu Santo*. In [Teror](/wiki/Teror) the shrine of *[Virgen del Pino](/wiki/Virgen_del_Pino)* ("Virgin of the Pine"), patron saint of Gran Canaria, can be found. Its feast is celebrated on 8 September.

The town of [Agüimes](/wiki/Ag%C3%BCimes), on the eastern part of the island, has been carefully restored and its town centre, centered on its old church and a peaceful square, now evokes the quiet living of a traditional Canarian town. The district also has some of the best preserved cave dwellings, in the protected area of the [Guayadeque ravine](/wiki/Guayadeque_ravine), where even the church has been built into the mountainside and visitors can find a number of popular cave restaurants. The district also includes the most renowned scuba diving area on the island: the marine reserve at the *playa de El Cabrón* just outside the town of Arinaga.

Other important towns are [Telde](/wiki/Telde), known among other things for their [surf](/wiki/Surfing) schools in Salinetas, Vecindario (within the municipality of Santa Lucía de Tirajana) and [Gáldar](/wiki/G%C3%A1ldar), that contains an important diving zone. In [Arucas](/wiki/Arucas) there is a Neogothic temple, popularly known as "Arucas' Cathedral", as well as a large fertile plain where bananas are grown. In [Gáldar](/wiki/G%C3%A1ldar) and its surroundings there is also a banana-growing plain and some remarkable archaeological remains, such as the [Painted cave of Galdar](/wiki/Painted_Cave,_Galdar) or the [cenobio de Valerón](/wiki/Cenobio_de_Valer%C3%B3n)'s communal silos, ancient tombs (among which the necropolis of Maipés), and the port of Sardina del Norte (one of the island's ports where, as in Las Palmas', [Christopher Columbus](/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) used to get supplies for his ships).

Heading west along the southern coast is the fishing city of [Arguineguín](/wiki/Arguinegu%C3%ADn) in the [Municipality of Mogán](/wiki/Mog%C3%A1n,_Las_Palmas).

## Protected natural areas

The "[Dunas de Maspalomas](/wiki/Maspalomas_Dunes)", in southern Gran Canaria

[Pinus canariensis](/wiki/Pinus_canariensis) forest in Tamadaba Natural Park

Nearly half of the island territory – 667 km2 (258 sq mi) (42.7% of island) – is under protection from the Red Canaria de Espacios Naturales Protegidos ([Canary Islands Network for Protected Natural Areas](/wiki/Canary_Islands_Network_for_Protected_Natural_Areas)). Of the 146 protected sites under control of network in the Canary Islands archipelago, a total of 33 are located in Gran Canaria, the second most protected island in the group.
 There are seven different categories of protection:

1. Six [nature reserves](/wiki/Nature_reserve) – El Brezal, Azuaje, Los Tilos de Moya, Los Marteles, Las Dunas de Maspalomas and Güigüi (total 7,153.1 ha)
2. Two integral nature reserves – Inagua and Barranco Oscuro (total 3,955,5 ha)
3. Two [natural parks](/wiki/Nature_park) – Tamadaba and [Pilancones](/wiki/Pilancones) (total 13,333 ha)
4. Two rural parks – Nublo and Doramas (total 29,893.4 ha)
5. Ten [natural monuments](/wiki/Natural_monument) – Amagro, Bandama, Montañón Negro, Roque de Aguayro, Tauro, Arinaga, [Barranco de Guayadeque](/wiki/Barranco_de_Guayadeque), Riscos de Tirajana, Roque Nublo and Barranco del Draguillo (total 5,264.9 ha)
6. Seven [protected landscapes](/wiki/Protected_area) – La Isleta (in the capital [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](/wiki/Las_Palmas)), Pino Santo, Tafira, Las Cumbres, Lomo Magullo, Fataga and Montaña de Agüimes (total 12,680.9 ha)
7. Four [sites of scientific interest](/wiki/Site_of_Special_Scientific_Interest) – Jinámar, Tufia, Roque de Gando and Juncalillo del Sur (total 276.2 ha).

## Science and technology

In the 1960s, Gran Canaria was selected as the location for one of the 14 [ground stations](/wiki/Ground_stations) in the [Manned Space Flight Network](/wiki/Manned_Space_Flight_Network) (MSFN) to support the [NASA](/wiki/NASA) space program. [Maspalomas Station](/wiki/Maspalomas_Station), located in the south of the island, took part in a number of space missions including the [Apollo 11](/wiki/Apollo_11) Moon landings and [Skylab](/wiki/Skylab). Today it continues to support satellite communications as part of the [ESA](/wiki/ESA) network.

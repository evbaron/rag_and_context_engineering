***Godzilla*** ([Japanese](/wiki/Japanese_language): ゴジラ, [Hepburn](/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Gojira*) is a Japanese *[kaiju](/wiki/Kaiju)* franchise centering on the [titular character](/wiki/Godzilla), a prehistoric reptilian monster awakened and powered by nuclear radiation. The films series are recognized by the *[Guinness World Records](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records)* as the "longest continuously running film series", having been in ongoing production since 1954, with several hiatuses of varying lengths. There are 38 *Godzilla* films: 33 Japanese films produced and distributed by [Toho Co., Ltd.](/wiki/Toho), and five American films; one by [TriStar Pictures](/wiki/TriStar_Pictures) and four films (part of the [Monsterverse](/wiki/Monsterverse) franchise) by [Legendary Pictures](/wiki/Legendary_Entertainment).

The original film, *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1954_film))*, was directed by and co-written by [Ishirō Honda](/wiki/Ishir%C5%8D_Honda) and released by Toho in 1954. It became an influential classic of the genre. It featured political and social undertones relevant to Japan at the time. The 1954 film and its special effects director [Eiji Tsuburaya](/wiki/Eiji_Tsuburaya) are largely credited for establishing the template for *[tokusatsu](/wiki/Tokusatsu)*, a technique of practical special effects filmmaking that would become essential in Japan's film industry since the release of *Godzilla* (1954). For its North American release, the film was localized in 1956 as *[Godzilla, King of the Monsters!](/wiki/Godzilla,_King_of_the_Monsters!)* and featured new footage with [Raymond Burr](/wiki/Raymond_Burr) edited together with the original Japanese footage.

The popularity of the films has led to the film series expanding to other media, such as [television, music, literature](/wiki/Godzilla_in_popular_culture) and [video games](/wiki/List_of_Godzilla_games). Godzilla has become one of the most recognizable symbols in Japanese pop culture worldwide and a well-known facet of [Japanese cinema](/wiki/Cinema_of_Japan). It is also considered one of the first examples of the popular *kaiju* and *tokusatsu* subgenres in Japanese entertainment.

Godzilla films vary in the complexity of themes and targeted audience. Several of the films have political themes, dark tones, and [complex internal mythology](/wiki/Shared_universe), while others are simple action films featuring aliens or other monsters or have simpler themes accessible to children. Godzilla's role varies from purely a destructive force to an [anti-hero](/wiki/Anti-hero), ally of humans, a symbolic protector or destroyer of Japanese values, or a hero to children.

The name "Godzilla" is a romanization of the original Japanese name Gojira (ゴジラ)—which is a combination of two Japanese words: *gorira* (ゴリラ), "gorilla", and *kujira* (クジラ), "whale". The word alludes to the size, power and aquatic origin of Godzilla. As developed by Toho, the monster is an offshoot of the combination of radioactivity and ancient [dinosaur](/wiki/Dinosaur_size)-like creatures, indestructible and possessing special powers (see [Godzilla characteristics](/wiki/Godzilla#Characteristics)).

## History

The *Godzilla* film series is broken into several different eras reflecting a characteristic style and corresponding to the same eras used to classify all *kaiju eiga* ([monster movies](/wiki/Monster_movies)) in Japan. The first, second, and fourth eras refer to the [Japanese emperor](/wiki/Japanese_emperor) during production: the [Shōwa era](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dwa_era), the [Heisei era](/wiki/Heisei_era), and the [Reiwa era](/wiki/Reiwa_era). The last phase of the second is called the Millennium era, as the emperor ([Heisei](/wiki/Akihito)) is the same, but these films are considered to have a different style and storyline than the Heisei era.

Over the series' history, the films have reflected [the social and political climate in Japan](/wiki/History_of_Japan). In the original film, Godzilla was an [allegory](/wiki/Allegory) for the effects of the [atomic bomb](/wiki/Nuclear_weapon), and the consequences that such weapons might have on Earth. The radioactive contamination of the Japanese fishing boat *[Lucky Dragon No. 5](/wiki/Daigo_Fukury%C5%AB_Maru)* through the United States' [Castle Bravo](/wiki/Castle_Bravo) [thermonuclear](/wiki/Thermonuclear) device test at [Bikini Atoll](/wiki/Bikini_Atoll) on March 1, 1954, led to much press coverage in Japan preceding the release of the first film in 1954. The Heisei and Millennium series have largely continued this concept. Toho was inspired to make the original *Godzilla* film after the commercial success of the 1952 re-release of *[King Kong](/wiki/King_Kong_(1933_film))* and the success of *[The Beast from 20,000 Fathoms](/wiki/The_Beast_from_20,000_Fathoms)* (1953), the first live-action film to feature a giant monster awakened following an atomic bomb detonation. The success of the *Godzilla* franchise itself would go on to inspire other giant monster films worldwide.

### Shōwa era (1954–1975)

The *Shōwa* iterations of Godzilla featured in *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1954_film))* (1954) and *[Godzilla vs. Megalon](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Megalon)* (1973).

The initial series of films are named after the [Shōwa era](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dwa_era) (as all of these films were produced during [Emperor Shōwa](/wiki/Hirohito)'s reign). This Shōwa timeline spanned from 1954, with *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1954_film))*, to 1975, with *[Terror of Mechagodzilla](/wiki/Terror_of_Mechagodzilla)*.

The first *Godzilla* film initially began as a Japanese-Indonesian co-production titled *In the Shadow of Glory* (栄光のかげに, *Eikō no Kage ni*). However, the project was cancelled after the Indonesian government denied visas to Toho's crew due to anti-Japanese sentiments and political pressure. On his flight back to Japan after a failed attempt to renegotiate with the Indonesian government, film producer [Tomoyuki Tanaka](/wiki/Tomoyuki_Tanaka) conceived an idea for a giant monster film inspired by *[The Beast from 20,000 Fathoms](/wiki/The_Beast_from_20,000_Fathoms)* and the then-recent *[Daigo Fukuryū Maru](/wiki/Daigo_Fukury%C5%AB_Maru)* incident. Tanaka then succeeded in convincing executive producer Iwao Mori to replace *In the Shadow of Glory* with his monster idea, after special effects director [Eiji Tsuburaya](/wiki/Eiji_Tsuburaya) agreed to do the film.

Tsuburaya initially proposed a giant octopus-like monster, and later a gorilla-like or whale-like monster to reflect the creature's name *Gojira*, a combination of the Japanese words for gorilla (ゴリラ, *gorira*) and whale (クジラ, *kujira*). But Tsuburaya settled on a dinosaur-like monster designed by Teizō Toshimitsu and [Akira Watanabe](/wiki/Akira_Watanabe_(art_director)) under his supervision. Tanaka handpicked [Ishirō Honda](/wiki/Ishir%C5%8D_Honda) to direct and co-write the film, feeling that his wartime experience was ideal for the film's anti-nuclear themes, despite Honda not being Toho's first choice. Principal photography ran 51 days, and special effects photography ran 71 days.

*Godzilla* was first released in [Nagoya](/wiki/Nagoya) on October 27, 1954, and released nationwide on November 3, 1954. Despite mixed reviews, it was a box office success. It became the eighth best-attended film in Japan that year, and earned ¥183 million (just under $510,000) in [distributor rentals](/wiki/Distributor_rentals) during its initial run, with total lifetime gross receipts of $2.25 million. The film was nominated for Best Picture and Best Special Effects at the Japanese Movie Association Awards, where it won the latter.

Starting with *[Ghidorah, the Three-Headed Monster](/wiki/Ghidorah,_the_Three-Headed_Monster)*, Godzilla began evolving into a friendlier, more playful [antihero](/wiki/Antihero) (this transition was complete by *[Son of Godzilla](/wiki/Son_of_Godzilla)*, where Godzilla is depicted as a more virtuous character) and, as years went by, it evolved into an [anthropomorphic](/wiki/Anthropomorphic) [superhero](/wiki/Superhero). *Ghidorah the Three-Headed Monster* was also significant for introducing Godzilla's archenemy and the main antagonist of the film series, [King Ghidorah](/wiki/King_Ghidorah).

*Son of Godzilla* and *[All Monsters Attack](/wiki/All_Monsters_Attack)* were aimed at youthful audiences, featuring the appearance of Godzilla's son, [Minilla](/wiki/Minilla). *[Godzilla vs. Mechagodzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Mechagodzilla)* was notable for introducing [Mechagodzilla](/wiki/Mechagodzilla), Godzilla's robot duplicate and the secondary antagonist of the film series. The Shōwa period loosely tied in a number of Toho-produced films in which Godzilla himself did not appear and consequently saw the addition of many monsters into the *Godzilla* continuity, three of which ([Rodan](/wiki/Rodan), [Varan](/wiki/Varan), and [Mothra](/wiki/Mothra)) originated in their own solo films and another five ([Anguirus](/wiki/Anguirus), [Manda](/wiki/Manda_(kaiju)), [Baragon](/wiki/Baragon), [Gorosaurus](/wiki/Gorosaurus) and [Kumonga](/wiki/Kumonga)) appeared in their first films as either secondary antagonists or secondary kaiju.

[Haruo Nakajima](/wiki/Haruo_Nakajima) mainly portrayed Godzilla since 1954 until his retirement in 1972. However, other stunt actors have portrayed the character in his absence, such as [Katsumi Tezuka](/wiki/Katsumi_Tezuka), Yū Sekida, Ryosaku Takasugi, Seiji Onaka, Shinji Takagi, Isao Zushi, and [Toru Kawai](/wiki/Toru_Kawai). [Eiji Tsuburaya](/wiki/Eiji_Tsuburaya) directed the special effects for the first six films of the series. His protege Sadamasa Arikawa took over the effects work for the next three films (with Tsuburaya supervising), while [Teruyoshi Nakano](/wiki/Teruyoshi_Nakano) directed the special effects for the last six films of the series.

[The Criterion Collection](/wiki/The_Criterion_Collection) released the Shōwa era films as part of a Blu-ray box set in the United States and Canada on October 29, 2019.

### Heisei era (1984–1995)

The *Heisei* Godzilla featured in *[Godzilla vs. Mothra](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Mothra)* (1992).

Toho rebooted the series in 1984 with *[The Return of Godzilla](/wiki/The_Return_of_Godzilla)*, starting the second era of *Godzilla* films, known as the Heisei series. *The Return of Godzilla* serves as a direct sequel to the original 1954 film and ignores the subsequent events of the Shōwa series. Despite being released within [Japan's political Shōwa era](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dwa_era) five years before the [new Emperor's](/wiki/Akihito) [reign](/wiki/Heisei_era), *The Return of Godzilla* is part of the Heisei series because it is a direct predecessor to *[Godzilla vs. Biollante](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Biollante)* (1989), which came out in the first year of the new Emperor's reign.

The Heisei films are set in a single, continuous timeline that brings [Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(Heisei)) back as a destructive force of nature that is feared by humans. The biological nature and science behind Godzilla became more discussed in the films, showing an increased focus on the moral aspects of genetics. *[Godzilla vs. King Ghidorah](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._King_Ghidorah)* gave Godzilla's first concrete birth story, featuring a dinosaur named *Godzillasaurus* that was mutated by nuclear radiation into Godzilla. Godzilla was portrayed by [Kenpachiro Satsuma](/wiki/Kenpachiro_Satsuma) for the Heisei films while the special effects were directed by [Koichi Kawakita](/wiki/Koichi_Kawakita), with the exception of *The Return of Godzilla*, for which the effects were directed by Teruyoshi Nakano.

### Millennium era (1999–2004)

The Millennium iterations of Godzilla.

Toho rebooted the franchise for a second time with the 1999 film *[Godzilla 2000: Millennium](/wiki/Godzilla_2000)*, starting the third era of *Godzilla* films, known as the Millennium series. Despite them all being released during the Heisei period of Japan, this era is named as such to tie in with the first few years of the third millennium. The Millennium series is treated similarly to an [anthology series](/wiki/Anthology_series) where each film is a standalone story, with the 1954 film serving as the only previous point of reference. *[Godzilla Against Mechagodzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_Against_Mechagodzilla)* and *[Godzilla: Tokyo S.O.S.](/wiki/Godzilla:_Tokyo_S.O.S.)* are the only films in the Millennium era to share continuity with each other and are also connected to 1961's *[Mothra](/wiki/Mothra_(film))* and to 1966's *[The War of the Gargantuas](/wiki/The_War_of_the_Gargantuas)*. Another exception to this rule is 2004's *[Godzilla: Final Wars](/wiki/Godzilla:_Final_Wars)*, that continues the trend of being a standalone story, but the events of the 1954 film are ignored here, making this the first Toho produced film in the series to do so.

After the release of *Godzilla: Final Wars*, marking the 50th anniversary of the *Godzilla* film franchise, Toho decided to put the series on hiatus for another 10 years. Toho also demolished the water stage on its lot used in numerous *Godzilla*, kaiju and tokusatsu films. [Yoshimitsu Banno](/wiki/Yoshimitsu_Banno), who had directed 1971's *[Godzilla vs. Hedorah](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Hedorah)*, secured the rights from Toho to make an [IMAX 3D](/wiki/IMAX) short film production, based on a story similar to his *Hedorah* film. This project eventually led to the development of [Legendary's *Godzilla*](/wiki/Godzilla_(2014_film)). [Tsutomu Kitagawa](/wiki/Tsutomu_Kitagawa) portrayed Godzilla for the majority of the Millennium films, with the exception of *[Godzilla, Mothra and King Ghidorah: Giant Monsters All-Out Attack](/wiki/Godzilla,_Mothra_and_King_Ghidorah:_Giant_Monsters_All-Out_Attack)*, in which Godzilla was portrayed by [Mizuho Yoshida](/wiki/Mizuho_Yoshida_(actor)). Unlike the Shōwa and later Heisei films, the special effects for the Millennium films were directed by multiple effects directors such as [Kenji Suzuki](/wiki/Kenji_Suzuki_(director)) (*Godzilla 2000*, *[Godzilla vs. Megaguirus](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Megaguirus)*), [Makoto Kamiya](/wiki/Makoto_Kamiya) (*Godzilla, Mothra and King Ghidorah: Giant Monsters All-Out Attack*), Yuichi Kikuchi (*Godzilla Against Mechagodzilla*), and Eiichi Asada (*Godzilla: Tokyo S.O.S.*, *Godzilla: Final Wars*).

### Reiwa era (2016–present)

Main articles: [Shin Godzilla (character)](/wiki/Shin_Godzilla_(character)), [Godzilla Earth](/wiki/Godzilla_Earth), and [Godzilla (Takashi Yamazaki)](/wiki/Godzilla_(Takashi_Yamazaki))

The Reiwa iterations of Godzilla.

In December 2014, Toho announced plans for a new *Godzilla* film of its own for a 2016 release. The film was Toho's reboot of the *Godzilla* franchise, after [Legendary Pictures' reboot](/wiki/Godzilla_(2014_film)) in 2014, and was co-directed by [Hideaki Anno](/wiki/Hideaki_Anno) and [Shinji Higuchi](/wiki/Shinji_Higuchi) (both of whom collaborated on the [anime](/wiki/Anime) *[Neon Genesis Evangelion](/wiki/Neon_Genesis_Evangelion)*), with the screenplay written by Anno and the visual effects directed by Higuchi. Principal photography began in September 2015 and ended in October of the same year, with the special effects work following in November. *[Shin Godzilla](/wiki/Shin_Godzilla)* was released in Japan on July 29, 2016, in IMAX, 4DX, and MX4D to positive reviews and was a box office success.

After the release of Shin Godzilla, Toho established a "Godzilla Room", a group consisting of 14 individuals that were tasked with studying all the previous films that involved the character and to ensure that further movies would avoid damaging the brand. The group wrote up a new set of mandated guidelines that all feature films and merchandise had to follow, which involved the prohibition of permanently killing off the character and keeping him from preying on "people or things" to ensure that every appearance remained authentic.

In August 2016, Toho announced plans for a trilogy of anime *Godzilla* films with [Polygon Pictures](/wiki/Polygon_Pictures) animating the films and [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) distributing the trilogy worldwide, except in Japan where each film will be given a theatrical release by Toho. The first film, titled *[Godzilla: Planet of the Monsters](/wiki/Godzilla:_Planet_of_the_Monsters)*, was released on November 17, 2017. The second film, titled *[Godzilla: City on the Edge of Battle](/wiki/Godzilla:_City_on_the_Edge_of_Battle)*, was released on May 18, 2018. The third and final film in the trilogy, titled *[Godzilla: The Planet Eater](/wiki/Godzilla:_The_Planet_Eater)*, was released on November 9, 2018.

In January 2018, Toho announced its plans to invest ¥15 billion (US$135 million) for the next three years beginning in 2019 to co-produce content with Hollywood and Chinese studios who have licensed Toho's properties, such as *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla)*, *[Your Name](/wiki/Your_Name)* and *[Pokémon](/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon)*. Toho would invest 25% in production costs and would earn a higher share in revenue and manage creators rights, so its creative input would be reflected in each work. In May 2018, Toho's Chief Godzilla Officer Keiji Ota revealed that a sequel to *Shin Godzilla* would not happen, but expressed interest in a potential shared cinematic series between Godzilla and other Toho monsters akin to the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe).

In 2019, Toho invested ¥15.4 billion (US$140 million) into its Los Angeles-based subsidiary [Toho International Inc.](/wiki/Toho_International) as part of its "Toho Vision 2021 Medium-term Management Strategy", a strategy to increase content, platform, real-estate, surpass ¥50 billion in profits, and increase character businesses on Toho intellectual properties such as *Godzilla*. Hiroyasu Matsuoka was named the representative director of the project. In 2019, Toho launched the first official English-language website and the first official English-language Twitter and Instagram accounts for the franchise.

In June 2019, Toho revealed plans to present the Toho Godzilla at [San Diego Comic-Con](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con) for the first time to commemorate the franchise's 65th anniversary, as well as being part of its plan to expand the franchise in the United States. At San Diego Comic-Con, Akito Takahashi, the project manager of Toho's Godzilla Strategic Conference, revealed Toho's intentions to have the Toho and Legendary *Godzilla* films expand together. He also revealed that the option to reintroduce political themes and old or new monsters would be available to filmmakers, should they choose to pursue it. Akito also expressed interest in re-introducing Mechagodzilla and Jet Jaguar in the future.

In October 2020, Toho announced plans for an anime series titled *[Godzilla Singular Point](/wiki/Godzilla_Singular_Point)* released on Netflix in 2021, revealing artwork for Godzilla and its principal characters. The project was directed by Atsushi Takahashi, with music by Kan Sawada, written by Toh Enjoe, character designs by [Kazue Kato](/wiki/Kazue_Kato), and animations by Eiji Yamamori. The series was produced by [Bones Inc.](/wiki/Bones_(studio)) in partnership with [Orange Co., Ltd.](/wiki/Orange_(animation_studio)), featured hand-drawn and CG animation, and had no relation to Polygon's anime film trilogy.

On November 3, 2022, during the franchise's 68th anniversary known as "Godzilla Day", Toho announced plans to release a new live-action *Godzilla* film, *[Godzilla Minus One](/wiki/Godzilla_Minus_One)*, on November 3, 2023, to commemorate the franchise's 70th anniversary. Toho also stated that [Takashi Yamazaki](/wiki/Takashi_Yamazaki) was the director, writer, and visual effects supervisor for this new film and that it had entered post-production after recently completed filming. According to *[Collider](/wiki/Collider_(website))*, *Minus One* became the most commercially successful Japanese film in the series and "helped the *Godzilla* series become more popular than ever before". In 2024, *Minus One* won the [Academy Award for Best Visual Effects](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects), becoming the first *Godzilla* film to win an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award), as well as the first Japanese film to win Best Visual Effects.

On November 1, 2024, Toho announced plans to move forward with a new Godzilla film with Yamazaki returning to direct, write, and handle visual effects. In July 2025, Toho International president Koji Ueda revealed that a *Shin Godzilla* sequel is among the many *Godzilla* projects in development.

### American films

#### *The Volcano Monsters* (1957)

Main article: [Gigantis, the Fire Monster](/wiki/Gigantis,_the_Fire_Monster)

The producers of *[Godzilla, King of the Monsters!](/wiki/Godzilla,_King_of_the_Monsters!)* – Harry Rybnick, Richard Kay, Edward Barison, Paul Schreibman, and Edmund Goldman – purchased the North American rights to the 1955 sequel *[Godzilla Raids Again](/wiki/Godzilla_Raids_Again)* but rather than localize or dub the film in English, they chose to produce a new film that would repurpose the effects footage from *Godzilla Raids Again*; filming was expected to begin in June 1957. Rybnick hired [Ib Melchior](/wiki/Ib_Melchior) and Edwin Watson to write a script, titled *The Volcano Monsters*, that focused on a new story with American characters centered around the effects footage. Toho approved of the idea in early 1957 and shipped the Godzilla and Anguirus suits for additional photography to be shot at Howard A. Anderson's special effects studio. Rybnick and Barison originally made a deal with AB-PT Pictures Corp. to co-finance the film but plans for *The Volcano Monsters* were cancelled after AB-PT Pictures folded. Schreibman, Goldman, and new financier Newton P. Jacobs, decided to dub *Godzilla Raids Again* into English instead.

#### Unproduced 3D film (1983)

Storyboard by William Stout for Steve Miner's unproduced 3D *Godzilla* film. Stout chose to reinvent his Godzilla design as an amalgam between a Tyrannosaurus and Toho's original design.

Main article: [Godzilla: King of the Monsters in 3D](/wiki/Godzilla:_King_of_the_Monsters_in_3D)

In 1983, director [Steve Miner](/wiki/Steve_Miner) pitched his idea for an American 3D production of *Godzilla* to Toho, with storyboards by William Stout and a script written by [Fred Dekker](/wiki/Fred_Dekker), titled *Godzilla: King of the Monsters in 3D*, which would have featured Godzilla rampaging through San Francisco in an attempt to find its offspring. Various studios and producers expressed interest but passed it over due to high budget concerns. The film would have featured a full scale animatronic Godzilla head built by [Rick Baker](/wiki/Rick_Baker), stop motion animation executed by [David W. Allen](/wiki/David_W._Allen), an articulated stop motion Godzilla figure created by Stephen Czerkas, and additional storyboards by Doug Wildey. The production design would have been overseen by William Stout.

#### TriStar Pictures (1998–2000)

Main articles: [Godzilla (1998 film)](/wiki/Godzilla_(1998_film)), [Godzilla: The Series](/wiki/Godzilla:_The_Series), and [Godzilla (TriStar)](/wiki/Godzilla_(TriStar))

In October 1992, [TriStar Pictures](/wiki/TriStar_Pictures) acquired the rights from Toho with plans to produce a trilogy. Director [Jan de Bont](/wiki/Jan_de_Bont) and writers [Terry Rossio](/wiki/Terry_Rossio) and [Ted Eliott](/wiki/Ted_Elliott_(screenwriter)) developed a script that had Godzilla battling a shape-shifting alien called "the Gryphon". De Bont later left the project after budget disagreements with the studio. [Roland Emmerich](/wiki/Roland_Emmerich) was hired to direct and co-write a new script with producer [Dean Devlin](/wiki/Dean_Devlin).

A co-production between [Centropolis Entertainment](/wiki/Centropolis_Entertainment), [Fried Films](/wiki/Robert_N._Fried), Independent Pictures, and TriStar Pictures, *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1998_film))* was theatrically released on May 20, 1998, to negative reviews and grossed $379 million worldwide against a production budget between $130–150 million. Despite grossing nearly three times its budget, it was considered a box office disappointment. Two planned sequels were cancelled and an [animated TV series](/wiki/Godzilla:_The_Series) was produced instead. TriStar let the license expire in 2003. In 2004, a new iteration of TriStar's Godzilla appeared in Toho's *[Godzilla: Final Wars](/wiki/Godzilla:_Final_Wars)* as "**Zilla**." This variant has since appeared in various media under the "Zilla" trademark, while the incarnations from the 1998 film and animated series retain the Godzilla copyright and trademark.

#### Legendary Pictures (2014–present)

Main articles: [Monsterverse](/wiki/Monsterverse) and [Godzilla (Monsterverse)](/wiki/Godzilla_(Monsterverse))

In 2004, director [Yoshimitsu Banno](/wiki/Yoshimitsu_Banno) acquired permission from Toho to produce a short [IMAX](/wiki/IMAX) *Godzilla* film. In 2009, the project was turned over to [Legendary Pictures](/wiki/Legendary_Entertainment) to be redeveloped as a feature film. Announced in March 2010, the film was co-produced with [Warner Bros. Pictures](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures) and was directed by [Gareth Edwards](/wiki/Gareth_Edwards_(filmmaker)).

*[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(2014_film))* was theatrically released on May 16, 2014, to positive reviews and was a box office success, grossing $529 million worldwide against a production budget of $160 million. The film's success prompted Toho to produce a [reboot of their own](/wiki/Shin_Godzilla) and Legendary to proceed with sequels and a shared cinematic franchise dubbed the [Monsterverse](/wiki/Monsterverse): with *[Godzilla: King of the Monsters](/wiki/Godzilla:_King_of_the_Monsters_(2019_film))* released on May 31, 2019; *[Godzilla vs. Kong](/wiki/Godzilla_vs._Kong)* released on March 24, 2021; the TV series *[Monarch: Legacy of Monsters](/wiki/Monarch:_Legacy_of_Monsters)* released on November 17, 2023, on [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B); and *[Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire](/wiki/Godzilla_x_Kong:_The_New_Empire)* released on March 29, 2024.

### Guest appearances

In 2007, a [CGI](/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery) Godzilla appeared in the Toho [slice of life](/wiki/Slice_of_life) film *[Always: Sunset on Third Street 2](/wiki/Always:_Sunset_on_Third_Street_2)*. In an imaginary sequence, Godzilla destroys part of 1959 Tokyo, with one of the main protagonists getting angry that Godzilla damaged his car showroom. The making of the sequence was kept a secret. Godzilla has been referenced in, and has briefly appeared in, several other films. Godzilla guest starred in the show *[Crayon Shin-chan](/wiki/Crayon_Shin-chan)* as an antagonist. Godzilla also appears in cave paintings (alongside Rodan, Mothra and King Ghidorah) in a [post-credits scene](/wiki/Post-credits_scene) in *[Kong: Skull Island](/wiki/Kong:_Skull_Island)*. In 2019, Godzilla made an appearance in the anime film *[Shinkansen Henkei Robo Shinkalion the Movie: Mirai Kara Kita Shinsoku no ALFA-X](/wiki/Shinkansen_Henkei_Robo_Shinkalion#Theatrical_film)*.

### Localized releases

In 1956, Jewell Enterprises Inc., released *[Godzilla, King of the Monsters!](/wiki/Godzilla,_King_of_the_Monsters!)*, an American localization of *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1954_film))* (1954). This version removed most of the political themes and social commentaries, resulting in 30 minutes of footage from the Japanese version replaced with new footage featuring [Raymond Burr](/wiki/Raymond_Burr) interacting with Japanese actors and look-alikes to make it seem like Burr was a part of the original Japanese production. In addition, the soundtrack and sound effects were slightly altered and some dialogue was dubbed into English. This release is referred to as an "[Americanization](/wiki/Americanization)" or the "Americanized" version by some sources. Similar localizations (or Americanizations) occurred for the U.S. releases of *[The Return of Godzilla](/wiki/The_Return_of_Godzilla)*, released in the U.S. as *[Godzilla 1985](/wiki/Godzilla_1985)*, the latter which had Burr reprising the role of Steve Martin from *Godzilla, King of the Monsters!*.

In 1957, the same American producers of *Godzilla, King of the Monsters!* attempted to produce *The Volcano Monsters*, a new film that would have repurposed the effects footage of *[Godzilla Raids Again](/wiki/Godzilla_Raids_Again)* around a new story with American characters. However, funding from AB-PT Pictures collapsed after the company closed down and *Godzilla Raids Again* was instead re-cut, dubbed in English, and released in 1959 by [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) as *[Gigantis, the Fire Monster](/wiki/Gigantis,_the_Fire_Monster)*.

In the early 1960s, American producer [John Beck](/wiki/John_Beck_(producer)) sold the *King Kong vs. Prometheus* script to Toho (later redeveloped as *[King Kong vs. Godzilla](/wiki/King_Kong_vs._Godzilla)*), and was given exclusive distribution rights (theatrical and television) for the United States, Canada, Alaska, the United Kingdom, and Israel, while Toho retained exclusive distribution for the Far East. [The American version](/wiki/King_Kong_vs._Godzilla#American_version_3) slightly altered the story by adding new footage with Michael Keith as Eric Carter, a [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations) reporter covering the events of the film, and [Harry Holcombe](/wiki/Harry_Holcombe) as Dr. Arnold Johnson, the head of the Museum of Natural History in New York City who tries to explain Godzilla's origin and his and Kong's motivations. Akira Ifukube's original score was replaced with [stock music](/wiki/Stock_music) recycled from other films such as *[Creature from the Black Lagoon](/wiki/Creature_from_the_Black_Lagoon)* (1954), and brief stock footage from *[The Mysterians](/wiki/The_Mysterians)* (1957).

In 1976, Italian director [Luigi Cozzi](/wiki/Luigi_Cozzi) intended to re-release *Godzilla* in Italy (known by fans as "*Cozzilla*"). Facing resistance from exhibitors to showing a black-and-white film, Cozzi instead licensed a negative of *Godzilla, King of the Monsters* from Toho and created a new film in color, adding much stock footage of graphic death and destruction and short scenes from newsreel footage from World War II, which he released as *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla,_King_of_the_Monsters!#Italian_re-release)* in 1977. The film was colorized using a process called Spectrorama 70, where color gels are put on the original black-and-white film, becoming one of the first black-and-white films to be colorized. Dialogue was dubbed into Italian and new music was added. After the initial Italian run, the negative became Toho's property and prints have only been exhibited in Italy from that time onward. Italian firm Yamato Video at one time intended to release the colorized version on a two-disc DVD along with the original *Godzilla*.

## Other media

### Television

#### Japan

In 1973, Godzilla was featured in Toho's *[tokusatsu](/wiki/Tokusatsu)* series *[Zone Fighter](/wiki/Zone_Fighter)*, which also featured King Ghidorah and Gigan in a few episodes. Several filmmakers who had worked on previous Godzilla films participated in the series; [Tomoyuki Tanaka](/wiki/Tomoyuki_Tanaka) produced the series, directors [Ishirō Honda](/wiki/Ishir%C5%8D_Honda) and [Jun Fukuda](/wiki/Jun_Fukuda) directed some episodes. Fukuda wrote episode four, effects director [Teruyoshi Nakano](/wiki/Teruyoshi_Nakano) contributed to the special effects, while [Kōichi Kawakita](/wiki/K%C5%8Dichi_Kawakita) (who would direct the effects for Toho's [Heisei era](/wiki/Heisei_era) films) served as assistant effects director.

In 1992, Toho produced a children's educational animated series titled *[Godzilland](/wiki/Godzilland)* which featured live-action segments mixed with [chibi](/wiki/Chibi_(style))-styled animation. In 1997, Toho produced a children's series titled *Godzilla Island*, centered on Godzilla toys. Toho made the series available worldwide on their official YouTube channel in November 2022. In 2018, GEMSTONE, a web content subsidiary of Toho, held a competition for filmmakers to produce short films based on the Godzilla franchise with finalists receiving a cash prize and the opportunity to work with Toho in an official capacity. One of these finalists, the puppet short *Godziban*, would become an ongoing [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) web series in August 2019, with a selection of 20 episodes being offered on [Amazon Prime Video](/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video) and [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) in Japan in 2024.

In October 2020, Toho announced *[Godzilla Singular Point](/wiki/Godzilla_Singular_Point)*; an [anime](/wiki/Anime) series directed by Atsushi Takahashi, written by [Toh EnJoe](/wiki/Toh_EnJoe), and animated by Japanese studios [Bones](/wiki/Bones_(studio)) and [Orange](/wiki/Orange_(animation_studio)). *Godzilla Singular Point* aired on Japanese television in April 2021 and was released worldwide on [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) in June 2021. *[Chibi Godzilla Raids Again](/wiki/Chibi_Godzilla_Raids_Again)*, a short anime series produced by Toho and Pie in the sky, aired in 2023.

### Attractions

Godzilla has been featured in several theme park attractions across Japan and been the subject of various exhibitions of suits and puppets.
[Monster Planet of Godzilla](/wiki/Monster_Planet_of_Godzilla) was a motion simulator that once ran at [Sanrio Puroland](/wiki/Sanrio_Puroland) in the 1990s.
[Universal Studios Japan](/wiki/Universal_Studios_Japan) featured temporary 4D film attractions based around Shin Godzilla, including a crossover with [Neon Genesis Evangelion](/wiki/Neon_Genesis_Evangelion) as part of their Cool Japan event.
In 2019, [Nijigen no Mori](/wiki/Nijigen_no_Mori) debuted a *[Shin Godzilla](/wiki/Shin_Godzilla)* themed zipline experience titled "Godzilla Interception Operation" accompanied by a permanent Godzilla museum.
The Eiji Tsuburaya Museum in [Sukagawa, Fukushima](/wiki/Sukagawa,_Fukushima) features an exclusive short film *Dream Challenge: Godzilla Appears in Sukugawa*, starring the 1954 Godzilla rampaging through the effects director's hometown.
The [Seibu-en](/wiki/Seibu-en) amusement park's [Flying theater](/wiki/Flying_theater) features two Godzilla ride films playing in rotation: *[Godzilla the Ride: Giant Monsters Ultimate Battle](/wiki/Godzilla_the_Ride:_Giant_Monsters_Ultimate_Battle)* and *[Godzilla the Ride: Great Clash](/wiki/Godzilla_the_Ride:_Great_Clash)*, which released in 2021 and 2025 respectively.
[Lotte World](/wiki/Lotte_World) in [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea) would receive the first Godzilla attraction outside of Japan in the form of the MonsterVerse inspired *Kong X Godzilla: The Ride* in late 2025.

### Novels

A *Godzilla* series of novels was published by [Random House](/wiki/Random_House) during the late 1990s and the first half of 2000. The company created different series for different age groups, the [Scott Ciencin series](/wiki/Godzilla_(Scott_Ciencin_series)) being aimed at preteens and the [Marc Cerasini series](/wiki/Godzilla_(Marc_Cerasini_series)) being aimed at teens and young adults.

To tie-in with the 2014 film, three books were published. [Titan Books](/wiki/Titan_Books) published a novelization of the film in May 2014, written by Greg Cox. The graphic novel *Godzilla: Awakening* by Max Borenstein, Greg Borenstein and Eric Battle served as a prequel, and *Godzilla: The Art of Destruction* by Mark Cotta told about the making of the film.

### Music

*[Godzilla: The Album](/wiki/Godzilla:_The_Album)*, the [soundtrack album](/wiki/Soundtrack_album) of *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1998_film))* (1998), sold 2.5 million copies worldwide. The album's lead single, "[Come with Me](/wiki/Come_with_Me_(Puff_Daddy_song))" by [Puff Daddy](/wiki/Puff_Daddy) featuring [Jimmy Page](/wiki/Jimmy_Page), sold a certified 2.025 million copies worldwide. Its Japan-exclusive single, "[Lose Control](/wiki/Shinshoku_(Lose_Control))" by [Japanese rock](/wiki/Japanese_rock) band [L'Arc-en-Ciel](/wiki/L%27Arc-en-Ciel), sold 938,401 copies in Japan. *[Shin Godzilla Ongakushuu](/wiki/Shin_Godzilla)*, the soundtrack album of *[Shin Godzilla](/wiki/Shin_Godzilla)* (2016), sold 43,951 copies in Japan. *[Mars](/wiki/Mars_(B%27z_album))* (1991), an album by the Japanese rock duo [B'z](/wiki/B%27z) featuring a Godzilla-themed song, sold 1,730,500 copies in Japan.

[Blue Öyster Cult](/wiki/Blue_%C3%96yster_Cult) released the song "[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(Blue_%C3%96yster_Cult_song))" in 1977. It was the first track, and the second of four singles, from their fifth studio album *[Spectres](/wiki/Spectres_(album))* (also 1977). Artists such as [Fu Manchu](/wiki/Fu_Manchu_(band)), [Racer X](/wiki/Racer_X_(band)) and [Double Experience](/wiki/Double_Experience) have included cover versions of this song on their albums. American musician [Michale Graves](/wiki/Michale_Graves) wrote a song titled "Godzilla" for his 2005 album *Punk Rock Is Dead*. The lyrics mention Godzilla and several on-screen adversaries such as Mothra, Hedorah, Destoroyah and Gigan. The Brazilian heavy metal band Sepultura has a song titled "Biotech is Godzilla" on its 1993 release *Chaos A.D*.

Composer [Eric Whitacre](/wiki/Eric_Whitacre) wrote a piece for wind ensemble titled "Godzilla Eats Las Vegas!" The work was commissioned by Thomas Leslie of the [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas) and was premiered in 1996 by the university's wind band. Annotations on the score instruct performers to dress in costume and a "script" is provided for the audience. Since the piece's premiere, it has been performed by notable ensembles including the [United States Marine Band](/wiki/United_States_Marine_Band) and the Scottish National Wind Symphony.

The French death metal band [Gojira](/wiki/Gojira_(band)) named the band after Godzilla's name in Japanese. The song "Simon Says" by Pharoahe Monch is a hip-hop remix of the "Godzilla March" theme song. The instrumental version of this song was notably used in the 2000 film *[Charlie's Angels](/wiki/Charlie%27s_Angels_(2000_film))*. The British band [Lostprophets](/wiki/Lostprophets) released a song called "[We Are Godzilla, You Are Japan](/wiki/We_Are_Godzilla,_You_Are_Japan)" on its second studio album *[Start Something](/wiki/Start_Something)*. The American punk band [Groovie Ghoulies](/wiki/Groovie_Ghoulies) released a song called "Hats off to You (Godzilla)" as a tribute to Godzilla. It is featured on the EP *Freaks on Parade* released in 2002.

The American artist [Doctor Steel](/wiki/Doctor_Steel) released a song called 'Atomic Superstar' about Godzilla on his album *[People of Earth](/wiki/People_of_Earth_(album))* in 2002. In 2003, the British singer [Siouxsie Sioux](/wiki/Siouxsie_Sioux) released the album *[Hái!](/wiki/H%C3%A1i!)* with her band [The Creatures](/wiki/The_Creatures); the album had a Japanese theme with a song dedicated to the monster, simply titled "[Godzilla!](/wiki/Godzilla!)". The record label Shifty issued the compilation album *Destroysall* with 15 songs from 15 bands, ranging from hardcore punk to doom-laden death metal. Not all of the songs are dedicated to Godzilla, but all do appear connected to monsters from Toho Studios. Fittingly, the disc was released on August 1, 2003, the 35th anniversary of the Japanese release of *Destroy All Monsters*.

King Geedorah (a.k.a. [MF Doom](/wiki/MF_Doom)) released *[Take Me to Your Leader](/wiki/Take_Me_to_Your_Leader_(King_Geedorah_album))*, a hip-hop album featuring guests from the group [Monsta Island Czars](/wiki/Monsta_Island_Czars), another Godzilla-themed hip-hop group. These albums include multiple Godzilla samples throughout the series. Taiwanese American electronic musician [Mochipet](/wiki/Mochipet) released the EP *Godzilla Rehab Center* on August 21, 2012, featuring songs named after monsters in the series including Gigan, King Ghidorah, Moguera and Hedorah.

In 2019, American rock band Think Sanity released their debut album featuring songs based on Godzilla, Mothra, and Hedorah. The songs are titled "Sad Kaiju", "Mothra", and "Sludge", respectively. The monsters are also mentioned by name on the track "News at Six" in which they are comically described by newscaster Chip Bentley as destroying a nearby town. The band has mentioned in interviews that they have also written songs based on Biollante, King Ghidorah, and Rodan as well.

### Geographic features

The largest [megamullion](/wiki/Megamullion), located 600 kilometres to the south-east of [Okinotorishima](/wiki/Okinotorishima), the southernmost Japanese island, is named the [Godzilla Megamullion](/wiki/Godzilla_Megamullion). The [Japan Coast Guard](/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard) played a role in name, reaching an agreement with [Toho](/wiki/Toho) who owns the rights to Godzilla. Toho's Chief Godzilla officer Keiji Ota stated that "I am truly honored that (the megamullion) bears Godzilla's name, the Earth's most powerful monster."

## Cultural impact

Main article: [Godzilla in popular culture](/wiki/Godzilla_in_popular_culture)

Godzilla is one of the most recognizable symbols of [Japanese popular culture](/wiki/Japanese_popular_culture) worldwide and is an important facet of Japanese films, embodying the [kaiju](/wiki/Kaij%C5%AB) subset of the [tokusatsu](/wiki/Tokusatsu) genre. It has been considered an allegory of nuclear weapons. The earlier *Godzilla* films, especially the original *Godzilla*, portrayed Godzilla as a frightening, nuclear monster. Godzilla represented the fears that many Japanese held about the nuclear attacks on [Hiroshima](/wiki/Hiroshima) and [Nagasaki](/wiki/Nagasaki) and the possibility of recurrence.

As the series progressed, so did Godzilla, changing into a less destructive and more heroic character. *[Ghidorah, the Three-Headed Monster](/wiki/Ghidorah,_the_Three-Headed_Monster)* (1964) was the turning point in Godzilla's transformation from villain to hero, by pitting him against a greater threat to humanity, [King Ghidorah](/wiki/King_Ghidorah). Godzilla has since been viewed as an [anti-hero](/wiki/Anti-hero). [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) cites Godzilla as a notable example of a villain-turned-hero, along with King Kong, the [James Bond films](/wiki/James_Bond_film)' [Jaws](/wiki/Jaws_(James_Bond)), the [Terminator](/wiki/Terminator_(character)), and *[Rambo](/wiki/Rambo_(franchise))*.

Godzilla is considered "the original radioactive [superhero](/wiki/Superhero)" due to his accidental radioactive [origin story](/wiki/Origin_story) predating [Spider-Man](/wiki/Spider-Man) (1962 debut), though Godzilla did not become a hero until *Ghidorah* in 1964. By the 1970s, Godzilla came to be viewed as a superhero, with the magazine *King of the Monsters* in 1977 describing Godzilla as "Superhero of the '70s." In 1973, Godzilla was voted the most popular [movie monster](/wiki/Movie_monster) in *[The Monster Times](/wiki/The_Monster_Times)* poll, beating [Count Dracula](/wiki/Count_Dracula), [King Kong](/wiki/King_Kong), [Wolf Man](/wiki/Werewolf_fiction), [The Mummy](/wiki/The_Mummy_(Universal_film_franchise)), [Creature From the Black Lagoon](/wiki/Creature_From_the_Black_Lagoon), and [Frankenstein's monster](/wiki/Frankenstein%27s_monster).

In 2010, the [Sea Shepherd Conservation Society](/wiki/Sea_Shepherd_Conservation_Society) named their most recently acquired scout vessel MV *Gojira*. Toho, the people in charge of the *Godzilla* franchise, served them with a notice to remove the name and in response the boat's name was changed in May 2011 to [MV *Brigitte Bardot*](/wiki/MV_Brigitte_Bardot).

[Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg) cited *Godzilla* as an inspiration for *[Jurassic Park](/wiki/Jurassic_Park)* (1993), specifically *[Godzilla, King of the Monsters!](/wiki/Godzilla,_King_of_the_Monsters!)* (1956), which he grew up watching. During its production, Spielberg described *Godzilla* as "the most masterful of all the dinosaur movies because it made you believe it was really happening." *Godzilla* also influenced the Spielberg film *[Jaws](/wiki/Jaws_(film))* (1975). *Godzilla* has also been cited as an inspiration by actor [Tim Allen](/wiki/Tim_Allen) and filmmakers [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) and [Tim Burton](/wiki/Tim_Burton).

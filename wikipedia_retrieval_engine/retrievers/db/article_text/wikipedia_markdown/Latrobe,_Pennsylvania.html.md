**Latrobe** ([/ləˈtroʊb/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*lə-TROHB*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [borough with home-rule status](/wiki/Home_rule_municipality_(Pennsylvania)) in [Westmoreland County, Pennsylvania](/wiki/Westmoreland_County,_Pennsylvania), United States. The population was 8,060 at the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census). It is located near [Chestnut Ridge](/wiki/Chestnut_Ridge_(Laurel_Highlands)) within the [Pittsburgh metropolitan area](/wiki/Pittsburgh_metropolitan_area).

Latrobe was the home of the [Latrobe Brewing Company](/wiki/Latrobe_Brewing_Company), the original brewer of [Rolling Rock](/wiki/Rolling_Rock) beer. Latrobe was the birthplace and childhood home of children's television personality [Fred Rogers](/wiki/Fred_Rogers) and former professional golfer [Arnold Palmer](/wiki/Arnold_Palmer). The [banana split](/wiki/Banana_split) was invented there by David Strickler in 1904. Latrobe is also home to the [training camp](/wiki/Training_camp_(National_Football_League)) of the [Pittsburgh Steelers](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers). Latrobe was long believed to be the site of the first professional [American football](/wiki/American_football) game in 1895 until research found an 1892 game with paid players.

## History

The [Latrobe Athletic Association](/wiki/Latrobe_Athletic_Association) in 1897

In 1852, [Pennsylvania Railroad](/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad) civil engineer Oliver Barnes laid out the plans for the community that was incorporated in 1854 as the borough of Latrobe. Barnes named the town for his best friend and college classmate, [Benjamin Henry Latrobe II](/wiki/Benjamin_Henry_Latrobe_II), who was chief engineer for the [Baltimore and Ohio Railroad](/wiki/Baltimore_and_Ohio_Railroad). Its location along the route of the Pennsylvania Railroad helped Latrobe develop into a significant industrial hub. Latrobe was also served by the [Ligonier Valley Railroad](/wiki/Ligonier_Valley_Railroad) from 1877 to 1952.

From 1895 until 1909, Latrobe was the home of the [Latrobe Athletic Association](/wiki/Latrobe_Athletic_Association), one of the earliest professional [football](/wiki/American_football) teams. The team's quarterback, [John Brallier](/wiki/John_Brallier), became the first football player to admit playing for money. In 1895, he accepted $10 and expenses to play for Latrobe in a 12–0 victory over the [Jeannette Athletic Club](/wiki/Jeannette_Athletic_Club). Brallier was thought to be the first professional football player until the 1960s. Then, documents surfaced showing that [Pudge Heffelfinger](/wiki/Pudge_Heffelfinger), a former three-time [All-American](/wiki/All-American) from [Yale](/wiki/Yale_University), was employed to play [guard](/wiki/Guard_(American_football)) for the [Allegheny Athletic Association](/wiki/Allegheny_Athletic_Association) three years earlier. In 1897, Latrobe was the first football team to play a full season with a team composed entirely of professional players. In 1898, Latrobe and two players from their rivals, the [Greensburg Athletic Association](/wiki/Greensburg_Athletic_Association), formed [the first professional football all-star team](/wiki/1898_Western_Pennsylvania_All-Star_football_team) for a game against the [Duquesne Country and Athletic Club](/wiki/Duquesne_Country_and_Athletic_Club), to be played at Pittsburgh's [Exposition Park](/wiki/Exposition_Park_(Pittsburgh)). Duquesne went on to win the game 16–0. On November 18, 1905, Latrobe defeated the [Canton Bulldogs](/wiki/Canton_Bulldogs), which later became a founding member, and two-time champion, of the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League), 6–0. Aside from Brallier, the Latrobe Athletic Association included several of the era's top players, such as [Ed Abbaticchio](/wiki/Ed_Abbaticchio), [Charles Barney](/wiki/Charles_L._Barney), [Alf Bull](/wiki/Alfred_E._Bull), [Jack Gass](/wiki/Jack_Gass), [Walter Okeson](/wiki/Walter_R._Okeson), [Harry Ryan](/wiki/Harry_Ryan_(American_football)), [Doggie Trenchard](/wiki/Thomas_Trenchard), and [Eddie Wood](/wiki/Eddie_Wood), and manager [Dave Berry](/wiki/Dave_Berry_(American_football)).

The [banana split](/wiki/Banana_split) was invented in 1904 in Latrobe by David Evans Strickler at the pharmacy that later became named Strickler's Drug Store. In 2004, the National Ice Cream Retailers Association (NICRA) certified Latrobe as the birthplace of the banana split. The town holds an annual festival in honor of the dessert.

Historically, two [interurban](/wiki/Interurban) lines served Latrobe. The Westmoreland County Railway Company connected Latrobe to Derry and operated from 1904 to 1932. The Latrobe Street Railway Company connected Latrobe to Kingston and began operations in 1900. This line was purchased by [West Penn Railways](/wiki/West_Penn_Railways), which eventually linked it with its network running through [Youngstown](/wiki/Youngstown,_Pennsylvania), [Pleasant Unity](/wiki/Pleasant_Unity,_Pennsylvania), and eventually to [Greensburg](/wiki/Greensburg,_Pennsylvania) and [Uniontown](/wiki/Uniontown,_Pennsylvania). Service ceased in 1952.

Coal mining was once an important industry in Westmoreland County. While mining activity has largely ceased, most of the city of Latrobe lies in a zone where abandoned underground mines are prevalent, according to a March 1, 2022, article in *The Latrobe Bulletin*. In 2017, a home in the city's Cramer Heights neighborhood started to collapse after its foundation shifted. The house was condemned and eventually had to be torn down. After this incident, the [Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection](/wiki/Pennsylvania_Department_of_Environmental_Protection) urged Latrobe residents to research whether their properties are undermined and consider applying for mine subsidence insurance.

Latrobe has two sites on the [National Register of Historic Places](/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places). [Pennsylvania Railroad Station at Latrobe](/wiki/Latrobe_(Amtrak_station)) was built by the [Pennsylvania Railroad](/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad) in 1903. [Citizens National Bank of Latrobe](/wiki/Citizens_National_Bank_of_Latrobe) is a 1926 structure designed by the Greensburg firm [Batholomew and Smith](/wiki/Paul_Bartholomew).

## Geography

Latrobe is located at [40°18′54″N 79°22′52″W / 40.31500°N 79.38111°W / 40.31500; -79.38111](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Latrobe,_Pennsylvania&params=40_18_54_N_79_22_52_W_type:city) (40.314940, -79.381171). According to the [United States Census Bureau](/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 2.3 square miles (6.0 km2), all land. It has a hot-summer [humid continental climate](/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfa*) and average monthly temperatures range from 28.9 °F in January to 72.0 °F in July.

[Loyalhanna Creek](/wiki/Loyalhanna_Creek), a popular fishing spot, flows through Latrobe. Latrobe shares borders with the townships of [Derry](/wiki/Derry_Township,_Westmoreland_County,_Pennsylvania) to the north, northwest, east, and southeast, and [Unity](/wiki/Unity_Township,_Pennsylvania) to the west and southwest.

As of the [census](/wiki/Census) of 2010, 8,338 people, 3,786 households, and 2,458 families resided in the city. The [population density](/wiki/Population_density) was 3,913.6 people per square mile (1,511.0 people/km2). The 4,258 housing units averaged 1,852.8 per square mile (715.4/km2). The [racial makeup](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)) of the city was 98.78% White, 0.32% African American, 0.08% Native American, 0.44% Asian, 0.07% from other races, and 0.31% from two or more races. Hispanics or Latinos of any race were 0.37% of the population.

Of 3,786 households, 26.0% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 48.1% were married couples living together, 11.0% had a female householder with no husband present, and 38.0% were not families. About 34.1% of all households were made up of individuals, and 17.9% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.22 and the average family size was 2.86.

In the city, the population was distributed as 1,730 persons under the age of 18, 429 persons from 20 to 24, 2583 persons from 25 to 49, 1780 persons from 50 to 64, and 1614 persons who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 42 years. For every 100 females, there were 88.0 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 83.2 males.

The median income for a household in the city was $33,268, and for a family was $42,168. Males had a median income of $31,802 versus $22,227 for females. The [per capita income](/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the city was $18,208. About 6.5% of families and 9.4% of the population were below the [poverty line](/wiki/Poverty_line), including 9.2% of those under age 18 and 7.6% of those age 65 or over.

## Economy

The [Latrobe Brewing Company](/wiki/Latrobe_Brewing_Company), founded in 1939, was one of the largest breweries in the United States and the maker of [Rolling Rock](/wiki/Rolling_Rock) beer. In May 2006, [Anheuser-Busch](/wiki/Anheuser-Busch) purchased the [Rolling Rock](/wiki/Rolling_Rock) brands, but not the brewery. Later in 2006, the plant was bought by [City Brewing Company](/wiki/City_Brewing_Company) and has since produced beers under contract for other vendors.

Metals manufacturer [Kennametal](/wiki/Kennametal) was founded in Latrobe in 1938.

## Government

Latrobe was incorporated as a [borough](/wiki/Borough_(Pennsylvania)) in 1854, and as a borough with [home-rule status](/wiki/Home_rule_municipality_(Pennsylvania)) known as the "City of Latrobe" in 1999. Federally, Latrobe is part of [Pennsylvania's 14th congressional district](/wiki/Pennsylvania%27s_14th_congressional_district).

## Education

Latrobe is served by the public [Greater Latrobe School District](/wiki/Greater_Latrobe_School_District), which includes three elementary schools, one junior high school, and [Greater Latrobe Senior High School](/wiki/Greater_Latrobe_Senior_High_School). Additionally, [Saint Vincent College](/wiki/Saint_Vincent_College) and [Saint Vincent Seminary](/wiki/Saint_Vincent_Seminary) are in the borough, both of which are operated by [Saint Vincent Archabbey](/wiki/Saint_Vincent_Archabbey).

## Media

Latrobe is part of the [Pittsburgh Media Market](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Media_Market). The local newspaper, the *Latrobe Bulletin*, has been in print since the 1870s. Radio stations [WJFG](/wiki/WJFG) and [WXVE](/wiki/WXVE) are licensed to the borough.

## Transportation

Platform at Latrobe station

[Latrobe station](/wiki/Latrobe_station) is served by Amtrak's [*Pennsylvanian*](/wiki/Pennsylvanian_(train)). Westmoreland Transit Daily runs locally and commuter runs to Pittsburgh and [Johnstown](/wiki/Johnstown,_Pennsylvania). [Arnold Palmer Regional Airport](/wiki/Arnold_Palmer_Regional_Airport) is two miles (3 km) southwest of Latrobe.

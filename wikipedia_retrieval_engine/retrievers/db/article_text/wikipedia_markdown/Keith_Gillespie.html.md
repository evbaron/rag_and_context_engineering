**Keith Robert Gillespie** ([/ɡɪˈlɛspi/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*ghih-LES-pee*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born 18 February 1975) is a Northern Irish former professional [footballer](/wiki/Association_football) who plays as a [winger](/wiki/Winger_(association_football)) for FC Mindwell in the [Mid-Ulster Football League](/wiki/Mid-Ulster_Football_League).

He began his career at [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) after winning the [FA Youth Cup](/wiki/FA_Youth_Cup) in 1992, before moving to [Newcastle United](/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.), where he played in the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League). Gillespie also played in the [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League) for [Blackburn Rovers](/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.), [Leicester City](/wiki/Leicester_City) and [Sheffield United](/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.), helping Blackburn win the [Football League Cup](/wiki/Football_League_Cup) in 2002. Towards the end of his career, he played for [Glentoran](/wiki/Glentoran_F.C.) in the [Irish League](/wiki/NIFL_Premiership) and [Longford Town](/wiki/Longford_Town_F.C.) in the [League of Ireland](/wiki/League_of_Ireland).

Gillespie earned 86 caps for [Northern Ireland](/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team) between 1994 and 2008, putting him 10th in their most capped players of all time. He had well-publicised issues with [problem gambling](/wiki/Problem_gambling) during his career, and has since spoken out about gambling.

## Club career

### Early career

Gillespie was born in [Larne](/wiki/Larne), County Antrim. His first years were spent in [Islandmagee](/wiki/Islandmagee), County Antrim, where he attended [Whitehead Primary School](https://archive.today/20130410124820/http://schoolappkit.appspot.com/whiteheadps). He later moved to [Bangor, County Down](/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down) where he attended Rathmore Primary School and [Bangor Grammar School](/wiki/Bangor_Grammar_School). Gillespie played for [Lisburn Youth](/wiki/Lisburn_Youth_F.C.) in [Drumbo](/wiki/Drumbo). He was scouted playing for St Andrews FC from Belfast, playing in the [DAWFL](/wiki/DAWFL), and was the first professional footballer to come from this club.

### Manchester United

Gillespie signed for [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) on leaving school in the summer of 1991, being a member of the [FA Youth Cup](/wiki/FA_Youth_Cup) winning side in 1992. Also in that team were [Ryan Giggs](/wiki/Ryan_Giggs), [Paul Scholes](/wiki/Paul_Scholes), [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham), [Gary Neville](/wiki/Gary_Neville) and [Robbie Savage](/wiki/Robbie_Savage).

Gillespie made his first senior appearance for Manchester United in the [1992–93 season](/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Manchester_United_F.C._season). He scored on his debut against [Bury](/wiki/Bury_F.C.) in a 2–0 [FA Cup](/wiki/FA_Cup) [third round](/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_FA_Cup#Third_round_proper) triumph on 5 January 1993. He was issued with the number 31 shirt for the 1993–94 season with the introduction of squad numbers, but did not play any first team games and was loaned to Division Three club Wigan Athletic, scoring four goals in eight games.

Occasional appearances for United followed in 1994–95, but he was never able to displace [Andrei Kanchelskis](/wiki/Andrei_Kanchelskis) as United's first-choice right winger.

Gillespie scored his only league goal for Manchester United against [Newcastle United](/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) in a 2–0 victory at [Old Trafford](/wiki/Old_Trafford) in October 1994. He subsequently signed for Newcastle on 10 January 1995, as a £1 million component in the £7 million deal (£6 million cash) which took [Andy Cole](/wiki/Andy_Cole) to [Old Trafford](/wiki/Old_Trafford_(football_ground)).

### Newcastle United

On 20 August 1995, the *[News of the World](/wiki/News_of_the_World)* carried reports that Gillespie was subject of an approach from [Alex Ferguson](/wiki/Alex_Ferguson) to return to Manchester United to fill the gap on the right wing being left by the sale of [Andrei Kanchelskis](/wiki/Andrei_Kanchelskis) to [Everton](/wiki/Everton_F.C.), but the return to Old Trafford never happened and up-and-coming youngster [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham) would go on to occupy that position. 18 years later, Gillespie confirmed that Ferguson did contact him regarding a possible return, but claims that he heard nothing more about the prospective transfer after that original telephone conversation with his former manager.

Gillespie stayed at Newcastle for three and a half years and during this time he played 143 games, including 15 European ties (in both the [Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League) and [UEFA Cup](/wiki/UEFA_Cup)) and scored 13 goals. In both the [1995–96](/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_in_English_football) and [1996–97](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_in_English_football) seasons he helped Newcastle to finish second in the Premier League (runners up to Gillespie's former club, Manchester United, on both occasions), being a key member of "The Entertainers". On the first occasion, the Magpies very nearly beat Gillespie's old club to the title, having been 10 points ahead of them by Christmas 1995 before a dismal final three months of the season saw the title sealed by Gillespie's former teammates. Initially injured from a [Phil Neville](/wiki/Phil_Neville) tackle in a 0–2 defeat at Old Trafford on 27 December 1995 causing him to miss the following three games, at the end of the 1995–96 season [Gary Lineker](/wiki/Gary_Lineker), then a BBC pundit, said that one of the main reasons that Newcastle lost out on the title race was because they dropped Gillespie (who had been supplying Newcastle's forwards with a stream of good crosses) for several key games in the latter part of the season.

In September 1996, Gillespie was outed as a [problem gambler](/wiki/Problem_gambler) by [*The Sun*](/wiki/The_Sun_(United_Kingdom)). He then accepted a £5,000 payment by the same tabloid to publicly thank them for helping out his problem. Gillespie has publicly said that he would bet on matches involving his own team, and once lost £52,000 because Newcastle scored a late goal in a game against Stoke City that he had bet on them beating by less than 4 goals.

In his final full season at Newcastle, the [1997–98](/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_in_English_football) season, Gillespie assisted two of [Faustino Asprilla](/wiki/Faustino_Asprilla)'s three goals in a 3–2 Champions League victory over [FC Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) on 17 September 1997. He also scored what he later claimed was his best goal for the club when playing up front due to an injury crisis at home to [Blackburn Rovers](/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.) in 1-1 draw on 25 October 1997. He also helped Newcastle to reach the FA Cup final. However, a foot injury sustained in a 0–2 defeat at [Tottenham Hotspur](/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) on 25 April 1998 meant, despite a fitness test, he was not in the squad for the final and Newcastle lost to [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.). Despite being in manager [Kenny Dalglish](/wiki/Kenny_Dalglish)'s plans, the following pre-season Gillespie failed a medical at [Middlesbrough](/wiki/Middlesbrough_F.C.) but eventually departed Tyneside in a £2.3 million move to [Blackburn Rovers](/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers) following the appointment of [Ruud Gullit](/wiki/Ruud_Gullit) as manager and a final appearance in a 2–2 draw at Middlesbrough on 6 December 1998.

### Blackburn Rovers

Gillespie was unable to help Blackburn avoid relegation in [1998–99](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_in_English_football), and manager [Brian Kidd](/wiki/Brian_Kidd) was sacked later in the year. Gillespie was initially out of favour with new manager [Graeme Souness](/wiki/Graeme_Souness), and another loan spell at Wigan Athletic brought his total appearances for Athletic to 15, from which he scored four goals. He returned to the Blackburn side for the final months of the [2000–01 season](/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_Blackburn_Rovers_F.C._season), as the club gained promotion back to the Premier League. He also started in the [2002 Football League Cup Final](/wiki/2002_Football_League_Cup_Final), setting up a goal by [Matt Jansen](/wiki/Matt_Jansen) in the 2–1 victory over Tottenham Hotspur. Five seasons at [Ewood Park](/wiki/Ewood_Park) brought 137 appearances and 6 goals.

### Leicester City

Gillespie moved to newly promoted [Leicester City](/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C.) on a [free transfer](/wiki/Free_transfer_(football)) on 8 July 2003, signing a two-year contract. He played 48 games and scored two goals in two seasons.

In March 2004, Gillespie and teammates [Frank Sinclair](/wiki/Frank_Sinclair) and [Paul Dickov](/wiki/Paul_Dickov) were held in a prison cell for a week in [La Manga](/wiki/La_Manga), Spain, while charges were made against six more Leicester players. All nine men were cleared when forensic tests proved that none of them had any contact with the three women who accused them.

### Sheffield United

Gillespie signed for [Sheffield United](/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.) on 5 August 2005, signing a one-year contract. This was then extended to June 2007 a month later. In his first season at the club, Gillespie played a role in Sheffield United's successful promotion campaign to the [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League). Gillespie's most memorable goal for Sheffield United came against Charlton, where he scored the winner in the 88th minute with a stunning volley from 25 yards. This goal was also a nominee for the December *[Goal of the Month](/wiki/BBC_Goal_of_the_Month)* competition, which [Paul Scholes](/wiki/Paul_Scholes) eventually won.

On 20 January 2007, during a Premier League match against [Reading](/wiki/Reading_F.C.) at the [Madejski Stadium](/wiki/Madejski_Stadium), Gillespie was sent off for violent conduct, "throwing an elbow in the direction of" Reading's [Stephen Hunt](/wiki/Stephen_Hunt_(footballer,_born_1981)), "within 10 seconds" of coming on as a substitute - play had not even restarted following the substitution. As he made his way off the pitch, Gillespie "threw another punch" at Hunt. He submitted a transfer request soon afterwards, but in July of that year Gillespie recanted and signed a new two-year contract.

In July 2008, he limped out of a pre-season friendly at [Bury](/wiki/Bury_F.C.) and missed the start of the 2008–09 season. Having regained fitness he was unable to regain a first team place and was eventually loaned out to [Charlton Athletic](/wiki/Charlton_Athletic_F.C.). He made only six appearances for the Addicks before being recalled to Bramall Lane as cover for mounting injuries.

Despite being recalled to Sheffield United, Gillespie failed to make another appearance and on 30 January 2009 his contract was terminated by mutual consent. He went to [Bradford City](/wiki/Bradford_City_A.F.C.) with whose manager [Stuart McCall](/wiki/Stuart_McCall) Gillespie had been a teammate at Sheffield United. However, McCall insisted Gillespie was only training with the club to stay fit and help out the younger players, and not on trial.

### Bradford City

Gillespie impressed McCall during training and told the manager he was very keen to gain match experience; as a result, Gillespie signed for Bradford City in March for the rest of the [2008–09 season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Bradford_City_A.F.C._season). Gillespie was an unused substitute in their 1–0 defeat to [Exeter City](/wiki/Exeter_City_F.C.) and so had to wait for his debut which came as a second-half substitute with City already 4–1 down to [AFC Bournemouth](/wiki/AFC_Bournemouth) three days later. After just three appearances Gillespie was not offered a long-term deal by Bradford City. In the summer of 2009 he had a trial with Hungarian side [Ferencváros](/wiki/Ferencv%C3%A1rosi_TC) and had been linked with a move to the [IFA Premiership](/wiki/IFA_Premiership).

### Glentoran

In 2009, Gillespie made a shock move to the east Belfast club, [Glentoran](/wiki/Glentoran_F.C.). It was believed that Gillespie's agents approached Glentoran. He made his debut for Glentoran against Ballymena United in the league, he had previously played for Glentoran the night before for the reserves against Ballymena; Glentoran lost 2–1 in his 1st senior appearance for, at the time, the current league champions. In June 2010, the club announced that Gillespie was to leave after just one season after he and the club failed to agree terms on a new deal.

### Shamrock Rovers

In August 2010, Gillespie's former international teammate [Michael O'Neill](/wiki/Michael_O%27Neill_(footballer)) invited him to play in a friendly for [Shamrock Rovers](/wiki/Shamrock_Rovers_F.C.) in a reserve game against his first club, Manchester United.

### Darlington

In October 2010, Gillespie joined up with [Conference National](/wiki/Conference_National) side [Darlington](/wiki/Darlington_F.C.), later signing with the club. He made three appearances before being released on 23 December 2010.

### Longford Town

On 24 March 2011, Gillespie signed for [League of Ireland First Division](/wiki/League_of_Ireland_First_Division) side [Longford Town](/wiki/Longford_Town_F.C.). Gillespie made his debut from the bench in a local derby against Athlone Town on 2 April with Gillespie's new side coming out on top by two goals to nil.

He was named on the League of Ireland First Division team of the season for [2012](/wiki/2012_League_of_Ireland_First_Division) and was also awarded Longford Town F.C. player of the year, as they lost in the promotion playoffs to [Waterford United](/wiki/Waterford_F.C.).

Gillespie scored his one and only Longford goal in May 2013 against [Cobh Ramblers](/wiki/Cobh_Ramblers). Injuries hampered Gillespie during the 2013 season and he announced his retirement prior to the season's end. His final appearance was as a substitute during Longford's 3–1 victory at home to [Finn Harps](/wiki/Finn_Harps_F.C.) on 7 September 2013.

### FC Mindwell

Gillespie came out of retirement in 2020, aged 45, to play for newly-formed [Mid-Ulster Football League](/wiki/Mid-Ulster_Football_League) side FC Mindwell, a team set up to raise awareness of mental health issues.

## International career

Gillespie is currently tenth place in the list of appearances for Northern Ireland with 86 [caps](/wiki/Cap_(sport)). He made his debut in September 1994 in a 2–1 home defeat by [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal_national_football_team). He played an important role in his country's 3–2 qualifying win against [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) at [Windsor Park](/wiki/Windsor_Park) on 6 September 2006. Gillespie was investigated by the Irish FA for his involvement in a fracas with [George McCartney](/wiki/George_McCartney_(footballer)) on the trip back home from a game in [Iceland](/wiki/Iceland) in September 2007. He was not involved with the Northern Ireland set-up since being omitted from the squad that faced [San Marino](/wiki/San_Marino_national_football_team) in February 2009. His final cap was won in a 2–0 defeat by [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) at Windsor Park in November 2008.

## Personal life

Gillespie was declared legally bankrupt on 1 October 2010 by the Belfast High Court. In 2013, he released his autobiography, *How Not to Be a Football Millionaire*, in which he describes his gambling addiction, estimating he lost more than £7 million. Reviewer Robbie Meredith of *[When Saturday Comes](/wiki/When_Saturday_Comes)* wrote that "It is too cliched to claim that Gillespie achieves redemption at the end of his tale. Rather he gains the uncertain gift of a better understanding of himself. In doing so, he provides a compelling glimpse into the dark void inherent in the modern age of adrenaline-fuelled football celebrity". Gillespie has supported the Gambling with Lives charity, which calls for tighter regulation of gambling and offers support for people with gambling problems.

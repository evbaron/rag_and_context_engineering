The [Commonwealth of Nations](/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) is a [voluntary association](/wiki/Voluntary_association) of 56 [sovereign states](/wiki/Sovereign_state), referred to as **Commonwealth countries**. Most of them were [British colonies](/wiki/Crown_colony) or [dependencies](/wiki/Dependent_territory) of those [colonies](/wiki/Colony).

No government in the Commonwealth exercises power over the others, as is the case in a [political union](/wiki/Political_union). Rather, the Commonwealth is an [international organisation](/wiki/International_organisation) in which countries with diverse social, political, and economic backgrounds are regarded as equal in status, and cooperate within a framework of common values and goals, as outlined in the [Singapore Declaration](/wiki/Singapore_Declaration) issued in 1971. Such common values and goals include the promotion of [democracy](/wiki/Democracy), [human rights](/wiki/Human_rights), [good governance](/wiki/Good_governance), the [rule of law](/wiki/Rule_of_law), [civil liberties](/wiki/Civil_liberties), [equality before the law](/wiki/Equality_before_the_law), [free trade](/wiki/Free_trade), [multilateralism](/wiki/Multilateralism), and [world peace](/wiki/World_peace), which are promoted through multilateral projects and meetings, such as the [Commonwealth Games](/wiki/Commonwealth_Games), held once every four years.

The symbol of this free association is the [Head of the Commonwealth](/wiki/Head_of_the_Commonwealth), currently [King Charles III](/wiki/King_Charles_III). All heads of the Commonwealth to date have been monarchs of the United Kingdom. The office of Head of the Commonwealth does not imbue the holder with any political or executive power over any Commonwealth member states; the position is purely symbolic and titular, and it is the [Commonwealth Secretary-General](/wiki/Commonwealth_Secretary-General) who is the chief executive of the Commonwealth.

The Commonwealth was first officially formed in 1926 when the [Balfour Declaration](/wiki/Balfour_Declaration_of_1926) of the [Imperial Conference](/wiki/1926_Imperial_Conference) recognised the full sovereignty of [Dominions](/wiki/Dominion). Known as the *British Commonwealth of Nations*, the original and therefore earliest members were [Australia](/wiki/Australia), [Canada](/wiki/Canada), the [Irish Free State](/wiki/Irish_Free_State), [Newfoundland](/wiki/Dominion_of_Newfoundland), [New Zealand](/wiki/Dominion_of_New_Zealand), [South Africa](/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa), and the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom). It was re-stated by the 1930 conference and incorporated in the [Statute of Westminster](/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931) the following year (although Australia and New Zealand did not adopt the statute until 1942 and 1947, respectively). In 1949, the [London Declaration](/wiki/London_Declaration) marked the birth of the modern Commonwealth and the adoption of its present name. The members have a combined population of 2.6 billion, almost a third of [the world's population](/wiki/World_population), of whom 1.419 billion live in [India](/wiki/India), and 95% live in [Africa](/wiki/Africa) and [Asia](/wiki/Asia) combined.

The most recent members to join were the Francophone African nations of [Gabon](/wiki/Gabon) and [Togo](/wiki/Togo) on 29 June 2022, who along with [Mozambique](/wiki/Mozambique) and [Rwanda](/wiki/Rwanda), are unusual in not having a historical constitutional relationship with the United Kingdom or other Commonwealth states.

As of 2026, fifteen of the member states are [Commonwealth realms](/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), with the [Head of the Commonwealth](/wiki/Head_of_the_Commonwealth) as their [heads of state](/wiki/Head_of_state), five others are [monarchies](/wiki/Monarchy) with their own individual monarchs ([Brunei](/wiki/Brunei) (since 1984), [Eswatini](/wiki/Eswatini) (since 1968; formerly Swaziland), [Lesotho](/wiki/Lesotho) (since 1966), [Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia) (since 1963), and [Tonga](/wiki/Tonga) (since 1970)), and [the rest are republics](/wiki/Republics_in_the_Commonwealth_of_Nations).

The [Republic of Ireland](/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland) (as of 1949 according to the Commonwealth; 1936 according to the Irish government) and [Zimbabwe](/wiki/Zimbabwe) (2003) are former members of the Commonwealth. Zimbabwe is in the process of trying to return to its membership of the Commonwealth since [Emmerson Mnangagwa](/wiki/Emmerson_Mnangagwa) became [President of Zimbabwe](/wiki/President_of_Zimbabwe) when [Robert Mugabe](/wiki/Robert_Mugabe) was overthrown in the [2017 Zimbabwean coup d'etat](/wiki/2017_Zimbabwean_coup_d%27etat).

The [2007 Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting](/wiki/2007_Commonwealth_Heads_of_Government_Meeting) agreed on the core criteria for membership. An applicant country should have historic constitutional association with an existing Commonwealth member, aside from exceptional circumstances which are only considered on a case-by-case basis.

Most Commonwealth members have constitutional links with the United Kingdom and the former [British Empire](/wiki/British_Empire). Former British dependencies are eligible to join the Commonwealth providing they agree and commit to the Commonwealth principles, which were laid out in the [Singapore Declaration](/wiki/Singapore_Declaration) and reaffirmed in the [Lusaka Declaration](/wiki/Lusaka_Declaration), the [Langkawi Declaration](/wiki/Langkawi_Declaration) and the [Harare Declaration](/wiki/Harare_Declaration).

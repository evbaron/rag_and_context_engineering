For the [national arrondissement](/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France) of the city of [Paris](/wiki/Paris), see [Arrondissement of Paris](/wiki/Arrondissement_of_Paris).

The [City of Paris](/wiki/Paris) is divided into twenty *[arrondissements municipaux](/wiki/Municipal_arrondissements_of_France)*, administrative districts, referred to as ***arrondissements*** (French: [[aʁɔ̃dismɑ̃]](/wiki/Help:IPA/French) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:LL-Q150_(fra)-0x010C-arrondissement.wav)). These are not to be confused with departmental [arrondissements](/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France), which subdivide the larger French [departments](/wiki/Departments_of_France).

The number of the arrondissement is indicated by the last two digits in most Parisian [postal codes](/wiki/Postal_codes_in_France), 75001 up to 75020. In addition to their number, each arrondissement has a name, often for a local monument. For example, the [5th arrondissement](/wiki/5th_arrondissement_of_Paris) is also called "Panthéon" in reference to the [eponymous building](/wiki/Panth%C3%A9on). The first four arrondissements have a shared administration, called [Paris Centre](/wiki/Paris_Centre).

## Description

The twenty arrondissements (French: "rounding") are arranged in the form of a [clockwise](/wiki/Clockwise) [spiral](/wiki/Spiral), often likened to a [snail shell](/wiki/Gastropod_shell#Morphology), starting from the middle of the city, with the first on the [Right Bank](/wiki/Rive_Droite) (north bank) of the [Seine](/wiki/Seine).

In French, notably on street signs, the number is often given in Roman numerals. For example, the [Eiffel Tower](/wiki/Eiffel_Tower) belongs to the *[VIIe arrondissement](/wiki/7th_arrondissement_of_Paris)*, while [Gare de l'Est](/wiki/Gare_de_l%27Est_(Paris_M%C3%A9tro)) is in the [*Xe arrondissement*.](/wiki/10th_arrondissement_of_Paris) In daily speech, people use the [ordinal number](/wiki/Ordinal_number_(linguistics)) corresponding to the arrondissement, e.g. "Elle habite dans le sixième", "She lives in the 6th (arrondissement)".

Due to [suburbanization](/wiki/Suburbanization), the population of Paris has gradually shifted outward, with only two arrondissements still growing.

## Governance

See also: [Council of Paris](/wiki/Council_of_Paris)

Uniquely among French cities, Paris is both a municipality (*commune*) and a [department](/wiki/Departments_of_France) (*département*). Under the PLM Law (*Loi PLM*) of 1982, which redefined the governance of Paris, Lyon, and Marseille, hence the PLM [acronym](/wiki/Acronym), there are both a city council called the [Council of Paris](/wiki/Council_of_Paris), and 20 arrondissement councils in Paris. The PLM Law set limits to the prerogatives of the mayor of Paris, who has to deal with the powers granted to the prefect of police on security issues.

The 20 [arrondissement councils](/wiki/Conseil_d%27arrondissement) (conseils d'arrondissement) are similar in operation to a municipal council (conseil municipal), but with very few powers. Its members are elected at municipal elections in the same way as in municipalities with more than 3,500 inhabitants. Two-thirds of each arrondissement council is made up of members elected specifically as arrondissement councillors. The remaining third are Council of Paris members representing the arrondissement, sitting *[ex officio](/wiki/Ex_officio)*.

For example, the council of the [19th arrondissement](/wiki/19th_arrondissement_of_Paris) has 42 members. 28 are *conseillers d'arrondissement* who only sit on the arrondissement council. 14 are *conseillers de Paris* who also sit on the city council. At its first meeting after the elections, each arrondissement council elects its mayor.

Each arrondissement is subdivided administratively into four *quartiers*. Paris thus has 80 *quartiers administratifs*, each containing a police station. For a table giving the names of the eighty *quartiers*, see [Quarters of Paris](/wiki/Quarters_of_Paris).

## History

A map showing the twelve original arrondissements in 1795. The surrounding grey area shows the size of Paris after [the expansion in 1860](/wiki/Haussmann%27s_renovation_of_Paris#Paris_doubles_in_size_–_the_annexation_of_1860).

On 11 October 1795, Paris was divided into [twelve arrondissements](/wiki/Arrondissements_of_Paris_(1795-1859)). They were numbered from west to east. The numbers 1–9 were on the [Right Bank](/wiki/Rive_Droite) of the [Seine](/wiki/Seine). The numbers were 10–12 on the [Left Bank](/wiki/Rive_Gauche). Each arrondissement was subdivided into four *quartiers*, which corresponded to the 48 original districts created in 1790.

In the late 1850s, Emperor [Napoleon III](/wiki/Napol%C3%A9on_III_of_France) and the Prefect of the [Seine](/wiki/Seine_(department)) Baron [Haussmann](/wiki/Georges-Eug%C3%A8ne_Haussmann) developed a plan to incorporate several of the surrounding communes into the Paris jurisdiction. In 1859, Parliament passed the necessary legislation, and the expansion took effect when the law was promulgated on 3 November 1859. City taxes were extended to the new neighborhoods in July 1860.

The previous twelve arrondissements were done away with, and [twenty new arrondissements were created](/wiki/Haussmann%27s_renovation_of_Paris#Paris_doubles_in_size_–_the_annexation_of_1860). In historical records, when it is necessary to distinguish between the two systems, the original arrondissements are indicated by adding the term *ancienne* ("former" or "old"), for example, *2ème ancienne* or *7ème anc*.

Before the reorganization, non-married couples who [lived together](/wiki/Cohabitation) were said to have "married at the town hall of the 13th arrondissement" ("*se marier à la mairie du 13e arrondissement*"), as a jocular reference to there being no 13th. When Haussmann released his plan for the new boundaries and numbering system, residents of [Passy](/wiki/Passy) objected because it placed them in the new 13th arrondissement. The mayor of Passy, Jean-Frédéric Possoz, devised the numbering of the arrondissements in a spiral pattern, beginning on the Right Bank, which put Passy in the [16th](/wiki/16th_arrondissement_of_Paris). This system turned the Louvre area, which contained the [Tuileries Palace](/wiki/Tuileries_Palace) and other imperial palaces, into the [1st](/wiki/1st_arrondissement_of_Paris). The Gobelins area became the [13th](/wiki/13th_arrondissement_of_Paris) instead.

In early 2016, mayor [Anne Hidalgo](/wiki/Anne_Hidalgo) proposed that the first four arrondissements should have their administrations merged. The [Council of Paris](/wiki/Council_of_Paris) approved this in February 2016. The four have a combined population of about 100,000, with the [1st](/wiki/1st_arrondissement_of_Paris), [2nd](/wiki/2nd_arrondissement_of_Paris), [4th](/wiki/4th_arrondissement_of_Paris), and [3rd](/wiki/3rd_arrondissement_of_Paris) arrondissements in that order being the four smallest in Paris. In August 2016, the matter was taken up in the [National Assembly](/wiki/National_Assembly_(France)), and approved in February 2017.

In October 2018, in a postal referendum, the town hall of the 3rd arrondissement was chosen to house the new shared administration. The name "[Paris Centre](/wiki/Paris_Centre)" was chosen for the sector. In June 2020, the reform was implemented, the day after the second round of the [2020 Paris municipal election](/wiki/2020_Paris_municipal_election). The four arrondissements now share a mayor and a district council. The four arrondissements continue to exist, but are no longer used as administrative and electoral sectors.

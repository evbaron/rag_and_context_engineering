# 1965 Leeds City Council election

The **1965 municipal elections for [Leeds](/wiki/County_Borough_of_Leeds)** were held on Thursday 13 May 1965, with one third of the council and an extra vacancy in Allerton to be elected.

Building upon the [previous year](/wiki/1964_Leeds_City_Council_election), the [Conservatives](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)) fully reversed the downward trend they'd been on since [1960](/wiki/1960_Leeds_City_Council_election). With a whopping 10.2% swing their way, they defeated the [Labour Party](/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK)) in a manner not seen since [1951](/wiki/1951_Leeds_City_Council_election), with Labour's share reduced to the thirties - surpassing even their record low then.

The Conservatives six gains were largely a regaining of Labour's [1963 gains](/wiki/1963_Leeds_City_Council_election), with the notable exceptions of [Beeston](/wiki/Beeston,_Leeds), which they already held, and [Kirkstall](/wiki/Kirkstall) - a first for the ward, which had been monolithically Labour since the boundary changes in 1951.

The Conservatives also recovered [Roundhay](/wiki/Roundhay) from Labour who gained it in a by-election in 1963.

Elsewhere, the [Liberals](/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK)) continued their decline from the [1962](/wiki/1962_Leeds_City_Council_election) highs, now at near enough where they were pre-spike. In contrast, the [Communists](/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Great_Britain), having steadily raised their candidates in each election since the mid-1950s were now fielding a record of 12, achieving party records in both vote and share. Turnout fell again by just over two percent on last year's figure to 34.5%.

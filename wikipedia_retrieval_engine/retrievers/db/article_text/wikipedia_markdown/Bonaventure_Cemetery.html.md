**Bonaventure Cemetery** is a [rural cemetery](/wiki/Rural_cemetery) located on a scenic bluff of the [Wilmington River](/wiki/Wilmington_River_(Georgia)), southeast of downtown [Savannah, Georgia](/wiki/Savannah,_Georgia). The cemetery's prominence grew when it was featured in the 1994 novel *[Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil](/wiki/Midnight_in_the_Garden_of_Good_and_Evil)* by [John Berendt](/wiki/John_Berendt), and in [the subsequent movie](/wiki/Midnight_in_the_Garden_of_Good_and_Evil_(film)), directed by [Clint Eastwood](/wiki/Clint_Eastwood), based on the book. It is the largest of the city's municipal cemeteries, containing nearly 160 acres (0.65 km2).

The entrance to the cemetery is located at 330 Bonaventure Road. Immediately inside the gates is the large and ornate [Gaston Tomb](/wiki/The_Gaston_Tomb), built in memory of [William Gaston](/wiki/William_Gaston_(merchant)), a prominent merchant.

## History

The cemetery is located on the former site of [Bonaventure Plantation](/wiki/Bonaventure_Plantation), originally owned by [Colonel](/wiki/Colonel) John Mullryne. On March 10, 1846, [Commodore](/wiki/Commodore_(United_States)) [Josiah Tattnall III](/wiki/Josiah_Tattnall_III) sold the 600-acre (2.4 km2) plantation and its private cemetery to Peter Wiltberger. The first burials took place in 1850, and three years later, Peter Wiltberger himself was entombed in a family vault.

Major William H. Wiltberger, the son of Peter, formed the Evergreen Cemetery Company on June 12, 1868. On July 7, 1907, the City of Savannah purchased the Evergreen Cemetery Company, making the cemetery public and changing the name to Bonaventure Cemetery.

In 1867, [John Muir](/wiki/John_Muir) began his *Thousand-Mile Walk* to Florida and the Gulf. In October, he sojourned for six days and nights in the cemetery, sleeping upon graves overnight, this being the safest and cheapest accommodation that he could find while he waited for money to be expressed from home. He found the cemetery breathtakingly beautiful and inspiring and wrote a lengthy chapter upon it, ["Camping in the Tombs"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Thousand-Mile_Walk_To_The_Gulf/Chapter_4).

[Greenwich Cemetery](/wiki/Greenwich_Cemetery_(Savannah,_Georgia)) became an addition to Bonaventure in 1933.

## Operations

Citizens of Savannah and others may purchase interment rights in Bonaventure.

The cemetery is open to the public daily from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. There is no admission fee.

Adjacent to Bonaventure Cemetery is the privately owned and newer Forest Lawn Cemetery and [Columbarium](/wiki/Columbarium).

## Department of Cemeteries

The main office of the City of Savannah's Department of Cemeteries is located on the Bonaventure Cemetery grounds in the Bonaventure Administrative Building at the entrance.

## Bonaventure Historical Society

The cemetery became the subject of a non-profit group, the Bonaventure Historical Society, in May 1997. The group has compiled an index of the burials at the cemetery.

## *Bird Girl*

The cover photograph for the best-selling book *[Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil](/wiki/Midnight_in_the_Garden_of_Good_and_Evil)*, taken by [Jack Leigh](/wiki/Jack_Leigh), featured an evocative sculpture of a young girl, the so-called *[Bird Girl](/wiki/Bird_Girl)*, that had been in the cemetery, essentially unnoticed, for over 50 years. After the publication of the book, the sculpture was relocated from the cemetery in 1997 for display in [Telfair Museums](/wiki/Telfair_Museums) in Savannah. In late 2014, the statue was moved to a dedicated space in the Telfair Museums' [Jepson Center for the Arts](/wiki/Jepson_Center_for_the_Arts) on West York Street, in Savannah.

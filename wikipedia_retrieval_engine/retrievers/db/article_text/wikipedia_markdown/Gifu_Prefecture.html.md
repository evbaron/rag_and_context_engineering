**Gifu Prefecture** (岐阜県, *Gifu-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [[ɡʲi.ɸɯ, ɡʲi.ɸɯ̥ꜜ.keɴ]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese)) is a [prefecture](/wiki/Prefectures_of_Japan) of [Japan](/wiki/Japan) located in the [Chūbu region](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu_region) of [Honshu](/wiki/Honshu). Gifu Prefecture has a population of 2,040,000 (as of April 1, 2025) and has a geographic area of 10,621 [square kilometres](/wiki/Square_kilometre) (4,101 [sq mi](/wiki/Square_mile)). Gifu Prefecture borders [Toyama Prefecture](/wiki/Toyama_Prefecture) to the north; [Ishikawa Prefecture](/wiki/Ishikawa_Prefecture) to the northwest, [Fukui Prefecture](/wiki/Fukui_Prefecture) and [Shiga Prefecture](/wiki/Shiga_Prefecture) to the west, [Mie Prefecture](/wiki/Mie_Prefecture) to the southwest, [Aichi Prefecture](/wiki/Aichi_Prefecture) to the south, and [Nagano Prefecture](/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to the east.

[Gifu](/wiki/Gifu) is the capital and largest city of Gifu Prefecture, with other major cities including [Ōgaki](/wiki/%C5%8Cgaki), [Kakamigahara](/wiki/Kakamigahara), and [Tajimi](/wiki/Tajimi).

Gifu Prefecture is located in the center of Japan, one of only eight [landlocked](/wiki/Landlocked) prefectures, and features the country's [center of population](/wiki/Center_of_population). Gifu Prefecture has served as the historic [crossroads](/wiki/Intersection_(road)) of Japan with routes connecting the east to the west, including the [Nakasendō](/wiki/Nakasend%C5%8D), one of the [Five Routes](/wiki/Edo_Five_Routes) of the [Edo period](/wiki/Edo_period). Gifu Prefecture was a long-term residence of [Oda Nobunaga](/wiki/Oda_Nobunaga) and [Saitō Dōsan](/wiki/Sait%C5%8D_D%C5%8Dsan), two influential figures of Japanese history in the [Sengoku period](/wiki/Sengoku_period), spawning the popular phrase "control Gifu and you control Japan" in the late [Medieval era](/wiki/History_of_Japan#Feudal_Japan). Gifu Prefecture is known for its traditional *[Washi](/wiki/Washi)* paper industry, including [Gifu lanterns](/wiki/Gifu_lanterns) and [Gifu umbrellas](/wiki/Gifu_umbrellas), and as a center for the [Japanese swordsmithing](/wiki/Japanese_swordsmithing) and [cutlery](/wiki/Japanese_kitchen_knife) industries. Gifu Prefecture is home to [Gifu Castle](/wiki/Gifu_Castle), the 1,300-year-old tradition of [cormorant fishing on the Nagara River](/wiki/Cormorant_fishing_on_the_Nagara_River), and the site of the [Battle of Sekigahara](/wiki/Battle_of_Sekigahara).

## History

See also: [List of Historic Sites of Japan (Gifu)](/wiki/List_of_Historic_Sites_of_Japan_(Gifu))

The land area that makes up modern-day Gifu became part of the [Yamato Court](/wiki/Yamato_period) around the middle of the fourth century. Because it is in the middle of the island of [Honshu](/wiki/Honshu), it has been the site of many decisive battles throughout Japan's history, the oldest major one being the [Jinshin War](/wiki/Jinshin_War) in 672, which led to the establishment of [Emperor Tenmu](/wiki/Emperor_Tenmu) as the 40th emperor of Japan.

The area of Gifu Prefecture consists of the [old provinces](/wiki/Old_provinces_of_Japan) of [Hida](/wiki/Hida_Province) and [Mino](/wiki/Mino_Province), as well as smaller parts of [Echizen](/wiki/Echizen_Province) and [Shinano](/wiki/Shinano_Province). The name of the prefecture derives from its capital city, [Gifu](/wiki/Gifu), which was named by [Oda Nobunaga](/wiki/Oda_Nobunaga) during [his campaign](/wiki/Siege_of_Inabayama_Castle) to unify all of Japan in 1567. The first character used comes from Qishan (**岐**山), a legendary mountain from which most of China was unified, whereas the second character comes from [Qufu](/wiki/Qufu) (曲**阜**), the birthplace of [Confucius](/wiki/Confucius). Nobunaga chose those characters because he wanted to unify all of Japan and he wanted to be viewed as a great mind.

Historically, the prefecture served as the center of swordmaking for the whole of Japan, with [Seki](/wiki/Seki,_Gifu) being known for making the best swords in Japan. More recently, its strengths have been in fashion (primarily in the city of Gifu) and aerospace engineering ([Kakamigahara](/wiki/Kakamigahara)).

On October 28, 1891, the present-day city of [Motosu](/wiki/Motosu,_Gifu) was the [epicenter](/wiki/Epicenter) for the [Mino–Owari earthquake](/wiki/1891_Mino%E2%80%93Owari_earthquake), the second largest earthquake to ever hit Japan. The earthquake, estimated at 8.0 ([surface-wave magnitude](/wiki/Surface-wave_magnitude)), left a [fault scarp](/wiki/Fault_scarp) that can still be seen today.

## Geography

One of the few landlocked prefectures in Japan, Gifu shares borders with seven other prefectures: [Toyama](/wiki/Toyama_Prefecture), [Ishikawa](/wiki/Ishikawa_Prefecture), [Fukui](/wiki/Fukui_Prefecture), [Shiga](/wiki/Shiga_Prefecture), [Mie](/wiki/Mie_Prefecture), [Aichi](/wiki/Aichi_Prefecture), and [Nagano](/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture). Japan's [postal codes](/wiki/Postal_code#Japan) all start with a three-digit number, ranging from 001 to 999. Part of Gifu has the 500 prefix, reflecting its location in the center of Japan.
The [center of Japanese population](/wiki/Center_of_population) is currently located in Seki City, Gifu Prefecture. The center of population is a hypothetical point at which a country is perfectly balanced assuming each person has a uniform weight. The spot was calculated using the 2005 census.

As of 31 March 2019, 18 percent of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](/wiki/Prefectural_Natural_Park), namely the [Hakusan](/wiki/Hakusan_National_Park) and [Chūbu-Sangaku](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu-Sangaku_National_Park) National Parks, [Hida-Kisogawa](/wiki/Hida-Kisogawa_Quasi-National_Park) and [Ibi-Sekigahara-Yōrō](/wiki/Ibi-Sekigahara-Y%C5%8Dr%C5%8D_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks, and fifteen Prefectural Natural Parks.

### Regions

Gifu has five unofficial regions, which allows local municipalities to work together to promote the surrounding area. The five regions are [Seinō](/wiki/Sein%C5%8D), [Gifu](/wiki/Gifu_(region)), [Chūnō](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABn%C5%8D), [Tōnō](/wiki/T%C5%8Dn%C5%8D) and [Hida](/wiki/Hida_(region)). The borders of the regions are loosely defined, but they are usually delineated among major cities.

### Topography

The northern [Hida region](/wiki/Hida_(region)) is dominated by tall mountains, including parts of the [Japanese Alps](/wiki/Japanese_Alps). The southern [Mino](/wiki/Mino_Province) region is mostly parts of the fertile [Nōbi Plain](/wiki/N%C5%8Dbi_Plain), a vast plains area with arable soil. Most of the prefecture's population lives in the southern part of the prefecture, near the [designated city](/wiki/Cities_designated_by_government_ordinance_of_Japan) of [Nagoya](/wiki/Nagoya,_Aichi).

The mountainous Hida region contains the [Hida Mountains](/wiki/Hida_Mountains), which are referred to as the "Northern Alps" in Japan. The [Ryōhaku Mountains](/wiki/Ry%C5%8Dhaku_Mountains) are also in the Hida region. Other major ranges include the [Ibuki Mountains](/wiki/Ibuki_Mountains) and the [Yōrō Mountains](/wiki/Y%C5%8Dr%C5%8D_Mountains).

Much of the Mino region is made up of the [alluvial plain](/wiki/Alluvial_plain) of the [Kiso Three Rivers](/wiki/Kiso_Three_Rivers), which are the [Kiso River](/wiki/Kiso_River), [Nagara River](/wiki/Nagara_River) and [Ibi River](/wiki/Ibi_River). The sources of Kiso river is in Nagano prefecture, and those of the others are in Gifu prefecture. They eventually run through Aichi and Mie prefectures before emptying into [Ise Bay](/wiki/Ise_Bay). Other major rivers in the prefecture include the [Miya](/wiki/Jinz%C5%AB_River), [Takahara](/wiki/Takahara_River), [Shō](/wiki/Sh%C5%8D_River), [Toki (Shōnai)](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dnai_River), [Yahagi](/wiki/Yahagi_River), and [Itoshiro](/wiki/Itoshiro_River) rivers.

### Climate

View from the top of a hill in [Magome-juku](/wiki/Magome-juku), Nakatsugawa, Gifu Prefecture

Gifu's climate varies from [humid subtropical climate](/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) in the south, eventually making the transition to [humid continental climate](/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) in the north.

Because the Mino region is surrounded by low mountains, the temperature fluctuates through the year, from hot summers to cold winters. The eastern city of [Tajimi](/wiki/Tajimi,_Gifu), for example, often records the hottest temperature in Japan each year and is considered to be the hottest city within Honshu boasting an average daytime high of 34.1 °C (93.4 °F) during the peak of summer. On August 16, 2007, Tajimi set the record for the hottest day recorded in Japan's history—40.9 °C (105.6 °F). Summers are hotter, as the landlocked area becomes a heat island, and the temperature rises even further when hot, dry [foehn winds](/wiki/Foehn_wind) blow over the Ibuki Mountains from the [Kansai region](/wiki/Kansai_region). The Hida region, with its higher elevation and northerly latitude, is significantly cooler than the Mino region, although there are sometimes extremely hot days there too. The Hida region is more famous for its harsh winters, bringing extremely heavy snowfall, especially in the northwestern areas. Gifu boasts a high amount of skiing locations. [Shōkawa-chō](/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dkawa,_Gifu), part of the city of [Takayama](/wiki/Takayama,_Gifu), is up in the mountains, and its location has led it to be called the coldest inhabited place on Honshū.

## Economy

*[Mitarashi dango](/wiki/Dango)*, a traditional sweet dumpling from [Hida, Gifu](/wiki/Hida,_Gifu)

Traditional industries such as paper-making and agriculture are found in Gifu, but its economy is dominated by the manufacturing sector including aerospace and automotive, with industrial complexes extending from the Nagoya area. A wealth of small component manufacturing is also found, such as precision machines, dye and mold making, and plastic forming.

### Traditional industries

[Cormorant fishing](/wiki/Cormorant_fishing) in Nagara River

Gifu is famous for [cormorant fishing](/wiki/Cormorant_Fishing_on_the_Nagara_River), which has a history of over 1,300 years. Agriculture is also a major industry because of Gifu's vast, arable plains. The forests in the north provide materials for woodworking and for the viewing boats used in cormorant fishing.

The Mino region has long been known for its high-quality paper called *[Mino washi](/wiki/Mino_washi)*, which is stronger and thinner than most other papers in Japan, and was used by the Japanese military during World War II. Other paper-based products include [Gifu Lanterns](/wiki/Gifu_Lanterns) and [Gifu Umbrellas](/wiki/Gifu_Umbrellas), made in the prefectural capital of [Gifu](/wiki/Gifu,_Gifu). Other traditional goods include *mino-yaki* pottery in [Tajimi](/wiki/Tajimi,_Gifu), [Toki](/wiki/Toki,_Gifu), and [Mizunami](/wiki/Mizunami,_Gifu), [cutlery](/wiki/Cutlery) in [Seki](/wiki/Seki,_Gifu), and [lacquerware](/wiki/Lacquerware) in [Takayama](/wiki/Takayama,_Gifu). [Sake](/wiki/Sake) is often brewed with clear water from the rivers.

### Modern industries

[Kakamigahara](/wiki/Kakamigahara,_Gifu) has a large role in the prefecture's modern industries. It boasts large aerospace facilities of both [Kawasaki Heavy Industries](/wiki/Kawasaki_Heavy_Industries) and [Mitsubishi Heavy Industries](/wiki/Mitsubishi_Heavy_Industries), as well as many [metalworking](/wiki/Metalworking) and [manufacturing](/wiki/Manufacturing) companies.

Information technology (IT) is gaining a foothold in the prefecture with both Softopia Japan in [Ōgaki](/wiki/%C5%8Cgaki,_Gifu) and VR Techno Japan (part of Techno Plaza) in Kakamigahara. The capital city of Gifu, located between Ōgaki and Kakamigahara, is also working to strengthen its IT fields, too.

### Tourism

Traditional housing in Shirakawa-gō

Gifu has many popular tourist attractions, bringing visitors to all parts of the prefecture. The most popular places are Gifu, [Gero](/wiki/Gero,_Gifu), [Shirakawa](/wiki/Shirakawa,_Gifu_(village)) and [Takayama](/wiki/Takayama,_Gifu). Gero is known for its relaxing [hot springs](/wiki/Hot_spring), which attract visitors throughout the year. Shirakawa's [historic villages](/wiki/Historic_Villages_of_Shirakawa-g%C5%8D_and_Gokayama) are a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](/wiki/World_Heritage_Site). Takayama is famous for retaining its original appearance and is often referred to as *Little [Kyoto](/wiki/Kyoto)*.

In addition to international tourists, Gifu also plays host to many international events. The World Event and Convention Complex Gifu is available for many types of events. Other areas of Gifu, too, bring international events. The [World Rowing Championships](/wiki/World_Rowing_Championships) were held in the city of [Kaizu](/wiki/Kaizu,_Gifu) in 2005. The [FIS Snowboard World Cup](/wiki/FIS_Snowboard_World_Cup) was held in the city of [Gujo](/wiki/Gujo,_Gifu) in 2008. The [APEC](/wiki/APEC) Japan 2010 SME Ministerial Meetings were held in [Gifu City](/wiki/Gifu_City).

### Science

The Kamioka area of the city of [Hida](/wiki/Hida,_Gifu) is home to the [Kamioka Observatory](/wiki/Kamioka_Observatory) underground laboratory. Located 1,000 m (3,281 ft) underground in Kamioka Mining and Smelting Co.'s Mozumi Mine, the [Super-Kamiokande](/wiki/Super-Kamiokande) experiment searches for neutrinos from the high atmosphere, the sun and supernovae, while the [KamLAND](/wiki/KamLAND) experiment searches for antineutrinos from regional nuclear reactors. The Super-Kamiokande consists of a cylindrical stainless steel tank that is 41.4 m (136 ft) tall and 39.3 m (129 ft) in diameter holding 50,000 tons of ultra-pure water. Some of the 11,146 photomultiplier tubes are on display at the [Miraikan](/wiki/Miraikan) in Tokyo. The same facility also hosts the [CLIO](/wiki/CLIO) prototype and [KAGRA](/wiki/KAGRA) gravitational wave detector.

## Demographics

Gifu prefecture population pyramid in 2020

The prefecture's population was 2,04,000, as of 1 April 2025, with approximately 1.8 million people in the cities and the rest in towns and villages. The percentage of male and female residents is 48.4% and 51.6%, respectively. 14.4% of the population is no more than 14 years old, with 22.1% of the population being at least 65 years old.

According to Japan's [census](/wiki/Census#Japan), the country's [center of population](/wiki/Center_of_population) is located in Gifu Prefecture. In 2000, it was located in the former town of Mugi, which has since merged with [Seki](/wiki/Seki,_Gifu). In the most recent census in 2005, the center of population has moved slightly more to the east but is still located within Gifu.

## Prefectural symbols

Prefectural Logo

Gifu's symbol comes from the first character *gi* (岐) of its Japanese name, written in a stylized script, surrounded by a circle, which represents the peace and harmony of the prefectural citizen. It was chosen by contest in 1932.

The prefectural logo (*see right*) expands from the red dot into the center to the outer two lines and, finally, the yellow plain. This symbol was chosen in 1991 for the development and expansion of the prefecture.

The prefecture also has two plants (the [milk vetch](/wiki/Milk_vetch) (renge) and the [Japanese yew](/wiki/Taxus_cuspidata)) and two animals (the [snow grouse](/wiki/Rock_ptarmigan) and the [ayu](/wiki/Ayu_sweetfish)) as symbols. The milk vetch was chosen in 1954, because the prefecture is well known for its abundance of blooming milk vetch each spring. The yew was chosen in 1966, because it is the tree used to make ornamental scepters for the emperor, many of which came from the Hida district. The snow grouse was chosen in 1961, as the birds live up in the Japanese alps and is a nationally protected species. Ayu were chosen in 1989, because the fish is found in many prefectural rivers and is prized for its sweet taste.

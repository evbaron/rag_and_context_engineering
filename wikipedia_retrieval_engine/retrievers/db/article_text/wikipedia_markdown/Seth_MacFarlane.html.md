**Seth Woodbury MacFarlane** ([/məkˈfɑːrlɪn/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English); born October 26, 1973) is an American actor, animator, writer, producer, director, comedian, and singer. He is the creator and star of the television series *[Family Guy](/wiki/Family_Guy)* and *[The Orville](/wiki/The_Orville)*, and co-creator of the television series *[American Dad!](/wiki/American_Dad!)* and *[The Cleveland Show](/wiki/The_Cleveland_Show)*. He also co-wrote, co-produced, directed, and starred in the films *[Ted](/wiki/Ted_(film))*, its sequel *[Ted 2](/wiki/Ted_2)*, and *[A Million Ways to Die in the West](/wiki/A_Million_Ways_to_Die_in_the_West)*.

MacFarlane is a graduate of the [Rhode Island School of Design](/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) (RISD), where he studied animation. He was recruited to Hollywood as an animator and writer for [Hanna-Barbera](/wiki/Hanna-Barbera)'s television series *[Johnny Bravo](/wiki/Johnny_Bravo)*, *[Cow and Chicken](/wiki/Cow_and_Chicken)* and *[Dexter's Laboratory](/wiki/Dexter%27s_Laboratory)*, During this time, he created the animated short *[Larry & Steve](/wiki/The_Life_of_Larry_and_Larry_%26_Steve)*—a loose precursor of *Family Guy*—for [Fred Seibert](/wiki/Fred_Seibert)'s *[What a Cartoon!](/wiki/What_a_Cartoon!)*. In 2008, he created the online series *[Seth MacFarlane's Cavalcade of Cartoon Comedy](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane%27s_Cavalcade_of_Cartoon_Comedy)*. MacFarlane has also made guest appearances as an actor on live action shows including *[Gilmore Girls](/wiki/Gilmore_Girls)*, *[Star Trek: Enterprise](/wiki/Star_Trek:_Enterprise)*, *[The War at Home](/wiki/The_War_at_Home_(TV_series))*, and *[FlashForward](/wiki/FlashForward)*. MacFarlane has won several awards for his work on *Family Guy*, including five [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards). In 2009, he won the [Webby Award](/wiki/Webby_Awards) for Film & Video Person of the Year.

MacFarlane has performed as a vocalist at the [Hollywood Bowl](/wiki/Hollywood_Bowl), [Carnegie Hall](/wiki/Carnegie_Hall), and the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall). He has released nine studio albums, in the vein of [Frank Sinatra](/wiki/Frank_Sinatra), with influences from [jazz orchestrations](/wiki/Big_band), and [Hollywood musicals](/wiki/Musical_film) beginning with *[Music Is Better Than Words](/wiki/Music_Is_Better_Than_Words)* (2011). MacFarlane has received five [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) nominations. He has frequently collaborated with artists such as [Sara Bareilles](/wiki/Sara_Bareilles), [Norah Jones](/wiki/Norah_Jones), and [Elizabeth Gillies](/wiki/Elizabeth_Gillies) on his albums. He hosted the [85th Academy Awards](/wiki/85th_Academy_Awards) in 2013 and was nominated for [Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) for "[Everybody Needs a Best Friend](/wiki/Everybody_Needs_a_Best_Friend)" from *Ted*.

MacFarlane was executive producer of the [Neil deGrasse Tyson](/wiki/Neil_deGrasse_Tyson)-hosted *[Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey](/wiki/Cosmos:_A_Spacetime_Odyssey)*, an update of the 1980s [*Cosmos* series](/wiki/Cosmos:_A_Personal_Voyage) hosted by [Carl Sagan](/wiki/Carl_Sagan). He received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 2019 and was inducted into the [Television Hall of Fame](/wiki/Television_Hall_of_Fame) in 2020.

## Early life and education

Seth Woodbury MacFarlane was born on October 26, 1973, in [Kent](/wiki/Kent,_Connecticut), [Connecticut](/wiki/Connecticut). His parents, Ronald Milton MacFarlane (born 1946) and Ann Perry ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Sager; 1947–2010), were born in [Newburyport, Massachusetts](/wiki/Newburyport,_Massachusetts). His younger sister [Rachael](/wiki/Rachael_MacFarlane) is also a voice actress. His maternal grandfather, [Arthur Sager](/wiki/Arthur_Sager), competed in the [1928 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) in [track and field](/wiki/Track_and_field). MacFarlane's parents met in 1970 when they lived and worked in [Boston](/wiki/Boston), and married later that year. They moved to Kent in 1972, where Ann began working in the admissions office at [South Kent School](/wiki/South_Kent_School). She later worked in the college guidance and admissions offices at the [Kent School](/wiki/Kent_School), a selective [college preparatory school](/wiki/College_preparatory_school), where Ronald was a teacher.

As a child, MacFarlane developed an interest in illustration, and at the age of two he began drawing [cartoon](/wiki/Cartoon) characters such as [Fred Flintstone](/wiki/Fred_Flintstone) and [Woody Woodpecker](/wiki/Woody_Woodpecker). By age five, he knew he wanted to pursue a career in animation, and began by creating [flip books](/wiki/Flip_book) after his parents found a book on the subject for him. Four years later, at nine, he began publishing a weekly comic strip, *Walter Crouton*, for *The Kent Good Times Dispatch*, the local newspaper; it paid him five dollars per week. MacFarlane said in an October 2011 interview that as a child he was always "weirdly fascinated by the [Communion](/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)) ceremony". He created a strip with a character kneeling at the altar taking Communion and asking "Can I have fries with that?" The paper printed it and he got an "angry letter" from the local priest; it led to "sort of a little mini-controversy" in the town. MacFarlane received his high school diploma in 1991 from the Kent School. While there, he continued experimenting with animation, and his parents gave him an [8 mm](/wiki/8_mm_film) camera.

After graduating from high school, MacFarlane attended the [Rhode Island School of Design](/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) (RISD), where he majored in animation. As a student, he intended to work for [Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios), but changed his mind after seeing *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)*. During his time at RISD, he performed stand-up comedy. He also starred in many student films, meeting future *Family Guy* cast member [Mike Henry](/wiki/Mike_Henry_(voice_actor)), whose brother Patrick was MacFarlane's classmate. In his senior year, he made his thesis film, *[The Life of Larry](/wiki/The_Life_of_Larry_and_Larry_%26_Steve)*, which became the inspiration for *Family Guy*. A professor submitted his film to the animation studio [Hanna-Barbera](/wiki/Hanna-Barbera), where he was later hired. He graduated in 1995 with a [Bachelor of Fine Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Fine_Arts) degree.

## Career

### Television career

#### Hanna-Barbera years

MacFarlane was recruited during the senior film festival by development executive Ellen Cockrill and President [Fred Seibert](/wiki/Fred_Seibert). He went to work at Hanna-Barbera (then Hanna-Barbera Cartoons) based on the writing content of *The Life of Larry*, rather than on his drawing abilities. He was one of only a few people hired by the company solely based on writing talent. He worked as an animator and writer for Cartoon Network's *[Cartoon Cartoons](/wiki/Cartoon_Cartoons)* series. He created *Larry & Steve*, a sequel to *The Life of Larry* featuring middle-aged Larry and his intellectual dog, Steve. The short was broadcast as one of [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network)'s *[World Premiere Toons](/wiki/What-A-Cartoon!)*. He described the atmosphere at Hanna-Barbera as resembling an "old-fashioned Hollywood structure, where you move from one show to another or you jump from a writing job on one show to a storyboard job on another". MacFarlane worked on three television series during his tenure at the studio: *[Dexter's Laboratory](/wiki/Dexter%27s_Laboratory)*, *[Cow and Chicken](/wiki/Cow_and_Chicken)*, and *[Johnny Bravo](/wiki/Johnny_Bravo)*. Working as both a writer and [storyboard artist](/wiki/Storyboard_artist), MacFarlane spent the most time on *Johnny Bravo*. He found it easier to develop his own style at *Johnny Bravo* through the show's process of scriptwriting, which *Dexter's Laboratory* and *Cow and Chicken* did not use. As a part of the *Johnny Bravo* crew, he met actors and voiceover artists such as [Adam West](/wiki/Adam_West) and [Jack Sheldon](/wiki/Jack_Sheldon) of *[Schoolhouse Rock!](/wiki/Schoolhouse_Rock!)* fame. These meetings later became significant to the production and success of his *Family Guy* series.

He also did freelance work for [Walt Disney Television Animation](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Television_Animation), writing for *[Jungle Cubs](/wiki/Jungle_Cubs)*, and for [Nelvana](/wiki/Nelvana), where he wrote for *[Ace Ventura: Pet Detective](/wiki/Ace_Ventura:_Pet_Detective_(TV_series))*. Through strict observation of writing elements such as story progression, character stakes and plot points, MacFarlane found the work for Disney was, from a writing standpoint, very valuable in preparation for his career (particularly on *Ace Ventura*). He also created and wrote a short titled *Zoomates* for [Frederator Studios](/wiki/Frederator_Studios)' *[Oh Yeah! Cartoons](/wiki/Oh_Yeah!_Cartoons)* on [Nickelodeon](/wiki/Nickelodeon_(TV_network)). Executives at the [Fox Broadcasting Company](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) saw both *Larry* shorts and negotiations soon began for a prime-time animated series.

#### *Family Guy*

Main article: [Family Guy](/wiki/Family_Guy)

Although MacFarlane enjoyed working at Hanna-Barbera, he felt his real calling was for prime-time animation, which would allow a much edgier style of humor. He first pitched *Family Guy* to Fox during his tenure at Hanna-Barbera. A development executive there, who was trying to get back into prime-time business, introduced MacFarlane to Leslie Kolins and Mike Darnell, heads of the alternative comedy department at Fox. After the success of *[King of the Hill](/wiki/King_of_the_Hill)* in 1997, MacFarlane called Kolins once more to ask about a possible second pitch for the series. Fox offered the young writer a strange deal: They gave him a budget of $50,000 (equivalent to $100,000 in 2025) to produce a pilot that could lead to a series (most episodes of animated prime-time productions cost at least $1 million). Recalling the experience in an interview with *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, MacFarlane said: "I spent about six months with no sleep and no life, just drawing like crazy in my kitchen and doing this pilot."

After six months, MacFarlane returned to Fox with a "very, very simply, crudely animated film—with just enough to get the tone of the show across" to present to the executives, who loved the pilot and immediately ordered the series. In July 1998, they announced the purchase of *Family Guy* for a January 1999 debut. *Family Guy* was originally intended to be a series of shorts on *[MADtv](/wiki/MADtv)*, much in the same way *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)* had begun on *[The Tracey Ullman Show](/wiki/The_Tracey_Ullman_Show)* a decade earlier. Negotiations for the show's *MADtv* connection fell through early on as a result of budgetary concerns. At age 24, MacFarlane was television's youngest executive producer.

*Family Guy* first aired January 31, 1999. MacFarlane's work in animating *Family Guy* was influenced by [Jackie Gleason](/wiki/Jackie_Gleason) and Hanna-Barbera along with examples from *The Simpsons* and *[All in the Family](/wiki/All_in_the_Family)*. In addition to writing three episodes, "[Death Has a Shadow](/wiki/Death_Has_a_Shadow)", "[Family Guy Viewer Mail 1](/wiki/Family_Guy_Viewer_Mail_1)", and "[North by North Quahog](/wiki/North_by_North_Quahog)", MacFarlane voices *Family Guy*'s main male characters of [Peter Griffin](/wiki/Peter_Griffin), [Stewie Griffin](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin), [Brian Griffin](/wiki/Brian_Griffin), and [Glenn Quagmire](/wiki/Glenn_Quagmire), as well as Tom Tucker, his son Jake Tucker, and other characters. Bolstered by high DVD sales and fan loyalty, *Family Guy* developed into a $1-billion franchise. On May 4, 2008, after approximately 2+1⁄2 years of negotiations, MacFarlane reached a $100-million agreement with Fox to keep *Family Guy* and *American Dad!* until 2012. It made him the world's highest paid television writer.

MacFarlane's success with *Family Guy* opened doors to other ventures relating to the show. On April 26, 2005, he and composer [Walter Murphy](/wiki/Walter_Murphy) created *[Family Guy: Live in Vegas](/wiki/Family_Guy:_Live_in_Vegas)*. The soundtrack features a Broadway show tune theme, and MacFarlane voiced Stewie in the track "Stewie's Sexy Party". A fan of Broadway musicals, MacFarlane comments on using musicals as a component of *Family Guy*:

> I love the lush orchestration and old-fashioned melody writing ... it just gets you excited, that kind of music", he said. "It's very optimistic. And it's fun. The one thing that's missing for me from popular music today is fun. Guys like [Bing] Crosby, or [[Frank] Sinatra](/wiki/Frank_Sinatra), or [Dean Martin](/wiki/Dean_Martin), or [Mel Tormé](/wiki/Mel_Torm%C3%A9) [...] these are guys who sounded like they were having a great time.

A [*Family Guy* video game](/wiki/Family_Guy_Video_Game!) was released in 2006. Two years later, in August 2007, MacFarlane closed a digital content production deal with AdSense. He takes cast members on the road to voice characters in front of live audiences. *Family Guy Live* provides fans with the opportunity to hear future scripts. In mid-2007, Chicago fans had the opportunity to hear the then upcoming sixth-season premiere "[Blue Harvest](/wiki/Blue_Harvest)". Shows have played in [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), New York City, Chicago, and Los Angeles.

MacFarlane at a [Rhode Island School of Design](/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) reception on June 1, 2007

On July 22, 2007, in an interview with *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*, MacFarlane announced that he might start work on a feature film, although "nothing's official". In September 2007, [Ricky Blitt](/wiki/Ricky_Blitt) gave [TV.com](/wiki/TV.com) an interview confirming that he had already started working on the script. Then in *[TV Week](/wiki/TV_Week)* on July 18, 2008, MacFarlane confirmed plans to produce a theatrically released *Family Guy* feature film sometime "within the next year". He came up with an idea for the story, "something that you could not do on the show, which [to him] is the only reason to do a movie". He later went on to say he imagines the film to be "an old-style musical with dialogue" similar to *[The Sound of Music](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music_(film))*, saying that he would "really be trying to capture, musically, that feel". On October 13, 2011, MacFarlane confirmed that a deal for a *Family Guy* film had been made, and that he would write it with series co-producer Ricky Blitt. On November 30, 2012, he confirmed plans for the project. The project was put on hold while MacFarlane worked on *[Ted 2](/wiki/Ted_2)*. In 2018, Fox announced that a live-action/animated film based on the series is in development. MacFarlane stepped away from the series in 2011 to work on *[Ted](/wiki/Ted_(film))* and other projects, and has only been associated with the show as a voice actor since then.

Despite its popularity, *Family Guy* has often been [criticized](/wiki/Criticism_of_Family_Guy). The [Parents Television Council](/wiki/Parents_Television_Council) has been a frequent critic. It organized a letter-writing campaign to remove it from Fox's lineup, and filed complaints with the [Federal Communications Commission](/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) alleging that some of its episodes contained indecent content. MacFarlane has responded to the PTC's criticism by saying, among other things: "That's like getting hate mail from [Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler). They're literally terrible human beings."

*Family Guy* has been canceled twice, although strong fan support and DVD sales have caused Fox to reconsider. MacFarlane mentioned how these cancellations affected the lineup of writers: "One of the positive aspects of *Family Guy* constantly being pulled off [the air] is that we were always having to restaff writers."

During its sixth season, episodes of *Family Guy* and *American Dad!* were delayed from regular broadcast due to the [2007–2008 Writers Guild of America strike](/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike). MacFarlane participated in the strike to support the writers, and Fox aired three *Family Guy* episodes without his permission. The strike ended on February 12, 2008, and the series resumed airing regularly, beginning with "[Back to the Woods](/wiki/Back_to_the_Woods_(Family_Guy))".

#### *American Dad!*

Main article: [American Dad!](/wiki/American_Dad!)

MacFarlane has a second long-running, successful [adult](/wiki/Adult_animation) [animated series](/wiki/Animated_sitcom) in *[American Dad!](/wiki/American_Dad!)*, which has been in production since early 2005. To date, it is his only animated series that has never been canceled, though it has undergone two network relocations: from Fox to [TBS](/wiki/TBS_(TV_network)) following its [11th season](/wiki/American_Dad!_(season_11)), and from TBS back to Fox following its [21st](/wiki/American_Dad!_season_21). MacFarlane provides the voices of protagonist [Stan Smith](/wiki/Stan_Smith_(American_Dad!)) and prominent secondary character [Roger](/wiki/Roger_(American_Dad!)). His sister [Rachael](/wiki/Rachael_MacFarlane) provides the voice of [Hayley](/wiki/Hayley_Smith_(American_Dad!)), Stan's daughter. Additionally, he co-wrote the show's [first episode](/wiki/Pilot_(American_Dad!)). Aside from his voice acting work, MacFarlane has left much of *American Dad!'*s [creative direction](/wiki/Creative_direction) to the show's other co-creators, [Matt Weitzman](/wiki/Matt_Weitzman) and [Mike Barker](/wiki/Mike_Barker_(producer)) (though the latter departed after ten seasons), feeling it helps give the series its own voice and identity.

MacFarlane has stated that his inspiration to create *American Dad!* derived from his and Weitzman's exasperation with the [George W. Bush administration](/wiki/George_W._Bush_administration). MacFarlane has described the initial seasons of *American Dad!* as being similar to *[All in the Family](/wiki/All_in_the_Family)*, likening Stan's originally [bigoted](/wiki/Bigotry) persona to [Archie Bunker](/wiki/Archie_Bunker). After the early couple of seasons however, the series discontinued using these elements of [political satire](/wiki/Political_satire) and began to serve up its own brand of entertainment and humor. MacFarlane was described as having difficulty understanding the series in its early going; however, he warmed up to the series after its early seasons once he felt the show truly came into its own. His fellow co-creators have sensed this through MacFarlane's greatly increased attention to the series after its early seasons. MacFarlane has also revealed he is an *American Dad!* fan himself. He has taken note of the positive reaction to the Roger character by fans via his Twitter.

#### *The Cleveland Show*

Main article: [The Cleveland Show](/wiki/The_Cleveland_Show)

MacFarlane developed a *Family Guy* spin-off called *The Cleveland Show*, which focuses on the character of [Cleveland Brown](/wiki/Cleveland_Brown) and his family. The idea for the show originated from a suggestion by *Family Guy* writer and voice of Cleveland, Mike Henry. Fox ordered 22 episodes and the series first aired on September 27, 2009. The show, which was picked up to air a first season consisting of 22 episodes, was picked up by Fox for a second season, consisting of 13 episodes, bringing the total number to 35 episodes. The announcement was made on May 3, 2009, before the first season even premiered. Due to strong ratings, Fox picked up the back nine episodes of season 2, making a 22-episode season and bringing the total episode count of the show to 44. The series ended on May 19, 2013, with a total of 4 seasons and 88 episodes. The character of Cleveland and his family returned to *Family Guy* in the episode "[He's Bla-ack!](/wiki/He%27s_Bla-ack!)".

This is the only animated series created by MacFarlane that does not have him voicing the main character. MacFarlane did, however, play the character Tim the Bear until season 3 episode 10. [Jess Harnell](/wiki/Jess_Harnell) voiced Tim from season 3 episode 11 onwards.

#### *Cavalcade of Cartoon Comedy*

Main article: [Seth MacFarlane's Cavalcade of Cartoon Comedy](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane%27s_Cavalcade_of_Cartoon_Comedy)

In 2008, MacFarlane released a series of [webisodes](/wiki/Webisode) known as *Seth MacFarlane's Cavalcade of Cartoon Comedy* with its animated shorts sponsored by [Burger King](/wiki/Burger_King) and released weekly.

#### *The Orville*

Main article: [The Orville](/wiki/The_Orville)

In 2016, MacFarlane began producing the sci-fi comedy-drama series *[The Orville](/wiki/The_Orville)*, in which he also stars as [Captain Edward "Ed" Mercer](/wiki/Ed_Mercer). MacFarlane originally wrote *The Orville* as a [spec script](/wiki/Spec_script), which was given a 13-episode order by Fox in May 2016, making it the first live-action television series created by MacFarlane. The series premiered on September 10, 2017. Despite the first season receiving negative reviews, it was renewed for a second season. The second season premiered on December 30, 2018, and received better reviews.

The series was renewed for a third season by Fox, however the series would move over to [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu). This season is the show's first on Hulu, after airing its previous two seasons on Fox, as well as the first to premiere since The Walt Disney Company's March 2019 [acquisition of 20th Century Fox](/wiki/Acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney). The season was originally scheduled to premiere in 2020 but was delayed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_television_in_the_United_States). The third season titled as *The Orville: New Horizons* premiered on June 2, 2022. Due to the pandemic, an episode of third season was scrapped, which MacFarlane subsequently turned into a novel, titled *The Orville: Sympathy for the Devil*.

#### Television producing

MacFarlane was the executive producer of a live-action sitcom starring [Rob Corddry](/wiki/Rob_Corddry) called *[The Winner](/wiki/The_Winner_(TV_series))*. The plot has a man named Glen discussing the time he matured at 32 and has him pursuing his only love after she moves in next door. Glen meets her son and both become good friends. The show ran on Fox for six episodes in Spring 2007.

In August 2011, Fox ordered a 13-part updated series of *[Cosmos: A Spacetime Odyssey](/wiki/Cosmos:_A_Spacetime_Odyssey)*. MacFarlane co-produced the series with [Ann Druyan](/wiki/Ann_Druyan) and [Steven Soter](/wiki/Steven_Soter). The new series is hosted by [Neil deGrasse Tyson](/wiki/Neil_deGrasse_Tyson) and began airing on the channel in March 2014, with repeats airing on the [National Geographic Channel](/wiki/National_Geographic_Channel) on the next night. In addition to serving as one of the executive producers, MacFarlane provided voices for characters during the animated portions of the series. MacFarlane returned to executive produce and provide voices to its sequel series, *[Cosmos: Possible Worlds](/wiki/Cosmos:_Possible_Worlds)*, which aired in 2020.

In 2013 and 2014, MacFarlane produced one season of a live-action sitcom called *[Dads](/wiki/Dads_(2013_TV_series))*. The series, revolves around Eli, played by [Seth Green](/wiki/Seth_Green), and Warner, played by [Giovanni Ribisi](/wiki/Giovanni_Ribisi), two successful guys in their 30s whose world is turned upside down when their dads move in with them. MacFarlane, [Alec Sulkin](/wiki/Alec_Sulkin) and [Wellesley Wild](/wiki/Wellesley_Wild) executive-produced the series, with Sulkin and Wild writing.

In 2014, MacFarlane executive produced a two-season, 20-episode series called *[Blunt Talk](/wiki/Blunt_Talk)* for [Starz](/wiki/Starz_(TV_network)). The series followed an English newscaster who moves to Los Angeles with his alcoholic manservant and the baggage of several failed marriages to host a sanctimonious talk show.

In 2009, MacFarlane began work on the animated series *[Bordertown](/wiki/Bordertown_(2016_animated_TV_series))*. The series is set in Texas and follows a border patrol agent and a Mexican immigrant, satirizing America's changing cultural landscape. It ran for 13 episodes in the first half of 2016, on Fox.

#### Television hosting

MacFarlane has participated in the [Comedy Central Roasts](/wiki/Comedy_Central_Roast). MacFarlane is the only person to serve as roastmaster for more than one roast. In 2010, he filled this role for the roast of [David Hasselhoff](/wiki/David_Hasselhoff). The following year he was roastmaster at the roasts of [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump) and [Charlie Sheen](/wiki/Charlie_Sheen).

On October 1, 2012, it was announced that MacFarlane would host the [85th Academy Awards](/wiki/85th_Academy_Awards) on February 24, 2013. He also presented the nominees with actress [Emma Stone](/wiki/Emma_Stone), on January 10, 2013. In addition to hosting, MacFarlane was also nominated in the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) category for co-writing the theme song "[Everybody Needs a Best Friend](/wiki/Everybody_Needs_a_Best_Friend)" for his film *Ted* with Walter Murphy. Critical response to MacFarlane's performance was mixed. Columnist [Owen Gleiberman](/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman) of *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* commented "By calling constant attention to the naughty factor," MacFarlane created "an echo chamber of outrage, working a little too hard to top himself with faux-scandalous gags about race, Jews in Hollywood, and the killing of Abraham Lincoln." Tim Goodman of *The Hollywood Reporter* praised MacFarlane's performance saying that he did "impressively better than one would have wagered". He also noted that he added "plenty of niceties with a little bit of the [Ricky Gervais](/wiki/Ricky_Gervais) bite-the-hand-that-feeds-you thing and worked the juxtaposition rather nicely". He stirred up controversy in the form of a musical number titled "We Saw Your Boobs".

On October 29, 2014, it was announced that MacFarlane would host the [Breakthrough Prize](/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize) ceremony. The event was held in [Silicon Valley](/wiki/Silicon_Valley) and televised on November 15, 2014, on [Discovery Channel](/wiki/Discovery_Channel) and [Science](/wiki/Science_(TV_network)), and globally on November 22, 2014, on [BBC World News](/wiki/BBC_World_News). He returned to host the following year.

### Film career

#### *Ted*

Main article: [Ted (franchise)](/wiki/Ted_(franchise))

MacFarlane made his directorial live-action film debut with the release of *Ted* in 2012. He announced that he was directing it on an episode of *[Conan](/wiki/Conan_(talk_show))* that aired on February 10, 2011. Along with directing the film, he also wrote the screenplay, served as producer, and starred as the title character.

*Ted* tells the story of John Bennett ([Mark Wahlberg](/wiki/Mark_Wahlberg)) and his talking teddy bear (MacFarlane) who keeps John and his girlfriend Lori Collins ([Mila Kunis](/wiki/Mila_Kunis)) from moving on with their lives. The film received generally favorable reviews from both critics and audiences, and was a box office success, opening with the highest weekend gross of all time for an original R-rated comedy. Internationally, the movie is currently the highest-grossing original R-rated comedy of all time, beating *[The Hangover](/wiki/The_Hangover)*. A sequel, *Ted 2*, was released on June 26, 2015.

It was announced in June 2021 that [Peacock](/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service)) had given a straight to series order for a prequel series. In addition to serving as executive producer for the series, MacFarlane reprises his role as Ted. Due to its prequel nature, Wahlberg and Kunis do not reprise their roles. In August 2024, it was announced that an animated series based on the franchise was in development.

#### *A Million Ways to Die in the West*

Main article: [A Million Ways to Die in the West](/wiki/A_Million_Ways_to_Die_in_the_West)

MacFarlane co-wrote and starred in his second film, *A Million Ways to Die in the West*. Alec Sulkin and Wellesley Wild were also co-writers for the film. The film follows a cowardly sheep farmer (MacFarlane) who loses a gunfight and sees his girlfriend leave him for another man. When a mysterious woman rides into town, she helps him find his courage. But when her outlaw husband arrives seeking revenge, the farmer must put his newfound courage to the test. The film was met with mixed to negative reviews from critics.

On January 27, 2014, MacFarlane announced that he wrote a [companion novel](/wiki/A_Million_Ways_to_Die_in_the_West_(novel)) based on the film's script, which was released on March 4, 2014. An audio-book version was also made available, narrated by [Jonathan Frakes](/wiki/Jonathan_Frakes). MacFarlane wrote the book on weekends during shooting for the film, partially due to boredom.

### Music career

#### Record deal and albums

MacFarlane performing in 2014

In 2010, MacFarlane signed a record deal with [Universal Republic Records](/wiki/Universal_Republic_Records). He released his debut album, *[Music Is Better Than Words](/wiki/Music_Is_Better_Than_Words)*, in 2011. The album is a [big band](/wiki/Big_band)/[standards](/wiki/Jazz_standards) album drawing on his training in and attraction to "the [Great American Songbook](/wiki/Great_American_Songbook) and particularly the early- to late-'50s era of orchestration". It received a score of 52 out of 100 on [Metacritic](/wiki/Metacritic)'s compilation of music critic reviews. In 2014, he released his second studio album and first Christmas album *[Holiday for Swing](/wiki/Holiday_for_Swing)*. It received mostly positive reviews. In 2015, his third studio album *[No One Ever Tells You](/wiki/No_One_Ever_Tells_You)* was nominated for a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album. In 2016, he was honored by [Barbara Sinatra](/wiki/Barbara_Sinatra) at the 28th annual Frank Sinatra Celebrity Invitational.

He released his fourth studio album, *[In Full Swing](/wiki/In_Full_Swing_(Seth_MacFarlane_album))*, in 2017, again featuring songs composed by [Joel McNeely](/wiki/Joel_McNeely). Three singles were released from it: "That Face", "[Almost Like Being in Love](/wiki/Almost_Like_Being_in_Love)", and "[Have You Met Miss Jones?](/wiki/Have_You_Met_Miss_Jones%3F)" The album was nominated for two Grammy Awards for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Album and [Best Arrangement, Instrumental and Vocals](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Arrangement,_Instrumental_and_Vocals). In 2019, for his fifth studio album *[Once in a While](/wiki/Once_in_a_While_(Seth_MacFarlane_album))*, MacFarlane worked with composer Andrew Cottee.

In 2020, MacFarlane released his sixth studio album, *[Great Songs from Stage & Screen](/wiki/Great_Songs_from_Stage_%26_Screen)*, with composer [Bruce Broughton](/wiki/Bruce_Broughton), who he works with on *The Orville*, to compose the album. Like his previous four albums, he recorded a majority of the songs at [Abbey Road Studios](/wiki/Abbey_Road_Studios). However, much of the album's post-production work was done at home due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_the_music_industry). In 2022, he released his seventh studio album, *[Blue Skies](/wiki/Blue_Skies_(Seth_MacFarlane_album))*. In November 2023, MacFarlane released a collaborative Christmas album with Gillies titled *[We Wish You the Merriest](/wiki/We_Wish_You_the_Merriest)*. The lead single of the same name was dropped on the day of the announcement.

In June 2025, MacFarlane released his ninth album, *[Lush Life: The Lost Sinatra Arrangements](/wiki/Lush_Life:_The_Lost_Sinatra_Arrangements)*. The album, conducted by [John Wilson](/wiki/John_Wilson_(conductor)), features music originally arranged by [Nelson Riddle](/wiki/Nelson_Riddle), [Billy May](/wiki/Billy_May), and [Don Costa](/wiki/Don_Costa) for [Frank Sinatra](/wiki/Frank_Sinatra), but were never produced, and were discovered by MacFarlane in Sinatra's library of music material.

## Artistry

### Musical style

[Frank Sinatra](/wiki/Frank_Sinatra) significantly influenced MacFarlane and his music.

MacFarlane has a [baritone](/wiki/Baritone) voice. He is a pianist and singer who, in his early years, trained with [Lee and Sally Sweetland](/wiki/Sally_Sweetland), the vocal coaches of Barbra Streisand and Frank Sinatra. In an interview with [NPR](/wiki/NPR), he commented on their training style: "They really drill you. They teach you the old-style way of singing, back when you had no electronic help ... [They teach you to] show your teeth. If you look at old photos of Sinatra while he's singing, there's a lot of very exposed teeth. That was something Lee Sweetland hit on day in and day out, and correctly so, because it just brightens the whole performance."
In 2009, MacFarlane appeared as a vocalist at the [BBC Proms](/wiki/BBC_Proms) with the [John Wilson Orchestra](/wiki/John_Wilson_Orchestra) in Prom 22, *A Celebration of Classic MGM Film Musicals*. In 2010, he reappeared at the Proms with the John Wilson Orchestra in a Christmas concert special. In 2012, it was announced he would again appear at the Proms with the John Wilson Orchestra in a concert celebrating Broadway musicals. In 2015, MacFarlane again appeared at The Proms as a vocalist with the John Wilson Orchestra, this time in a Sinatra program. Regarding his musical passion, MacFarlane has said, "I love and am fascinated by exciting orchestration—what you can do with a band that size—and I think in many ways it's a lost art." His music is predominantly [vocal jazz](/wiki/Vocal_jazz), show tunes and [swing](/wiki/Swing_music). He also uses [musical comedy](/wiki/Comedy_music) in his shows and movies.

### Influences

MacFarlane has said that his comedy influences include [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen), [Jackie Gleason](/wiki/Jackie_Gleason), [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks), [Monty Python](/wiki/Monty_Python), [Matt Groening](/wiki/Matt_Groening), and [Norman Lear](/wiki/Norman_Lear); while his musical influences include Frank Sinatra, Dean Martin, [Vic Damone](/wiki/Vic_Damone), [Johnny Mercer](/wiki/Johnny_Mercer), [Bing Crosby](/wiki/Bing_Crosby), [Bobby Darin](/wiki/Bobby_Darin), [Gordon MacRae](/wiki/Gordon_MacRae) and the [Rat Pack](/wiki/Rat_Pack).

## Activism

### Political views

MacFarlane is a supporter of the [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)). He has donated over $200,000 to various [Democratic congressional committees](/wiki/Democratic_Congressional_Campaign_Committee) and to the [2008 presidential campaign](/wiki/Barack_Obama_2008_presidential_campaign) of then-US Senator [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama). In 2015, MacFarlane endorsed [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) for the [2016 US presidential election](/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), and he introduced Sanders onstage at a Los Angeles rally. After the primaries, he supported [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) for president during the general election. In 2019, he supported [Pete Buttigieg](/wiki/Pete_Buttigieg) in the [2020 US presidential election](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election). After the primaries, he endorsed [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) for president during the general election. He endorsed Biden again for the [2024 US presidential election](/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election). After Biden [withdrew his candidacy](/wiki/Withdrawal_of_Joe_Biden_from_the_2024_United_States_presidential_election), MacFarlane endorsed [Kamala Harris](/wiki/Kamala_Harris).

### Gay rights advocacy

MacFarlane is a supporter of [gay rights](/wiki/Gay_rights). In 2008, prior to the holding of the *[Obergefell v. Hodges](/wiki/Obergefell_v._Hodges)* case by the [United States Supreme Court](/wiki/United_States_Supreme_Court), MacFarlane called it "infuriating and idiotic" that two gay partners "have to go through this fucking [dog and pony act](/wiki/Dog_and_pony_show) when they stop at a hotel and the guy behind the counter says, 'You want one room or two?'" He went on to say: "I'm incredibly passionate about my support for the [gay community](/wiki/LGBT_community) and what they're dealing with at this current point in time."

In recognition of "his active, passionate commitment to [humanist](/wiki/Secular_humanism) values, and his fearless support of equal marriage rights and other social justice issues", MacFarlane was named the Harvard Humanist of the Year in 2011.

### 2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike

MacFarlane speaking at a [Writers Guild of America](/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America) rally in [Culver City](/wiki/Culver_City,_California) on November 9, 2007

During the [2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike), MacFarlane publicly sided with the Writers Guild, and fully participated in the strike. Official production of *Family Guy* was halted for most of December 2007 and various periods afterwards. Fox continued producing episodes without MacFarlane's final approval, and although he refused to work on the show during the strike, his contract with Fox required him to contribute to any episodes it subsequently produced. Rumors of continued production on *Family Guy* prompted the statement from MacFarlane that ".....it would just be a colossal dick move if they did that". During the strike, MacFarlane wrote an inside joke into an episode of *Family Guy* about [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart)'s choice to return to the air and undermine the writers of *[The Daily Show](/wiki/The_Daily_Show)*, causing Stewart to respond with an angry phone-call, harassing MacFarlane and arguing his point. The strike ended on February 12, 2008.

## Personal life

MacFarlane lives in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California). He is not married and has no children. From 2012 to 2013, he was in a relationship with British actress [Emilia Clarke](/wiki/Emilia_Clarke).

MacFarlane gave a speech at his alma mater, the Rhode Island School of Design, on September 10, 2001; the next morning he was scheduled to return to Los Angeles on [American Airlines Flight 11](/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_11), one of the planes hijacked in the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks). Due to a [hangover](/wiki/Hangover) after the previous night's celebrations and an incorrect departure time (8:15 a.m. instead of 7:45 a.m.) from his [travel agent](/wiki/Travel_agent), he arrived at [Logan International Airport](/wiki/Logan_International_Airport) about ten minutes too late to board the flight, as the gates had been closed. Speaking of his experience of missing the fatal trip, MacFarlane said:

> The only reason it hasn't really affected me as it maybe could have is I didn't really know that I was in any danger until after it was over, so I never had that panic moment. After the fact, it was sobering, but people have a lot of close calls; you're crossing the street and you almost get hit by a car... This one just happened to be related to something massive. I really can't let it affect me because I'm a comedy writer. I have to put that in the back of my head.

On July 16, 2010, MacFarlane's mother, Ann Perry Sager, died from cancer. Her death was reported by [Larry King](/wiki/Larry_King) on his show *[Larry King Live](/wiki/Larry_King_Live)*, who acknowledged a conversation he had with her during an interview with MacFarlane in May 2010.

MacFarlane is an [atheist](/wiki/Atheism), explaining his beliefs, "I do not believe in God. I'm an atheist. I consider myself a critical thinker, and it fascinates me that in the 21st century most people still believe in, as [George Carlin](/wiki/George_Carlin) puts it, 'the invisible man living in the sky'."

## Lawsuits

On October 3, 2007, [Bourne Co. Music Publishers](/wiki/Bourne_Co._Music_Publishers) filed a lawsuit accusing *Family Guy* of [infringing its copyright](/wiki/Copyright_infringement) on the song "[When You Wish Upon a Star](/wiki/When_You_Wish_Upon_a_Star)", through a parody song titled "I Need a Jew" appearing in the episode "[When You Wish Upon a Weinstein](/wiki/When_You_Wish_Upon_a_Weinstein)". Bourne Co., which holds the copyright, alleged the parody pairs a "thinly veiled" copy of their music with [antisemitic](/wiki/Antisemitism) lyrics. Named in the suit were MacFarlane, 20th Century Fox Film Corp., Fox Broadcasting Co., Cartoon Network and Walter Murphy; the suit sought to stop the program's distribution and asked for unspecified damages. Bourne argued that "I Need a Jew" uses the copyrighted melody of "When You Wish Upon a Star" without commenting on that song, and that it was therefore not a [First Amendment](/wiki/First_Amendment)-protected parody per the ruling in *[Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.](/wiki/Campbell_v._Acuff-Rose_Music,_Inc.)* On March 16, 2009, United States District Judge [Deborah Batts](/wiki/Deborah_Batts) held that *Family Guy* did not infringe on Bourne's copyright when it transformed the song for comical use in an episode.

In December 2007, *Family Guy* was again accused of copyright infringement when actor [Art Metrano](/wiki/Art_Metrano) filed a lawsuit regarding a scene in *[Stewie Griffin: The Untold Story](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin:_The_Untold_Story)*, in which Jesus performs Metrano's signature magic parody act, involving absurd faux magical hand gestures while humming the distinctive tune "[Fine and Dandy](/wiki/Fine_and_Dandy)". MacFarlane, [20th Century Fox](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox), [Steve Callaghan](/wiki/Steve_Callaghan), and Alex Borstein were all named in the suit. In July 2009, a federal district court judge rejected Fox's motion to dismiss, saying that the first three fair use factors involved—"purpose and character of the use", "nature of the infringed work", and "amount and substantiality of the taking"—counted in Metrano's favor, while the fourth—"economic impact"—had to await more fact-finding. In denying the dismissal, the court held that the reference in the scene made light of Jesus and his followers—not Metrano or his act. The case was settled out of court in 2010 with undisclosed terms.

On July 16, 2014, MacFarlane was served with a lawsuit from the production company of a series of Internet videos called *Charlie the Abusive Teddy Bear* claiming that *Ted* infringes on the copyright of its videos due to the Ted bear largely matching the background story, persona, voice tone, attitude, and dialogue of the Charlie bear. The suit was dismissed with prejudice on March 23, 2015, after the plaintiffs conceded Ted was independently created and withdrew the suit.

In 2019, MacFarlane received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) at 6259 Hollywood Blvd. In 2020, he was inducted into the [Television Hall of Fame](/wiki/Television_Hall_of_Fame).

In 2022, a new species of *[Hyloscirtus](/wiki/Hyloscirtus)* [frog](/wiki/Frog) (*[Hyloscirtus sethmacfarlanei](/wiki/Hyloscirtus_sethmacfarlanei)*) was described from [Ecuador](/wiki/Ecuador) and named after him.

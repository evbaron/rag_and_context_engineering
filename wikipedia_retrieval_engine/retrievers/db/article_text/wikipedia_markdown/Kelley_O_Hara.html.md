**Kelley Maureen O'Hara** (born August 4, 1988) is an American former professional [soccer](/wiki/Association_football) player. She represented the [United States national team](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team) on 160 occasions, winning two [FIFA Women's World Cups](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) and an [Olympic](/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) gold medal. She spent most of her club career with [National Women's Soccer League](/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League) club [NJ/NY Gotham FC](/wiki/NJ/NY_Gotham_FC). Known for her intensity, she played primarily as a [wingback](/wiki/Wingback_(soccer)) but played as a [forward](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)) in college and occasionally played an attacking role in her professional career.

O'Hara was awarded the [Hermann Trophy](/wiki/Hermann_Trophy) while playing for the [Stanford Cardinal](/wiki/Stanford_Cardinal_women%27s_soccer) in 2009. She then played for [FC Gold Pride](/wiki/FC_Gold_Pride) and the [Boston Breakers](/wiki/Boston_Breakers) in [Women's Professional Soccer](/wiki/Women%27s_Professional_Soccer). When the NWSL was formed in 2013, she joined Gotham FC (then known as Sky Blue FC). She later played for [Utah Royals FC](/wiki/Utah_Royals_FC) and the [Washington Spirit](/wiki/Washington_Spirit), with whom she scored the winning goal in the [NWSL Championship](/wiki/2021_NWSL_Championship) in 2021. She returned to Gotham and won her second NWSL Championship in 2023. She was named to the [FIFA FIFPRO Women's World 11](/wiki/FIFA_FIFPRO_Women%27s_World_11) in 2019.

O'Hara played internationally for the United States from 2010 to 2023. She appeared at four Women's World Cups in [2011](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup); [2015](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), helping the team win the title; [2019](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), defending the title; and [2023](/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup). She appeared at three Olympic Games, including at the [2012 Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), where she was one of three U.S. players that played every minute of the tournament as they won gold; [2016](/wiki/Football_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament); and [2020](/wiki/Football_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), where she won bronze.

O'Hara announced that she would retire from professional soccer at the end of the [2024 NWSL season](/wiki/2024_National_Women%27s_Soccer_League_season). On September 21, 2024, she was put on the season-ending injury list due to chronic knee degeneration, thus effectively ending her career.

## Early life

O'Hara was born in [Fayetteville, Georgia](/wiki/Fayetteville,_Georgia), near [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta) to parents Dan and Karen O'Hara. She has a brother named Jerry and a sister named Erin. O'Hara has Irish heritage. O'Hara grew up in [Peachtree City, Georgia](/wiki/Peachtree_City,_Georgia) and graduated from [Starr's Mill High School](/wiki/Starr%27s_Mill_High_School) in [Fayette County](/wiki/Fayette_County,_Georgia) where she played four years on the [varsity](/wiki/Varsity_team) soccer team and captained the team during her [junior](/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey) and [senior](/wiki/Senior_(education)) years. O'Hara helped lead the Panthers to the [5A](/wiki/Georgia_High_School_Association) state title in 2006 with 20 goals and 16 assists. The team finished second in the state championships during her [sophomore](/wiki/Tenth_grade#United_States) year. O'Hara was named [Parade All-American](/wiki/Parade_All-American) as a junior and a senior and All-League, All-County and All-State all four years. In 2006, she was named the 2006 [Atlanta Journal-Constitution](/wiki/Atlanta_Journal-Constitution) (AJC) Player of the Year and Gatorade Georgia State Player of the Year. She was also named NSCAA All-American.

O'Hara played for club teams, the Peachtree City Lazers and AFC Lightning before playing for the U.S. U-16s in 2004 and then joining the [U-17](/wiki/U-17_World_Cup) youth women's national team of that same year. She played on the Concorde Fire South '88 Elite that went on to win the 2007 GA U19G State Cup and advance to the Semi Finals of Regionals.

### Stanford Cardinal (2006–2009)

A two-time [Parade All-American](/wiki/Parade_All-American) coming into her freshman year at [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University), O'Hara led the [Cardinal](/wiki/Stanford_Cardinal) in scoring in 2006 with nine goals. She repeated that feat during her sophomore year, helping the Cardinal to the third round of the [NCAA](/wiki/NCAA) Tournament.

During O'Hara's junior year, Stanford advanced to the [College Cup](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Women%27s_Soccer_Championship) for the first time since 1993, defeating 2005 national champion [Portland](/wiki/Portland_Pilots_women%27s_soccer), 1–0. The Cardinal would fall in the semi-final, 0–1, to [Notre Dame](/wiki/Notre_Dame_Fighting_Irish).

As a senior, she had one of the best seasons in Division I history, scoring 26 goals with 13 assists. O'Hara's senior year ended in the 2009 [College Cup](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Women%27s_Soccer_Championship), where the Cardinal lost to [North Carolina](/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_women%27s_soccer). O'Hara received two yellow cards in the second half, ejecting her from the game, forcing the Cardinal to finish the game a woman down. The game ended with a score of 1–0, thus marking North Carolina's twentieth National Championship. She finished her college career at Stanford with 57 goals and 32 assists, both school records at the time.

O'Hara was awarded the 2009 [Hermann Trophy](/wiki/Hermann_Trophy) as collegiate soccer's top player. She had been on the MAC [Hermann Trophy](/wiki/Hermann_Trophy) watch list for three consecutive seasons. O'Hara was also a member of the [Kappa Kappa Gamma](/wiki/Kappa_Kappa_Gamma) sorority during her time at Stanford.

On August 5, 2025, O'Hara was named to the [Stanford Athletics Hall of Fame](/wiki/Stanford_Athletics_Hall_of_Fame) Class of 2025.

## Club career

Prior to graduating from [Stanford](/wiki/Stanford_University), O'Hara played for the [Pali Blues](/wiki/Pali_Blues) of the [USL W-League](/wiki/USL_W-League_(1995%E2%80%932015)) (semi-pro) in the summer of 2009, scoring four goals during her tenure with the club.

### WPS: FC Gold Pride, Boston Breakers (2010–2011)

[FC Gold Pride](/wiki/FC_Gold_Pride) won the 2010 WPS Championship Trophy.

O'Hara was drafted third overall by [FC Gold Pride](/wiki/FC_Gold_Pride) at the [2010 WPS Draft](/wiki/2010_WPS_Draft). In addition to the close proximity of home stadium [Pioneer Stadium](/wiki/Pioneer_Stadium) to O'Hara's alma mater [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University), O'Hara had previously worked with [FC Gold Pride](/wiki/FC_Gold_Pride) head coach [Albertin Montoya](/wiki/Albertin_Montoya) when he served as an assistant coach at [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University) in 2008.

The team dominated the season finishing first during the regular season after defeating the [Philadelphia Independence](/wiki/Philadelphia_Independence) 4–1 with goals from O'Hara, [Christine Sinclair](/wiki/Christine_Sinclair) and [Marta](/wiki/Marta_(footballer)). As the regular season champion, the team earned a direct route to the championship playoff game where they faced the Philadelphia Independence. During the final, FC Gold Pride defeated the Independence 4–0 to clinch the WPS Championship. Despite their successful season, the club ceased operations on November 16, 2010, due to not meeting the league's financial reserve requirement.

After [FC Gold Pride](/wiki/FC_Gold_Pride) folded in November 2010, O'Hara was signed by the [Boston Breakers](/wiki/Boston_Breakers). She scored 10 goals during her two seasons in the WPS playing primarily as an outside midfielder. On January 5, 2012, it was announced O'Hara would be going back to her hometown because she had signed with the [Atlanta Beat](/wiki/Atlanta_Beat_(WPS)). However, the league folded just before the 2012 season began.

### NWSL: Sky Blue FC, 2013–2017

On January 11, 2013, O'Hara joined [Sky Blue FC](/wiki/Sky_Blue_FC) in the new [National Women's Soccer League](/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League). Because the club's head coach, [Jim Gabarra](/wiki/Jim_Gabarra), played O'Hara as a forward, she reverted to a role she filled with success in college.

Over her career at Sky Blue, O'Hara has been played in several roles including forward, winger, right-back, and central midfielder.

### Utah Royals FC, 2017–2020

On December 29, 2017, O'Hara was traded to [Utah Royals FC](/wiki/Utah_Royals_FC). Due to a hamstring injury, O'Hara only appeared in 8 games for Utah in 2018. O'Hara contributed to Utah's first-ever franchise win, scoring a goal in the team's 2–0 victory over the Washington Spirit in May 2018.

Utah finished the [season](/wiki/2018_Utah_Royals_FC_season) in 5th place, just 2 points shy of making the playoffs. O'Hara underwent ankle surgery after the 2018 season.

In 2019, she made only 2 starts in 4 appearances for Utah due to injuries and [World Cup](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) duties. She was still recuperating from an off-season ankle injury at the start of the [NWSL season](/wiki/2019_National_Women%27s_Soccer_League_season) and saw limited minutes as a substitute in two late-April games. Following her World Cup win, O'Hara started in two games for Utah at the end of July, notching an assist in the team's 2–2 draw against Portland. She was named to the [2019 NWSL second XI](/wiki/NWSL_Second_XI#2019).

O'Hara played only 65 minutes for the Royals in the abbreviated [2020 NWSL season](/wiki/2020_National_Women%27s_Soccer_League_season). She was still recovering from an injury at the start of the [Challenge Cup](/wiki/2020_NWSL_Challenge_Cup) and did not dress for the first few games. She saw limited minutes in Utah's July 13 game against Chicago and the July 18 game against Houston.

Starting in August 2020, rumors of a O'Hara trade to the [Washington Spirit](/wiki/Washington_Spirit) began to circulate and O'Hara announced in August that she would opt out of the 2020 NWSL Fall Series, set to begin in early September.

### Washington Spirit, 2021–2022

O'Hara's trade to the Spirit was officially announced on December 2, 2020. The deal sent $75,000 in allocation money to the Utah Royals and a 2022 first round draft pick. O'Hara stated that she requested the move to Spirit so that she could live, work in a same city with her partner.
The Spirit won their first NWSL Championship on November 20, 2021, when they defeated the Chicago Red Stars, 2–1 in extra time at Lynn Family Stadium in Louisville, Kentucky. O'Hara scored the winning goal in the 97th minute of the game.

### NJ/NY Gotham FC, 2023–2024

After the 2022 season, O'Hara announced on November 15 that she would sign with [NJ/NY Gotham FC](/wiki/NJ/NY_Gotham_FC), returning to the club she formerly played for when it was called Sky Blue. The move made her the first NWSL player to sign with a new team in [free agency](/wiki/Free_agent). Gotham FC officially announced her signing on January 25, 2023. O'Hara won her second NWSL championship with Gotham in 2023.

O'Hara announced on May 2, 2024, that she would retire from professional soccer at the end of the season. On September 21, 2024, O'Hara was put on the season-ending injury list due to chronic knee degeneration, thus effectively ending her career.

## International career

### Youth national teams (2005–2010)

O'Hara playing for the USWNT in 2016

O'Hara represented the United States in various youth national teams from 2005 through 2010. She scored 24 goals in her 35 under-20 caps, the third-most ever for a U.S. player in the U-20 age group. She was a member of the fourth-place [United States U-20 women's national soccer team](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_under-20_soccer_team) that competed in the [2006 FIFA U-20 Women's World Championship](/wiki/2006_FIFA_U-20_Women%27s_World_Championship) in [Russia](/wiki/Russia). O'Hara scored two goals in the tournament: one against the [Congo](/wiki/DR_Congo_women%27s_national_football_team) (for which game she was named [FIFA](/wiki/FIFA)'s player of the match) and one against [Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_football_team). She was also the first player in the tournament to be ejected from a game, having picked up two [yellow cards](/wiki/Penalty_card) in the game against [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_women%27s_national_football_team).

O'Hara rejoined the U-20 national team at the [2007 Pan American Games](/wiki/2007_Pan_American_Games). She scored four goals in the [women's football tournament](/wiki/Football_at_the_2007_Pan_American_Games_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), against [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_women%27s_national_football_team), [Panama](/wiki/Panama_women%27s_national_football_team), and [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_women%27s_national_football_team). The United States, which only sent their [U-20](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_under-20_soccer_team) women to the tournament, would fall in the final game, 0–5, to a full-strength [Brazilian senior team](/wiki/Brazil_women%27s_national_football_team) featuring Brazilian powerhouse, [Marta](/wiki/Marta_(footballer)).

In February 2008, O'Hara returned to the [U-20 women's national team](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_under-20_soccer_team) to play in the U-20 Four Nations Tournament in [Chile](/wiki/Chile). Her last appearance for the U-20 team occurred in July 2008, at the [2008 CONCACAF Women's U-20 Championship](/wiki/2008_CONCACAF_Women%27s_U-20_Championship) in [Puebla, Mexico](/wiki/Puebla,_Puebla). O'Hara helped the U-20 team qualify for the [2008 FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup](/wiki/2008_FIFA_U-20_Women%27s_World_Cup) in [Chile](/wiki/Chile). She did not play in the U-20 World Cup, instead remaining with her college team in its [NCAA](/wiki/NCAA) postseason campaign.

### Senior national team (2010–2023)

She was called into the [senior national team](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team)'s training camp in December 2009 and attended the January 2010 training camp in the lead-up to the 2010 [Algarve Cup](/wiki/Algarve_Cup). O'Hara earned her first senior national team cap in March 2010, coming in as a substitute during a friendly match against [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_women%27s_national_football_team).

### 2011 FIFA Women's World Cup

After falling short of making the 21 player World Cup roster, O'Hara was called up to replace [Lindsay Tarpley](/wiki/Lindsay_Tarpley) who tore her [ACL](/wiki/Anterior_cruciate_ligament) in a send-off match against [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team) on May 14, 2011. O'Hara earned just one cap at [right midfield](/wiki/Right_Midfielder) in the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in the final [group stage](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) game against [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_women%27s_national_football_team). The United States went on to win the silver medal in that tournament.

### 2012 Olympics

Throughout her national U-20s, collegiate, and club career, O'Hara was one of the top young offensive players in the United States, but under head coach [Pia Sundhage](/wiki/Pia_Sundhage), O'Hara was converted to play outside back in 2012 after teammate [Ali Krieger](/wiki/Ali_Krieger) went down with an [ACL](/wiki/Anterior_cruciate_ligament) injury in the [2012 CONCACAF Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](/wiki/2012_CONCACAF_Women%27s_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament). Against Guatemala on January 22, 2012, in the Olympic Qualifiers, she made her first start at left back and registered three assists. O'Hara started at right back against [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_women%27s_national_football_team) in the match that qualified the United States for the [2012 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics) in London. O'Hara played in every minute of the United States' gold medal run, one of three American players to do so.

### 2015 FIFA Women's World Cup

In the [United States](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team)' first four games of the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), O'Hara did not see any playing time. O'Hara made her first start of the [tournament](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in the quarter-final game against [China PR](/wiki/China_women%27s_national_football_team). She was replaced by [Christen Press](/wiki/Christen_Press) in the 61st minute. O'Hara scored her first career international goal in the [United States](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team)' 2–0 victory over [Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_football_team) in the semi-final. In the final against [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team), O'Hara entered the game in the 61st minute to replace [Megan Rapinoe](/wiki/Megan_Rapinoe). The United States went on to defeat [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team) 5–2, winning the first World Cup title since [1999](/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) and the third overall World Cup title for the United States since the inaugural Women's World Cup in [1991](/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup).

### 2016 Olympics

During their 2016 appearance at the Olympics, the United States soccer team gained their worst-ever tournament finish. O'Hara played in the first game, which resulted in a 2-0 defeat of New Zealand. Against France, the United States won 1-0. The final game of the play-in round was against Colombia, which resulted in a 2-2 tie, in which O'Hara received a foul. Once they progressed to the quarter-finals, the United States' played their final game of the Olympics against Sweden, losing to the team on penalties. During this final contest, O'Hara posted 2 shots.

### 2019 FIFA Women's World Cup

Despite injuries which kept her from playing regularly for the United States in the year leading up to the World Cup, O'Hara was named to [Jill Ellis](/wiki/Jill_Ellis)' roster for the [2019 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2019_FIFA_World_Cup) in France. She played in five of the United States' seven games and appeared in all knockout stage games.
In the team's [opening game](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Group_F#United_States_vs_Thailand)
against [Thailand](/wiki/Thailand_women%27s_national_football_team), O'Hara crossed the ball to [Alex Morgan](/wiki/Alex_Morgan) in the 12th minute who converted O'Hara's service to notch the team's first goal of the tournament. The U.S. went on to beat Thailand 13–0. O'Hara made her second assist of the tournament in the [semifinal](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_knockout_stage#England_vs_United_States) against [England](/wiki/England_women%27s_national_football_team) when she delivered a cross from the right flank to [Christen Press](/wiki/Christen_Press) whose 10th minute goal put the U.S. in the lead.
O'Hara started in the [final](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Final) against the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_women%27s_national_football_team) but was substituted at halftime due to a collision just before the break with the Dutch winger [Lieke Martens](/wiki/Lieke_Martens). The U.S. won the match 2–0 and O'Hara won her second World Cup.

### 2020 Olympics

Due to COVID-19, the 2020 Olympics were postponed until the following summer. During the first game of Olympics, the United States lost against Sweden 3-0. After this loss, O'Hara noted that the team needed to be "ruthless" in their next game against New Zealand, which they won 6-1. Against Australia, the United States ended the game in a draw of 0-0.

The United States progressed to the quarter-final games, which they played against the Netherlands. O'Hara played the full game, pushing up the flanks in the final third. The game ended 2-2, with the United States winning 4-2 on penalties.

During the semi-final game, the United States faced off against Canada. O'Hara started the game, receiving a yellow card 30 minutes in after making a late challenge against Nichelle Prince. Canada scored after receiving a penalty. O'Hara was replaced by Sam Mewis at 80 minutes. The United States were unable to mount a comeback and lost to Canada for the first time in 20 years.

### 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup

On June 21, 2023, [Vlatko Andonovski](/wiki/Vlatko_Andonovski) named O'Hara to the United States squad for the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in Australia, her fourth World Cup tournament. She made her tournament debut in the 84th minute of the group-stage opener against Vietnam. While she did not feature in the next two games against the Netherlands and Portugal, she made her next appearance during the game against Sweden, coming on in the last minute of extra time before the penalty shootout. O'Hara was the third U.S. player to miss her penalty shot, and Sweden advanced on penalties, eliminating the U.S. from the World Cup in the Round of 16.

## Endorsements

O'Hara has appeared in multiple commercials and advertisements for [Under Armour](/wiki/Under_Armour). In 2015, she appeared in television commercials and promotional materials promoting chocolate milk on behalf of the National Fluid Milk Processor Promotion Board.

## Podcast

O'Hara at the Just Women's Sports pregame show before the 2022 NWSL Championship.

In July 2020, O'Hara launched a podcast with sports website *[Just Women's Sports](/wiki/Just_Women%27s_Sports)*. The podcast was rebranded as *The Players' Pod* in April 2022. Website founder Haley Rosen had asked O'Hara to join the advisory board. O'Hara said that she instead asked to host their podcast because she'd "always thought hosting a podcast would be fun." O'Hara says her goal is to generate "open, candid conversations" about the lives of athletes, particularly female athletes. The podcast posted its last episode on August 9, 2023.

## Personal life

O'Hara was one of many out [LGBT](/wiki/LGBT) athletes to compete in the [2019 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in [France](/wiki/France).
As of 2019, during her [off-season](/wiki/Season_(sports)#Off-season), she resides with her partner, Kameryn Stanhouse, in [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) She got engaged to Stanhouse on New Year's Eve 2022, announcing it to fans via Instagram. On April 23, 2024, O'Hara announced that she was one of the executive producers of "Ripe!," a queer short film in which two young women develop feelings for each other after a competitive moment in a pickup soccer game. "Ripe!," which celebrates the ripening of LGBTQIA+ identities and shedding queer shame, premiered at the 2024 Tribeca Film Festival on June 9, 2024.

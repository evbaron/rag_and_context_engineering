The **Social Democratic Party of Finland**, nicknamed ***demarit*** in Finnish, is a [social democratic](/wiki/Social_democracy) [political party in Finland](/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Finland). It is the third-largest party in the [Parliament of Finland](/wiki/Parliament_of_Finland) with a total of 42 seats.

Founded in 1899 as the **Workers' Party of Finland**, the SDP is Finland's oldest active political party and has a close relationship with the [Central Organisation of Finnish Trade Unions](/wiki/Central_Organisation_of_Finnish_Trade_Unions). It is also a member of the [Party of European Socialists](/wiki/Party_of_European_Socialists), [Progressive Alliance](/wiki/Progressive_Alliance) and [Socialist International](/wiki/Socialist_International).

Following the resignation of [Antti Rinne](/wiki/Antti_Rinne) in December 2019, [Sanna Marin](/wiki/Sanna_Marin) became the country's 46th [prime minister](/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Finland). The SDP formed a new [coalition government](/wiki/Marin_Cabinet) on the basis of its predecessor, the [Rinne Cabinet](/wiki/Rinne_Cabinet), in effect continuing its cooperation with the [Centre Party](/wiki/Centre_Party_(Finland)), [Green League](/wiki/Green_League), [Left Alliance](/wiki/Left_Alliance_(Finland)) and [Swedish People's Party](/wiki/Swedish_People%27s_Party_of_Finland). Of the nineteen ministerial spots that were decided upon in conjunction, seven of them were designated to the SDP in the [Marin Cabinet](/wiki/Marin_Cabinet). In September 2023, [Antti Lindtman](/wiki/Antti_Lindtman) was elected as leader of the party following Marin's resignation after the [2023 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2023_Finnish_parliamentary_election).

## History

The SDP's party conference in Oulu, 1906.

The party was founded as the Workers' Party of Finland in 1899, with its first meeting being held from 17–20 July in [Turku](/wiki/Turku). At the beginning of the 1900s the party presented demands as well as solutions to the [tenant farmer](/wiki/Tenant_farmer) question, the managing of employment, improvement of [workers' rights](/wiki/Labor_rights), [freedom of speech](/wiki/Freedom_of_speech) and an [8-hour work day](/wiki/Eight-hour_day).

In its 1903 second party conference in [Forssa](/wiki/Forssa), the party's name was renamed to the present form: Social Democratic Party of Finland, but the [Grand Duchy of Finland](/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland)'s then [governor-general](/wiki/Governor-general) [Nikolay Bobrikov](/wiki/Nikolay_Bobrikov) had outlawed the SDP from using "social democratic" in their name before, but this ban was not followed on by the party members when the name was changed. At the same time, the at the time radical [Forssa Programme](/wiki/Forssa_Program) was agreed upon, which served as the official party platform until 1952. The goals of the programme were as follows: an 8-hour workday, a [minimum wage](/wiki/Minimum_wage), [universal compulsory education](/wiki/Compulsory_education) and [prohibition](/wiki/Prohibition).

The [Forssa Programme](/wiki/Forssa_Program) is based on the [Erfurt Programme](/wiki/Erfurt_Program) approved by the [Social Democratic Party of Germany](/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany) in 1891 and the [Social Democratic Party of Austria](/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Austria)'s programme, respectively. Its immediate demands have been fulfilled, but the most significant and currently unfulfilled requirement is the right to vote directly on laws ([direct democracy](/wiki/Direct_democracy), as opposed to [representative democracy](/wiki/Representative_democracy) [except for two times, and then just on advisory referendums: once about prohibition in 1931 and another on the [1994 Finnish European Union membership referendum](/wiki/1994_Finnish_European_Union_membership_referendum).) The demands on total separation of church and state, abolition of [religious education](/wiki/Religious_education) in all schools and the prohibition of alcohol have all since then been abandoned.

The SDP was closely associated with the [Finnish Trade Union Federation](/wiki/Finnish_Trade_Union_Federation) (SAJ), established in 1907, with all of its members also being members of the party. The party remained a chiefly extra-parliamentary movement until [universal suffrage](/wiki/Universal_suffrage) was introduced in 1906, after which the SDP's share of the votes reached 47% in the [1916 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/1916_Finnish_parliamentary_election), when the party secured a [majority](/wiki/Majority_government) in the parliament, the only time in the history of Finland when one party has had such a majority. The party lost its majority in the [1917 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/1917_Finnish_parliamentary_election) after the [Russian Provisional Government](/wiki/Russian_Provisional_Government) had rejected its *Valtalaki 1917* proposal and disbanded the Finnish government, starting a rebellion with the broader Finnish labour movement that quickly escalated into the [Finnish Civil War](/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War) in 1918.

SDP members declared Finland a [socialist republic](/wiki/Finnish_Socialist_Workers%27_Republic), but they were defeated by the forces of the [White Guard](/wiki/White_Guard_(Finland)). The war resulted in most of the party leaders being killed, imprisoned or left to seek refuge in [Soviet Russia](/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic). In addition, the process leading to the civil war and the war itself had stripped the party of its [political legitimacy](/wiki/Legitimacy_(political)) and respectability in the eyes of the right-wing majority. However, the political support for the party remained strong. In the [1919 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/1919_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the party, reorganised by [Väinö Tanner](/wiki/V%C3%A4in%C3%B6_Tanner), received 80 of the 200 seats of the parliament. In 1918, former exiled SDP members founded the [Communist Party of Finland](/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Finland) (SKP) in Moscow. Although the SKP was banned in Finland until 1944, it was represented by front organisations, leading to the support of the Finnish working class being divided between the SDP and the SKP.

It became the life's work of [Väinö Tanner](/wiki/V%C3%A4in%C3%B6_Tanner) to re-establish the SDP as a serious, governing party. The result was a much more patriotic SDP which leaned less to the left and was relatively isolated from its [Nordic](/wiki/Nordic_countries) sister parties, namely the [Danish Social Democrats](/wiki/Social_Democrats_(Denmark)), the [Norwegian Labour Party](/wiki/Labour_Party_(Norway)) and the [Swedish Social Democratic Party](/wiki/Swedish_Social_Democratic_Party). President [Pehr Evind Svinhufvud](/wiki/Pehr_Evind_Svinhufvud)'s animosity kept the SDP out of government during his presidency from 1931 to 1937. With the exception of a brief period in 1926, when Tanner formed a minority government, the SDP was excluded from cabinet participation until [Kyösti Kallio](/wiki/Ky%C3%B6sti_Kallio) was elected president in 1937. During [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), the party played a central role in a series of broad coalition cabinets, symbolising national unity forged in response to the threat of the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union) in the [Winter War](/wiki/Winter_War) of 1939–1940. The SDP was a member of the [Labour and Socialist International](/wiki/Labour_and_Socialist_International) from 1923 to 1940.

During the first few months of the [Continuation War](/wiki/Continuation_War) (1941–1944), the country, the parliament and the cabinet were divided on the question of whether Finland's army should stop at the old border and thereby demonstratively refrain from any attempt of conquests. However, the country's dangerous position called for national unity and the SDP's leadership chose to refrain from any visible protests. This decision is sometimes indicated as one of the main reasons behind the post-war division between the main left-wing parties (the SKP and the SDP) and the high percentage of SKP voters in the first elections after the Continuation War. After the war, the SKP was allowed to continue working and the main feature of Finnish political life during the 1944–1949 period was the competition between the SDP and the SKP, both for voters and for the control of the labour unions. During this time, the political field was divided roughly equally between the SDP, the SKP and the [Agrarian League](/wiki/Centre_Party_(Finland)), each party commanding some 25% of the vote. In the post-war era, the SDP adopted a line of defending Finnish sovereignty and democracy in line with the Agrarian League and other [bourgeois](/wiki/Bourgeoisie) political parties, finally leading to the expulsion of the SKP from the cabinet in 1948. As a result, the Soviet Union remained more openly critical towards the SDP than the centre-right parties.

SDP municipal election poster from 1933 ("Municipal power to those who work").

Because of the SDP's [anti-communism](/wiki/Anti-communism), the United States [Central Intelligence Agency](/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency) supported the party by means of funds laundered through Nordic sister parties or through organisations that bought luxury goods such as coffee abroad, then imported and sold them for a high profit as post-war [rationing](/wiki/Rationing) served to inflate prices. In the [1956 Finnish presidential election](/wiki/1956_Finnish_presidential_election), the SDP candidate [Karl-August Fagerholm](/wiki/Karl-August_Fagerholm) lost by only one electoral vote to [Urho Kekkonen](/wiki/Urho_Kekkonen). Fagerholm would act as prime minister in the [Fagerholm II Cabinet](/wiki/Fagerholm_II_Cabinet) (1956–1957) and the [Fagerholm III Cabinet](/wiki/Fagerholm_III_Cabinet) (1958–1959). The latter cabinet was forced to resign due to Soviet pressure, leading to a series of cabinets led by the Agrarian League. In 1958, due to the election of [Väinö Tanner](/wiki/V%C3%A4in%C3%B6_Tanner) as party chairman, a faction of the SDP resigned and formed the [Social Democratic Union of Workers and Smallholders](/wiki/Social_Democratic_Union_of_Workers_and_Smallholders) (TPSL) around the former SDP chairman [Emil Skog](/wiki/Emil_Skog). The dispute was over several issues, namely whether the party should function as an interest group and whether it should co-operate with the anti-communists and right-wingers or with president Kekkonen, the Agrarian League and the SKP. During the 1960s, the TPSL dwindled, its members returning one by one to the SDP or joining the SKP, with Skog himself returning to the SDP in 1965. In the [1970 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/1970_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the TPSL failed to gain any seats in parliament. Only in 1966 was the SDP able to satisfy the Soviet Union about its friendly attitude towards it and could thus return to the cabinet. Since then, the SDP has been represented in most Finnish cabinets, often cooperating with the [centrist](/wiki/Centrism)-[agrarian](/wiki/Nordic_agrarian_parties) Centre Party (formerly the Agrarian League), but sometimes with the [liberal-conservative](/wiki/Liberal_conservatism) [National Coalition Party](/wiki/National_Coalition_Party). The SDP was in opposition from 1991 to 1995, when the main parties in the cabinet were the Centre Party and the [National Coalition Party](/wiki/National_Coalition_Party) (NCP).

The [1995 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/1995_Finnish_parliamentary_election) saw a landslide victory for the SDP, achieving their best results since [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). The SDP rose to government from the opposition and leader [Paavo Lipponen](/wiki/Paavo_Lipponen) headed two consecutive cabinets from 1995 to 2003. During this time, the party adopted a [pro-European](/wiki/Pro-Europeanism) stance and contributed actively to the Finnish membership in the European Union in 1995 in concert with the cabinet. In the [2003 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2003_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the SDP won 53 of the 200 seats, ending up a close second to the Centre Party. As a result, Lipponen became the [Speaker of Parliament](/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Parliament_of_Finland) and the Centre Party leader [Anneli Jäätteenmäki](/wiki/Anneli_J%C3%A4%C3%A4tteenm%C3%A4ki) became the new [Prime Minister](/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Finland), leading a [coalition cabinet](/wiki/J%C3%A4%C3%A4tteenm%C3%A4ki_Cabinet) that included the SDP which got eight ministerial posts. After two months in office, Jäätteenmäki resigned due to a scandal relating to the [Iraq leak](/wiki/Iraq_leak) and was replaced by [Matti Vanhanen](/wiki/Matti_Vanhanen), another Centre Party representative, who commanded the [Vanhanen I Cabinet](/wiki/Vanhanen_I_Cabinet).

[Sanna Marin](/wiki/Sanna_Marin), the party's leader from August 2020 to September 2023.

Support for the Social Democrats by municipality in the [2011 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2011_Finnish_parliamentary_election) which saw the party faring strongest in southern and eastern parts of the country.

In the [2007 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2007_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the SDP gained the third-most votes. The chairman of the then-largest Centre Party, Matti Vanhanen, became the Prime Minister and formed a [coalition cabinet](/wiki/Vanhanen_II_Cabinet) consisting of the [Green League](/wiki/Green_League), the NCP and the [Swedish People's Party of Finland](/wiki/Swedish_People%27s_Party_of_Finland) (SPP), leaving the SDP to the opposition. SDP leader [Eero Heinäluoma](/wiki/Eero_Hein%C3%A4luoma) did not immediately resign as party chairman, but he did announce his withdrawal from running for party chairman in the following party conference. He was replaced by [Jutta Urpilainen](/wiki/Jutta_Urpilainen). The SDP suffered further losses in the [2008 Finnish municipal elections](/wiki/2008_Finnish_municipal_elections) and the [2009 European Parliament election](/wiki/2009_European_Parliament_election_in_Finland). In the [2011 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2011_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the SDP lost three more seats, ending up with 19.1 percent of the vote which corresponded to 42 seats, the party's worst-ever result. However, as the Centre Party lost even more voters, the SDP became the second-largest party in the country after the NCP, receiving only some 1,500 votes more than the [Finns Party](/wiki/Finns_Party) which came in third. After lengthy negotiations, a six-party coalition government, the [Katainen Cabinet](/wiki/Katainen_Cabinet), was formed with the NCP and the SDP as the two main parties. SDP leader Jutta Urpilainen became the cabinet's [Minister of Finance](/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_(Finland)), with NCP chairman [Jyrki Katainen](/wiki/Jyrki_Katainen) serving as prime minister.

In the 2014 party conference, Urpilainen was narrowly defeated by her challenger [Antti Rinne](/wiki/Antti_Rinne) in a 257 to 243 vote. Urpilainen subsequently stepped down as the Minister of Finance, passing the seat on to Rinne. In the [2015 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2015_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the drop of support continued for the SDP. The party lost eight more seats compared to the 2011 parliamentary election, ending up with 34 seats and 16.5 percent of the vote. With the repeat of the worst-ever result, the SDP dropped to being the fourth largest political party in Finland, receiving 50,110 fewer votes than the NCP, yet 237,000 more votes than the Green League. The SDP was left in the opposition and provided extensive criticism on the actions of the [Sipilä Cabinet](/wiki/Sipil%C3%A4_Cabinet) on matters such as alcohol policy, cuts to education spending and the so-called active model. On 22 June 2016, [Maria Tolppanen](/wiki/Maria_Tolppanen), a Finns Party representative, joined the SDP. This increased the SDP's parliamentary seat number to 35.

In the [2019 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2019_Finnish_parliamentary_election), the SDP gained 6 seats in comparison to the 2015 parliamentary election and became the largest party in the parliament. Based on the answers and initial talks with all parties, [Rinne](/wiki/Antti_Rinne) announced that he would negotiate forming a government with the [Centre Party](/wiki/Centre_Party_(Finland)), the [Green League](/wiki/Green_League), the [Left Alliance](/wiki/Left_Alliance_(Finland)) and the [Swedish People's Party](/wiki/Swedish_People%E2%80%99s_Party_of_Finland). The negotiations were ultimately successful and the [Rinne Cabinet](/wiki/Rinne_Cabinet) was formally inaugurated on 6 June 2019. On 3 December 2019, Rinne resigned as Prime Minister after the Centre Party had expressed a lack of confidence in Rinne for his handling of the events surrounding a postal strike in Finland. He was followed in the position by [Sanna Marin](/wiki/Sanna_Marin), who was appointed as prime minister on 10 December 2019.

## Ideology

The SDP is a centre-left [social-democratic](/wiki/Social_democracy) party.

In its 2020 declaration of principles the party's ideals and priorities are: [sustainable development](/wiki/Sustainable_development), [all-encompassing equality](/wiki/Egalitarianism), [peace](/wiki/Peace), [solidarity](/wiki/Solidarity), [freedom](/wiki/Freedom), [co-operation](/wiki/Cooperation), [a clean and pristine environment](/wiki/Environmental_protection) together with [democratic socialism](/wiki/Democratic_socialism). The SDP also embraces [humanism](/wiki/Humanism)'s values as well as the [Nordic model](/wiki/Nordic_model)'s accomplishments.

In the 1900s, the party known as the *Finnish Workers' Party* was founded on the basis of [social issues](/wiki/Social_issue), class and [socialism](/wiki/Socialism). SDP was the only political party in Finland for a long time. In 1907, the SDP was the strongest socialist party in Europe, as evidenced by the qualified majority in the [Senate of Finland](/wiki/Senate_of_Finland) in 1917. At the beginning of the 20th century, the party received its main support from groups of the [landless population](/wiki/Landlessness) and the [rural population](/wiki/Rural_area). In 1919, at the SDP's meeting, a split was made with the radical communists, as a result of which they broke away and founded the [SSTP](/wiki/Socialist_Workers%27_Party_of_Finland). As a result of the [civil war](/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War) and the [October Revolution](/wiki/October_Revolution), the workers' movement became even more divided.

Up until the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), the party opposed Finland joining [NATO](/wiki/NATO) and instead preferred for it to remain in the [Partnership for Peace](/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace).

The SDP is in favour of [queer rights](/wiki/LGBT_rights_by_country_or_territory), the construction of [nuclear power plants](/wiki/Nuclear_power_plant), the conservation of [Swedish](/wiki/Swedish_language) as one of Finland's two [official languages](/wiki/Official_language), the [separation of church and state](/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state), and to the increase of funding given by the state to [public schools](/wiki/State_school) and universities. The party is advocating for Finland to become coal-free by 2030. The SDP had advocated for policies preventing foreigners from outside the EU from working in Finland, but has since then softened its positions on immigration and has come to support certain immigration reforms. In its 2023 parliamentary [election programme](/wiki/Party_platform) its self-declared goal was the increase of work-based immigration coming to Finland as a way of responding to the country's labour shortage and low [birth rate](/wiki/Birth_rate). In 2023, the SDP, along with the [NCP](/wiki/National_Coalition_Party), both criticised the [Finns Party](/wiki/Finns_Party) for their lack of willingness to the easing of [work permit](/wiki/Work_permit) requirements for foreigners coming from outside the European Union.

The party opposed certain economic reforms both in the [2011 Finnish parliamentary election](/wiki/2011_Finnish_parliamentary_election) and in the subsequent negotiations about the government programme. The SDP maintains a close relationship with trade unions. The party has opposed social reforms that would reduce the role of earnings-related unemployment benefits. The government pays the benefits to recipients through financial middlemen that are almost exclusively trade unions.

## Controversies

Some of the SDP's politicians have received criticism about their defense of Russia for years by the media and academics, for example, SDP politicians [Eero Heinäluoma](/wiki/Eero_Hein%C3%A4luoma), [Paavo Lipponen](/wiki/Paavo_Lipponen), [Erkki Tuomioja](/wiki/Erkki_Tuomioja), Antton Rönnholm and [Tarja Halonen](/wiki/Tarja_Halonen) have received critique on their positions towards Russia.

In 2005, according to Halonen, Russia's goals were: "...[democracy](/wiki/Democracy), [human rights](/wiki/Human_rights) and good governance." Nine years later, in 2014 after [Russia annexed Crimea](/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), Halonen thought that Russia should not have been punished by sanctions or isolation.

SDP's former party secretary Antton Rönnholm has also taken his part. Through his [consulting firm's services](/wiki/Consulting_firm) offered to [Gazprom](/wiki/Gazprom), he was sent an invoice for almost 200,000 euros for assisting in the [South Stream gas pipeline project](/wiki/South_Stream). More than half of Gazprom is owned by the Russian state, and it is partly used as a geopolitical tool in Europe and the rest of the world.

In February 2022, politician [Erkki Tuomioja](/wiki/Erkki_Tuomioja) published a work with the title "Finland and NATO – Why Finland should have the opportunity to apply for NATO membership and why that opportunity should not be used now". In his work Tuomioja estimated that Russia was viewed rather unanalytically.

Also in February 2022, when Russia had already been pressuring Ukraine for a long time, the social-democratic [MEP](/wiki/Member_of_the_European_Parliament) [Eero Heinäluoma](/wiki/Eero_Hein%C3%A4luoma) and [Mauri Pekkarinen](/wiki/Mauri_Pekkarinen) from the [Centre Party](/wiki/Centre_Party_(Finland)) both said in a Finnish current affairs television programme that preparing for a Russian threat was part of the problem. According to Heinäluoma, placing defensive armaments in Russia's neighbouring countries was instead a threat to Russia.

[Paavo Lipponen](/wiki/Paavo_Lipponen) has lobbied for and been a consultant for Russia's [Nord Stream](/wiki/Nord_Stream) project since 2008. In that year [Russia went to war with Georgia](/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War). In a report published by the [European Parliament](/wiki/European_Parliament)'s special [committee](/wiki/Committee) in 2022, the Social Democratic Party prime minister Lipponen and Centre Party prime minister [Esko Aho](/wiki/Esko_Aho) were stated to be among the European politicians that Russia and China had hijacked to promote their special interests. Some current SDP politicians have therefore appeased Russia in the past.

In September 2023, when [Antti Lindtman](/wiki/Antti_Lindtman) got elected [chair](/wiki/Chairperson) of the SDP a scandal broke out due to an old picture of him posing near four other naked young adults, nude, wearing a [pointed hat](/wiki/Pointed_hat) in the style of a [christmas elf](/wiki/Christmas_elf), covered by a [balaclava](/wiki/Balaclava_(clothing)) and with an [airsoft gun](/wiki/Airsoft_gun) in hand while in his [adolescent years](/wiki/Adolescence), while two others were doing a [nazi salute](/wiki/Nazi_salute) in the same picture. Because of this, Lindtman was accused of being a Nazi. He responded by stating that the image had been taken during his time in a high school film group by the name of "Team Paha, English: Team Bad" in a [Pikkujoulu](/wiki/Pikkujoulu) party while they were messing around and posted online. Lindtman additionally firmly denied the allegation of being a [national socialist](/wiki/Nazism).

The [party secretary](/wiki/Secretary_(title)), [Mikkel Näkkäläjärvi](/wiki/Mikkel_N%C3%A4kk%C3%A4l%C3%A4j%C3%A4rvi)'s, nomination and subsequent appointment to his role during the SDP's 2023 [conference](/wiki/Party_conference) in [Jyväskylä](/wiki/Jyv%C3%A4skyl%C3%A4) was criticised because of his criminal background. In 2011 he had [driven a car while under the presence of alcohol](/wiki/Drunk_driving), and was charged with a 30-day [suspended sentence](/wiki/Suspended_sentence) and an accompanying fine. Näkkäläjärvi had also broken into a retired old lady's [summer cottage](/wiki/Cottage) as a 15-year-old teenager with three others around the same age as him and participated in the killing and burning of a grown-up cat in a [bonfire](/wiki/Bonfire). Following this, he was charged with burglary, vandalism and [animal cruelty](/wiki/Cruelty_to_animals) as a young person. Näkkäläjärvi has apologised for all of his past misdeeds.

## Voter base

The average age of an SDP member is 61.5 years. Over one half of all SDP voters are active members of the workforce.

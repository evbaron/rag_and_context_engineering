The **Brazil national football team** ([Portuguese](/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Seleção Brasileira de Futebol* ), nicknamed ***Seleção Canarinho*** , represents [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil) in men's international [football](/wiki/Association_football) and is administered by the [Brazilian Football Confederation](/wiki/Brazilian_Football_Confederation), the governing body of [football in Brazil](/wiki/Football_in_Brazil). It has been a member of [FIFA](/wiki/FIFA) since 1923 and was a founding member of [CONMEBOL](/wiki/CONMEBOL) in 1916. It was also a member of [PFC](/wiki/Panamerican_Championship#Panamerican_Football_Confederation), the unified confederation of the Americas, from 1946 to 1961.

Brazil is the most successful national team in the [FIFA World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup), winning the tournament five times: [1958](/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), [1962](/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), [1970](/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup), [1994](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2002](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). In 114 World Cup matches played, the team has 76 wins, 19 losses, 247 points and a 129 [goal difference](/wiki/Goal_difference). It is the only national team to have played in all [World Cup editions](/wiki/National_team_appearances_in_the_FIFA_World_Cup#Comprehensive_team_results_by_tournament) without any absence nor need for playoffs, and was the most successful team in the now-defunct [FIFA Confederations Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup), with victories in [1997](/wiki/1997_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), [2005](/wiki/2005_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), [2009](/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), and [2013](/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup).

In FIFA's ranking system, Brazil holds the record for most [Team of the Year](/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings#Performances_by_countries) first ranking wins with 14. Many commentators, football experts, and former players consider the Brazil team of 1970 to be the greatest national squad of all time. Other Brazilian squads are also highly esteemed and regularly appear listed among the best teams of all time, such as the teams of 1958–62 and of 1994–02 period. In 1996, Brazil went undefeated for 35 consecutive matches, a world record which held for 25 years.

Brazil has developed many rivalries through the years, the most notable being with [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Brazil_football_rivalry), [Italy](/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry) and [Uruguay](/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry). Brazil has the highest average [Elo football rating](/wiki/World_Football_Elo_Ratings) over time, and the fourth-highest peak Elo rating of all time.

## History

Main article: [History of the Brazil national football team](/wiki/History_of_the_Brazil_national_football_team)

### 1914–1929: Early history

It is widely believed that Brazil's first-ever game was a 1914 match between a [Rio de Janeiro](/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro) and [São Paulo](/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo) select team and the English club [Exeter City](/wiki/Exeter_City_F.C.), held in the stadium of [Fluminense FC](/wiki/Fluminense_FC). Brazil won 2–0 with goals by [Oswaldo Gomes](/wiki/Oswaldo_Gomes) and [Osman Medeiros](/wiki/Osman_Medeiros), though it has also been claimed that the match was a 3–3 draw. In 1916, Brazil played its first matches against [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) and [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team).

Brazil achieved third place at the first two editions of the South American Championship—which would later become the [Copa América](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica)—in [1916](/wiki/1916_South_American_Championship) and [1917](/wiki/1917_South_American_Championship). Led by the goalscoring abilities of [Arthur Friedenreich](/wiki/Arthur_Friedenreich), they were champions in the [1919](/wiki/1919_South_American_Championship) edition, which was held in their own country. Brazil finished third in [1920](/wiki/1920_South_American_Championship) and second in 1921. They won the [1922](/wiki/1922_South_American_Championship) championship, again on home soil, and achieved second place in [1925](/wiki/1925_South_American_Championship).

### 1930–1949: First World Cup appearance and title drought

In 1930, Brazil played in [the first FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), held in [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay). After losing to [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) and defeating [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia_national_football_team) in the group stage, they were eliminated from the competition. Four years later, Brazil lost in the first round to [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) in the all-knockout [1934 World Cup](/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) in Italy.

After finishing second at the [1937 South American Championship](/wiki/1937_South_American_Championship), Brazil reached the semi-finals at the [1938 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) in France. They were eliminated by eventual champions [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team), but managed to achieve third place after defeating [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team). Brazil was the only [South American](/wiki/South_America) team to participate in the tournament; other teams held a boycott to protest the tournament being held in Europe twice in a row. During [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), Brazil finished third in the [1942 South American Championship](/wiki/1942_South_American_Championship) and second in the [1945](/wiki/1945_South_American_Championship) edition. After the war, Brazil achieved second place at the [1946](/wiki/1946_South_American_Championship) edition, and won their third title at the [1949](/wiki/1949_South_American_Championship) edition, held in Brazil. The victory ended the team's 27-year drought without a South American title.

### 1950 World Cup and the *Maracanazo*

The Brazil national team at the 1950 World Cup

After World War II caused widespread destruction in Europe, Brazil's bid to host the [1950 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) went unopposed. During the tournament's group stage, Brazil defeated [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team) 4–0 at the [Maracanã](/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium) in [Rio de Janeiro](/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro) (then the Brazilian capital), tied with [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland_national_football_team) 2–2 at the [Pacaembu](/wiki/Pacaembu_Stadium) in [São Paulo](/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo), and returned to the Maracanã to defeat [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) 2–0.

Unlike World Cup tournaments before and after, this edition had a final group stage to decide the champion. At this stage, Brazil defeated [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) 7-1 and [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) 6–1. In the last game of the final round against [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team) at the Maracanã, Brazil needed a win or draw to achieve a World Cup victory. Uruguay defeated Brazil 2–1, winning the World Cup in a game known as the [Maracanazo](/wiki/Uruguay_v_Brazil_(1950_FIFA_World_Cup)). The defeat led to a period of national mourning in Brazil, and marked the end of the Brazil team wearing white as their default uniform.

### 1951–1957: Modern uniform and the "Battle of Berne"

After the disappointment of the Maracanazo, Brazil finished second at the [1953 South American Championship](/wiki/1953_South_American_Championship) in Peru. At the [1954 World Cup](/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland), Brazil debuted a new uniform designed by [Aldyr Schlee](/wiki/Aldyr_Schlee). It featured yellow shirts with green details, blue shorts, and white socks, and is still used today. In Barzil's two group stage matches, they defeated [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team) 5-0 and tied with [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) 1–1. In the quarter-finals, Brazil lost 4–2 to [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) in one of the ugliest matches in football history, known as the "[Battle of Berne](/wiki/Battle_of_Berne_(1954_FIFA_World_Cup))". Brazil did not play in the [1955 South American Championship](/wiki/1955_South_American_Championship), but finished fourth at both the [1956](/wiki/1956_South_American_Championship) and [1957](/wiki/1957_South_American_Championship) editions.

### 1958–1970: Pelé and the First Golden Era

The Brazil national team at the [1959 Copa América](/wiki/1959_South_American_Championship_(Argentina))

During the [1958 World Cup](/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden), Brazil defeated [Austria](/wiki/Austria_national_football_team) 3–0 in their first group match, then tied [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) 0–0. Before their third group match against the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team), coach [Vicente Feola](/wiki/Vicente_Feola) added [Zito](/wiki/Zito_(footballer,_born_1932)), [Garrincha](/wiki/Garrincha) and [Pelé](/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) to the starting line-up. From the [kick-off](/wiki/Kick-off_(association_football)), Brazil applied relentless pressure; in his biography of Garrincha, [Ruy Castro](/wiki/Ruy_Castro) described the first three minutes of the match as "the greatest three minutes in the history of football". [Vavá](/wiki/Vav%C3%A1) gave Brazil the lead, and they won the match 2–0. Pelé scored the only goal of their quarter-final victory over [Wales](/wiki/Wales_national_football_team), and they defeated [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) 5–2 in the semi-final. Brazil then achieved a 5–2 victory over [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) in [the final](/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup_final), winning their first World Cup and becoming the first nation to win a World Cup outside of its own continent. Pelé described the moment as "a nation coming of age".

Brazil competed in two South American championships held in 1959. They finished second in the [first tournament](/wiki/1959_South_American_Championship_(Argentina)), and third in the [second tournament](/wiki/1959_South_American_Championship_(Ecuador)). At the [1962 World Cup](/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Chile](/wiki/Chile), Brazil defeated [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team) 2–0 in their first group match, then tied 0–0 with [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team). Pelé was injured during the second match and was unable to play for the rest of the tournament. Brazil then defeated Spain 2–1 in their final group stage match. Brazil eliminated [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) 3–1 in the quarterfinals, defeated [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) 4–2 in the semifinals, and secured their second consecutive World Cup title with a [3–1 win](/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_final) in the final against Czechoslovakia. Brazil finished second at the [1963 South American Championship](/wiki/1963_South_American_Championship) in Bolivia.

Defending champions Brazil at the [1962 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup)

In the [1966 World Cup](/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup) held in [England](/wiki/England), Brazil had their worst performance in a World Cup. Brazil defeated [Bulgaria](/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) 2–0, but lost to [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 3–1. The tournament was remembered for its excessively physical play, and Pelé was one of the players most affected. Against [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal_national_football_team), several violent tackles by the Portuguese defenders caused forward player Pelé to leave the match and the tournament. Brazil lost this match 3-1 and was eliminated in the first round of the World Cup for the first time since 1934. They have not failed to reach the knockout stages of the competition since. Brazil became the second defending champion to be eliminated in the first round after [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) in 1950. France in 2002 (without scoring a single goal and drawing a match), Italy in 2010, [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) in 2014 and [Germany](/wiki/Germany_national_football_team) 2018 also repeated the unglorious feat. After the tournament, Pelé declared that he did not wish to play in the World Cup again. Nonetheless, he returned in 1970.

After the early exit at the World Cup in 1966, Brazil did not take part in the [1967 South American Championship](/wiki/1967_South_American_Championship) held in Uruguay. This was the last time Brazil did not take part at the tournament, and that would be the last edition before the tournament was renamed as the Copa América.

The [1970 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup)-winning Brazil team, considered by many commentators as the greatest football team ever

At the [1970 World Cup](/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico), Brazil fielded what is widely considered the best World Cup squad in history. It was led by Pelé, captain [Carlos Alberto Torres](/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Torres), [Jairzinho](/wiki/Jairzinho), [Tostão](/wiki/Tost%C3%A3o), [Gérson](/wiki/G%C3%A9rson) and [Rivellino](/wiki/Rivellino). The team won all six of their games, starting with the group stage matches: 4–1 over [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team), 1–0 over [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) (despite a famous save by Gordon Banks), and 3–2 over [Romania](/wiki/Romania_national_football_team). Brazil then won 4–2 against [Peru](/wiki/Peru_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals, defeated [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team) 3–1 in the semi-finals, and achieved a 4–1 victory over [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) in [the final](/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Jairzinho ranked second in total goals scored in the tournament with seven, and is the only player to score in every match in a World Cup; Pelé finished with four goals. Brazil became the first nation to lift the [Jules Rimet trophy](/wiki/Jules_Rimet_trophy) for the third time, which meant they were allowed to keep it. A replacement trophy was then commissioned, though it would be 24 years before Brazil won it again. The Jules Rimet trophy [was stolen](/wiki/1983_theft_of_the_Jules_Rimet_Trophy) from the [Confederação Brasileira de Futebol](/wiki/Brazilian_Football_Confederation) (CBF) in 1983, and was never recovered. CBF was later awarded a replica trophy.

### 1971–1993: The first dry spell

After the international retirement of Pelé and other stars from the 1970 squad, Brazil played at the [1974 World Cup](/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) hosted by [West Germany](/wiki/West_Germany). Goalless draws with [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) and [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) were followed by a 3–0 win over [Zaire](/wiki/DR_Congo_national_football_team) at the first group stage, finishing second in the group. At the second group stage, Brazil defeated [East Germany](/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) 1-0 and [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) 2–1, but was not able to overcome the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), being defeated 2–0, and then Brazil finished in fourth place after losing the third place game 1–0 to [Poland](/wiki/Poland_national_football_team).

Brazil finished third place at the [1975 Copa América](/wiki/1975_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), the first with no fixed hosts.

Then, at the [1978 World Cup](/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) held in [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina), Brazil tied [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) 1-1, tied [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) 0-0 and defeated [Austria](/wiki/Austria_national_football_team) 1–0 at the first group stage. Brazil finished second, the last time Brazil failed to win their group on the first stage. In the second group stage, Brazil defeated [Peru](/wiki/Peru_national_football_team) 3–0, tied with tournament hosts [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) 0-0, and defeated [Poland](/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 3–1. However, Argentina defeated Peru 6–0 in a match held later the same day, and thus Argentina qualified for the final in a match accused of ultimately-unproven [match fixing](/wiki/Match_fixing) at the expense of Brazil. [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil_at_the_1978_FIFA_World_Cup#Starting_11) subsequently beat [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) 2–1 in the third place play-off, and were the only team to remain unbeaten in the tournament.

Brazil then ended at third place at the [1979 Copa América](/wiki/1979_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), also without a single host country.

At the [1982 World Cup](/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), held in [Spain](/wiki/Spain), Brazil were the tournament favorites, and easily moved through the first group stage, with a 2–1 win against the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team), a 4–1 victory against [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and a 4–0 thrashing of [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_men%27s_national_football_team). In the second group stage, Brazil defeated [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) 3–1, but a [3–2 defeat](/wiki/Brazil_vs_Italy_(1982)) in [Barcelona](/wiki/Barcelona) to [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team), in a classic World Cup match, eliminated them from the tournament in the match that they refer to as "Sarriá's Tragedy", referencing the [stadium](/wiki/Sarri%C3%A0_Stadium)'s name. The [1982 team](/wiki/Brazil_at_the_1982_FIFA_World_Cup#Starting_11), with a midfield of [Sócrates](/wiki/S%C3%B3crates), [Zico](/wiki/Zico_(footballer)), [Falcão](/wiki/Paulo_Roberto_Falc%C3%A3o) and [Toninho Cerezo](/wiki/Toninho_Cerezo), is remembered as perhaps the greatest team never to win a [World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup).

At the [1983 Copa América](/wiki/1983_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), the third and final tournament without a fixed host, Brazil lost the title to [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team) and finished runners-up.

Several players, including Sócrates and Zico, from 1982 returned to play at the [1986 World Cup](/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico). Brazil, still a very good team and more disciplined defensively than four years earlier, won the three group stage matches (1–0 against [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team), 1–0 against [Algeria](/wiki/Algeria_national_football_team), and 3–0 against [Northern Ireland](/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team)), without conceding a goal. Then Brazil defeated [Poland](/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) 4–0 in the round of 16, and in the quarterfinals Brazil faced the [Michel Platini](/wiki/Michel_Platini)-led [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) in a classic of [Total Football](/wiki/Total_Football). The game played to a 1–1 draw in regulation time where Zico missed a penalty kick, and after a goalless extra time, it all came down to a penalty shoot-out, where Brazil was defeated 4–3.

Brazil finished fifth place at the [1987 Copa América](/wiki/1987_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina), and in 1989, Brazil hosted and won the [1989 Copa América](/wiki/1989_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), thus ending Brazil's 19-year streak without an official title.

At the [1990 World Cup](/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Italy](/wiki/Italy), Brazil was coached by [Sebastião Lazaroni](/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A3o_Lazaroni), who had been the coach in the 1989 Copa América. With a [defensive scheme](/wiki/Brazil_at_the_1990_FIFA_World_Cup#Starting_11), whose main symbol was midfielder [Dunga](/wiki/Dunga), forward [Careca](/wiki/Careca) and three centre-backs, the team lacked creativity but made it to the second round after defeating [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) 2–1, [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) 1–0, and [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) 1–0. Brazil was eliminated by [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona)-led [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) in the round of 16 in [Turin](/wiki/Turin), losing to their South American archrivals 1–0 thanks to a goal by [Claudio Caniggia](/wiki/Claudio_Caniggia).

Brazil would struggle in the next cycle, as they finished runners-up at the [1991 Copa América](/wiki/1991_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Chile](/wiki/Chile), losing the title to [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team). In the [1993 Copa América](/wiki/1993_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Ecuador](/wiki/Ecuador), Brazil was knocked out by Argentina on the quarterfinals on penalties. In the [qualifiers for the 1994 World Cup](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(CONMEBOL)), Brazil lost a qualifying match for the first time when [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia_national_football_team) defeated the team 2–0 in [La Paz](/wiki/La_Paz). Brazil qualified for the tournament after defeating Uruguay at Maracanã with two Romário goals.

### The Second Golden Era (1994–2005)

Brazil's [Ronaldo](/wiki/Ronaldo_(Brazilian_footballer)) in the 2002 FIFA World Cup

Brazil went 24 years without winning a World Cup or even participating in a final. Their struggles ended at the [1994 tournament](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) held in the [United States](/wiki/United_States), where a solid side headed by [Romário](/wiki/Rom%C3%A1rio) and [Bebeto](/wiki/Bebeto) in attack, captain [Dunga](/wiki/Dunga) in midfield, goalkeeper [Cláudio Taffarel](/wiki/Cl%C3%A1udio_Taffarel) and defender [Jorginho](/wiki/Jorginho_(footballer,_born_1964)), won the World Cup for a then-record fourth time. Brazil started by defeating [Russia](/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) 2–0, then winning 3–0 against [Cameroon](/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) and tying with [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) 1-1. Their campaign proceeded a 1–0 victory over the [United States](/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_soccer_team) in the round of 16 at [Stanford Stadium](/wiki/Stanford_Stadium) on the [4th of July](/wiki/Independence_Day_(United_States)), a 3–2 win over the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals in [Dallas](/wiki/Dallas), and a 1–0 victory over [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) in a rematch of the group stage match at the semi-finals at [Pasadena](/wiki/Pasadena,_California)'s [Rose Bowl](/wiki/Rose_Bowl_(stadium)). This set up Brazil–[Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) in the final in Pasadena. A game played in searing heat ended as a goalless draw, with Italy's defence led by [Franco Baresi](/wiki/Franco_Baresi) keeping out Romário, penalty kicks loomed, and Brazil became champions with [Roberto Baggio](/wiki/Roberto_Baggio) missing Italy's last penalty. Despite the triumph, the 1994 World Cup winning team is not held in the same high esteem in Brazil as their other World Cup winning teams. *[FourFourTwo](/wiki/FourFourTwo)* magazine labelled the 1994 team "unloved" in Brazil due to their pragmatic, defensive style over the more typical Brazilian style of attacking flair, in spite of the players' individual status as idols (mainly Romário, who was known as a clinical striker and whose contributions to the team are widely regarded as responsible both for allowing the team to qualify following a rocky start and for winning the tournament).

In the meantime, Brazil lost the [1995 Copa América](/wiki/1995_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) to hosts [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team) [on penalties](/wiki/1995_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final), followed by victory in the [1997](/wiki/1997_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) tournament against [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia_national_football_team), the host of the latter tournament, after a [3–1 win](/wiki/1997_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final), the first time Brazil won the Copa América held outside of Brazil. That same year, Brazil won the [Confederations Cup](/wiki/1997_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in [Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Saudi_Arabia) with a [6-0 win](/wiki/1997_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final) against [Australia](/wiki/Australia_men%27s_national_soccer_team).

Entering the [1998 World Cup](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) as defending champions, Brazil finished runner-up. Having topped their group after beating [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) 2–1, defeating [Morocco](/wiki/Morocco_national_football_team) 3-0 and losing 2–1 to [Norway](/wiki/Norway_national_football_team), Brazil thrashed [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) 4–1 in the round of 16, defeated [Denmark](/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) 3–2 in the quarterfinals, and then Brazil beat the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) on penalties in the semi-final following a 1–1 draw. Player of the tournament [Ronaldo](/wiki/Ronaldo_(Brazilian_footballer)) scored four goals and made three assists en route to the [final](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_final). The build up to the final itself was overshadowed by Ronaldo suffering a [convulsive fit](/wiki/Epileptic_seizure) only hours before kick off. The starting line up without Ronaldo was released to a shocked world media, but after pleading that he felt fine and requested to play, Ronaldo was reinstated by the coach, before giving a below par performance as [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team), led by [Zidane](/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane), won [3-0](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

Brazil won the [1999 Copa América](/wiki/1999_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay) with a [3–0 win](/wiki/1999_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final) over [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team) in the final, but lost the [1999 Confederations Cup](/wiki/1999_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) [final](/wiki/1999_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final) to hosts [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team), before being eliminated from the [2001 Copa América](/wiki/2001_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia) in the quarterfinals with a 2–0 loss to [Honduras](/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team). The team would also struggle in the [2002 World Cup qualifiers](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(CONMEBOL)), only securing a spot on the final matchday with a 3–0 victory over [Venezuela](/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team).

2002 World Cup-winning Brazil plane livery, from [Varig](/wiki/Varig).

However, the [2002 World Cup](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), co-hosted by [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea) and [Japan](/wiki/Japan), would be different, as Brazil topped their group with nine points and three wins. Brazil played the group stage in South Korea, and their first match was against [Turkey](/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team), in [Ulsan](/wiki/Ulsan). In that match, Rivaldo fell to the ground clutching his face after Turkey's [Hakan Ünsal](/wiki/Hakan_%C3%9Cnsal) had kicked the ball at his legs. Rivaldo escaped suspension but was fined £5,180 for play-acting, and became the first player ever to be punished in [FIFA](/wiki/FIFA)'s crackdown on [diving](/wiki/Diving_(association_football)). Brazil came from behind to defeat the Turks 2–1. This was followed by a 4–0 win over [China](/wiki/China_national_football_team) and a 5–2 victory against [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team). For the knockout round matches, Brazil traveled to Japan, continuing their campaign by defeating [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team) 2–0 in [Kobe](/wiki/Kobe). They then proceeded to defeat [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) 2–1 in the quarter-finals in [Shizuoka](/wiki/Shizuoka,_Shizuoka), with the winning goal coming from a free-kick by [Ronaldinho](/wiki/Ronaldinho) from 40 yards out, but Ronaldinho was also sent off that match. The semi-final was against Turkey in [Saitama](/wiki/Saitama,_Saitama), a rematch of the group stage, with Brazil winning 1–0 to advance to their third consecutive final. The [final](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) was against [Germany](/wiki/Germany_national_football_team) in [Yokohama](/wiki/Yokohama), where Ronaldo scored both goals in Brazil's 2–0 triumph. Ronaldo also won the [Golden Shoe](/wiki/Adidas_Golden_Shoe) as the tournament's leading scorer with eight goals. Among the highlights of the campaign were the "Three R's" (Ronaldo, [Rivaldo](/wiki/Rivaldo) and Ronaldinho). 2002 was Brazil's fifth world championship, their last to date, with the success seeing them receive the [Laureus World Sports Award for Team of the Year](/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award_for_Team_of_the_Year).

### The second dry spell (2006–2014)

After their World Cup victory in [Asia](/wiki/Asia), Brazil won the [2004 Copa América](/wiki/2004_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) in [Peru](/wiki/Peru), defeating [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) on penalties after a [2–2 draw in the final](/wiki/2004_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final), with a late goal by [Adriano](/wiki/Adriano_(footballer,_born_February_1982)) forcing the shootout. That was their third win in four competitions since 1997. They also won the [FIFA Confederations Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) for the second time in [2005](/wiki/2005_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), with a [4–1 thumping](/wiki/2005_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final) of [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team). Manager [Carlos Alberto Parreira](/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Parreira) built his side through a 4–2–2–2 formation. Nicknamed the "Magic Quartet", the attack was built around four players: Ronaldo, [Adriano](/wiki/Adriano_(footballer,_born_February_1982)), [Kaká](/wiki/Kak%C3%A1) and Ronaldinho.

Brazil and Japan entering the [Signal Iduna Park](/wiki/Signal_Iduna_Park) field at the [2006 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)

In the [2006 World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) held in [Germany](/wiki/Germany), Brazil won their three group games against [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) (1–0), [Australia](/wiki/Australia_men%27s_national_soccer_team) (2–0) and [Japan](/wiki/Japan_national_football_team) (4–1). Ronaldo scored twice and equalled the record for the most goals scored across all World Cups. In the round of 16, Brazil beat [Ghana](/wiki/Ghana_national_football_team) 3–0. Ronaldo's goal was his 15th in World Cup history, breaking the record, which would later be broken by [Miroslav Klose](/wiki/Miroslav_Klose) in 2014. Brazil, however, were eliminated in the quarter-finals against [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team), losing 1–0 to a [Thierry Henry](/wiki/Thierry_Henry) goal.

[Dunga](/wiki/Dunga) was appointed as Brazil's new team manager in 2006. Brazil then won the [2007 Copa América](/wiki/2007_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Venezuela](/wiki/Venezuela), with a [3-0 win](/wiki/2007_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final) against [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) in the final. Two years later, Brazil won the [2009 FIFA Confederations Cup](/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), defeating the [United States](/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_soccer_team) 3–2 in [the final](/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_final), to seal their third [Confederations Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) title.

Brazil's [Kaká](/wiki/Kak%C3%A1) against Chile at the [2010 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup) in South Africa

At the [2010 World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup) in [South Africa](/wiki/South_Africa), Brazil won their first two matches against [North Korea](/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) (2–1) and the [Ivory Coast](/wiki/Ivory_Coast_national_football_team) (3–1), respectively. Their last match, against [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal_national_football_team), ended in a 0–0 draw. They faced [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) in the round of 16, winning 3–0, although in the quarter-final they fell to the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) 2–1.

In July 2010, [Mano Menezes](/wiki/Mano_Menezes) was named as Brazil's new coach. At the [2011 Copa América](/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina), Brazil lost against [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_national_football_team) on penalties and was eliminated in the quarter-finals. Brazil failed to score any penalty in the shootout. In November 2012, coach Mano Menezes was sacked and replaced by [Luiz Felipe Scolari](/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari).

Brazilian players celebrate winning the [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), where they won all five matches.

On 6 June 2013, Brazil was ranked 22nd in the [FIFA World Rankings](/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings), their lowest ever. At the [2013 Confederations Cup](/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), Brazil defended their title, beating Spain in the [final](/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_Final), winning 3–0 and sealing their fourth Confederations Cup title.

#### 2014 FIFA World Cup

Main article: [Brazil at the 2014 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/Brazil_at_the_2014_FIFA_World_Cup)

In the [opening match of the 2014 World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup#Group_A) on home soil against [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), two goals from [Neymar](/wiki/Neymar) and one from [Oscar](/wiki/Oscar_(footballer,_born_1991)) sealed a 3–1 comeback win after a [Marcelo](/wiki/Marcelo_(footballer,_born_1988)) own goal. The team then drew 0–0 with [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team), before confirming qualification to the [knockout stage](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage) by defeating [Cameroon](/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) 4–1. Brazil faced [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) in the [round of 16](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_round_of_16), needing penalties to prevail to the next round following a 1–1 draw.

Brazil line-up against Colombia at the [2014 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup). Neymar (front row, second from right) would play his last game at the tournament after being stretchered off with a fractured vertebra.

The team again faced South American opposition in the quarter-final, defeating [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia_national_football_team) 2–1. However, late in the match, Neymar was stretchered off after a horrible challenge by [Zuniga](/wiki/Juan_Camilo_Z%C3%BA%C3%B1iga), which led to the Brazilian suffering a [fractured](/wiki/Bone_fracture) [vertebra](/wiki/Vertebra_(anatomy)), ruling him out for the remainder of the tournament.

The *Seleção* went on to lose [7–1](/wiki/Brazil_v_Germany_(2014_FIFA_World_Cup)) to the [Germans](/wiki/Germany_national_football_team), their biggest ever defeat at the World Cup and first home loss in a competitive match since 1975. The match has been nicknamed the *Mineirazo*, making reference to the nation's previous World Cup defeat on home soil, the [Maracanazo](/wiki/Uruguay_v_Brazil_(1950_FIFA_World_Cup)) against Uruguay in 1950, and the [Mineirão stadium](/wiki/Mineir%C3%A3o) where the match took place. In that match, [Miroslav Klose](/wiki/Miroslav_Klose) scored, making him the top goalscorer at the FIFA World Cup with 16 goals. Curiously, Ronaldo Fenômeno was at the stadium as a commentator for Rede Globo.

Brazil subsequently lost 3–0 to the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in the third-place play-off match. Following the tournament, Scolari announced his resignation.

#### After the 2014 FIFA World Cup (2014–2022)

Brazil's 1994 World Cup winning captain, [Dunga](/wiki/Dunga), was coach from 2006 to 2010 and 2014 to 2016.

On 22 July 2014, Dunga was announced as the new manager of Brazil, returning to the position for his second stint leading the national team.

At the [2015 Copa América](/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in [Chile](/wiki/Chile), Brazil finished first in Group C to advance to the knockout stages. However, they were eliminated in the next round, again losing on penalties to [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_national_football_team).

At the 2016 [Copa América Centenario](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario) held in the [United States](/wiki/United_States), Brazil began the tournament with a goalless draw with [Ecuador](/wiki/Ecuador_national_football_team) before beating [Haiti](/wiki/Haiti_national_football_team) 7–1 in the next match. Needing only a draw to progress to the knockout stage of the tournament, Brazil suffered a controversial 1–0 loss to [Peru](/wiki/Peru_national_football_team), with [Raúl Ruidíaz](/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Ruid%C3%ADaz) scoring in the 75th minute. This defeat saw Brazil eliminated from the tournament in the group stage for the first time since [1987](/wiki/1987_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica)., and also resulted in Dunga being fired from the national team.

Brazil team prior to their group game against Costa Rica at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup) in Russia

Brazil supporters at the 2018 World Cup

On 14 June 2016, [Tite](/wiki/Tite_(football_manager)) replaced Dunga as manager of Brazil. At the [2018 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup), Brazil finished top of their group, tying with [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland_national_football_team) 1-1, defeating [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) 2-0 and [Serbia](/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team) 2–0. After defeating [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team) 2–0 in the round of 16, Brazil were eliminated in the quarter-finals by [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team), losing 2–1. Despite elimination from the tournament, Tite remained as head coach.

The [2019 Copa América](/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) was held on home soil, with the team beginning the campaign with a 3–0 win against [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia_national_football_team), where the Brazilians notoriously wore a white uniform. Then Brazil had a goalless draw with [Venezuela](/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team), and thumped [Peru](/wiki/Peru_national_football_team) 5–0. In the quarterfinals, Brazil defeated [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_national_football_team) on penalties after another goalless draw. After beating rivals [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) 2–0 in the semi-finals, Brazil beat Peru 3–1 in the final to win their ninth Copa América title, and their first since 2007. The team only conceded once during the tournament, during the final from a penalty kick. The next Copa América was originally scheduled to be played the following year, but due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), the competition was postponed to [2021](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). As a result, [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia) and [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina) resigned as co-hosts, and Brazil was selected to host the tournament. The hosts reached the [final](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final) once again, this time being defeated by [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) 1–0 in the [Maracanã Stadium](/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium); this was the first time Brazil failed to win the Copa América on home soil.

At the [2022 World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup), Brazil finished first in their group, with the team defeating [Serbia](/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team) 2–0, [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland_national_football_team) 1-0 and losing to [Cameroon](/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) 1–0. The team then faced [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea_national_football_team) in the round of 16, winning by 4–1, and progressed to the quarter-finals where they lost on penalties to [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) after a 1–1 draw. Following their exit from the World Cup, Tite resigned as head coach.

#### Recent years (2023–present)

After Tite left, [Ramon Menezes](/wiki/Ramon_Menezes) served as manager from March to July 2023, before being replaced by [Fernando Diniz](/wiki/Fernando_Diniz) until the end of the year. Diniz's tenure ended after Brazil lost to [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) 1–0 at the Maracanã, with this defeat being their first home loss in FIFA World Cup qualifiers.

The CBF then appointed [Dorival Júnior](/wiki/Dorival_J%C3%BAnior) as manager. At the [2024 Copa América](/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) held in the [United States](/wiki/United_States), Brazil tied 0–0 with [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team), thrashed [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_national_football_team) 4-1 and tied 1–1 with [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia_national_football_team). Brazil was eliminated on penalties by [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals following a 0–0 draw. Dorival was fired after losing 4–1 to Argentina at the [Monumental de Nuñez](/wiki/Estadio_Monumental_(Buenos_Aires)), and in his place the federation appointed Italian manager [Carlo Ancelotti](/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti) as a replacement.

Brazil qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup) after a 1–0 victory against [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_national_football_team) on 10 June 2025.

## Team image

### Uniforms

Brazil's first team colors were white with blue collars, but following the defeat at [Maracanã](/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_(stadium)) in the 1950 World Cup, the colors were criticized for lacking patriotism. With permission from the Brazilian Sports Confederation, the newspaper *[Correio da Manhã](/wiki/Correio_da_Manh%C3%A3_(Brazil))* held a competition to design a new kit incorporating the four colors of the Brazilian flag. The winning design was a yellow jersey with green trim and blue shorts with white trim drawn by [Aldyr Garcia Schlee](/wiki/Aldyr_Garcia_Schlee), a nineteen-year-old from [Pelotas](/wiki/Pelotas). The new colors were first used in March 1954 in a match against [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team), and have been used ever since. [Topper](/wiki/Topper_(sports)) were the manufacturers of Brazil's kit up to and including the match against [Wales](/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) on 11 September 1991; [Umbro](/wiki/Umbro) took over before the next match, versus [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) in October 1991. [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) began making the kits for Brazil in late 1996, in time for the [1997 Copa América](/wiki/1997_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and the [1998 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup).

The use of blue and white as the second kit colors owes its origins to the defunct latter-day Portuguese monarchy and dates from the 1930s, but it became the permanent second choice accidentally in the [1958 FIFA World Cup final](/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Brazil's opponents were [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team), who also wore yellow, and a draw gave the home team, Sweden, the right to play in yellow. Brazil, who traveled with no second kit, hurriedly purchased a set of blue shirts and sewed the badges taken from their yellow shirts on them.

### Nicknames

The Brazil national team is known by different names in various parts of the world. Nicknames for the squad in Brazil include:

- *Canarinho*, meaning 'Little [Canary](/wiki/Saffron_Finch)', a reference to a species of bird commonly found in Brazil that has a vivid yellow color, this phrase was popularized by the late cartoonist Fernando "Mangabeira" Pieruccetti during the [1950 World Cup](/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) despite the team not wearing the color yet back then
- *Amarelinha* (Little Yellow One)
- *Seleção* (The National Squad)
- *Verde-amarela* (The Green and Yellow)
- *Pentacampeão* (Five-time Champions)
- *Esquadrão de Ouro* (The Golden Squad)

Some Latin American commentators often refer to the Brazil team as *El Scratch* or *Scratch du Oro* (The Scratch), among others. In the [2022 World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup), [FIFA](/wiki/FIFA)'s [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) channel referred to the team as *Samba Boys*.

### Training camp

[Granja Comary complex](/wiki/Granja_Comary_(CBF)) is the training camp of the national team.

Brazil's training camp is the [Granja Comary](/wiki/Granja_Comary_(CBF)) in [Teresópolis](/wiki/Teres%C3%B3polis), located 90 km (56 mi) from Rio de Janeiro. Granja Comary was opened in 1987, and underwent significant renovations in 2013 and 2014.

### Manager records

[Mário Zagallo](/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Zagallo) became the first person to win the [FIFA World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup) both as a player ([1958](/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1962](/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup)) and as a manager ([1970](/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup)). In 1970, at the age of 38, he became the second-youngest coach to win the tournament. While still in Brazil as an assistant coach, the team won the [1994 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).

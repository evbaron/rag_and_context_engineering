**John Leonard Swigert Jr.** (August 30, 1931 – December 27, 1982) was an American [NASA astronaut](/wiki/NASA_astronaut), [test pilot](/wiki/Test_pilot), [mechanical engineer](/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), [aerospace engineer](/wiki/Aerospace_engineer), [United States Air Force](/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) pilot, and [politician](/wiki/Politician). In April 1970, as command module pilot of [Apollo 13](/wiki/Apollo_13), he became one of [24 Apollo astronauts who reached the Moon](/wiki/List_of_Apollo_astronauts). Due to the "slingshot" route around the Moon they chose to safely return to Earth, the Apollo 13 astronauts flew farther away from Earth than any other astronauts until the [Artemis II](/wiki/Artemis_II) lunar flyby in 2026, though they had to abort the Moon landing.

Before joining NASA in 1966, Swigert was a civilian [test pilot](/wiki/Test_pilot) and [fighter pilot](/wiki/Fighter_pilot) in the [Air National Guard](/wiki/Air_National_Guard). After leaving NASA, he ran to represent Colorado in the [U.S. Senate](/wiki/United_States_Senate) but lost in a primary election against [Bill Armstrong](/wiki/William_L._Armstrong). He subsequently was elected to represent [Colorado's 6th congressional district](/wiki/Colorado%27s_6th_congressional_district) in the [U.S. House of Representatives](/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) in 1982, but died of cancer before being sworn into office.

## Early life

John Leonard Swigert Jr. was born on August 30, 1931, in [Denver](/wiki/Denver), Colorado, to parents Dr. John Leonard Swigert Sr. and Virginia Anne Swigert ([née](/wiki/Birth_name) Seep). Swigert's father was an [ophthalmologist](/wiki/Ophthalmology). At the age of 14, he became fascinated by aviation. While he would have been content just watching planes take off from nearby Combs Field, young Jack became determined to do more than be a spectator. He took on a newspaper route to earn money for flying lessons, and by age 16 he was a [licensed private pilot](/wiki/Private_pilot_licence). He was a member of the [Boy Scouts of America](/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America) and attained the rank of [Second Class Scout](/wiki/Second_Class_(Boy_Scouts_of_America)). He attended Blessed Sacrament School, [Regis Jesuit High School](/wiki/Regis_Jesuit_High_School) and [East High School](/wiki/East_High_School_(Denver)), from which he graduated in 1949.

Swigert received a [Bachelor of Science](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in [mechanical engineering](/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) from [University of Colorado](/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) in 1953, where he also played [football](/wiki/American_football) for the [Buffaloes](/wiki/Colorado_Buffaloes_football). He later earned a [Master of Science](/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree in [aerospace engineering](/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) from [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute) (Hartford campus) in 1965, and a [Master of Business Administration](/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) degree from [University of Hartford](/wiki/University_of_Hartford) in 1967.

His recreational interests included [golf](/wiki/Golf), [handball](/wiki/American_handball), [bowling](/wiki/Bowling), [skiing](/wiki/Skiing), [swimming](/wiki/Human_swimming), and [basketball](/wiki/Basketball). His hobbies included [photography](/wiki/Photography).

## Flight experience

Swigert wearing his Air Force flight suit with a [118th Fighter Squadron](/wiki/118th_Fighter_Squadron), [Connecticut Air National Guard](/wiki/Connecticut_Air_National_Guard) patch

Following his graduation from Colorado in 1953, Swigert joined the [United States Air Force](/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) (USAF). Upon graduation from the Pilot Training Program and [Gunnery School](/wiki/USAF_Weapons_School#Origins) at [Nellis Air Force Base](/wiki/Nellis_Air_Force_Base), [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada), he was assigned as a [fighter pilot](/wiki/Fighter_pilot) in [Japan](/wiki/Japan) and [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea). In 1953, he survived his plane crashing into a radar unit on a Korean airstrip.

After completing his tour of active duty in the USAF, he served as a jet fighter pilot with the [Massachusetts](/wiki/Massachusetts_Air_National_Guard) (1957–1960) and [Connecticut Air National Guard](/wiki/Connecticut_Air_National_Guard) (1960–1965). Swigert held a position as engineering test pilot for [North American Aviation](/wiki/North_American_Aviation) before joining NASA. He was previously an engineering test pilot for [Pratt & Whitney](/wiki/Pratt_%26_Whitney), from February 1957 to June 1964.

He logged over 7,200 hours in flight, including more than 5,725 hours in [jet aircraft](/wiki/Jet_aircraft).

## NASA career

After unsuccessfully applying for NASA's [second](/wiki/Astronaut_Group_2) and [third](/wiki/Astronaut_Group_3) astronaut selections, Swigert was accepted into the [NASA Astronaut Corps](/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Corps) as part of [NASA Astronaut Group 5](/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_5) in April 1966. Swigert became a specialist on the [Apollo command module](/wiki/Apollo_command_module): he was one of the few astronauts who requested to be command module pilots.

Swigert was a member of [Apollo 7](/wiki/Apollo_7)'s astronaut support crew, the first support crew for an Apollo mission; he served as [capsule communicator](/wiki/Capsule_communicator) (CAPCOM) during the ascent phase of the flight.

### Apollo 13

Swigert (right) with the "mailbox" rig improvised to adapt the [Apollo 13](/wiki/Apollo_13) [command module](/wiki/Apollo_command_module) *Odyssey'*s square [carbon dioxide](/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) scrubber cartridges to fit the [Apollo Lunar Module](/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Module) *Aquarius*, which took a round cartridge

Main article: [Apollo 13](/wiki/Apollo_13)

Swigert was one of three astronauts aboard the [Apollo 13](/wiki/Apollo_13) Moon mission launched April 11, 1970. Originally part of the backup crew for the mission, he was assigned to the mission three days before launch, replacing astronaut [Ken Mattingly](/wiki/Ken_Mattingly). The prime crew had been exposed to [German measles](/wiki/German_measles) (the rubella virus) from [Charles Duke](/wiki/Charles_Duke) and, because Mattingly had no immunity to the disease, NASA did not want to risk him falling ill during critical phases of the flight.

Apollo 13 was the third crewed lunar-landing attempt, but was aborted after the rupture of an oxygen tank in the spacecraft's [service module](/wiki/Apollo_service_module). Swigert was the astronaut who first announced, "[Houston, we've had a problem here](/wiki/Houston,_we_have_a_problem)". The statement was then repeated by commander of the flight [Jim Lovell](/wiki/Jim_Lovell). Swigert, along with fellow astronauts Lovell and [Fred Haise](/wiki/Fred_Haise), traveled around the Moon and returned safely to Earth on April 17 after about 5 days and 23 hours, and received the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) the next day.

### Apollo–Soyuz Test Project

NASA Director of Flight Crew Operations [Deke Slayton](/wiki/Deke_Slayton), who selected the astronauts, recommended Swigert as [command module pilot](/wiki/Command_module_pilot) for the [Apollo–Soyuz Test Project](/wiki/Apollo%E2%80%93Soyuz_Test_Project), the first joint mission with the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union). Slayton felt Swigert deserved another chance to fly after having been selected for Apollo 13 two days before launch, and performing well.

During 1972, the [Apollo 15 postal covers incident](/wiki/Apollo_15_postal_covers_incident) caused NASA investigators to inquire into other astronauts. A number of Apollo astronauts, including Swigert, had made agreements with West German stamp dealer Hermann Sieger, who originated the idea for the [Apollo 15](/wiki/Apollo_15) covers, to autograph philatelic items in exchange for a payment of about $2,500. Swigert originally denied involvement when interviewed by NASA investigators. According to [Christopher C. Kraft](/wiki/Christopher_C._Kraft), the investigators subpoenaed his bank records, finding more funds than expected, and records of a predated charitable donation. Swigert's subsequent admission caused NASA Deputy Administrator [George M. Low](/wiki/George_M._Low) to remove him from Apollo–Soyuz.

## Post-NASA career

Aware that his spaceflight career was most likely over, Swigert took a leave of absence from NASA in April 1973 and went to [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) to become executive director of the [Committee on Science and Astronautics](/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Science,_Space_and_Technology), [U.S. House of Representatives](/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives).

Swigert eventually left NASA and the committee in August 1977 to enter politics. He ran for the [U.S. Senate](/wiki/United_States_Senate) from Colorado in 1978, but was soundly defeated in the Republican primary in September by [Congressman](/wiki/Colorado%27s_5th_congressional_district) [Bill Armstrong](/wiki/William_L._Armstrong), who was far better known. In 1979, Swigert became vice president of B.D.M. Corporation in [Golden](/wiki/Golden,_Colorado). He left in 1981 to join International Gold and Minerals Limited as vice president for financial and corporate affairs.

In February 1982, Swigert left International Gold and Minerals Limited to run for [U.S. Congress](/wiki/98th_United_States_Congress) in the newly created [6th district](/wiki/Colorado%27s_6th_congressional_district) as a Republican. When a [malignant tumor](/wiki/Malignant_tumor) in his right nasal passage was found, the astronaut-politician disclosed this to the voters. Doctors told him he would finish radiation treatments on June 15 and make a complete recovery. However, in August, Swigert developed back pain and was diagnosed with [bone marrow cancer](/wiki/Bone_marrow_cancer). On November 2, 1982, he won the seat with 64% of the vote.

## Death

On December 19, 1982, seven weeks after his election, he was airlifted from his home in [Littleton](/wiki/Littleton,_Colorado) to [Georgetown University Hospital](/wiki/MedStar_Georgetown_University_Hospital) in Washington, D.C. He died of [respiratory failure](/wiki/Respiratory_failure) at its [Lombardi Cancer Center](/wiki/Georgetown_Lombardi_Comprehensive_Cancer_Center) on December 27, seven days before the beginning of his congressional term, aged 51. He was the last member-elect of the House to die before taking office until [Luke Letlow](/wiki/Luke_Letlow)'s death from [COVID-19](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic) in December 2020. Following a special election [in March 1983](/wiki/1983_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections) Swigert was succeeded as representative by [Daniel Schaefer](/wiki/Daniel_Schaefer).

Fifteen astronauts, including fellow Apollo 13 crewmates [Jim Lovell](/wiki/Jim_Lovell) and [Fred Haise](/wiki/Fred_Haise), were among the thousand mourners at his [full military honors](/wiki/Military_funeral) funeral in Denver, presided over by [Archbishop](/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Denver) [James Casey](/wiki/James_Vincent_Casey), which included a [missing man flyover](/wiki/Missing_man_formation) by [A-7 Corsairs](/wiki/LTV_A-7_Corsair_II) of the [Colorado Air National Guard](/wiki/Colorado_Air_National_Guard). He is buried alongside his parents in [Mount Olivet Cemetery](/wiki/Mount_Olivet_Cemetery_(Wheat_Ridge,_Colorado)) in [Wheat Ridge](/wiki/Wheat_Ridge,_Colorado).

## Awards, honors, and organizations

Swigert during suit-up before the launch of [Apollo 13](/wiki/Apollo_13), April 11, 1970

Swigert received the [American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics](/wiki/American_Institute_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics) (AIAA) [Octave Chanute Award](/wiki/Octave_Chanute_Award) for 1966 for his participation in demonstrating the [Rogallo wing](/wiki/Rogallo_wing) as a feasible land landing system for returning space vehicles and astronauts.

President Richard Nixon awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) to the Apollo 13 crew shortly after the conclusion of their mission. Following a sparse parade, Swigert received the City of New York Gold Medal on June 3. He received the City of [Houston](/wiki/Houston) Medal for Valor, 1970. Swigert received the [American Astronautical Society](/wiki/American_Astronautical_Society) Flight Achievement Award for 1970. He was given University of Colorado-Boulder's Distinguished Engineering Alumnus Award in 1970. Vice President [Spiro Agnew](/wiki/Spiro_Agnew) presented the crews of Apollo 11, 12, and 13 with the [NASA Distinguished Service Medal](/wiki/NASA_Distinguished_Service_Medal) in 1970. The Apollo 13 crew also received the AIAA Haley Astronautics Award in 1971, which included a small monetary award and a medal.

Swigert was awarded the 1972 Antonian Gold Medal.

He was presented an [Honorary Doctorate of Science](/wiki/Honorary_degree) degree from [American International College](/wiki/American_International_College) in 1970, an Honorary Doctorate of Laws degree from [Western State University](/wiki/Western_State_University) in 1970, and an Honorary Doctorate of Science from [Western Michigan University](/wiki/Western_Michigan_University) in 1970.

In 1983, Swigert was among 14 Apollo astronauts inducted into the [International Space Hall of Fame](/wiki/New_Mexico_Museum_of_Space_History).

In 1988, Swigert was inducted into the [Colorado Aviation Hall of Fame](/wiki/Colorado_Aviation_Hall_of_Fame).

In 1995, Swigert was portrayed by [Kevin Bacon](/wiki/Kevin_Bacon) in [Ron Howard](/wiki/Ron_Howard)'s film *[Apollo 13](/wiki/Apollo_13_(film))*.

In 1997, Swigert, along with 23 other Apollo astronauts, was posthumously inducted into the [U.S. Astronaut Hall of Fame](/wiki/United_States_Astronaut_Hall_of_Fame) which is located at the Kennedy Space Center.

He was elected in September 2003 to the [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute) Alumni Hall of Fame.

In 1997, a [statue of Swigert](/wiki/Jack_Swigert_(Lundeen)) made by [George and Mark Lundeen](/wiki/George_Lundeen) was placed on display in the [U.S. Capitol Building](/wiki/U.S._Capitol_Building) as one of two statues given by the state of Colorado to the [National Statuary Hall Collection](/wiki/National_Statuary_Hall_Collection). As of December 2008 the statue is on display in Emancipation Hall in the [United States Capitol Visitor Center](/wiki/United_States_Capitol_Visitor_Center). A duplicate statue is currently on display at [Denver International Airport](/wiki/Denver_International_Airport) Terminal B, where passengers exit the airport's train system.

The Space Foundation was founded in 1983 in part to honor the memory and accomplishments of Swigert. In 2004, the Space Foundation launched the [John L. "Jack" Swigert Jr. Award for Space Exploration](/wiki/Space_Foundation#John_L._Jack,_Swigert,_Jr.,_Award_for_Space_Exploration), which is presented annually to an individual, group, or organization that has made a significant contribution to space exploration. On August 18, 2009, the [Space Foundation](/wiki/Space_Foundation) and Colorado Springs District 11 partnered to open the Jack Swigert Aerospace Academy.

Swigert was a member of numerous organizations. He was a [fellow](/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Astronautical Society](/wiki/American_Astronautical_Society); associate fellow of the [Society of Experimental Test Pilots](/wiki/Society_of_Experimental_Test_Pilots) and the [American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics](/wiki/American_Institute_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics); and member of the [Quiet Birdmen](/wiki/Quiet_Birdmen), [Phi Gamma Delta](/wiki/Phi_Gamma_Delta), [Pi Tau Sigma](/wiki/Pi_Tau_Sigma), and [Sigma Tau](/wiki/Sigma_Tau).

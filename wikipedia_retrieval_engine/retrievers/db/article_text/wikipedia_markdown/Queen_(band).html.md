**Queen** are a British [rock](/wiki/Rock_music) band formed in London in 1970 by [Freddie Mercury](/wiki/Freddie_Mercury) (lead vocals, piano), [Brian May](/wiki/Brian_May) (guitar, vocals), and [Roger Taylor](/wiki/Roger_Taylor_(Queen_drummer)) (drums, vocals), later joined by [John Deacon](/wiki/John_Deacon) (bass). Their earliest works were influenced by [progressive rock](/wiki/Progressive_rock), [hard rock](/wiki/Hard_rock) and [heavy metal](/wiki/Heavy_metal_music), but the band gradually ventured into more conventional and radio-friendly works by incorporating further styles, such as [arena rock](/wiki/Arena_rock) and [pop rock](/wiki/Pop_rock).

Before forming Queen, May and Taylor had played together in the band [Smile](/wiki/Smile_(band)). Mercury was a fan of Smile and encouraged them to experiment with more elaborate stage and recording techniques. He joined in 1970 and suggested the name "Queen". Deacon was recruited in February 1971, before the band released their [self-titled debut album](/wiki/Queen_(Queen_album)) in 1973. Queen first charted in the UK with their second album, *[Queen II](/wiki/Queen_II)*, in 1974. *[Sheer Heart Attack](/wiki/Sheer_Heart_Attack)* later that year and *[A Night at the Opera](/wiki/A_Night_at_the_Opera_(Queen_album))* in 1975 brought them international success. The latter featured "[Bohemian Rhapsody](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody)", which topped the [UK singles chart](/wiki/UK_singles_chart) for nine weeks and helped popularise the music video format. The band's 1977 album *[News of the World](/wiki/News_of_the_World_(album))* contained "[We Will Rock You](/wiki/We_Will_Rock_You)" and "[We Are the Champions](/wiki/We_Are_the_Champions)", which have become [anthems](/wiki/Stadium_anthem) at sporting events. By the early 1980s, Queen were one of the biggest stadium rock bands in the world. "[Another One Bites the Dust](/wiki/Another_One_Bites_the_Dust)" from *[The Game](/wiki/The_Game_(Queen_album))* (1980) became their best-selling single, and their 1981 compilation album *[Greatest Hits](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Queen_album))* is the [best-selling album in the UK](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_in_the_United_Kingdom) and has been certified [9× Platinum](/wiki/RIAA_certification) in the US by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA). Their performance at the 1985 [Live Aid](/wiki/Live_Aid) concert is ranked among the greatest in rock history by various publications. In August 1986, Mercury gave his last performance with Queen at [Knebworth](/wiki/Knebworth_Festival), England.

Mercury was diagnosed with [AIDS](/wiki/HIV/AIDS) in 1987. The band released two more albums, *[The Miracle](/wiki/The_Miracle_(album))* in 1989 and *[Innuendo](/wiki/Innuendo_(album))* in 1991. On 23 November 1991, Mercury publicly revealed his AIDS diagnosis, and the next day died of [bronchopneumonia](/wiki/Bronchopneumonia), a complication of AIDS. One more album was released featuring Mercury's vocals, 1995's *[Made in Heaven](/wiki/Made_in_Heaven)*. Deacon retired in 1997, while May and Taylor continued to make sporadic appearances together. Since 2004, they have toured as "Queen +", with vocalists [Paul Rodgers](/wiki/Paul_Rodgers) until 2009 and [Adam Lambert](/wiki/Adam_Lambert) since 2011.

Queen have been a global presence in popular culture for more than half a century. Estimates of their record sales range from 250 million to 300 million, making them one of the world's [best-selling music artists](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists). In 1990, Queen received the [Brit Award](/wiki/Brit_Award) for [Outstanding Contribution to British Music](/wiki/Brit_Award_for_Outstanding_Contribution_to_Music). They were inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame) in 2001, and with each member having composed hit singles, all four were inducted into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame) in 2003. In 2005, they received the [Ivor Novello Award](/wiki/Ivor_Novello_Award) for Outstanding Song Collection from the [British Academy of Songwriters, Composers, and Authors](/wiki/British_Academy_of_Songwriters,_Composers,_and_Authors). In 2018, they were presented the [Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award](/wiki/Grammy_Lifetime_Achievement_Award), and they were awarded the [Polar Music Prize](/wiki/Polar_Music_Prize) in 2025.

## History

### 1968–1971: Foundations

The founding members of Queen met in west London during the late 1960s. Guitarist [Brian May](/wiki/Brian_May) had built [his own guitar](/wiki/Red_Special) with his father in 1963, and formed the group 1984 (named after [Orwell's novel](/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four)) the following year with singer [Tim Staffell](/wiki/Tim_Staffell). May left the group in early 1968 to focus on his degree in Physics and Infrared Astronomy at [Imperial College](/wiki/Imperial_College_London) and find a group that could write original material. He formed the group [Smile](/wiki/Smile_(band)) with Staffell (now playing bass) and keyboardist Chris Smith. To complete the line-up, May placed an advertisement on a college notice board for a "[Mitch Mitchell](/wiki/Mitch_Mitchell)/[Ginger Baker](/wiki/Ginger_Baker) type" drummer; [Roger Taylor](/wiki/Roger_Taylor_(Queen_drummer)), a young dental student, auditioned and got the job. Smith left the group in early 1969, immediately before a gig at the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) with [Free](/wiki/Free_(band)) and the [Bonzo Dog Doo-Dah Band](/wiki/Bonzo_Dog_Doo-Dah_Band).

Queen in 1970. Left to right; Mike Grose (who was the first of the band's three early bass players before John Deacon joined in 1971), Roger Taylor, Freddie Mercury and Brian May

While attending [Ealing Art College](/wiki/Ealing_Art_College) in west London, Staffell became friends with fellow student [Farrokh "Freddie" Bulsara](/wiki/Freddie_Mercury), who was from [Zanzibar](/wiki/Zanzibar) and of Indian [Parsi](/wiki/Parsi) descent. Bulsara had studied fashion design for a year before switching to graphic art and design, and soon became a keen fan of Smile. He asked if he could join the group as lead singer, but May felt Staffell would not give up that role. He also ran a stall in [Kensington Market](/wiki/Kensington_Market,_London) with Taylor.

[PRS for Music](/wiki/PRS_for_Music) heritage award at [Prince Consort Road](/wiki/Prince_Consort_Road) commemorating Queen's first performance in London

In 1970, Staffell quit Smile, feeling his interests in [soul](/wiki/Soul_music) and [R&B](/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues) clashed with the group's hard rock sound and being fed up with the lack of success. He formed the group [Humpy Bong](/wiki/Humpy_Bong) with former [Bee Gees](/wiki/Bee_Gees) drummer [Colin Petersen](/wiki/Colin_Petersen). The remaining members accepted Bulsara as lead singer, and recruited Taylor's friend [Mike Grose](/wiki/Mike_Grose) as bassist. The four played their first gig at a fundraising event in [Truro](/wiki/Truro) on 27 June 1970. Bulsara suggested the group should be renamed to "Queen". The others were uncertain at first, but he said, "it's wonderful, dear, people will love it". At the same time, he decided to [change his surname](/wiki/Name_change#United_Kingdom) to Mercury, inspired by the line "Mother Mercury, look what they've done to me" in the song "[My Fairy King](/wiki/Queen_(Queen_album)#"My_Fairy_King")". The group played their first London gig on 18 July. The early set consisted of material that later appeared on the first two albums, along with various rock and roll covers, such as [Cliff Richard](/wiki/Cliff_Richard) and [the Shadows](/wiki/The_Shadows)' "[Please Don't Tease](/wiki/Please_Don%27t_Tease)". They attracted the attention of producer [John Anthony](/wiki/John_Anthony_(record_producer)), who was interested in the group's sound but thought they had the wrong bass player. After three live gigs, Mike Grose decided not to continue with the band and was replaced by [Barry Mitchell](/wiki/Barry_Mitchell_(musician)) (ex [Crushed Butler](/wiki/Crushed_Butler)). Mitchell played thirteen gigs with Queen between August 1970 and January 1971. In turn, Mitchell left in January 1971 and was replaced by [Doug Bogie](/wiki/Doug_Bogie) for two live gigs.

### 1971–1974: *Queen* and *Queen II*

In February 1971, [John Deacon](/wiki/John_Deacon) joined Queen. In addition to being an experienced bassist, his quiet demeanour complemented the band, and he was skilled in electronics. On 2 July, Queen played their first show with the classic line-up of Mercury, May, Taylor and Deacon at a Surrey college outside London. May called Terry Yeadon, an engineer at Pye Studios where Smile had recorded, to see if he knew anywhere where Queen could record. Yeadon had since moved to [De Lane Lea Studios](/wiki/De_Lane_Lea_Studios)' new premises in [Wembley](/wiki/Wembley), and they needed a group to test out the equipment and recording rooms. He tried asking [the Kinks](/wiki/The_Kinks) but couldn't get hold of them. Therefore, he told Queen they could record some demos in exchange for the studio's acoustic tests. They recorded five of their own songs, "[Liar](/wiki/Liar_(Queen_song))", "[Keep Yourself Alive](/wiki/Keep_Yourself_Alive)", "[Great King Rat](/wiki/Queen_(Queen_album)#"Great_King_Rat")", "[The Night Comes Down](/wiki/Queen_(Queen_album)#"The_Night_Comes_Down")" and "[Jesus](/wiki/Queen_(Queen_album)#"Jesus")". During the recording, producers John Anthony and [Roy Thomas Baker](/wiki/Roy_Thomas_Baker) visited the band. The two were taken with "Keep Yourself Alive" and began promoting the band to several record companies.

Queen guitar (right, next to a Rolling Stones guitar) at the [Cavern Club](/wiki/The_Cavern_Club) in Liverpool, marking a 31 October 1970 Queen concert at the venue

Promoter Ken Testi managed to attract the interest of [Charisma Records](/wiki/Charisma_Records), who offered Queen an advance of around £25,000, but the group turned them down as they realised the label would promote [Genesis](/wiki/Genesis_(band)) as a priority. Testi then entered discussions with [Trident Studios](/wiki/Trident_Studios)' [Norman Sheffield](/wiki/Norman_Sheffield), who offered the band a management deal under Neptune Productions, a subsidiary of Trident, to manage the band and enable them to use their facilities, while the management searched for a deal. This suited both parties, as Trident were expanding into management, and under the deal, Queen were able to make use of the hi-tech recording facilities used by signed musicians. Taylor later described these early off-peak studio hours as "gold dust".

Queen began 1972 with a gig at [Bedford College](/wiki/Bedford_College,_London), London, where only six people turned up. After a few more shows, they stopped live performances for eight months to work on the album with Anthony and Baker. During the sessions at Trident, they saw [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie) with [the Spiders from Mars](/wiki/The_Spiders_from_Mars) live and realised they needed to make an impact with the album, otherwise they would be left behind. Co-producers Anthony and Baker initially clashed with the band (May in particular) on the direction of the album, bringing the band's inexperience in the studio to bear. The band's fighting centered around their efforts to integrate technical perfection with the reality of live performances, leading to what Baker referred to as "kitchen sink overproduction". The resulting album was a mix of [heavy metal](/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) and [progressive rock](/wiki/Progressive_rock). The group were unhappy with the re-recording of "The Night Comes Down", so the finished album uses the De Lane Lea demo. Another track, "[Mad the Swine](/wiki/Headlong_(song)#B-sides)" was dropped from the running order after the band and Baker could not agree on a mix. [Mike Stone](/wiki/Mike_Stone_(record_producer)) created the final mix for "Keep Yourself Alive", and he went on to work on several other Queen albums. By January 1972, the band finished recording their debut album, but had yet to secure a record contract. In order to attract record company interest, Trident booked a "showcase" gig on 6 November at [The Pheasantry](/wiki/The_Pheasantry), followed by a show at the [Marquee Club](/wiki/Marquee_Club) on 20 December.

Queen promoted the unreleased album in February 1973 on [BBC Radio 1](/wiki/BBC_Radio_1), still unsigned. The following month, Trident managed to strike a deal with [EMI Records](/wiki/EMI_Records). "Keep Yourself Alive" was released as a single on 6 July, with the album *[Queen](/wiki/Queen_(Queen_album))* appearing a week later. The front cover showed a shot of Mercury live on stage taken by Taylor's friend Douglass Puddifoot. Deacon was credited as "Deacon John" while Taylor used his full name, Roger Meddows-Taylor. The album was received well by critics; Gordon Fletcher of *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* called it "superb", and Chicago's *[Daily Herald](/wiki/Daily_Herald_(Arlington_Heights))* called it an "above-average debut". However, it drew little mainstream attention, and "Keep Yourself Alive" sold poorly. Retrospectively, it is cited as the album's highlight, and in 2008 *Rolling Stone* ranked it 31st in the "100 Greatest Guitar Songs of All Time", describing it as "an entire album's worth of riffs crammed into a single song". The album was certified gold in the UK and the US.

The group began to record their second album, *[Queen II](/wiki/Queen_II)* in August 1973. Now able to use regular studio time, they decided to make full use of the facilities available. May created a multi-layer guitar introduction "Procession", while Mercury wrote "The Fairy Feller's Master Stroke" based on [the painting of the same name](/wiki/The_Fairy_Feller%27s_Master-Stroke) by [Richard Dadd](/wiki/Richard_Dadd). The group spent the remainder of the year touring the UK, supporting [Mott the Hoople](/wiki/Mott_The_Hoople), and began to attract an audience. The tour ended with two shows at the [Hammersmith Odeon](/wiki/Hammersmith_Odeon) on 14 December, playing to 7,000 people.

In January 1974, Queen played the [Sunbury Pop Festival](/wiki/Sunbury_Pop_Festival) in Australia. They arrived late, and were jeered and taunted by the audience who expected to see home grown acts. Before leaving, Mercury announced, "when we come back to Australia, Queen will be the biggest band in the world!" *Queen II* was released in March and features [Mick Rock](/wiki/Mick_Rock)'s iconic [Dietrich](/wiki/Marlene_Dietrich)-inspired image of the band on the cover. This image would later be used as the basis for "Bohemian Rhapsody" music video production. The album reached number five on the [British album chart](/wiki/UK_Album_Chart) and became the first Queen album to chart in the UK. The Mercury-written lead single "[Seven Seas of Rhye](/wiki/Seven_Seas_of_Rhye)" reached number 10 in the UK, giving the band their first hit. The album featured a "layered" sound which would become their signature and features long complex instrumental passages, fantasy-themed lyrics and instrumental virtuosity. Aside from its only single, the album also included the song "[The March of the Black Queen](/wiki/The_March_of_the_Black_Queen)", a six-minute epic which lacks a chorus. Critical reaction was mixed; the *[Winnipeg Free Press](/wiki/Winnipeg_Free_Press)*, while praising the band's debut album, described *Queen II* as an "over-produced monstrosity". [AllMusic](/wiki/AllMusic) has described the album as a favourite among the band's hardcore fans, and it is the first of three Queen albums to feature in the book *[1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die](/wiki/1001_Albums_You_Must_Hear_Before_You_Die)*. The group ended their early 1974 UK tour with a show at the [Rainbow Theatre](/wiki/Rainbow_Theatre) on 31 March. Mercury chose to a wear a [Zandra Rhodes](/wiki/Zandra_Rhodes)-designed tunic for the gig, changing into a slashed black top midway through the show.

### 1974–1976: *Sheer Heart Attack* to *A Night at the Opera*

In May 1974, a month into the band's first US tour opening for Mott the Hoople, May collapsed and was diagnosed with hepatitis, forcing the cancellation of their remaining dates. May was still absent when the band started work on their third album, but he returned midway through the recording process. Released in 1974, *[Sheer Heart Attack](/wiki/Sheer_Heart_Attack)* reached number two in the UK, sold well throughout Europe, and went gold in the US. It gave the band their first real experience of international success, and was a hit on both sides of the Atlantic. The album experimented with a variety of musical genres, including [British music hall](/wiki/Music_hall), [heavy metal](/wiki/Heavy_metal_music), ballads, ragtime, and [Caribbean](/wiki/List_of_Caribbean_music_genres). May's "[Now I'm Here](/wiki/Now_I%27m_Here)" documented the group's curtailed American tour, and "Brighton Rock" served as a vehicle for his regular on-stage solo guitar spot. Deacon wrote his first song for the group, "Misfire", while the live favourite "[Stone Cold Crazy](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Crazy)" was credited to the whole band. Mercury wrote the closing number, "In the Lap of the Gods… Revisited", with the intention that the audience could sing along to the chorus when played live. This would be repeated later on, more successfully, in songs such as "We Are the Champions".

Queen performed "[Killer Queen](/wiki/Killer_Queen)" at [BBC Television Centre](/wiki/BBC_Television_Centre) (pictured) on the music chart show *[Top of the Pops](/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops)* in December 1974. This was their second appearance on the show; their previous (performing "[Seven Seas of Rhye](/wiki/Seven_Seas_of_Rhye)") brought them overnight fame.

The single "[Killer Queen](/wiki/Killer_Queen)" was written by Mercury about a high-class prostitute. It reached number two on the British charts and became their first US hit, reaching number 12 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100). The song was partly recorded at [Rockfield Studios](/wiki/Rockfield_Studios) in Wales. With Mercury playing the grand piano, it combines camp, [vaudeville](/wiki/Vaudeville), and British music hall with May's guitar. "Now I'm Here" was released as the second single and reached number eleven. In 2006, *[Classic Rock](/wiki/Classic_Rock_(magazine))* ranked *Sheer Heart Attack* number 28 in "The 100 Greatest British Rock Albums Ever", and in 2007, *[Mojo](/wiki/Mojo_(magazine))* ranked it No.88 in "The 100 Records That Changed the World". It is also the second of three Queen albums to feature in the book *1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die*.

In January 1975, Queen left for a world tour with an upgraded light show. They toured the US as headliners and played in Canada for the first time. Several dates were cancelled after Mercury contracted [laryngitis](/wiki/Laryngitis). The band then toured Japan from mid-April to the beginning of May. They were greeted by thousands of screaming fans and played eight times in seven cities. Despite the success, Queen were still tied to the original Trident deal and wages. They were all living in relative poverty in bedsits, and Deacon was refused money for a deposit on a house. EMI contacted lawyer [Jim Beach](/wiki/Jim_Beach), who tried to find a way of extracting them from their contract. Trident complained that they had invested £200,000 in Queen and wanted their money back first. In August, after an acrimonious split with Trident, the band negotiated themselves out of their contract and searched for new management. One of the options they considered was an offer from [Led Zeppelin](/wiki/Led_Zeppelin)'s manager, [Peter Grant](/wiki/Peter_Grant_(music_manager)), who wanted them to sign with Led Zeppelin's own production company, [Swan Song Records](/wiki/Swan_Song_Records). The band were concerned about being a lower priority than Zeppelin and [Bad Company](/wiki/Bad_Company) (also signed to Swan Song) and instead contacted [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John)'s manager, [John Reid](/wiki/John_Reid_(music_manager)), who accepted the position. Reid's first instruction to the band was "I'll take care of the business; you make the best record you can".

Queen started work on their fourth album *[A Night at the Opera](/wiki/A_Night_at_the_Opera_(Queen_album))*, taking its name from the popular [Marx Brothers](/wiki/Marx_Brothers) movie. At the time, it was the most expensive album ever produced, costing £40,000 and using three different studios. Like its predecessor, the album features diverse musical styles and experimentation with stereo sound. Mercury wrote the opening song "[Death on Two Legs](/wiki/Death_on_Two_Legs_(Dedicated_to...))", a savage dig at perceived wrongdoers (and later dedicated to Trident in concert) and the camp vaudeville "Lazing on a Sunday Afternoon" and "Seaside Rendezvous". May's "[The Prophet's Song](/wiki/The_Prophet%27s_Song)" was an eight-minute epic; the middle section is a [canon](/wiki/Canon_(music)), with simple phrases layered to create a full-choral sound. The Mercury penned ballad, "[Love of My Life](/wiki/Love_of_My_Life_(Queen_song))", featured a harp and overdubbed vocal harmonies.

> He knew exactly what he was doing. It was Freddie's baby. We just helped him bring it to life. We realized we'd look odd trying to mime such a hugely complex thing on TV. It had to be presented in some other way.

— Brian May on Mercury writing "[Bohemian Rhapsody](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody)" and the groundbreaking music video.

The best-known song on the album, "[Bohemian Rhapsody](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody)", originated from pieces of music that Mercury had written at Ealing College. Mercury played a run-through of the track on piano in his flat to Baker, stopping suddenly to announce, "This is where the opera section comes in". When the rest of the band started recording the song, they were unsure as to how it would be pieced together. After recording the backing track, Baker left a 30-second section of tape to add the operatic vocals. Reportedly, 180 overdubs were used, to the extent that the original tape wore thin. EMI initially refused to release the single, thinking it too long, and demanded a radio edit which Queen refused. Mercury's close friend and advisor, [Capital London](/wiki/Capital_London) radio DJ [Kenny Everett](/wiki/Kenny_Everett), played a pivotal role in giving the single exposure. He was given a promotional copy on the condition he didn't play it, but ended up doing so fourteen times over a single weekend. Capital's switchboard was overwhelmed with callers inquiring when the song would be released. With EMI forced to release "Bohemian Rhapsody" due to public demand, the single reached number one in the UK for nine weeks. It is the [third-best-selling single](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_singles_in_the_United_Kingdom) of all time in the UK, surpassed only by [Band Aid](/wiki/Band_Aid_(band))'s "[Do They Know It's Christmas?](/wiki/Do_They_Know_It%27s_Christmas%3F)" and [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John)'s "[Candle in the Wind 1997](/wiki/Candle_in_the_Wind_1997)", and is the best-selling commercial single (i.e. *not* for charity) in the UK. It also reached number nine in the US (a 1992 re-release reached number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100 for five weeks). It is the only single ever to sell a million copies on two separate occasions and became the [Christmas number one](/wiki/List_of_Christmas_number_one_singles_(UK)) twice in the UK, the only single ever to do so. It has also been voted the greatest song of all time in three different polls.

"Bohemian Rhapsody" was promoted with a music video directed by [Bruce Gowers](/wiki/Bruce_Gowers), who had already shot several of Queen's live concerts. The group wanted a video so they could avoid appearing on the BBC's *[Top of the Pops](/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops)*, which would clash with tour dates, and it would have looked strange miming to such a complex song. Filmed at [Elstree Studios](/wiki/Elstree_Studios) in Hertfordshire, the video cost £3,500, five times the typical promotional budget, and was shot in three hours. The operatic section featured a reprise of the *Queen II* cover, with the band member's heads animated. On the impact of the "Bohemian Rhapsody" promotional video, *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* states: "Its influence cannot be overstated, practically inventing the music video seven years before MTV went on the air." Ranking it number 31 on their list of the 50 key events in rock music history, *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* stated it "ensured videos would henceforth be a mandatory tool in the marketing of music". Radio broadcaster [Tommy Vance](/wiki/Tommy_Vance) states, "It became the first record to be pushed into the forefront by virtue of a video. Queen were certainly the first band to create a 'concept' video. The video captured the musical imagery perfectly. You cannot hear that music without seeing the visuals in your mind's eye."

*A Night at the Opera* was very successful in the UK  and went [triple platinum](/wiki/Music_recording_sales_certification) in the United States. The British public voted it the 13th-greatest album of all time in a 2004 [Channel 4](/wiki/Channel_4) poll. It has also ranked highly in international polls; in a worldwide [Guinness](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) poll, it was voted the 19th-greatest of all time, and an [ABC](/wiki/Australian_Broadcasting_Corporation) poll saw the Australian public vote it the 28th-greatest of all time. *A Night at the Opera* has frequently appeared in "greatest albums" lists reflecting the opinions of critics. Among other accolades, it was ranked number 16 in *[Q](/wiki/Q_(magazine))* magazine's "The 50 Best British Albums Ever" in 2004, a poll done in collaboration with British music and entertainment retailer [HMV](/wiki/HMV). It was also placed at number 230 on *Rolling Stone* magazine's list of "[The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Albums_of_All_Time)" in 2003. *A Night at the Opera* is the third and final Queen album to be featured in the book *1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die*. The second single from the album was Deacon's "[You're My Best Friend](/wiki/You%27re_My_Best_Friend_(Queen_song))", which peaked at number sixteen on the US *Billboard* Hot 100, and went on to become a worldwide top-ten hit. The band's [A Night at the Opera Tour](/wiki/A_Night_at_the_Opera_Tour) began in November 1975, and covered Europe, the US, Japan, and Australia. On 24 December, Queen played a special concert at the Hammersmith Odeon which was broadcast live on the BBC show *[The Old Grey Whistle Test](/wiki/The_Old_Grey_Whistle_Test)*, with the audio being later broadcast on BBC Radio 1. It became one of the band's most popular [bootleg recordings](/wiki/Bootleg_recording) for decades before being [officially released](/wiki/A_Night_at_the_Odeon_%E2%80%93_Hammersmith_1975) in 2015.

### 1976–1979: *A Day at the Races* to *Live Killers*

By 1976, Queen were back in the studio recording *[A Day at the Races](/wiki/A_Day_at_the_Races_(album))*, which is often regarded as a sequel album to *A Night at the Opera*. It again borrowed the name of a Marx Brothers movie, and its cover was similar to that of *A Night at the Opera*, a variation on the same Queen logo. The most recognisable of the Marx Brothers, [Groucho Marx](/wiki/Groucho_Marx), invited Queen to visit him in his Los Angeles home in March 1977; there the band thanked him in person, and performed "['39](/wiki/%2739)" *[a cappella](/wiki/A_cappella)*. Baker did not return to produce the album; instead the band self-produced with assistance from Mike Stone, who performed several of the backing vocals. The major hit on the album was "[Somebody to Love](/wiki/Somebody_to_Love_(Queen_song))", a gospel-inspired song in which Mercury, May, and Taylor multi-tracked their voices to create a gospel choir. The song went to number two in the UK, and number thirteen in the US. The album also featured one of the band's heaviest songs, May's "[Tie Your Mother Down](/wiki/Tie_Your_Mother_Down)", which became a staple of their live shows. Musically, *A Day at the Races* was by both fans' and critics' standards a strong effort, reaching number one in the UK and Japan, and number five in the US.

Queen played a [landmark gig](/wiki/Queen_Hyde_Park_1976) on 18 September 1976, a free concert in [Hyde Park](/wiki/Hyde_Park,_London), London, organised by the entrepreneur [Richard Branson](/wiki/Richard_Branson). It set an attendance record at the park, with 150,000 people confirmed in the audience. Queen were late arriving onstage and ran out of time to play an encore; the police informed Mercury that he would be arrested if he attempted to go on stage again. May enjoyed the gig particularly, as he had been to see previous concerts at the park, such as the first one organised by [Blackhill Enterprises](/wiki/Blackhill_Enterprises) in 1968, featuring [Pink Floyd](/wiki/Pink_Floyd).

Queen press photo in early 1977 in promotion of *News of the World*

On 1 December 1976, Queen were the intended guests on London's early evening *Today* programme, but they pulled out at the last minute, which caused their late replacement on the show, EMI labelmate the [Sex Pistols](/wiki/Sex_Pistols), to give their infamous expletive-strewn interview with [Bill Grundy](/wiki/Bill_Grundy). During the [A Day at the Races Tour](/wiki/A_Day_at_the_Races_Tour) in 1977, Queen performed sold-out shows at [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden), New York, in February, supported by [Thin Lizzy](/wiki/Thin_Lizzy), and Mercury and Taylor socialised with that group's leader [Phil Lynott](/wiki/Phil_Lynott). They ended the tour with two concerts at [Earls Court](/wiki/Earls_Court_Exhibition_Centre), London, in June, which commemorated the [Silver Jubilee of Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Silver_Jubilee_of_Queen_Elizabeth_II), and at the cost of £50,000 the band used a lighting rig in the shape of a crown for the first time.

The band's sixth studio album *[News of the World](/wiki/News_of_the_World_(album))* was released in 1977, which has gone [four times platinum](/wiki/Platinum_album) in the US, and twice in the UK. The album contained many songs tailor-made for live performance, including two of rock's most recognisable anthems, "[We Will Rock You](/wiki/We_Will_Rock_You)" and the rock ballad "[We Are the Champions](/wiki/We_Are_the_Champions)", both of which became enduring international [sports anthems](/wiki/Stadium_anthem), and the latter reached number four in the US. Queen commenced the [News of the World Tour](/wiki/News_of_the_World_Tour) in November 1977, and [Robert Hilburn](/wiki/Robert_Hilburn) of the *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* called this concert tour the band's "most spectacularly staged and finely honed show". During the tour they sold out another two shows at MSG, and in 1978 they received the [Madison Square Garden Gold Ticket Award](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Gold_Ticket_Award) for passing more than 100,000 unit ticket sales at the venue.

Queen in [New Haven](/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut), Connecticut in November 1977

In 1978, Queen released *[Jazz](/wiki/Jazz_(Queen_album))*, which reached number two in the UK and number six on the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) in the US. The album included the hit singles "[Fat Bottomed Girls](/wiki/Fat_Bottomed_Girls)" and "[Bicycle Race](/wiki/Bicycle_Race)" on a double-sided record. Critical reviews of the album in the years since its release have been more favourable than initial reviews. Another notable track from *Jazz*, "[Don't Stop Me Now](/wiki/Don%27t_Stop_Me_Now)", provides another example of the band's exuberant vocal harmonies.

In 1978, Queen [toured](/wiki/Jazz_Tour) the US and Canada, and spent much of 1979 touring in Europe and Japan. They released their first live album, *[Live Killers](/wiki/Live_Killers)*, in 1979; it went platinum twice in the US. Queen also released the very successful single "[Crazy Little Thing Called Love](/wiki/Crazy_Little_Thing_Called_Love)", a [rockabilly](/wiki/Rockabilly) inspired song done in the style of [Elvis Presley](/wiki/Elvis_Presley). The song made the top 10 in many countries, topped the Australian [ARIA Charts](/wiki/ARIA_Charts) for seven consecutive weeks, and was the band's first number one single in the US where it topped the *Billboard* Hot 100 for four weeks. Having written the song on guitar and played rhythm on the record, Mercury played rhythm guitar when performing the song live, the first time he ever played guitar in concert. On 26 December 1979, Queen played the opening night at the [Concert for the People of Kampuchea](/wiki/Concert_for_the_People_of_Kampuchea) in London, having accepted a request by the event's organiser, [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney). The concert was the last date of their [Crazy Tour](/wiki/Crazy_Tour) of London.

### 1980–1982: *The Game*, *Hot Space* and stadium tours

Queen began their 1980s career with *[The Game](/wiki/The_Game_(Queen_album))*. It featured the singles "Crazy Little Thing Called Love" and "[Another One Bites the Dust](/wiki/Another_One_Bites_the_Dust)", both of which reached number one in the US. After attending a Queen concert in Los Angeles, [Michael Jackson](/wiki/Michael_Jackson) suggested to Mercury backstage that "Another One Bites the Dust" be released as a single, and in October 1980 it spent three weeks at number one. The album topped the *Billboard* 200 for five weeks, and sold over four million copies in the US. It was also the first appearance of a synthesiser on a Queen album. Heretofore, their albums featured a distinctive "No Synthesisers!" sleeve note. The note is widely assumed to reflect an anti-synth, pro-"hard"-rock stance by the band, but was later revealed by producer [Roy Thomas Baker](/wiki/Roy_Thomas_Baker) to be an attempt to clarify that those albums' multi-layered solos were created with guitars, not synths, as record company executives kept assuming at the time. In September 1980, Queen performed three sold-out shows at Madison Square Garden. In 1980, Queen also released [the soundtrack](/wiki/Flash_Gordon_(album)) they had recorded for *[Flash Gordon](/wiki/Flash_Gordon_(film))*. At the [1981 American Music Awards](/wiki/American_Music_Awards_of_1981) in January, "Another One Bites the Dust" won the award for Favorite Pop/Rock Single, and Queen were nominated for Favorite Pop/Rock Band, Duo, or Group.

In February 1981, Queen travelled to South America as part of [The Game Tour](/wiki/The_Game_Tour), and became the first rock band outside [the Americas](/wiki/The_Americas) to play stadiums in Latin America. On playing the concerts, *[Classic Rock](/wiki/Classic_Rock_(magazine))* magazine states, "They were under the spotlight from all quarters, as the entire music industry waited to see if their ambitious plans would bear fruit". Tom Pinnock in the March 1981 issue of *[Melody Maker](/wiki/Melody_Maker)* wrote,

> Queen chalked up a major international "first" by becoming the band to do for popular music in South America what The Beatles did for North America 17 years ago. Half a million Argentinians and Brazilians, starved of appearances of top British or American bands at their peak, gave Queen a heroic welcome which changed the course of pop history in this uncharted territory of the world rock map. The ecstatic young people saw eight Queen concerts at giant stadia, while many more millions saw the shows on TV and heard the radio broadcasts live.

Queen played to over 250,000 over two concerts at the [Morumbi Stadium](/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Morumbi) in São Paulo, Brazil during the South American part of The Game Tour.

The tour included five shows in Argentina, one of which drew the largest single concert crowd in Argentine history with an audience of 300,000 in [Buenos Aires](/wiki/Buenos_Aires) and two concerts at the [Morumbi Stadium](/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Morumbi) in São Paulo, Brazil, where they played to more than 131,000 people in the first night (then the largest paying audience for a single band anywhere in the world) and more than 120,000 people the following night. A region then largely ruled by military dictatorships, the band were greeted with scenes of fan-fever, and the promoter of their first shows at the [Vélez Sarsfield Stadium](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Amalfitani_Stadium) in Buenos Aires was moved to say: "For music in Argentina, this has been a case of before the war and after the war. Queen have liberated this country, musically speaking." The group's second show at Vélez Sarsfield was broadcast on national television and watched by over 30 million. Backstage, they were introduced to footballer [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona).

Topping the charts in Brazil and Argentina, the ballad "[Love of My Life](/wiki/Love_of_My_Life_(Queen_song))" stole the show in South American concerts. Mercury would stop singing and would then conduct the audience as they took over, with [Lesley-Ann Jones](/wiki/Lesley-Ann_Jones) writing "the fans knew the song by heart. Their English was word-perfect." Later that year Queen performed for more than 150,000 on 9 October at Monterrey ([Estadio Universitario](/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_(UANL))) and 17 and 18 at Puebla ([Estadio Zaragoza](/wiki/Estadio_Ol%C3%ADmpico_Ignacio_Zaragoza)), Mexico. Though the gigs were successful, they were marred by a lack of planning and suitable facilities, with audiences throwing projectiles on stage. Mercury finished the final gig saying, "Adios, amigos, you motherfuckers!" On 24 and 25 November, Queen played two nights at the [Montreal Forum](/wiki/Montreal_Forum), Quebec, Canada. One of Mercury's most notable performances of *The Game*'s final track, "[Save Me](/wiki/Save_Me_(Queen_song))", took place in Montreal, and the concert is recorded in the live album, *[Queen Rock Montreal](/wiki/Queen_Rock_Montreal)*.

Queen worked with [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie) on the 1981 single "[Under Pressure](/wiki/Under_Pressure)". The first-time collaboration with another artist was spontaneous, as Bowie happened to drop by the studio while Queen were recording. Mercury and Bowie recorded their vocals on the track separately to each other, each coming up with individual ideas. The song topped the UK charts. In October, Queen released their first compilation album, titled *[Greatest Hits](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Queen_album))*, which showcased the group's highlights from 1974 to 1981. The [best-selling album](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_in_the_United_Kingdom) in UK chart history, it is the only album to sell over seven million copies in the UK. As of July 2022, it has spent over [1000 weeks in the UK Album Chart](/wiki/List_of_albums_which_have_spent_the_most_weeks_on_the_UK_Albums_Chart). According to *The Telegraph*, approximately one in three families in the UK own a copy. The album is certified [nine times platinum](/wiki/RIAA_certification) in the US. As of August 2024, it has spent over [600 weeks on the US *Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200#Most_weeks_on_the_chart). *Greatest Hits* has sold over 25 million copies worldwide.

> We moved out to Munich to isolate ourselves from normal life so we could focus on the music. We all ended up in a place that was rather unhealthy. A difficult period. We weren't getting along together. We all had different agendas. It was a difficult time for me, personally – some dark moments.

— May on the recording of *Hot Space* during a difficult period for the band.

In 1982, the band released the album *[Hot Space](/wiki/Hot_Space)*, a departure from their trademark seventies sound, this time being a mixture of [pop rock](/wiki/Pop_rock), dance, disco, [funk](/wiki/Funk), and [R&B](/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues). Most of the album was recorded in [Munich](/wiki/Munich) during the most turbulent period in the band's history. Mercury and Deacon enjoyed the new soul and funk influences, but Taylor and May were less favourable, and were critical of the influence Mercury's personal manager [Paul Prenter](/wiki/Paul_Prenter) had on him. According to [Mack](/wiki/Reinhold_Mack), Queen's producer, Prenter loathed rock music and was in Mercury's ear throughout the *Hot Space* sessions. May was also scathing of Prenter—Mercury's manager from 1977 to 1984—for being dismissive of the importance of radio stations and their vital connection between the artist and the community, and for denying them access to Mercury. May states, "this guy, in the course of one tour, told every record station to fuck off". Queen roadie Peter Hince wrote "None of the band cared for him [Prenter], apart from Freddie", with Hince regarding Mercury's favouring of Prenter as an act of "misguided loyalty". During the Munich sessions, Mercury spent time with Mack and his family, becoming godfather to Mack's first child. [*Q*](/wiki/Q_(magazine)) magazine would list *Hot Space* as one of the top fifteen albums where great rock acts lost the plot. Though the album confused some fans with the change of musical direction, it still reached number 4 in the UK.

Queen performing in Norway during the [Hot Space Tour](/wiki/Hot_Space_Tour) in 1982

Queen toured to promote *Hot Space*, but found some audience unreceptive to the new material. At a gig in [Frankfurt](/wiki/Frankfurt), Mercury told some people heckling the new material, "If you don't want to listen to it, go home!" Former Mott the Hoople keyboardist [Morgan Fisher](/wiki/Morgan_Fisher) joined as an additional touring member. Shows were planned at [Arsenal Stadium](/wiki/Arsenal_Stadium) and [Old Trafford](/wiki/Old_Trafford), but these were cancelled as [Pope John Paul II](/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II) was touring Britain, leading to a lack of available outdoor facilities such as toilets. The gigs were moved to the [Milton Keynes Bowl](/wiki/Milton_Keynes_Bowl) and [Elland Road](/wiki/Elland_Road), [Leeds](/wiki/Leeds) instead. The Milton Keynes concert was filmed by [Tyne Tees Television](/wiki/ITV_Tyne_Tees) and later released on DVD. On 14 and 15 September 1982, the band performed their last two gigs in the US with Mercury on lead vocals, playing at [The Forum](/wiki/The_Forum_(Inglewood,_California)) in Inglewood, California. Fisher was replaced as touring keyboardist by [Fred Mandel](/wiki/Fred_Mandel) for the North American shows. The band stopped touring North America after their Hot Space Tour, as their success there had waned, although they performed on American television for the only time during the eighth-season premiere of *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* on 25 September of the same year; it became the final public performance of the band in North America before the death of their frontman. Their fall in popularity in the US has been partially attributed to [homophobia](/wiki/Homophobia): Mercury had adopted what *Rolling Stone'*s [Mikal Gilmore](/wiki/Mikal_Gilmore) referred to as a "gay clone look," with a thick mustache. Gilmore wrote, "At some shows on the band's 1980 American tour, fans tossed disposable razor blades onstage: They didn't like this identity of Mercury—what they perceived as a brazenly gay rock & roll hero—and they wanted him to shed it." The group finished the year with a Japanese tour.

### 1983–1984: *The Works*

After the Hot Space Tour concluded with a concert at [Seibu Lions Stadium](/wiki/MetLife_Dome) in [Tokorozawa](/wiki/Tokorozawa,_Saitama), Japan in November 1982, Queen decided they would take a significant amount of time off. May later said at that point, "we hated each other for a while". The band reconvened nine months later to begin recording a new album at the [Record Plant Studios](/wiki/Record_Plant_Studios), Los Angeles and [Musicland Studios](/wiki/Musicland_Studios), Munich. Several members of the band also explored side projects and solo work. Taylor released his second solo album, *[Strange Frontier](/wiki/Strange_Frontier)*. May released the mini-album *[Star Fleet Project](/wiki/Star_Fleet_Project)*, collaborating with [Eddie Van Halen](/wiki/Eddie_Van_Halen). Queen left [Elektra Records](/wiki/Elektra_Records), their label in the US, Canada, Japan, Australia, and New Zealand, and signed onto [EMI](/wiki/EMI)/[Capitol Records](/wiki/Capitol_Records).

Queen on stage in Frankfurt, West Germany in 1984

In February 1984, Queen released their eleventh studio album, *[The Works](/wiki/The_Works_(Queen_album))*. Hit singles included "[Radio Ga Ga](/wiki/Radio_Ga_Ga)", which makes a nostalgic defence of the radio format, "[Hammer to Fall](/wiki/Hammer_to_Fall)" and "[I Want to Break Free](/wiki/I_Want_to_Break_Free)". *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* hailed the album as "the *[Led Zeppelin II](/wiki/Led_Zeppelin_II)* of the eighties." In the UK *The Works* went triple platinum and remained in the albums chart for two years. The album failed to do well in the US, where, in addition to issues with their new record label Capitol Records (who had recently severed ties with their independent promotions teams due to a government report on [payola](/wiki/Payola)), the cross-dressing video for "I Want to Break Free", a spoof of the British soap opera *[Coronation Street](/wiki/Coronation_Street)*, proved controversial and was banned by [MTV](/wiki/MTV). The concept of the video came from Roger Taylor via a suggestion from his girlfriend. He told *[Q](/wiki/Q_(magazine))* magazine: "We had done some really serious, epic videos in the past, and we just thought we'd have some fun. We wanted people to know that we didn't take ourselves too seriously, that we could still laugh at ourselves." Director of the video [David Mallet](/wiki/David_Mallet_(director)) said Mercury was reluctant to do it, commenting "it was a hell of a job to get him out of the dressing room".

That year, Queen began [The Works Tour](/wiki/The_Works_Tour), the first tour to feature keyboardist [Spike Edney](/wiki/Spike_Edney) as an extra live musician. The tour featured nine sold-out dates in October in [Bophuthatswana](/wiki/Bophuthatswana), South Africa, at the arena in [Sun City](/wiki/Sun_City,_North_West). Upon returning to England, they were the subject of outrage, having played in South Africa during the height of [apartheid](/wiki/Apartheid) and in violation of worldwide [divestment efforts](/wiki/Disinvestment_from_South_Africa) and a United Nations cultural boycott. The band responded to the critics by stating that they were playing music for fans in South Africa, and they also stressed that the concerts were played before integrated audiences. Queen donated to a school for the deaf and blind as a philanthropic gesture but were fined by the [British Musicians' Union](/wiki/Musicians%27_Union_(UK)) and placed on the United Nations' [blacklisted](/wiki/Blacklisting) artists. In 2021, Taylor voiced his regret for the decision to perform at Sun City, saying that "we went with the best intentions, but I still think it was kind of a mistake."

### 1985–1986: Live Aid, *A Kind of Magic* and tours

In January 1985, Queen headlined two nights of the first [Rock in Rio](/wiki/Rock_in_Rio) festival at [Rio de Janeiro](/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro), Brazil, and played in front of over 300,000 people each night. *[The Boston Globe](/wiki/The_Boston_Globe)* described it as a "mesmerising performance". Highlights from both nights were released on VHS as *Queen: Live in Rio*, which was broadcast on MTV in the US. In April and May 1985, Queen completed the Works Tour with sold-out shows in Australia and Japan.

> Queen were absolutely the best band of the day ... they just went and smashed one hit after another ... it was the perfect stage for Freddie: the whole world.

— [Bob Geldof](/wiki/Bob_Geldof), on Queen's performance at [Live Aid](/wiki/Live_Aid).

At [Live Aid](/wiki/Live_Aid), held at [Wembley](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_(1923)) on 13 July 1985, in front of the biggest-ever TV audience of an estimated 400 million, Queen performed some of their greatest hits. Many of the sold-out stadium audience of 72,000 people clapped, sang, and swayed in unison. The show's organisers, [Bob Geldof](/wiki/Bob_Geldof) and [Midge Ure](/wiki/Midge_Ure); other musicians such as [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John) and [Cliff Richard](/wiki/Cliff_Richard); and journalists writing for the [BBC](/wiki/BBC), [CNN](/wiki/CNN), *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*, [MTV](/wiki/MTV), *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* and *[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)*, among others, described Queen as the highlight. Interviewed backstage, [Roger Waters](/wiki/Roger_Waters) stated: "Everybody's been buzzing about Queen that I've run into. They had everybody completely spellbound." An industry poll in 2005 ranked it the greatest rock performance of all time. Mercury's powerful, sustained note—"Aaaaaay-o"—during the [call-and-response](/wiki/Call-and-response) *a cappella* segment came to be known as "The Note Heard Round the World". The band were revitalised by the response to Live Aid—a "shot in the arm" Roger Taylor called it—and the ensuing increase in record sales. In 1986 Mercury commented: "From our perspective, the fact that Live Aid happened when it did was really lucky. It came out of nowhere to save us. For sure that was a turning point. Maybe you could say that in the history of Queen, it was a really special moment."

Queen ended 1985 by releasing the single "[One Vision](/wiki/One_Vision)" and a limited-edition boxed set of Queen albums, *[The Complete Works](/wiki/The_Complete_Works_(Queen_album))*. The package included the 1984 Christmas single "[Thank God It's Christmas](/wiki/Thank_God_It%27s_Christmas)" and previously unreleased material. In early 1986, Queen recorded the album *[A Kind of Magic](/wiki/A_Kind_of_Magic)*, containing several reworkings of songs written for the fantasy action film *[Highlander](/wiki/Highlander_(film))*. The album was successful in the UK, West Germany and several other countries, producing a string of hits including "[A Kind of Magic](/wiki/A_Kind_of_Magic_(song))", "[Friends Will Be Friends](/wiki/Friends_Will_Be_Friends)", "[Princes of the Universe](/wiki/Princes_of_the_Universe)" and "[Who Wants to Live Forever](/wiki/Who_Wants_to_Live_Forever)"; the latter featuring an orchestra conducted by [Michael Kamen](/wiki/Michael_Kamen). The album was less successful in North America, reaching 46 in the US, and was described by biographer [Mark Blake](/wiki/Mark_Blake_(writer)) as "a so-so album" and "a somewhat uneven listening experience". In 2007, *[Classic Rock](/wiki/Classic_Rock_(magazine))* ranked it the 28th greatest soundtrack album of all time.

Mercury's yellow military jacket (one of three designed by Diana Moseley) worn during the 1986 [Magic Tour](/wiki/Magic_Tour_(Queen))

In mid-1986, Queen went on the [Magic Tour](/wiki/Magic_Tour_(Queen)), their final tour with Mercury. They once again hired Spike Edney. Queen began the tour at the [Råsunda Stadium](/wiki/R%C3%A5sunda_Stadium) in Stockholm, Sweden, and later performed a [concert](/wiki/Slane_Concert) at [Slane Castle](/wiki/Slane_Castle), Ireland, in front of an audience of 95,000, which broke the venue's attendance record. The band also played behind the [Iron Curtain](/wiki/Iron_Curtain) when they performed to a crowd of 80,000 at the [Népstadion](/wiki/Ferenc_Pusk%C3%A1s_Stadium_(1953)) in [Budapest](/wiki/Budapest) (released in the concert film *[Hungarian Rhapsody: Queen Live in Budapest](/wiki/Hungarian_Rhapsody:_Queen_Live_in_Budapest)*), in what was one of the biggest rock concerts ever held in Eastern Europe. More than one million people saw Queen on the tour—400,000 in the UK alone, a record at the time.

The Magic Tour's highlight was at Wembley Stadium and resulted in the live double album *[Queen at Wembley](/wiki/Queen_at_Wembley)*, released on CD and as a live concert VHS/DVD, which has gone five times platinum in the US and four times platinum in the UK. The demand for tickets saw extra dates added to the tour, with concert promoter [Harvey Goldsmith](/wiki/Harvey_Goldsmith) stating "they seem to have an endless market." Queen could not book Wembley for a third night, but played at [Knebworth Park](/wiki/Knebworth_Festival) in Hertfordshire on 9 August. The show sold out within two hours and over 120,000 fans packed the park for what was Queen's final performance with Mercury. At the end of the concert the band appeared on stage for the final time to an encore of "God Save the Queen", with Mercury, in his crown and gown, bidding the crowd "goodnight and sweet dreams". Roadie Peter Hince states, "At Knebworth, I somehow felt it was going to be the last for all of us"; Brian May recalled Mercury saying "I'm not going to be doing this forever. This is probably the last time."

### 1988–1992: *The Miracle*, *Innuendo* and Mercury's final years

> There was all that time when we knew Freddie was on the way out, we kept our heads down.

— Brian May

In October 1986, two months after his final concert, Mercury, on his return to London from holiday, was confronted by a tabloid frenzy at [Heathrow Airport](/wiki/Heathrow_Airport) with the British newspapers the *[News of the World](/wiki/News_of_the_World)* and *[The Sun](/wiki/The_Sun_(United_Kingdom))* reporting he had his blood tested for HIV/AIDS at a [Harley Street](/wiki/Harley_Street) clinic, but Mercury was quoted as saying he was "perfectly fit and healthy". Fans noticed his increasingly gaunt appearance in 1988, with Mercury insisting he was merely "exhausted" and too busy to provide interviews; he was now 42 years old and had been involved in music for nearly two decades. He had in fact been diagnosed as HIV positive in 1987, but did not make his illness public, with only his inner circle of colleagues and friends aware of his condition.

Queen recorded six studio albums at [Mountain Studios](/wiki/Mountain_Studios) in Montreux, Switzerland from 1978 to 1995, with Mercury making his final recording here in June 1991.

The tribute wall in the studio. The free tour, [Queen: The Studio Experience](/wiki/Queen:_The_Studio_Experience), was opened in December 2013, with fans asked for a donation to the [Mercury Phoenix Trust](/wiki/Mercury_Phoenix_Trust) charity.

After working on various solo projects during 1988 (including Mercury's collaboration with [Montserrat Caballé](/wiki/Montserrat_Caball%C3%A9), *[Barcelona](/wiki/Barcelona_(Freddie_Mercury_and_Montserrat_Caball%C3%A9_album))*), the band released *[The Miracle](/wiki/The_Miracle_(album))* in 1989. The album continued the direction of *A Kind of Magic*, using a pop-rock sound mixed with a few heavy numbers. It spawned the hit singles "[I Want It All](/wiki/I_Want_It_All_(Queen_song))"—which became an anti-apartheid anthem in South Africa—"[Scandal](/wiki/Scandal_(song))", and "[The Miracle](/wiki/The_Miracle_(song))". *The Miracle* also began a change in direction of Queen's songwriting philosophy. Beforehand, nearly all songs had been written by and credited to a single member. With *The Miracle*, their songwriting became more collaborative, and they vowed to credit the final product only to Queen as a group.

In 1990, Queen ended their contract with Capitol and signed with [Hollywood Records](/wiki/Hollywood_Records); through the deal, [Disney](/wiki/Disney_Music_Group) acquired the North American distribution rights to Queen's catalogue for $10 million, and remains the group's recorded-music rights owner and distributor in the US and Canada; the band retained ownership of the global rights through the UK-based Queen Productions [Ltd.](/wiki/Private_company_limited_by_shares) In February that year, Mercury made what would prove to be his final public appearance when he joined the rest of Queen onstage at the [Dominion Theatre](/wiki/Dominion_Theatre) in London to collect the [Brit Award](/wiki/Brit_Awards) for Outstanding Contribution to British Music.

Their fourteenth studio album, *[Innuendo](/wiki/Innuendo_(album))*, was released in early 1991 with "[Innuendo](/wiki/Innuendo_(song))" and other charting singles released later in the year. The music video for "[The Show Must Go On](/wiki/The_Show_Must_Go_On_(Queen_song))" featured archive footage of Queen's performances between 1981 and 1989, and along with the manner of the song's lyrics, fuelled reports that Mercury was dying. Mercury was increasingly ill and could barely walk when the band recorded "The Show Must Go On" in 1990. Because of this, May had concerns about whether he was physically capable of singing it, but May recalled that he "completely killed it". The rest of the band were ready to record when Mercury felt able to come into the studio, for an hour or two at a time. May says of Mercury: "He just kept saying. 'Write me more. Write me stuff. I want to just sing this and do it and when I am gone you can finish it off.' He had no fear, really." The band's second-greatest hits compilation, *[Greatest Hits II](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_II_(Queen_album))*, followed in October 1991; it is the [tenth best-selling album](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the UK, the [seventh best-selling album in Germany](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_in_Germany#By_Units), is certified Diamond in France where it is one of the [best-selling albums](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_in_France#List_of_the_20_best-selling_albums_of_all-time_in_France_by_sales), and has sold 16 million copies worldwide.

Following Mercury's death on 24 November 1991, [his tribute concert](/wiki/The_Freddie_Mercury_Tribute_Concert) was held at the original [Wembley Stadium](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_(1923)) in London on 20 April 1992, the same venue where Queen performed at Live Aid in July 1985.

On 23 November 1991, in a prepared statement made on his deathbed, Mercury confirmed that he had AIDS. Within 24 hours of the statement, he [died](/wiki/Death_of_Freddie_Mercury) of [bronchial pneumonia](/wiki/Bronchopneumonia), which was brought on as a complication of the disease. His funeral service on 27 November in [Kensal Green](/wiki/Kensal_Green), West London was private, and held in accordance with the [Zoroastrian](/wiki/Zoroastrianism) religious faith of his family.

"Bohemian Rhapsody" was re-released as a single shortly after Mercury's death, with "[These Are the Days of Our Lives](/wiki/These_Are_the_Days_of_Our_Lives)" as the double A-side. The [music video](/wiki/These_Are_the_Days_of_Our_Lives#Music_video) for the latter contains Mercury's final scenes in front of the camera. Ron Hart of *Rolling Stone* wrote, "the conga-driven synth ballad "These Are the Days of Our Lives" is *Innuendo*'s most significant single, given that its video marked the last time his fans were able to see the singer alive." The video was recorded on 30 May 1991 (which proved to be Mercury's final work with Queen). The single went to [number one](/wiki/List_of_number-one_singles_(UK)) in the UK, remaining there for five weeks—the only recording to top the Christmas chart twice and the only one to be number one in four different years (1975, 1976, 1991, and 1992). Initial proceeds from the single—approximately £1,000,000—were donated to the [Terrence Higgins Trust](/wiki/Terrence_Higgins_Trust), an AIDS charity.

Queen's popularity was stimulated in North America when "Bohemian Rhapsody" was featured in the 1992 comedy film *[Wayne's World](/wiki/Wayne%27s_World_(film))*. Its inclusion helped the song reach number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100 for five weeks in 1992 (including its 1976 chart run, it remained in the Hot 100 for a combined 41 weeks), and won the band an [MTV Award](/wiki/MTV_Award) at the [1992 MTV Video Music Awards](/wiki/1992_MTV_Video_Music_Awards). The compilation album *[Classic Queen](/wiki/Classic_Queen)* also reached number four on the *Billboard* 200, and is certified three times platinum in the US. *Wayne's World* footage was used to make a new music video for "Bohemian Rhapsody", with which the band and management were delighted.

On 20 April 1992, [The Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert](/wiki/The_Freddie_Mercury_Tribute_Concert) was held at London's [Wembley Stadium](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_(1923)) to a crowd of 72,000. Performers, including [Def Leppard](/wiki/Def_Leppard), [Robert Plant](/wiki/Robert_Plant), [Tony Iommi](/wiki/Tony_Iommi), [Roger Daltrey](/wiki/Roger_Daltrey), [Guns N' Roses](/wiki/Guns_N%27_Roses), [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John), [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie), [George Michael](/wiki/George_Michael), [Annie Lennox](/wiki/Annie_Lennox), [Seal](/wiki/Seal_(musician)), [Extreme](/wiki/Extreme_(band)), and [Metallica](/wiki/Metallica) performed various Queen songs along with the three remaining Queen members (and [Spike Edney](/wiki/Spike_Edney).) The concert is listed in the *Guinness Book of Records* as "The largest rock star [benefit concert](/wiki/Benefit_concert)", as it was televised to over 1.2 billion viewers worldwide, and raised over £20,000,000 for AIDS charities.

### 1995–2003: *Made in Heaven* to 46664 Concert

Statue of Mercury overlooking [Lake Geneva](/wiki/Lake_Geneva) in [Montreux](/wiki/Montreux), Switzerland

Queen's last album with Mercury, titled *[Made in Heaven](/wiki/Made_in_Heaven)*, was released in 1995, four years after his death. Featuring tracks such as "[Too Much Love Will Kill You](/wiki/Too_Much_Love_Will_Kill_You)" and "[Heaven for Everyone](/wiki/Heaven_for_Everyone)", it was constructed from Mercury's final recordings in 1991, material left over from their previous studio albums and re-worked material from May, Taylor, and Mercury's solo albums. The album also featured the song "[Mother Love](/wiki/Mother_Love_(song))", the last vocal recording Mercury made, which he completed using a drum machine, over which May, Taylor and Deacon later added the instrumental track. After completing the penultimate verse, Mercury had told the band he "wasn't feeling that great" and stated, "I will finish it when I come back, next time". Mercury never returned to the studio afterwards, leaving May to record the final verse of the song. Both stages of recording, before and after Mercury's death, were completed at the [band's studio](/wiki/Mountain_Studios) in [Montreux](/wiki/Montreux), Switzerland. The album reached number one in the UK following its release, their [ninth number one album](/wiki/List_of_artists_by_number_of_UK_Albums_Chart_number_ones), and sold 20 million copies worldwide. On 25 November 1996, a statue of Mercury was unveiled in Montreux overlooking [Lake Geneva](/wiki/Lake_Geneva), almost five years to the day since his death.

> You guys should go out and play again. It must be like having a Ferrari in the garage waiting for a driver.

— [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John), on Queen being without a lead singer since the death of Freddie Mercury.

In 1997, Queen returned to the studio to record "[No-One but You (Only the Good Die Young)](/wiki/No-One_but_You_(Only_the_Good_Die_Young))", a song dedicated to Mercury and all those who die too soon. It was released as a bonus track on the *[Queen Rocks](/wiki/Queen_Rocks)* compilation album later that year, and features in *[Greatest Hits III](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_III_(Queen_album))*. In January 1997, Queen performed "[The Show Must Go On](/wiki/The_Show_Must_Go_On_(Queen_song))" live with Elton John and the [Béjart Ballet](/wiki/B%C3%A9jart_Ballet) in Paris on a night Mercury was remembered, and it marked the last performance and public appearance of John Deacon, who chose to retire; this left May and Taylor as the two remaining original members. The Paris concert was only the second time Queen had played live since Mercury's death, prompting Elton John to urge them to perform again. Brian May and Roger Taylor performed together at several award ceremonies and charity concerts, sharing vocals with various guest singers. During this time, they were billed as *Queen +* followed by the guest singer's name. In 1998, the duo appeared at [Luciano Pavarotti](/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti)'s benefit concert with May performing "[Too Much Love Will Kill You](/wiki/Too_Much_Love_Will_Kill_You)" with Pavarotti, later playing "Radio Ga Ga", "We Will Rock You", and "We Are the Champions" with [Zucchero](/wiki/Zucchero). They again attended and performed at Pavarotti's benefit concert in [Modena](/wiki/Modena), Italy in May 2003. Several of the guest singers recorded new versions of Queen's hits under the *Queen +* name, such as [Robbie Williams](/wiki/Robbie_Williams) providing vocals for "We Are the Champions" for the soundtrack of *[A Knight's Tale](/wiki/A_Knight%27s_Tale)* (2001).

Queen's star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) at 6358 Hollywood Boulevard

In November 1999, *[Greatest Hits III](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_III_(Queen_album))* was released. This featured, among others, "Queen + [Wyclef Jean](/wiki/Wyclef_Jean)" on a [rap](/wiki/Hip_hop_music) version of "Another One Bites the Dust". A live version of "Somebody to Love" by [George Michael](/wiki/George_Michael) and a live version of "The Show Must Go On" with [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John) were also featured in the album. By this point, Queen's vast amount of record sales made them the second-bestselling artist in the UK of all time, behind [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles). In November 2000, the band released the box set, *[The Platinum Collection](/wiki/The_Platinum_Collection_(Queen_album))*. It is certified seven times platinum in the UK and five times platinum in the US. On 18 October 2002, Queen were awarded the 2,207th star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame), for their work in the music industry, which is located at 6358 [Hollywood Blvd](/wiki/Hollywood_Blvd.). On 29 November 2003, May and Taylor performed at the [46664 Concert](/wiki/46664_(concerts)) hosted by [Nelson Mandela](/wiki/Nelson_Mandela) at [Green Point Stadium](/wiki/Green_Point_Stadium), Cape Town, to raise awareness of the spread of HIV/AIDS in South Africa. A new song, "Invincible Hope", featuring Mandela's speech and credited to Queen + Nelson Mandela, was performed during the concert and later released on the *46664: One Year On* EP. During that period May and Taylor spent time at Mandela's home, discussing how Africa's problems might be approached, and two years later the band were made ambassadors for the 46664 cause.

### 2004–2009: Queen + Paul Rodgers

Main article: [Queen + Paul Rodgers](/wiki/Queen_%2B_Paul_Rodgers)

Queen performing with [Paul Rodgers](/wiki/Paul_Rodgers) during their 2005 [tour](/wiki/Queen_%2B_Paul_Rodgers_Tour)

At the end of 2004, May and Taylor announced that they would reunite and return to touring in 2005 with [Paul Rodgers](/wiki/Paul_Rodgers) (founder and former lead singer of [Free](/wiki/Free_(band)) and [Bad Company](/wiki/Bad_Company)). Brian May's website also stated that Rodgers would be "featured with" Queen as "Queen + Paul Rodgers", not replacing Mercury. In November 2004, Queen were among the inaugural inductees into the [UK Music Hall of Fame](/wiki/UK_Music_Hall_of_Fame), and the award ceremony was the first event at which Rodgers joined May and Taylor as vocalist.

Between 2005 and 2006, Queen + Paul Rodgers embarked on a [world tour](/wiki/Queen_%2B_Paul_Rodgers_Tour), which was the first time Queen toured since their [last tour](/wiki/Magic_Tour_(Queen)) with Freddie Mercury in 1986. Taylor said: "We never thought we would tour again, Paul came along by chance and we seemed to have a chemistry. Paul is just such a great singer. He's not trying to be Freddie." The first leg was in Europe, the second in Japan, and the third in the US in 2006. Queen received the inaugural [VH1 Rock Honors](/wiki/VH1_Rock_Honors) at the [Mandalay Bay Events Center](/wiki/Mandalay_Bay_Events_Center) in [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley), Nevada, on 25 May 2006. [Foo Fighters](/wiki/Foo_Fighters) performed "[Tie Your Mother Down](/wiki/Tie_Your_Mother_Down)" to open the ceremony before being joined on stage by May, Taylor, and Rodgers, who played a selection of Queen hits.

Queen + Paul Rodgers concert in [Kharkiv's Freedom Square](/wiki/Freedom_Square,_Kharkiv), Ukraine, 12 September 2008

Queen + Paul Rodgers performed at the [Nelson Mandela 90th Birthday Tribute](/wiki/Nelson_Mandela_90th_Birthday_Tribute) held in [Hyde Park](/wiki/Hyde_Park,_London), London on 27 June 2008, to commemorate Mandela's ninetieth birthday, and again promote awareness of the HIV/AIDS pandemic. The first Queen + Paul Rodgers album, titled *[The Cosmos Rocks](/wiki/The_Cosmos_Rocks)*, was released in Europe on 12 September 2008 and in the US on 28 October 2008. The band again toured Europe, opening on [Kharkiv's Freedom Square](/wiki/Freedom_Square_(Kharkiv)) in front of 350,000 Ukrainian fans; the concert was [released on DVD](/wiki/Live_in_Ukraine). The tour then moved to Russia, and the band performed two sold-out shows at the [Moscow Arena](/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_(Moscow_arena)). Having completed the first leg of the European tour, which saw the band play 15 sold-out dates across nine countries, the UK leg of the tour sold out within 90 minutes of going on sale and included three London dates, the first of which was [the O2 Arena](/wiki/The_O2_Arena) on 13 October. The last leg of the tour was in South America, and included a sold-out concert at [José Amalfitani Stadium](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Amalfitani_Stadium), Buenos Aires.

Queen and Paul Rodgers split up on 12 May 2009. Rodgers stated: "My arrangement with [Queen] was similar to my arrangement with [Jimmy](/wiki/Jimmy_Page) [Page] in [The Firm](/wiki/The_Firm_(rock_band)) in that it was never meant to be a permanent arrangement". Rodgers did not rule out the possibility of working with Queen again.

### 2009–2011: Departure from EMI, 40th anniversary

On 20 May 2009, May and Taylor performed "We Are the Champions" live on the season finale of *[American Idol](/wiki/American_Idol)* with winner [Kris Allen](/wiki/Kris_Allen) and runner-up [Adam Lambert](/wiki/Adam_Lambert) providing a vocal duet. A new greatest hits compilation *[Absolute Greatest](/wiki/Absolute_Greatest)* was released on 16 November and peaked at number 3 in the UK. It contains 20 of Queen's hits spanning their career and was released in four different formats: single disc, double disc (with commentary), double disc with feature book, and a [vinyl record](/wiki/Vinyl_record). Before its release, Queen ran an online competition to guess the track listing as a promotion for the album. On 15 November 2009, May and Taylor performed "Bohemian Rhapsody" live on the British TV show *[The X Factor](/wiki/The_X_Factor_(UK))* alongside the finalists.

> Many of you will have read bits and pieces on the internet about Queen changing record companies and so I wanted to confirm to you that the band have signed a new contract with Universal Music ... we would like to thank the EMI team for all their hard work over the years, the many successes and the fond memories, and of course we look forward to continuing to work with EMI Music Publishing who take care of our songwriting affairs. Next year we start working with our new record company to celebrate Queen's 40th anniversary and we will be announcing full details of the plans over the next 3 months. As Brian has already said Queen's next moves will involve 'studio work, computers and live work.

— [Jim Beach](/wiki/Jim_Beach), Queen's Manager, on the change of record label.

On 20 August 2010, Queen's manager [Jim Beach](/wiki/Jim_Beach) announced that the band had signed a new contract with [Universal Music Group](/wiki/Universal_Music_Group) after almost 40 years with [EMI](/wiki/EMI). On 22 September, May confirmed that the band's new deal was with [Island Records](/wiki/Island_Records), a subsidiary of Universal. [Hollywood Records](/wiki/Hollywood_Records) remained as the group's label and owner of recorded-music rights in the US and Canada. For the first time since the late 1980s, Queen's catalogue now has the same distributor worldwide, as Universal distributes for both the Island and Hollywood labels (for a time in the late 1980s, Queen was on EMI-owned [Capitol Records](/wiki/Capitol_Records) in the US).

On 14 March 2011, the band's 40th anniversary, Queen's first five albums were re-released in the UK and some other territories as remastered deluxe editions; the US versions were released on 17 May. The second five albums of Queen's back catalogue were released worldwide on 27 June, and on 27 September in the US and Canada. The final five were released in the UK on 5 September.

In May 2011, [Jane's Addiction](/wiki/Jane%27s_Addiction) vocalist [Perry Farrell](/wiki/Perry_Farrell) said that Queen were scouting their live bassist [Chris Chaney](/wiki/Chris_Chaney) to join the band. In the same month, Paul Rodgers stated he might tour with Queen again in the future. At the 2011 [Broadcast Music, Incorporated](/wiki/Broadcast_Music,_Incorporated) (BMI) Awards held in London on 4 October, Queen received the BMI Icon Award for their airplay success in the US. At the [2011 MTV Europe Music Awards](/wiki/2011_MTV_Europe_Music_Awards) on 6 November, Queen received the [Global Icon Award](/wiki/MTV_Europe_Music_Award_for_Global_Icon), which [Katy Perry](/wiki/Katy_Perry) presented to Brian May. Queen closed the awards ceremony, with Lambert singing "The Show Must Go On", "We Will Rock You" and "We Are the Champions". The collaboration received a positive response from fans and critics, resulting in speculation about future projects together.

## Music style and influences

Brian May playing his custom-made [Red Special](/wiki/Red_Special) at [the O2 Arena](/wiki/The_O2_Arena) in London in 2017. He has used this guitar almost exclusively since the band's advent in the early 1970s.

Queen drew artistic influence from British rock acts of the 1960s and early 1970s, such as [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles), [the Kinks](/wiki/The_Kinks), [Cream](/wiki/Cream_(band)), [Led Zeppelin](/wiki/Led_Zeppelin), [Pink Floyd](/wiki/Pink_Floyd), [the Who](/wiki/The_Who), [Black Sabbath](/wiki/Black_Sabbath), [Slade](/wiki/Slade), [Deep Purple](/wiki/Deep_Purple), [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie), [Genesis](/wiki/Genesis_(band)), and [Yes](/wiki/Yes_(band)), with Mercury also inspired by the [rock and roll](/wiki/Rock_and_roll) singers [Little Richard](/wiki/Little_Richard), [Elvis Presley](/wiki/Elvis_Presley) and the [gospel](/wiki/Gospel_music) singer [Aretha Franklin](/wiki/Aretha_Franklin). On the Beatles, Brian May stated they "built our bible as far as musical composition, arrangement and production went. *[The White Album](/wiki/The_Beatles_(album))* is a complete catalogue of how you should use a studio to build songs." Mercury said, "[John Lennon](/wiki/John_Lennon) was larger than life, and an absolute genius. Even at a very early stage when they were the Beatles, I always preferred John Lennon's things. I don't know why. He just had that magic." May and Mercury were influenced by [Jimi Hendrix](/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix), with Mercury saying "he really had everything any rock 'n' roll star should have", and May saying "Jimi is, of course, my number one. And I've always said that [...] I never stop learning from Jimi." Mercury's thesis for his Ealing College diploma was on Hendrix, and Mercury and Taylor closed their Kensington Market stall on 18 September 1970 to commemorate his death.

At their outset in the early 1970s, Queen's music has been characterised as "Led Zeppelin meets Yes" due to its combination of "acoustic/electric guitar extremes and fantasy-inspired multi-part song epics". Although Mercury stated [Robert Plant](/wiki/Robert_Plant) as his favourite singer and Led Zeppelin as "the greatest" rock band, he also said Queen "have more in common with [Liza Minnelli](/wiki/Liza_Minnelli) than Led Zeppelin. We're more in the showbiz tradition than the rock'n'roll tradition". In his book on *Essential Hard Rock and Heavy Metal*, [Eddie Trunk](/wiki/Eddie_Trunk) described Queen as "a [hard rock](/wiki/Hard_rock) band at the core but one with a high level of majesty and theatricality that delivered a little something for everyone", as well as observing that the band "sounded British". [Rob Halford](/wiki/Rob_Halford) of [Judas Priest](/wiki/Judas_Priest) commented, "It's rare that you struggle to label a band. If you're a [heavy metal](/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) band you're meant to look and sound like a heavy metal band but you can't really call Queen anything. They could be a pop band one day or the band that wrote 'Bicycle Race' the next and a full-blown metal band the next. In terms of the depth of the musical landscape that they covered, it was very similar to some extent to the Beatles." While stating they were influenced by various artists and genres, Joe Bosso of *[Guitar World](/wiki/Guitar_World)* magazine writes, "Queen seemed to occupy their own lane."

Queen composed music that drew inspiration from many different genres of music, often with a tongue-in-cheek attitude. The music styles and genres they have been associated with include [progressive rock](/wiki/Progressive_rock) (also known as symphonic rock), [art rock](/wiki/Art_rock), [glam rock](/wiki/Glam_rock), [arena rock](/wiki/Arena_rock), heavy metal, [operatic pop](/wiki/Operatic_pop), [pop rock](/wiki/Pop_rock), [psychedelic rock](/wiki/Psychedelic_rock), [baroque pop](/wiki/Baroque_pop), and [rockabilly](/wiki/Rockabilly). Queen also wrote songs that were inspired by musical styles not typically associated with rock groups, such as opera, [music hall](/wiki/Music_hall), [folk music](/wiki/Folk_music), gospel, [ragtime](/wiki/Ragtime), and dance/[disco](/wiki/Disco). Their 1980 single "Another One Bites the Dust" became a major hit single in the [funk rock](/wiki/Funk_rock) genre. Known for their [anthemic](/wiki/Stadium_anthem) songs which are a staple of sports arenas and stadiums the world over, several Queen songs were written with audience participation in mind, such as "We Will Rock You" and "We Are the Champions". Similarly, "Radio Ga Ga" became a live favourite for the band because it would have "crowds clapping like they were at a [Nuremberg rally](/wiki/Nuremberg_Rally)".

In 1963, the teenage Brian May and his father [custom-built](/wiki/Experimental_musical_instrument) his signature guitar [Red Special](/wiki/Red_Special), which was purposely designed to [feedback](/wiki/Feedback). May has used [Vox](/wiki/Vox_(company)) [AC30](/wiki/Vox_AC30) amplifiers almost exclusively since a meeting with [Rory Gallagher](/wiki/Rory_Gallagher) at a gig in London during the late 1960s or early 1970s. He also uses a [sixpence](/wiki/Sixpence_(British_coin)) as a plectrum. Sonic experimentation figured heavily in Queen's songs. A distinctive characteristic of Queen's music are the [vocal harmonies](/wiki/Vocal_harmonies) which are usually composed of the voices of May, Mercury, and Taylor best heard on the studio albums *A Night at the Opera* and *A Day at the Races*. Some of the development of this sound is attributed to the producer [Roy Thomas Baker](/wiki/Roy_Thomas_Baker) and engineer [Mike Stone](/wiki/Mike_Stone_(music_producer)). Queen were also known for [multi-tracking](/wiki/Multitrack_recording) voices to imitate the sound of a large choir through [overdubs](/wiki/Overdubbing). For instance, according to Brian May, there are over 180 vocal overdubs in "Bohemian Rhapsody". The band's vocal structures have been compared with [the Beach Boys](/wiki/The_Beach_Boys).

## Media

### Logo

Having studied graphic design in art college, Mercury also designed Queen's logo, called the Queen crest, shortly before the release of the band's first album. The logo combines the [zodiac](/wiki/Zodiac) signs of all four members: two lions for [Leo](/wiki/Leo_(astrology)) (Deacon and Taylor), a crab for [Cancer](/wiki/Cancer_(astrology)) (May), and two [fairies](/wiki/Fairy) for [Virgo](/wiki/Virgo_(astrology)) (Mercury). The lions embrace a stylised letter Q, the crab rests atop the letter with flames rising directly above it, and the fairies are each sheltering below a lion. There is also a crown inside the Q and the whole logo is over-shadowed by an enormous [phoenix](/wiki/Phoenix_(mythology)). The whole symbol bears a passing resemblance to the [royal coat of arms of the United Kingdom](/wiki/Royal_coat_of_arms_of_the_United_Kingdom), particularly with the lion supporters. The original logo, as found on the reverse side of the cover of the band's first album, was a simple line drawing. Later sleeves bore more intricately coloured versions of the logo.

### Music videos

Mercury in a Harlequin outfit. He appears in a half black, half white version in the 1977 music video for "[We Are the Champions](/wiki/We_Are_the_Champions)".

Directed by [Bruce Gowers](/wiki/Bruce_Gowers), the groundbreaking ["Bohemian Rhapsody" promotional video](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody#Promotional_video) sees the band adopt a "decadent 'glam' sensibility". Replicating [Mick Rock](/wiki/Mick_Rock)'s photograph of the band from the cover of *[Queen II](/wiki/Queen_II)*—which itself was inspired by a photo of actress [Marlene Dietrich](/wiki/Marlene_Dietrich) from *[Shanghai Express](/wiki/Shanghai_Express_(film))* (1932)—the video opens with "Queen standing in diamond formation, heads tilted back like [Easter Island statues](/wiki/Easter_Island_statues)" in near darkness as they sing the *a cappella* part.

One of the industry's leading music video directors, [David Mallet](/wiki/David_Mallet_(director)), directed some of their subsequent videos. Some of their later videos use footage from classic films: "[Under Pressure](/wiki/Under_Pressure)" incorporates 1920s silent films, [Sergei Eisenstein](/wiki/Sergei_Eisenstein)'s *[Battleship Potemkin](/wiki/Battleship_Potemkin)* and [F. W. Murnau](/wiki/F._W._Murnau)'s *[Nosferatu](/wiki/Nosferatu)*; the 1984 video for "[Radio Ga Ga](/wiki/Radio_Ga_Ga)" includes footage from [Fritz Lang](/wiki/Fritz_Lang)'s *[Metropolis](/wiki/Metropolis_(1927_film))* (1927); "[Calling All Girls](/wiki/Calling_All_Girls)" was a homage to [George Lucas](/wiki/George_Lucas)'s *[THX 1138](/wiki/THX_1138)*; and the 1995 video "[Heaven for Everyone](/wiki/Heaven_for_Everyone)" shows footage from [Georges Méliès](/wiki/Georges_M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s)' *[A Trip to the Moon](/wiki/A_Trip_to_the_Moon)* (1902) and *[The Impossible Voyage](/wiki/The_Impossible_Voyage)* (1904). The first part of Mallet's music video for "[I Want to Break Free](/wiki/I_Want_to_Break_Free)" spoofed the popular long-running British soap opera *[Coronation Street](/wiki/Coronation_Street)*.

The music video for "[Innuendo](/wiki/Innuendo_(song))" combines [stop motion](/wiki/Stop_motion) animation with [rotoscoping](/wiki/Rotoscoping) and band members appear as illustrations and images taken from earlier Queen music videos on a cinema screen akin to the dystopian film *[Nineteen Eighty-Four](/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four_(1984_film))* (1984). The music videos for "[Flash](/wiki/Flash_(Queen_song))" (from *Flash Gordon*) and "[Princes of the Universe](/wiki/Princes_of_the_Universe)" (from *Highlander*) are themed on the films the band recorded soundtracks for, with the latter featuring Mercury briefly re-enact the sword-fighting scene with the titular character. Queen also appeared in conventional music videos. "We Will Rock You" was filmed outdoors in Roger Taylor's back garden during a cold day in early January 1977. Filmed at the [New London Theatre](/wiki/Gillian_Lynne_Theatre) later that year, the music video for "We Are the Champions" features the band—with Mercury in a trademark Harlequin outfit—performing in front of an enthusiastic crowd who wave Queen scarves in a manner similar to English football fans. The last music video of the group while Mercury was alive, "[These Are the Days of Our Lives](/wiki/These_Are_the_Days_of_Our_Lives)", was filmed in black and white to hide the full extent of his illness.

### Musical theatre

Statue of Mercury at the West End's [Dominion Theatre](/wiki/Dominion_Theatre) where Queen and [Ben Elton](/wiki/Ben_Elton)'s musical *[We Will Rock You](/wiki/We_Will_Rock_You_(musical))* was performed from 2002 to 2014

In May 2002, a musical or "rock theatrical" based on the songs of Queen, titled *[We Will Rock You](/wiki/We_Will_Rock_You_(musical))*, opened at the [Dominion Theatre](/wiki/Dominion_Theatre) in London's [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre). The musical was written by British comedian and author [Ben Elton](/wiki/Ben_Elton) in collaboration with Brian May and Roger Taylor, and produced by [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro). It has since been staged in many cities around the world. The launch of the musical coincided with [Queen Elizabeth II's](/wiki/Elizabeth_II) [Golden Jubilee](/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II). As part of the Jubilee celebrations, Brian May performed a [guitar solo](/wiki/Guitar_solo) of "[God Save the Queen](/wiki/God_Save_the_Queen)", as featured on Queen's *A Night at the Opera*, from the roof of [Buckingham Palace](/wiki/Buckingham_Palace). The recording of this performance was used as video for the song on the 30th Anniversary DVD edition of *A Night at the Opera*. Following the Las Vegas premiere on 8 September 2004, Queen were inducted into the [Hollywood RockWalk](/wiki/Guitar_Center#Hollywood's_RockWalk) in [Sunset Boulevard](/wiki/Sunset_Boulevard), Los Angeles.

*We Will Rock You* musical in Tokyo, Japan, November 2006

The original London production was scheduled to close on Saturday, 7 October 2006, at the [Dominion Theatre](/wiki/Dominion_Theatre), but due to public demand, the show ran until May 2014. *We Will Rock You* has become the longest-running musical ever to run at this prime London theatre, overtaking the previous record holder, the musical *[Grease](/wiki/Grease_(musical))*. Brian May stated in 2008 that they were considering writing a sequel to *We Will Rock You*. The musical toured around the UK in 2009, playing at [Manchester Palace Theatre](/wiki/Palace_Theatre,_Manchester), [Sunderland Empire](/wiki/Sunderland_Empire_Theatre), [Birmingham Hippodrome](/wiki/Birmingham_Hippodrome), [Bristol Hippodrome](/wiki/Bristol_Hippodrome), and [Edinburgh Playhouse](/wiki/Edinburgh_Playhouse). By December 2022 the musical had been seen by 20 million people across 28 countries. In the summer of 2023 it returned to London with a 12-week run at the [London Coliseum](/wiki/London_Coliseum).

Sean Bovim created "[Queen at the Ballet](/wiki/Queen_at_the_Ballet)", a tribute to Mercury, which uses Queen's music as a soundtrack for the show's dancers, who interpret the stories behind tracks such as "Bohemian Rhapsody", "Radio Ga Ga", and "Killer Queen". Queen's music also appears in the Off-Broadway production *[Power Balladz](/wiki/Power_Balladz)*, most notably the song "We Are the Champions", with the show's two performers believing the song was "the apex of artistic achievement in its day".

### Software and digital releases

In conjunction with [Electronic Arts](/wiki/Electronic_Arts), Queen released the computer game *[Queen: The eYe](/wiki/Queen:_The_eYe)* in 1998. The game received mixed reviews. Several reviewers described the fight sequences as frustrating, due to unresponsive controls and confusing camera angles. *[PC Zone](/wiki/PC_Zone)* found the game's graphics unimpressive, although *[PC PowerPlay](/wiki/PC_PowerPlay)* considered them "absolutely stunning". The extremely long development time resulted in graphic elements that already seemed outdated by the time of release.

2012 DVD and CD set of *[Hungarian Rhapsody: Queen Live in Budapest](/wiki/Hungarian_Rhapsody:_Queen_Live_in_Budapest)*

Under the supervision of May and Taylor, numerous restoration projects have been under way involving Queen's lengthy audio and video catalogue. DVD releases of their 1986 Wembley concert (titled *[Live at Wembley Stadium](/wiki/Queen_at_Wembley)*), 1982 Milton Keynes concert (*[Queen on Fire – Live at the Bowl](/wiki/Queen_on_Fire_%E2%80%93_Live_at_the_Bowl)*), and two Greatest Video Hits (Volumes 1 and 2, spanning the 1970s and 1980s) have seen the band's music remixed into [5.1](/wiki/Surround_sound) and [DTS](/wiki/DTS_(sound_system)) [surround sound](/wiki/Surround_sound). So far, only two of the band's albums, *A Night at the Opera* and *The Game*, have been fully remixed into high-resolution multichannel surround on [DVD-Audio](/wiki/DVD-Audio). *A Night at the Opera* was re-released with some revised 5.1 mixes and accompanying videos in 2005 for the 30th anniversary of the album's original release (CD+DVD-Video set). In 2007, a Blu-ray edition of Queen's previously released concerts, *[Queen Rock Montreal](/wiki/Queen_Rock_Montreal)* & [Live Aid](/wiki/Live_Aid), was released, marking their first project in 1080p HD.

Queen have been featured multiple times in the *[Guitar Hero](/wiki/Guitar_Hero)* franchise: a cover of "Killer Queen" in the original *[Guitar Hero](/wiki/Guitar_Hero_(video_game))*, "We Are The Champions", "Fat Bottomed Girls", and the Paul Rodgers collaboration "C-lebrity" in a track pack for *[Guitar Hero World Tour](/wiki/Guitar_Hero_World_Tour)*, "Under Pressure" with David Bowie in *[Guitar Hero 5](/wiki/Guitar_Hero_5)*, "I Want It All" in *[Guitar Hero: Van Halen](/wiki/Guitar_Hero:_Van_Halen)*, "Stone Cold Crazy" in *[Guitar Hero: Metallica](/wiki/Guitar_Hero:_Metallica)*, and "Bohemian Rhapsody" in *[Guitar Hero: Warriors of Rock](/wiki/Guitar_Hero:_Warriors_of_Rock)*. On 13 October 2009, Brian May revealed there was "talk" going on "behind the scenes" about a dedicated Queen *Rock Band* game.

Queen have also been featured multiple times in the *Rock Band* franchise: a track pack of 10 songs which are compatible with *Rock Band*, *[Rock Band 2](/wiki/Rock_Band_2)*, and *[Rock Band 3](/wiki/Rock_Band_3)* (three of those are also compatible with *[Lego Rock Band](/wiki/Lego_Rock_Band)*). Their hit "Bohemian Rhapsody" was featured in *Rock Band 3* with full harmony and keys support.
The band also appeared in the video game *[Lego Rock Band](/wiki/Lego_Rock_Band)* as playable Lego avatars. In March 2009, [Sony Computer Entertainment](/wiki/Sony_Computer_Entertainment) released a Queen branded version of the company's karaoke franchise, *[SingStar](/wiki/SingStar)*. The game, which is available on [PlayStation 2](/wiki/PlayStation_2) and [PlayStation 3](/wiki/PlayStation_3), is titled *[SingStar Queen](/wiki/List_of_songs_in_SingStar_games_(PlayStation_2))* and has 25 songs on the PS3 and 20 on the PS2. "We Will Rock You" and other songs by Queen also appear in *[DJ Hero](/wiki/DJ_Hero)*.

### *Bohemian Rhapsody*

Main article: [*Bohemian Rhapsody* (film)](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody_(film))

In a September 2010 BBC interview, Brian May announced that [Sacha Baron Cohen](/wiki/Sacha_Baron_Cohen) was to play Mercury in a biographical film about the band. *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* commented with approval on his singing ability and visual similarity to Mercury. However, in July 2013, Baron Cohen dropped out of the role due to "creative differences" between him and the surviving band members. In December 2013, it was announced that [Ben Whishaw](/wiki/Ben_Whishaw), known for playing [Q](/wiki/Q_(James_Bond)) in the [James Bond film](/wiki/James_Bond_in_film) *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)*, was a possible replacement for Baron Cohen in the role of Mercury, but Whishaw pulled out a few months later amidst uncertainty about how the film was progressing.

The two remaining members of Queen's classic line-up, May and Taylor (pictured in 2017), were creative consultants for *[Bohemian Rhapsody](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody_(film))*.

The project regained momentum in 2016. It was announced on 4 November that the film had secured the backing of [20th Century Fox](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox), [New Regency](/wiki/New_Regency) and [GK Films](/wiki/GK_Films). By this time, the film's working title was *Bohemian Rhapsody*, after the band's [song of the same name](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody_(song)). Freddie Mercury was to be played by [Rami Malek](/wiki/Rami_Malek), and shooting was scheduled to begin in early 2017. The motion picture was written by [Anthony McCarten](/wiki/Anthony_McCarten), with a story by McCarten and [Peter Morgan](/wiki/Peter_Morgan), who received [Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations for his screenplays *[The Queen](/wiki/The_Queen_(2006_film))* and *[Frost/Nixon](/wiki/Frost/Nixon_(film))*.

Released in October 2018, *Bohemian Rhapsody* focuses on Queen's formative years and the period leading up to the celebrated performance at the [1985 Live Aid](/wiki/Live_Aid) concert. The film has grossed over $900 million worldwide, making it the highest-grossing musical biographical film of all time. Despite receiving mixed reviews, it won the [Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama). Malek received wide acclaim and numerous accolades for his portrayal of Mercury, including the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor). The Live Aid segment was praised, but there was criticism from not exploring more complex themes involving Mercury, with the *New York Post'*s Johnny Oleksinski stating, "What we ultimately wanted from Bohemian Rhapsody was not carbon-copied concerts, but behind-closed-doors insight into a deeply private, complicated, internationally beloved superstar." After the release of the film, "Bohemian Rhapsody" re-entered the US *Billboard* Hot 100 for the third time (having previously charted in 1976 and 1992), charting at number 33 on 12 November 2018. *[Bohemian Rhapsody: The Original Soundtrack](/wiki/Bohemian_Rhapsody:_The_Original_Soundtrack)* received an [American Music Award](/wiki/American_Music_Award) for [Top Soundtrack](/wiki/American_Music_Award_for_Top_Soundtrack).

## Legacy

Upon being awarded the [Polar Music Prize](/wiki/Polar_Music_Prize) in 2025, Queen were described as being "a band synonymous with the very fabric of pop culture" who "have made an impact on music that spans decades, generations and genres", and they are "beloved the world over".
In 2002, "Bohemian Rhapsody" was voted "the UK's favourite hit of all time" in a poll conducted by the *[Guinness World Records British Hit Singles Book](/wiki/British_Hit_Singles_%26_Albums)*. In 2004, the song was inducted into the [Grammy Hall of Fame](/wiki/Grammy_Hall_of_Fame). Many scholars consider the "Bohemian Rhapsody" music video groundbreaking, crediting it with popularising the medium. Rock historian Paul Fowles stated that the song is "widely credited as the first global hit single for which an accompanying video was central to the marketing strategy". It has been hailed as paving the way for the [MTV Generation](/wiki/MTV_Generation). In December 2018, "Bohemian Rhapsody" became the most-streamed song from the 20th century, and the most-streamed classic rock song of all time. The song and original video were downloaded more than 1.6 billion times. In March 2021 it was [certified Diamond](/wiki/RIAA_certification#List_of_certifications) in the US for combined digital sales and streams equal to 10 million units. Acclaimed for their [stadium rock](/wiki/Arena_rock), in 2005 an industry poll ranked Queen's performance at Live Aid in 1985 as the best live act in history. In 2007, they were also voted the greatest British band in history by [BBC Radio 2](/wiki/BBC_Radio_2) listeners.

> If you have to go back and watch one performance it has to be Queen. That was not just a career-defining moment ... rock 'n' roll might have peaked right there. Freddie Mercury on stage at Live Aid is the summit of rock 'n' roll. For a band some people said was washed up, that was just the most stunning, breathtaking, flawless, powerful, jubilant performance. It was everything you want in rock 'n' roll.

— 1980s [MTV](/wiki/MTV) VJ [Martha Quinn](/wiki/Martha_Quinn) speaking in July 2015 on the 30th anniversary of Live Aid.

As of 2005, according to the *Guinness Book of World Records*, Queen albums have spent a total of 1,322 weeks (twenty-six years) on the UK Album Charts, more time than any other act. Also in 2005, with the release of their live album with Paul Rodgers, Queen moved into third place on the list of acts with the most aggregate time spent on the British record charts. In 2022, *[Greatest Hits](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Queen_album))* was the [best-selling album](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_albums_in_the_United_Kingdom) in UK chart history, and the only album to sell over seven million copies in the UK. As of August 2024, the album has spent over 600 weeks on the US *Billboard* 200. Their *[Greatest Hits II](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_II_(Queen_album))* album is the UK's tenth best seller, with sales of 3,746,404 copies. Based on record sales, [*Billboard* charts](/wiki/Billboard_charts) performance, online views and popularity on [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify), in 2018 *[Business Insider](/wiki/Business_Insider)* in the US ranked Queen the third most popular rock band of all time, after the Beatles and Led Zeppelin.

Gold and silver discs issued by [EMI](/wiki/EMI) in 1982 for *[Greatest Hits](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Queen_album))* (1981). With more than 25 million copies sold it is Queen's best selling album.

The band have released a total of 18 number-one albums, 18 number-one singles, and 10 number-one DVDs worldwide, making them one of the world's [best-selling music artists](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists). Estimates of their record sales range from 250 million to 300 million worldwide. Inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame) in 2001, the band is the only group in which every member has composed more than one chart-topping single, and all four members were inducted into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame) in 2003. In 2009, "We Will Rock You" and "We Are the Champions" were inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame, and the latter was voted the world's favourite song in a 2005 [Sony Ericsson](/wiki/Sony_Ericsson) global music poll. The band received [Ivor Novello Awards](/wiki/Ivor_Novello_Awards) for Outstanding Contribution to British Music, in 1987, and Outstanding Song Collection, in 2005, from the [British Academy of Songwriters, Composers, and Authors](/wiki/British_Academy_of_Songwriters,_Composers,_and_Authors). In recognition of the vocal harmonies of Mercury, May and Taylor, in 2006 Queen were inducted into the [Vocal Group Hall of Fame](/wiki/Vocal_Group_Hall_of_Fame). In 2018 they were presented the [Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award](/wiki/Grammy_Lifetime_Achievement_Award). In their list for the [Global Recording Artist of the Year](/wiki/Global_Recording_Artist_of_the_Year), the [IFPI](/wiki/International_Federation_of_the_Phonographic_Industry) named Queen the sixth best-selling artist worldwide in 2018, and the fifth best-selling artist in 2019, in both cases the most popular act of their contemporaries. In January 2020, Queen became the first band to join Elizabeth II on a British coin, with the [commemorative £5 coin](/wiki/Commemorative_coins_of_the_United_Kingdom), issued by the [Royal Mint](/wiki/Royal_Mint), featuring the instruments of all four bandmembers. In July 2020, Queen became the third band (after the Beatles and [Pink Floyd](/wiki/Pink_Floyd)) to feature on a [series of UK postage stamps](/wiki/Great_Britain_commemorative_stamps_2020%E2%80%932029#2020) issued by the [Royal Mail](/wiki/Royal_Mail).

Queen are one of the most bootlegged bands ever, according to Nick Weymouth, who manages the band's official website. A 2001 survey discovered the existence of 12,225 websites dedicated to Queen bootlegs, the highest number for any band. Bootleg recordings have contributed to the band's popularity in certain countries where Western music is censored, such as Iran. In a project called *Queen: The Top 100 Bootlegs*, many of these have been made officially available to download for a nominal fee from Queen's website, with profits going to the [Mercury Phoenix Trust](/wiki/The_Mercury_Phoenix_Trust). In 2004, Queen became the first Western rock act to be officially accepted in Iran following the release of their *Greatest Hits* album. *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* ranked Queen at number 52 on its list of the "[100 Greatest Artists of All Time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_100_Greatest_Artists_of_All_Time)", while ranking Mercury the 18th-greatest singer, and May the twenty-sixth-greatest guitarist. *Rolling Stone* readers voted Mercury the second-greatest frontman. Queen were named 13th on [VH1](/wiki/VH1)'s 100 Greatest Artists of Hard Rock list, and in 2010 were ranked 17th on VH1's 100 Greatest Artists of All Time list. In 2012, *[Gigwise](/wiki/Gigwise)* readers named Queen the best band of the past 60 years. Eddie Trunk argued that Queen's importance, in common with [Thin Lizzy](/wiki/Thin_Lizzy) and [Deep Purple](/wiki/Deep_Purple), was "not as fully recognized" in the US because elsewhere they "often played to much bigger crowds in stadium venues". Queen had stopped touring the US in 1982 as their success there had started to wane, but they remained a touring "juggernaut", filling stadiums and arenas internationally in the 1980s until their final tour (with Mercury) in 1986.

### Influence

At 10 years old, [Thom Yorke](/wiki/Thom_Yorke) of [Radiohead](/wiki/Radiohead) built a homemade guitar in an attempt to emulate Brian May.

Queen have been credited with making a significant contribution to genres such as hard rock and heavy metal. In the early 1970s, they helped spur the metal genre's evolution by discarding much of its [blues](/wiki/Blues) influence. Their 1974 song "[Stone Cold Crazy](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Crazy)" has been cited as a precursor of [speed metal](/wiki/Speed_metal). [Metallica](/wiki/Metallica) recorded a cover version of "Stone Cold Crazy", which first appeared on the *[Rubáiyát: Elektra's 40th Anniversary](/wiki/Rub%C3%A1iy%C3%A1t:_Elektra%27s_40th_Anniversary)* album in 1990, and they still performed it live in the 2000s. Swedish guitarist [Yngwie Malmsteen](/wiki/Yngwie_Malmsteen), a pioneer of "[neo-classical metal](/wiki/Neo-classical_metal)", listened to Queen as a child, and spoke of Brian May being "very inventive when it comes to tones and having all those pickup configurations. Brian tends not to stick to just pentatonics, either; he mixes it all up, which is how I like to think, too."

The band have been cited as an influence by many other musicians. Moreover, like their music, the bands and artists that have claimed to be influenced by Queen or have expressed admiration for them are diverse, spanning different generations, countries, and genres, including heavy metal: [Judas Priest](/wiki/Judas_Priest), [Iron Maiden](/wiki/Iron_Maiden), [Dream Theater](/wiki/Dream_Theater), [Trivium](/wiki/Trivium_(band)), [Megadeth](/wiki/Megadeth), [Anthrax](/wiki/Anthrax_(American_band)), [Melvins](/wiki/Melvins), [Slipknot](/wiki/Slipknot_(band)), [Rob Zombie](/wiki/Rob_Zombie), and [Rage Against the Machine](/wiki/Rage_Against_the_Machine); hard rock: [Guns N' Roses](/wiki/Guns_N%27_Roses), [Def Leppard](/wiki/Def_Leppard), [Mötley Crüe](/wiki/M%C3%B6tley_Cr%C3%BCe), [Steve Vai](/wiki/Steve_Vai), [the Cult](/wiki/The_Cult), [the Darkness](/wiki/The_Darkness_(band)), and [Foo Fighters](/wiki/Foo_Fighters); alternative rock: [Nirvana](/wiki/Nirvana_(band)), [Trent Reznor](/wiki/Trent_Reznor) of [Nine Inch Nails](/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails), [Muse](/wiki/Muse_(band)), [Red Hot Chili Peppers](/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers), [Jane's Addiction](/wiki/Jane%27s_Addiction), [the Flaming Lips](/wiki/The_Flaming_Lips), [Kid Rock](/wiki/Kid_Rock), and [Billy Corgan](/wiki/Billy_Corgan) of [the Smashing Pumpkins](/wiki/The_Smashing_Pumpkins); shock rock: [Marilyn Manson](/wiki/Marilyn_Manson_(band)); pop rock: [the Killers](/wiki/The_Killers), [My Chemical Romance](/wiki/My_Chemical_Romance), and [Panic! at the Disco](/wiki/Panic!_at_the_Disco); country: [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill), and [Carrie Underwood](/wiki/Carrie_Underwood); pop: [George Michael](/wiki/George_Michael), [Robbie Williams](/wiki/Robbie_Williams), [Adele](/wiki/Adele), [Lady Gaga](/wiki/Lady_Gaga), and [Katy Perry](/wiki/Katy_Perry); and K-pop: [Psy](/wiki/Psy), and [BTS](/wiki/BTS).

> Musicians have been saying for decades Queen are in the pantheon of all-time greats. In the countless interviews I have done over the years they have been name checked by numerous superstars. What is most impressive though is the diversity of artists they've influenced. Why Queen were able to cut across such an incredibly wide range of musicians is easy to see – they are one of the most versatile bands that rock has ever had.

— Steve Baltin writing for *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* magazine in January 2019: *Queen's Greatest Legacy: Shaping The Last Two Decades Of Pop And Rock Music*.

[Thom Yorke](/wiki/Thom_Yorke) of [Radiohead](/wiki/Radiohead) received his first guitar at seven years old, encouraged after seeing May in a broadcast of a Queen concert. At 10 years old, he made his own homemade guitar, trying to imitate what May had done with his Red Special, but he was not satisfied with the result. Subsequently, Queen became one of the first influences of his band.

# Emmy Rossum

**Emmanuelle Grey Rossum** (born September 12, 1986) is an American actress and singer-songwriter. Her accolades include a [Saturn Award](/wiki/Saturn_Awards) and [Critics' Choice Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Award), with nominations for a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and an [Independent Spirit Award](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Awards).

Born and raised in New York City, Rossum began professionally performing as a child with the [Metropolitan Opera](/wiki/Metropolitan_Opera). Early on-screen roles included *[Genius](/wiki/Genius_(1999_film))* (1999), *[Songcatcher](/wiki/Songcatcher)* (2000), *[Passionada](/wiki/Passionada)* (2002), and *[Nola](/wiki/Nola_(film))* (2003). At sixteen, she was cast in her breakthrough role in *[Mystic River](/wiki/Mystic_River_(film))* (2003). Rossum starred in the 2004 sci-fi film *[The Day After Tomorrow](/wiki/The_Day_After_Tomorrow)*, and she received critical acclaim for her performance in the leading role of [Christine Daaé](/wiki/Christine_Daa%C3%A9) in the film adaptation of *[The Phantom of the Opera](/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_(2004_film))* (2004). She acted in the films *[Poseidon](/wiki/Poseidon_(film))* (2006), *[Dragonball: Evolution](/wiki/Dragonball_Evolution)* (2009), *[Inside](/wiki/Inside_(2011_film))* (2011), *[Beautiful Creatures](/wiki/Beautiful_Creatures_(2013_film))* (2013), *[Comet](/wiki/Comet_(film))* (2014), *[You're Not You](/wiki/You%27re_Not_You)* (2014), and *[Cold Pursuit](/wiki/Cold_Pursuit)* (2019).

In television, Rossum is best known for her portrayal of [Fiona Gallagher](/wiki/List_of_Shameless_(American_TV_series)_characters#Fiona_Gallagher) in the television series *[Shameless](/wiki/Shameless_(U.S._TV_series))* (2011–2019). Since the mid-2010s, she has directed and produced television, including the 2022 [Peacock](/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service)) series *[Angelyne](/wiki/Angelyne_(miniseries))* in which she also stars.

In 2007, Rossum released her debut album, *[Inside Out](/wiki/Inside_Out_(Emmy_Rossum_album))*. She released a Christmas [EP](/wiki/Extended_play) the same year, titled *Carol of the Bells*. In 2013, she released a follow-up album called *[Sentimental Journey](/wiki/Sentimental_Journey_(Emmy_Rossum_album))*.

## Early life

Emmanuelle Grey Rossum was born on September 12, 1986, in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City). She is the only child of Cheryl Rossum, a single mother who worked as a corporate photographer. Her parents separated while her mother was pregnant, and as of 2007, she had met her father only twice.

Rossum's mother is of [Russian Jewish](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia) descent, and her father is [Protestant](/wiki/Protestant) of English and Dutch ancestry. Rossum identifies as Jewish and has stated that her mother instilled in her the "Jewish code of ethics and morals". She was named for her maternal great-grandfather, whose first name was Emanuel, using the feminine spelling.

Upon singing "[Happy Birthday](/wiki/Happy_Birthday_to_You)" in all 12 keys, Rossum was welcomed to join the [Metropolitan Opera](/wiki/Metropolitan_Opera) Children's Chorus by chorus director Elena Doria at age seven. Over the course of five years she sang onstage with the chorus and had the chance to perform with singers such as [Plácido Domingo](/wiki/Pl%C3%A1cido_Domingo) and [Luciano Pavarotti](/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti). For anywhere from $5 to $10 a night, Rossum sang in six languages in 20 operas, including *[La bohème](/wiki/La_boh%C3%A8me)*, *[Turandot](/wiki/Turandot)*, a [Carnegie Hall](/wiki/Carnegie_Hall) presentation of *[La damnation de Faust](/wiki/La_damnation_de_Faust)*, and  *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream_(opera))*. She also worked under the direction of [Franco Zeffirelli](/wiki/Franco_Zeffirelli) in *[Carmen](/wiki/Carmen)*.

An increasing interest in pursuing acting led to taking classes with Flo Salant Greenberg of [The New Actors Workshop](/wiki/The_New_Actors_Workshop) in New York City. She has also worked with acting coach Terry Knickerbocker.

Rossum attended the [Spence School](/wiki/Spence_School), a private school in [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan), before dropping out to pursue career opportunities. She received her high school diploma at 15 years old via online-extension courses offered by [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University)'s [Education Program for Gifted Youth](/wiki/Education_Program_for_Gifted_Youth) (EPGY). She then enrolled at [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University), studying French, art history and philosophy.

## Career

### Acting

Rossum's television debut was in August 1997, as the original [Abigail Williams](/wiki/Abigail_Williams_(As_the_World_Turns)) in the daytime soap opera *[As the World Turns](/wiki/As_the_World_Turns)*. She also had a guest role as Caroline Beels in *[Snoops](/wiki/Snoops_(1999_TV_series))*. Rossum was nominated for a [Young Artist Award](/wiki/Young_Artist_Award) in 1999 for Best Performance in a TV movie for her work in the Disney Channel Original Movie *[Genius](/wiki/Genius_(1999_film))*. She then portrayed a young [Audrey Hepburn](/wiki/Audrey_Hepburn) in the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) television film *[The Audrey Hepburn Story](/wiki/The_Audrey_Hepburn_Story)* (2000).

Rossum made her film debut at 13, in 2000's *[Songcatcher](/wiki/Songcatcher)*, as Deladis Slocumb, an [Appalachian](/wiki/Appalachia) orphan. Debuting at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival), the film won the Special Jury Award for Outstanding Ensemble Performance. For her role, Rossum received an [Independent Spirit Award](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award) nomination for Best Debut Performance and also had the opportunity to sing a duet with [Dolly Parton](/wiki/Dolly_Parton) on the *[Songcatcher](/wiki/Songcatcher)* soundtrack. *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine)) magazine* named Rossum as "One of the Ten to Watch" in 2000.

In *[Nola](/wiki/Nola_(film))* (2003), Rossum played the title character, an aspiring songwriter. In her first major studio film, [Clint Eastwood](/wiki/Clint_Eastwood)'s *[Mystic River](/wiki/Mystic_River_(film))* (2003), Rossum starred as Katie Markum, the ill-fated daughter of small-business owner Jimmy Markum, played by [Sean Penn](/wiki/Sean_Penn).

Rossum had a breakthrough role as Laura Chapman in the sci-fi-disaster film *[The Day After Tomorrow](/wiki/The_Day_After_Tomorrow)* (2004). She later returned to New York, where she was the last to audition, in full costume and make-up, for the role of [Christine Daaé](/wiki/Christine_Daa%C3%A9) in *[The Phantom of the Opera](/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_(2004_film))* (2004), the onscreen adaptation of composer [Andrew Lloyd Webber](/wiki/Andrew_Lloyd_Webber)'s [musical of the same name](/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_(1986_musical)). After having nearly missed the audition due to a family engagement, Rossum was asked to audition in person for Lloyd Webber at his home in New York. For her performance, Rossum received a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nomination for Best Actress in a musical or comedy. She also received a [Critics' Choice Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Awards) for Best Young Actress, along with a [Saturn Award for Best Performance by a Younger Actor](/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Performance_by_a_Younger_Actor).

In 2006, Rossum appeared in *[Poseidon](/wiki/Poseidon_(film))*, [Wolfgang Petersen](/wiki/Wolfgang_Petersen)'s remake of the disaster film *[The Poseidon Adventure](/wiki/The_Poseidon_Adventure_(1972_film))*. She played Jennifer Ramsey, the daughter of [Kurt Russell](/wiki/Kurt_Russell)'s character. Rossum described the character as being proactive and strong in all situations, rather than a [damsel in distress](/wiki/Damsel_in_distress). Rossum also appeared as [Juliet Capulet](/wiki/Juliet_Capulet) in a 2006 [Williamstown Theatre Festival](/wiki/Williamstown_Theatre_Festival) production of [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare)'s *[Romeo and Juliet](/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet)*. In early 2009, Rossum appeared in the poorly received *[Dragonball Evolution](/wiki/Dragonball_Evolution)*. Her next big screen venture was the indie *[Dare](/wiki/Dare_(film))*, which was an official selection of the 2009 [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival). In November 2009, Rossum appeared in Broadway's 24 Hour Plays in which actors, writers, and directors collaborate to produce, and perform six [one-act plays](/wiki/One-act_play) within 24 hours to benefit the Urban Arts Partnership. Rossum appeared in [Warren Leight](/wiki/Warren_Leight)'s "Daily Bread", directed by Lucie Tiberghien.

In December 2009, Rossum joined the cast of the [Showtime](/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network)) [dramedy](/wiki/Dramedy) series *[Shameless](/wiki/Shameless_(U.S._TV_series))*, based on the British series of [the same title](/wiki/Shameless_(UK_TV_series)). The series co-stars [William H. Macy](/wiki/William_H._Macy). Rossum plays the oldest sister of a large, motherless family, functioning as a guardian/surrogate mother to her five younger siblings. The series has received consistent acclaim, with Rossum's performance being universally praised. She made her directorial debut with episode four of season seven, "I Am a Storm." In December 2016, Rossum was in a contract dispute with the show's producers over her desire for a salary equal to that of co-star Macy and back pay for the differential over seven seasons, which was resolved later that month. Following this news, *Shameless* was renewed for its eighth season beginning production in 2017. In August 2018, Rossum announced her departure from *Shameless* after nine seasons.

Rossum in 2011

In mid-2011, Rossum starred in [D. J. Caruso](/wiki/D._J._Caruso)'s [social film](/wiki/Social_film), *[Inside](/wiki/Inside_(2011_film))*; the online film aired in several segments, incorporating multiple social media platforms including Facebook, YouTube, and Twitter. The following year, Rossum played Ridley, a siren Caster in *[Beautiful Creatures](/wiki/Beautiful_Creatures_(2013_film))* (2013), based on the [young adult fantasy novel](/wiki/Young_adult_novel) of the [same name](/wiki/Beautiful_Creatures_(novel)). Although the film was released to mixed reviews, Rossum's performance was praised by critics and fans. She also starred in the drama *[You're Not You](/wiki/You%27re_Not_You)* as Bec, an inexperienced caregiver who looks after and befriends Kate ([Hilary Swank](/wiki/Hilary_Swank)), who suffers from the degenerative disease [ALS](/wiki/ALS). By June 2013, Rossum was cast as the female lead opposite [Justin Long](/wiki/Justin_Long) in the independent romantic comedy *[Comet](/wiki/Comet_(film))*, which was directed by [Sam Esmail](/wiki/Sam_Esmail), to whom she later became engaged. Afterward she appeared in the film *[Before I Disappear](/wiki/Before_I_Disappear)* (2014), based on the [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award)-winning short film *[Curfew](/wiki/Curfew_(2012_film))*.

In 2019, it was announced that Rossum would star in *[Angelyne](/wiki/Angelyne_(miniseries))*, a drama series co-produced by her husband and longtime collaborator [Sam Esmail](/wiki/Sam_Esmail), for [Peacock](/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service)), the new streaming service by [NBC](/wiki/NBC). The [limited series](/wiki/Limited_series_(television)) was her first lead role after *[Shameless](/wiki/Shameless_(American_TV_series))* and was based on *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* senior writer Gary Baum's investigative feature on the LA billboard model [Angelyne](/wiki/Angelyne).

In 2024, Rossum starred alongside [Zoë Winters](/wiki/Zo%C3%AB_Winters) in an [Off-Broadway](/wiki/Off-Broadway) staging of Amy Berryman's play Walden, directed by [Whitney White](/wiki/Whitney_White_(director)). The play was the final production by [Second Stage Theater](/wiki/Second_Stage_Theater) in the Tony Kiser Theater.

### Music

After her role in *The Phantom of the Opera*, Rossum was offered several deals to record Classical music albums, but refused, opting to create an album of contemporary, more mainstream music. She explained it by feeling frustrated because of the lack of "emotional honesty" in music played in radios. Regarding the sound and style of her music, she said, "It's pop music, but not [Britney Spears](/wiki/Britney_Spears) [bubblegum pop](/wiki/Bubblegum_pop). I want it to have a [David Gray](/wiki/David_Gray_(British_musician)) or [Annie Lennox](/wiki/Annie_Lennox) feel. I've been spending up to 12 hours a day in the studio." Rossum cites [Sarah McLachlan](/wiki/Sarah_McLachlan), [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston), [Celine Dion](/wiki/Celine_Dion) and [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill) as some of her influences.
Rossum's album *[Inside Out](/wiki/Inside_Out_(Emmy_Rossum_album))* was produced by Stuart Brawley. It was released on October 23, 2007, and peaked at 199 in the U.S. [*Billboard* charts](/wiki/Billboard_charts). For the promotion of the record, [Geffen Records](/wiki/Geffen_Records) featured the song, "Slow Me Down," as part of the second volume of [Hollywood Records](/wiki/Hollywood_Records)' *Girl Next* compilation album, which was released on July 10, 2007. Later that year, she was chosen as [Yahoo](/wiki/Yahoo)'s "Who's Next" artist of the month and a "One to Watch" by [MSN](/wiki/MSN). In December 2007, Rossum released three Christmas songs on the EP *Carol of the Bells.* It was also this year that Rossum sang the national anthem at the Save Mart 350 NASCAR NEXTEL Cup Series race at Infineon Raceway and performed at the first ever [Perez Hilton](/wiki/Perez_Hilton) Presents event at the [El Rey Theatre](/wiki/El_Rey_Theatre_(Los_Angeles)) in Hollywood. On October 27, 2007, Rossum again sang the national anthem at the [New Jersey Devils](/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils)'s first home game of the [2007–08 NHL season](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_NHL_season), which was also the first game the team played in the newly constructed [Prudential Center](/wiki/Prudential_Center). She also performed at the Hollywood Christmas Celebration at the Grove in Los Angeles and the Lighting of the Great Tree in Atlanta, Georgia.

She joined [Counting Crows](/wiki/Counting_Crows), [Augustana](/wiki/Augustana_(band)), and [Michael Franti & Spearhead](/wiki/Michael_Franti) as a "special guest" for select performances of the "Traveling Circus and Medicine Show" tour in early 2009.

In 2010, Rossum sang a song called "Cruel One" on singer [Alex Band](/wiki/Alex_Band)'s debut solo album *[We've All Been There](/wiki/We%27ve_All_Been_There)*. On the track she sings with Band, and [Chantal Kreviazuk](/wiki/Chantal_Kreviazuk).

Rossum released the [Warner Bros. Records](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records) album *[Sentimental Journey](/wiki/Sentimental_Journey_(Emmy_Rossum_album))* on January 29, 2013. Unlike *Inside Out* on which Rossum wrote the songs herself, the album is a collection of covers of songs from the 1920s to 1960s. *Sentimental Journey* peaked at 92 on the U.S. *Billboard* charts, and number one in Jazz Albums.

Rossum describes herself as a [lyric soprano](/wiki/Lyric_soprano), while saying her voice is still developing. She continues to train vocally at ZajacStudio, a studio run by soprano Joann C. Zajac.

## Charity work and advocacy

Rossum was a [YouthAIDS](/wiki/YouthAIDS) ambassador. In 2008, she was signed as the spokesperson for Pinkitude, an apparel line inspired by the [Pink Panther](/wiki/Pink_Panther_(character)) character, to help raise breast cancer awareness. In 2010, she appeared in public service announcements for the [Natural Resources Defense Council](/wiki/Natural_Resources_Defense_Council) (NRDC) Action Fund. She also works with [Global Green USA](/wiki/Global_Green_USA) to raise money for environmental protection and awareness of ecological issues. In 2015, Rossum campaigned with the [Best Friends Animal Society](/wiki/Best_Friends_Animal_Society) to encourage animal adoption.

On May 26, 2009, Rossum attended a march in [West Hollywood](/wiki/West_Hollywood) California protesting the California Supreme Court's ruling to uphold [Proposition 8](/wiki/Proposition_8).

In February 2024, Rossum was one of 400 celebrities who signed an open letter by [Creative Community for Peace](/wiki/Creative_Community_for_Peace) denouncing attempts to remove [Israel](/wiki/Israel) from Eurovision 2024 amid the [Gaza war](/wiki/Gaza_war).

## Personal life

Rossum and her husband [Sam Esmail](/wiki/Sam_Esmail) in September 2018

Rossum married Justin Siegel in February 2008, although they publicly presented themselves as in an unmarried relationship. 19 months later, in September 2009, he filed for divorce, citing irreconcilable differences. The divorce was finalized on December 28, 2010.

Rossum dated [Counting Crows](/wiki/Counting_Crows) frontman [Adam Duritz](/wiki/Adam_Duritz) from 2009 to 2010.

In 2013, Rossum began dating writer/director [Sam Esmail](/wiki/Sam_Esmail). They got engaged two years later, in August 2015. They married on May 29, 2017, at the [Central Synagogue](/wiki/Central_Synagogue_(Manhattan)) in New York City. The couple has two children: a daughter, born May 2021, and a son, born April 2023.

Rossum has [celiac disease](/wiki/Celiac_disease) and predominantly hyperactive [ADHD](/wiki/ADHD) and has been on medication for the latter since childhood.

The designer [Vera Wang](/wiki/Vera_Wang) was married to Rossum's cousin, investor and real-estate developer [Arthur P. Becker](/wiki/Arthur_P._Becker) from 1989 to 2012.

Rossum has described herself as "[a spiritual person, but not especially religious](/wiki/Spiritual_but_not_religious)."

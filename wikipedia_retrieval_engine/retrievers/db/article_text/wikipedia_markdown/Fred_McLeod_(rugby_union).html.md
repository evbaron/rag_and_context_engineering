**Fred McLeod** (2 May 1939 – 25 December 2019) was a [Scottish](/wiki/Scotland) [rugby union](/wiki/Rugby_union) player. He became the 110th President of the [Scottish Rugby Union](/wiki/Scottish_Rugby_Union).

## Rugby Union career

### Amateur career

McLeod went to Daniel Stewart's College in Edinburgh. He played rugby for the school team.

He then played for [Stewart's College FP](/wiki/Stewart%27s_College_FP). He played for the club for 23 years until in 1970 a detached retina forced him to quit his playing career.

### Administrative career

He became a club selector of [Stewart's College FP](/wiki/Stewart%27s_College_FP) in 1970.

When Stewart's College FP and [Melville College FP](/wiki/Melville_College_FP) merged in 1973 to form [Stewart's Melville](/wiki/Stewart%27s_Melville_RFC), McLeod became Secretary of the new club. He later became President of [Stewart's Melville](/wiki/Stewart%27s_Melville_RFC).

In 1979 he was secretary of the [Co-Optimists](/wiki/Co-Optimist_Rugby_Club). He managed them to the final of the Hong Kong Sevens in 1981 and the quarter-finals in 1986. He also took the side on tour to Zimbabwe and later became a President of the [Co-Optimists](/wiki/Co-Optimist_Rugby_Club).

He was elected to the SRU committee in 1981.

He became SRU Vice-president in 1985.

He became the 110th President of the Scottish Rugby Union. He served one year from [1996 to 1997](/wiki/List_of_Scottish_Rugby_Union_Presidents).

His spell of the Presidency was notable as he had to deal with the new professional era of [rugby union](/wiki/Rugby_union) in Scotland. Despite the SRU professionalising the District sides, the situation was tense as many still argued that the club sides should be turned professional instead.

McLeod was one of the three IRB members that recommended professionalism for the sport in a 1995 report. *The National* newspaper concluded: 'Fred got pelters for that around Murrayfield but stood his ground and was proven correct' and also backed [Jim Telfer](/wiki/Jim_Telfer) in his view that the District sides should be professional and not the club sides.

McLeod also had to deal with England's expulsion from the Five Nations Championship in 1996. This was short-term and England played in the next tournament.

He had to step in as SRU chairman in 2005 when the previous chairman David Mackay and chief executive Phil Anderton were forced out of the post. This lasted until the summer of that year when Allan Munro took over as chairman; and Gordon McKie became the chief executive.

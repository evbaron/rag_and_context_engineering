**NGC 6441**, sometimes also known as the **Silver Nugget Cluster**, is a [globular cluster](/wiki/Globular_cluster) in the southern [constellation](/wiki/Constellation) of [Scorpius](/wiki/Scorpius). It was discovered by the Scottish astronomer [James Dunlop](/wiki/James_Dunlop) on May 13, 1826, who described it as "a small, well-defined rather bright nebula, about 20″ in diameter". The cluster is located 5 [arc minutes](/wiki/Arc_minute) east-northeast of the star [G Scorpii](/wiki/G_Scorpii), and is some 43,000 [light-years](/wiki/Light-year) from the Sun.

This is one of the most massive and luminous globular clusters in the Milky Way, with an estimated 1.6 million [solar masses](/wiki/Solar_mass) of stars. It is located in the [bulge](/wiki/Bulge_(astronomy)) of the galaxy at a distance of 13 kilolight-years (3.9 kpc) from the [core](/wiki/Galactic_Center), and is considered metal "rich". That is, it has a relatively high abundance of elements with higher mass than helium. The core region of the cluster subtends an angle of 0.11 [arc minutes](/wiki/Arc_minute), compared to the half-mass radius of 0.64 arc minutes. The density of stars in the core region is indicated by the luminosity density: 5.25 [L☉](/wiki/Solar_luminosity) pc−3. The cluster has a half-light radius of 7.1 ly (2.18 pc).

This cluster has an abnormally large number of [RR Lyrae variables](/wiki/RR_Lyrae_variable)—68 candidates as of 2006, and their periods are longer than is typical for their respective [metallicities](/wiki/Metallicity). (The mean period for the cluster's RRab stars is 0.759 day.) There are also several [type II Cepheid](/wiki/Type_II_Cepheid) stars, which is unusual given the high metallicity of this cluster. Examination of the [red giant branch](/wiki/Red_giant_branch) section of the [color-magnitude diagram](/wiki/Color-magnitude_diagram) suggests that there are at least two and possibly three distinct populations in the cluster. The brightest and higher temperature members of the [red clump](/wiki/Red_clump) stars are more concentrated toward the center of the cluster. This group may be a helium-enriched second generation of stars.

The cluster contains at least four [millisecond pulsars](/wiki/Millisecond_pulsar), of which two are in [binary systems](/wiki/Binary_star). One of these binaries, PSR J1750−37A, is in a highly eccentric orbit with an [eccentricity](/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity) of 0.71. The cluster has an [X-ray burster](/wiki/X-ray_burster), X1746-370, which has the longest period known in any globular cluster and is consistent with the galaxy as a whole. Finally, there is a [planetary nebula](/wiki/Planetary_nebula), JaFu 2, one of only four planetary nebulas known to inhabit globular clusters in the Milky Way.

## Possible central black hole

According to a study from 2021, the cluster's core may contain an intermediate-mass black hole with a mass of up to 13,200 M⊙.

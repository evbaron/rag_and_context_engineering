This article is about the town. For the unincorporated hamlet within the town, see [Oyster Bay (hamlet), New York](/wiki/Oyster_Bay_(hamlet),_New_York). For other uses, see [Oyster Bay, New York (disambiguation)](/wiki/Oyster_Bay,_New_York_(disambiguation)).

The **Town of Oyster Bay** is the easternmost of the three [towns](/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_New_York#Town) that make up [Nassau County, New York](/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York), United States. Part of the [New York metropolitan area](/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), it is the only town in Nassau County to extend from the [North Shore](/wiki/North_Shore_(Long_Island)) to the [South Shore](/wiki/South_Shore_(Long_Island)) of [Long Island](/wiki/Long_Island). As of the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), it had a population of 301,332, making it the 5th most populous city or town in the state.

There are 18 villages and 18 [hamlets](/wiki/Hamlet_(place)) within the town of Oyster Bay. The [United States Postal Service](/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) has organized these 36 places into 30 five-digit [ZIP Codes](/wiki/ZIP_Code), served by 20 post offices. Each post office shares the name of one of the hamlets or villages, but their boundaries are usually not coterminous.

Oyster Bay is also the name of [a hamlet on the North Shore](/wiki/Oyster_Bay_(hamlet),_New_York), within the town of Oyster Bay. Near this hamlet, in the village of [Cove Neck](/wiki/Cove_Neck,_New_York), is [Sagamore Hill](/wiki/Sagamore_Hill_(house)), the former residence and [summer White House](/wiki/Summer_White_House) of [U.S. president](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Theodore Roosevelt](/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) and now a museum. At least six of the 36 villages and hamlets of the town have shores on [Oyster Bay Harbor](/wiki/Oyster_Bay_(inlet),_New_York), an inlet of [Long Island Sound](/wiki/Long_Island_Sound), and many of these at one time or another have also been referred to as being part of the hamlet of Oyster Bay.

## History

George Bradford Brainerd (American, 1845–1887). *Camp Fire, Oyster Bay, Long Island, ca. 1872–1887*. Collodion silver glass wet plate negative. [Brooklyn Museum](/wiki/Brooklyn_Museum)

Succeeding cultures of indigenous peoples had lived in the area for thousands of years. At the time of European contact, the [Lenape](/wiki/Lenape) (Delaware) nation inhabited western Long Island. By 1600 the band inhabiting the local area was called the [Matinecock](/wiki/Matinecock) after their location, but they were Lenape people.

Following European colonization, the area became part of the colony of [New Netherland](/wiki/New_Netherland). In 1639, the [Dutch West India Company](/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company) made its first purchase of land on Long Island from the local Native Americans. The English also had colonies on Long Island at this time. The Dutch did not dispute English claims to what is now Suffolk County, but when settlers from [New England](/wiki/New_England) arrived in (present-day) Oyster Bay in 1640, they were soon arrested as part of a boundary dispute. In 1643, Englishmen purchased land in the present-day town of [Hempstead](/wiki/Hempstead,_New_York) from the Indians that included land purchased by the Dutch in 1639. Nevertheless, in 1644, the Dutch director granted a patent for Hempstead to the English.

The Dutch also granted other English settlements in [Flushing](/wiki/Flushing,_Queens), [Newtown](/wiki/Elmhurst,_Queens), and [Jamaica](/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens). In 1650, the [Treaty of Hartford](/wiki/Treaty_of_Hartford_(1650)) established a boundary between Dutch and English claims at "Oysterbay", by which the Dutch meant present-day [Cold Spring Harbor](/wiki/Cold_Spring_Harbor,_New_York) (to the east) and the English meant all of the water connected to present-day Oyster Bay Harbor. Meanwhile, the government of England came under the control of [Oliver Cromwell](/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) as a republic, and [smugglers](/wiki/Smuggling) took advantage of the unresolved border dispute. In 1653, English settlers made their first purchase of land in Oyster Bay from the local [Matinecock tribe](/wiki/Lenape), though there were already some rogue English settlements there. For this purchase, the English settlers paid to the Native American Moheness (aka Assiapum), "six kettles, six fathoms of wampum, six hoes, six hatchets, three pairs of stockings, thirty awl-blades or muxes, twenty knives, three shirts and as much Peague as will amount to four pounds sterling." The monarchy was restored in England in 1660, and in 1664 King Charles gave Long Island (and much else) to his brother James, leading to the Dutch relinquishing control of all of New Amsterdam.

In 1667 the settlement at Oyster Bay received its charter from the new English colony of [New York](/wiki/Province_of_New_York), becoming the Township of Oyster Bay. By 1687, the last piece of land was sold by the Indians, and few remained by 1709.

The *[Census of slaves](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/32/Census_of_slaves%2C_1755._%28IA_censusofslaves1700ocal%29.pdf),* conducted in the [Province of New York](/wiki/Province_of_New_York) in 1755, contains a long list of enslaved individuals in Oyster Bay, including the hamlets of [Jericho](/wiki/Jericho,_New_York) and what is now [North Hempstead](/wiki/North_Hempstead,_New_York). It is followed by a remarkable additional list of "free Negroes Melattoes [people of Afro-European ancestry] and Mustees [people of Afro-Indigenous ancestry] Resideing within ye Township of Oysterbay that may probably Be Likely In case of Insurrections To be as Mischevious as ye Slaves." (Free individuals were not supposed to be reported for the Census; a local militia captain supplied it on his own initiative, with the expectation "that ye Other Captains in Oysterbay will acquaint Your Honour [governor of New York] of those Resideing in ye Other parts of ye Township.")

During most of the [American Revolution](/wiki/American_Revolution) the town was under the control of British forces.

The town was originally part of [Queens County](/wiki/Queens), until the western portion of that county was amalgamated into [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) in 1898 and Nassau County was created in 1899. In 1918, [Glen Cove](/wiki/Glen_Cove,_New_York), to the west, incorporated as a city and formed a governing system separate from the town. Following [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), housing replaced farmland as the population grew from about 40,000 in 1950 to more than 290,000 in 1990.

Oyster Bay is home to the [Seawanhaka Corinthian Yacht Club](/wiki/Seawanhaka_Corinthian_Yacht_Club), one of the oldest yacht clubs in the Western Hemisphere, which opened in 1871. There are 40 buildings and sites presently named [Town of Oyster Bay Landmarks](/wiki/List_of_Town_of_Oyster_Bay_Landmarks).

## Geography

Aerial view from a plane after taking off from [LaGuardia](/wiki/LaGuardia_Airport) of Mill Neck Creek and Oyster Bay on the North Shore of Long Island.

The town of Oyster Bay extends from [Long Island Sound](/wiki/Long_Island_Sound) in the north, south to the waters of [South Oyster Bay](/wiki/South_Oyster_Bay) and the Atlantic Ocean. It is bordered by the town of [North Hempstead](/wiki/North_Hempstead,_New_York) on the northwest and the town of [Hempstead](/wiki/Hempstead,_New_York) on the southwest. It is the easternmost of the three towns of Nassau County, with [Suffolk County](/wiki/Suffolk_County,_New_York) immediately to the east.

According to the [United States Census Bureau](/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the town has a total area of 169.5 square miles (439 km2), of which 104.4 square miles (270 km2) is land and 65.1 square miles (169 km2), or 38.42%, is water. As with most of Long Island, the north shore is hilly, the south shore has sandy beaches, and the area between is a [plain](/wiki/Plain).

Between the 1990 Census and the 2000 census, the town exchanged territory with the towns of Hempstead (Nassau County) and [Babylon](/wiki/Babylon_(town),_New_York) (Suffolk County). It also gained territory from the town of [Huntington](/wiki/Huntington,_New_York) in Suffolk County.

### 2019 data

The 2019 [American Community Survey](/wiki/American_Community_Survey) determined the population was 293,576, estimating a 1.6% increase from the [2010 United States census](/wiki/2010_United_States_census). The racial and ethnic makeup of Oyster Bay was 75.5% non-Hispanic White, 2.3% Black or African American, 0.2% American Indian or Alaska Native, 12.5% Asian, 2.0% from two or more races, and 8.1% Hispanic or Latin American of any race.

### 2010 census

As of the 2010 census the population was 85% [White](/wiki/White_(U.S._Census)) (80% [Non-Hispanic White](/wiki/Non-Hispanic_Whites)), 2.3% [Black](/wiki/African_American_(U.S._Census)) or [African American](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)), 0.2% [Native American](/wiki/Native_American_(U.S._Census)), 9.1% [Asian](/wiki/Asian_(U.S._Census)), 0.0% [Pacific Islander](/wiki/Pacific_Islander_(U.S._Census)), 1.9% from [other races](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)), and 1.6% from two or more races. [Hispanic](/wiki/Hispanic_(U.S._Census)) or [Latino](/wiki/Latino_(U.S._Census)) of any race were 7.5% of the population.

As of the [census](/wiki/Census) of 2000, there were 293,925 people, 99,355 households, and 80,278 families residing in the town. The population density was 2,816.2 inhabitants per square mile (1,087.3/km2). There were 101,076 housing units at an average density of 968.4 per square mile (373.9/km2). The racial makeup of the town was 90.83% [White](/wiki/White_(U.S._Census)), 1.64% [Black](/wiki/African_American_(U.S._Census)) or [African American](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)), 0.07% [Native American](/wiki/Native_American_(U.S._Census)), 4.85% [Asian](/wiki/Asian_(U.S._Census)), 0.02% [Pacific Islander](/wiki/Pacific_Islander_(U.S._Census)), 1.36% from [other races](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)), and 1.23% from two or more races. [Hispanic](/wiki/Hispanic_(U.S._Census)) or [Latino](/wiki/Latino_(U.S._Census)) of any race were 5.06% of the population.

There were 99,355 households, out of which 36.0% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 68.9% were married couples living together, 8.9% had a female householder with no husband present, and 19.2% were non-families. 16.1% of all households were made up of individuals, and 8.5% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.93 and the average family size was 3.27.

In the town, the population was spread out, with 24.5% under the age of 18, 6.0% from 18 to 24, 28.7% from 25 to 44, 24.9% from 45 to 64, and 15.9% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 40 years. For every 100 females, there were 94.1 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 90.6 males.

According to a 2007 estimate, the median income for a household in the town was $99,873, and the median income for a family was $115,095. Males had a median income of $60,726 versus $39,420 for females. The [per capita income](/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the town was $35,895. About 2.0% of families and 3.3% of the population were below the [poverty line](/wiki/Poverty_line), including 3.0% of those under age 18 and 4.6% of those age 65 or over.

## Economy

[Aer Lingus](/wiki/Aer_Lingus) operates its United States office in Oyster Bay, centered on the hamlet of [Jericho](/wiki/Jericho,_New_York). [Cablevision Systems](/wiki/Cablevision_Systems), a major cable company in the tri-state area has its corporate headquarters in [Bethpage, New York](/wiki/Bethpage,_New_York), as well as a satellite office in [Jericho, New York](/wiki/Jericho,_New_York) that contains its medium to large business solutions division, [Lightpath](/wiki/Optimum_Lightpath). [Acclaim Entertainment](/wiki/Acclaim_Entertainment) was originally located in the hamlet of [Oyster Bay](/wiki/Oyster_Bay_(hamlet),_New_York). It originally occupied a one-room office in Oyster Bay. At a later time it occupied a brick structure with two stories. In 1994 Acclaim bought a headquarters building in [Glen Cove](/wiki/Glen_Cove,_New_York).

## Education

Both the [State University of New York at Old Westbury](/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_at_Old_Westbury) and [New York Institute of Technology](/wiki/New_York_Institute_of_Technology) or NYIT (and its affiliated [New York Institute of Technology College of Osteopathic Medicine](/wiki/New_York_Institute_of_Technology_College_of_Osteopathic_Medicine)) are located in [Old Westbury](/wiki/Old_Westbury). ## Government

Oyster Bay Town Hall in 2016

The Town of Oyster Bay has a government comprising a town supervisor and a town council consisting of six members. Council members are elected on a town-wide basis, and there are no election districts within the town. Two other elected positions are town clerk and receiver of taxes. At one point, the town had its own police force, but it no longer does.

In New York, a [town](/wiki/Town#New_York) is a major division within a county. Larger towns may contain a number of named [incorporated villages](/wiki/Political_subdivisions_of_New_York_State#Village) that provides numerous local services to the village residents. Towns may contain named [unincorporated hamlets](/wiki/Political_subdivisions_of_New_York_State#Hamlet), governed and administered by the town council.

## Transportation

### Rail lines

The [Hicksville LIRR station](/wiki/Hicksville_station) in 2019

The [Long Island Rail Road](/wiki/Long_Island_Rail_Road)'s [Oyster Bay Branch](/wiki/Oyster_Bay_Branch) serves the town's vicinity from [Glen Head](/wiki/Glen_Head_(LIRR_station)) to [Oyster Bay](/wiki/Oyster_Bay_(LIRR_station)). The [Main Line](/wiki/Main_Line_(Long_Island_Rail_Road)) runs through the center of the town with stations in [Hicksville](/wiki/Hicksville_(LIRR_station)), and [Bethpage](/wiki/Bethpage_(LIRR_station)). The [Port Jefferson Branch](/wiki/Port_Jefferson_Branch) begins at Hicksville, and goes through Hicksville and [Syosset](/wiki/Syosset_(LIRR_station)). Rail freight service also exists along the [Central Branch](/wiki/Central_Branch_(Long_Island_Rail_Road)) which begins in [Bethpage](/wiki/Bethpage_station). Further south in the town, the [Babylon Branch](/wiki/Babylon_Branch) runs from [Seaford](/wiki/Seaford_station_(LIRR)) to the Suffolk County Line with stations in [Massapequa](/wiki/Massapequa_(LIRR_station)) and [Massapequa Park](/wiki/Massapequa_Park_(LIRR_station)).

### Bus service

The Town of Oyster Bay is served primarily by [Nassau Inter-County Express](/wiki/Nassau_Inter-County_Express) bus routes, though some routes from [Suffolk County Transit](/wiki/Suffolk_County_Transit) also enter the town from the east.

**Square Enix Holdings Co., Ltd.** is a Japanese [multinational](/wiki/Multinational_corporation) [holding company](/wiki/Holding_company), [video game publisher](/wiki/Video_game_publisher) and entertainment conglomerate. It releases [role-playing game](/wiki/Role-playing_video_game) franchises, such as *[Final Fantasy](/wiki/Final_Fantasy)*, *[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest)*, and *[Kingdom Hearts](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts)*, among numerous others. Outside of video game publishing and development, it is also in the business of [merchandise](/wiki/Merchandise), [arcade facilities](/wiki/Arcade_game), and [manga](/wiki/Manga) publication under its [Gangan Comics](/wiki/Gangan_Comics) brand.

The original **Square Enix Co., Ltd.** was formed in April 2003 from a [merger](/wiki/Mergers_and_acquisitions) between [Square](/wiki/Square_(video_game_company)) and [Enix](/wiki/Enix), with the latter as the surviving company. Each share of Square's [common stock](/wiki/Common_stock) was [exchanged](/wiki/Stock_swap) for 0.85 shares of Enix's common stock. At the time, 80% of Square Enix staff were made up of former Square employees. As part of the merger, former Square [president](/wiki/President_(corporate_title)) [Yoichi Wada](/wiki/Yoichi_Wada) was appointed the president of the new corporation, while former Enix president Keiji Honda was named vice president. Yasuhiro Fukushima, the largest shareholder of the combined corporation and founder of Enix, became [chairman](/wiki/Chairperson). In October 2008, Square Enix conducted a company split between its corporate business and video game operations, reorganizing itself as the holding company Square Enix Holdings Co., Ltd., while its internally domestic video game operations were formed under the subsidiary Square Enix Co., Ltd. The group operates American, Chinese and European branches, based in [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles), [Beijing](/wiki/Beijing), [Paris](/wiki/Paris), [Hamburg](/wiki/Hamburg), and [London](/wiki/London) respectively.

Several of Square Enix's franchises have sold over 10 million copies worldwide after 2020, with *Final Fantasy* selling 173 million, *Dragon Quest* selling 85 million, and *Kingdom Hearts* shipping 36 million. In 2005, Square Enix acquired arcade corporation [Taito](/wiki/Taito). In 2009, Square Enix acquired [Eidos plc](/wiki/SCi_Games), the parent company of British game publisher [Eidos Interactive](/wiki/Eidos_Interactive), which was then absorbed into its European branch. Square Enix is headquartered at the Shinjuku Eastside Square Building in [Shinjuku](/wiki/Shinjuku), Tokyo, along with a second office at [Osaka](/wiki/Osaka). It has over 5,000 employees worldwide through its base operations and subsidiaries.

## Corporate history

### Origins and pre-merger (1975–2003)

#### Enix (1975–2003)

Main article: [Enix § History](/wiki/Enix#History)

Enix was founded on September 22, 1975, as Eidansha Boshu Service Center by Japanese architect-turned-entrepreneur Yasuhiro Fukushima. Enix focused on publishing games, often by companies who exclusively partnered with the company. In the 1980s, in a partnership with developers [Chunsoft](/wiki/Chunsoft), the company began publishing the *[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest)* series of console games.

Key members of the developer's staff consisted of director [Koichi Nakamura](/wiki/Koichi_Nakamura), writer [Yuji Horii](/wiki/Yuji_Horii), artist [Akira Toriyama](/wiki/Akira_Toriyama), and composer [Koichi Sugiyama](/wiki/Koichi_Sugiyama), among others. The first game, *[Dragon Warrior](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_(video_game))*, in the Famicom-based RPG series, was released in 1986 and would eventually sell 1.5 million copies in Japan, establishing *Dragon Quest* as the company's most profitable franchise. Despite the announcement that Enix's long-time competitor Square would develop exclusively for [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation_(console)), Enix announced in January 1997 that it would release games for both [Nintendo](/wiki/Nintendo) and [Sony](/wiki/Sony) consoles. This caused a significant rise in stock for both Enix and Sony. By November 1999, Enix was listed in the [Tokyo Stock Exchange](/wiki/Tokyo_Stock_Exchange)'s first section, indicating it as a "large company".

#### Square (1983–2003)

Main article: [Square (video game company) § History](/wiki/Square_(video_game_company)#History)

Square was started in October 1983 by [Masafumi Miyamoto](/wiki/Masafumi_Miyamoto) as a computer game software division of Den-Yu-Sha, a [power line](/wiki/Electric_power_transmission) construction company owned by his father. While at the time, game development was usually conducted by only one programmer, Miyamoto believed that it would be more efficient to have graphic designers, programmers and professional story writers working together.

In September 1986, the division was spun off into an independent company led by Miyamoto, officially named Square Co., Ltd. After releasing several unsuccessful games for the [Famicom](/wiki/Famicom), Square relocated to [Ueno, Tokyo](/wiki/Ueno,_Tokyo) in 1987 and developed *[Final Fantasy](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_(video_game))*, a [role-playing video game](/wiki/Role-playing_video_game) inspired by [Enix](/wiki/Enix)'s success in the genre with the 1986 *[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_(video_game))*. *Final Fantasy* was a success with over 400,000 copies sold, and it became Square's leading franchise, spawning dozens of games in a series that continues to the present.

Buoyed by the success of their *Final Fantasy* franchise, Square developed notable games and franchises such as *[Chrono](/wiki/Chrono_(series)),* *[Mana](/wiki/Mana_(series))*, *[Kingdom Hearts](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts)* (in collaboration with [The Walt Disney Company](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company)), and *[Super Mario RPG](/wiki/Super_Mario_RPG)* (in collaboration with [Nintendo](/wiki/Nintendo)). By late 1994 they had developed a reputation as a producer of high-quality role-playing video games. Square was one of the many companies that had planned to develop and publish its games for the [Nintendo 64](/wiki/Nintendo_64), but with the cheaper costs associated with developing games on CD-based consoles such as the [Sega Saturn](/wiki/Sega_Saturn) and the [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation_(console)), Square decided to develop titles for the latter system. *[Final Fantasy VII](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII)* was one of these games, and it sold 9.8 million copies, making it the second-best-selling game for the PlayStation.

### Merger (2003)

A merger between Square and Enix had been considered since at least 2000; the financial failure in 2001 of Square's first film, *[Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within](/wiki/Final_Fantasy:_The_Spirits_Within)*, made Enix reluctant to proceed while Square suffered record losses. With the company facing its second year of financial losses, Square approached Sony for a capital injection, and on October 8, 2001, Sony purchased an 18.6% stake in Square. Following the success of both *Final Fantasy X* and *Kingdom Hearts*, the company's finances stabilized, and it recorded the highest operating margin in its history in the fiscal year 2002. It was announced on November 25, 2002, that Square and Enix's previous plans to merge were to officially proceed, intending to decrease development costs and to compete with foreign developers. As described by Square's president and CEO Yoichi Wada: "Square has also fully recovered, meaning this merger is occurring at a time when both companies are at their height."

Some shareholders expressed concerns about the merger, notably Miyamoto (the founder and largest shareholder of Square), who would find himself holding a significantly smaller percentage of the combined companies. Other criticism came from Takashi Oya of Deutsche Securities, who expressed doubts about the benefits of such a merger: "Enix outsources game development and has few in-house creators, while Square does everything by itself. The combination of the two provides no negative factors but would bring little in the way of operational synergies." Miyamoto's concerns were eventually resolved by altering the exchange ratio of the merger so that each Square share would be exchanged for 0.85 Enix shares rather than 0.81 shares, and the merger was greenlit. The merger was set for April 1, 2003, on which date the newly merged entity Square Enix came into being. At the time of the merger, 80% of Square Enix staff were made up of former Square employees. As part of the merger, former Square president Yoichi Wada was appointed the president of the new corporation, while former Enix president Keiji Honda became its vice president. The founder of Enix and the largest [shareholder](/wiki/Shareholder) of the newly combined corporation, Yasuhiro Fukushima, was made its honorary chairman.

As a result of the merger, Enix was the surviving company and Square Co., Ltd. was dissolved. In July of that year, the Square Enix headquarters were moved to Yoyogi, Shibuya, Tokyo, to help combine the two companies.

### Post-merger and acquisitions (2003–2013)

To strengthen its wireless market, Square Enix acquired mobile application developer UIEvolution in March 2004, which was sold in December 2007, and the company instead founded its own Square Enix MobileStudio in January 2008 to focus on mobile products. In January 2005, Square Enix founded Square Enix China, expanding its interests in the People's Republic of China.

In September 2005, Square Enix bought the gaming developer and publisher [Taito](/wiki/Taito), renowned for their arcade hits such as *[Space Invaders](/wiki/Space_Invaders)* and the *[Bubble Bobble](/wiki/Bubble_Bobble)* series; Taito's home and portable console games divisions were merged into Square Enix itself by March 2010. In August 2008, Square Enix made plans for a similar expansion by way of a friendly takeover of video game developer [Tecmo](/wiki/Tecmo) by purchasing shares at a 30 percent premium, but Tecmo rejected the proposed takeover. Tecmo would later merge with [Koei](/wiki/Koei) in April 2009 to form [Koei Tecmo](/wiki/Koei_Tecmo). In April 2007, Square Enix Ltd. CEO John Yamamoto also became CEO of Square Enix, Inc. In 2008–2009, Square Enix was reportedly working with [Grin](/wiki/Grin_(company)) on a *Final Fantasy* spin-off codenamed *[Fortress](/wiki/Fortress_(cancelled_video_game))*. The project was allegedly canceled by Square Enix after introducing seemingly impossible milestones and without payments made, resulting in Grin declaring bankruptcy and its co-founders blaming Square Enix for being "betrayed". On January 8, 2009, Square Enix signed an agreement with Ubisoft where the former would work to assist the latter in distributing its video games in Japan.

In February 2009, Square Enix announced a takeover deal for [Eidos](/wiki/SCi_Games) (formerly SCi Entertainment), the [holding company](/wiki/Holding_company) for [Eidos Interactive](/wiki/Eidos_Interactive). The UK-based publisher's assets include *[Tomb Raider](/wiki/Tomb_Raider)*, *[Hitman](/wiki/Hitman_(franchise))*, *[Deus Ex](/wiki/Deus_Ex)*, *[Thief](/wiki/Thief_(series))*, and *[Legacy of Kain](/wiki/Legacy_of_Kain)* franchises, along with subsidiary development studios Crystal Dynamics, Eidos-Montréal and [IO Interactive](/wiki/IO_Interactive) that developed the games. The acquisition of Eidos was completed in April 2009, and in November it was merged with Square Enix's European publishing organization, business unit [Square Enix Europe](/wiki/Square_Enix_Europe). Eidos' US operations were merged with Square Enix Incorporated. In April 2010, a new Japanese label for Western games bearing [CERO](/wiki/Computer_Entertainment_Rating_Organization) restrictions called *Square Enix Extreme Edges* was announced. In July 2010, Mike Fischer was appointed CEO of Square Enix, Inc. Square Enix founded the mobile development studio Hippos Lab in March 2011 and [Square Enix Montréal](/wiki/Square_Enix_Montr%C3%A9al) in 2012. In May 2011, [VG24/7](/wiki/VG24/7) reported [Stainless Games](/wiki/Stainless_Games) had purchased the rights to *[Carmageddon](/wiki/Carmageddon)* from Square Enix. In July 2011, it was reported that Square Enix closed its Los Angeles Studio. In January 2012, Square Enix North American office could pursue smaller niche, mobile and social media games due to its existing revenue streams. In October 2012, Square Enix was perceived as a "force in mobile" games by *[Kotaku](/wiki/Kotaku)*. The price of *Final Fantasy Dimensions* and *Demons' Score*, $30 and $44 respectively, was criticized.

### Restructuring (2013)

On March 26, 2013, citing sluggish sales of major Western games, Square Enix announced major restructuring, expected loss of ¥10 billion and resignation of President Yoichi Wada, whom Yosuke Matsuda replaced. Phil Rogers was elected as a new Director, among others. With the restructuring, Square Enix of America CEO Mike Fischer left the company in May, with former Square Enix Europe CEO Phil Rogers becoming CEO of Americas and Europe. Further executive changes at Square Enix Western studios were mentioned in a statement. Square Enix Europe was hit with layoffs and Life President [Ian Livingstone](/wiki/Ian_Livingstone) departed from the company in September 2013.

It said with the fiscal year report in March 2013, sales of *Tomb Raider* (2013) and *Hitman: Absolution* were weak, despite critical acclaim. The North American sales force was said to be ineffective and price pressure was intense. Matsuda noted the long development time of its important games and said it needs to shift to a business model with frequent customer interactions, noting [Kickstarter](/wiki/Kickstarter) as an example.

### Post-restructuring and RPG development (2013–2021)

In March 2013, Square Enix India opened in Mumbai; however the office was closed in April 2014 and reopened five years later. As well as Square Enix Latin America in Mexico, which was closed in 2015. A mobile studio called Smileworks was founded in Indonesia in June 2013; however it was closed in January 2015. In 2014, [Square Enix Collective](/wiki/Square_Enix_Collective) launched, an indie developer service provider headed by Phil Elliot. Also in 2014, Square Enix signed a [strategic alliance](/wiki/Strategic_alliance) and cooperation with Japanese and French video game companies, [Bandai Namco Entertainment](/wiki/Bandai_Namco_Entertainment) and [Ubisoft](/wiki/Ubisoft); it has served as the Japanese publisher of video games and crossover productions since 2009.

In March 2014, following the success of *[Bravely Default](/wiki/Bravely_Default)*, Square Enix said it will "go back to their roots" and focus on creating content that will appeal to its core audience. Karl Stewart, vice president of strategic marketing at Square Enix for North America and Europe, left the company that month. In 2015, Square created a new studio known as [Tokyo RPG Factory](/wiki/Tokyo_RPG_Factory) to develop what was then dubbed *[Project Setsuna](/wiki/I_Am_Setsuna).* Around 2015, Square Enix's Western divisions began "officially working across LA and London".

In January 2017, Norwegian studio Artplant purchased former Eidos franchise [Project I.G.I.](/wiki/Project_I.G.I.) On February 21, 2017, the formation of a new studio Studio Istolia was announced. The studio, headed by [Hideo Baba](/wiki/Hideo_Baba), would be working on the new RPG *Project Prelude Rune*. In November 2017, IO Interactive conducted a management buyout from Square Enix and the Hitman IP was transferred to the studio. In September 2018, [COO](/wiki/Chief_operating_officer) Mike Sherlock died, with Square Enix's executive team assuming his immediate responsibilities. In 2018, Square Enix branded its third party publishing division [Square Enix External Studios](/wiki/Square_Enix_External_Studios), which is headed by Jon Brooke and Lee Singleton. John Heinecke was appointed as [CMO](/wiki/Chief_marketing_officer) for Americas and Europe in October 2018.

Baba departed the studio in early 2019, and shortly after this, Studio Istolia was closed, and *Project Prelude Rune* cancelled following an assessment of the project, with its staff being reassigned to different projects within the company. In 2019, Square Enix opened an Indian office again, now in [Bangalore](/wiki/Bangalore), which expanded into publishing mobile games for the Indian market in 2021. In June 2020, Square Enix donated $2.4 million to charities around its Western studios and offices for the [Black Lives Matter](/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) cause and [COVID-19](/wiki/COVID-19), which were partially raised from sales of its discounted Square Enix Eidos Anthology bundle. In March 2021, Forever Entertainment, a Polish studio, was reported to be working to bring several of Square Enix's properties to modern systems. A new mobile studio called Square Enix London Mobile, working on *[Tomb Raider Reloaded](/wiki/Tomb_Raider_Reloaded)* and an unannounced title based on [*Avatar: The Last Airbender*](/wiki/Avatar:_The_Last_Airbender_(franchise)) with Navigator Games, was announced on October 20, 2021.

### Divestment of studios and business changes (2022–present)

In March 2022, Square Enix announced that it would donate $500,000 to the [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations) fund for Ukrainian refugees in the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).

On May 1, 2022, Square Enix announced that it would sell several assets of subsidiary [Square Enix Limited](/wiki/Square_Enix_Europe) to Swedish games holding company [Embracer Group](/wiki/Embracer_Group) for $300 million. This included studios [Crystal Dynamics](/wiki/Crystal_Dynamics), [Eidos-Montréal](/wiki/Eidos-Montr%C3%A9al), and [Square Enix Montreal](/wiki/Square_Enix_Montreal), IPs *[Deus Ex](/wiki/Deus_Ex)*, *[Legacy of Kain](/wiki/Legacy_of_Kain)*, *[Thief](/wiki/Thief_(series))*, and *[Tomb Raider](/wiki/Tomb_Raider)* and rights to "over 50 games". Square Enix stated that the sale will further help it in investment into [blockchain](/wiki/Blockchain) and other technologies, and to "assist the company in adapting to the changes underway in the global business environment by establishing a more efficient allocation of resources". Square Enix also stated that it would retain the *[Life Is Strange](/wiki/Life_Is_Strange)*, *[Outriders](/wiki/Outriders_(video_game))*, and *[Just Cause](/wiki/Just_Cause_(video_game_series))* franchises. However, during the Japanese publisher's full-year financial results briefing on May 13, president Yosuke Matsuda clarified the past statement and said the money from the sale will be used to strengthen the company's core games business. On July 25, 2022, Square Enix launched the English version of *[Manga Up!](/wiki/Manga_Up!).* The acquisition was closed by August 26, 2022, with the assets being held under CDE Entertainment which is headed from London by Phil Rogers, former CEO of Square Enix Americas and Europe.

In the company's financial statement for the following quarter, released in September 2022, Matsuda said it was moving away from outright owning studios due to rising costs of development, but were looking at means to invest in studios such as joint ventures or investment opportunities. In 2022, Square Enix invested in seven business strategic cooperations in the [blockchain](/wiki/Blockchain) and [cloud services](/wiki/Cloud_computing) such as Zebedee (United States), Blocklords (Estonia), Cross The Ages (France), Blacknut (France), [Animoca Brands](/wiki/Animoca_Brands)-owned [The Sandbox](/wiki/The_Sandbox_(2012_video_game)) (Australia and Hong Kong), and Ubitus (Japan).

On February 28, 2023, Square Enix Holdings announced that on May 1, [Luminous Productions](/wiki/Luminous_Productions) would reorganize and merge with Square Enix internally, citing the merging of the two would "enhance the group's abilities to develop HD games" for the 20th anniversary. On March 3, Square Enix issued a statement announcing a proposed change to the position of its president and representative director that, if implemented, would result in Yosuke Matsuda stepping down and being succeeded by Takashi Kiryu, who is presently the company's director. The change will become effective upon approval at the company's 43rd annual shareholders' meeting, which is planned for June 2023, and the board meeting which will follow ahead on the 20th anniversary of the merger. Kiryu succeeded on May 18 and was seen as part of the *[Final Fantasy XVI](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XVI)* launch event as one of his first appearances in public.

In March 2024, Square Enix announced it would be more selective with the games it develops, resulting in numerous unannounced titles being cancelled. The company lost ¥22.1 billion (approximately $140 million) due to "content abandonment", it is now making the third installment of the *[Final Fantasy VII Remake](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII_Remake)* its full focus after the release of *[Rebirth](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII_Rebirth)*.

## Corporate structure

On October 1, 2008, Square Enix transformed into a [holding company](/wiki/Holding_company) and was renamed Square Enix Holdings. At the same time, the development and publishing businesses were transferred to a spin-off company named Square Enix, sharing the same corporate leadership and offices with the holding company. The primary offices for Square Enix and Square Enix Holdings are in the Shinjuku Eastside Square Building in [Shinjuku](/wiki/Shinjuku), Tokyo.

Currently, focusing in different industries, the company is divided as the following: Five Creative Business Units for game development and production in Square Enix Co., Ltd; a dedicated publishing business unit for manga and books publishing; a digital storefront business division for its e-Store and merchandise production; its media and arts business unit for music production, concert and live performance coordination, and visual contents production (live action, animation, and CG for TV, movies, and games); and a blockchain business division.

### Development organization

After the merger in 2003, Square Enix's development department was organized into eight Square and two Enix Product Development Divisions (開発事業部, *kaihatsu jigyōbu*), each focused on different groupings of games. The divisions were spread around different offices; for example, Product Development Division 5 had offices both in Osaka and Tokyo.

According to Yoichi Wada, the development department was reorganized away from the Product Development Division System by March 2007 into a project-based system. Until 2013, the teams in charge of the *[Final Fantasy](/wiki/Final_Fantasy)* and *[Kingdom Hearts](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts)* series were still collectively referred to as the 1st Production Department (第1制作部, *dai-ichi seisakubu*). The 1st Production Department was formed from the fall 2010 combination of Square Enix's Tokyo and Osaka development studios, with [Shinji Hashimoto](/wiki/Shinji_Hashimoto) as its corporate executive.

In December 2013, Square Enix's development was restructured into 12 Business Divisions. In 2017, Business Division 9 was merged into Business Division 8, while Business Divisions 11 and 12 merged to become the new Business Division 9, while a new Business Division 11 was created with some staff from Business Division 6.

In 2019, Square Enix announced that all of its Business Divisions would be consolidated into four Creative Business Units. Creative Business Unit 1 was led by [Yoshinori Kitase](/wiki/Yoshinori_Kitase), Creative Business Unit 2 was led by Yu Miyake, Creative Business Unit 3 was led by [Naoki Yoshida](/wiki/Naoki_Yoshida) and Creative Business Unit 4 was led by Kei Hirono.

In April 2024, Square Enix restructured its development operations moving away from the Creative Business Unit structure into a new Creative Studio structure.

- **Creative Studio 1** is led by executive officer Yoshinori Kitase and studio head [Naoki Hamaguchi](/wiki/Naoki_Hamaguchi). They are responsible for various projects including the *[Final Fantasy VII](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII)* and *[Kingdom Hearts](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts)* series.
- **Creative Studio 2** is led by executive officer and studio head Yoshinori Kitase and deputy studio heads [Motomu Toriyama](/wiki/Motomu_Toriyama) and Shinpei Yashima. They are responsible for various projects including [SaGa](/wiki/SaGa) titles.
- **Creative Studio 3** is led by executive officer and studio head Naoki Yoshida. They are responsible for various titles including *Final Fantasy XIV* and *Final Fantasy XVI*.
- **Creative Studio 4** is led by executive officer Yosuke Saito.
- **Creative Studio 5** is led by executive officer and studio head Tomoya Asano and deputy studio head and senior manager Masashi Takahashi. They are responsible for HD-2D games, the *Bravely* series, *Dragon Quest* titles and the *Paranormasight* series.

### Business model

See also: [Localization of Square Enix video games](/wiki/Localization_of_Square_Enix_video_games)

The [business model](/wiki/Business_model) of post-merger Square Enix is centered on the idea of "polymorphic content", which consists of developing franchises on multiple potential media rather than being restricted by a single gaming platform. An early example of this strategy is Enix's *[Fullmetal Alchemist](/wiki/Fullmetal_Alchemist)* manga series, which has been adapted into two anime television series, five movies (two animated, three live-action), and several novels and video games. Other polymorphic projects include the *[Compilation of Final Fantasy VII](/wiki/Compilation_of_Final_Fantasy_VII)*, *[Code Age](/wiki/Code_Age)*, *[World of Mana](/wiki/World_of_Mana)*, *[Ivalice Alliance](/wiki/Ivalice_Alliance)*, and *[Fabula Nova Crystallis Final Fantasy](/wiki/Fabula_Nova_Crystallis_Final_Fantasy)* subseries. According to [Yoichi Wada](/wiki/Yoichi_Wada), "It's very difficult to hit the jackpot, as it were. Once we've hit it, we have to get all the juice possible out of it". Similar to Sony's [Greatest Hits](/wiki/List_of_Sony_Greatest_Hits_games) program, Square Enix also re-releases its best-selling games at a reduced price under a label designated "Ultimate Hits".

The standard game design model Square Enix employs is to establish the plot, characters, and art of the game first. Battle systems, field maps, and [cutscenes](/wiki/Cutscenes) are created next. According to [Taku Murata](/wiki/Taku_Murata), this process became the company's model for development after the success of Square's *Final Fantasy VII* in 1997. The team size for *[Final Fantasy XIII](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIII)* peaked at 180 artists, 30 programmers, and 36 game designers, but analysis and restructuring were done to outsource large-scale development in the future.

## Business

### Video games and franchises

Main articles: [List of Square Enix video games](/wiki/List_of_Square_Enix_video_games), [List of Square Enix Europe games](/wiki/List_of_Square_Enix_Europe_games), [List of Square Enix mobile games](/wiki/List_of_Square_Enix_mobile_games), [List of Taito games](/wiki/List_of_Taito_games), and [List of Square Enix video game franchises](/wiki/List_of_Square_Enix_video_game_franchises)

Square Enix's primary concentration is on video gaming, and it is primarily known for its role-playing video game franchises. Of its properties, the *[Final Fantasy](/wiki/Final_Fantasy)* franchise, begun in 1987, is the best-selling, with worldwide sales exceeding 173 million units as of March 2022. The *[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest)* franchise, whose first title was introduced in 1986, is also a best-seller; it is considered one of the most popular game series in Japan and has sold over 85 million units globally. More recently, the *[Kingdom Hearts](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts)* series (developed in collaboration with [Disney](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company) beginning in 2002) has become popular, with 36 million units shipped as of March 2022. Other popular series developed by Square Enix include the *[SaGa](/wiki/SaGa)* series with nearly 10 million copies sold since 1989, the *[Mana](/wiki/Mana_(series))* series with over 6 million sales since 1991, and the *[Chrono](/wiki/Chrono_(series))* series with over 5 million sold since 1995. In addition to its sales numbers, many Square Enix games have been highly reviewed; 27 Square Enix games were included in *[Famitsu](/wiki/Famitsu)* magazine's 2006 "Top 100 Games Ever", with 7 in the top 10 and *[Final Fantasy X](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_X)* claiming the number 1 position. The company also won [IGN](/wiki/IGN)'s award for Best Developer of 2006 for the PlayStation 2.

Square and Enix initially targeted Nintendo home consoles with its games, but Square Enix currently develops games for a wide variety of systems. In the [seventh generation of video game consoles](/wiki/History_of_video_game_consoles_(seventh_generation)), Square Enix released new installments from its major series across all three major systems, including *[Final Fantasy XIII](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIII)* on both the [PlayStation 3](/wiki/PlayStation_3) and [Xbox 360](/wiki/Xbox_360) and *[Dragon Quest X](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_X)* on the [Wii](/wiki/Wii). Square Enix has also developed titles for [handheld game consoles](/wiki/Handheld_game_console), including the [Game Boy Advance](/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance), [Nintendo DS](/wiki/Nintendo_DS), [PlayStation Portable](/wiki/PlayStation_Portable), [Nintendo 3DS](/wiki/Nintendo_3DS), and [PlayStation Vita](/wiki/PlayStation_Vita). Also, it has published games for [Microsoft Windows](/wiki/Microsoft_Windows)-based personal computers and various models of mobile phones and modern smartphones. Square Enix mobile phone games became available in 2004 on the [Vodafone](/wiki/Vodafone) network in some European countries, including Germany, the United Kingdom, Spain, and Italy.

Before its launch, Michihiro Sasaki, senior vice president of Square Enix, spoke about the [PlayStation 3](/wiki/PlayStation_3), saying, "We don't want the PlayStation 3 to be the overwhelming loser, so we want to support them, but we don't want them to be the overwhelming winner either, so we can't support them too much." Square Enix continued to reiterate its devotion to multi-platform publishing in 2007, promising more support for the North American and European gaming markets where console pluralism is generally more prevalent than in Japan. Their interest in multi-platform development was made evident in 2008 when the previously [PlayStation 3](/wiki/PlayStation_3)-exclusive game *[Final Fantasy XIII](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIII)* was announced for release on the [Xbox 360](/wiki/Xbox_360).

In 2008, Square Enix released its first game for the [iPod](/wiki/IPod), *[Song Summoner: The Unsung Heroes](/wiki/Song_Summoner:_The_Unsung_Heroes)*. Square Enix made a new brand for younger children gaming that same year, known as Pure Dreams. Pure Dreams' first two games, *[Snoopy DS: Let's Go Meet Snoopy and His Friends!](/wiki/Snoopy_DS:_Let%27s_Go_Meet_Snoopy_and_His_Friends!)* and *Pingu's Wonderful Carnival*, were released that year. After acquiring Eidos in 2009, Square Enix combined it with its European publishing wing to create [Square Enix Europe](/wiki/Square_Enix_Europe), which continues to publish Eidos franchises such as *[Tomb Raider](/wiki/Tomb_Raider)* (88 million sales), *[Deus Ex](/wiki/Deus_Ex_(series))* (4 million), *[Thief](/wiki/Thief_(series))* and *[Legacy of Kain](/wiki/Legacy_of_Kain)* (3.5 million). Square Enix were served as the Japanese publisher for [Activision Blizzard](/wiki/Activision_Blizzard) and [Ubisoft](/wiki/Ubisoft) from 2009 to 2024 (followed by the [Microsoft acquisition](/wiki/Acquisition_of_Activision_Blizzard_by_Microsoft) and [Ubisoft's partnership](/wiki/Ubisoft#Financial_concerns_(2023–present)) with [Tencent](/wiki/Tencent) and [Sega Sammy Holdings](/wiki/Sega_Sammy_Holdings)). In May 2022, Square Enix sold several assets of Square Enix Europe $300 million to Embracer Group, including former Eidos Interactive franchises such as *[Tomb Raider](/wiki/Tomb_Raider),* *[Deus Ex](/wiki/Deus_Ex),* *[Thief](/wiki/Thief_(series)),* *[Legacy of Kain](/wiki/Legacy_of_Kain)* and more than 50 others.

Square Enix owned franchises and games include:

- former Square franchises, such as *[Mana](/wiki/Seiken_Densetsu)*;
- former Enix franchises, such as *[Star Ocean](/wiki/Star_Ocean)*;
- Square Enix created franchises, such as *[Drakengard](/wiki/Drakengard)*;
- Quest franchises, such as *[Ogre](/wiki/Ogre_Battle)*;
- Taito franchises, such as *[Space Invaders](/wiki/Space_Invaders)*;
- Retained former Crystal Dynamics franchise, such as *[Gex](/wiki/Gex_(video_game_series))*;
- Retained former Eidos Interactive franchise, such as *[Just Cause](/wiki/Just_Cause_(video_game_series))*;
- Square Enix America created games, such as *[Quantum Conundrum](/wiki/Quantum_Conundrum)*;
- Square Enix Europe created franchises, such as *[Life Is Strange](/wiki/Life_Is_Strange)*.

### Game technology

In 2004, Square Enix began to work on a "common 3D format" that would allow the entire company to develop titles without being restricted to a specific platform: this led to the creation of a [game engine](/wiki/Game_engine) named [Crystal Tools](/wiki/Crystal_Tools), which is compatible with the [PlayStation 3](/wiki/PlayStation_3), the [Xbox 360](/wiki/Xbox_360), [Windows](/wiki/Microsoft_Windows)-based [PCs](/wiki/Personal_computer), and to some extent the [Wii](/wiki/Wii). It was first shown off at a tech demo shown off at E3 2005 and was later used for *Final Fantasy XIII* based on the demo's reception. Crystal Tools was also used for *Final Fantasy Versus XIII* before its re-branding as *[Final Fantasy XV](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XV)* and its shift onto next-gen platforms. Refinement of the engine continued through the development of *Final Fantasy XIII-2*, and it underwent a major overhaul for *[Lightning Returns: Final Fantasy XIII](/wiki/Lightning_Returns:_Final_Fantasy_XIII)*. Since that release, no new titles have been announced using Crystal Tools, and it is believed that the development of the engine has halted permanently.

[Luminous Engine](/wiki/Luminous_Engine) was originally intended for [eighth-generation consoles](/wiki/Eighth_generation_of_video_game_consoles) and unveiled at E3 2012 through a tech demo titled *Agni's Philosophy*. The first major console title to be developed with Luminous Engine was *Final Fantasy XV*; the engine's development was done in tandem with the game, and the game's development helped the programming team optimize the engine.

In addition, Square Enix uses commercial engines, including [Epic Games](/wiki/Epic_Games)' [Unreal Engine 3](/wiki/Unreal_Engine_3) (such as *[The Last Remnant](/wiki/The_Last_Remnant)*), [Unreal Engine 4](/wiki/Unreal_Engine_4) (*such as [Dragon Quest XI](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_XI)*, *[Kingdom Hearts III](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts_III)*, *[Final Fantasy VII Remake](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII_Remake))* and [Unity](/wiki/Unity_(game_engine)) (such as *[I Am Setsuna](/wiki/I_Am_Setsuna)*, *[Lost Sphear](/wiki/Lost_Sphear)*, *[SaGa: Scarlet Grace](/wiki/SaGa:_Scarlet_Grace)*).

### Online gaming

Before the merger, Enix published its first online game *[Cross Gate](/wiki/Cross_Gate)* in Japan, mainland China, and Taiwan in 2001, and Square released *[Final Fantasy XI](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XI)* in Japan in 2002 for the PlayStation 2 and later the personal computer. With the huge success of *Final Fantasy XI*, the game was ported to the [Xbox 360](/wiki/Xbox_360) two years later and was the first [MMORPG](/wiki/MMORPG) on the console. All versions of the game used [PlayOnline](/wiki/PlayOnline), a cross-platform internet gaming platform and internet service developed by Square Enix. The platform was used as the online service for many games Square Enix developed and published throughout the decade. Due to the success of its [MMORPG](/wiki/Massively_multiplayer_online_role-playing_game), Square Enix began a new project called *[Fantasy Earth: The Ring of Dominion](/wiki/Fantasy_Earth:_ZERO)*. GamePot, a Japanese game portal, received the license to publish *Fantasy Earth* in Japan, and it was released in Japan as "Fantasy Earth ZERO". In 2006, however, Square Enix dropped the *Fantasy Earth Zero* project and sold it to GamePot. Square Enix released *[Concerto Gate](/wiki/Concerto_Gate)*, the sequel to *Cross Gate*, in 2007.

A next-gen MMORPG code named *Rapture* was developed by the *[Final Fantasy XI](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XI)* team using the company's [Crystal Tools](/wiki/Crystal_Tools) engine. It was unveiled at [E3 2009](/wiki/E3_2009) as *[Final Fantasy XIV](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XIV_(2010_video_game))* for PlayStation 3 and Microsoft Windows and would be released on September 30, 2010. *[Dragon Quest X](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_X:_Mezameshi_Itsutsu_no_Shuzoku_Online)* was announced in September 2011 as an MMORPG being developed for [Nintendo](/wiki/Nintendo)'s [Wii](/wiki/Wii) and [Wii U](/wiki/Wii_U) consoles, which released on August 2, 2012, and March 30, 2013, respectively. Like *XIV*, it used Crystal Tools.

Square Enix also made browser games and Facebook games, like *Legend World*, *[Chocobo's Crystal Tower](/wiki/Chocobo_(series))* and *Knights of the Crystals*, and online games for [Yahoo! Japan](/wiki/Yahoo!_Japan), such as *Monster x Dragon*, *Sengoku Ixa*, *Bravely Default: Praying Brage*, *Star Galaxy*, and *Crystal Conquest*.

### Cloud gaming

In 2013, *[Dragon Quest X](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_X)* was brought to iOS and Android in Japan using [NTT DoCoMo](/wiki/NTT_DoCoMo) as the release platform and Ubitus for the streaming technology. In 2014, it was also brought to 3DS in Japan using Ubitus.

On May 8, 2012, Square Enix announced a collaboration with [Bigpoint Games](/wiki/Bigpoint_Games) to create a free-to-play [Cloud gaming](/wiki/Cloud_gaming) platform that "throws players into 'limitless game worlds' directly through their web browser". The service was launched under the name *CoreOnline* in August 2012. Stating "limited commercial take-up", the service was cancelled on November 29, 2013.

In September 2014, a cloud gaming company called Shinra Technologies (previously Project Flare) was created; however, it was closed in January 2016. On October 9, 2014, Square Enix launched another online game service in Japan called *Dive In*, which allowed players to stream console games to their [iOS](/wiki/IOS) or [Android](/wiki/Android_(operating_system)) devices. The service was monetized by the amount of time the players spent playing, with each game offered for free for thirty minutes. The service was cancelled on September 13, 2015. Some Square Enix games are available in Japan on the G-cluster streaming service.

### Arcade facilities

With the merger of [Taito](/wiki/Taito) businesses into Square Enix, the company gained possession of Taito's arcade infrastructure and facilities and entered the arcade market in 2005. In 2010 Taito revealed [NESiCAxLive](/wiki/NESiCAxLive), a cloud-based system of storing games and changing them through the internet instead of acquiring physical copies. This system was added to its many arcade gaming locations. The company continues to cater to the arcade audience in Japan with arcade-only titles, with game producers in 2015 stating that Square Enix has a loyal fan base that values the arcade gaming experience. In November 2019, Square Enix announced a "Ninja Tower Tokyo" theme park by its newly established *Live Interactive Works* division.

### Film

The company has made three forays into the film industry. The first, *[Final Fantasy: The Spirits Within](/wiki/Final_Fantasy:_The_Spirits_Within)* (2001), was produced by Square subsidiary [Square Pictures](/wiki/Square_Pictures) before the Enix merger; Square Pictures is now a consolidated subsidiary of Square Enix. Its box-office failure caused Enix to delay the merger, which was already under consideration before the creation of the film until Square became profitable once again. In 2005, Square Enix released *[Final Fantasy VII Advent Children](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII_Advent_Children)*, a [CGI](/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery)-animation film based on the [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation) game *[Final Fantasy VII](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII)*, set two years after the events of the game. A *[Deus Ex](/wiki/Deus_Ex_(video_game))* film was in pre-production in 2012 and, as of 2014, was undergoing rewrites. In 2016 Square Enix revealed a film *[Kingsglaive: Final Fantasy XV](/wiki/Kingsglaive:_Final_Fantasy_XV)* based in the world of *[Final Fantasy XV](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_XV)* and *[Brotherhood: Final Fantasy XV](/wiki/Brotherhood:_Final_Fantasy_XV)*, a new web series released on YouTube and [Crunchyroll](/wiki/Crunchyroll).

### Publishing

Main articles: [List of Gangan Comics manga franchises](/wiki/List_of_Gangan_Comics_manga_franchises) and [List of Square Enix companion books](/wiki/List_of_Square_Enix_companion_books)

The company has a [manga](/wiki/Manga) publishing division in Japan (originally from Enix) called [Gangan Comics](/wiki/Gangan_Comics), which publishes content for the Japanese market only. In 2010, however, Square Enix launched a digital manga store for North American audiences via its Members services, which contains several notable series published in Gangan anthologies. Titles published by Gangan Comics include *[Fullmetal Alchemist](/wiki/Fullmetal_Alchemist)*, *[Soul Eater](/wiki/Soul_Eater_(manga))*, and many others. Other titles include manga adaptations of various Square Enix games, like *[Dragon Quest](/wiki/Dragon_Quest_(manga))*, *[Kingdom Hearts](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts#Printed_adaptations)*, and *[Star Ocean](/wiki/Star_Ocean)*. Some of these titles have also been adapted into [anime](/wiki/Anime) series. *Fullmetal Alchemist* is the most successful title of Square Enix's manga branch, with more than 64 million volumes sold worldwide. It is licensed in North America by [Viz Media](/wiki/Viz_Media), while its two anime adaptations were licensed by [Funimation](/wiki/Funimation) (now known as [Crunchyroll](/wiki/Crunchyroll)) in North America. Starting in Q4 2019, Square Enix began publishing some of its manga series in English.

### Merchandise

Square Enix has created merchandise for virtually all of its video game franchises. Starting in 2000, Square Enix's former online gaming portal [PlayOnline](/wiki/PlayOnline) sold merchandise from game franchises including *[Parasite Eve](/wiki/Parasite_Eve_(video_game))*, *[Vagrant Story](/wiki/Vagrant_Story)*, *[Chocobo Racing](/wiki/Chocobo_Racing)*, *[Front Mission](/wiki/Front_Mission)*, *[Chrono Cross](/wiki/Chrono_Cross)*, and *[Final Fantasy](/wiki/Final_Fantasy)*. [Mascots](/wiki/Mascot) from game franchises are a popular focus for merchandise, such as the [Chocobo](/wiki/Chocobo) from *Final Fantasy*, which has been seen as a [rubber duck](/wiki/Rubber_duck), a plush baby Chocobo, and on coffee [mugs](/wiki/Mug). Square Enix also designed a Chocobo [character costume](/wiki/Mascot) for the release of *[Chocobo Tales](/wiki/Final_Fantasy_Fables:_Chocobo_Tales)*. The [Slime](/wiki/Slime_(Dragon_Quest)) character from *Dragon Quest* has also been frequently used in Square Enix merchandise, especially in Japan. On the Japanese language Square Enix shopping website, there is also a Slime-focused section called "Smile Slime". Slime merchandise includes [plush](/wiki/Plush) toys, [game controllers](/wiki/Game_controller), figurines, and several board games, including one titled *Dragon Quest Slime Racing*. In Japan, pork-filled steam buns shaped like slimes have been sold in 2010. For *Dragon Quest's* 25th anniversary, special items were sold, including [business cards](/wiki/Business_cards), tote bags, and crystal figurines. Rabites from the [*Mana* series](/wiki/Mana_(series)) have appeared in several pieces of Square Enix merchandise, including plush dolls, cushions, lighters, mousepads, straps, telephone cards, and T-shirts. Square Enix has also made merchandise for third party series, including figures *[Mass Effect](/wiki/Mass_Effect)* and *[Halo](/wiki/Halo_(series))* in 2012. Beginning in 2012, it operates shops called "Square Enix Cafe" in Tokyo, Osaka and Shanghai, which display and sell merchandise, as well as serve café food.

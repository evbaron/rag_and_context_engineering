**Sandwell** is a [metropolitan borough](/wiki/Metropolitan_borough) of the [West Midlands](/wiki/West_Midlands_(county)) county in [England](/wiki/England). The borough is named after the [Sandwell Priory](/wiki/Sandwell_Priory), and spans a densely populated part of the [West Midlands conurbation](/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation). [Sandwell Metropolitan Borough Council](/wiki/Sandwell_Metropolitan_Borough_Council) defines the borough as the six amalgamated towns of [Oldbury](/wiki/Oldbury,_West_Midlands), [Rowley Regis](/wiki/Rowley_Regis), [Smethwick](/wiki/Smethwick), [Tipton](/wiki/Tipton), [Wednesbury](/wiki/Wednesbury) and [West Bromwich](/wiki/West_Bromwich). Rowley Regis includes the towns of [Blackheath](/wiki/Blackheath,_West_Midlands) and [Cradley Heath](/wiki/Cradley_Heath).

Sandwell's Strategic Town Centre is designated as West Bromwich, the largest town in the borough, while Sandwell Council House (the headquarters of the local authority) is situated in Oldbury. In 2019 Sandwell was ranked 12th most deprived of England's 317 boroughs.

Bordering Sandwell is the [City of Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) to the east, the [Metropolitan Borough of Dudley](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Dudley) to the south and west, the [Metropolitan Borough of Walsall](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Walsall) to the north, and the [City of Wolverhampton](/wiki/Wolverhampton) to the north-west. Spanning the borough are the parliamentary constituencies of [West Bromwich](/wiki/West_Bromwich_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), [Smethwick](/wiki/Smethwick_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), the majority of [Tipton and Wednesbury](/wiki/Tipton_and_Wednesbury_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) and also part of [Halesowen](/wiki/Halesowen_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), which both cross into the Dudley borough.

The borough covers an area of 86 square kilometres (33 sq mi). At the 2011 census, it had a population of 309,000.

## History and culture

Street nameplate on Rutland Road in [Smethwick](/wiki/Smethwick) in April 2007, showing painted out "County Borough" lettering.

The Metropolitan Borough of Sandwell was formed on 1 April 1974 as an [amalgamation](/wiki/Amalgamation_(politics)) of the [county boroughs](/wiki/County_borough) of [Warley](/wiki/County_Borough_of_Warley) ([ceremonially](/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) within [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire)) and [West Bromwich](/wiki/County_Borough_of_West_Bromwich) (ceremonially within [Staffordshire](/wiki/Staffordshire)), under the [Local Government Act 1972](/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972). Warley had been formed in 1966 by a merger of the [county borough](/wiki/County_borough) of Smethwick with the [municipal boroughs](/wiki/Municipal_borough) of Rowley Regis and Oldbury; at the same time, West Bromwich had absorbed the boroughs of Tipton and Wednesbury.

For its first 12 years of existence, Sandwell had a two-tier system of local government; Sandwell Council shared power with the [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council). In 1986 the county council was abolished, and Sandwell effectively became a [unitary authority](/wiki/Unitary_authority). The borough is divided into 24 [wards](/wiki/Ward_(politics)) and is represented by 72 ward [councillors](/wiki/Councillor) on the borough council.

The borough was named after Sandwell Priory, the ruins of which are located in [Sandwell Valley](/wiki/Sandwell_Valley). Gaining widespread acceptance for the identity of Sandwell and unifying the distinct communities within the borough has been a protracted affair. The local council has considered changing its name over confusion outside the West Midlands as to the whereabouts of the borough. A survey of borough residents in June 2002 found that 65 per cent of respondents favoured retaining the name.

Landmarks and attractions in Sandwell include [Sandwell Aquatics Centre](/wiki/Sandwell_Aquatics_Centre), [Wednesbury Museum and Art Gallery](/wiki/Wednesbury_Museum_and_Art_Gallery), [Bishop Asbury Cottage](/wiki/Bishop_Asbury_Cottage), [West Bromwich Manor House](/wiki/West_Bromwich_Manor_House), [Oak House, West Bromwich](/wiki/Oak_House,_West_Bromwich), and [Sandwell Valley Country Park](/wiki/Sandwell_Valley_Country_Park). It is also the home of [West Bromwich Albion F.C.](/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.)

Sandwell used to be a popular hotspot for [car cruising](/wiki/Car_cruising). In 2015 a High Court order was introduced to ban car cruising in the area. An extension has been secured to run until at least 2021.

## Politics

From the borough's creation in 1974 until 2010, all [Members of Parliament](/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom)) (MPs) within its boundaries were Labour. However, in the [2010 general election](/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election), Conservative party candidate [James Morris](/wiki/James_Morris_(British_politician)) was elected to the [Halesowen and Rowley Regis](/wiki/Halesowen_and_Rowley_Regis_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) seat which incorporates the Sandwell communities of [Rowley Regis](/wiki/Rowley_Regis), [Blackheath](/wiki/Blackheath,_West_Midlands) and [Cradley Heath](/wiki/Cradley_Heath), and the neighbouring area of [Halesowen](/wiki/Halesowen) which is situated within [Dudley's](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Dudley) borders. This was the first time any part of Sandwell had elected a Conservative MP – or indeed an MP from any party other than Labour. In the December 2019 general election, however, Conservative candidates [Nicola Richards](/wiki/Nicola_Richards) and [Shaun Bailey](/wiki/Shaun_Bailey_(West_Bromwich_MP)) were elected to represent [West Bromwich East](/wiki/West_Bromwich_East) and [West Bromwich West](/wiki/West_Bromwich_West) respectively. This was the first time since the borough's creation that [West Bromwich](/wiki/West_Bromwich) had returned any Conservative MPs to Parliament, and the first time that a constituency fully within the boundaries of Sandwell Metropolitan Borough Council had been represented by a Conservative MP.

## Education

See also: [List of schools in Sandwell](/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Sandwell)

Sandwell is home to nearly 100 primary schools, 25 secondary schools, 4 special schools and 1 college.

[Sandwell College](/wiki/Sandwell_College), the only [further education college](/wiki/Further_education) in the borough, was opened in September 1986 following the merger of [Warley College](/wiki/Warley_College) and West Bromwich College. It was originally based in the old Warley College buildings on Pound Road, Oldbury, and the West Bromwich College buildings on West Bromwich High Street, as well as a building in Smethwick town centre, but moved into a new single site campus in West Bromwich town centre in September 2012. In 2004, a debt-ridden Sandwell College was subject to a police investigation.

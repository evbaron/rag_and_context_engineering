**Richmond Harold Shreve** (June 25, 1877 – September 11, 1946) was a Canadian-American architect.

## Biography

He was born on June 25, 1877, in [Cornwallis, Nova Scotia](/wiki/Cornwallis,_Nova_Scotia), the son of Richmond Shreve, an Anglican priest, and Mary Catherine Parker Hocken. Shreve attended [Cornell University](/wiki/Cornell_University), taught there from 1902 to 1906, and was a member of the [Sphinx Head Society](/wiki/Sphinx_Head_Society).

He was president of the [American Institute of Architects](/wiki/American_Institute_of_Architects) from 1941 through 1943.

He died on September 11, 1946, in [Hastings-on-Hudson, New York](/wiki/Hastings-on-Hudson,_New_York).

## Legacy

His company [Shreve, Lamb and Harmon](/wiki/Shreve,_Lamb_and_Harmon) led the construction of the [Empire State Building](/wiki/Empire_State_Building) as well as several [Cornell University](/wiki/Cornell_University) buildings. Shreve was also the lead architect for the landmark 1937 [Williamsburg Houses](/wiki/Williamsburg_Houses) housing development in Brooklyn and the [Parkchester](/wiki/Parkchester,_Bronx) planned community developed by the [Metropolitan Life Insurance Company](/wiki/MetLife) in the east Bronx in 1941.

He was profiled in the book *The 100 Most Notable Cornellians*.

The [1998 season](/wiki/1998_NFL_season) was the [Denver Broncos](/wiki/Denver_Broncos)' 29th in the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League) (NFL) and their 39th overall. The Broncos entered the season as the [defending Super Bowl champions](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXII) and looked to become only the fifth team in league history to win consecutive Super Bowls.

Finishing with a record of 12–4 the previous year, the Broncos improved on that mark by two wins and tied [the Atlanta Falcons](/wiki/1998_Atlanta_Falcons_season) for second best record at 14–2. They won their first thirteen games, the best start since the [unbeaten](/wiki/Perfect_season) [1972 Dolphins](/wiki/1972_Miami_Dolphins_season).

After 16 seasons, [John Elway](/wiki/John_Elway) retired following the Super Bowl. He finished his Broncos career with 51,475 yards passing and 300 touchdowns. Until [Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning) won in [Super Bowl 50](/wiki/Super_Bowl_50), Elway stood as the only Broncos quarterback to win a Super Bowl. However, Elway even played a large role in that victory as the [general manager](/wiki/General_manager#Sports_teams) and president of football operations for the Broncos.

Running back [Terrell Davis](/wiki/Terrell_Davis) set a team single season rushing mark. His final total was 2,008 yards, making him only the fourth player to rush for [over 2,000 yards in single season](/wiki/2,000-yard_club).

A multi-year investigation from 2001 to 2005 revealed that between 1996 and 1998, the team had circumvented the salary cap by deferring other money to Elway and Davis outside of the team’s salary. Denver claimed it gave them no competitive advantage. The NFL did not issue any official statements but the team was subsequently fined nearly $2 million and were forced to give up two third-round picks in the [2002](/wiki/2002_NFL_draft) and [2005](/wiki/2005_NFL_draft) drafts.

In 2007, the 1998 Broncos were ranked as the 12th greatest Super Bowl champions on the [NFL Network](/wiki/NFL_Network)'s documentary series *[America's Game: The Super Bowl Champions](/wiki/America%27s_Game:_The_Super_Bowl_Champions)*. They ranked #14 on the 100 greatest teams of all time presented by the NFL on its 100th anniversary.

## Season summary

The Denver [superfan](/wiki/Fan_(person)) [Barrel Man](/wiki/Barrel_Man_(Denver_Broncos)) seen at the regular season opener at [Mile High Stadium](/wiki/Mile_High_Stadium) against [New England](/wiki/1998_New_England_Patriots_season)

The Broncos won their first 13 games of the season. There was much speculation that they might finish 19–0 and the Broncos were featured on the cover of *[Sports Illustrated](/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)*. However, they were upset by [the New York Giants](/wiki/1998_New_York_Giants_season) (who would end another attempt at a 19–0 season [nine seasons later](/wiki/2007_New_York_Giants_season)) in week 15 by a score of 20–16. They finished the regular season 14–2 after losing to [the Dolphins](/wiki/1998_Miami_Dolphins_season) in their first encounter with that team since [1985](/wiki/1985_Denver_Broncos_season).

They finished first in the [AFC West](/wiki/AFC_West) and won their divisional playoff game against [the Miami Dolphins](/wiki/1998_Miami_Dolphins_season) 38–3 for their first win over the Dolphins since [1968](/wiki/1968_Denver_Broncos_season). They then won the [AFC Championship](/wiki/AFC_Championship) over the [Bill Parcells](/wiki/Bill_Parcells) coached [New York Jets](/wiki/1998_New_York_Jets_season) 23–10 after coming back from a 10–0 deficit. Many had expected Denver to play [the Minnesota Vikings](/wiki/1998_Minnesota_Vikings_season), the team with the number one record that year at 15–1, in the Super Bowl. However, the Vikings lost the [NFC Championship Game](/wiki/1998_NFC_Championship_Game) to [the Atlanta Falcons](/wiki/1998_Atlanta_Falcons_season) in overtime.

The Broncos defeated the Falcons 34–19 in Super Bowl XXXIII. Elway was the [Super Bowl MVP](/wiki/Super_Bowl_MVP) and Davis rushed for over 100 yards. It was Elway's last game, and Denver would not reach the Super Bowl again until [the 2013 season](/wiki/2013_Denver_Broncos_season).

## Statistics

### Team stats

The Broncos had 3,808 [yards](/wiki/Yards) passing, sixth in the league. They had 2,468 yards rushing, second in the league and 26 rushing touchdowns, first in the league. They had 6,276 total yards, third best.

They gave up 3,983 passing yards, a low 28 out of 30 in the NFL, but were third in rushing yards given up with 1,287. They gave up 5,270 yards, 12th in the NFL. They scored 501 points, second in the league and gave up 309, eighth fewest in the league.

The Broncos’ 14–2 record remains their best regular season record (most wins and equal fewest losses) in franchise history.

### Player stats

For the season Elway threw for 2,806 yards, 22 touchdowns and ten interceptions. Davis rushed for 2,008 yards and 21 touchdowns. [Rod Smith](/wiki/Rod_Smith_(wide_receiver)) had 86 receptions for 1,222 yards and six touchdowns. [Ed McCaffrey](/wiki/Ed_McCaffrey) had 64 receptions for 1,053 yards. [Shannon Sharpe](/wiki/Shannon_Sharpe) had 64 receptions for 768 yards. [Jason Elam](/wiki/Jason_Elam) kicked 23 out of 27 field goals and 58 out of 58 extra points including a 63-yard field goal to tie Tom Dempsey with the longest field goal in NFL history at that time.

The **Moose Jaw Warriors** are a Canadian major [junior ice hockey](/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey) team based in [Moose Jaw](/wiki/Moose_Jaw), Saskatchewan. The Warriors play in the East Division of the [Western Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Hockey_League)'s Eastern Conference, hosting games at the [Temple Gardens Centre](/wiki/Temple_Gardens_Centre). The team was founded in 1980 as the [Winnipeg Warriors](/wiki/Winnipeg_Warriors), and relocated to Moose Jaw in 1984. The Warriors won their first league championship in 2024.

## History

The [Moose Jaw Canucks](/wiki/Moose_Jaw_Canucks) were a founding franchise of the then-Western Canada Hockey League in 1966, and were the new league's first champion. However, after just two seasons and with the WCHL barred by the [Canadian Amateur Hockey Association](/wiki/Canadian_Amateur_Hockey_Association) (CAHA) from competing for the [Memorial Cup](/wiki/Memorial_Cup), the Canucks opted to return to the revived [Saskatchewan Junior Hockey League](/wiki/Saskatchewan_Junior_Hockey_League) in 1968. When the WCHL became recognized by CAHA in 1970, Moose Jaw was left without top-level junior hockey.

The Warriors franchise was established as the Winnipeg Warriors prior to the start of the [1980–81 WHL season](/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_WHL_season), and played out of [Winnipeg Arena](/wiki/Winnipeg_Arena), which they shared with the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League)'s [Winnipeg Jets](/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets_(1972-96)). In 1984, the franchise relocated to Moose Jaw, bringing major junior hockey back to the city for the first time since 1970.

The Warriors played in the [Moose Jaw Civic Centre](/wiki/Moose_Jaw_Civic_Centre), also known as "The Crushed Can", for 26 seasons, before moving to Mosaic Place, now the Moose Jaw Events Centre, in the city centre in 2011.

The team's first few seasons in Moose Jaw saw [Theoren Fleury](/wiki/Theoren_Fleury) emerge as the team's primary star—Fleury finished among the top five scorers in the WHL during his tenure with the team. Led by Fleury, [Kelly Buchberger](/wiki/Kelly_Buchberger), [Mike Keane](/wiki/Mike_Keane), and [Lyle Odelein](/wiki/Lyle_Odelein), the Warriors made the franchise's first playoff appearance after the relocation during the [1985–86 season](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_WHL_season). The team captured its first regular season division title in [2003–04](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_WHL_season). In [2005–06](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_WHL_season), led by [Troy Brouwer](/wiki/Troy_Brouwer) and [Dustin Boyd](/wiki/Dustin_Boyd), the Warriors followed their best regular season with their first ever trip to the championship series, in which they were swept by the [Vancouver Giants](/wiki/Vancouver_Giants). In [2017–18](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_WHL_season), the Warriors captured their first [Scotty Munro Memorial Trophy](/wiki/Scotty_Munro_Memorial_Trophy) as regular season champions with their first 50-win and 100-point season, before bowing out in the second round of the playoffs to the eventual champion [Swift Current Broncos](/wiki/Swift_Current_Broncos).

In the [2023–24 season](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_WHL_season)—the Warriors' 40th in Moose Jaw—the Warriors won their first [Ed Chynoweth Cup](/wiki/Ed_Chynoweth_Cup) as league champions. Led by [Jagger Firkus](/wiki/Jagger_Firkus), [Denton Mateychuk](/wiki/Denton_Mateychuk), and [Brayden Yager](/wiki/Brayden_Yager), the Warriors advanced to their second league final by defeating the regular-season champion [Saskatoon Blades](/wiki/Saskatoon_Blades) in a seven game series that featured a WHL-record six overtime games, including the seventh and deciding game. In the final, they won four straight games versus the [Portland Winterhawks](/wiki/Portland_Winterhawks) to secure the championship and a berth in the [2024 Memorial Cup](/wiki/2024_Memorial_Cup) tournament. At their first Memorial Cup, the Warriors advanced to the semi-final, where they were eliminated by the host and eventual champion [Saginaw Spirit](/wiki/Saginaw_Spirit).

## Logo and uniforms

[Quinton Howden](/wiki/Quinton_Howden) with the Warriors in 2010.

The Warriors' original branding, based on the founding franchise in Winnipeg, featured Indigenous imagery, including a long-used logo featuring a silhouetted side-profile of a head wearing an Indigenous [headdress](/wiki/Native_American_headdress). In 2020, in light of the [Native American mascot controversy](/wiki/Native_American_mascot_controversy), the Warriors announced that they were reviewing their branding. In 2022, the team unveiled a new brand and logo based on the city's aviation history and connection with the [Royal Canadian Air Force](/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force) and [Snowbirds](/wiki/Snowbirds_(aerobatic_team))—the logo features a [CT-114 Tutor](/wiki/CT-114_Tutor) jet emblazoned with the Snowbirds logo. The team retained its original colour scheme featuring red, black, and white.

**1844 Susilva**, provisional designation 1972 UB, is a stony Eoan [asteroid](/wiki/Asteroid) from the outer region of the [asteroid belt](/wiki/Asteroid_belt), approximately 22 kilometers in diameter. It was discovered on 30 October 1972, by Swiss astronomer [Paul Wild](/wiki/Paul_Wild_(Swiss_astronomer)) at [Zimmerwald Observatory](/wiki/Zimmerwald_Observatory) near Bern, Switzerland, and later named after a schoolfriend of the discoverer.

## Classification and orbit

*Susilva* is a member of the [Eos family](/wiki/Eos_family), a collisional group of more than 4,000 asteroids, which are well known for mostly being of [silicaceous](/wiki/S-type_asteroid) composition. It orbits the Sun in the [outer](/wiki/Kirkwood_gap) main-belt at a distance of 2.9–3.2 [AU](/wiki/Astronomical_unit) once every 5 years and 3 months (1,912 days). Its orbit has an [eccentricity](/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity) of 0.05 and an [inclination](/wiki/Orbital_inclination) of 12[°](/wiki/Degree_(angle)) with respect to the [ecliptic](/wiki/Ecliptic).
First identified as 1943 EU at [Turku Observatory](/wiki/Iso-Heikkil%C3%A4_Observatory), *Susilva*'s first used observation was taken at [Uccle Observatory](/wiki/Uccle_Observatory) in 1953, extending the body's [observation arc](/wiki/Observation_arc) by 19 years prior to its official discovery observation.

## Physical characteristics

According to the survey carried out by NASA's [Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer](/wiki/Wide-field_Infrared_Survey_Explorer) with its subsequent [NEOWISE](/wiki/NEOWISE) mission, the asteroid measures between 19.0 and 26.8 kilometers in diameter, and its surface has an [albedo](/wiki/Astronomical_albedo) of 0.118 to 0.236. The *Collaborative Asteroid Lightcurve Link* assumes an albedo of 0.14, taken from [221 Eos](/wiki/221_Eos), the family's largest member and namesake – and calculates a diameter of 22.4 kilometers based on an absolute [magnitude](/wiki/Magnitude_(astronomy)) of 11.0. *Susilva*'s [rotation period](/wiki/Rotation_period) has not yet been measured.

## Naming

The discoverer named a pair of asteroids after two of his former schoolmates, Susi and Helen, both from the small village of [Wald, Zürich](/wiki/Wald,_Z%C3%BCrich) in Switzerland. This one was dedicated to Susi Petit–Pierre, while the subsequently numbered asteroid, [1845 Helewalda](/wiki/1845_Helewalda), was given to Helen Gachnang. The official [naming citation](/wiki/Meanings_of_minor-planet_names:_1001%E2%80%932000#m844) was published by the [Minor Planet Center](/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center) on 18 April 1977 ([M.P.C.](/wiki/Minor_Planet_Circulars) 4156).

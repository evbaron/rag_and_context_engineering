**Damien Sayre Chazelle** ([/ʃəˈzɛl/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English); born January 19, 1985) is an American and French filmmaker. He directed the psychological drama *[Whiplash](/wiki/Whiplash_(2014_film))* (2014), the musical romance *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)* (2016), the biographical drama *[First Man](/wiki/First_Man_(film))* (2018), and the [period](/wiki/Period_film) [black comedy](/wiki/Dark_humor) *[Babylon](/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film))* (2022).

For *Whiplash*, he was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay). His biggest commercial success came with *La La Land*, which was nominated for 14 Academy Awards, winning six including [Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director), making him the [youngest](/wiki/List_of_oldest_and_youngest_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Youngest_winners) person to win the award at age 32.

## Early life and education

Chazelle was born in [Providence, Rhode Island](/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island), to a Catholic family. His French-American father, [Bernard Chazelle](/wiki/Bernard_Chazelle), is the Eugene Higgins Professor of [computer science](/wiki/Computer_science) at [Princeton University](/wiki/Princeton_University). His mother, [Celia Chazelle](/wiki/Celia_Chazelle), is from an English-Canadian family based in [Calgary](/wiki/Calgary), Alberta, and teaches medieval history at [The College of New Jersey](/wiki/The_College_of_New_Jersey).

Chazelle was raised in [Princeton, New Jersey](/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), where, although a Catholic, he attended a [Hebrew school](/wiki/Hebrew_school) for four years due to his parents' dissatisfaction with his religious education at a church Sunday school.

Chazelle has a sister, [Anna Chazelle](/wiki/Anna_Chazelle), who is an actress. Their English-born maternal grandfather, John Martin, is the son of stage actress Eileen Earle.

Filmmaking was Chazelle's first love, but he subsequently wanted to be a musician and struggled to make it as a jazz drummer at [Princeton High School](/wiki/Princeton_High_School_(New_Jersey)). He has said that he had an intense music teacher in the Princeton High School Studio Band, who was the inspiration for the character of Terence Fletcher ([J. K. Simmons](/wiki/J._K._Simmons)) in Chazelle's breakout film *[Whiplash](/wiki/Whiplash_(2014_film))*. Unlike the film's protagonist Andrew Neiman ([Miles Teller](/wiki/Miles_Teller)), Chazelle stated that he knew instinctively that he never had the talent to be a great drummer and after high school, pursued filmmaking again. He studied filmmaking in the Visual and Environmental Studies department at [Harvard University](/wiki/Harvard_University) and graduated in 2007.

At Harvard, he lived in [Currier House](/wiki/Currier_House_(Harvard_College)) as roommates with composer and frequent collaborator [Justin Hurwitz](/wiki/Justin_Hurwitz). The two were among the original members of the indie-pop group [Chester French](/wiki/Chester_French), formed during their freshman year.

## Career

### 2008–2013: Early work and career beginnings

Chazelle wrote and directed his debut feature, *[Guy and Madeline on a Park Bench](/wiki/Guy_and_Madeline_on_a_Park_Bench)*, as part of his senior thesis project with classmate Justin Hurwitz at Harvard. The film premiered at [Tribeca Film Festival](/wiki/Tribeca_Festival) in 2009 and received various awards on the festival circuit, before being picked up by Variance Films for limited release and opening to critical acclaim.

After graduation, Chazelle moved to Los Angeles with the ultimate goal of attracting interest to produce his musical romantic drama *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land_(film))*. He worked as a freelance writer in Hollywood; among his writing credits are *[The Last Exorcism Part II](/wiki/The_Last_Exorcism_Part_II)* (2013) and *[Grand Piano](/wiki/Grand_Piano_(film))* (2013). He was also brought in by [J. J. Abrams](/wiki/J._J._Abrams)' [Bad Robot](/wiki/Bad_Robot) to re-write a draft of *[10 Cloverfield Lane](/wiki/10_Cloverfield_Lane)* (2016) with the intention of also directing, but Chazelle ultimately chose to direct *[Whiplash](/wiki/Whiplash_(2014_film))* instead.

### 2014–2019: Breakthrough, acclaim and accolades

Chazelle on the set of *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)* in 2015

Chazelle initially described *Whiplash* as a writing reaction to being stuck on another script: "I just thought, that's not working, let me put it away and write this thing about being a jazz drummer in high school." He stated he initially did not want to show the script around, as it felt too personal, and "I put it in a drawer". Although nobody was initially interested in producing the film, his script was featured on [The Black List](/wiki/The_Black_List_(survey)) in 2012 as one of the best unmade films of that year. The project was eventually picked up by Right of Way Films and [Blumhouse Productions](/wiki/Blumhouse_Productions), who suggested that Chazelle turn a portion of his script into a short film as proof-of-concept. The 18-minute short was accepted at the 2013 [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival), where it was well-received; financing was then raised for the feature film, and, in 2014, it was released to a positive critical reaction. *Whiplash* received numerous awards on the festival circuit and earned five [Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations, including [Best Adapted Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay) for Chazelle, winning three.

Thanks to the success of *Whiplash*, Chazelle was able to attract financiers for his musical romantic drama *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)*. The film opened the [Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/Venice_International_Film_Festival) on August 31, 2016, and began a limited release in the United States on December 9, 2016, with a wider release on December 16, 2016. It received universal acclaim and numerous awards. Chazelle received praise for his work on the film and received several top honors, including a [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director) and an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) for Best Director, making Chazelle the youngest director to win each award, at age 32. A stage musical adaptation of the film is in development, with [Ayad Akhtar](/wiki/Ayad_Akhtar) and [Matthew Decker](/wiki/Matthew_Decker_(writer)) adapting from Chazelle's script and Hurwitz, [Benj Pasek](/wiki/Benj_Pasek) and [Justin Paul](/wiki/Justin_Paul) returning as songwriters after winning Golden Globes and Academy Awards for [the score](/wiki/La_La_Land_(soundtrack)) and original song "[City of Stars](/wiki/City_of_Stars)". [Marc Platt](/wiki/Marc_Platt_(producer)), another collaborator of Chazelle who produced this film and *[Babylon](/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film))*, will also return to produce the stage adaptation.

Chazelle next directed the [biographical drama](/wiki/Biographical_drama) *[First Man](/wiki/First_Man_(film))* (2018) for [Universal Pictures](/wiki/Universal_Pictures). With a screenplay by [Josh Singer](/wiki/Josh_Singer), the biopic is based on author [James R. Hansen](/wiki/James_R._Hansen)'s *[First Man: The Life of Neil A. Armstrong](/wiki/First_Man:_The_Life_of_Neil_A._Armstrong)*, written about [the astronaut](/wiki/Neil_Armstrong). The film starred [Ryan Gosling](/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) as [Neil Armstrong](/wiki/Neil_Armstrong) and [Claire Foy](/wiki/Claire_Foy) as Janet Armstrong. The film received positive reviews, with [Owen Gleiberman](/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman) of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* writing that "Chazelle orchestrates a dashingly original mood of adventure drenched in anxiety". It earned four [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) nominations for [Best Production Design](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Production_Design), [Best Sound Editing](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Sound_Editing), and [Best Sound Mixing](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Sound_Mixing), winning for [Best Visual Effects](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects) at the [91st Academy Awards](/wiki/91st_Academy_Awards).

In December 2019, he listed the films that inspired him for LaCinetek, a French streaming platform that collects lists of favorite films from filmmakers. Featuring 64 films, his selection includes works by [Jacques Demy](/wiki/Jacques_Demy) (*[The Umbrellas of Cherbourg](/wiki/The_Umbrellas_of_Cherbourg)*), [Stanley Kubrick](/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick) (*[Eyes Wide Shut](/wiki/Eyes_Wide_Shut)*, *[Barry Lyndon](/wiki/Barry_Lyndon)*), and [Richard Linklater](/wiki/Richard_Linklater) (*[Before Sunset](/wiki/Before_Sunset)*).

### 2020–present

Chazelle directed the first two episodes of the May 2020-released [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) musical drama television miniseries *[The Eddy](/wiki/The_Eddy)*. The series is written by [Jack Thorne](/wiki/Jack_Thorne), with Grammy-winning songwriter [Glen Ballard](/wiki/Glen_Ballard) and [Alan Poul](/wiki/Alan_Poul) attached as executive producers. The series starred [André Holland](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Holland) and [Joanna Kulig](/wiki/Joanna_Kulig) and was set in [Paris](/wiki/Paris) consisting of eight episodes.

In July 2019, *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* reported that his next film, called *[Babylon](/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film))*, set in 1920s Hollywood, was scheduled to be released in 2021, co-produced by his wife, [Olivia Hamilton](/wiki/Olivia_Hamilton_(actress)). Chazelle was eyeing [Emma Stone](/wiki/Emma_Stone) to star; In December 2020, it was reported that [Margot Robbie](/wiki/Margot_Robbie) was in talks to replace Stone. *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* reported that *Babylon* would have a limited release on December 25, 2022, before expanding into wide release on January 6, 2023. The film's final cast included [Margot Robbie](/wiki/Margot_Robbie), [Brad Pitt](/wiki/Brad_Pitt), [Li Jun Li](/wiki/Li_Jun_Li), [Jovan Adepo](/wiki/Jovan_Adepo) and [Jean Smart](/wiki/Jean_Smart). The film was a [box office bomb](/wiki/Box_office_bomb) receiving $4.5 million opening weekend. Many industry experts predicted that the film would need to make $250 million just to break even against its $80 million budget and marketing costs. The film received polarizing reviews. Manhola Dargis of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* wrote, "Throughout this disappointing movie, what's missing is the one thing that defined the silent era at its greatest and to which Chazelle remains bafflingly oblivious: its art." At the same time, *[Wall Street Journal](/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal)* film critic Kyle Smith called the movie "one of the year's most ambitious and impressive works."

In December 2022, Chazelle and Hamilton signed a [first-look deal](/wiki/First-look_deal) with [Paramount Pictures](/wiki/Paramount_Pictures). In April 2024, it was reported that Chazelle would produce his next film, a prison drama set for release in 2025, alongside his wife Hamilton under their Wild Chickens Productions banner. In December 2024, it was reported that Chazelle's next film would instead be a biopic about [Evel Knievel](/wiki/Evel_Knievel), with [Leonardo DiCaprio](/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio) and [Adrien Brody](/wiki/Adrien_Brody) in talks to star, from a script by [William Monahan](/wiki/William_Monahan). However, once production plans were on pause as DiCaprio weighed up his involvement, Chazelle pivoted back to working on the prison drama.

## Personal life

Chazelle and Hamilton in 2018

Chazelle married producer Jasmine McGlade in 2010; they divorced in 2014. In October 2017, Chazelle and actress [Olivia Hamilton](/wiki/Olivia_Hamilton_(actress)), a [Princeton University](/wiki/Princeton_University) graduate and former [McKinsey & Company](/wiki/McKinsey_%26_Company) consultant, announced their engagement, and they married September 22, 2018. They have a son who was born in November 2019. Their second child was born in December 2022. Chazelle is fluent in French.

# Wolfgang Hutter

**Wolfgang Hutter** (December 13, 1928 – September 26, 2014) was a painter, draughtsman, printmaker and stage designer. Hutter's [imagery](/wiki/Image) is characterised by an artificial paradise of gardens and fantastical fairytale-like scenes. His work is said to have been influenced by his psychedelic experiences.

Hutter was born in [Vienna](/wiki/Vienna). With [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish) origins, the son of [A. P. von Gütersloh](/wiki/A._P._von_G%C3%BCtersloh), Hutter studied at the [Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna](/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts_in_Vienna) under Professor Robin C. Andersen and then under his father. Together with [Ernst Fuchs](/wiki/Ernst_Fuchs_(artist)), [Maître Leherb (Helmut Leherb)](/wiki/Helmut_Leherb), [Rudolf Hausner](/wiki/Rudolf_Hausner), [Fritz Janschka](/wiki/Fritz_Janschka), [Anton Lehmden](/wiki/Anton_Lehmden) and [Arik Brauer](/wiki/Arik_Brauer), he is one of the main representatives and founding members of the [Vienna School of Fantastic Realism](/wiki/Vienna_School_of_Fantastic_Realism). Hutter was awarded the [UNESCO](/wiki/UNESCO) Prize at the Venice Biennale in 1954. In 1966, he was appointed a professorship at the [University of Applied Arts Vienna](/wiki/University_of_Applied_Arts_Vienna).

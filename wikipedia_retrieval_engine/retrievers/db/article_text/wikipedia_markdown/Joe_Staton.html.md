**Joe Staton** ([/ˈsteɪtən/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English); born January 19, 1948) is an American [comics artist](/wiki/Comics_artist) and writer. He co-created the [Bronze Age](/wiki/Bronze_Age_of_Comic_Books) [Huntress (Helena Wayne)](/wiki/Huntress_(Helena_Wayne)), as well as the third [Huntress (Helena Bertinelli)](/wiki/Huntress_(Helena_Bertinelli)), [Kilowog](/wiki/Kilowog) and the [Omega Men](/wiki/Omega_Men) for [DC Comics](/wiki/DC_Comics). He was the artist of the *[Dick Tracy](/wiki/Dick_Tracy)* comic strip from 2011 to October 2021.

## Early life

Joe Staton grew up in Tennessee and graduated from [Murray State University](/wiki/Murray_State_University) in 1970.

## Career

Staton started his comics career at [Charlton Comics](/wiki/Charlton_Comics) in 1971 and gained notability as the artist of the [super-hero](/wiki/Super-hero) series *[E-Man](/wiki/E-Man)*. Staton produced art for various comics published by Charlton, [Marvel Comics](/wiki/Marvel_Comics), and [Warren Publishing](/wiki/Warren_Publishing) during the 1970s.

Hired initially by [Roy Thomas](/wiki/Roy_Thomas) to work for Marvel, Staton was then recruited by [Paul Levitz](/wiki/Paul_Levitz) to work on [DC Comics](/wiki/DC_Comics)' revival of the [Justice Society of America](/wiki/Justice_Society_of_America) in *[All Star Comics](/wiki/All_Star_Comics)* and later *[Adventure Comics](/wiki/Adventure_Comics)*. In these titles he illustrated stories including the origin of the JSA in *[DC Special](/wiki/DC_Special)* #29 and the death of the [Earth-Two](/wiki/Earth-Two) [Batman](/wiki/Batman_(Earth-Two)). Staton also illustrated the solo adventures of two female JSA members created during the JSA revival – drawing [Power Girl](/wiki/Power_Girl) in *[Showcase](/wiki/Showcase_(comics))* and the [Huntress](/wiki/Huntress_(Helena_Wayne)). During that time, Staton additionally drew *[Superboy and the Legion of Super-Heroes](/wiki/Legion_of_Super-Heroes)*, the 1970s revival of the [Doom Patrol](/wiki/Doom_Patrol) in *Showcase*, and the *[Metal Men](/wiki/Metal_Men)*. In 1979, Staton began a two-and-a-half-year run on *[Green Lantern](/wiki/Green_Lantern_(comic_book))*, during which he co-created the [Omega Men](/wiki/Omega_Men) with writer [Marv Wolfman](/wiki/Marv_Wolfman).

Staton sketching at the 2011 New York Comic Con

Staton served as [art director](/wiki/Art_director) for [First Comics](/wiki/First_Comics) for three years in the 1980s. He returned to DC Comics afterwards for a second run on *Green Lantern* and with writer [Steve Englehart](/wiki/Steve_Englehart), oversaw the title's name change to *Green Lantern Corps*. Staton and Englehart also created the DC weekly crossover series *[Millennium](/wiki/Millennium_(comics))* (Jan.–Feb. 1988). Staton was one of the contributors to the *[DC Challenge](/wiki/DC_Challenge)* limited series in 1986. In addition, he illustrated *[Guy Gardner](/wiki/Guy_Gardner_(character))*, *[The Huntress](/wiki/Huntress_(Helena_Bertinelli))*, *The [New Guardians](/wiki/New_Guardians)*, and *[Superman & Bugs Bunny](/wiki/Superman_%26_Bugs_Bunny)*.

In the early 1990s, Staton provided the artwork on the *Mike Danger* Sunday comic strip, written by [Max Allan Collins](/wiki/Max_Allan_Collins). From the late 90s to the late 2000s, Staton drew DC's *[Scooby-Doo](/wiki/Scooby-Doo)* title for younger readers, as well as the more mature-themed *Femme Noir* for Ape Entertainment. On January 19, 2011, [Tribune Media Services](/wiki/Tribune_Media_Services) announced that Staton and writer [Mike Curtis](/wiki/Mike_Curtis_(writer)) would replace [Dick Locher](/wiki/Dick_Locher) as the creative team of the *[Dick Tracy](/wiki/Dick_Tracy)* [comic strip](/wiki/Comic_strip). The new creative team has worked together on *Scooby-Doo*, *[Richie Rich](/wiki/Richie_Rich_(character))*, and *[Casper the Friendly Ghost](/wiki/Casper_the_Friendly_Ghost)* and started on March 14, 2011. He pencilled *DC Retroactive: Green Lantern – The '80s* #1, written by [Len Wein](/wiki/Len_Wein), published the same year. Staton also illustrated Charles Santino's [graphic novel](/wiki/Graphic_novel) adaptation of [Ayn Rand](/wiki/Ayn_Rand)'s *[Anthem](/wiki/Anthem_(novella))* (2011).

## Awards

Joe Staton received an [Inkpot Award](/wiki/Inkpot_Award) in 1983.

Staton and writer Mike Curtis received the Best Syndicated Strip [Harvey Award](/wiki/Harvey_Award) for *[Dick Tracy](/wiki/Dick_Tracy)* in 2013, 2014, and 2015.

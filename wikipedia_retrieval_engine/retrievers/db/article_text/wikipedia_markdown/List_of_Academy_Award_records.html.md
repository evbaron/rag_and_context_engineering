# List of Academy Award records

[Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney), the record-holder for most Academy Awards won (22 Oscars)

This **list of [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards) records** is current as of the [98th Academy Awards](/wiki/98th_Academy_Awards) ceremony held on March 15, 2026.

## Most awards or nominations

- **Most awards won by a single film**: 11
  - Three films have won 11 Academy Awards:
    - *[Ben-Hur](/wiki/Ben-Hur_(1959_film))* (1959): nominated in 12 of the 15 possible categories
    - *[Titanic](/wiki/Titanic_(1997_film))* (1997): nominated in 14 of the 17 possible categories
    - *[The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King)* (2003): nominated in 11 of the 17 possible categories
- **Most nominations received by a single film**: 16
  - *[Sinners](/wiki/Sinners_(2025_film))* (2025): out of 17 possible categories
- **Most nominations lost by a single film**: 12
  - *[Sinners](/wiki/Sinners_(2025_film))* (2025): won 4 of its 16 nominations
- **Most nominations received by a single film without winning an award**: 11
  - Two films have received 11 nominations and won no awards:
    - *[The Turning Point](/wiki/The_Turning_Point_(1977_film))* (1977): from 14 possible categories
    - *[The Color Purple](/wiki/The_Color_Purple_(1985_film))* (1985): from 17 possible categories
- **Largest sweep (winning awards in every nominated category)**: 11
  - *[The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King)* (2003) won in every category for which it was nominated: Best Picture, Director, Adapted Screenplay, Art Direction, Makeup, Costume Design, Film Editing, Original Score, Original Song, Sound Mixing, and Visual Effects.
- **Most total awards won by a person**: 22
  - [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney)
  - [Dennis Muren](/wiki/Dennis_Muren) holds the record for the most awards by a living person: 9
- **Most total nominations received and awards won by a woman**: 35 and 8
  - [Edith Head](/wiki/Edith_Head), all for Best Costume Design
  - [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep) holds the record for the most nominations for a living woman: 21
- **Most total nominations for a person**: 59
  - [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney)
  - [John Williams](/wiki/John_Williams) holds the record for the most nominations by a living person: 54
- **Most nominations and awards for a person in a single year**: 6 and 4
  - In 1954, [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) received six nominations and won four awards, both records. He won Best Documentary, Features for *[The Living Desert](/wiki/The_Living_Desert)*; Best Documentary, Short Subjects for *[The Alaskan Eskimo](/wiki/The_Alaskan_Eskimo)*; Best Short Subject, Cartoons for *[Toot, Whistle, Plunk and Boom](/wiki/Toot,_Whistle,_Plunk_and_Boom)*; and Best Short Subject, Two-reel for *[Bear Country](/wiki/Bear_Country_(1953_film))*. He had two additional nominations in Best Short Subject, Cartoons for *[Rugged Bear](/wiki/Rugged_Bear)*; and Best Short Subject, Two-reel for *[Ben and Me](/wiki/Ben_and_Me)*.
  - In 2025, [Sean Baker](/wiki/Sean_Baker_(filmmaker)) won four awards from four nominations. He won Best Picture, Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Editing, all for *[Anora](/wiki/Anora)*.
- **Most competitive awards won by a living person**: 8
  - Composer [Alan Menken](/wiki/Alan_Menken) has won eight competitive awards
  - [Milena Canonero](/wiki/Milena_Canonero), [Colleen Atwood](/wiki/Colleen_Atwood), and [Frances McDormand](/wiki/Frances_McDormand) have all won four competitive awards, making them the most awarded living women
  - Note: Visual Effects Supervisor [Dennis Muren](/wiki/Dennis_Muren) has won nine Academy Awards: six competitive awards, two "Special Achievement" awards, and one "Technical Achievement" award
- **Most competitive awards won for Acting**: 4
  - [Katharine Hepburn](/wiki/Katharine_Hepburn), all for Best Actress
- **Most competitive awards won for Directing**: 4
  - [John Ford](/wiki/John_Ford)
- **Most competitive awards won for Writing**: 3
  - [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen), all for Best Original Screenplay
  - [Charles Brackett](/wiki/Charles_Brackett), for both Best Adapted and Original Screenplay
  - [Paddy Chayefsky](/wiki/Paddy_Chayefsky), for both Best Adapted and Original Screenplay
  - [Francis Ford Coppola](/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola), for both Best Adapted and Original Screenplay
  - [Billy Wilder](/wiki/Billy_Wilder), for both Best Adapted and Original Screenplay
- **Most competitive awards won for Film Editing**: 3
  - [Ralph Dawson](/wiki/Ralph_Dawson)
  - [Michael Kahn](/wiki/Michael_Kahn_(film_editor))
  - [Daniel Mandell](/wiki/Daniel_Mandell)
  - [Thelma Schoonmaker](/wiki/Thelma_Schoonmaker)
- **Most competitive awards won for Cinematography**: 4
  - [Joseph Ruttenberg](/wiki/Joseph_Ruttenberg)
  - [Leon Shamroy](/wiki/Leon_Shamroy)
- **Most competitive awards won for Film Music Composition and Songwriting**: 9
  - [Alfred Newman](/wiki/Alfred_Newman), all for Best Original Score
  - Note:
    - [Alan Menken](/wiki/Alan_Menken) has won eight awards in musical categories
    - [John Williams](/wiki/John_Williams) has won five awards and holds the record for the most nominations by a living person at 54.
    - [Sammy Cahn](/wiki/Sammy_Cahn) won four awards, all for Best Original Song
    - [Johnny Mercer](/wiki/Johnny_Mercer) won four awards, all for Best Original Song
    - [Jimmy Van Heusen](/wiki/Jimmy_Van_Heusen) won four awards, all for Best Original Song
- **Most competitive awards won for Art Direction**: 11
  - [Cedric Gibbons](/wiki/Cedric_Gibbons), who designed the Oscar statuette, received 38 nominations
- **Most competitive awards won for Costume Design**: 8
  - [Edith Head](/wiki/Edith_Head) has received 35 nominations in total and holds the records for the most nominations and awards for a woman
- **Most competitive awards won for Makeup**: 7
  - [Rick Baker](/wiki/Rick_Baker), who has received 11 nominations in total
- **Most competitive awards won for Visual Effects**: 8
  - [Dennis Muren](/wiki/Dennis_Muren), who has received 15 nominations in total
- **Most competitive awards won for Special Effects** (discontinued in 1962): 3
  - [A. Arnold Gillespie](/wiki/A._Arnold_Gillespie), who received 12 nominations in total
- **Most awards won for Animated Feature Film**: 3
  - [Pete Docter](/wiki/Pete_Docter), who has received four nominations in total
- **Most nominations received for Animated Feature Film**: 4
  - [Pete Docter](/wiki/Pete_Docter), winning 3
  - [Hayao Miyazaki](/wiki/Hayao_Miyazaki), winning 2
  - [Chris Sanders](/wiki/Chris_Sanders), winning 0
  - [Byron Howard](/wiki/Byron_Howard), winning 2
- **Most awards won by a country for Best International Feature Film**: 14
  - [Italy](/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Academy_Awards_for_Best_International_Feature_Film), which has received 33 nominations in total
- **Most nominations received by a country for Best International Feature Film:** 43
  - [France](/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Academy_Awards_for_Best_International_Feature_Film), which has won the award 12 times
- **Most nominations received by a country for Best International Feature Film without an award**: 10
  - [Israel](/wiki/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Academy_Awards_for_Best_International_Feature_Film)
- **Most awards won by a non-English language film**: 4
  - Four non-English language films have won four Academy Awards:
    - *[Fanny and Alexander](/wiki/Fanny_and_Alexander)* (1982) won Best Foreign Language Film, Best Art Direction, Best Cinematography, and Best Costume Design
    - *[Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon](/wiki/Crouching_Tiger,_Hidden_Dragon)* (2000) won Best Foreign Language Film, Best Art Direction, Best Cinematography, and Best Original Score
    - *[Parasite](/wiki/Parasite_(2019_film))* (2019) won Best International Feature Film, Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Original Screenplay
    - *[All Quiet on the Western Front](/wiki/All_Quiet_on_the_Western_Front_(2022_film))* (2022) won Best International Feature Film, Best Production Design, Best Cinematography, and Best Original Score
- **Most nominations received by a non-English language film**: 13
  - One non-English language film has been nominated for thirteen Academy Awards:
    - *[Emilia Pérez](/wiki/Emilia_P%C3%A9rez)* (2024): Best International Feature Film, Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, Best Actress, Best Supporting Actress, Best Cinematography, Best Film Editing, Best Sound, Best Makeup and Hairstyling, Best Original Score, and Best Original Song (x2)

## Most consecutive awards in each category

- **Any awards**
  - [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) received record 10 awards in the eight consecutive years from 1931/32 through 1939. Eight (listed below) are for Short Subject (Cartoon), and two were Special Awards: one for the creation of [Mickey Mouse](/wiki/Mickey_Mouse), and one recognizing the innovation of *[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs](/wiki/Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_(1937_film))*.
- **Best Picture**
  - [David O. Selznick](/wiki/David_O._Selznick) produced two consecutive Best Picture winners *[Gone with the Wind](/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film))* in 1939 and *[Rebecca](/wiki/Rebecca_(1940_film))* in 1940 (He himself was not awarded the Oscars as at the time the statuette went to the [studio](/wiki/Film_studio) instead of the producer)
- **Best Director**
  - Three directors have won two consecutive awards (of which, one of each of their films (**in bold**) won the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture), and one did not):
    - [John Ford](/wiki/John_Ford) – *[The Grapes of Wrath](/wiki/The_Grapes_of_Wrath_(film))* (1940) and ***[How Green Was My Valley](/wiki/How_Green_Was_My_Valley_(film))*** (1941)
    - [Joseph L. Mankiewicz](/wiki/Joseph_L._Mankiewicz) – *[A Letter to Three Wives](/wiki/A_Letter_to_Three_Wives)* (1949) and ***[All About Eve](/wiki/All_About_Eve)*** (1950)
    - [Alejandro G. Iñárritu](/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu) – ***[Birdman or (The Unexpected Virtue of Ignorance)](/wiki/Birdman_(film))*** (2014) and *[The Revenant](/wiki/The_Revenant_(2015_film))* (2015)
- **Best Actor**
  - Two actors have won two consecutive awards:
    - [Spencer Tracy](/wiki/Spencer_Tracy) – *[Captains Courageous](/wiki/Captains_Courageous_(1937_film))* (1937) and *[Boys Town](/wiki/Boys_Town_(film))* (1938)
    - [Tom Hanks](/wiki/Tom_Hanks) – *[Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia_(film))* (1993) and *[Forrest Gump](/wiki/Forrest_Gump)* (1994)
- **Best Actress**
  - Two actresses have won two consecutive awards:
    - [Luise Rainer](/wiki/Luise_Rainer) – *[The Great Ziegfeld](/wiki/The_Great_Ziegfeld)* (1936) and *[The Good Earth](/wiki/The_Good_Earth_(film))* (1937)
    - [Katharine Hepburn](/wiki/Katharine_Hepburn) – *[Guess Who's Coming to Dinner](/wiki/Guess_Who%27s_Coming_to_Dinner)* (1967) and *[The Lion in Winter](/wiki/The_Lion_in_Winter_(1968_film))* (1968)
- **Best Supporting Actor**
  - [Jason Robards](/wiki/Jason_Robards) won two consecutive awards for *[All the President's Men](/wiki/All_the_President%27s_Men_(film))* in 1976 and *[Julia](/wiki/Julia_(1977_film))* in 1977
- **Best Supporting Actress**
  - No consecutive winner for Best Supporting Actress
- **Best Adapted Screenplay**
  - Two screenwriters have won two consecutive awards:
    - [Joseph L. Mankiewicz](/wiki/Joseph_L._Mankiewicz) – *[A Letter to Three Wives](/wiki/A_Letter_to_Three_Wives)* (1949) and *[All About Eve](/wiki/All_About_Eve)* (1950)
    - [Robert Bolt](/wiki/Robert_Bolt) – *[Doctor Zhivago](/wiki/Doctor_Zhivago_(film))* (1965) and *[A Man for All Seasons](/wiki/A_Man_for_All_Seasons_(1966_film))* (1966)
- **Best Original Screenplay**
  - No consecutive winner for Best Original Screenplay
- **Best Art Direction**
  - [Thomas Little](/wiki/Thomas_Little) won four consecutive awards for Best Art Direction. He won **Best Art Direction, Black and White**, for the films *[How Green Was My Valley](/wiki/How_Green_Was_My_Valley_(film))* in 1941, *[This Above All](/wiki/This_Above_All_(film))* in 1942, and *[The Song of Bernadette](/wiki/The_Song_of_Bernadette_(film))* in 1943, and then he won an Oscar the next year in 1944 for **Best Art Direction, Color** for the film *[Wilson](/wiki/Wilson_(1944_film))*
- **Best Cinematography**
  - [Emmanuel Lubezki](/wiki/Emmanuel_Lubezki) won three consecutive awards for *[Gravity](/wiki/Gravity_(2013_film))* in 2013, *[Birdman or (The Unexpected Virtue of Ignorance)](/wiki/Birdman_(film))* in 2014 and *[The Revenant](/wiki/The_Revenant_(2015_film))* in 2015
- **Best Costume Design**
  - Of [Edith Head](/wiki/Edith_Head)'s eight awards won for Best Costume Design, three were won in consecutive years: in 1949 for *[The Heiress](/wiki/The_Heiress_(film))*, in 1950 for *[All About Eve](/wiki/All_About_Eve)*, and in 1951 for *[A Place in the Sun](/wiki/A_Place_in_the_Sun_(1951_film))* for **Best Costume Design, Black-and-White**; in 1950 she also won for *[Samson and Delilah](/wiki/Samson_and_Delilah_(1949_film))* for **Best Costume Design, Color**
- **Best Film Editing**
  - [Angus Wall](/wiki/Angus_Wall) and [Kirk Baxter](/wiki/Kirk_Baxter) won for *[The Social Network](/wiki/The_Social_Network)* in 2010, and *[The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo](/wiki/The_Girl_with_the_Dragon_Tattoo_(2011_film))* in 2011
- **Best Original Score**
  - [Roger Edens](/wiki/Roger_Edens) won three consecutive awards for composing the scores for *[Easter Parade](/wiki/Easter_Parade_(film))* (1948), *[On the Town](/wiki/On_the_Town_(film))* (1949), and *[Annie Get Your Gun](/wiki/Annie_Get_Your_Gun_(film))* (1950)
  - [Alfred Newman](/wiki/Alfred_Newman) won two consecutive awards in **Best Scoring of a Musical Picture** for *[With a Song in My Heart](/wiki/With_a_Song_in_My_Heart_(film))* (1952), and *[Call Me Madam](/wiki/Call_Me_Madam_(film))* (1953).
  - [André Previn](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Previn) won two consecutive awards on two occasions; first in **Best Scoring of a Musical Picture** for *[Gigi](/wiki/Gigi_(1958_film))* (1958) and *[Porgy and Bess](/wiki/Porgy_and_Bess_(film))* (1959), then in **Best Scoring of Music-Adaptation and Treatment** for *[Irma La Douce](/wiki/Irma_la_Douce)* (1963) and *[My Fair Lady](/wiki/My_Fair_Lady_(film))* (1964)
  - [Alan Menken](/wiki/Alan_Menken) won two consecutive awards for composing the scores for *[Beauty and the Beast](/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_(1991_film))* (1991) and *[Aladdin](/wiki/Aladdin_(1992_Disney_film))* (1992)
  - [Gustavo Santaolalla](/wiki/Gustavo_Santaolalla) won two consecutive awards for composing the scores for *[Brokeback Mountain](/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain_(film))* (2005) and *[Babel](/wiki/Babel_(film))* (2006)
- **Best Original Song**
  - Three composers have won two consecutive awards for best original song, but under different award names:
    - [Henry Mancini](/wiki/Henry_Mancini) (music) and [Johnny Mercer](/wiki/Johnny_Mercer) (lyrics) shared the awards in **Best Music (Song)** for "[Moon River](/wiki/Moon_River)" from *[Breakfast at Tiffany's](/wiki/Breakfast_at_Tiffany%27s_(film))* in 1961, and "[Days of Wine and Roses](/wiki/Days_of_Wine_and_Roses_(song))" from *[Days of Wine and Roses](/wiki/Days_of_Wine_and_Roses_(film))* in 1962
    - [Alan Menken](/wiki/Alan_Menken) (music) won twice consecutively in **Best Music (Original Song)** for "[Beauty and the Beast](/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_(Disney_song))" from *[Beauty and the Beast](/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_(1991_film))* (lyrics by [Howard Ashman](/wiki/Howard_Ashman)) in 1991, and "[A Whole New World](/wiki/A_Whole_New_World)" from *[Aladdin](/wiki/Aladdin_(1992_Disney_film))* (lyrics by [Tim Rice](/wiki/Tim_Rice)) in 1992
- **Best Sound Mixing**
  - [Thomas Moulton](/wiki/Thomas_T._Moulton) won three consecutive awards for *[The Snake Pit](/wiki/The_Snake_Pit)* in 1948, *[Twelve O'Clock High](/wiki/Twelve_O%27Clock_High)* in 1949, and *[All About Eve](/wiki/All_About_Eve)* in 1950
- **Best Visual Effects**
  - [Glen Robinson](/wiki/Glen_Robinson_(visual_effects)) won four consecutive non-competitive wins *[Earthquake](/wiki/Earthquake_(1974_film))* in 1974, *[The Hindenburg](/wiki/The_Hindenburg_(film))* in 1975, and both *[King Kong](/wiki/King_Kong_(1976_film))* and *[Logan's Run](/wiki/Logan%27s_Run_(film))* in 1976
  - Of [Dennis Muren](/wiki/Dennis_Muren)'s eight Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects, three of them were consecutive wins (under different names); *[E.T. The Extra Terrestrial](/wiki/E.T._The_Extra_Terrestrial)* in 1982, *[Return of the Jedi](/wiki/Return_of_the_Jedi)* in 1983, and *[Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom](/wiki/Indiana_Jones_and_the_Temple_of_Doom)* in 1984.
  - [Jim Rygiel](/wiki/Jim_Rygiel) and [Randall William Cook](/wiki/Randall_William_Cook) won three consecutive visual effects Oscars for *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* (2001), *[The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Two_Towers)* (2002), and *[The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Return_of_the_King)* (2003)
- **Best Documentary (Feature)**
  - [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) won two consecutive awards for *[The Living Desert](/wiki/The_Living_Desert)* in 1953 and *[The Vanishing Prairie](/wiki/The_Vanishing_Prairie)* in 1954
- **Best Short Subject (Cartoon)**
  - Of [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney)'s many awards for Best Animated Short, eight of these wins were in consecutive years, for *[Flowers and Trees](/wiki/Flowers_and_Trees)* in 1931/32, *[Three Little Pigs](/wiki/Three_Little_Pigs_(film))* in 1932/33, *[The Tortoise and the Hare](/wiki/The_Tortoise_and_the_Hare_(film))* in 1934, *[Three Orphan Kittens](/wiki/Three_Orphan_Kittens)* in 1935, *[The Country Cousin](/wiki/The_Country_Cousin)* in 1936, *[The Old Mill](/wiki/The_Old_Mill)* in 1937, *[Ferdinand the Bull](/wiki/Ferdinand_the_Bull_(film))* in 1938, and *[The Ugly Duckling](/wiki/The_Ugly_Duckling_(animated_film))* in 1939
- **Best Short Subject (Two-Reel)**
  - Of [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney)'s multiple awards for Best Live Action Short, four of his wins were in consecutive years, in 1950 for *[In Beaver Valley](/wiki/In_Beaver_Valley)*, in 1951 for *[Nature's Half Acre](/wiki/Nature%27s_Half_Acre)*, in 1952 for *[Water Birds](/wiki/Water_Birds)*, and in 1953 for *[Bear Country](/wiki/Bear_Country_(1953_film))*

## Academy Award firsts

**Overall firsts**

- **First Best Picture winner**
  - *[Wings](/wiki/Wings_(1927_film))* (1927)
- **First Best Picture winning sound film**
  - *[The Broadway Melody](/wiki/The_Broadway_Melody)* (1929)
- **First Best Picture winning color film**
  - *[Gone with the Wind](/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film))* (1939)
- **First Best Director co-winners (for the same film)**
  - [Robert Wise](/wiki/Robert_Wise) and [Jerome Robbins](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins) for *[West Side Story](/wiki/West_Side_Story_(1961_film))* (1961)

**Demographic firsts (age, nationality, ethnicity, sex/gender, disability)**

- **First person born in the 20th century to be nominated for (and win) an Academy Award**
  - [Janet Gaynor](/wiki/Janet_Gaynor), for Best Actress, *[7th Heaven](/wiki/7th_Heaven_(1927_film))*, *[Street Angel](/wiki/Street_Angel_(1928_film))*, *[Sunrise](/wiki/Sunrise:_A_Song_of_Two_Humans)* (1928)
- **First person born in the 21st century to win an Academy Award**
  - [Billie Eilish](/wiki/Billie_Eilish), for [Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song), "[No Time to Die](/wiki/No_Time_to_Die_(song))" from *[No Time to Die](/wiki/No_Time_to_Die)* (2021)
- **First person born in the 21st century to be nominated for an Academy Award**
  - [Quvenzhané Wallis](/wiki/Quvenzhan%C3%A9_Wallis), for Best Actress, *[Beasts of the Southern Wild](/wiki/Beasts_of_the_Southern_Wild)* (2012)
- **First child actor to receive an acting nomination**
  - [Jackie Cooper](/wiki/Jackie_Cooper), age 9, was nominated for Best Actor for *[Skippy](/wiki/Skippy_(film))* (1931)
- **First [Millennial](/wiki/Millennial) to be nominated for (and win) an Academy Award**

**Acting firsts**

- **First actor to receive ten nominations for acting**
  - [Bette Davis](/wiki/Bette_Davis) received her tenth acting nomination (all for Best Actress) for the film *[What Ever Happened to Baby Jane?](/wiki/What_Ever_Happened_to_Baby_Jane%3F_(film))* (1962)
- **First male actor to receive ten nominations for acting**
  - [Laurence Olivier](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier) received his tenth acting nomination (nine for Best Actor and one for Best Supporting Actor) for the film *[The Boys from Brazil](/wiki/The_Boys_from_Brazil_(film))* (1978)
- **First actor to receive twenty nominations for acting**
  - [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep) received her twentieth nomination (sixteen for Best Actress and four for Best Supporting Actress) for the film *[Florence Foster Jenkins](/wiki/Florence_Foster_Jenkins_(film))* (2016)
- **First person to direct themselves to an Oscar win**
  - [Laurence Olivier](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier) won Best Actor for *[Hamlet](/wiki/Hamlet_(1948_film))* (1948), which he also directed, produced, and adapted
- **First film to win both an Academy Award and a [Golden Raspberry Award](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Awards)**
  - *[Wall Street](/wiki/Wall_Street_(1987_film))* (1987), [Michael Douglas](/wiki/Michael_Douglas) won an Academy Award for [Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) and [Daryl Hannah](/wiki/Daryl_Hannah) won a Golden Raspberry Award for [Worst Supporting Actress](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Supporting_Actress)
- **First actor to be nominated for both an Academy Award and a Golden Raspberry Award for the same performance in a film**
  - [James Coco](/wiki/James_Coco) was nominated for both Best Supporting Actor and [Worst Supporting Actor](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Supporting_Actor) for *[Only When I Laugh](/wiki/Only_When_I_Laugh_(film))* (1981)
- **First actress to be nominated for both an Academy Award and a Golden Raspberry Award for the same performance in a film**
  - [Amy Irving](/wiki/Amy_Irving) was nominated for both Best Supporting Actress and Worst Supporting Actress for *[Yentl](/wiki/Yentl_(film))* (1983)
- **First posthumous win for acting**
  - [Peter Finch](/wiki/Peter_Finch) won Best Actor for *[Network](/wiki/Network_(1976_film))* (1976)
- **First posthumous nomination for acting**
  - [Jeanne Eagels](/wiki/Jeanne_Eagels), nominated for Best Actress for *[The Letter](/wiki/The_Letter_(1929_film))* (1929)
- **First posthumous nomination for an actor**
  - [James Dean](/wiki/James_Dean), nominated for Best Actor for *[East of Eden](/wiki/East_of_Eden_(film))* (1955)
- **First posthumous nomination for a Black actor**
  - [Chadwick Boseman](/wiki/Chadwick_Boseman), nominated for Best Actor for *[Ma Rainey's Black Bottom](/wiki/Ma_Rainey%27s_Black_Bottom_(film))* (2020)
- **First person to be nominated for acting and songwriting in the same year**
  - [Mary J. Blige](/wiki/Mary_J._Blige), nominated for Best Supporting Actress and Best Original Song ("[Mighty River](/wiki/Mighty_River_(song))") for *[Mudbound](/wiki/Mudbound_(film))* (2017)
- **First person to win for acting and producing in the same year**
  - [Frances McDormand](/wiki/Frances_McDormand) won Best Actress and Best Picture for *[Nomadland](/wiki/Nomadland_(film))* (2020)
- **First actress to be nominated for performing in a language other than English**
  - [Melina Mercouri](/wiki/Melina_Mercouri) was nominated for Best Actress for *[Never on Sunday](/wiki/Never_on_Sunday)* (1960), performing in [Greek](/wiki/Greek_language)
- **First actress to win for performing in a language other than English**
  - [Sophia Loren](/wiki/Sophia_Loren) won Best Actress for *[Two Women](/wiki/Two_Women)* (1961), performing in [Italian](/wiki/Italian_language)
- **First actor to be nominated for performing in a language other than English**
  - [Marcello Mastroianni](/wiki/Marcello_Mastroianni) was nominated for Best Actor for *[Divorce Italian Style](/wiki/Divorce_Italian_Style)* (1961), performing in [Italian](/wiki/Italian_language)
- **First actor to win for performing in a language other than English**
  - [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro) won Best Supporting Actor for *[The Godfather Part II](/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II)* (1974), performing in [Italian](/wiki/Italian_language)
- **First acting win for a portrayal of a character of the opposite gender**
  - [Linda Hunt](/wiki/Linda_Hunt) won Best Supporting Actress for *[The Year of Living Dangerously](/wiki/The_Year_of_Living_Dangerously_(film))* (1982)
- **First portrayals of living persons to win in each acting category**
  - Best Actor: [Spencer Tracy](/wiki/Spencer_Tracy) as [Father Edward J. Flanagan](/wiki/Edward_J._Flanagan) in *[Boys Town](/wiki/Boys_Town_(film))* (1938)
  - Best Actress: [Sissy Spacek](/wiki/Sissy_Spacek) as [Loretta Lynn](/wiki/Loretta_Lynn) in *[Coal Miner's Daughter](/wiki/Coal_Miner%27s_Daughter_(film))* (1980)
  - Best Supporting Actor: [Jason Robards](/wiki/Jason_Robards) as [Ben Bradlee](/wiki/Ben_Bradlee) in *[All the President's Men](/wiki/All_the_President%27s_Men_(film))* (1976)
  - Best Supporting Actress: [Patty Duke](/wiki/Patty_Duke) as [Helen Keller](/wiki/Helen_Keller) in *[The Miracle Worker](/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(1962_film))* (1962)
    - Note: While [Joanne Woodward](/wiki/Joanne_Woodward)'s portrayal of Eve White in *[The Three Faces of Eve](/wiki/The_Three_Faces_of_Eve)* (1957) was based on a real person, [Chris Costner Sizemore](/wiki/Chris_Costner_Sizemore); her identity was not known until 1977.

## Acting records

See also: [List of actors with two or more Academy Awards in acting categories](/wiki/List_of_actors_with_two_or_more_Academy_Awards_in_acting_categories)

- **Most awards for Best Actress**
  - [Katharine Hepburn](/wiki/Katharine_Hepburn) with 4 awards (1933, 1967, 1968, 1981)
- **Most awards for Best Actor**
  - [Daniel Day-Lewis](/wiki/Daniel_Day-Lewis) with 3 awards (1989, 2007, 2012)
- **Most awards for Best Supporting Actor**
  - [Walter Brennan](/wiki/Walter_Brennan) with 3 awards (1936, 1938, 1940)
- **Most awards for Best Supporting Actress**
  - [Shelley Winters](/wiki/Shelley_Winters) (1959, 1965) and [Dianne Wiest](/wiki/Dianne_Wiest) (1986, 1994) with 2 awards each
- **Most consecutive Best Actress nominations**
  - Two actresses have been nominated for five consecutive years:
    - [Bette Davis](/wiki/Bette_Davis) (1938–1942)
    - [Greer Garson](/wiki/Greer_Garson) (1941–1945)
- **Most consecutive Best Actor nominations**
  - [Marlon Brando](/wiki/Marlon_Brando) with 4 nominations (1951 to 1954)
- **Most consecutive Best Supporting Actress nominations**
  - [Thelma Ritter](/wiki/Thelma_Ritter) with 4 nominations (1950 to 1953)
- **Most consecutive acting nominations across categories**
  - Two actors have been nominated for four consecutive years:
    - [Jennifer Jones](/wiki/Jennifer_Jones) (1943 Best Actress; 1944 Best Supporting Actress; 1945–1946 Best Actress)
    - [Al Pacino](/wiki/Al_Pacino) (1972 Best Supporting Actor; 1973–1975 Best Actor)
- **Actor with most nominations for acting**
  - [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson) with 12 nominations
- **Actress with most nominations for acting**
  - [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep) with 21 nominations
- **Most nominations for an actor without a win**
  - [Peter O'Toole](/wiki/Peter_O%27Toole) with 8 nominations (received an Honorary Award in 2002, prior to eighth nomination)
- **Most nominations for an actress without a win**
  - [Glenn Close](/wiki/Glenn_Close) with 8 nominations
- **Most nominations for an actor performing in a non-English language**
  - [Marcello Mastroianni](/wiki/Marcello_Mastroianni) with 3 nominations: He was nominated for Best Actor for *[Divorce, Italian Style](/wiki/Divorce,_Italian_Style)* (1962); *[A Special Day](/wiki/A_Special_Day)* (1977); and *[Dark Eyes](/wiki/Dark_Eyes_(1987_film))* (1987), performing in [Italian](/wiki/Italian_language)
- **Longest gap between first and second award**
  - [Helen Hayes](/wiki/Helen_Hayes) won in 1932 for *[The Sin of Madelon Claudet](/wiki/The_Sin_of_Madelon_Claudet)* and in 1971 for *[Airport](/wiki/Airport_(1970_film))*, a 39-year gap.
- **Longest gap between first and second nomination**
  - [Judd Hirsch](/wiki/Judd_Hirsch) was nominated in 1981 for *[Ordinary People](/wiki/Ordinary_People_(1980_film))* and in 2023 for *[The Fabelmans](/wiki/The_Fabelmans)*, a 42-year gap.
  - [Amy Madigan](/wiki/Amy_Madigan) was nominated in 1986 for *[Twice in a Lifetime](/wiki/Twice_in_a_Lifetime_(film))* and in 2026 for *[Weapons](/wiki/Weapons_(2025_film))*, a 40-year gap; winning for the latter.
- **Longest time span between first and last wins**
  - [Katharine Hepburn](/wiki/Katharine_Hepburn) won in 1934 for *[Morning Glory](/wiki/Morning_Glory_(1933_film))* and in 1982 for *[On Golden Pond](/wiki/On_Golden_Pond_(1981_film))*, a 48-year gap.
- **Longest time span between first and last nomination**
  - [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro) was nominated in 1975 for *[The Godfather Part II](/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II)* (and won); and in 2024 for *[Killers of the Flower Moon](/wiki/Killers_of_the_Flower_Moon_(film))*, a 49-year gap.
- **Most acting nominations before first win**
  - [Geraldine Page](/wiki/Geraldine_Page) and [Al Pacino](/wiki/Al_Pacino), won on their eighth nomination
- **Most posthumous nominations**
  - [James Dean](/wiki/James_Dean) with 2: *[East of Eden](/wiki/East_of_Eden_(film))* (1955) and *[Giant](/wiki/Giant_(1956_film))* (1956)
- **Most awards by a Black actor**
  - Two Black actors have won two Oscars:
    - [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington) won Best Supporting Actor for *[Glory](/wiki/Glory_(1989_film))* (1989) and Best Actor for *[Training Day](/wiki/Training_Day)* (2001)

  - [Harold Russell](/wiki/Harold_Russell) played Homer Parrish in *[The Best Years of Our Lives](/wiki/The_Best_Years_of_Our_Lives)* in 1946. For this role he received two Oscars, one for Best Supporting Actor and an Honorary Oscar "for bringing hope and courage to his fellow veterans through his appearance in *The Best Years of Our Lives*."

  - To date, technically no one has. However [Lee Grant](/wiki/Lee_Grant) won for Best Supporting Actress for *[Shampoo](/wiki/Shampoo_(film))* (1975) and she directed the Best Documentary Feature, *[Down and Out in America](/wiki/Down_and_Out_in_America)* (1986), but under the Academy rules at the time, only producers were eligible to win the award, so the award went to her producer husband and another co-producer. Under the present rules, the director would now be recognized with the Oscar.

  - [Sean Baker](/wiki/Sean_Baker_(filmmaker)) won Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Editing for *[Anora](/wiki/Anora)* (2024); as an *Anora* producer, he also received an Oscar for Best Picture.

## Oscar speeches

Main article: [Oscar speech](/wiki/Oscar_speech)

- **Longest speech**
  - [Adrien Brody](/wiki/Adrien_Brody) spoke for 5 minutes and 40 seconds while accepting [Best Actor](/wiki/Best_Actor_Academy_Award) for *[The Brutalist](/wiki/The_Brutalist)* at the [97th Academy Awards](/wiki/97th_Academy_Awards) in 2025. Prior to that, the longest Oscar speech was given by [Greer Garson](/wiki/Greer_Garson), who spoke for 5 minutes and 30 seconds at the [15th Academy Awards](/wiki/15th_Academy_Awards) after she was named [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress) for [1942](/wiki/1942_in_film) for *[Mrs. Miniver](/wiki/Mrs._Miniver)*. It was shortly after this incident that the academy set forty-five seconds as the allotted time for an acceptance speech and began to cut the winners off after this time limit. When presenting the Best Actor award at the [24th Academy Awards](/wiki/24th_Academy_Awards), Garson quipped, "I think I have ten minutes left over from a highly emotional speech I made a few years ago. I'd be glad to give it to them."
- **Shortest speech**
  - The shortest Oscar speech was that given by [Patty Duke](/wiki/Patty_Duke) at the [35th Academy Awards](/wiki/35th_Academy_Awards) after she was named [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress) for [1962](/wiki/1962_in_film) for *[The Miracle Worker](/wiki/The_Miracle_Worker_(1962_film))*. Duke, age 16, was the youngest person at that time to receive an Academy Award in a competitive category. Her acceptance speech was two words, "Thank you", after which she walked off the stage.

**James Alan Hetfield** (born August 3, 1963) is an American musician. He is the lead vocalist, rhythm guitarist, co-founder, and a primary songwriter of [heavy metal](/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) band [Metallica](/wiki/Metallica). He is mainly known for his raspy voice and intricate rhythm playing, but occasionally performs lead guitar duties and solos both live and in studio. Hetfield co-founded Metallica in October 1981 after answering an advertisement by drummer [Lars Ulrich](/wiki/Lars_Ulrich) in the Los Angeles newspaper *[The Recycler](/wiki/The_Recycler)*. Metallica has won 10 [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Award) and released 11 studio albums, three live albums, four extended plays, and 24 singles. Hetfield is often regarded as one of the greatest rhythm guitar players of all time.

In 2009, Hetfield was ranked at No. 8 in [Joel McIver](/wiki/Joel_McIver)'s book *The 100 Greatest Metal Guitarists* and No. 24 by [Hit Parader](/wiki/Hit_Parader) on their list of the *100 Greatest Metal Vocalists of All Time*. In [Guitar World](/wiki/Guitar_World)'s poll, Hetfield was placed as the 19th greatest guitarist of all time, as well as being placed second (along with Metallica lead guitarist [Kirk Hammett](/wiki/Kirk_Hammett)) in *The 100 Greatest Metal Guitarists* poll of the same magazine. *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* placed him along with bandmate Kirk Hammett as the 23rd greatest guitarist of all time.

## Early life

Hetfield was born on August 3, 1963, in [Downey, California](/wiki/Downey,_California), the son of Cynthia Bassett (*[née](/wiki/N%C3%A9e)* Nourse), a [light opera](/wiki/Light_opera) singer, and Virgil Lee Hetfield, a truck driver. He is of English, German, Irish, and Scottish descent. He has two older half-brothers from his mother's first marriage and one younger sister. His parents divorced in 1976 when Hetfield was 13. They were strict [Christian Scientists](/wiki/Christian_Science), and in accordance with their beliefs, they strongly disapproved of medicine or any other medical treatment and remained loyal to their faith, even as Cynthia was dying from cancer. This upbringing became the inspiration for many of Hetfield's lyrics during his career with Metallica, most notably in the songs "[Dyers Eve](/wiki/Dyers_Eve)" and "[The God That Failed](/wiki/The_God_That_Failed_(song))" from *[...And Justice for All](/wiki/...And_Justice_for_All_(album))* and *[Metallica](/wiki/Metallica_(album))*, respectively. Cynthia died of cancer in February 1980, when Hetfield was 16 years old. After the death of his mother, Hetfield went to live with his older half-brother David. Virgil died in early 1996, before the release of Metallica's *[Load](/wiki/Load_(album))*. Hetfield attended [Downey High School](/wiki/Downey_High_School) for his freshman and sophomore years and graduated from [Brea Olinda High School](/wiki/Brea_Olinda_High_School) in 1981.

Hetfield was nine years old when he first began piano lessons, after which he took on his half-brother David's drums and finally, at the age of 14, he began to play guitar with Robert Okner. He was also in a few bands as a teenager – one being [Leather Charm](/wiki/Leather_Charm), and another, Obsession. Hetfield identifies [Aerosmith](/wiki/Aerosmith) as having been his main musical influence as a child, and has said they were the reason he wanted to play guitar.

## Career

Hetfield in 2008

In the early days of the band, Metallica experimented with a few different vocal and guitar combinations, essentially creating a setup similar to that of British metal band [Diamond Head](/wiki/Diamond_Head_(English_band)), another major influence on Hetfield. Some of the options considered included adding another guitar player, having John Roads play lead guitar, as well as asking [John Bush](/wiki/John_Bush_(singer)) from [Armored Saint](/wiki/Armored_Saint) (who later joined [Anthrax](/wiki/Anthrax_(American_band))) to sing for the band. The finalized lineup of the band became Hetfield (lead vocals and rhythm guitar), [Lars Ulrich](/wiki/Lars_Ulrich) (drums), [Dave Mustaine](/wiki/Dave_Mustaine) (lead guitar), and [Ron McGovney](/wiki/Ron_McGovney) (bass), who was soon replaced by [Cliff Burton](/wiki/Cliff_Burton). Hetfield referred to their early sound as [power metal](/wiki/Power_metal). The term "[thrash metal](/wiki/Thrash_metal)" was first used when *[Kerrang](/wiki/Kerrang)* journalist [Malcolm Dome](/wiki/Malcolm_Dome) described the Anthrax song "[Metal Thrashing Mad](/wiki/Metal_Thrashing_Mad)" in an issue of *Kerrang* in February 1984.

From 1982 to 1983, Mustaine's aggressive behavior and [drinking problems](/wiki/Alcohol_abuse) led to mounting tensions between himself and Hetfield. Mustaine once poured beer onto McGovney's bass. On April 1, 1983, the band recruited lead guitarist [Kirk Hammett](/wiki/Kirk_Hammett) from the band [Exodus](/wiki/Exodus_(band)), and 10 days later, Hetfield and Ulrich officially fired Mustaine from the band due to his erratic indifference. Mustaine was sent home on a four-day bus journey from New York to Los Angeles, and went on to form the [heavy metal](/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) band [Megadeth](/wiki/Megadeth).

Since the recording of *[Load](/wiki/Load_(album))*, Hammett has been recording rhythm guitars as well. Hetfield occasionally plays guitar solos on songs such as "[Nothing Else Matters](/wiki/Nothing_Else_Matters)", "My Friend of Misery", "Just a Bullet Away", the outro solo on "The Outlaw Torn", the second solo on "[To Live Is to Die](/wiki/To_Live_Is_to_Die)", the first solo on "Suicide and Redemption", the first solo on "[Master of Puppets](/wiki/Master_of_Puppets_(song))", and the harmonized solo on " [Orion](/wiki/Orion_(instrumental))". He also writes the majority of the guitar harmonies, as well as writing the lyrics, vocal melodies, and co-arranging the songs with Ulrich.

Hetfield was involved in an on-stage accident at [Olympic Stadium](/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_(Montreal)) in Montreal during the [Guns N' Roses/Metallica Stadium Tour](/wiki/Guns_N%27_Roses/Metallica_Stadium_Tour) on August 8, 1992. During the song "Fade to Black", a pyrotechnic charge reacted. Hetfield's guitar protected him from the full force of the blast, but it struck his left side, burning his hand, arm, eyebrows, face and hair. He suffered second and third-degree burns, but was back on stage 17 days later, although his guitar duties were delegated to former guitar tech and [Metal Church](/wiki/Metal_Church) guitarist [John Marshall](/wiki/John_Marshall_(guitarist)) for four weeks while he made a full recovery.

Hetfield also suffered a broken arm a number of times while skateboarding, which prevented him from playing guitar on stage, and subsequently caused Hetfield's management company, [Q Prime](/wiki/Q_Prime), to put a clause in Hetfield's contract forbidding him to ride a skateboard while Metallica was touring. During a live performance on tour for *Metallica*, Hetfield experienced complications with his vocals after performing a cover of the [Anti-Nowhere League](/wiki/Anti-Nowhere_League) song "[So What?](/wiki/So_What%3F_(Anti-Nowhere_League_song))", forcing him to take vocal lessons for the first time. He did basic warm-up exercises to piano keys with his vocal coach, who also gave him a cassette tape of the piano warm-up for future use. Hetfield still uses the same cassette he was given in the early 1990s to this day before any live performance or any recording Metallica does. Hetfield talks about his vocal training endeavors in the Metallica documentary film, *[Metallica: Some Kind of Monster](/wiki/Metallica:_Some_Kind_of_Monster)*, produced and directed by [Joe Berlinger](/wiki/Joe_Berlinger) and [Bruce Sinofsky](/wiki/Bruce_Sinofsky).

During the recording of the band's eighth studio album *[St. Anger](/wiki/St._Anger)* in 2001, Hetfield went into [rehab](/wiki/Substance-abuse_rehabilitation) to address his alcohol usage. He rejoined the band after seven months in rehab and four months recovering with his family. His health problems are featured in *Some Kind of Monster*. *Some Kind of Monster* also shows the making of the *St. Anger* album, and documents the various conflicts and issues the band were facing at the time including the departure of [Jason Newsted](/wiki/Jason_Newsted), alcoholism, family commitments, and the future of the band.

Hetfield and Metallica recruited [Ozzy Osbourne](/wiki/Ozzy_Osbourne) bassist [Robert Trujillo](/wiki/Robert_Trujillo). Osbourne replaced him with Jason Newsted shortly after Trujillo's transfer. The new lineup has continued to make music and tour worldwide. Metallica's ninth studio album, *[Death Magnetic](/wiki/Death_Magnetic)*, was released on September 12, 2008. Like *St. Anger* and every album of original material released by Metallica since 1991's *Metallica*, *Death Magnetic* went to #1 on the Billboard charts in over 30 countries during its first week of release.

On April 4, 2009, Hetfield, along with remaining Metallica members Ulrich, Hammett, Trujillo, as well as former bassist Newsted, and the deceased Cliff Burton (who was represented by his father), were inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame). In an interview after their nomination, Hetfield commented that everyone who had appeared on an album with the band would be inducted. This excluded original guitarist [Dave Mustaine](/wiki/Dave_Mustaine) and original bassist [Ron McGovney](/wiki/Ron_McGovney), as both had appeared only on the band's early demo tapes. Hetfield and the rest of Metallica, including Newsted, performed "[Master of Puppets](/wiki/Master_of_Puppets_(song))" and "[Enter Sandman](/wiki/Enter_Sandman)" to end the ceremony.

## Playing style

Hetfield in 2024

Hetfield has been called "The King of [Downpicking](/wiki/Downpicking)" for his right-hand picking speed by [Annihilator](/wiki/Annihilator_(band)) guitarist [Jeff Waters](/wiki/Jeff_Waters). He has explained he holds the pick with his thumb, index and middle fingers, rather than just the thumb and index, citing additional stability he gains by it.

## Personal life

Hetfield backstage at the [Big Day Out](/wiki/Big_Day_Out) 2004

Hetfield married Francesca Tomasi on August 17, 1997, and together they have three children. He currently resides in [Vail, Colorado](/wiki/Vail,_Colorado), citing a "multitude of reasons" for moving there, including it being his ex-wife's childhood hometown, the natural beauty, and the quiet environment. During an interview on [NPR](/wiki/NPR)'s *[Fresh Air](/wiki/Fresh_Air)*, Hetfield stated that his ex-wife had helped him to mature and learn to deal with his anger issues more constructively, explaining that after they met, his destructive tendencies embarrassed both of them. Hetfield filed for divorce in 2022 citing irreconcilable differences. He is currently engaged to Adriana Gillett.

Hetfield's son Castor is also a musician, playing for the [Savannah, Georgia](/wiki/Savannah,_Georgia)-based band Bastardane as their drummer.

Hetfield has struggled with [substance abuse](/wiki/Substance_abuse) and [addiction](/wiki/Addiction) at various points throughout his life. In 2001, he entered a [rehab](/wiki/Drug_rehabilitation) program (documented in the film *[Metallica: Some Kind of Monster](/wiki/Metallica:_Some_Kind_of_Monster)*), and by early 2002, he began maintaining total [abstinence](/wiki/Abstinence) from alcohol. In a 2010 interview with *So What!*, the official magazine of Metallica's fan club, Hetfield stated that he is a "reborn [straight edge](/wiki/Straight_edge)". However, in 2019, Hetfield once again entered rehab, forcing Metallica to cancel their Australia/New Zealand leg of the [WorldWired Tour](/wiki/WorldWired_Tour). Hetfield made his first public appearance since leaving rehab at the [Petersen Automotive Museum](/wiki/Petersen_Automotive_Museum) reception on January 30, 2020.

Hetfield enjoys a variety of activities, most notably [hunting](/wiki/Hunting); [farming](/wiki/Agriculture) and [beekeeping](/wiki/Beekeeping); [customizing cars and motorcycles in his garage](/wiki/Car_tuning); watching the [Las Vegas Raiders](/wiki/Las_Vegas_Raiders), the [San Francisco Giants](/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants), and the [San Jose Sharks](/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks); and attending [hot rod](/wiki/Hot_rod) shows. He put his 1968 [Chevrolet Camaro](/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro) up for sale on [eBay](/wiki/EBay), with the proceeds going to a Music for Schools program. The car was used in the video for "[I Disappear](/wiki/I_Disappear)" and was given to him as a gift upon the video's completion. "Slowburn", his 1936 Auburn boat tail speedster, won the 2010 [Goodguys](/wiki/Goodguys_Rod_%26_Custom_Association) West Coast Custom of the Year.

Hetfield has a number of [tattoos](/wiki/Tattoo), including one which shows flames encasing four playing cards – ace (1), 9, 6, and 3 – representing the year of his birth, and the words "[Carpe Diem](/wiki/Carpe_Diem)" ("seize the day"). The flames on the tattoo are in reference to the pyrotechnic accident which he suffered in 1992 during a concert in [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal). He has tattooed an "M" and "81" on his right hand for "Metallica" and the year Metallica was founded, 1981, and an "F" on his left hand for "Francesca", which has since been turned into "Forgiven". He also has some Christian tattoos, including [crosses](/wiki/Cross) and one of [Jesus](/wiki/Jesus) on his right arm. He has a tattoo of two razors forming the [straight edge X symbol](/wiki/Straight_edge#X_symbol) on his left wrist.

The first single Hetfield ever bought was "[Sweet Home Alabama](/wiki/Sweet_Home_Alabama)" by [Lynyrd Skynyrd](/wiki/Lynyrd_Skynyrd). He was featured in a documentary called *Absent*, directed by [Justin Hunt](/wiki/Justin_Hunt_(filmmaker)), which takes a look at the effects of absent fathers on their children and the "father wound" that they leave behind.

Hetfield has expressed his [disdain for politics](/wiki/Apoliticism) and celebrities who "[soapbox](/wiki/Soapbox) their opinions", stating that "for us, people are people – you should all have your own opinion". In 2007, Metallica performed at London's [Live Earth concert](/wiki/Live_Earth_concert,_London). When Hetfield was asked for his thoughts on [climate change](/wiki/Climate_change), he responded by saying:

> I really avoided the press around the Live Earth day. I didn't quite agree with what was going on there. Politics drive me crazy, and I don't like talking politics. Politics get in the way of things; they get in the way of getting things done, and getting our music across is what we wanna do – we don't wanna cloud it with Democrat, Republican, whatever. Our philosophy is 'think for yourself' at the end of the day – do what you think feels right. I really believe that humans will survive. I have a lot of faith in mankind that we will overcome and adapt – whatever it is; whether it's man-made or God-made, or Earth/Mother Nature – we have a lot of smart people on this planet that will make something good out of bad.

In 2008, responding to the use of Metallica's music to torture Iraqi prisoners, Hetfield was unoffended and replied, "If the Iraqis aren't used to freedom, then I'm glad to be part of their exposure." British band [Chumbawamba](/wiki/Chumbawamba) subsequently released a song called "Torturing James Hetfield" in response. In a 2017 interview, Hetfield reaffirmed his previous stance, saying, "I'm honored my country is using something to help us stay safe, if they are. But then again, once the music is out, I don't have control over that. Just like how someone's giving it away online. They're using it to do what they do."

### Guitars

Hetfield has been a major endorser of [ESP Guitars](/wiki/ESP_Guitars) since the 1980s, and is best known for playing custom-made [Explorer](/wiki/Gibson_Explorer)-style guitars with an [EMG 81](/wiki/EMG_81)/[EMG 60](/wiki/EMG_60) set for [pickups](/wiki/Pick_up_(music_technology)) and since then he has his own signature EMG JH pickups. During the mid-1990s, [ESP](/wiki/ESP_Guitars) produced the first of his [signature model guitars](/wiki/ESP_James_Hetfield). To date, Hetfield has had six signature guitars with the company. However, Hetfield often uses guitars from [Gibson](/wiki/Gibson_Guitars) and other companies instead of ESP despite his endorsement.

### Amplifiers and cabinets

Throughout Metallica's career, Hetfield has used a wide range of [amplifiers](/wiki/Amplifier). For the first two albums, he used [Marshall](/wiki/Marshall_Amplification) heads and cabinets, with occasional effects. The specific Marshall that he used for *Kill 'Em All* was stolen after a concert prior to the recording of *Ride the Lightning*; Hetfield was upset by the theft, as his mother had helped him purchase the amplifier before her death. In 1985, for the recording of *[Master of Puppets](/wiki/Master_of_Puppets)*, he and [Kirk Hammett](/wiki/Kirk_Hammett) each bought a [Mesa/Boogie](/wiki/Mesa/Boogie) [Mark IIC+](/wiki/Mark_IIC%2B) amplifier (the preamp sections of which were connected to Marshall power amplifiers), and since then he has mostly used Mesa/Boogies, including the Triaxis and [Rectifier](/wiki/Mesa/Boogie_Rectifier) models. Around the time of *[St. Anger](/wiki/St._Anger)*, Hetfield began using the [Diezel](/wiki/Diezel) VH4 head. The majority of his clean tones come from a [Roland JC-120 Jazz Chorus Guitar Amplifier](/wiki/Roland_JC-120_Jazz_Chorus_Guitar_Amplifier), although many different amplifiers have been used over the years.

In December 2011, it was announced by Fortin Amps that they would team up with [Randall Amplifiers](/wiki/Randall_Amplifiers) to start a new line of tube amplifiers based on the Fortin Meathead amplifier. [Kirk Hammett](/wiki/Kirk_Hammett) currently has two prototypes, and Hetfield will eventually be receiving one.

The amplifiers currently used on tour by Hetfield are:

- [Mesa/Boogie](/wiki/Mesa/Boogie) TriAxis [preamp](/wiki/Preamp) (x4)
- Mesa/Boogie Simul-Class 2:90 power amp (x2)
- Mesa/Boogie [Triple Rectifier](/wiki/Triple_Rectifier) heads
- [Diezel](/wiki/Diezel) VH4 heads
- Mesa/Boogie 4x12 cabinets with [Celestion](/wiki/Celestion) V30 speakers in [isolation cabinets](/wiki/Isolation_cabinet_(guitar))
- [Roland JC-120](/wiki/Roland_JC-120_Jazz_Chorus_Guitar_Amplifier) combo amplifiers

### Effects

To avoid problems with pedals being damaged during live performances, Hetfield keeps his effect pedals in a rack along with his amplifiers and his guitar technician controls them through a pedalboard sidestage. The pedal controller allows him to change between different effect pedals and amplifiers.

## Guest appearances

### On stage

- Hetfield sang "[Baby Hold On](/wiki/Baby_Hold_On)" at the [Eddie Money](/wiki/Eddie_Money) Tribute Concert on February 23, 2020, in Beverly Hills.
- Hetfield sang "[Stone Cold Crazy](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Crazy)" at the [Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert](/wiki/Freddie_Mercury_Tribute_Concert), accompanied by the remaining members of [Queen](/wiki/Queen_(band)) and [Tony Iommi](/wiki/Tony_Iommi) on rhythm guitar.
- Hetfield performed at the Outlaws Concert in 2004, alongside [Hank Williams Jr.](/wiki/Hank_Williams_Jr.), [Cowboy Troy](/wiki/Cowboy_Troy), [Big & Rich](/wiki/Big_%26_Rich), [Gretchen Wilson](/wiki/Gretchen_Wilson), and [Kid Rock](/wiki/Kid_Rock). Hetfield played one of close friend [Waylon Jennings](/wiki/Waylon_Jennings)'s songs, "[Don't You Think This Outlaw Bit's Done Got Out of Hand](/wiki/Don%27t_You_Think_This_Outlaw_Bit%27s_Done_Got_Out_of_Hand)", the very same he recorded on the tribute album to Jennings, *[I've Always Been Crazy](/wiki/I%27ve_Always_Been_Crazy)*. At this same event, he performed Metallica's "[Mama Said](/wiki/Mama_Said_(Metallica_song))" with Jennings' widow Jessi Colter, though this was cut from the television broadcast.
- Hetfield appeared onstage with [Alice in Chains](/wiki/Alice_in_Chains) on June 2, 2006, at [Rock am Ring](/wiki/Rock_am_Ring_and_Rock_im_Park), singing deceased vocalist [Layne Staley](/wiki/Layne_Staley)'s parts on the song "[Would?](/wiki/Would%3F)". He again appeared with Alice in Chains to perform "Would?" when they played at [The Warfield](/wiki/The_Warfield) in San Francisco on November 26, 2006.
- On November 17, 2000, Hetfield was at a [Misfits](/wiki/Misfits_(band)) concert accompanied by his body guard at [Maritime Hall](/wiki/Maritime_Hall) in San Francisco, where he eventually got up on the stage and sang "[Last Caress](/wiki/Beware_(EP))", then "[Die, Die My Darling](/wiki/Die,_Die_My_Darling)" whilst being accompanied by the band.

### Musical collaborations

- Hetfield provided backing vocals on "Man or Ash" on the [Corrosion of Conformity](/wiki/Corrosion_of_Conformity) album *[Wiseblood](/wiki/Wiseblood_(Corrosion_of_Conformity_album))*.
- Hetfield sang backing vocals on "Twist of Cain" and "Possession" on [Danzig](/wiki/Danzig_(band))'s debut album *[Danzig](/wiki/Danzig_(album))*.
- Hetfield played guitar on "Eclectic Electric" from the [Primus](/wiki/Primus_(band)) album *[Antipop](/wiki/Antipop)*.
- Hetfield performed guest vocals on "Drivin' Rain" on the [Gov't Mule](/wiki/Gov%27t_Mule) album *[The Deep End, Volume 2](/wiki/The_Deep_End,_Volume_2)* and on the soundtrack *NASCAR on Fox: Crank It Up* (2002).
- Hetfield performed guest vocals on the [Heart](/wiki/Heart_(band)) song "Beautiful Broken" from their [album of the same name](/wiki/Beautiful_Broken) which was released on July 8, 2016.

### Film

Though previously believed to have been sung by [Trey Parker](/wiki/Trey_Parker), the song "Hell Isn't Good" from the film *[South Park: Bigger, Longer and Uncut](/wiki/South_Park:_Bigger,_Longer_and_Uncut)* was actually sung by Hetfield. He admitted to doing so in 2000, confirming what many Hetfield fans had already recognized as his trademark growling style.

# List of African-American United States presidential and vice presidential candidates

In [1848](/wiki/1848_United_States_presidential_election), [Frederick Douglass](/wiki/Frederick_Douglass) became the first African-American presidential candidate of the US. His candidacy largely preceded [black suffrage](/wiki/Black_suffrage#United_States) and coincided with [legal slavery](/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) in the U.S.

In 2008, [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) became the first African-American presidential candidate nominated by a [major party](/wiki/Major_party), namely [the Democrats](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)). He was the first African-American to be [elected](/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election) (and [re-elected](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election)) president of the United States.

In [2021](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election), [Kamala Harris](/wiki/Kamala_Harris) became the first African-American, first female, and first Asian-American vice president of the United States. In [2024](/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election), she became the first African-American woman nominated for president by a major [political party](/wiki/Political_party).

The following is a list of [African-American](/wiki/African_Americans) United States [presidential](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) and [vice presidential](/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States) nominees and candidates for nomination. Nominees are candidates [nominated](/wiki/Nominate) or otherwise selected by [political parties](/wiki/Political_parties) for particular offices. Listed are those African-Americans who achieved [ballot access](/wiki/Ballot_access) for the national election in at least one [state](/wiki/U.S._state). They may have won the [nomination](/wiki/Nomination) of one of the [US political parties](/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_the_United_States) (either one of the major parties, or one of the [third parties](/wiki/Third_party_(United_States))), or made the [ballot](/wiki/Ballot) as an [independent](/wiki/Independent_(politician)), and in either case must have [votes](/wiki/Voting) in the [election](/wiki/Election) to qualify for this list. Exception is made for candidates whose parties lost ballot status for additional runs.

Not included in the first and second sections are African-Americans who ran unsuccessful campaigns in [nominating conventions](/wiki/United_States_presidential_nominating_convention) or [primary elections](/wiki/Partisan_primary) for their party's nomination (or who have not yet completed that process), [write-in candidates](/wiki/Write-in_candidates), potential candidates (suggested by media, objects of [draft movements](/wiki/Draft_(politics)), etc.), or fictional candidates. The third section includes African-Americans who ran for their party's presidential nomination but who were not nominated, as well as those who are currently pursuing their party's presidential nomination (when applicable).

There have been two African Americans on major party tickets in U.S. history: Democratic presidential nominee [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) in [2008](/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election) and [2012](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election) and Democratic vice presidential nominee [Kamala Harris](/wiki/Kamala_Harris) in [2020](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election) and presidential nominee in [2024](/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election).

Barack Obama was the first African American and first biracial president of the United States, being elected in the [2008 election](/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election) and re-elected in the [2012 election](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election).

Kamala Harris became the first African-American vice president of the United States of America, being elected in the 2020 election alongside President [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden). She is also the first female vice president. She is the second biracial vice president, the first being Republican [Charles Curtis](/wiki/Charles_Curtis).

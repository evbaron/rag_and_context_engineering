This article is about the town in Wales. For the South African town near Cape Town, see [Llandudno, Western Cape](/wiki/Llandudno,_Western_Cape).

**Llandudno** ([/lænˈdɪdnoʊ/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English), Welsh: [[ɬanˈdɨdnɔ]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Welsh) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:LL-Q9309_(cym)-Jason.nlw-Llandudno.wav)) is a [seaside resort](/wiki/Seaside_resort), town and [community](/wiki/Community_(Wales)) in [Conwy County Borough](/wiki/Conwy_County_Borough), Wales, located on the [Creuddyn peninsula](/wiki/Creuddyn_peninsula), which protrudes into the [Irish Sea](/wiki/Irish_Sea). In the [2021 UK census](/wiki/2021_United_Kingdom_census), the community – which includes [Gogarth](/wiki/Great_Orme), [Penrhyn Bay](/wiki/Penrhyn_Bay), Craigside, [Glanwydden](/wiki/Glanwydden), Penrhynside, and [Bryn Pydew](/wiki/Bryn_Pydew) – had a population of 19,700 (rounded to the nearest 100). The town's name means "Church of [Saint Tudno](/wiki/Saint_Tudno)".

Llandudno is a major seaside resort in Wales, and as early as 1861 was being called 'the Queen of the Welsh Watering Places' (a phrase later also used in connection with [Tenby](/wiki/Tenby) and [Aberystwyth](/wiki/Aberystwyth); the word 'resort' came a little later). [Historically](/wiki/Historic_counties_of_Wales) a part of [Caernarfonshire](/wiki/Caernarfonshire), Llandudno was formerly in the [district of Aberconwy](/wiki/District_of_Aberconwy) within [Gwynedd](/wiki/Gwynedd) until 1996.

## History

Llandudno, from the parade, 1860

The town of Llandudno developed from [Stone Age](/wiki/Stone_Age) all the way through to Iron Age settlements over many hundreds of years on the slopes of the limestone headland, known to seafarers as the [Great Orme](/wiki/Great_Orme) and to landsmen as the Creuddyn Peninsula. The origins in recorded history are with the Manor of Gogarth conveyed by [King Edward I](/wiki/Edward_I_of_England) to Annan, [Bishop of Bangor](/wiki/Bishop_of_Bangor) in 1284. The manor comprised three townships, *Y Gogarth* in the southwest, *Y Cyngreawdr* in the north (with the parish church of St Tudno) and *Yr Wyddfid* in the southeast.

Modern Llandudno takes its name from the ancient parish of Saint Tudno. The modern town has grown beyond the ancient parish boundaries to encompass several neighbouring area, including [Craig-y-Don](/wiki/Craig-y-Don) and Penrhyn Bay, which were in the parish of [Llanrhos](/wiki/Llanrhos) (or Eglwys Rhos), which also included Llanrhos village and [Deganwy](/wiki/Deganwy). The ancient parishes of Llandudno, Llanrhos and [Llangystennin](/wiki/Llangystennin) were in the medieval [commote](/wiki/Commote) of [Creuddyn](/wiki/Creuddyn,_Rhos) in the [Kingdom of Gwynedd](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Gwynedd), which was made part of the new county of Caernarfonshire under the [Statute of Rhuddlan](/wiki/Statute_of_Rhuddlan) in 1284.

### Great Orme

Main article: [Great Orme](/wiki/Great_Orme)

Llandudno South Parade (on the north shore) viewed from the Great Orme, with the twin mounds of Deganwy Castle in the distance

Mostly owned by Mostyn Estates, the Great Orme is home to several large herds of wild Kashmiri goats originally descended from a pair presented by [the Shah of Persia](/wiki/Mohammad_Shah_Qajar) to [Queen Victoria](/wiki/Queen_Victoria) and subsequently given to [Lord Mostyn](/wiki/Edward_Mostyn_Lloyd-Mostyn,_2nd_Baron_Mostyn). These goats are also known to frequently roam the main streets of Llandudno. The summit of the Great Orme stands at 679 feet (207 m). The Summit Hotel, now a tourist attraction, was once the home of world middleweight champion boxer [Randolph Turpin](/wiki/Randolph_Turpin).

The limestone headland is a haven for flora and fauna, with some rare species such as peregrine falcons and a species of wild cotoneaster (*[cambricus](/wiki/Cotoneaster_cambricus)*) which can only be found on the Great Orme. The sheer limestone cliffs provide ideal nesting conditions for a wide variety of sea birds, including cormorants, shags, guillemots, razorbills, puffins, kittiwakes, fulmars and numerous gulls.

There are several attractions including the [Great Orme Tramway](/wiki/Great_Orme_Tramway) and the [Llandudno Cable Car](/wiki/Llandudno_Cable_Car) that takes tourists to the summit. The Great Orme also has the longest toboggan run in Britain at 750m long.

### Development

A view of the [Great Orme](/wiki/Great_Orme) from the Llandudno [Lighthouse](/wiki/Lighthouse)

By 1847 the town had grown to a thousand people, served by the new church of St George, built in 1840. The great majority of the men worked in the copper mines, with others employed in fishing and subsistence agriculture.

In 1848, Owen Williams, an architect and surveyor from [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool), presented Lord Mostyn with plans to develop the marshlands behind Llandudno Bay as a holiday resort. These were enthusiastically pursued by Lord Mostyn. The influence of the Mostyn Estate and its agents over the years was paramount in the development of Llandudno, especially after the appointment of George Felton as surveyor and architect in 1857. Between 1857 and 1877 much of central Llandudno was developed under Felton's supervision. Felton also undertook architectural design work, including the design and execution of Holy Trinity Church in Mostyn Street.

## Transport

[Llandudno Cable Car](/wiki/Llandudno_Cable_Car)

### Railway

The town is served by [Llandudno railway station](/wiki/Llandudno_railway_station), which is the northern terminus of a 3 miles (4.8 km) long [branch line](/wiki/Llandudno_branch_line) from [Llandudno Junction](/wiki/Llandudno_Junction_railway_station). There are generally half-hourly services between the two stations; some trains continue to [Blaenau Ffestiniog](/wiki/Blaenau_Ffestiniog_railway_station) or to [Manchester Airport](/wiki/Manchester_Airport_railway_station).

Llandudno Junction station is sited on the [North Wales Coast railway line](/wiki/North_Wales_Coast_Line), which was opened as the [Chester and Holyhead Railway](/wiki/Chester_and_Holyhead_Railway) in 1848. It became part of the [London and North Western Railway](/wiki/London_and_North_Western_Railway) in 1859 and part of the [London, Midland and Scottish Railway](/wiki/London,_Midland_and_Scottish_Railway) in 1923. Llandudno was specifically built as a mid-[Victorian era](/wiki/Victorian_era) holiday destination and the branch line opened in 1858, with an intermediate station at [Deganwy](/wiki/Deganwy_railway_station).

### Buses

The town is served by [Arriva Buses Wales](/wiki/Arriva_Buses_Wales), with services to [Rhyl](/wiki/Rhyl), [Bangor](/wiki/Bangor,_Gwynedd), [Caernarfon](/wiki/Caernarfon) and the [Great Orme](/wiki/Great_Orme) summit. In addition, Llew Jones provides services to [Betws-y-coed](/wiki/Betws-y-coed) and [Llanrwst](/wiki/Llanrwst).

### Trams

The [Great Orme Tramway](/wiki/Great_Orme_Tramway) runs from the centre of the town to the summit of the Great Orme and is [Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain)'s only remaining cable-operated street tramway.

The former [Llandudno and Colwyn Bay Electric Railway](/wiki/Llandudno_and_Colwyn_Bay_Electric_Railway) operated an electric tramway service between Llandudno and [Rhos-on-Sea](/wiki/Rhos-on-Sea) from 1907; this was extended to [Colwyn Bay](/wiki/Colwyn_Bay) in 1908. The service closed in 1956. In Llandudno, the original tramway went up the middle of Gloddaeth Street, down Mostyn Street, through Penrhyn Bay and across to Colwyn Bay.

## Attractions

### Llandudno Bay and the North Shore

Venue Cymru – the North Wales Theatre near the centre of the promenade

For most of the length of Llandudno's *North Shore* there is a wide curving Victorian promenade. The road, collectively known as The Parade, has a different name for each block and it is on these parades and crescents that many of Llandudno's hotels are built. The *North Wales Theatre, Arena and Conference Centre*, built in 1994, and extended in 2006 and renamed "[Venue Cymru](/wiki/Venue_Cymru)" is located near the centre of the bay.

### Llandudno Pier

Llandudno pier from Marine Drive

The [Llandudno Pier](/wiki/Llandudno_Pier) is on the *North Shore*. Built in 1877, it is a Grade II [listed building](/wiki/Listed_building). The pier was extended in 1884 in a landward direction along the side of what was the Baths Hotel (where the [Grand Hotel](/wiki/Grand_Hotel_(Llandudno)) now stands).

### Happy Valley and Haulfre Gardens

The Happy Valley, a former quarry, was the gift of Lord Mostyn to the town in celebration of the [Golden Jubilee](/wiki/Golden_Jubilee) of [Queen Victoria](/wiki/Victoria_of_the_United_Kingdom) in 1887. The area was landscaped and developed as gardens, two miniature golf courses, a putting green, a popular open-air theatre and extensive lawns. The ceremonies connected with the [Welsh National Eisteddfod](/wiki/Welsh_National_Eisteddfod) were held there in 1896 and again in 1963. The gardens are listed at Grade II on the [Cadw/ICOMOS Register of Parks and Gardens of Special Historic Interest in Wales](/wiki/Cadw/ICOMOS_Register_of_Parks_and_Gardens_of_Special_Historic_Interest_in_Wales).

Haulfre Gardens were developed as the private gardens to a house, Sunny Hill, in the north-west of the town. Later acquired by the council, they were opened as a public park in 1929, the opening ceremony being conducted by [David Lloyd George](/wiki/David_Lloyd_George). The gardens are listed Grade II on the Cadw/ICOMOS register.

### Marine Drive

The first route round the perimeter of the Great Orme was a footpath constructed in 1858 by Reginald Cust, a trustee of the Mostyn Estate. In 1872 the Great Ormes Head Marine Drive Co. Ltd. was formed to turn the path into a carriage road. Following bankruptcy, a second company completed the road in 1878. The contractors for the scheme were Messrs Hughes, Morris, Davies, a consortium led by Richard Hughes of Madoc Street, Llandudno. The road was bought by Llandudno Urban District Council in 1897.

The 4 miles (6.4 km) one-way drive starts at the foot of the Happy Valley. After about 1.5 miles (2.4 km) a side road leads to St Tudno's Church, the Great Orme Bronze Age Copper Mine and the summit of the Great Orme. Continuing on the Marine Drive the Great Orme Lighthouse (now a small hotel) is passed, and, shortly afterwards on the right, the Rest and Be Thankful Cafe and information centre. Below the Marine Drive at its western end is the site of the wartime Coast Artillery School (1940–1945), now a [scheduled monument](/wiki/Scheduled_monument).

### West Shore

The West Shore is a quiet beach on the estuary of the [River Conwy](/wiki/River_Conwy). It was here at Pen Morfa that [Alice Liddell](/wiki/Alice_Liddell) (of *[Alice in Wonderland](/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland)* fame) spent the long summer holidays of her childhood.

### Mostyn Street

Running behind the promenade is Mostyn Street leading to Mostyn Broadway and then Mostyn Avenue. These are the main shopping streets of Llandudno and Craig-y-Don. Mostyn Street accommodates the high street shops, the major high street banks and building societies, two churches, amusement arcades and the town's public library. The last is the starting point for the Llandudno Town Trail.

### Victorian Extravaganza

All the fun of the fair in Trinity Square at the Victorian Extravaganza

Every year in May [bank holiday](/wiki/Bank_holiday) weekend, Llandudno has a three-day Victorian Carnival and Mostyn Street becomes a funfair. Madoc Street and Gloddaeth Street and the Promenade become part of the route each day of a mid-day carnival parade. The Bodafon Farm fields become the location of a Festival of Transport.

## Llandudno Lifeboat

Main article: [Llandudno Lifeboat Station](/wiki/Llandudno_Lifeboat_Station)

The Llandudno Lifeboat on the promenade

Until 2017, Llandudno was unique within the United Kingdom in that its lifeboat station was located inland, allowing it to launch with equal facility from either the West Shore or the North Shore as needed. In 2017, a new lifeboat station was completed, and new, high-speed, offshore and inshore lifeboats, and a modern launching system, were acquired. This station is close to the paddling pool on North Shore.
Llandudno's active volunteer crews are called out more than ever with the rapidly increasing numbers of small pleasure craft sailing in coastal waters. The Llandudno Lifeboat is normally on display on the promenade every Sunday and bank holiday Monday from May until October.

## Places of worship

The ancient [parish church](/wiki/Parish_church) dedicated to [Saint Tudno](/wiki/Saint_Tudno) stands in a hollow near the northern point of the Great Orme and two miles (3 km) from the present town. It was established as an [oratory](/wiki/Oratory_(worship)) by Tudno, a 6th-century monk, but the present church dates from the 12th century and it is still used on summer Sunday mornings. It was the [Anglican](/wiki/Anglican) parish church of Llandudno until that status was transferred first to St George's (now closed) and later to Holy Trinity Church in Mostyn Street.

The principal [Christian Churches](/wiki/Christian_Churches) of Llandudno are members of [Cytûn](/wiki/Cyt%C3%BBn) (churches together) and include the [Church in Wales](/wiki/Church_in_Wales) (Holy Trinity and also Saint Paul's at Craig-y-Don), the [Roman Catholic Church](/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) of Our Lady Star of the Sea, Saint John's [Methodist Church](/wiki/Methodist_Church), Gloddaeth United Church ([Presbyterian](/wiki/Presbyterian)), Assemblies of God ([Pentecostal](/wiki/Pentecostal)), Llandudno [Baptist Church](/wiki/Baptist_Church), St. David's Methodist Church at Craig-y-Don, the [Coptic Orthodox Church](/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church) of [Saint Mary and Saint Abasikhiron](/wiki/Saint_Mary_and_Saint_Abasikhiron_Coptic_Orthodox_Church), and Eglwys Unedig Gymraeg Llandudno (the United Welsh Church of Llandudno). There is also a [Christadelphian](/wiki/Christadelphian) meeting hall in the town.

A member of the local Methodist community is [Roger Roberts](/wiki/Roger_Roberts,_Baron_Roberts_of_Llandudno), now Lord Roberts of Llandudno, [Liberal Democrat](/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_(UK)) Spokesman for [International Development](/wiki/International_Development) in the [House of Lords](/wiki/House_of_Lords).

Llandudno is home to a Jewish centre in Church Walks, which serves local and visiting Jews – one of few in North Wales. There is also a Buddhist centre, Kalpa Bhadra, on Mostyn Avenue in Craig-y-Don.

## Sport

The town is host to [Llandudno F.C.](/wiki/Llandudno_F.C.), who currently compete in [Cymru North](/wiki/Cymru_North), the second tier of Welsh football; the team have competed previously in the top level [Cymru Premier](/wiki/Cymru_Premier) division. The club plays its home matches at [Maesdu Park](/wiki/Maesdu_Park) and competed in the [Europa League](/wiki/Europa_League) in 2016. [Llandudno Albion](/wiki/Llandudno_Albion_F.C.) currently play in the third tier of Welsh football and [Llandudno Amateurs](/wiki/Llandudno_Amateurs_(2017)_F.C.) play in the fourth tier.

A football club is mentioned in Llandudno as far back as 1865. Gloddaeth Rovers dated back to 1878 and played for a decade; they were then replaced by [Llandudno Swifts](/wiki/Llandudno_Swifts_F.C.) as the town's main club. Following the demise of Swifts in 1901, a new club called [Llandudno Amateurs](/wiki/Llandudno_Amateurs_F.C.) were formed; a different team to that which exists now.

The annual [Welsh Open](/wiki/Welsh_Open_(snooker)) snooker tournament has been held in February at [Venue Cymru](/wiki/Venue_Cymru) since [2023](/wiki/2023_Welsh_Open_(snooker)).

[Llandudno Rugby Club](/wiki/Llandudno_RFC) plays in the town and was established in 1952. It is a member of the [Welsh Rugby Union](/wiki/Welsh_Rugby_Union).

There are also local [pool](/wiki/Pool_(cue_game)), [snooker](/wiki/Snooker) and [dominoes](/wiki/Dominoes) tournaments.

## Demographics

The 2011 census counted 20,701 usual residents of which 50.8% were born in Wales and 40.7% in England.

The population in 2021, according to the census, decreased to 19,700. 30% of the population are aged 65+, higher than the national average of 21.3%. The largest ethnic group is White with 95.9% of the population, higher than the national average of 93.8%, with the second largest being Asian/Asian British at 1.8%, lower than the national average of 2.9%. The largest religious group is Christianity who make up 52.6% of the population, higher than the national average of 43.6%, with the second largest being No religion who make up 38.4% of the population, lower than the national average of 46.5%.

## Governance

Electoral wards of Llandudno

[Llandudno Town Hall](/wiki/Llandudno_Town_Hall)

There are two tiers of local government covering Llandudno, at [community](/wiki/Community_(Wales)) (town) level and [principal area](/wiki/Principal_areas_of_Wales) ([county borough](/wiki/County_borough)) level: Llandudno Town Council and [Conwy County Borough Council](/wiki/Conwy_County_Borough_Council). The town council is based at [Llandudno Town Hall](/wiki/Llandudno_Town_Hall) on Lloyd Street. Llandudno is now divided into five [electoral wards](/wiki/Electoral_wards): [Craig-y-Don](/wiki/Craig-y-Don), Gogarth, [Mostyn](/wiki/Mostyn_(Llandudno_electoral_ward)), [Penrhyn](/wiki/Penrhyn_(electoral_ward)) and [Tudno](/wiki/Tudno_(electoral_ward)). The wards elect county councillors to [Conwy County Borough Council](/wiki/Conwy_County_Borough_Council) and four community councillors each to Llandudno Town Council.

### Administrative history

Llandudno was an [ancient parish](/wiki/Ancient_parish). In 1854 Llandudno was made an [Improvement Commissioners District](/wiki/Improvement_commissioners). The district covered part of the parish of Llandudno and part of the neighbouring parish of [Eglwys Rhos](/wiki/Eglwys_Rhos).

Improvement commissioners districts such as Llandudno were converted into [urban districts](/wiki/Urban_district_(England_and_Wales)) under the [Local Government Act 1894](/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1894). The 1894 Act also directed that parishes could no longer straddle district boundaries; the parishes of Llandudno and Eglwys Rhos were therefore both reduced to just cover the parts within the urban district; the parts outside the urban district made a separate parish called [Penrhyn](/wiki/Penrhyn_Bay). A single parish called 'Llandudno-cum-Eglwysrhos' was created in 1905 covering the same area as the urban district. The urban district's name remained 'Llandudno', with 'Llandudno-cum-Eglwysrhos' being the name of the single [civil parish](/wiki/Civil_parish) the district contained. The urban district and parish were enlarged in 1934 to absorb the Penhryn and part of the abolished parish of [Llangystennin](/wiki/Llangystennin) (the rest of which, around [Llandudno Junction](/wiki/Llandudno_Junction), went to the borough of [Conwy](/wiki/Conwy)).

The urban district council built [Llandudno Town Hall](/wiki/Llandudno_Town_Hall) to serve as its headquarters in 1902. In 1974 the urban district was abolished, with the area becoming a community within the [Aberconwy](/wiki/District_of_Aberconwy) district in the new county of [Gwynedd](/wiki/Gwynedd). Further local government reform in 1996 saw the area become part of the [principal area](/wiki/Principal_areas_of_Wales) of Conwy County Borough.

Llandudno falls under the UK parliamentary constituency of [Bangor Aberconwy](/wiki/Bangor_Aberconwy_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), whose is represented by the [Labour](/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK)) MP [Claire Hughes](/wiki/Claire_Hughes), and the Senedd constituency of Aberconwy, whose MS is the Conservative [Janet Finch-Saunders](/wiki/Janet_Finch-Saunders). It falls under the North Wales electoral region.

## Links with Wormhout and Mametz

The town council's coat of arms

Llandudno is [twinned](/wiki/Town_twinning) with the [Flemish](/wiki/Nord_(d%C3%A9partement)) town of [Wormhout](/wiki/Wormhout) 10 miles (16 km) from [Dunkirk](/wiki/Dunkirk). It was there that many members of the Llandudno-based 69th [Territorial](/wiki/Territorial_Army_(United_Kingdom)) [Regiment](/wiki/Regiment) were ambushed and taken prisoner. Later, at nearby [Esquelbecq](/wiki/Esquelbecq) on 28 May 1940, the prisoners were shot.

The 1st (North Wales) Brigade was headquartered in Llandudno in December 1914 and included a battalion of the [Royal Welch Fusiliers](/wiki/Royal_Welch_Fusiliers), which had been raised and trained in Llandudno. During the 1914–18 war this Brigade, a major part of the 38th Welsh Division, took part in the [Battle of the Somme](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme) and the Brigade was ordered to take [Mametz Wood](/wiki/Mametz_Wood). Two days of fighting brought about the total destruction of [Mametz](/wiki/Mametz,_Somme) village by shelling. After the war, the people of Llandudno (including returning survivors from the 38th Welsh Division) contributed generously to the fund for the reconstruction of the village of Mametz.

Llandudno is also twinned with [Champery](/wiki/Champery), Switzerland since August 2022.

## Cultural connections

[Carmen Sylva](/wiki/Carmen_Sylva), the pseudonym of [Queen Elisabeth of Romania](/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Wied), in Llandudno

Llandudno hosted the Welsh [National Eisteddfod](/wiki/National_Eisteddfod) in 1864, 1896 and 1963, and in 2008 welcomed the [Urdd](/wiki/Urdd) National [Eisteddfod](/wiki/Eisteddfod) to Gloddaeth Isaf Farm, [Penrhyn Bay](/wiki/Penrhyn_Bay). The town also hosted the Liverpool Olympic Festival in 1865 and 1866.

[Matthew Arnold](/wiki/Matthew_Arnold) gives a vivid and lengthy description of 1860s Llandudno – and of the ancient tales of [Taliesin](/wiki/Taliesin) and [Maelgwn Gwynedd](/wiki/Maelgwn_Gwynedd) that are associated with the local landscape – in the first sections of the preface to *On the Study of Celtic Literature* (1867). It is also used as a location for dramatic scenes in the stage play and film *[Hindle Wakes](/wiki/Hindle_Wakes_(play))* by [Stanley Houghton](/wiki/Stanley_Houghton), and the 1911 novel, *[The Card](/wiki/The_Card)*, by [Arnold Bennett](/wiki/Arnold_Bennett), and its subsequent [film version](/wiki/The_Card_(1952_film)).

[Elisabeth of Wied](/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Wied), the Queen Consort of Romania and also known as writer Carmen Sylva, stayed in Llandudno for five weeks in 1890. On leaving, she described Wales as "a beautiful haven of peace". Translated into Welsh as "*hardd*, *hafan*, *hedd*", it became the town's official motto.

Other famous people with links to Llandudno include the Victorian statesman [John Bright](/wiki/John_Bright) and multi-capped Welsh international footballers [Neville Southall](/wiki/Neville_Southall), [Neil Eardley](/wiki/Neil_Eardley), [Chris Maxwell](/wiki/Chris_Maxwell_(footballer)) and [Joey Jones](/wiki/Joey_Jones). Australia's 7th Prime Minister [Billy Hughes](/wiki/Billy_Hughes) attended school in Llandudno. [Gordon Borrie](/wiki/Gordon_Borrie,_Baron_Borrie) QC (Baron Borrie), Director General of the Office of Fair Trading from 1976 to 1992, was educated at the town's [John Bright Grammar School](/wiki/John_Bright_Grammar_School) when he lived there as a wartime evacuee.

The international art gallery *[Oriel Mostyn](/wiki/Oriel_Mostyn)* is in Vaughan Street next to the post office. It was built in 1901 to house the art collection of Lady Augusta Mostyn. It was requisitioned in 1914 for use as an army drill hall and later became a warehouse, before being returned to use as an art gallery in 1979. Following a major revamp the gallery was renamed simply 'Mostyn' in 2010.

Llandudno has its own mini arts festival '*LL*AWN' (Llandudno Arts Weekend) which has been running for the past three years (*LL*AWN*01* −2013, *LL*AWN*02 – 2014*, *LL*AWN*03 – 2015*). *LLAWN* is a mini festival that rediscovers and celebrates Llandudno's past in rather a unique way; via art, architecture, artefact, sound, performance, and participation. The festival takes place over three days of the weekend in late September, originally conceived as a way to promote what those in the hospitality sector refer to as the ‘shoulder season’, which means a lull in the tourist calendar. The festival is supported by Arts Council Wales, Mostyn Estates, Conwy County Borough Council, MOSTYN and Llandudno Town Council.

**Jane Seymour** (born **Joyce Penelope Wilhelmina Frankenberg**; 15 February 1951) is a British actress. After making her screen debut as an uncredited teenage extra in the 1969 musical comedy *[Oh! What a Lovely War](/wiki/Oh!_What_a_Lovely_War)*, Seymour moved to roles in film and television, including a leading role in the television series *[The Onedin Line](/wiki/The_Onedin_Line)* (1972–1973) the role of psychic [Bond girl](/wiki/Bond_girl) [Solitaire](/wiki/Solitaire_(James_Bond)) in the [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond) film *[Live and Let Die](/wiki/Live_and_Let_Die_(film))* (1973) and as Serina in the original 1978 series of *[Battlestar Galactica](/wiki/Battlestar_Galactica)*.

Critical acclaim came with a nomination for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Limited Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie) for her role in *[Captains and the Kings](/wiki/Captains_and_the_Kings_(miniseries))* (1976). In 1982, Seymour won her first [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Miniseries or Motion Picture Made for Television](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Miniseries_or_Television_Film) for her role in the miniseries *[East of Eden](/wiki/East_of_Eden_(miniseries))* (1981). She received three additional Golden Globe nominations in that same category: one for her portrayal of [Wallis Simpson](/wiki/Wallis_Simpson), the twice-divorced American wife of the former King Edward VIII, in the television film *[The Woman He Loved](/wiki/The_Woman_He_Loved)* (1988), and another two (in consecutive years) for her role in the miniseries *[War and Remembrance](/wiki/War_and_Remembrance_(miniseries))* (1988-1989). Her *War and Remembrance* role also garnered her a nomination for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Miniseries or Special](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie).

Seymour also won a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Miniseries or Special](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie) for her portrayal of [Maria Callas](/wiki/Maria_Callas) in *[Onassis: The Richest Man in the World](/wiki/Onassis:_The_Richest_Man_in_the_World)* (1988).

In 1993, Seymour was cast as [Dr. Michaela Quinn](/wiki/Michaela_Quinn) in the television series *[Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman](/wiki/Dr._Quinn,_Medicine_Woman)*, a medical drama set in the 1880s [Wild West](/wiki/Wild_West). For her performance in this role, over the course of its six-season run she received nominations twice for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Drama_Series), twice for the [Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Drama Series](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series), and four times for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Television Series – Drama](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Television_Series_Drama). Winning one of the latter awards.

She later earned a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame), and, in 2000, was appointed an [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire).

Seymour also had roles in numerous films, including *[Somewhere in Time](/wiki/Somewhere_in_Time_(film))* (1980), *[The Scarlet Pimpernel](/wiki/The_Scarlet_Pimpernel_(1982_film))* (1982), *[La Révolution française](/wiki/La_R%C3%A9volution_fran%C3%A7aise_(film))* (1989), *[Wedding Crashers](/wiki/Wedding_Crashers)* (2005), *[Love, Wedding, Marriage](/wiki/Love,_Wedding,_Marriage)* (2011), *[Little Italy](/wiki/Little_Italy_(2018_film))* (2018), *[The War with Grandpa](/wiki/The_War_with_Grandpa)* (2020) and *[Friendsgiving](/wiki/Friendsgiving_(film))* (2020).

In addition to her acting career, Seymour established a nonprofit, the Open Hearts Foundation, co-authored several children's books and self-help books, and created jewellery, scarves, furniture, rugs, handbags, paintings and sculptures under the label *Jane Seymour Designs*.

## Early life

Joyce Penelope Wilhelmina Frankenberg was born on 15 February 1951 in [Uxbridge](/wiki/Uxbridge), [Middlesex](/wiki/Middlesex) (now part of [Greater London](/wiki/Greater_London)), England, to Mieke van Tricht (1914–2007), a nurse, and Benjamin John Frankenberg [FRCOG](/wiki/Royal_College_of_Obstetricians_and_Gynaecologists) (1914–1990), a distinguished [gynaecologist](/wiki/Gynaecologist) and [obstetrician](/wiki/Obstetrician). Her father was [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish); born in England to a family from [Nowe Trzepowo](/wiki/Nowe_Trzepowo), a village in Poland. Her mother was a [Dutch](/wiki/Dutch_people) [Protestant](/wiki/Protestantism) (with family from [Deventer](/wiki/Deventer)) who was a [prisoner of war](/wiki/Prisoner_of_war) during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), and had lived in the [Dutch East Indies](/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies) (now [Indonesia](/wiki/Indonesia)). Seymour stated that she'd learned Dutch from her mother and the fellow survivors from the Japanese internment camp, who frequently spent holidays together in the Netherlands, when she was a child. Encouraged by her parents (who sent her to live with family friends in [Geneva](/wiki/Geneva) to practise her languages) --- she learned to speak fluent French.

Her paternal grandfather Lee Grahame had come to live in the East End of London, after escaping the Czarist [pogroms](/wiki/Pogrom) when he was just 14. He is listed in the 1911 census as living in [Bethnal Green](/wiki/Bethnal_Green), working as a hairdresser. Then went on to establish his own company. Seymour's father Benjamin qualified at the [UCL Medical School](/wiki/UCL_Medical_School) in 1938. Joining the medical branch of the [RAFVR](/wiki/RAFVR) after the outbreak of war. Serving in [England](/wiki/England), [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium), [Italy](/wiki/Italy) and [South Africa](/wiki/South_Africa). Ending his service as a [squadron leader](/wiki/Squadron_leader) with a [mention in despatches](/wiki/Mention_in_despatches). After the war, Frankenberg continued his career at various London hospitals, including [St Leonard's Hospital, Hackney](/wiki/St_Leonard%27s_Hospital,_Hackney), the East End Maternity Hospital, the City of London Maternity Hospital and finally [Hillingdon Hospital](/wiki/Hillingdon_Hospital), for which he designed the maternity unit. A close associate of [Patrick Steptoe](/wiki/Patrick_Steptoe), he assisted in pioneering discussions on [in-vitro fertilisation](/wiki/In-vitro_fertilisation) and published papers on adolescent and teenage sexual behaviours.

Seymour was educated at [Tring Park School for the Performing Arts](/wiki/Tring_Park_School_for_the_Performing_Arts) in [Hertfordshire](/wiki/Hertfordshire). Choosing the screen name Jane Seymour, after the English queen [Jane Seymour](/wiki/Jane_Seymour), because it seemed more saleable. One of her notable features is [heterochromia](/wiki/Heterochromia), making her right eye brown and her left eye green.

## Acting career

In 1969, Seymour appeared uncredited in her first film, [Richard Attenborough](/wiki/Richard_Attenborough)'s *[Oh! What a Lovely War](/wiki/Oh!_What_a_Lovely_War)*. In 1970, she appeared in her first major film role in the war drama *[The Only Way](/wiki/The_Only_Way_(1970_film))*. Playing Lillian Stein; a Jewish woman seeking shelter from [Nazi](/wiki/Nazi) persecution. In 1973, she gained her first major television role as Emma Callon, in the successful 1970s series *[The Onedin Line](/wiki/The_Onedin_Line)*. During this time, she appeared as the female lead Prima, in the two-part television miniseries *Frankenstein: The True Story*. Later appearing as [Winston Churchill](/wiki/Winston_Churchill)'s girlfriend Pamela Plowden in *Young Winston*, produced by her father-in-law Richard Attenborough.

In 1973, she achieved international fame in her role as [Bond girl](/wiki/Bond_girl) [Solitaire](/wiki/Solitaire_(James_Bond)) in the [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond) film *Live and Let Die*. IGN ranking her as 10th in a Top 10 Bond Babes list. In 1975, she was cast as Princess Farah in *[Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger](/wiki/Sinbad_and_the_Eye_of_the_Tiger)*, the third part of [Ray Harryhausen](/wiki/Ray_Harryhausen)'s *[Sinbad](/wiki/Sinbad)* trilogy. Filmed in 1975, it was not released until its [stop motion](/wiki/Stop_motion) animation sequences had been completed in 1977. Then in 1978, she appeared as [Serina](/wiki/Serina_(Battlestar_Galactica)) in the *[Battlestar Galactica](/wiki/Saga_of_a_Star_World)* film, and in the first five episodes of the television series. She later returned to the big screen in the comedy *[Oh Heavenly Dog](/wiki/Oh_Heavenly_Dog)* opposite [Chevy Chase](/wiki/Chevy_Chase).

Seymour at the Emmy Awards, 1988

In 1980, she played the role on stage of Constanze in [Peter Shaffer](/wiki/Peter_Shaffer)'s play *[Amadeus](/wiki/Amadeus_(play))*, opposite [Ian McKellen](/wiki/Ian_McKellen) as [Salieri](/wiki/Antonio_Salieri). With [Tim Curry](/wiki/Tim_Curry) as [Mozart](/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart). The play premiering on Broadway in 1980, running for 1,181 performances. And was nominated for seven Tony Awards, of which it won five.

Also in 1980, she was given the role of young theatre actress Elise McKenna in the period romance *[Somewhere in Time](/wiki/Somewhere_in_Time_(film))*. Though the film was made with a markedly limited budget, the role enticed her with a character she'd felt she knew. The effort decided a break from her earlier work, and marked the start of her friendship with co-star [Christopher Reeve](/wiki/Christopher_Reeve).

In 1981, she appeared in the television film *[East of Eden](/wiki/East_of_Eden_(miniseries))*, based on [the novel](/wiki/East_of_Eden_(novel)) by [John Steinbeck](/wiki/John_Steinbeck). Her portrayal of main antagonist [Cathy Ames](/wiki/Cathy_Ames) which won her a [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe). In 1982, she appeared in *[The Scarlet Pimpernel](/wiki/The_Scarlet_Pimpernel_(1982_film))* with [Anthony Andrews](/wiki/Anthony_Andrews), and her *Amadeus* costar [Ian McKellen](/wiki/Ian_McKellen). In 1984, she appeared nude in the film *[Lassiter](/wiki/Lassiter_(film))*, co-starring [Tom Selleck](/wiki/Tom_Selleck). But the film was a box office flop. In 1987, she was the subject of a pictorial in *[Playboy](/wiki/Playboy)* magazine, although she did not pose nude.

In 1988, she won the female lead in the twelve-part television miniseries *[War and Remembrance](/wiki/War_and_Remembrance_(miniseries))*, the continued story from the miniseries *[The Winds of War](/wiki/The_Winds_of_War_(miniseries))*. Playing Natalie Henry, an American Jewish woman trapped in Europe during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). That same year, she won an [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Award) for playing [Maria Callas](/wiki/Maria_Callas) in the television movie *[Onassis: The Richest Man in the World](/wiki/Onassis:_The_Richest_Man_in_the_World)*.

In 1989, on the occasion of the 200th anniversary of the [French Revolution](/wiki/French_Revolution), she appeared in the television film *[La révolution française](/wiki/La_R%C3%A9volution_fran%C3%A7aise_(film))*, filmed in both French and English. Then appeared as the doomed French queen, [Marie Antoinette](/wiki/Marie_Antoinette); her two children, Katherine and Sean, appearing as the queen's children.

Seymour at the Emmy Awards, 1994

In the 1990s, Seymour earned popular and critical praise for her onging headlining role as [Dr. Michaela "Mike" Quinn](/wiki/Michaela_A._Quinn) in the television series *[Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman](/wiki/Dr._Quinn,_Medicine_Woman)* and its television sequels Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman: The Movie (1999) and Dr. Quinn, Medicine Woman: The Heart Within (2001). Her work on the original series earning her a second Golden Globe Award. And while working in the series, she met her fourth husband, actor-director [James Keach](/wiki/James_Keach).

In the 2000s, she continued to work primarily in television. In 2004 and 2005, she made six guest appearances in [The WB](/wiki/WB_Network) series *[Smallville](/wiki/Smallville)*, playing Genevieve Teague, the wealthy, scheming mother of Jason Teague ([Jensen Ackles](/wiki/Jensen_Ackles)). In 2005, she returned to the big screen in the comedy *[Wedding Crashers](/wiki/Wedding_Crashers)*, playing Kathleen Cleary, wife of fictional [United States Secretary of the Treasury](/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Treasury) William Cleary, played by [Christopher Walken](/wiki/Christopher_Walken). In spring 2006, she appeared in the short-lived The WB series *[Modern Men](/wiki/Modern_Men)*. Later that year, she guest-starred as a law school professor on an episode of the [CBS](/wiki/CBS) sitcom *[How I Met Your Mother](/wiki/How_I_Met_Your_Mother),* and as a wealthy client on the [Fox](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) legal drama *[Justice](/wiki/Justice_(2006_TV_series))*. In 2007, she guest-starred in the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) sitcom *[In Case of Emergency](/wiki/In_Case_of_Emergency_(TV_series))*. She also appeared in [ITV](/wiki/ITV_(TV_network))'s *Marple: Ordeal By Innocence*, based on the [Agatha Christie](/wiki/Agatha_Christie) novel. Appearing as a contestant on [season five](/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series)_season_5) of the American reality show *[Dancing with the Stars](/wiki/Dancing_with_the_Stars_(American_TV_series))*; where she finished in sixth place, along with her partner [Tony Dovolani](/wiki/Tony_Dovolani). Then guest starred in "One Life to Lose", a soap opera-themed episode of the ABC crime dramedy *[Castle](/wiki/Castle_(TV_series))*.

She later appeared in the Hallmark Channel film *[Dear Prudence](/wiki/Dear_Prudence_(2008_film))* (2008); the romantic comedy *[Love, Wedding, Marriage](/wiki/Love,_Wedding,_Marriage)* (2011); and the Hallmark Movie Channel film *Lake Effects* (2012).

In April 2016, she starred as Florence Lancaster in [Noël Coward](/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward)'s play *[The Vortex](/wiki/The_Vortex)*, presented in Singapore by the [British Theatre Playhouse](/wiki/British_Theatre_Playhouse). In 2022, she began playing the title role on the Irish [Acorn TV](/wiki/Acorn_TV) series *[Harry Wild](/wiki/Harry_Wild_(TV_series)).*

In 2020, she starred in [Ruby's Choice](/wiki/Ruby%27s_Choice), an Australian comedy/drama produced and directed by [Michael Budd](/wiki/Michael_Budd). Which follows Ruby as a woman with early [dementia](/wiki/Dementia), and its impact on her and her family, when she is no longer able to live independently. And so moves in with the family. Winning the [Australian screen industry](/wiki/Australian_screen_industry) Network Award for best actress.

The film released theatrically across Australia and New Zealand on 3 March 2022. Then on 7 March 2022, Ruby's Choice premiered in Santa Barbara, California, at the 37th Santa Barbara International Film Festival. Where it was a Nominee Best International Feature Film. Seymour's 'Ruby's Choice' made the U.S. premiere at SBIFF|first=Joe|last=Buttitta|date=March 8, 2022</ref> On 24 September 2023, at the Burbank International Film Festival (BIFF) Winning Best Foreign Film and Best Feature Film. The event coinciding with the honouring of the legendary filmmaker [Tim Burton](/wiki/Tim_Burton). 'Ruby's Choice' releasing nationwide in North America on May 7th, 2024."

Seymour then appeared in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) movie, *[Irish Wish](/wiki/Irish_Wish),*  released on 14 March 2024, on Netflix. Debuing at number one on Netflix's most watched films list - two days following its release.

In 2025, she began hosting an [Acorn TV](/wiki/Acorn_TV)/[BBC America](/wiki/BBC_America) series called *Relative Secrets*.

## Personal life

Jane Seymour at the [82nd Academy Awards](/wiki/82nd_Academy_Awards) in 2010

Seymour has been married and divorced four times. Her first marriage was to [Michael Attenborough](/wiki/Michael_Attenborough). She was then briefly married to Geoffrey Planer.

In 1981, she married David Flynn. The marriage produced two children: Katherine Flynn (born on 7 February 1982) and Sean Flynn (born on 31 July 1985). Flynn involved her in the housing market, which left her "completely beyond bankrupt". The couple divorced in 1992. The following year, she married actor/producer/director [James Keach](/wiki/James_Keach). The couple had twins, John Stacy and Kristopher Steven, born on 30 November 1995. The twins were named after family friends [Johnny Cash](/wiki/Johnny_Cash) and Christopher Reeve, and James's brother, actor [Stacy Keach](/wiki/Stacy_Keach).

In February 2005, Seymour became a naturalised citizen of the United States.

Jane is a celebrity ambassador for [Childhelp](/wiki/Childhelp), a national nonprofit organisation dedicated to helping victims of [child abuse](/wiki/Child_abuse) and [neglect](/wiki/Child_neglect). In 2007, she sponsored a children's Art Pillow contest as part of the Jane Seymour Collection, with the proceeds going to Childhelp.

On 12 April 2013, it was announced that she was divorcing Keach. The divorce was finalised in December 2015.

In February 2018, she posed for *Playboy* for a third time, becoming at the age of 67 the oldest woman to be photographed for the magazine. In the *Playboy* interview, she revealed that she had briefly quit acting after being sexually harassed by an unnamed film producer, in the early 1970s. She had earlier referred to the incident in her 1986 book *Jane Seymour's Guide to Romantic Living*.

Her [Malibu](/wiki/Malibu,_California) house almost burned down during the [2018 fires](/wiki/Woolsey_Fire), but was saved, according to her, "by an incredibly devoted gardener and neighbour".

Since 2023, she has been in a relationship with musician John Zambetti.

## Writing and fashion career

In the 1980s, Seymour began a parallel career as a writer of self-help and inspirational books, including *Jane Seymour's Guide to Romantic Living* (1986), *Two at a Time: Having Twins* (2002), *Remarkable Changes* (2003) and *[Among Angels](/wiki/Among_Angels_(book))* (2010). She also co-wrote several children's books, with her then-husband [James Keach](/wiki/James_Keach), for the *This One 'N That One* series.

In 1985, she appeared at [Fashion Aid](/wiki/Fashion_Aid), a one-time fashion show fundraiser held at the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) in London and organised by [Bob Geldof](/wiki/Bob_Geldof) to raise funds for the ongoing [Ethiopian famine](/wiki/1983%E2%80%931985_famine_in_Ethiopia) caused by the policies of [dictator](/wiki/Dictator) [Mengistu Haile Mariam](/wiki/Mengistu_Haile_Mariam). The finale of the show showing her partake in a mock wedding with [Freddie Mercury](/wiki/Freddie_Mercury). Jane wearing a white lace wedding dress designed by [David](/wiki/David_Emanuel_(fashion_designer)) and [Elizabeth Emanuel](/wiki/Elizabeth_Emanuel) – who had previously created [Princess Diana's wedding gown](/wiki/Wedding_dress_of_Lady_Diana_Spencer).

In 2008, she had replaced [Selina Scott](/wiki/Selina_Scott) as the new face of fashion label [CC](/wiki/Country_Casuals) (formerly known as Country Casuals) under the [Austin Reed](/wiki/Austin_Reed_(retailer)) banner of retailers.

Likewise in 2008, she teamed up with and designed the "Open Heart Collection" for [Kay Jewelers](/wiki/Kay_Jewelers), which promoted it with the advice, "Keep your heart open and love will *always* find its way in." Beginning that year, seeing to it that she would always be wearing one of the collection's necklaces whenever seen in public, while not in character for any of her acting performances. In the same year, also writing and publishing the books *Open Hearts: If Your Heart Is Open, love Will Always Find Its Way In* and *Open Hearts Family.*

A 2.08-carat cushion-cut fancy vivid blue diamond in an 18-karat rose-gold-plated platinum setting was named "The Jane Seymour" in her honour, by World of Diamonds Group. Which had mined it in Russia, then cut and set it. The ring presented to Seymour in April 2016 in Singapore, while she was there to star in *[The Vortex](/wiki/The_Vortex)*.

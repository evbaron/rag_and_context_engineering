# List of Billboard Hot 100 number ones of 1988

[Steve Winwood](/wiki/Steve_Winwood) (pictured) earned his second Hot 100 number-one single with "[Roll With It](/wiki/Roll_with_It_(Steve_Winwood_song))", which stayed at the top position for four straight weeks.

These are the *[Billboard magazine](/wiki/Billboard_magazine)* **[Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100)** number one hits of 1988. The [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) is a chart that ranks the best-performing [singles](/wiki/Single_(music)) of the United States. Published by *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* magazine, the data are based collectively on each single's weekly [physical](/wiki/CD_single) sales, and [airplay](/wiki/Heavy_rotation). R&B/pop megastar [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston)'s two singles released from her second studio album *[Whitney](/wiki/Whitney_(album))*, "[So Emotional](/wiki/So_Emotional)" and "[Where Do Broken Hearts Go](/wiki/Where_Do_Broken_Hearts_Go)", both peaked at number one, which gave her a total of seven consecutive number one hits, breaking the record of six previously shared by [The Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles) and [The Bee Gees](/wiki/Bee_Gees). The longest running number-one single of 1988 was "[Roll With It](/wiki/Roll_with_It_(Steve_Winwood_song))" by [Steve Winwood](/wiki/Steve_Winwood), which obtained four weeks at the top of the chart. When "[Dirty Diana](/wiki/Dirty_Diana)" reached number-one on the chart, it became [Michael Jackson](/wiki/Michael_Jackson)'s fifth single from the album *[Bad](/wiki/Bad_(album))* to reach number-one. Jackson became the first artist to have five singles from one album reach number-one on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) and is the only male to do so. [George Michael](/wiki/George_Michael)'s "[Faith](/wiki/Faith_(George_Michael_song))" was the best selling single of 1988 in the United States, despite most of its weeks at number one occurring in December 1987. (It carried over into 1988 at number one as the result of a frozen chart.) 1988 is tied with 1989 for the second most #1 hits with 32 songs going to number one.

That year, 17 acts earned their first number one song, such as [INXS](/wiki/INXS), [Exposé](/wiki/Expos%C3%A9_(group)), [Rick Astley](/wiki/Rick_Astley), [Terence Trent D'Arby](/wiki/Terence_Trent_D%27Arby), [Gloria Estefan](/wiki/Gloria_Estefan), [Miami Sound Machine](/wiki/Miami_Sound_Machine), [Debbie Gibson](/wiki/Debbie_Gibson), [Cheap Trick](/wiki/Cheap_Trick), [Richard Marx](/wiki/Richard_Marx), [Guns N' Roses](/wiki/Guns_N%27_Roses), [Bobby McFerrin](/wiki/Bobby_McFerrin), [Def Leppard](/wiki/Def_Leppard), [UB40](/wiki/UB40), [The Escape Club](/wiki/The_Escape_Club), [Will to Power](/wiki/Will_to_Power_(band)), [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago_(band)) and [Poison](/wiki/Poison_(band)). [George Michael](/wiki/George_Michael), [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston), [Michael Jackson](/wiki/Michael_Jackson), and Rick Astley were the only acts to hit number one more than once, with George Michael having the most with four, Michael Jackson with three, and Whitney Houston and Rick Astley with two.

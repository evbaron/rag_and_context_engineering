**Joseph Wright** (born 25 August 1972) is an English filmmaker. He began his career directing [music videos](/wiki/Music_video) and television series, before making his feature directorial debut with *[Pride & Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_%26_Prejudice_(2005_film))* (2005), based on the [Jane Austen](/wiki/Jane_Austen) [novel](/wiki/Pride_and_Prejudice). His subsequent works include the period drama adaptations *[Atonement](/wiki/Atonement_(2007_film))* (2007), *[Anna Karenina](/wiki/Anna_Karenina_(2012_film))* (2012), and *[Cyrano](/wiki/Cyrano_(film))* (2021), the action thriller *[Hanna](/wiki/Hanna_(film))* (2011), the [Peter Pan](/wiki/Peter_Pan_(character)) origin story *[Pan](/wiki/Pan_(2015_film))* (2015), and the [Winston Churchill](/wiki/Winston_Churchill) biopic *[Darkest Hour](/wiki/Darkest_Hour_(film))* (2017). He also directed the historical drama television series *[Mussolini: Son of the Century](/wiki/Mussolini:_Son_of_the_Century)* (2025).

Wright has been nominated for six [BAFTA Film Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards) and won [Outstanding Debut by a British Writer, Director or Producer](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Outstanding_Debut_by_a_British_Writer,_Director_or_Producer) for *Pride & Prejudice*. He also won a [BAFTA TV Award for Best Drama Series](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Drama_Series) for the [BBC Television](/wiki/BBC_Television) serial [*Charles II: The Power and the Passion*](/wiki/Charles_II:_The_Power_and_the_Passion) (2003), and was nominated for a [Golden Globe Award for Best Director](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director) for *Atonement*.

## Early life and career

Wright always had an interest in the arts, especially painting. He also made films on his [Super 8](/wiki/Super_8_film) camera and spent time in the evenings acting in a drama club. Wright is [dyslexic](/wiki/Dyslexia). He went to [Islington Green](/wiki/Islington_Green_School) Secondary School, but left without any [GCSEs](/wiki/General_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education).

He began his career working at his parents' puppet theatre, the [Little Angel Theatre](/wiki/Little_Angel_Theatre) in Islington. He also took classes at the Anna Scher Theatre School and acted professionally on stage and camera. He spent an art [foundation](/wiki/Foundation_Course) year at [Camberwell College of Arts](/wiki/Camberwell_College_of_Arts), before taking a degree in fine art and film at [Central St Martins](/wiki/Central_St_Martins) where he was tutored by [Malcolm Le Grice](/wiki/Malcolm_Le_Grice) and [Vera Neubauer](/wiki/Vera_Neubauer). In his last year of studies he received a scholarship to make a [short film](/wiki/Short_film) for the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) that won several awards.

## Career

### Music videos

During the 1990s, he worked at Oil Factory, a [music video](/wiki/Music_video) [production company](/wiki/Production_company) based in Caledonian Road, Kings Cross. He worked on a variety of productions in numerous roles, including casting director. Here, he was able to get the opportunity to direct some music videos. Alongside this, particularly on the strength of his short film work, he was also developing *The End*, his second short film. During this decade, he also worked part-time as a roadie for Vegetable Vision, who created visuals for electronic music bands such as [Chemical Brothers](/wiki/Chemical_Brothers), [Darren Emerson](/wiki/Darren_Emerson), [Underworld](/wiki/Underworld_(band)) and [Andrew Weatherall](/wiki/Andrew_Weatherall).

Wright attributes some of the aesthetic and emotion of the UK [rave](/wiki/Rave) scene as an influence on his work.

### Television

On the success of his first short film, Wright was offered the script for the serial *Nature Boy* (2000).

He followed this up with the serials *Bodily Harm* (2002) with [Timothy Spall](/wiki/Timothy_Spall) and the highly acclaimed *[Charles II: The Power and the Passion](/wiki/Charles_II:_The_Power_and_the_Passion)* (2003) with [Rufus Sewell](/wiki/Rufus_Sewell), which won the [BAFTA Award](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards) for Best Drama Serial.

In 2022, Wright began directing an [eight-part adaptation](/wiki/M._Son_of_the_Century_(TV_series)) of the bestselling novel [*M: Son of the Century*](/wiki/M._Son_of_the_Century), a [historical novel](/wiki/Historical_novel) by [Antonio Scurati](/wiki/Antonio_Scurati) recounting the rise of Italian dictator [Benito Mussolini](/wiki/Benito_Mussolini). [*Mussolini: Son of the Century*](/wiki/Mussolini:_Son_of_the_Century) premiered at the [81st Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/81st_Venice_International_Film_Festival) on 5 September 2024 and began airing on [Sky Atlantic](/wiki/Sky_Atlantic_(Italian_TV_channel)) on 10 January 2025.

In 2023, Wright was developing an adaptation for [HBO](/wiki/HBO) of the bestselling non-fiction book [*Empty Mansions: The Mysterious Life of Huguette Clark and the Spending of a Great American Fortune*](/wiki/Empty_Mansions) about [heiress](/wiki/Inheritance) [Huguette Clark](/wiki/Huguette_Clark), daughter of [copper](/wiki/Copper) baron and [United States Senator](/wiki/United_States_Senator) [William A. Clark](/wiki/William_A._Clark).

### Feature films

In 2005, Wright made the transition to feature films with his critically acclaimed adaptation of *[Pride & Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_%26_Prejudice_(2005_film))* starring [Keira Knightley](/wiki/Keira_Knightley) and [Matthew Macfadyen](/wiki/Matthew_Macfadyen). It received numerous accolades, nominations and awards, including four [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) nominations (including [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress)) and six [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA) nominations (Wright won for Most Promising Newcomer).

Wright's next feature was an adaptation of [Ian McEwan](/wiki/Ian_McEwan)'s [Booker Prize](/wiki/Booker_Prize)-shortlisted novel *[Atonement](/wiki/Atonement_(novel))* (2007), which reunited Wright with [Keira Knightley](/wiki/Keira_Knightley), and also stars [James McAvoy](/wiki/James_McAvoy) and [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan). It was nominated for seven [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe) Awards, more than any other film that year. Though Wright was not nominated for director, the film received seven Academy Award nominations, winning only for Best Original Score. At the BAFTA Awards, it received 14 nominations and won Best Film and Best Production Design.

Wright's next film was *[The Soloist](/wiki/The_Soloist)*, starring [Jamie Foxx](/wiki/Jamie_Foxx) and [Robert Downey, Jr.](/wiki/Robert_Downey,_Jr.) It is about the "true story of musical prodigy [Nathaniel Ayers](/wiki/Nathaniel_Ayers), who developed schizophrenia in his second year at [Juilliard](/wiki/Juilliard) and ended up homeless on the streets of downtown L.A. where he performs the violin and cello." It was to be released on 21 November 2008, but was pushed back to 24 April 2009.

Wright reunited with *Atonement* star [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan) for the 2011 action thriller *Hanna*. The title character is a 15-year-old girl trained since birth to be an assassin by her father ([Eric Bana](/wiki/Eric_Bana)), a rogue CIA asset. It received mostly positive reviews, with [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) calling it a "first-rate thriller". It received an aggregate score of 65 from Metacritic ("generally positive" reviews).

Wright directed the [2012 screen adaptation](/wiki/Anna_Karenina_(2012_film)) by Sir [Tom Stoppard](/wiki/Tom_Stoppard) of [Leo Tolstoy](/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy)'s classic novel *[Anna Karenina](/wiki/Anna_Karenina)*. The cast included [Keira Knightley](/wiki/Keira_Knightley) as Anna, [Jude Law](/wiki/Jude_Law) as her husband, [Aaron Taylor-Johnson](/wiki/Aaron_Taylor-Johnson) as her young love, Irish actor [Domhnall Gleeson](/wiki/Domhnall_Gleeson) as Konstantin Levin, as well as [Kelly Macdonald](/wiki/Kelly_Macdonald), [Olivia Williams](/wiki/Olivia_Williams), [Matthew Macfadyen](/wiki/Matthew_Macfadyen) and [Michelle Dockery](/wiki/Michelle_Dockery). [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan) and [Andrea Riseborough](/wiki/Andrea_Riseborough) were initially cast, but dropped out and were replaced by [Alicia Vikander](/wiki/Alicia_Vikander) and [Ruth Wilson](/wiki/Ruth_Wilson_(actress)), respectively.

Wright then directed the [2015 prequel](/wiki/Pan_(2015_film)) to *[Peter Pan](/wiki/Peter_Pan_(play_and_novel))* for Warner Bros. The film starred [Hugh Jackman](/wiki/Hugh_Jackman), [Garrett Hedlund](/wiki/Garrett_Hedlund), [Rooney Mara](/wiki/Rooney_Mara), [Amanda Seyfried](/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) and [Levi Miller](/wiki/Levi_Miller) as Peter. The screenplay by actor-turned-screenwriter [Jason Fuchs](/wiki/Jason_Fuchs) was from the 2013 [Hollywood Black List](/wiki/Black_List_(survey)), a selection of popular unproduced scripts. The film was negatively received by critics and was considered a commercial flop, failing to recoup its budget at the box office. [Rooney Mara](/wiki/Rooney_Mara)'s casting as Tiger Lily caused a [controversy](/wiki/Racebending), due to her being of [European](/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) ancestry, while Tiger Lily is traditionally portrayed as [Native American](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States).

Wright's 2017 film *[Darkest Hour](/wiki/Darkest_Hour_(film))* covers a pivotal month in the life of former British Prime Minister [Winston Churchill](/wiki/Winston_Churchill). It stars [Gary Oldman](/wiki/Gary_Oldman) as Churchill, along with [Ben Mendelsohn](/wiki/Ben_Mendelsohn), [Ronald Pickup](/wiki/Ronald_Pickup), [David Schofield](/wiki/David_Schofield_(actor)), [Kristin Scott Thomas](/wiki/Kristin_Scott_Thomas), [Samuel West](/wiki/Samuel_West) and [Lily James](/wiki/Lily_James). Wright said the film was a rebuke to [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump).

In 2021, Wright directed *[The Woman in the Window](/wiki/The_Woman_in_the_Window_(2021_film)),* a psychological thriller starring [Amy Adams](/wiki/Amy_Adams), which received negative reviews. The same year, he also directed *[Cyrano](/wiki/Cyrano_(film)),* a musical based on the [*Cyrano de Bergerac*](/wiki/Cyrano_de_Bergerac_(play)). The film was well-received and earned multiple [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and [BAFTA](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards) nominations.

Wright is also set to direct AI thriller *Alignment,* written by Nathan Dotan and distributed by [Fifth Season](/wiki/Fifth_Season_(company)).

## Directorial trademarks

Wright won a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award) for best newcomer for *Pride & Prejudice* and was the youngest director to have a film open the [Venice Film Festival](/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival) with *Atonement*. According to the director's commentary on *Pride & Prejudice*, Wright is influenced by the work of British film director [David Lean](/wiki/David_Lean), and possessing a certain knowledge of [art history](/wiki/Art_history), tries sometimes to compose his shots after classical paintings.

*Charles II: The Power and The Passion*, *Pride & Prejudice*, *Atonement* and *Hanna* all have long [tracking shots](/wiki/Tracking_shot) in them. *Atonement* has a continuous five-minute and five second shot of the [Dunkirk evacuation](/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation). "Basically, I just like showing off", Wright told the audience at the [Hay Festival](/wiki/Hay_Festival).

## Personal life

After meeting on the set of *[Pride & Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_%26_Prejudice_(2005_film))*, Wright began a relationship with actress [Rosamund Pike](/wiki/Rosamund_Pike). They were engaged from 2007 to 2008.

Wright was married to British-American [sitarist](/wiki/Sitarist) [Anoushka Shankar](/wiki/Anoushka_Shankar) from 2010 until 2019. They have two sons, Zubin and Mohan, born in 2011 and 2015. They separated in December 2017 and their divorce was finalized in September 2019 as a judge concluded that Wright had committed adultery and that his wife found living with him "intolerable".

Since 2017, Wright has been in a relationship with American actress [Haley Bennett](/wiki/Haley_Bennett). Their daughter was born in 2018 in [Brooklyn Heights](/wiki/Brooklyn_Heights). As of 2019, the family resides in the UK in [Somerset](/wiki/Somerset).

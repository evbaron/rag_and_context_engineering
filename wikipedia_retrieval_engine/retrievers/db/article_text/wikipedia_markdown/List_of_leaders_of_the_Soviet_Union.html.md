# List of leaders of the Soviet Union

## Infobox

- **Type:** Supreme leader Party leader Commander-in-chief
- **Term length:** Life tenure
- **Formation:** 30 December 1922; 103 years ago ( 1922-12-30 )
- **First holder:** Vladimir Lenin
- **Final holder:** Mikhail Gorbachev
- **Abolished:** 26 December 1991; 34 years ago ( 1991-12-26 )
- **Succession:** General Secretary of the Commonwealth of Independent States President of Russia

During [its 69-year history](/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union), the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union) usually had a *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* leader who would not always necessarily be [head of state](/wiki/Head_of_state) or even [head of government](/wiki/Head_of_government) but almost always held office as [Communist Party General Secretary](/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union). The office of the chairman of the Council of Ministers was comparable to a [prime minister](/wiki/Prime_minister) in the [First World](/wiki/First_World) whereas the office of the chairman of the Presidium was comparable to a [president](/wiki/President_(government_title)). According to [Marxist–Leninist ideology](/wiki/Leninism), the head of the Soviet state was a [collegiate body](/wiki/Collegiate_body) of the [vanguard party](/wiki/Vanguardism) (as described in Lenin's *[What Is to Be Done?](/wiki/What_Is_to_Be_Done%3F)*).

Following [Joseph Stalin](/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)'s [consolidation of power](/wiki/Rise_of_Joseph_Stalin) in the late 1920s, the post of the general secretary of the [Central Committee of the Communist Party](/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) became synonymous with leader of the Soviet Union, because the post controlled both the [Communist Party](/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) and (via party membership) the [Soviet government](/wiki/Government_of_the_Soviet_Union). Often the general secretary also held high positions in the government. Since the post of general secretary lacked clear guidelines of succession, the office's successor needed the support of the [Political Bureau](/wiki/Politburo_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) (Politburo), the Central Committee, or another government or party apparatus to consolidate power. The [President of the Soviet Union](/wiki/President_of_the_Soviet_Union), an office created in March 1990, replaced the general secretary as the highest Soviet political office.

Contemporaneously to the establishment of the office of the president, representatives of the [Congress of People's Deputies](/wiki/Congress_of_People%27s_Deputies_of_the_Soviet_Union) voted to remove [Article 6](/wiki/Article_6_of_the_Soviet_Constitution) from the [Soviet constitution](/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Soviet_Union) which stated that the Soviet Union was a [one-party state](/wiki/One-party_state) controlled by the Communist Party which in turn played the leading role in society. This vote weakened the party and its hegemony over the Soviet Union and [its people](/wiki/Soviet_people). Upon the departure of an incumbent president from office, the [Vice President of the Soviet Union](/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_of_the_Soviet_Union) would assume the office, though the [Soviet Union dissolved](/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) before this was actually tested. After the [failed coup in August 1991](/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat_attempt), the vice president was replaced by an elected member of the [State Council of the Soviet Union](/wiki/State_Council_of_the_Soviet_Union).

## Summary

[Lenin](/wiki/Lenin) was elected [chairman](/wiki/Premier_of_the_Soviet_Union) of the [Council of People's Commissars of the Soviet Union](/wiki/Council_of_People%27s_Commissars) (Sovnarkom) on 30 December 1922 by the [Congress of Soviets](/wiki/Congress_of_Soviets_of_the_Soviet_Union). At the age of 53, his health declined from the effects of two bullet wounds, later aggravated by three [strokes](/wiki/Stroke) which culminated with his death in 1924. Irrespective of his health status in his final days, Lenin was already losing much of his power to [Joseph Stalin](/wiki/Joseph_Stalin). [Alexei Rykov](/wiki/Alexei_Rykov) succeeded Lenin as chairman of the Sovnarkom, and although he was *[de jure](/wiki/De_jure)* the most powerful person in the country, in fact, all power was concentrated in the hands of the "[troika](/wiki/Triumvirate#Soviet_Union)" – the union of three influential party figures: [Grigory Zinoviev](/wiki/Grigory_Zinoviev), [Joseph Stalin](/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), and [Lev Kamenev](/wiki/Lev_Kamenev). Stalin continued to increase his influence in the party, and by the end of the 1920s, he became the sole dictator of the USSR, defeating all his political opponents. The post of general secretary of the party, which was held by Stalin, became the most important post in the Soviet hierarchy.

Stalin's early policies pushed for rapid [industrialisation](/wiki/Industrialisation), [nationalisation](/wiki/Nationalization) of private industry and the [collectivisation](/wiki/Collective_farming) of private plots created under Lenin's [New Economic Policy](/wiki/New_Economic_Policy). As leader of the Politburo, Stalin consolidated near-absolute power by 1938 after the [Great Purge](/wiki/Great_Purge), a series of campaigns of political murder, repression and persecution. On 22 June 1941 [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) [invaded the Soviet Union](/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), but by December the [Soviet Army](/wiki/Red_Army) managed to stop the attack just shy of Moscow. On Stalin's orders, the Soviet Union launched a counter-attack on Nazi Germany, which finally succeeded in 1945. Stalin died in March 1953 and his death triggered a power struggle in which [Nikita Khrushchev](/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev) ultimately emerged victorious over [Georgy Malenkov](/wiki/Georgy_Malenkov).

Khrushchev denounced Stalin on two occasions, [first in 1956](/wiki/On_the_Cult_of_Personality_and_Its_Consequences) and then in 1962. His policy of [de-Stalinisation](/wiki/De-Stalinization) earned him many enemies within the party, especially from old [Stalinist](/wiki/Stalinism) appointees. Many saw this approach as destructive and destabilizing. A group known as [Anti-Party Group](/wiki/Anti-Party_Group) tried to oust Khrushchev from office in 1957, but it failed. As Khrushchev grew older, his erratic behaviour became worse, usually making decisions without discussing or confirming them with the Politburo. [Leonid Brezhnev](/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev), a close companion of Khrushchev, was elected the first secretary the same day of Khrushchev's removal from power. [Alexei Kosygin](/wiki/Alexei_Kosygin) became the new premier, and [Anastas Mikoyan](/wiki/Anastas_Mikoyan) kept his office as chairman of the [Presidium of the Supreme Soviet](/wiki/Presidium_of_the_Supreme_Soviet). On the orders of the Politburo, Mikoyan was forced to retire in 1965, and [Nikolai Podgorny](/wiki/Nikolai_Podgorny) took over the office of chairman of the Presidium. The Soviet Union in the post-Khrushchev 1960s was governed by a [collective leadership](/wiki/Collective_leadership). [Henry Kissinger](/wiki/Henry_Kissinger), the American [National Security Advisor](/wiki/National_Security_Advisor_(United_States)), mistakenly believed that Kosygin was the leader of the Soviet Union and that he was at the helm of Soviet foreign policy because he represented the Soviet Union at the 1967 [Glassboro Summit Conference](/wiki/Glassboro_Summit_Conference). The "[Era of Stagnation](/wiki/Era_of_Stagnation)", a derogatory term coined by [Mikhail Gorbachev](/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev), was a period marked by low socio-economic efficiency in the country and a [gerontocracy](/wiki/Gerontocracy) ruling the country. [Yuri Andropov](/wiki/Yuri_Andropov) (aged 68 at the time) succeeded Brezhnev in his post as general secretary in 1982. In 1983, Andropov was hospitalized and rarely met up at work to chair the politburo meetings due to his declining health. [Nikolai Tikhonov](/wiki/Nikolai_Tikhonov) usually chaired the meetings in his place. Following Andropov's death fifteen months after his appointment, an even older leader, 72-year-old [Konstantin Chernenko](/wiki/Konstantin_Chernenko), was elected to the general secretariat. His rule lasted for little more than a year until his death thirteen months later on 10 March 1985.

At the age of 54, Mikhail Gorbachev was elected to the general secretariat by Politburo on 11 March 1985. In May 1985, Gorbachev publicly admitted the slowing down of the economic development and inadequate living standards, being the first Soviet leader to do so while also beginning a series of fundamental reforms. From 1986 to around 1988, he dismantled [central planning](/wiki/Planned_economy), allowed state enterprises to set their own outputs, enabled private investment in businesses not previously permitted to be privately owned, and allowed foreign investment, among other measures. He also opened up the management of and decision-making within the Soviet Union and allowed greater public discussion and criticism, along with the warming of relationships with the West. These twin policies were known as *[perestroika](/wiki/Perestroika)* (literally meaning "reconstruction", though it varies) and *[glasnost](/wiki/Glasnost)* ("openness" and "transparency"), respectively. The dismantling of the principal defining features of [Soviet communism](/wiki/Ideology_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1988 and 1989 in the Soviet Union led to the unintended consequence of the [Soviet Union breaking up](/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) after the failed [August 1991 coup](/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_attempt) led by [Gennady Yanayev](/wiki/Gennady_Yanayev).

## List of troikas

Further information: [Collective leadership in the Soviet Union](/wiki/Collective_leadership_in_the_Soviet_Union)

Over the course of the Soviet Union's existence, there were four intervals where the country was ruled not by one figure but a [troika](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/troika) (i.e."[triumvirate](/wiki/Triumvirate)") comprising three leading figures within the [Politburo](/wiki/Politburo_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union). Such instances included: (1) the 2- to 3-year period between [Lenin](/wiki/Lenin)'s incapacitation and the rise of [Joseph Stalin](/wiki/Joseph_Stalin); (2) the 3 months immediately following Stalin's death; (3) the years between [Nikita Khrushchev](/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev)'s fall and [Leonid Brezhnev](/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev)'s consolidation of power; and (4) the ailing [Konstantin Chernenko](/wiki/Konstantin_Chernenko)'s tenure as *[de jure](/wiki/De_jure)* leader of the Soviet Union.

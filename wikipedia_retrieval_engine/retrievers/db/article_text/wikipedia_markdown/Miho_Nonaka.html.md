**Miho Nonaka** (野中 生萌, *Nonaka Mihō*; born May 21, 1997) is a Japanese [competition climber](/wiki/Competition_climber) who specializes in [competition bouldering](/wiki/Competition_bouldering). She is an Olympic silver medalist in [sport climbing](/wiki/Sport_climbing_at_the_Summer_Olympics).

## Early life

Nonaka's father and sister introduced her to climbing when she was nine years old.

## Climbing career

In 2013, Nonaka began competing on the international senior circuit at [IFSC Climbing World Cups](/wiki/IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup) at age 16, initially focusing on [competition lead climbing](/wiki/Competition_lead_climbing).

In 2014, Nonaka transitioned towards [competition bouldering](/wiki/Competition_bouldering), winning her first World Cup medal with a second-place finish at the Boulder World Cup in [Laval](/wiki/Laval,_Mayenne) and eventually finishing the [World Cup season](/wiki/2014_IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup) in fifth place in the overall ranking for [bouldering](/wiki/Competition_climbing#Competition_bouldering).

Nonaka at the [Bouldering World Cup](/wiki/Bouldering_World_Cup), [Munich](/wiki/Munich), 2015

Nonaka continued to move up the overall rankings for bouldering in the following World Cup seasons, finishing in third place in [2015](/wiki/2015_IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup) and second place in [2016](/wiki/2016_IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup). She won her first World Cup gold medal at the Boulder World Cup in [Navi Mumbai](/wiki/Navi_Mumbai) in 2016.

In 2018, Nonaka won the overall title in the [Boulder World Cup](/wiki/2018_IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup) by earning a place on the podium at every World Cup that season, winning one gold medal and six silver medals.

In 2019, Nonaka was deemed by the [IFSC](/wiki/International_Federation_of_Sport_Climbing) to have qualified to compete at the [2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) in [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo) by finishing in fifth place in the [combined event](/wiki/Competition_climbing#Combined_formats) at the [2019 IFSC Climbing World Championships](/wiki/2019_IFSC_Climbing_World_Championships). In December 2020, Nonaka's Olympic berth was confirmed after a dispute between the [IFSC](/wiki/International_Federation_of_Sport_Climbing) and the Japan Mountaineering and Sport Climbing Association was resolved in Nonaka's favor.

In preparation to compete in the combined event at the 2020 Summer Olympics (postponed to 2021), Nonaka competed at bouldering, lead climbing, and [speed climbing](/wiki/Competition_speed_climbing) events during the [2021 IFSC Climbing World Cup](/wiki/2021_IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup) season, making finals across all disciplines and winning a bronze medal at the Speed World Cup in [Salt Lake City](/wiki/Salt_Lake_City). At the Olympics, Nonaka won a silver medal in the [combined event](/wiki/Sport_climbing_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_combined).

Nonaka followed up her Olympic medal with two straight second-place finishes in the overall rankings of the Boulder World Cup in [2022](/wiki/2022_IFSC_Climbing_World_Cup) and [2023](/wiki/2023_IFSC_Climbing_World_Championships).

In 2024, Nonaka placed second in the rankings of the combined bouldering and lead climbing event at the [Olympic Qualifier Series](/wiki/2024_Olympic_Qualifier_Series#Boulder_&_lead), securing a spot for the combined event at the [2024 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in [Paris](/wiki/Paris). At the Olympics, she finished in ninth place in the semifinals of the [combined event](/wiki/Sport_climbing_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_combined) and did not move on to finals.

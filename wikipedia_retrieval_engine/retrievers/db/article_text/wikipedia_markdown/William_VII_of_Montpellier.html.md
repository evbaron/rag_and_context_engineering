# William VII of Montpellier

**William VII of Montpellier** (c. 1131 — c. 1172), the eldest son of [William VI](/wiki/William_VI_of_Montpellier) and of his wife Sibylle, was as 12th century [Lord of Montpellier](/wiki/Lords_of_Montpellier).

Aged around 15, he inherited the lordship of Montpellier from his father in 1146 under the tutelage of his grandmother, Ermessende of Melgueil. His father had retired to a monastery leaving him the lordship. The wealth of the estate was derived from Mediterranean trade at the port of [Maguelone](/wiki/Maguelone). Montpellier joined in a coalition aligned against the [Count of Toulouse](/wiki/Count_of_Toulouse). In 1156 he married Matilda of Burgundy, daughter of [Hugh II, Duke of Burgundy](/wiki/Hugh_II,_Duke_of_Burgundy).

He fell ill in 1171 and made his will on St Michael's day (29 September 1171), appointing his brother [Gui Guerrejat](/wiki/Gui_Guerrejat) and John of Montlaur, [bishop of Maguelonne](/wiki/Bishop_of_Maguelonne), as joint guardians of his 4 young sons. He probably died in September 1172.

He and Matilda had nine children:

- Sibylle, who married Rayomond Gaucelm of [Lunel](/wiki/Lunel,_H%C3%A9rault)
- [William VIII of Montpellier](/wiki/William_VIII_of_Montpellier)
- another William, who died between 1171 and 1180
- Gui, named Gui Burgundion after his mother and to distinguish him from his uncle [Gui Guerrejat](/wiki/Gui_Guerrejat). He married Azalais of Conas and had a daughter, Burgundione. He founded the [order of the Holy Ghost](/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Ghost)
- Raymond, who became a [Cistercian](/wiki/Cistercians) monk at [Grandselve](/wiki/Grandselve), then [bishop of Lodève](/wiki/Bishop_of_Lod%C3%A8ve), then (by 1192) [bishop of Agde](/wiki/Bishop_of_Agde)
- Guillemette, who married Raymond of [Anduze](/wiki/Anduze), son of Bernard V of Anduze
- Azalais or Adelaide
- Marie
- Clémence, who was for some time a nun, but in 1199 married Rostaing of Sabran, constable of the count of Toulouse

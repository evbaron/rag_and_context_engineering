**Johnell Davis** (born May 23, 2001) is an American [basketball](/wiki/Basketball) player for the [Osceola Magic](/wiki/Osceola_Magic) of the [NBA G League](/wiki/NBA_G_League). He played [college basketball](/wiki/College_basketball) for the [Florida Atlantic Owls](/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_men%27s_basketball) and the [Arkansas Razorbacks](/wiki/Arkansas_Razorbacks_men%27s_basketball).[[1]](https://arkansasrazorbacks.com/razorbacks-get-johnell-davis/)

## High school career

Davis played basketball for [21st Century Charter School](/wiki/21st_Century_Charter_School_of_Gary) in [Gary, Indiana](/wiki/Gary,_Indiana). As a senior, he averaged 31.4 points, 9.8 rebounds, 4.6 steals and 2.6 assists per game, earning player of the year honors from the *[Post-Tribune](/wiki/Post-Tribune_(Indiana_newspaper))* and *[The Times of Northwest Indiana](/wiki/The_Times_of_Northwest_Indiana)*. He helped his team win four straight sectional titles. Davis broke the school's career and single-game (53) scoring records held by [Eugene German](/wiki/Eugene_German). He committed to play college basketball for [Florida Atlantic](/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_men%27s_basketball) over offers from Northern Illinois, Miami (Ohio) and Kent State.

## College career

Johnell Davis playing with the Florida Atlantic Owls in 2023.

As a freshman at Florida Atlantic, Davis averaged 3.3 points and 2.9 rebounds per game. In his sophomore season, he averaged 6.8 points and 4.2 rebounds per game. Davis moved into a leading role as a junior, despite initially coming off the bench due to injuries. He helped Florida Atlantic achieve its best season in program history, with [Conference USA](/wiki/Conference_USA) regular season and tournament titles. Davis was named first-team All-C-USA and C-USA Sixth Player of the Year. In the second round of the [2023 NCAA tournament](/wiki/2023_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament), he recorded 29 points, 12 rebounds, five assists, five steals in a 78–70 win over [Fairleigh Dickinson](/wiki/Fairleigh_Dickinson_Knights_men%27s_basketball). In FAU's victory, Davis became the first player in NCAA Tournament history to tally 25 points, 10 rebounds, five assists and five steals in a single game.

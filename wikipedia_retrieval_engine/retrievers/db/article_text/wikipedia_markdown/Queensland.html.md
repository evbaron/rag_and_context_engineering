**Queensland** (locally [/ˈkwiːnzlænd/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*KWEENZ-land*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key), commonly abbreviated as **QLD**) is a [state](/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia) in northeastern [Australia](/wiki/Australia), the second-largest and third-most populous state in Australia. It is bordered by the [Northern Territory](/wiki/Northern_Territory), [South Australia](/wiki/South_Australia) and [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales) to the west, south-west and south, respectively. To the east, Queensland is bordered by the [Coral Sea](/wiki/Coral_Sea) and the [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean); to the state's north is the [Torres Strait](/wiki/Torres_Strait), separating the Australian mainland from [Papua New Guinea](/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea), and the [Gulf of Carpentaria](/wiki/Gulf_of_Carpentaria) to the north-west. With an area of 1,723,030 square kilometres (665,270 sq mi), Queensland is the world's [sixth-largest subdivision](/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_area) of any country on earth; it [is larger than all but 16 countries](/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area). Due to its size, Queensland's geographical features and climates are diverse, and include [tropical rainforests](/wiki/Tropical_rainforest), [rivers](/wiki/River), [coral reefs](/wiki/Coral_reef), [mountain ranges](/wiki/Mountain_range) and white sandy beaches in its [tropical](/wiki/Tropical_climate) and [sub-tropical](/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) coastal regions, as well as [deserts](/wiki/Desert) and [savanna](/wiki/Savanna) in the [semi-arid](/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) and [desert](/wiki/Desert_climate) climatic regions of its [interior](/wiki/Outback).

Queensland has a population of over 5.5 million, concentrated in [South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland), where nearly three in four reside. The capital and largest city in the state is [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane), Australia's [third-largest city](/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Australia_by_population) and comprising fully half of the state's population. Ten of Australia's thirty largest cities are located in Queensland, the largest outside Brisbane being the [Gold Coast](/wiki/Gold_Coast,_Queensland), the [Sunshine Coast](/wiki/Sunshine_Coast,_Queensland), [Townsville](/wiki/Townsville), [Cairns](/wiki/Cairns), [Ipswich](/wiki/City_of_Ipswich), and [Toowoomba](/wiki/Toowoomba). 24.2% of the state's population were [born overseas](/wiki/Immigration_to_Australia). The state has the highest inter-state net migration in Australia.

Queensland was first inhabited by [Aboriginal Australians](/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians), with the [Torres Strait Islands](/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islands) inhabited by [Torres Strait Islanders](/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islanders). Dutch navigator [Willem Janszoon](/wiki/Willem_Janszoon), the first European to land in Australia, explored the west coast of the [Cape York Peninsula](/wiki/Cape_York_Peninsula) in 1606. In 1770, [James Cook](/wiki/James_Cook) claimed the east coast of Australia for the [Kingdom of Great Britain](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain). In 1788, [Arthur Phillip](/wiki/Arthur_Phillip) founded the colony of New South Wales, which included all of what is now Queensland. Queensland was explored in subsequent decades, and the [Moreton Bay Penal Settlement](/wiki/Moreton_Bay_Penal_Settlement) was established at Brisbane in 1824 by [John Oxley](/wiki/John_Oxley). During the [Australian frontier wars](/wiki/Australian_frontier_wars) of the 19th century, colonists killed tens of thousands of Aboriginal people in Queensland while consolidating their control over the territory.

On 6 June 1859 (now commemorated as [Queensland Day](/wiki/Queensland_Day)), [Queen Victoria](/wiki/Queen_Victoria) signed the letters patent to establish the colony of Queensland, [separating it from New South Wales](/wiki/Separation_of_Queensland) and thereby establishing Queensland as a [self-governing](/wiki/Self-governing_colony) [Crown colony](/wiki/Crown_colony) with [responsible government](/wiki/Responsible_government). Queensland was named for Queen Victoria, by the authority of Queen Victoria, based on a suggestion from Queen Victoria . Queensland's colonial economy was largely agricultural, and sugar and cotton sectors relied on [blackbirded](/wiki/Blackbirding) South Sea Islander labour.

Queensland was among the six colonies which became the founding states of Australia with [Federation](/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) on 1 January 1901. Since the [Bjelke-Petersen](/wiki/Joh_Bjelke-Petersen) era of the late 20th century, Queensland has received a high level of internal migration from the other states and territories of Australia and remains a popular destination for interstate migration.

Queensland has the [third-largest economy](/wiki/List_of_Australian_states_and_territories_by_gross_state_product) among Australian states, with strengths in mining, agriculture, transportation, [international education](/wiki/International_student), insurance, and banking. Nicknamed the *Sunshine State* for its tropical and sub-tropical climates, [Great Barrier Reef](/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef), and numerous beaches, tourism is also important to the state's economy.

## History

Main article: [History of Queensland](/wiki/History_of_Queensland)

### Pre-European contact

Main article: [History of Indigenous Australians](/wiki/History_of_Indigenous_Australians)

Queensland was one of the largest regions of pre-colonial Aboriginal population in Australia. The Aboriginal ownership of Queensland is thought to predate 50,000 BC, and early migrants are believed to have arrived via boat or land bridge across [Torres Strait](/wiki/Torres_Strait). Through time, their descendants developed into more than 90 different language and cultural groups.

During the last [ice age](/wiki/Ice_age), Queensland's landscape became more arid and largely desolate, making food and other supplies scarce. The people developed the world's first seed-grinding technology. The end of the [glacial period](/wiki/Glacial_period) brought about a warming climate, making the land more hospitable. It brought high rainfall along the eastern coast, stimulating the growth of the state's tropical rainforests.

The [Torres Strait Islands](/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islands) is home to the [Torres Strait Islander peoples](/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islanders). Torres Strait Islanders are ethnically and culturally distinct from mainland Aboriginal peoples. They have a long history of interaction with both Aboriginal peoples of what is now Australia and the peoples of [New Guinea](/wiki/New_Guinea).

### European colonisation

Captain [James Cook](/wiki/James_Cook) claims the [east coast of Australia](/wiki/Eastern_states_of_Australia) for the [Kingdom of Great Britain](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) at [Possession Island](/wiki/Possession_Island_(Queensland)) in 1770

The peninsula of [Seventeen Seventy, Queensland](/wiki/Seventeen_Seventy,_Queensland), where Captain Cook landed in 1770

Fighting between [Burke and Wills](/wiki/Burke_and_Wills_expedition)'s supply party and Aboriginal Australians at Bulla in 1861

In February 1606, Dutch navigator [Willem Janszoon](/wiki/Willem_Janszoon) landed near the site of what is now [Weipa](/wiki/Weipa,_Queensland), on the western shore of [Cape York](/wiki/Cape_York_Peninsula). This was the first recorded landing of a European in [Australia](/wiki/Australia), and it also marked the first reported contact between Europeans and the [Aboriginal people of Australia](/wiki/Aboriginal_people_of_Australia). The region was also explored by French and Spanish explorers (commanded by [Louis Antoine de Bougainville](/wiki/Louis_Antoine_de_Bougainville) and [Luís Vaez de Torres](/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Vaez_de_Torres), respectively) before the arrival of Lieutenant [James Cook](/wiki/James_Cook) in 1770. Cook claimed the east coast under instruction from [King George III](/wiki/King_George_III) of the [Kingdom of Great Britain](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) on 22 August 1770 at [Possession Island](/wiki/Possession_Island_(Queensland)), naming eastern Australia, including Queensland, *New South Wales*.

The Aboriginal population declined significantly after a [smallpox epidemic](/wiki/History_of_smallpox#Pacific_epidemics) during the late 18th century and massacres by the European settlers.

In 1823, [John Oxley](/wiki/John_Oxley), a British explorer, sailed north from what is now [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney) to scout possible penal colony sites in [Gladstone](/wiki/Gladstone,_Queensland) (then [Port Curtis](/wiki/Port_Curtis)) and [Moreton Bay](/wiki/Moreton_Bay). At Moreton Bay, he found the [Brisbane River](/wiki/Brisbane_River). He returned in 1824 and established a penal settlement at what is now [Redcliffe](/wiki/Redcliffe_Peninsula). The settlement, initially known as [Edenglassie](/wiki/Edenglassie), was then transferred to the current location of the [Brisbane city centre](/wiki/Brisbane_central_business_district). [Edmund Lockyer](/wiki/Edmund_Lockyer) discovered outcrops of coal along the banks of the upper Brisbane River in 1825. In 1839 transportation of convicts was ceased, culminating in the closure of the Brisbane penal settlement. In 1842 free settlement, which had already commenced, was officially permitted. In 1847, the [Port of Maryborough](/wiki/Port_of_Maryborough) was opened as a wool port. While most early immigrants came from New South Wales, the first free immigrant ship to arrive in Moreton Bay from Europe was the [*Artemisia*](/wiki/Artemisia_(ship)), in 1848.

Earlier than this immigrant ship was the arrival of the Irish famine orphan girls to Queensland. Devised by the then British Secretary of State for the Colonies, The Earl Grey Scheme established a special emigration scheme which was designed to resettle destitute girls from the workhouses of Ireland during the Great Famine. The first ship, the "Earl Grey", departed Ireland for a 124-day sail to Sydney. After controversy developed upon their arrival in Australia, a small group of 37 young orphans, sometimes referred to as The Belfast Girls or the Feisty Colleens, never set foot on Sydney soil, and instead sailed up to Brisbane (then Moreton Bay) on 21 October 1848 on board the *Ann Mary*. This scheme continued until 1852.

In 1857, Queensland's first lighthouse was built at [Cape Moreton](/wiki/Cape_Moreton).

### Frontier wars and massacres

Further information: [Australian frontier wars § Queensland](/wiki/Australian_frontier_wars#Queensland), and [List of massacres of Indigenous Australians § Queensland](/wiki/List_of_massacres_of_Indigenous_Australians#Queensland)

The frontier wars fought between European settlers and Aboriginal tribes in Queensland were the bloodiest and most brutal in colonial Australia. Many of these conflicts are now seen as acts of genocide.

The wars featured the most frequent massacres of First Nations people, the three deadliest massacres on white settlers, the most disreputable frontier police force, and the highest number of white victims to frontier violence on record in any Australian colony. Across at least 644 collisions at least 66,680 were killed — with Aboriginal fatalities alone comprising no less than 65,180. Of these deaths, around 24,000 Aboriginal men, women and children were killed by the Native Police between 1859 and 1897.

The military force of the Queensland Government in this war was the [Native Police](/wiki/Australian_native_police), who operated from 1849 to the 1920s. The Native Police was a body of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander troopers that operated under the command of white officers. The Native Police were often recruited forcefully from far-away communities.

Aftermath of the 1861 [Cullin-La-Ringo massacre](/wiki/Cullin-La-Ringo_massacre) in which 19 settlers were killed by Aboriginal people, the deadliest attack on settlers in the frontier wars

Conflict spread quickly with free settlement in 1838, with settlement rapidly expanding in a great rush to take up the surrounding land in the [Darling Downs](/wiki/Darling_Downs), Logan and Brisbane Valley and South Burnett onwards from 1840, in many cases leading to widespread fighting and heavy loss of life. The conflict later spread north to the [Wide Bay](/wiki/Wide_Bay%E2%80%93Burnett) and [Burnett River](/wiki/Burnett_River) and [Hervey Bay](/wiki/Hervey_Bay) region, and at one stage the settlement of [Maryborough](/wiki/Maryborough,_Queensland) was virtually under siege.

The largest reasonably well-documented massacres in southeast Queensland were the [Kilcoy](/wiki/Kilcoy,_Queensland) and [Whiteside](/wiki/Whiteside,_Queensland) poisonings, each of which was said to have taken up to 70 Aboriginal lives by use of a gift of flour laced with [strychnine](/wiki/Strychnine). At the [Battle of One Tree Hill](/wiki/Battle_of_One_Tree_Hill) in September 1843, [Multuggerah](/wiki/Multuggerah) and his group of warriors ambushed one group of settlers, routing them and subsequently others in the skirmishes which followed, starting in retaliation for the Kilcoy poisoning.

Central Queensland was particularly hard hit during the 1860s and 1870s, several contemporary writers mention the Skull Hole, Bladensburg, or Mistake Creek massacre on [Bladensburg Station](/wiki/Bladensburg_Station) near [Winton](/wiki/Winton,_Queensland), which in 1901 was said to have taken up to 200 Aboriginal lives. First Nations warriors killed 19 settlers during the [Cullin-La-Ringo massacre](/wiki/Cullin-La-Ringo_massacre) on 17 October 1861. In the weeks afterwards, police, native police and civilians killed up to 370 members of the [Gayiri](/wiki/Gayiri) Aboriginal people in response.

Frontier violence peaked on the northern mining frontier during the 1870s, most notably in Cook district and on the [Palmer](/wiki/Palmer_River_(Queensland)) and Hodgkinson River goldfields, with heavy loss of Aboriginal lives and several well-known massacres. Raids conducted by the [Kalkadoon](/wiki/Kalkatungu) held settlers out of Western Queensland for ten years until September 1884 when they attacked a force of settlers and native police at Battle Mountain near modern [Cloncurry](/wiki/Cloncurry,_Queensland). The subsequent battle of Battle Mountain ended in disaster for the Kalkadoon, who suffered heavy losses. Fighting continued in [North Queensland](/wiki/North_Queensland), however, with First Nations raiders attacking sheep and cattle while Native Police mounted heavy retaliatory massacres.

### Blackbirding

Further information: [Blackbirding § Queensland](/wiki/Blackbirding#Queensland)

[Kanaka workers](/wiki/Kanaka_(Pacific_Island_worker)) in a sugar cane plantation, late 19th century

Tens of thousands of [South Sea Islanders](/wiki/South_Sea_Islanders) were forced, deceived or coerced into [indentured servitude](/wiki/Indentured_servitude) on Australia's agricultural plantations. The practice persisted despite slavery being outlawed in Australia and other parts of the [British Empire](/wiki/British_Empire) by the [Slavery Abolition Act 1833](/wiki/Slavery_Abolition_Act_1833). This was known as blackbirding. The appalling conditions have been compared to slavery, or for Brooke Kroeger, slavery's 'just-as-evil twin' . This trade in what were then known as [Kanakas](/wiki/Kanaka_(Pacific_Island_worker)) was in operation from 1863 to 1908, a period of 45 years. Some 55,000 to 62,500 were brought to Australia, most being recruited or blackbirded from islands in [Melanesia](/wiki/Melanesia), such as the [New Hebrides](/wiki/New_Hebrides) (now [Vanuatu](/wiki/Vanuatu)), the [Solomon Islands](/wiki/Solomon_Islands) and the islands around [New Guinea](/wiki/New_Guinea).

Blackbirded South Sea Islanders on a [Sugarcane](/wiki/Sugarcane) plantation in Queensland

The majority of those taken were male and around one quarter were under the age of sixteen. In total, approximately 15,000 South Sea Islanders (30%) died while labouring in Queensland – excluding those who died in transit or were killed in the recruitment process – mostly during three-year contracts. This is similar to the estimated 33% death rate among enslaved Africans in the first three years of arriving in America, Brazil, and the Caribbean; the conditions were often comparable to those of the [Atlantic slave trade](/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade).

The trade was legally sanctioned and regulated under Queensland law, and prominent men such as Robert Towns made massive fortunes through blackbirding, helping to establish some of the major cities in Queensland today. Towns' agent claimed that blackbirded labourers were "savages who did not know the use of money" and therefore did not deserve cash wages.

Following Federation in 1901, the [White Australia policy](/wiki/White_Australia_policy) came into effect, which saw most foreign workers in Australia deported under the *[Pacific Island Labourers Act 1901](/wiki/Pacific_Island_Labourers_Act_1901)*, which saw the Pacific Islander population of the state decrease rapidly.

### Independent governance

Further information: [Colony of Queensland](/wiki/Colony_of_Queensland)

[Sheep shearers](/wiki/Sheep_shearing) and farm hands at [Jondaryan Woolshed](/wiki/Jondaryan_Woolshed), c. 1872

Parade of troops in Brisbane, prior to departure for the [Boer War](/wiki/Second_Boer_War) in South Africa

A public meeting was held in 1851 to consider the proposed [separation of Queensland](/wiki/Separation_of_Queensland) from New South Wales. On 6 June 1859, Queen Victoria signed letters patent to form the separate colony of Queensland as a [self-governing](/wiki/Self-governing_colony) [Crown colony](/wiki/Crown_colony) with [responsible government](/wiki/Responsible_government). [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane) was selected as the capital city. On 10 December 1859, a proclamation was read by [George Bowen](/wiki/George_Bowen), the first [Governor of Queensland](/wiki/Governor_of_Queensland), formally establishing Queensland as a separate colony from New South Wales. On 22 May 1860 the first Queensland election was held and [Robert Herbert](/wiki/Robert_Herbert), Bowen's private secretary, was appointed as the first [Premier of Queensland](/wiki/Premier_of_Queensland).

In 1865, the first rail line in the state opened between [Ipswich](/wiki/Ipswich,_Queensland) and [Grandchester](/wiki/Grandchester). Queensland's economy expanded rapidly in 1867 after James Nash discovered gold on the [Mary River](/wiki/Mary_River_(Queensland)) near the town of [Gympie](/wiki/Gympie), sparking a gold rush and saving the Colony of Queensland from near economic collapse. While still significant, they were on a much smaller scale than the gold rushes of Victoria and New South Wales.

Immigration to Australia and Queensland, in particular, began in the 1850s to support the state economy. During the period from the 1860s until the early 20th century, many labourers, known at the time as [Kanakas](/wiki/Kanakas), were brought to Queensland from neighbouring Pacific Island nations to work in the state's sugar cane fields. Some of these people had been kidnapped under a process known as [blackbirding](/wiki/Blackbirding) or press-ganging, and their employment conditions constituted an allegedly exploitative form of indentured labour. [Italian immigrants](/wiki/Italian_Australians) entered the sugar cane industry from the 1890s.

During the 1890s, the six Australian colonies, including Queensland, held a series of referendums which culminated in the [Federation of Australia](/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) on 1 January 1901. During this time, Queensland had a population of half a million people. Since then, Queensland has remained a [federated state](/wiki/Federated_state) within Australia, and its population has significantly grown.

### 20th century

Returned World War II soldiers march in Queen Street, Brisbane, 1944

In 1905 women voted in state elections for the first time. The state's first university, the [University of Queensland](/wiki/University_of_Queensland), was established in Brisbane in 1909. In 1911, the first alternative treatments for polio were pioneered in Queensland and remain in use across the world today.

[World War I](/wiki/World_War_I) had a [major impact on Queensland](/wiki/Queensland_in_World_War_I). Over 58,000 Queenslanders fought in World War I and over 10,000 of them died.

Australia's first major airline, [Qantas](/wiki/Qantas) (originally standing for "Queensland and Northern Territory Aerial Services"), was founded in [Winton](/wiki/Winton,_Queensland) in 1920 to serve outback Queensland.

In 1922 Queensland abolished the [Queensland Legislative Council](/wiki/Queensland_Legislative_Council), becoming the only Australian state with a [unicameral](/wiki/Unicameral_legislature) [parliament](/wiki/Parliaments_of_the_Australian_states_and_territories).

In 1935 [cane toads](/wiki/Cane_toads_in_Australia) were deliberately introduced to Queensland from Hawaii in an unsuccessful attempt to reduce the number of French's cane and greyback [cane beetles](/wiki/Cane_beetle) that were destroying the roots of sugar cane plants, which are integral to Queensland's economy. The toads have remained an environmental pest since that time. In 1962, the first commercial production of oil in Queensland and Australia began at [Moonie](/wiki/Moonie,_Queensland).

During [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II) Brisbane became central to the [Allied](/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) campaign when the AMP Building (now called [MacArthur Central](/wiki/MacArthur_Central)) was used as the [South West Pacific headquarters](/wiki/South_West_Pacific_Area_(command)#Command) for [General Douglas MacArthur](/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur), chief of the Allied Pacific forces, until his headquarters were moved to [Hollandia](/wiki/Jayapura) in August 1944. In 1942, during the war, Brisbane was the site of a violent clash between visiting US military personnel and Australian servicemen and civilians, which resulted in one death and hundreds of injuries. This incident became known colloquially as the [Battle of Brisbane](/wiki/Battle_of_Brisbane).

[Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) visited Brisbane in October 1982 for the [Commonwealth Games](/wiki/1982_Commonwealth_Games)

The end of World War II saw a [wave of immigration](/wiki/Post-war_immigration_to_Australia) from across Europe, with many more immigrants coming from [southern](/wiki/Southern_Europe) and [eastern](/wiki/Eastern_Europe) Europe than in previous decades.

In the later decades of the 20th century, the [humid subtropical climate](/wiki/Subtropical_climate)—regulated by the availability of air conditioning—saw Queensland become a popular destination for migrants from interstate. Since that time, Queensland has continuously seen high levels of migration from the other states and territories of Australia.

In 1966, [Lyndon B. Johnson](/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) became the first U.S. president to visit Queensland. During his visit, he met with Australia prime minister [Harold Holt](/wiki/Harold_Holt).

The end of the [White Australia policy](/wiki/White_Australia_policy) in 1973 saw the beginning of a wave of immigration from around the world, and most prominently from Asia, which continues to the present.

In 1981 the [Great Barrier Reef](/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef) off Queensland's northeast coast, one of the world's largest coral reef systems, was declared a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_Site).

### 21st century

In 2003 Queensland adopted [maroon](/wiki/Maroon) as the state's official colour. The announcement was made as a result of an informal tradition to use maroon to represent the state in association with sporting events.

After three decades of record population growth, Queensland was impacted by major [floods between late 2010 and early 2011](/wiki/2010%E2%80%932011_Queensland_floods), causing extensive damage and disruption across the state.

In 2020, Queensland was impacted by the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). Despite a [low number and abrupt decline in cases](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Australia) from April 2020 onward, [social distancing](/wiki/Social_distancing) requirements were implemented from March 2020 including the closure of the state borders.

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Queensland](/wiki/Geography_of_Queensland)

Commonly designated [regions of Queensland](/wiki/Regions_of_Queensland), with [Central Queensland](/wiki/Central_Queensland) divided into [Mackay](/wiki/Mackay_Region) and [Fitzroy](/wiki/Shire_of_Fitzroy) subregions

The [Great Barrier Reef](/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef), which extends along most of Queensland's [Coral Sea](/wiki/Coral_Sea) coastline

The [Mossman River](/wiki/Mossman_River), flowing through the [Daintree Rainforest](/wiki/Daintree_Rainforest) in [Far North Queensland](/wiki/Far_North_Queensland)

The [McPherson Range](/wiki/McPherson_Range) at [Lamington National Park](/wiki/Lamington_National_Park) in [South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland)

Lake McKensie, [K'gari (Fraser Island)](/wiki/K%27gari_(Fraser_Island))

With a total area of 1,729,742 square kilometres (715,309 square miles), Queensland is an expansive state with a highly diverse range of climates and geographical features. If Queensland were an independent nation, it would be the world's 16th largest in terms of area.

Queensland's eastern coastline borders the [Coral Sea](/wiki/Coral_Sea), an arm of the Pacific Ocean. The state is bordered by the [Torres Strait](/wiki/Torres_Strait) to the north, with [Boigu Island](/wiki/Boigu_Island_(Queensland)) off the coast of [New Guinea](/wiki/New_Guinea) representing the northern extreme of its territory. The triangular [Cape York Peninsula](/wiki/Cape_York_Peninsula), which points toward New Guinea, is the northernmost part of the state's mainland. West of the peninsula's tip, northern Queensland is bordered by the [Gulf of Carpentaria](/wiki/Gulf_of_Carpentaria). To the west, Queensland is bordered by the [Northern Territory](/wiki/Northern_Territory), at the [138th meridian east](/wiki/138th_meridian_east), and to the southwest by northeastern [South Australia](/wiki/South_Australia). The state's southern border with New South Wales is constituted in the east by the [watershed](/wiki/Drainage_divide) from [Point Danger](/wiki/Point_Danger_(Tweed_Heads)) to the [Dumaresq River](/wiki/Dumaresq_River), and the Dumaresq, [Macintyre](/wiki/Macintyre_River) and [Barwon](/wiki/Barwon_River_(New_South_Wales)) rivers. The west of the southern border is defined by the [29th parallel south](/wiki/29th_parallel_south) (including some minor [historical encroachments](/wiki/Queensland_and_New_South_Wales_boundary_encroachments)) until it reaches South Australia.

Like much of eastern Australia, the [Great Dividing Range](/wiki/Great_Dividing_Range) runs roughly parallel with, and inland from, the coast, and areas west of the range are more arid than the humid coastal regions. The [Great Barrier Reef](/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef), which is the world's largest [coral reef](/wiki/Coral_reef) system, runs parallel to the state's [Coral Sea](/wiki/Coral_Sea) coast between the [Torres Strait](/wiki/Torres_Strait) and [K'gari (Fraser Island)](/wiki/K%27gari_(Fraser_Island)). Queensland's coastline includes the world's three largest sand islands: [K'gari (Fraser Island)](/wiki/K%27gari_(Fraser_Island)), [Moreton](/wiki/Moreton_Island), and [North Stradbroke](/wiki/North_Stradbroke_Island).

The state contains six [World Heritage](/wiki/World_Heritage_Site)-listed preservation areas: the Great Barrier Reef along the Coral Sea coast, K'gari (Fraser Island) on the [Wide Bay–Burnett](/wiki/Wide_Bay%E2%80%93Burnett) region's coastline, the [wet tropics](/wiki/Wet_Tropics_of_Queensland) in [Far North Queensland](/wiki/Far_North_Queensland) including the [Daintree Rainforest](/wiki/Daintree_Rainforest), [Lamington National Park](/wiki/Lamington_National_Park) in [South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland), the [Riversleigh fossil sites](/wiki/Australian_Fossil_Mammal_Sites_(Riversleigh)) in [North West Queensland](/wiki/Gulf_Country), and the [Gondwana Rainforests](/wiki/Gondwana_Rainforests) in South East Queensland.

The state is divided into several [unofficial regions](/wiki/Regions_of_Queensland) which are commonly used to refer to large areas of the state's vast geography. These include:

- **[South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland)** in the state's coastal extreme south-eastern corner, an urban region which includes the state's three largest cities: capital city Brisbane and popular coastal tourist destinations the [Gold Coast](/wiki/Gold_Coast,_Queensland) and [Sunshine Coast](/wiki/Sunshine_Coast,_Queensland). In some definitions, it also includes the city of [Toowoomba](/wiki/Toowoomba,_Queensland). South East Queensland accounts for more than 70% of the state's population.
- The **[Darling Downs](/wiki/Darling_Downs)** in the state's inland southeast, which consists of fertile agricultural (particularly cattle grazing) land and in some definitions includes the city of Toowoomba. The region also includes the mountainous [Granite Belt](/wiki/Granite_Belt), the state's coldest region which occasionally experiences snow.
- **[Wide Bay–Burnett](/wiki/Wide_Bay%E2%80%93Burnett)** in the state's coastal southeast, to the north of the South East Queensland region. It is rich in [sugar cane](/wiki/Sugar_cane) farms and includes the cities of [Bundaberg](/wiki/Bundaberg), [Hervey Bay](/wiki/Hervey_Bay) as well as [K'gari (Fraser Island)](/wiki/K%27gari_(Fraser_Island)), the world's largest sand island.
- **[Central Queensland](/wiki/Central_Queensland)** on the state's central coastline, which is dominated by cattle farmland and coal mining. It contains the [Capricorn Coast](/wiki/Capricorn_Coast) and [Whitsunday Islands](/wiki/Whitsunday_Islands) tourist regions, as well as the cities of [Rockhampton](/wiki/Rockhampton) and [Mackay](/wiki/Mackay,_Queensland).
- **[North Queensland](/wiki/North_Queensland)** on the state's northern coastline, which is dominated by cattle farmland and mining and which includes the city of [Townsville](/wiki/Townsville).
- **[Far North Queensland](/wiki/Far_North_Queensland)** on the state's extreme northern coastline along the [Cape York Peninsula](/wiki/Cape_York_Peninsula), which includes [tropical rainforest](/wiki/Tropical_rainforest), the state's highest mountain, [Mount Bartle Frere](/wiki/Mount_Bartle_Frere), the [Atherton Tablelands](/wiki/Atherton_Tablelands) pastoral region (dominated by [sugar cane](/wiki/Sugar_cane) and [tropical fruits](/wiki/Tropical_fruit)), the most visited section of the [Great Barrier Reef](/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef), as well as the city of [Cairns](/wiki/Cairns,_Queensland).
- **[South West Queensland](/wiki/South_West_Queensland)** in the state's inland south-west, which is a primarily agricultural region dominated by cattle farmland, and which includes the [Channel Country](/wiki/Channel_Country) region of [intertwining rivulets](/wiki/Braided_river).
- **[Central West Queensland](/wiki/Central_West_Queensland)** in the state's inland central-west, dominated by cattle farmland and which includes the city of [Longreach](/wiki/Longreach,_Queensland).
- The **[Gulf Country](/wiki/Gulf_Country)** (also known as North West Queensland), in the state's inland north-west along the [Gulf of Carpentaria](/wiki/Gulf_of_Carpentaria), which is dominated by [savanna](/wiki/Savanna) and mining and includes the [city of Mount Isa](/wiki/City_of_Mount_Isa).

### Climate

[Köppen climate types](/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) in Queensland

Because of its size, there is significant variation in climate across the state. There is ample rainfall along the coastline, with a [monsoonal](/wiki/Monsoon) wet season in the [tropical](/wiki/Tropical_climate) north, and [humid sub-tropical](/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) conditions along the southern coastline. Low rainfall and hot humid summers are typical for the inland and west. Elevated areas in the south-eastern inland can experience temperatures well below freezing in mid-winter providing [frost](/wiki/Frost) and, rarely, [snowfall](/wiki/Snowfall). The climate of the coastal regions is influenced by warm ocean waters, keeping the region free from extremes of temperature and providing moisture for rainfall.

There are six predominant climatic zones in Queensland, based on temperature and humidity:

- Hot humid summer, warm humid winter (far north and coastal): [Cairns](/wiki/Cairns), [Innisfail](/wiki/Innisfail,_Queensland)
- Hot humid summer, warm dry winter (north and coastal): [Townsville](/wiki/Townsville), [Mackay](/wiki/Mackay,_Queensland)
- Hot humid summer, mild dry winter (coastal elevated areas and coastal south-east): [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane), [Bundaberg](/wiki/Bundaberg), [Rockhampton](/wiki/Rockhampton)
- Hot dry summer, mild dry winter (central inland and north-west): [Mt Isa](/wiki/Mt_Isa), [Emerald](/wiki/Emerald,_Queensland), [Longreach](/wiki/Longreach)
- Hot dry summer, cool dry winter (southern inland): [Roma](/wiki/Roma,_Queensland), [Charleville](/wiki/Charleville,_Queensland), [Goondiwindi](/wiki/Goondiwindi)
- Warm humid summer, cold dry winter (elevated south-eastern areas): [Toowoomba](/wiki/Toowoomba), [Warwick](/wiki/Warwick,_Queensland), [Stanthorpe](/wiki/Stanthorpe)

The coastal far north of the state is the wettest region in Australia, with [Mount Bellenden Ker](/wiki/Mount_Bellenden_Ker), south of Cairns, holding many Australian rainfall records with its annual average rainfall of over 8 metres (26 ft). Snow is rare in Queensland, although it does fall with some regularity along the far southern border with New South Wales, predominantly in the Stanthorpe district although on rare occasions further north and west. The most northerly snow ever recorded in Australia occurred near [Mackay](/wiki/Mackay,_Queensland); however, this was exceptional.

Natural disasters are often a threat in Queensland: severe [tropical cyclones](/wiki/Tropical_cyclone) can impact the central and northern coastlines and cause severe damage, with recent examples including [Larry](/wiki/Cyclone_Larry), [Yasi](/wiki/Cyclone_Yasi), [Ita](/wiki/Cyclone_Ita) and [Debbie](/wiki/Cyclone_Debbie). Flooding from rain-bearing systems can also be severe and can occur anywhere in Queensland. One of the deadliest and most damaging floods in the history of the state occurred in [early 2011](/wiki/2010-11_Queensland_floods). Severe springtime [thunderstorms](/wiki/Thunderstorm) generally affect the south-east and inland of the state and can bring damaging winds, torrential rain, large [hail](/wiki/Hail) and even [tornadoes](/wiki/Tornado). The [strongest tornado](/wiki/Bucca_tornado) ever recorded in Australia occurred in Queensland near [Bundaberg](/wiki/Bundaberg) in November 1992. Droughts and [bushfires](/wiki/Bushfires_in_Australia) can also occur; however, the latter are generally less severe than those that occur in southern states.

The highest official maximum temperature recorded in the state was 49.5 °C (121.1 °F) at Birdsville Police Station on 24 December 1972. The lowest recorded minimum temperature is −10.6 °C (12.9 °F) at Stanthorpe on 23 June 1961 and at The Hermitage (near [Warwick](/wiki/Warwick,_Queensland)) on 12 July 1965.

In December 2021, Queensland had an estimated population of 5,265,043. Approximately half of the state's population lives in Brisbane, and over 70% live in [South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland). There have however been short periods where [Victoria](/wiki/Victoria_(Australia)) and [Western Australia](/wiki/Western_Australia) have grown faster.

### Language

At the [2016 census](/wiki/2016_Australian_census), 81.2% of inhabitants spoke only English at home, with the next most common languages being [Mandarin](/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) (1.5%), [Vietnamese](/wiki/Vietnamese_language) (0.6%), [Cantonese](/wiki/Cantonese) (0.5%), Spanish (0.4%) and Italian (0.4%).

At the [2021 census](/wiki/2021_Australian_census), 80.5% of inhabitants spoke only English at home, with the next most common languages being [Mandarin](/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) (1.6%), [Vietnamese](/wiki/Vietnamese_language) (0.6%), [Punjabi](/wiki/Punjabi_language) (0.6%) and Spanish (0.6%).

### Religion

At the [2016 census](/wiki/2016_Australian_census), the most commonly cited religious affiliations were ['No religion'](/wiki/Irreligion) (29.2%), [Catholicism](/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Australia) (21.7%) and [Anglicanism](/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Australia) (15.3%). In the 2016 Census the majority of Queenslanders were identified as Christian, most of which were of various Protestant denominations.

According to the [2021 census](/wiki/2021_Australian_census), 45.7% of the population follows Christianity, and 41.2% identified as having [No religion](/wiki/Irreligion) About 5% of people are affiliated with a non-Christian religion, mainly [Buddhism](/wiki/Buddhism) (1.4%), [Hinduism](/wiki/Hinduism) (1.3%) and [Islam](/wiki/Islam) (1.2%). The 2021 census found that Protestants of various denominations outnumbered Catholics in Queensland.

### Education

The Great Court at the [University of Queensland](/wiki/University_of_Queensland) in Brisbane, Queensland's oldest university

Queensland is home to numerous universities. The state's oldest university, the [University of Queensland](/wiki/University_of_Queensland), was established in 1909 and frequently [ranks among the world's top 50](/wiki/College_and_university_rankings). Other major universities include [Queensland University of Technology](/wiki/Queensland_University_of_Technology), [Griffith University](/wiki/Griffith_University), the [University of Southern Queensland](/wiki/University_of_Southern_Queensland), the [University of the Sunshine Coast](/wiki/University_of_the_Sunshine_Coast), [James Cook University](/wiki/James_Cook_University) (which was the state's first university outside of [South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland)), [Central Queensland University](/wiki/Central_Queensland_University) and [Bond University](/wiki/Bond_University) (which was Australia's first private university).

[International education](/wiki/International_student) is an important industry, with 134,312 [international students enrolled in the state](/wiki/International_students_in_Australia) in 2018, largely focused on Brisbane. Most of the state's international students are from Asia.

At the primary and secondary levels, Queensland is home to numerous [state](/wiki/Queensland_state_schools) and private schools.

Queensland has a [public library system](/wiki/List_of_libraries_in_Queensland#Public_libraries) which is managed by the State Library of Queensland. Some university libraries are also open to the public.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Queensland](/wiki/Economy_of_Queensland)

See also: [Economy of Australia](/wiki/Economy_of_Australia)

Skyline of the [Brisbane central business district](/wiki/Brisbane_central_business_district). Brisbane is a [global city](/wiki/Global_city) and the state's largest economic hub.

[Gold mine](/wiki/Gold_mining) at [Ravenswood](/wiki/Ravenswood,_Queensland) in [North Queensland](/wiki/North_Queensland). Mining is one of the state's major industries

The [Gold Coast](/wiki/Gold_Coast,_Queensland), Queensland's second-largest city and a major tourist destination

In 2019, Queensland had a [gross state product](/wiki/List_of_Australian_states_and_territories_by_gross_state_product) of A$357,044 million, the [third-highest in the nation](/wiki/List_of_Australian_states_and_territories_by_gross_state_product) after New South Wales and [Victoria](/wiki/Victoria_(Australia)). The construction of [sea ports](/wiki/Sea_port) and railways along Queensland's coast in the 19th century set up the foundations for the state's export-oriented mining and agricultural sectors. Since the 1980s, a sizeable influx of interstate and overseas migrants, large amounts of federal government investment, increased mining of vast mineral deposits and an expanding aerospace sector have contributed to the state's economic growth.

[Primary industries](/wiki/Primary_sector_of_industry) include bananas, [pineapples](/wiki/Pineapple), peanuts, a wide variety of other tropical and temperate fruit and vegetables, grain crops, [wineries](/wiki/Winery), cattle raising, cotton, [sugarcane](/wiki/Sugarcane), and [wool](/wiki/Wool). The mining industry includes [bauxite](/wiki/Bauxite), coal, silver, lead, [zinc](/wiki/Zinc), gold and copper.

[Secondary industries](/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) are mostly further processing of the above-mentioned primary produce. For example, bauxite is shipped by sea from [Weipa](/wiki/Weipa,_Queensland) and converted to alumina at [Gladstone](/wiki/Gladstone,_Queensland). There is also copper refining and the refining of sugar cane to sugar at a number of mills along the eastern coastline.

Major [tertiary industries](/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) are retail, tourism, and [international education](/wiki/International_student). In 2018, there were 134,312 [international students enrolled in the state](/wiki/International_students_in_Australia), largely focused on Brisbane. Most of the state's international students are from Asia.

Brisbane is [categorised](/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) as a [global city](/wiki/Global_city), and is among Asia-Pacific [cities with largest GDPs](/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP). It has strengths in mining, banking, insurance, transportation, information technology, [real estate](/wiki/Real_estate) and [food](/wiki/Food_industry). Some of the largest companies headquartered in Brisbane, all among Australia's largest, include [Suncorp Group](/wiki/Suncorp_Group), [Virgin Australia](/wiki/Virgin_Australia), [Aurizon](/wiki/Aurizon), [Bank of Queensland](/wiki/Bank_of_Queensland), [Flight Centre](/wiki/Flight_Centre), [CUA](/wiki/CUA_(company)), [Sunsuper](/wiki/Sunsuper), [QSuper](/wiki/QSuper), [Domino's Pizza Enterprises](/wiki/Domino%27s_Pizza_Enterprises), [Star Entertainment Group](/wiki/Star_Entertainment_Group), [ALS](/wiki/ALS_Limited), [TechnologyOne](/wiki/TechnologyOne), [NEXTDC](/wiki/NEXTDC), [Super Retail Group](/wiki/Super_Retail_Group), [New Hope Coal](/wiki/New_Hope_Coal), [Jumbo Interactive](/wiki/Jumbo_Interactive), [National Storage](/wiki/National_Storage), [Collins Foods](/wiki/Collins_Foods) and [Boeing Australia](/wiki/Boeing_Australia).

### Tourism

See also: [Tourism in Brisbane](/wiki/Tourism_in_Brisbane)

Hill Inlet at the [Whitsunday Islands](/wiki/Whitsunday_Islands).

As a result of its varied landscapes, warm climate, and abundant natural environment, tourism is Queensland's leading tertiary industry with millions of interstate and international visitors visiting the state each year. The industry generates $8.8 billion annually, accounting for 4.5% of Queensland's Gross State Product. It has an annual export of $4.0 billion annually. The sector directly employs about 5.7% of Queensland citizens. Accommodation in Queensland caters for nearly 22% of the total expenditure, followed by restaurants/meals (15%), airfares (11%), fuel (11%) and shopping/gifts (11%).

## Politics and government

Main article: [Politics of Queensland](/wiki/Politics_of_Queensland)

One of the six founding [states of Australia](/wiki/States_and_territories_of_Australia), Queensland has been a [federated state](/wiki/Federated_state) subject to the [Australian Constitution](/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia) since 1 January 1901. It may legislate on all matters not [ceded in the Australian Constitution to the federal government](/wiki/Section_51_of_the_Constitution_of_Australia). It is a [parliamentary](/wiki/Parliamentary_system) [constitutional monarchy](/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy). The [Constitution of Queensland](/wiki/Constitution_of_Queensland) sets out the operation of the state's government. The state's constitution contains several [entrenched provisions](/wiki/Entrenched_clause) which cannot be changed in the absence of a [referendum](/wiki/Referendum). There is also a [statutory](/wiki/Statutory_law) [charter of rights](/wiki/Charter_of_rights), the *Queensland Human Rights Act 2019*. Queensland's system of government is influenced by the [Westminster system](/wiki/Westminster_system) and [Australia's federal system of government](/wiki/Politics_of_Australia).

The government power [can be divided](/wiki/Separation_of_powers_in_Australia) into three groups:

- Legislature: the [unicameral](/wiki/Unicameralism) [Parliament of Queensland](/wiki/Parliament_of_Queensland), comprising the [Legislative Assembly](/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Queensland) and the [Monarch](/wiki/Monarchy_of_Australia) (represented by the [Governor](/wiki/Governor_of_Queensland));
- Executive: the [Queensland Government](/wiki/Queensland_Government), which consists of the [Executive Council of Queensland](/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Queensland), which formalises decisions of the [Cabinet of Queensland](/wiki/Cabinet_of_Queensland), which is composed of the [Premier](/wiki/Premier_of_Queensland) and other ministers of state appointed by the Governor on the advice of the premier;
- Judiciary: the [Supreme Court](/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Queensland) and other state courts, whose judges are appointed by the Governor on the advice of Parliament.

[Executive authority](/wiki/Executive_(government)) is nominally vested in the [Governor of Queensland](/wiki/Governor_of_Queensland) (currently [Jeannette Young](/wiki/Jeannette_Young)) who represents and is appointed by the [Monarch](/wiki/Monarchy_of_Australia) (currently [Charles III](/wiki/Charles_III)) on the advice of the [Premier of Queensland](/wiki/Premier_of_Queensland). The Premier, who is the state's [Head of government](/wiki/Head_of_government), along with the [Cabinet of Queensland](/wiki/Cabinet_of_Queensland) (whose decisions are formalised by the [Executive Council](/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Queensland)), exercise executive authority in practice. The Premier is appointed by the Governor and [must have support](/wiki/Responsible_government) of the [Legislative Assembly of Queensland](/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Queensland). The Premier is in practice a leading member of the Legislative Assembly and parliamentary leader of his or her political party, or coalition of parties, and members of the Cabinet will be drawn from the same party or coalition. The current Premier and [Deputy Premier](/wiki/Deputy_Premier_of_Queensland) are [David Crisafulli](/wiki/David_Crisafulli) and [Jarrod Bleijie](/wiki/Jarrod_Bleijie) of the [Liberal National Party](/wiki/Liberal_National_Party_of_Queensland) respectively. [Government House](/wiki/Government_House,_Brisbane) at [Paddington](/wiki/Paddington,_Queensland) in Brisbane is the seat of the Governor, having replaced [Old Government House](/wiki/Old_Government_House,_Queensland) at [Gardens Point](/wiki/Gardens_Point,_Brisbane) in Brisbane's [CBD](/wiki/Brisbane_central_business_district) in the early 20th century. The executive branch is simply referred to as the [Queensland Government](/wiki/Queensland_Government).

[Legislative authority](/wiki/Legislature) is exercised by the [Queensland Parliament](/wiki/Parliament_of_Queensland) which uniquely for Australian states is [unicameral](/wiki/Unicameralism), containing only one house, the Legislative Assembly. The Parliament was [bicameral](/wiki/Bicameral_legislature) until 1922 when the [Legislative Council](/wiki/Queensland_Legislative_Council) was abolished by the Labor "suicide squad", so called because they were appointed for the purpose of voting to abolish their own offices. [Bills](/wiki/Bill_(law)) receive [royal assent](/wiki/Royal_assent) from the [Governor](/wiki/Governor_of_Queensland) before being passed into law. The Parliament's seat is at [Parliament House](/wiki/Parliament_House,_Brisbane) at [Gardens Point](/wiki/Gardens_Point,_Brisbane) in Brisbane's CBD. Members of the Legislative Assembly represent [93 electoral districts](/wiki/Electoral_districts_of_Queensland). Elections in Queensland are held at the end of each fixed four-year parliamentary term and are determined by full [preferential voting](/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting).

The state's [judiciary](/wiki/Judiciary) consists of the [Supreme Court of Queensland](/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Queensland) and the [District Court of Queensland](/wiki/District_Court_of_Queensland), established by the Queensland Constitution, as well as the [Magistrates Court of Queensland](/wiki/Magistrates_Court_of_Queensland) and other courts and tribunals established by legislation. Cases may be appealed to the [High Court of Australia](/wiki/High_Court_of_Australia). As with all Australian states and territories, Queensland has a [common law](/wiki/Common_law) legal system. The Supreme and District courts are headquartered at the [Queen Elizabeth II Courts of Law](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II_Courts_of_Law,_Brisbane) in Brisbane's CBD.

The state's politics are traditionally regarded as being [conservative](/wiki/Conservatism_in_Australia) relative to other states. Historically, the lack of an [upper house](/wiki/Upper_house), the "[Bjelkemander](/wiki/Bjelkemander)" (a [malapportion](/wiki/Malapportionment) favouring rural electoral districts) has meant that Queensland had a long tradition of domination by strong-willed, [populist](/wiki/Populism) premiers, often accused of authoritarian tendencies, holding office for long periods. This tendency was exemplified by the government of the state's longest-serving Premier [Joh Bjelke-Petersen](/wiki/Joh_Bjelke-Petersen).

- [Queen Elizabeth II Courts of Law](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II_Courts_of_Law,_Brisbane), headquarters of the [Supreme Court of Queensland](/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Queensland) and [District Court of Queensland](/wiki/District_Court_of_Queensland)

### Local government

Main article: [Local government in Queensland](/wiki/Local_government_in_Queensland)

Local government is the mechanism by which [local government areas](/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_Queensland) can manage their own affairs to the extent permitted by the Local Government Act 2009. Queensland is divided into 77 local government areas, which are created by the state government under the legislation. Each local government area has a council responsible for providing a range of local services and utilities. Local councils derive their income from both rates and charges on resident ratepayers, and grants and subsidies from the state and federal governments.

## Culture

The Ekka (the [Royal Queensland Exhibition](/wiki/Ekka)) is held each August at the Brisbane Showgrounds.

### Music and performing arts

The [Queensland Cultural Centre](/wiki/Queensland_Cultural_Centre) in Brisbane is home to two of the state's major art galleries, the [Queensland Art Gallery](/wiki/Queensland_Art_Gallery) and the [Queensland Gallery of Modern Art](/wiki/Queensland_Gallery_of_Modern_Art).

Other cultural institutions based at the QCC are [Queensland Ballet](/wiki/Queensland_Ballet), [Opera Queensland](/wiki/Opera_Queensland), [Queensland Theatre Company](/wiki/Queensland_Theatre_Company), and [Queensland Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/Queensland_Symphony_Orchestra). Camerata – Queensland's Chamber Orchestra was founded as an ensemble of emerging artists in 1987 by music educator Elizabeth Morgan, evolving into a professional chamber orchestra in 2005. They perform without a conductor, and stage concert series in both Brisbane and [Toowoomba](/wiki/Toowoomba). The orchestra is based at the [Brisbane Powerhouse](/wiki/Brisbane_Powerhouse). The group performed on the 2026 *[Bluey](/wiki/Bluey_(TV_series))* TV series soundtrack album, *[Up Here](/wiki/Up_Here_(Bluey_soundtrack))* along with the Queensland Symphony Orchestra and many other musicians.

Many contemporary rock musicians are from Queensland, including the [Bee Gees](/wiki/Bee_Gees), [the Go-Betweens](/wiki/The_Go-Betweens), [the Veronicas](/wiki/The_Veronicas), [the Saints](/wiki/The_Saints_(Australian_band)), [Savage Garden](/wiki/Savage_Garden), and [Sheppard](/wiki/Sheppard_(band)), as well as writers such as [David Malouf](/wiki/David_Malouf), [Nick Earls](/wiki/Nick_Earls) and [Li Cunxin](/wiki/Li_Cunxin).

### Events

Major annual cultural events include the [Royal Queensland Exhibition](/wiki/Ekka) (known locally as the Ekka), an agricultural exhibition held each August at the [Brisbane Showgrounds](/wiki/Brisbane_Showgrounds) as well as the [Brisbane Festival](/wiki/Brisbane_Festival), which includes one of the nation's largest annual fireworks displays called "Riverfire", which is held each September.

## Sport

Main article: [Sport in Queensland](/wiki/Sport_in_Queensland)

[Cricket](/wiki/Cricket) game at [The Gabba](/wiki/The_Gabba), a 42,000-seat round stadium in [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane)

The state of Queensland is represented in all of Australia's national sporting competitions and it is also host to a number of domestic and international sporting events. The most popular winter and summer team sports are [rugby league](/wiki/Rugby_league) and [cricket](/wiki/Cricket), respectively.

In the [National Rugby League](/wiki/National_Rugby_League), the [Brisbane Broncos](/wiki/Brisbane_Broncos), [North Queensland Cowboys](/wiki/North_Queensland_Cowboys), [The Dolphins](/wiki/Dolphins_(NRL)) and [Gold Coast Titans](/wiki/Gold_Coast_Titans) are based in the state. Rugby league's annual [State of Origin series](/wiki/State_of_Origin_series) is a major event in the Queensland sporting calendar, with the [Queensland Maroons](/wiki/Queensland_rugby_league_team) representing the state.

In cricket, the [Queensland Bulls](/wiki/Queensland_Bulls) represent the state in the [Sheffield Shield](/wiki/Sheffield_Shield) and the [Ryobi One Day Cup](/wiki/Ryobi_One_Day_Cup), while the [Brisbane Heat](/wiki/Brisbane_Heat) compete in the [Big Bash League](/wiki/Big_Bash_League).

Queensland is also home to the [Brisbane Lions](/wiki/Brisbane_Lions) and the [Gold Coast Suns](/wiki/Gold_Coast_Suns) in the [Australian Football League](/wiki/Australian_Football_League) ([Australian rules football](/wiki/Australian_rules_football)), and the [Brisbane Roar FC](/wiki/Brisbane_Roar_FC) in the [A-League](/wiki/A-League) (soccer). In netball, the [Queensland Firebirds](/wiki/Queensland_Firebirds) went undefeated in the 2011 season as they went on to win the Grand Final. Other sports teams are the [Brisbane Bullets](/wiki/Brisbane_Bullets) and the [Cairns Taipans](/wiki/Cairns_Taipans), who compete in the [National Basketball League](/wiki/National_Basketball_League_(Australia)).

The state is represented by the [Queensland Reds](/wiki/Queensland_Reds) in the [Super Rugby](/wiki/Super_Rugby) (rugby union).

Swimming is also a popular sport in Queensland, with many Australian team members and international medalists hailing from the state.

Brisbane will host the [2032 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2032_Summer_Olympics), marking the third time Australia hosted the Olympic Games following [Melbourne 1956](/wiki/1956_Summer_Olympics) and [Sydney 2000](/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics). Major recurring sporting events hosted in Queensland include: the [Gold Coast 600](/wiki/Gold_Coast_600) (motorsport; since 1994), the [Gold Coast Marathon](/wiki/Gold_Coast_Marathon) (athletics; since 1979), the [NRL All Stars Game](/wiki/NRL_All_Stars_Game) (rugby league; since 2010), the [Townsville 400](/wiki/Townsville_400) (motorsport; since 2009), the [Quicksilver Pro and Roxy Pro](/wiki/World_championship_tour_(WCT)_surfing) (surfing) and [Australian PGA Championship](/wiki/Australian_PGA_Championship) (golf; since 2000).

## Symbols and emblems

Main article: [Symbols of Queensland](/wiki/Symbols_of_Queensland)

The official state emblems of Queensland are prescribed in the *Emblems of Queensland Act 2005*.

[Queen Victoria](/wiki/Queen_Victoria) granted the Queensland Coat of Arms to the Colony of Queensland in 1893, making it the oldest State Arms in Australia. It depicts Queensland's primary industries in the 19th century with a sheaf of wheat, the heads of a bull and a ram, and a column of gold rising from a heap of quartz. Two stalks of sugar cane which surround the state badge at the top, and below is Queensland's state motto, *Audax at Fidelis*, which means "Bold but Faithful". In 1977, [Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II) granted the supporting animals, the [brolga](/wiki/Brolga) and the [red deer](/wiki/Red_deer).

In November 2003 [maroon](/wiki/Maroon) was officially named Queensland's state colour, after many years of association with Queensland sporting teams. In 2025, Queensland Government branding changed to blue, with not yet an impact on the official state colour.

The [koala](/wiki/Koala) was officially named the animal or faunal, emblem of Queensland in 1971 after a newspaper poll showed strong public support. The Queensland Government introduced the poll due to a proposal by state tourism ministers for all states to adopt a faunal emblem. In January 1986, the brolga was announced as the official bird emblem of Queensland, after many years on the Coat of Arms.

The [Cooktown orchid](/wiki/Dendrobium_bigibbum) became known as Queensland's floral emblem in 1959, during celebrations to mark the state's centenary, and the [Barrier Reef Anemone Fish](/wiki/Amphiprion_akindynos) was officially named as Queensland's aquatic emblem in March 2005.

The [sapphire](/wiki/Sapphire) was named the official state gem for Queensland in August 1985.

## Infrastructure

See also: [Transportation in Australia](/wiki/Transportation_in_Australia), [Transport in Brisbane](/wiki/Transport_in_Brisbane), [TransLink (South East Queensland)](/wiki/TransLink_(South_East_Queensland)), and [Queensland Rail City network](/wiki/Queensland_Rail_City_network)

### Transport

Queensland is served by several [National Highways](/wiki/National_Highway_(Australia)) and, particularly in South East Queensland, a network of freeways such as the [M1](/wiki/M1_(Queensland)). The [Department of Transport & Main Roads](/wiki/Department_of_Transport_and_Main_Roads) oversees the development and operation of main roads and public transport, including taxis and local aviation.

[Principal rail services](/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Queensland) are provided by [Queensland Rail](/wiki/Queensland_Rail), predominantly between the major centres east of the Great Dividing Range. Freight rail services in Queensland have been provided mostly by [Aurizon](/wiki/Aurizon) and [Pacific National](/wiki/Pacific_National), with interstate intermodal services provided by Pacific National and [SCT Logistics](/wiki/SCT_Logistics). Major seaports include the [Port of Brisbane](/wiki/Port_of_Brisbane), Australia's third busiest by value of goods, as well as those at [Gladstone](/wiki/Gladstone,_Queensland), [Townsville](/wiki/Townsville), and [Bundaberg](/wiki/Bundaberg). There are large coal export facilities at [Hay Point](/wiki/Hay_Point,_Queensland), Gladstone, and [Abbot Point](/wiki/Abbot_Point). Major sugar export facilities are located at [Lucinda](/wiki/Lucinda,_Queensland) and [Mackay](/wiki/Mackay,_Queensland).

[Brisbane Airport](/wiki/Brisbane_Airport) is the main international and domestic gateway serving the state, and is the [third busiest in Australia](/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Australia). Other international airports include the [Gold Coast Airport](/wiki/Gold_Coast_Airport), [Cairns International Airport](/wiki/Cairns_International_Airport), and [Townsville Airport](/wiki/Townsville_Airport). Regional airports with scheduled domestic flights include [Toowoomba Wellcamp Airport](/wiki/Toowoomba_Wellcamp_Airport), [Great Barrier Reef Airport](/wiki/Great_Barrier_Reef_Airport), [Hervey Bay Airport](/wiki/Hervey_Bay_Airport), [Bundaberg Airport](/wiki/Bundaberg_Airport), [Mackay Airport](/wiki/Mackay_Airport), [Mount Isa Airport](/wiki/Mount_Isa_Airport), [Proserpine / Whitsunday Coast Airport](/wiki/Whitsunday_Coast_Airport), [Rockhampton Airport](/wiki/Rockhampton_Airport), and [Sunshine Coast Airport](/wiki/Sunshine_Coast_Airport).

[South East Queensland](/wiki/South_East_Queensland) has an integrated public transport system operated by [Translink](/wiki/TransLink_(South_East_Queensland)), which provides services [bus](/wiki/Bus_transport_in_Queensland), [rail](/wiki/Queensland_Rail_City_network), [light rail](/wiki/G:link) and [Brisbane's ferry services](/wiki/RiverCity_Ferries) through Queensland Rail and contracted operators. The region is divided into seven [Fare](/wiki/Fare) zones radiating outwards from the [Brisbane central business district](/wiki/Brisbane_central_business_district), which is the central hub for the system. The [Queensland Rail City network](/wiki/Queensland_Rail_City_network) consists of 152 train stations along 13 suburban rail lines and across the region, and predominantly within Brisbane's metropolitan area. There is also a large bus network including Brisbane's large dedicated [bus rapid transit](/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) network, the [Brisbane busway network](/wiki/Busways_in_Brisbane). Brisbane's popular [ferry services](/wiki/RiverCity_Ferries) include the CityCat, Cross River, and CityHopper services which have dedicated wharves along the [Brisbane River](/wiki/Brisbane_River). The [G:link](/wiki/G:link), Queensland's only [light rail](/wiki/Light_rail) network, operates on the Gold Coast.

The new Queensland [Cross River Rail](/wiki/Cross_River_Rail) is a metro network that is currently under development within [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane) and is part of infrastructure to prepare the city for the [2032 Olympic games](/wiki/2032_Olympic_Games).

- Passenger train at [Oxley railway station](/wiki/Oxley_railway_station) on the [Ipswich/Rosewood line](/wiki/Ipswich_and_Rosewood_railway_line) within the [Queensland Rail City network](/wiki/Queensland_Rail_City_network)
- Cargo ships at the [Port of Gladstone](/wiki/Port_of_Gladstone), Queensland's largest [commodity](/wiki/Commodity) [seaport](/wiki/Seaport)

### Other utilities

[Queensland Health](/wiki/Queensland_Health) operates and administers the state's [public health](/wiki/Public_health) system. There are sixteen regional Health and Hospital Services corresponding to geographical regions which are responsible for delivering public health services within their regions. Major public hospitals include the [Royal Brisbane and Women's Hospital](/wiki/Royal_Brisbane_and_Women%27s_Hospital), [Princess Alexandra Hospital](/wiki/Princess_Alexandra_Hospital,_Brisbane), the [Mater Hospital](/wiki/Mater_Group), the [Queen Elizabeth II Jubilee Hospital](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II_Jubilee_Hospital), and the [Queensland Children's Hospital](/wiki/Queensland_Children%27s_Hospital) in Brisbane, as well as the [Townsville University Hospital](/wiki/Townsville_University_Hospital), [Cairns Hospital](/wiki/Cairns_Hospital), [Gold Coast Hospital](/wiki/Gold_Coast_Hospital) and [Gold Coast University Hospital](/wiki/Gold_Coast_University_Hospital) in the regional cities. There are smaller public hospitals, as well as private hospitals, around the state.

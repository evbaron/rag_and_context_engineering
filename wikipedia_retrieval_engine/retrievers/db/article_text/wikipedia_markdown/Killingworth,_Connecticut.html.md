**Killingworth** is a [town](/wiki/New_England_town) in [Middlesex County](/wiki/Middlesex_County,_Connecticut), [Connecticut](/wiki/Connecticut), United States. The town is part of the [Lower Connecticut River Valley Planning Region](/wiki/Lower_Connecticut_River_Valley_Planning_Region,_Connecticut). The population was 6,174 at the [2020 United States census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census).

## History

Congregational Church along route 81

Killingworth was established from the area called Hammonasset, taken from the local [Native American](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) [tribe](/wiki/Tribe) of the same name. The area originally incorporated the area of the present town of [Clinton](/wiki/Clinton,_Connecticut), which was separated from Killingworth along ecclesiastical borders in 1838. Part of [New London County](/wiki/New_London_County,_Connecticut) prior to May 1785, Killingworth was then included in the newly formed [Middlesex County](/wiki/Middlesex_County,_Connecticut), where it remains today.

The New England town received its name from [Kenilworth](/wiki/Kenilworth), [England](/wiki/England), the previous home of one of the first settlers in [New England](/wiki/New_England), Edward Griswold. Kenilworth's name resembled "Killingworth" during the [colonial American period](/wiki/United_States#European_colonization), though over time the pronunciations and spellings of the names drifted toward the two distinct modern ones. A town and village in England called [Killingworth](/wiki/Killingworth) and [Killingworth Village](/wiki/Killingworth_Village), in the county of [Tyne and Wear](/wiki/Tyne_and_Wear), do not appear to have any connection with Killingworth, Connecticut.

In the late 17th century, Killingworth became the birthplace of what would eventually become [Yale University](/wiki/Yale_University). The Rev. [Abraham Pierson](/wiki/Abraham_Pierson), the college's first president, taught some of the first classes in his Killingworth home—which is actually in present-day [Clinton, Connecticut](/wiki/Clinton,_Connecticut). However, in 1701, the college's first official home was constructed in [Old Saybrook](/wiki/Old_Saybrook,_Connecticut) on the peninsula known as Saybrook Point donated by Yale's first Treasurer Nathanial Lynde. Eventually the school was moved to its present-day home in [New Haven](/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut).

## Geography

According to the [United States Census Bureau](/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the town has an area of 35.8 square miles (93 km2). Of this total, 35.3 square miles (91 km2) is dry land and 0.5 square miles (1.3 km2) – or 1.34% – is water-covered.

Killingworth also contains [Chatfield Hollow State Park](/wiki/Chatfield_Hollow_State_Park).

As of the [census](/wiki/Census) of July 1, 2015, there were 6,455 people, 2,513 households, and 1,765 families residing in the town. The population density was 184.7 inhabitants per square mile (71.3/km2). There were 2,598 housing units at an average density of 70.6 per square mile (24.9/km2). The racial makeup of the town was 96.4% [White](/wiki/White_(U.S._Census)), 0.7% [African American](/wiki/African_American_(U.S._Census)), 0.2% [Native American](/wiki/Native_American_(U.S._Census)), 1.1% [Asian](/wiki/Asian_(U.S._Census)), 0.1% Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander, 2.2% Hispanic or Latino, and 1.3% Two or More Races.

There were 2,513 households, with a 95.3% occupancy rate, out of which 23.9% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 73.8% were [married couples](/wiki/Marriage) living together, 4.3% had a female householder with no husband present, and 19.6% were non-families. 16.1% of all households were made up of individuals, and 8.0% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.74 and the average family size was 3.08.

In the town, the population was spread out, with 4% under the age of 5, 23.9% under the age of 18, 3.6% from 18 to 24, 29.4% from 25 to 44, 28.0% from 45 to 64, and 16% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 40 years. For every 100 females, there were 98.4 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 96.8 males.

The median income for a household in the town was $112,137. The [per capita income](/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the town was $48,537. None of the families and 1.7% of the population were living below the [poverty line](/wiki/Poverty_line), including no under eighteens and 1.4% of those over 64.

## Education

Students attending school in Killingworth are a part of Connecticut's Regional School District #17, which consists of [Haddam](/wiki/Haddam,_Connecticut) and its villages of Haddam Neck (located on the eastern bank of the [Connecticut River](/wiki/Connecticut_River)) and [Higganum](/wiki/Higganum,_Connecticut). The high school, Haddam-Killingworth High School (often abbreviated as simply "HK"), is located in Higganum. The middle-school, Haddam Killingworth Intermediate-Middle School, was built in Killingworth in 2006 and houses grades 4 through 8. The elementary schools, Burr Elementary School and Killingworth Elementary School are located in Higganum and Killingworth respectively. The school's sports teams are called the 'Cougars'.

## Infrastructure

The [Estuary Transit District](/wiki/Estuary_Transit_District) provides public transportation throughout Killingworth and the surrounding towns through its 9 Town Transit Service. Services include connections to the Old Saybrook Train Station, served by [Amtrak](/wiki/Amtrak) and [Shoreline East](/wiki/Shoreline_East) railroads.

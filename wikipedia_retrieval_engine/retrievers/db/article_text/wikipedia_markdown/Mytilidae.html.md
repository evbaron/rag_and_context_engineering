# Mytilidae

The **Mytilidae** are a [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) of small to large [marine](/wiki/Marine_life) and [brackish-water](/wiki/Brackish_water) [bivalve](/wiki/Bivalve) [molluscs](/wiki/Mollusc) in the [order](/wiki/Order_(biology)) [Mytilida](/wiki/Mytilida). One of the [genera](/wiki/Genera), *[Limnoperna](/wiki/Limnoperna_fortunei)*, inhabits [freshwater](/wiki/Fresh_water) environments. Mytilidae, which contains some 52 genera, is the only extant family within the order Mytilida.

Species in the family Mytilidae are found worldwide, but they are more abundant in colder seas, where they often form uninterrupted beds on rocky shores in the [intertidal zone](/wiki/Intertidal_zone) and the shallow subtidal. The subfamily Bathymodiolinae is found in [deep-sea](/wiki/Deep-sea) habitats.

Mytilids include the well-known, edible sea mussels.

A common feature of the shells of mussels is an asymmetrical shell, which has a thick, adherent [periostracum](/wiki/Periostracum). The animals attach themselves to a solid substrate using a [byssus](/wiki/Byssus).

A 2020 study of the phylogeny of the Mytilidae recovered two main [clades](/wiki/Clade) derived from an [epifaunal](/wiki/Epifaunal) ancestor, with subsequent lineages shifting to other lifestyles, and correlating [convergent evolution](/wiki/Convergent_evolution) of [siphon](/wiki/Siphon) traits.

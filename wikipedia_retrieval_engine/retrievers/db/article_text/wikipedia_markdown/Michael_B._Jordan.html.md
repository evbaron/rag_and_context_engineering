**Michael Bakari Jordan** ([/bɑːˈkɑːri/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*bah-KAR-ee*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born February 9, 1987) is an American actor, producer, and director. [His accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Michael_B._Jordan) include an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards), three [Actor Awards](/wiki/Actor_Awards), and a [Producers Guild Award](/wiki/Producers_Guild_of_America), in addition to nominations for a [British Academy Film Award](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) and two [Emmy Awards](/wiki/Emmy_Awards). Jordan was named one of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100) by *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* in 2020 and 2023, *[People](/wiki/People_(magazine))*'s [Sexiest Man Alive](/wiki/People_(magazine)#Sexiest_Man_Alive) in 2020, and in 2020 was ranked 15th on *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* list of the 25 greatest actors of the 21st century.

Jordan initially gained recognition in television, playing [Wallace](/wiki/Wallace_(The_Wire)) in the first season of the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) crime drama series *[The Wire](/wiki/The_Wire)* (2002). He starred in the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) soap opera *[All My Children](/wiki/All_My_Children)* (2003–2006) and the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) sports drama series *[Friday Night Lights](/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_(TV_series))* (2009–2011). He later starred in and produced the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) television film [*Fahrenheit 451*](/wiki/Fahrenheit_451_(2018_film)) (2018), for which he was nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Television Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Television_Movie).

Jordan's film breakthrough came as [Oscar Grant](/wiki/Killing_of_Oscar_Grant) in [Ryan Coogler](/wiki/Ryan_Coogler)'s biopic *[Fruitvale Station](/wiki/Fruitvale_Station)* (2013), his performance in which received critical praise. He earned further acclaim for his performances in Coogler's subsequent films, including *[Creed](/wiki/Creed_(film))* (2015), *[Black Panther](/wiki/Black_Panther_(film))* (2018), and *[Sinners](/wiki/Sinners_(2025_film))* (2025); the latter earned him the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor). Jordan reprised his role of [Donnie Creed](/wiki/Donnie_Creed) in *[Creed II](/wiki/Creed_II)* (2018) and *[Creed III](/wiki/Creed_III)* (2023), the latter of which also marked his directorial debut. His other films include *[Chronicle](/wiki/Chronicle_(film))* (2012), *[That Awkward Moment](/wiki/That_Awkward_Moment)* (2014), *[Fantastic Four](/wiki/Fantastic_Four_(2015_film))* (2015), and *[Just Mercy](/wiki/Just_Mercy)* (2019).

## Early life and education

Michael Bakari Jordan was born on February 9, 1987, in [Santa Ana, California](/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California), to Donna and Michael A. Jordan, a supervisor at the [John F. Kennedy International Airport](/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_International_Airport) who later started a catering business and was involved in [Pan-Africanism](/wiki/Pan-Africanism). He has an older sister and a younger brother. His family lived in Santa Ana for two years before moving to [Newark, New Jersey](/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), where Jordan grew up. He attended [Newark Arts High School](/wiki/Newark_Arts_High_School), where his mother worked as a teacher, and where he also played basketball.

## Career

### 1999–2012: Beginnings

Jordan worked as a child model for several companies and brands, including [Modell's Sporting Goods](/wiki/Modell%27s_Sporting_Goods) and [Toys "R" Us](/wiki/Toys_%22R%22_Us), before deciding to embark on a career as an actor. He launched his career as a professional actor in 1999, when he appeared briefly in single episodes of the television series *[Cosby](/wiki/Cosby_(TV_series))* and *[The Sopranos](/wiki/The_Sopranos)*. His first principal film role followed in 2001, when he was featured in *[Hardball](/wiki/Hardball_(film))*, which starred [Keanu Reeves](/wiki/Keanu_Reeves). In 2002, he gained more attention by playing the small but pivotal role of [Wallace](/wiki/Wallace_(The_Wire)) in the first season of HBO's *[The Wire](/wiki/The_Wire)*.

In March 2003, he joined the cast of *[All My Children](/wiki/All_My_Children)*, replacing [Chadwick Boseman](/wiki/Chadwick_Boseman), playing Reggie Montgomery, a troubled teenager, until June 2006, when Jordan was released from his contract. Jordan's other credits include guest-starring appearances on *[CSI: Crime Scene Investigation](/wiki/CSI:_Crime_Scene_Investigation)*, *[Without a Trace](/wiki/Without_a_Trace)*, and *[Cold Case](/wiki/Cold_Case)*. Thereafter, he had a lead role in the independent film *Blackout* and starred in *[The Assistants](/wiki/The_Assistants)* on [The-N](/wiki/The-N). In 2008, Jordan appeared in the music video "[Did You Wrong](/wiki/Did_You_Wrong)" by [rhythm and blues](/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues) artist [Pleasure P](/wiki/Pleasure_P).

Jordan in 2011

In 2009, Jordan began starring in the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) drama *[Friday Night Lights](/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_(TV_series))* as quarterback Vince Howard, and lived in an apartment in [Austin](/wiki/Austin,_Texas), where the show was filmed. He played the character for two seasons until the show ended in 2011. In 2009, he guest-starred on *[Burn Notice](/wiki/Burn_Notice)* in the episode "[Hot Spot](/wiki/Burn_Notice_(season_2))", playing a high-school football player who got into a fight and is being hunted by a local gangster. In 2010, he was considered one of the 55 faces of the future by [*Nylon*](/wiki/Nylon_(magazine)) in its "Young Hollywood" issue.

In 2010, he guest-starred in the *[Law & Order: Criminal Intent](/wiki/Law_%26_Order:_Criminal_Intent)* episode "Inhumane Society", as a boxer involved in a [Michael Vick](/wiki/Michael_Vick)-inspired [dog-fighting](/wiki/Dog_fighting_in_the_United_States) scandal. That year, he landed a recurring role on the NBC show *[Parenthood](/wiki/Parenthood_(2010_TV_series))* playing Alex (Haddie Braverman's love interest). This marked his second collaboration with [showrunner](/wiki/Showrunner) [Jason Katims](/wiki/Jason_Katims), who was in charge of *Friday Night Lights*. [BuddyTV](/wiki/BuddyTV) ranked him number 80 on its list of "TV's Sexiest Men of 2011". Jordan voiced Jace in the [Xbox 360](/wiki/Xbox_360) game *[Gears of War 3](/wiki/Gears_of_War_3)*. In 2012, Jordan appeared in the [George Lucas](/wiki/George_Lucas)-produced film *[Red Tails](/wiki/Red_Tails)*, and played lead character Steve Montgomery in *[Chronicle](/wiki/Chronicle_(film))*, a film about three teenaged boys who develop superhuman abilities. He also guest-starred in an episode of *[House](/wiki/House_(TV_series))*'s final season, playing a blind patient.

### 2013–2018: Rise to prominence

In 2013, Jordan starred as shooting victim [Oscar Grant](/wiki/Killing_of_Oscar_Grant) in *[Fruitvale Station](/wiki/Fruitvale_Station)*, directed by [Ryan Coogler](/wiki/Ryan_Coogler). His performance garnered critical acclaim, with [Todd McCarthy](/wiki/Todd_McCarthy) of *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* stating that Jordan reminded him of "a young [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington)". Following his role in *Fruitvale Station*, Jordan was named an "actor to watch" by *[People](/wiki/People_(magazine))* and *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))*. *Time* magazine named him, with Coogler, one of 30 people under 30 who are changing the world, and he was also named one of 2013's breakout stars by *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* and *[GQ](/wiki/GQ)*.

In 2015, he starred as [Johnny Storm, the Human Torch](/wiki/Human_Torch), in *[Fantastic Four](/wiki/Fantastic_Four_(2015_film))*. The film was universally panned by critics, holding a 9% approval rating on [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), and was a bust at the box office. Jordan rebounded with critical acclaim later that year when he starred as [Donnie Creed](/wiki/Donnie_Creed), the son of boxer [Apollo Creed](/wiki/Apollo_Creed), in the spinoff of the *[Rocky](/wiki/Rocky_(film_series))* film, *[Creed](/wiki/Creed_(film))*, his second collaboration with Coogler, which co-starred [Sylvester Stallone](/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone). Jordan prepared for his role as a boxer in *Creed* by undertaking one year of rigorous physical training and a stringent, low-fat diet. He did not have a body double during filming, and was "routinely bloodied, bruised, and dizzy" when fighting scenes were being filmed.

Jordan promoting *[Black Panther](/wiki/Black_Panther_(film))* at the 2017 [San Diego Comic-Con](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con)

In 2016, Jordan featured in the popular sports game *[NBA 2K17](/wiki/NBA_2K17)*, portraying Justice Young, a teammate of the player in the game's MyCareer mode. In October 2017, Jordan was announced as being cast in a supporting role as Mark Warren in the upcoming [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) superhero series *[Raising Dion](/wiki/Raising_Dion)*. In February 2018, Jordan starred as the villain [Erik Killmonger](/wiki/Erik_Killmonger) in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) film *[Black Panther](/wiki/Black_Panther_(film))*; this marked Jordan's third collaboration with Coogler. His performance in *Black Panther* received critical acclaim, with Dani Di Placido of *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* stating that Jordan "steals the show", while Jason Guerrasio of *[Business Insider](/wiki/Business_Insider)* wrote that the actor "plays a Killmonger fueled with hate and emptiness – we won't give away why – but he also delivers it with a swagger that's just a joy to watch ... the movie takes off more in story and viewing enjoyment whenever Jordan is on screen." In an interview with *The Hollywood Reporter,* Jordan shared how his preparation for the role of Killmonger left an impact on him, for which he sought out therapy.

Later in 2018, Jordan starred in *[Fahrenheit 451](/wiki/Fahrenheit_451_(2018_film))* with [Michael Shannon](/wiki/Michael_Shannon) and [Sofia Boutella](/wiki/Sofia_Boutella). The television film was distributed on [HBO](/wiki/HBO) by [HBO Films](/wiki/HBO_Films). That same year, Jordan reprised his role as boxer Adonis "Donnie" Creed in *[Creed II](/wiki/Creed_II)*, a sequel to *[Creed](/wiki/Creed_(film))* (2015) and the eighth installment in the [*Rocky* film series](/wiki/Rocky_(film_series)). *Creed II* was released in the United States by [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) on November 21, 2018. The film received generally positive reviews from critics, and it went on to debut to $35.3 million in its opening weekend (a five-day total of $55.8 million), marking the biggest debut ever for a live-action release over Thanksgiving.

### 2019–present: Established actor and awards success

He also voiced the character Julian Chase in [Rooster Teeth](/wiki/Rooster_Teeth)'s animated series *[Gen:Lock](/wiki/Gen:Lock)*, since January 2019, which he also co-produced through his production company, Outlier Society Productions. Jordan portrayed attorney [Bryan Stevenson](/wiki/Bryan_Stevenson) in a legal drama, *[Just Mercy](/wiki/Just_Mercy)*, which he also co-produced. The film, based on a real-life story, was released in December 2019 to positive reviews. Jordan starred in *[Without Remorse](/wiki/Without_Remorse_(film))*, based on the [book](/wiki/Without_Remorse) by [Tom Clancy](/wiki/Tom_Clancy), as [John Kelly](/wiki/John_Clark_(Ryanverse_character)), a former Navy SEAL and director of the elite counterterrorism unit Rainbow Six. Originally planned for release on September 18, 2020, it was released on April 30, 2021, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).

Jordan reprised his Marvel role as Erik "Killmonger" Stevens in two episodes of the first season of *[What If...?](/wiki/What_If...%3F_(TV_series))* (2021), and in *[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever](/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever)* (2022), and made a cameo appearance in *[Space Jam: A New Legacy](/wiki/Space_Jam:_A_New_Legacy)* (2021). He also starred in *[A Journal for Jordan](/wiki/A_Journal_for_Jordan)* (2021), directed by [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington), as a soldier who "kept a journal full of poignant life lessons for their newborn son, Jordan, while deployed overseas." Jordan made his [directorial debut](/wiki/List_of_directorial_debuts) with *[Creed III](/wiki/Creed_III)*, a sequel to *Creed II*, in addition to producing and reprising his starring role as boxer Adonis "Donnie" Creed. It was released on March 3, 2023. He next reteamed with Coogler in the period horror film *[Sinners](/wiki/Sinners_(2025_film))* (2025), playing twin brothers in a [dual role](/wiki/Dual_role) performance. He received the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) and the [Actor Award in the same category](/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), becoming the seventh [Black performer](/wiki/List_of_black_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) in history to win an Oscar in the lead acting category.

Jordan is slated to work with Coogler for the fifth time in *Wrong Answer*, a film based on the [Atlanta Public Schools cheating scandal](/wiki/Atlanta_Public_Schools_cheating_scandal). He is also set to direct, produce, and star in a second remake of *[The Thomas Crown Affair](/wiki/The_Thomas_Crown_Affair_(2027_film))*, as well as starring in the vampire film *Blood Brothers*. His production company Outlier Society signed a first-look deal with Amazon, and is also developing *[Val-Zod](/wiki/Val-Zod)*, an [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max) series featuring a black version of the [DC Comics](/wiki/DC_Comics) character [Superman](/wiki/Superman). As of March 2022, Jordan was to produce and star in the sequel of *[I Am Legend](/wiki/I_Am_Legend_(film))* with [Will Smith](/wiki/Will_Smith).

## Personal life

Jordan in 2018

Jordan has resided in Los Angeles since 2006. He grew up in a religious household and considers himself to be spiritual. As of 2018, he lived with his parents in a [Sherman Oaks](/wiki/Sherman_Oaks) home that he purchased.

Jordan is a fan of [anime](/wiki/Anime), citing *[Naruto: Shippuden](/wiki/Naruto:_Shippuden)* and the *[Dragon Ball](/wiki/Dragon_Ball)* franchise as his favorite series and *[Princess Mononoke](/wiki/Princess_Mononoke)* as his favorite film. Having grown up in [Newark, New Jersey](/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), he is a lifelong supporter of the [New York Giants](/wiki/New_York_Giants). He is also a supporter of [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League) club [Everton FC](/wiki/Everton_F.C.).

Michael B. Jordan considers [Tom Cruise](/wiki/Tom_Cruise) and [Will Smith](/wiki/Will_Smith) his career idols, citing their ability to build global careers and become movie stars. Jordan has also sought advice from Smith and Cruise and has received endorsements from Cruise, indicating a mentorship and respect among the three actors.

Jordan cites [Ryan Coogler](/wiki/Ryan_Coogler), [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg), [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese), [Alejandro G. Iñárritu](/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu), [Stanley Kubrick](/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick), [Alfonso Cuarón](/wiki/Alfonso_Cuar%C3%B3n), [George Lucas](/wiki/George_Lucas), and [Christopher Nolan](/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) as his influences in film making.

In November 2020, Jordan began dating model [Lori Harvey](/wiki/Lori_Harvey), adoptive daughter of television personality [Steve Harvey](/wiki/Steve_Harvey). In June 2022, the couple announced they had ended their relationship.

In December 2023, Jordan wrecked his [Ferrari 812 Superfast](/wiki/Ferrari_812_Superfast) when he crashed it into a parked [Kia](/wiki/Kia). He was uninjured and police investigated the incident for street racing. Another driver who was near the incident said they were "pacing" but insists they were not racing. Jordan was eventually not charged "due to lack of evidence".

### Sports ownership

In December 2022, Jordan was announced as part-owner of English soccer club [AFC Bournemouth](/wiki/AFC_Bournemouth). The club was taken over by the consortium group the Black Knights Football Club, led by fellow American businessman [Bill Foley](/wiki/Bill_Foley_(businessman)). Jordan led the minority ownership group with Kosmos founder Nullah Sarker.

He is also an investor in the [Alpine F1 Team](/wiki/Alpine_F1_Team) alongside fellow actors [Ryan Reynolds](/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and [Rob McElhenney](/wiki/Rob_McElhenney).

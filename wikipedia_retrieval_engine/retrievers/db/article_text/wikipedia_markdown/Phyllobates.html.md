# Phyllobates

***Phyllobates*** is a [genus](/wiki/Genus) of [poison dart frogs](/wiki/Poison_dart_frog) native to Central and South America. The genus contains seven known species, which are distributed from eastern [Nicaragua](/wiki/Nicaragua) to the Pacific coast of [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia).

*Phyllobates* contains the most poisonous species of frog, the [golden poison frog](/wiki/Golden_poison_frog) (*P. terribilis*). They are typical of the poison dart frogs, in that all species have bright [warning coloration](/wiki/Warning_coloration) (aposematism), and have varying degrees of [toxicity](/wiki/Toxicity). Only species of *Phyllobates* are used by natives of South American tribes as sources of poison for their hunting darts. The most toxic of the many poisonous [alkaloids](/wiki/Alkaloid) these frogs contain (in glands in their skin) is [batrachotoxin](/wiki/Batrachotoxin), alongside a wide variety of other toxic compounds. Some populations of *[Phyllobates lugubris](/wiki/Phyllobates_lugubris)* in Central America are not known to be toxic.

## Taxonomy

The genus name is derived from the Greek words φύλλον ("phyllon"; for "leaf") and βαίνω ("baino"; for "walk, step"). *Phyllobates* used to contain many of the species which are now within the genera *[Ranitomeya](/wiki/Ranitomeya)* and *[Andinobates](/wiki/Andinobates)*. However, it now just contains seven member species within the *Phyllobates bicolor*  and *Phyllobates lugubris* species groups. These are:

All these different species within the genus exhibit a diversity in color. Some examples are, *P. terribilis*, with color morphs of "mint", "yellow", and "orange". *P. vittatus*, another example, is always black as a ground color, but can show yellow stripes, orange stripes, red stripes,(stripes of all colors can be seen in two forms, narrow- and wide-banded) and turquoise, green, or blue legs, etc. The bicolor dart frog (*[Phyllobates bicolor](/wiki/Phyllobates_bicolor)*) can range from yellow to orange, from black legs to green legs, to almost a uniform color of any of the aforementioned color morphs. *P. aurotaenia* specimens are yellow-banded or orange. They are always smaller than *P. vittatus*, and beyond locality, this is the best way to differentiate between the two in the field or in the hobby.

## Source of toxin

The toxic alkaloid [batrachotoxin](/wiki/Batrachotoxin) is only present in frogs found in the wild; after extended captivity, they lose their toxin, indicating that they acquire it (or metabolize it) from their natural diet; this alkaloid has been found in [Papuan](/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea) beetle species in the family [Melyridae](/wiki/Melyridae), and other related genera can be found in Colombia and other areas where *Phyllobates* are found.

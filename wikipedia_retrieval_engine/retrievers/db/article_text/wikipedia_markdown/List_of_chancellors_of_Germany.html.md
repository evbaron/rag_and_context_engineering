# List of chancellors of Germany

- Top left: [Otto von Bismarck](/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck) was the first chancellor of Germany with the creation of the [North German Confederation](/wiki/North_German_Confederation) and later the [German Empire](/wiki/German_Empire).
- Top right: [Konrad Adenauer](/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer) was the first chancellor of the [Federal Republic of Germany](/wiki/West_Germany).
- Bottom left: [Helmut Kohl](/wiki/Helmut_Kohl) was chancellor during the period of [German reunification](/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990.
- Bottom right: [Angela Merkel](/wiki/Angela_Merkel) was the first female chancellor of Germany.

The [chancellor of Germany](/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany) is the political leader of [Germany](/wiki/Germany) and the head of the [federal government](/wiki/Federal_Government_of_Germany). The office holder is responsible for selecting all other members of the government and chairing [cabinet](/wiki/Cabinet_of_Germany) meetings.

The office was created in the [North German Confederation](/wiki/North_German_Confederation) in 1867, when [Otto von Bismarck](/wiki/Otto_von_Bismarck) became the first chancellor. With the [unification of Germany](/wiki/Unification_of_Germany) and establishment of the [German Empire](/wiki/German_Empire) in 1871, the Confederation evolved into a German nation-state and its leader became known as the chancellor of Germany. Originally, the chancellor was only responsible to the [emperor](/wiki/German_Emperor). This changed with the constitutional reform in 1918, when the Parliament was given the right to dismiss the chancellor. Under the 1919 [Weimar](/wiki/Weimar_Republic) [Constitution](/wiki/Weimar_Constitution) the chancellors were appointed by the directly elected [president](/wiki/President_of_Germany_(1919%E2%80%931945)), but were responsible to the [Reichstag](/wiki/Reichstag_(Weimar_Republic)) (parliament).

The constitution was set aside during the 1933–1945 [Nazi regime](/wiki/Nazi_Germany). During the [Allied occupation](/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany), no independent German government and no chancellor existed; and the office was not reconstituted in [East Germany](/wiki/East_Germany), thus the head of government of East Germany was chairman of the [Council of Ministers](/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_East_Germany). The [1949 Basic Law](/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) made the chancellor the most important office in [West Germany](/wiki/West_Germany), while diminishing the role of the [president](/wiki/President_of_Germany).

## North German Confederation (1867–1871)

The [North German Confederation](/wiki/North_German_Confederation) came into existence after the [German Confederation](/wiki/German_Confederation) was dissolved following the Prussian victory in the [Austro-Prussian War](/wiki/Austro-Prussian_War) of 1866. The chancellor was appointed by the *[Bundespräsidium](/wiki/Bundespr%C3%A4sidium),* a position that was held constitutionally by the [Prussian king](/wiki/Wilhelm_I).

## German Empire (1871–1918)

The [German Empire](/wiki/German_Empire) was born out of the North German Confederation as result of the [Franco-Prussian War](/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) (1870/71). The *Präsidium* (the Prussian king), which now had also the title *Emperor*, named the chancellor.

## Weimar Republic (1918–1933)

On 9 November 1918, Chancellor Max von Baden handed over his office to [Friedrich Ebert](/wiki/Friedrich_Ebert). Ebert continued to serve as head of government during the three months between the [end of the German Empire in November 1918](/wiki/German_Revolution_of_1918%E2%80%931919) and the first gathering of the National Assembly in February 1919 as Chairman of the [Council of the People's Deputies](/wiki/Council_of_the_People%27s_Deputies), until 29 December 1918 together with [USPD](/wiki/USPD) Leader [Hugo Haase](/wiki/Hugo_Haase).

The [Weimar Constitution](/wiki/Weimar_Constitution) of 1919 set the framework for the [Weimar Republic](/wiki/Weimar_Republic). The chancellors were officially installed by the president; in some cases the chancellor did not have a majority in parliament.

## Nazi Germany (1933–1945)

Soon after [Adolf Hitler was appointed as chancellor in 1933](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler%27s_rise_to_power), the German *Reichstag* (parliament) passed the [Enabling Act](/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1933) ([German](/wiki/German_language): *Ermächtigungsgesetz*), officially titled "Law for Removing the Distress of People and Reich" (German: *Gesetz zur Behebung der Not von Volk und Reich*), which effectively gave the chancellor the power of a [dictator](/wiki/Dictator). This event marked the end of the Weimar Republic and the beginning of [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany). Hitler thereupon destroyed all democratic systems and consolidated all power to himself. After the death of president [Paul von Hindenburg](/wiki/Paul_von_Hindenburg) in 1934, Hitler merged the offices of chancellor and president in his own person and called himself *[Führer](/wiki/F%C3%BChrer) und Reichskanzler*.

## Federal Republic of Germany (1949–*present*)

In 1949, two separate German states were established: the [Federal Republic of Germany](/wiki/West_Germany) (known as West Germany) and the [German Democratic Republic](/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) (known as East Germany). The list below gives the chancellors of West Germany; the [government](/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_East_Germany) of East Germany was headed by the [chairman of the Council of Ministers](/wiki/Leadership_of_East_Germany#Heads_of_government). In 1990, East Germany was dissolved as it merged with West Germany; Germany was [reunified](/wiki/German_reunification). It retained the name of the Federal Republic of Germany.

**Emily Jean "Emma" Stone** (born November 6, 1988) is an American actress and film producer. [Her accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Emma_Stone) include two [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards), two [British Academy Film Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards), two [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and a [Volpi Cup](/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actress). In 2017, she was the [world's highest-paid actress](/wiki/List_of_highest-paid_film_actors) and named by *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* magazine as one of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100).

As a child in Arizona, Stone started acting in local theater productions before relocating to Los Angeles to pursue a career in the film industry. As a teenager, she made her television debut in the reality show *[In Search of the New Partridge Family](/wiki/The_New_Partridge_Family)* (2004). After small television roles, she appeared in a string of successful comedy films, such as *[Superbad](/wiki/Superbad)* (2007), *[Zombieland](/wiki/Zombieland)* (2009), and *[Easy A](/wiki/Easy_A)* (2010), which became Stone's first leading role. Following this breakthrough, she starred in the romantic comedy *[Crazy, Stupid, Love](/wiki/Crazy,_Stupid,_Love)* (2011) and the period drama *[The Help](/wiki/The_Help_(film))* (2011), and gained wider recognition as [Gwen Stacy](/wiki/Gwen_Stacy_(The_Amazing_Spider-Man_film_series)) in [Marc Webb's *Spider-Man* films](/wiki/Spider-Man_in_film#Marc_Webb_duology) (2012–2014).

Stone cemented her [leading lady](/wiki/Leading_actor) status by taking on more eclectic and dramatic roles. She earned nominations for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress) for portraying a recovering drug addict in the surrealist dark comedy *[Birdman](/wiki/Birdman_(film))* (2014) and [Abigail Hill](/wiki/Abigail_Masham,_Baroness_Masham) in the absurdist period film *[The Favourite](/wiki/The_Favourite_(2018_film))* (2018); the latter marked her first of many collaborations with [Yorgos Lanthimos](/wiki/Yorgos_Lanthimos). She won two [Academy Awards for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress) for portraying an aspiring actress in the romantic musical *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)* (2016) and a resurrected suicide victim in Lanthimos's *[Poor Things](/wiki/Poor_Things_(film))* (2023); she was also nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) for the latter. Stone also earned recognition for portraying tennis player [Billie Jean King](/wiki/Billie_Jean_King) in *[Battle of the Sexes](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sexes_(2017_film))* (2017) and the [titular role](/wiki/Cruella_de_Vil) in *[Cruella](/wiki/Cruella_(film))* (2021). She has since collaborated twice more with Lanthimos, starring in the anthology film *[Kinds of Kindness](/wiki/Kinds_of_Kindness)* (2024) and the dark comedy *[Bugonia](/wiki/Bugonia_(film))* (2025); the latter earned her further nominations for the Academy Award for Best Actress and Best Picture, making her the first woman to be nominated as both a producer and an actress in each of two different films.

On [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre), Stone starred as [Sally Bowles](/wiki/Sally_Bowles) in a revival of the musical *[Cabaret](/wiki/Cabaret_(musical))* (2014–2015). On television, she has led the dark comedy miniseries *[Maniac](/wiki/Maniac_(miniseries))* (2018) and *[The Curse](/wiki/The_Curse_(American_TV_series))* (2023). She and her husband, [Dave McCary](/wiki/Dave_McCary), founded the production company [Fruit Tree](/wiki/Fruit_Tree_(company)) in 2020.

## Early life

Emily Jean Stone was born on November 6, 1988, in [Scottsdale, Arizona](/wiki/Scottsdale,_Arizona), a suburb of [Phoenix](/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona). Her father is Jeffrey Charles Stone, the founder and CEO of a general-contracting company, and her mother is Krista Jean Stone ([née](/wiki/N%C3%A9e) Yeager), a homemaker. She lived on the grounds of the [Camelback Inn](/wiki/Camelback_Inn) resort from ages 12 to 15. She has a younger brother, Spencer. Her paternal grandfather, Conrad Ostberg Sten, was from a Swedish family that [anglicized](/wiki/Anglicized) their surname to "Stone". She also has German, English, Scottish, and Irish ancestry. She was raised [Lutheran](/wiki/Lutheranism).

As an infant, Stone had [baby colic](/wiki/Baby_colic) and cried frequently. She consequently developed [nodules](/wiki/Vocal_cord_nodule) and calluses on her vocal cords while she was a child, which caused her to develop a low, husky voice. Stone has described herself as "loud" and "bossy" while growing up. She was educated at Sequoya Elementary School and attended [Cocopah Middle School](/wiki/Cocopah_Middle_School) for sixth grade. Stone did not like school, though once said that her controlling nature meant that "I made sure I got all [A's](/wiki/Grading_in_education)". She suffered [panic attacks](/wiki/Panic_attack) and [anxiety](/wiki/Generalized_anxiety_disorder) as a child, and said they caused a decline in her social skills. Stone underwent therapy but said it was her participation in local theater plays that helped cure the attacks, recalling:

[Valley Youth Theatre](/wiki/Valley_Youth_Theatre) in [Phoenix, Arizona](/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona), where Stone appeared in sixteen productions

> The first time I had a panic attack I was sitting in my friend's house, and I thought the house was burning down. I called my mom and she brought me home, and for the next three years it just would not stop. I would go to the nurse at lunch most days and just wring my hands. I would ask my mom to tell me exactly how the day was going to be, then ask again 30 seconds later. I just needed to know that no one was going to die and nothing was going to change.

Stone wanted to act since age four; she wanted a career in [sketch comedy](/wiki/Sketch_comedy) initially, but shifted her focus to musical theater, and took vocal lessons for several years. Her acting debut, at age 11, came in a stage production of *[The Wind in the Willows](/wiki/The_Wind_in_the_Willows)*, playing Otter. Stone was [homeschooled](/wiki/Homeschooled) for two years, during which time she appeared in 16 productions at Phoenix's [Valley Youth Theatre](/wiki/Valley_Youth_Theatre)—including *[The Princess and the Pea](/wiki/The_Princess_and_the_Pea)*, *[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](/wiki/Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland)*, and *[Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat](/wiki/Joseph_and_the_Amazing_Technicolor_Dreamcoat)*—and performed with the theater's [improvisational comedy](/wiki/Improvisational_theatre#Improvisational_comedy) troupe. Around this time, she traveled to Los Angeles and auditioned unsuccessfully for a role on [Nickelodeon](/wiki/Nickelodeon)'s *[All That](/wiki/All_That)*. Her parents later sent her for private acting lessons with a local acting coach, who had worked at the [William Morris Agency](/wiki/William_Morris_Agency) in the 1970s.

Stone attended [Xavier College Preparatory](/wiki/Xavier_College_Preparatory)—an all-girl [Catholic high school](/wiki/Catholic_high_school)—as a freshman, but dropped out after one semester to become an actress. She prepared a [PowerPoint](/wiki/PowerPoint) presentation for her parents titled "Project Hollywood" to convince them to let her move to California to pursue an acting career. In January 2004, she moved with her mother to an apartment in Los Angeles. She recalled, "I went up for every single show on the [Disney Channel](/wiki/Disney_Channel) and auditioned to play the daughter on every single sitcom", adding, "I ended up getting none." Between auditions for roles, she enrolled in online high-school classes and worked part-time at a dog-treat bakery.

## Career

### Career beginnings (2004–2009)

When Stone registered for the [Screen Actors Guild](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild) at age 16, the name "Emily Stone" was already taken, and she briefly went by "Riley Stone". She made her television debut as Laurie Partridge on the [VH1](/wiki/VH1) talent competition reality show *In Search of the New Partridge Family* (2004). The resulting show, retitled *[The New Partridge Family](/wiki/The_New_Partridge_Family)* (2004), remained an unsold pilot. After guest-starring in the television shows *[Medium](/wiki/Medium_(TV_series))* (2005) and *[Malcolm in the Middle](/wiki/Malcolm_in_the_Middle)* (2006), she decided to change her stage name to "Emma"—chosen in honor of [Emma Bunton](/wiki/Emma_Bunton) of the [Spice Girls](/wiki/Spice_Girls)—as she struggled to adapt to the name Riley. She next appeared in [Louis C.K.](/wiki/Louis_C.K.)'s [HBO](/wiki/HBO) series *[Lucky Louie](/wiki/Lucky_Louie)* (2006), and unsuccessfully auditioned to star as [Claire Bennet](/wiki/Claire_Bennet) in the NBC science fiction drama *[Heroes](/wiki/Heroes_(American_TV_series))* (2007); later called this her "rock bottom" experience. In April 2007, she played [Violet Trimble](/wiki/List_of_Drive_characters#Violet_Trimble) in the Fox action drama *[Drive](/wiki/Drive_(2007_TV_series))*, but the show was canceled after seven episodes.

Stone made her feature film debut in [Greg Mottola](/wiki/Greg_Mottola)'s comedy *[Superbad](/wiki/Superbad)* (2007), co-starring [Michael Cera](/wiki/Michael_Cera) and [Jonah Hill](/wiki/Jonah_Hill). The film tells the story of two high school students who go through a series of comic misadventures after they plan to buy alcohol for a party. To play Hill's romantic interest, she dyed her hair red. Stone has described the experience of acting in her first film as "amazing ... [but] very different than other experiences I've had since then". The film was a commercial success, and earned her the [Young Hollywood Award](/wiki/Young_Hollywood_Award) for Exciting New Face.

Stone in 2009

The next year, Stone starred in the comedy *[The Rocker](/wiki/The_Rocker_(film))* (2008) as Amelia Stone, the "straight face" [bass guitarist](/wiki/Bass_guitar) in a band; she learned to play the bass for the role. The actress, who has called herself "a big smiler and laugher", said she found it difficult to play a character whose personality was so different from her own. The film and her performance received negative reviews from critics and was a commercial failure. Her next release, the romantic comedy *[The House Bunny](/wiki/The_House_Bunny)*, performed better at the box office, becoming a moderate commercial success. The film saw her play the president of a sorority, and perform a [cover version](/wiki/Cover_version) of [the Waitresses](/wiki/The_Waitresses)' 1982 song "[I Know What Boys Like](/wiki/I_Know_What_Boys_Like)". Reviews were generally negative, but Stone was praised, with *[TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide)*'s Ken Fox writing that she "is well on her way to becoming a star".

Stone appeared in three films released in 2009. The first of these was opposite [Matthew McConaughey](/wiki/Matthew_McConaughey), [Jennifer Garner](/wiki/Jennifer_Garner) and [Michael Douglas](/wiki/Michael_Douglas) in [Mark Waters](/wiki/Mark_Waters)'s *[Ghosts of Girlfriends Past](/wiki/Ghosts_of_Girlfriends_Past)*. Loosely based on [Charles Dickens](/wiki/Charles_Dickens)'s 1843 [novella](/wiki/Novella) *[A Christmas Carol](/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol)*, the romantic comedy has her playing a ghost who haunts her former boyfriend. Critical reaction to the film was negative, but it was a modest commercial success. Her most financially profitable venture that year was [Ruben Fleischer](/wiki/Ruben_Fleischer)'s $102.3 million-grossing horror comedy film *[Zombieland](/wiki/Zombieland)*, in which she featured alongside [Jesse Eisenberg](/wiki/Jesse_Eisenberg), [Woody Harrelson](/wiki/Woody_Harrelson) and [Abigail Breslin](/wiki/Abigail_Breslin). In the film, she appeared as a con artist and survivor of a [zombie apocalypse](/wiki/Zombie_apocalypse), in a role which [Chris Hewitt](/wiki/Chris_Hewitt_(journalist)) of *[Empire](/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine))* magazine thought was "somewhat underwritten". In a more positive review, Tim Robey of *[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)* called "the hugely promising Stone […] a tough cookie who projects the aura of being wiser than her years". Stone's third release in 2009 was [Kieran and Michelle Mulroney's](/wiki/Kieran_Mulroney) *[Paper Man](/wiki/Paper_Man_(2009_film))*, a comedy-drama which disappointed critics.

### Rise to prominence (2010–2013)

Stone voiced an [Australian Shepherd](/wiki/Australian_Shepherd) in *[Marmaduke](/wiki/Marmaduke_(2010_film))* (2010), a comedy from director [Tom Dey](/wiki/Tom_Dey) based on [Brad Anderson](/wiki/Brad_Anderson_(cartoonist))'s [long-running comic strip of the same name](/wiki/Marmaduke). Her breakthrough came the same year with a starring role in *[Easy A](/wiki/Easy_A)*, a teen comedy directed by [Will Gluck](/wiki/Will_Gluck). Partially based on [Nathaniel Hawthorne](/wiki/Nathaniel_Hawthorne)'s 1850 novel *[The Scarlet Letter](/wiki/The_Scarlet_Letter)*, the film tells the story of Olive Penderghast (Stone), a high school student who becomes embroiled in a comic sex scandal after a false rumor circulates that she is sexually [promiscuous](/wiki/Promiscuous). Stone read the script before the project was optioned for production, and pursued it with her manager while production details were being finalized. She found the script "so different and unique from anything I'd read before", calling it "funny and sweet". When Stone discovered that the film had begun production, she met with Gluck, expressing her enthusiasm for the project. A few months later, the audition process started and she met again with Gluck, becoming one of the first actresses to audition. The film received positive critical reviews, and Stone's performance was considered its prime asset. Anna Smith of *[Time Out](/wiki/Time_Out_(magazine))* wrote, "Stone gives a terrific performance, her knowing drawl implying intellect and indifference with underlying warmth." The film was a commercial success, grossing $75 million against its $8 million budget. Stone was nominated for a [BAFTA Rising Star Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Rising_Star_Award) and a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress in a Musical or Comedy](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy), and won the [MTV Movie Award for Best Comedic Performance](/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award_for_Best_Comedic_Performance).

Stone at a screening of *[Easy A](/wiki/Easy_A)*, her breakthrough film, in 2010

In October 2010, Stone hosted an episode of NBC's sketch comedy *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*; her appearances included a sketch playing off her resemblance to [Lindsay Lohan](/wiki/Lindsay_Lohan). Stone called it "the greatest week of my life". She hosted again in 2011, appeared in an episode in 2014, and in [its 40th anniversary special](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_40th_Anniversary_Special) in 2015. A brief appearance in the sex comedy *[Friends with Benefits](/wiki/Friends_with_Benefits_(film))* (2011) reunited her with Gluck. She followed this with a supporting role in [Glenn Ficarra](/wiki/Glenn_Ficarra) and [John Requa](/wiki/John_Requa)'s romantic comedy *[Crazy, Stupid, Love](/wiki/Crazy,_Stupid,_Love)* (2011) alongside [Steve Carell](/wiki/Steve_Carell), [Ryan Gosling](/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) and [Julianne Moore](/wiki/Julianne_Moore). The film featured her as a law school graduate, and the love interest of Gosling's character. Despite finding "some inevitable collapses into convention" in the film, Drew McWeeny of [HitFix](/wiki/HitFix) wrote that Stone "ties the whole film together". At the [2012 Teen Choice Awards](/wiki/2012_Teen_Choice_Awards), she won the [Choice Movie Actress – Comedy](/wiki/Teen_Choice_Award_for_Choice_Movie_Actress_%E2%80%93_Comedy) award for her performance in the film. *Crazy, Stupid, Love* was a box office success, grossing $142.9 million worldwide against a production budget of $50 million.

Dismayed at being [typecast](/wiki/Typecast) as "the sarcastic interest of the guy", Stone co-starred with [Viola Davis](/wiki/Viola_Davis) in [Tate Taylor](/wiki/Tate_Taylor_(filmmaker))'s period drama *[The Help](/wiki/The_Help_(film))* (2011), a film she found challenging. The film is based on [Kathryn Stockett](/wiki/Kathryn_Stockett)'s [2009 novel of the same name](/wiki/The_Help) and is set in 1960s [Jackson, Mississippi](/wiki/Jackson,_Mississippi). She met with Taylor to express a desire to work on the film. The director said, "[Stone] was completely awkward and dorky, with her raspy voice, and she sat down and we got a little intoxicated and had a blast, and I just thought, 'God! God! This is Skeeter." She was cast as Eugenia "Skeeter" Phelan, an aspiring writer learning about the lives of the African-American maids. In preparation for the part, she learned to speak in a [Southern accent](/wiki/Southern_American_English) and educated herself on the [Civil Rights Movement](/wiki/Civil_Rights_Movement) through literature and film. With a worldwide gross of $216 million against a $25 million budget, *The Help* became Stone's highest-grossing film to that point. The film, and her performance, received positive reviews from critics. Writing for *Empire*, Anna Smith thought Stone was "well-meaning and hugely likable" despite finding flaws in the character. The film won Best Ensemble Cast from the [Women Film Critics Circle](/wiki/Women_Film_Critics_Circle) and the [Broadcast Film Critics Association](/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association).

Stone turned down a role in the action comedy *[21 Jump Street](/wiki/21_Jump_Street_(film))* after signing on to [Marc Webb](/wiki/Marc_Webb)'s 2012 film *[The Amazing Spider-Man](/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_(film))*, a [reboot](/wiki/Reboot_(fiction)) of [Sam Raimi](/wiki/Sam_Raimi)'s [*Spider-Man* series](/wiki/Spider-Man_(2002_film_series)). She portrayed [Gwen Stacy](/wiki/Gwen_Stacy_(The_Amazing_Spider-Man_film_series)), the love interest of the [titular superhero](/wiki/Peter_Parker_(The_Amazing_Spider-Man_film_series)) (played by [Andrew Garfield](/wiki/Andrew_Garfield)) based on the [Marvel Comics](/wiki/Marvel_Comics) [character of the same name](/wiki/Gwen_Stacy). Stone returned to her natural blonde hair color for the role, having dyed it red previously. She admitted to having never read the comics, and therefore felt responsible to educate herself about [Spider-Man](/wiki/Spider-Man): "My experience was with the Sam Raimi movies ... I always assumed that [Mary Jane](/wiki/Mary_Jane_Watson) was his first love", adding that she was only familiar with Stacy's character as portrayed by [Bryce Dallas Howard](/wiki/Bryce_Dallas_Howard) in *[Spider-Man 3](/wiki/Spider-Man_3)*. *The Amazing Spider-Man* was a commercial success and was the [seventh highest-grossing film of the year](/wiki/2012_in_film) with global revenues of $757.9 million. *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)*'s [Lisa Schwarzbaum](/wiki/Lisa_Schwarzbaum) found Stone to be "irresistible", and [Ian Freer](/wiki/Ian_Freer) of *Empire* was particularly impressed with Stone's and Garfield's performances. At the annual [People's Choice Awards](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards) ceremony, she was nominated for three awards, including Favorite Movie Actress. Later that year, Stone voiced a role in the crime-based video game *[Sleeping Dogs](/wiki/Sleeping_Dogs_(video_game))*, which earned her a [Spike Video Game Award](/wiki/Spike_Video_Game_Award).

Stone began 2013 with a voice role in the [DreamWorks Animation](/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation) film *[The Croods](/wiki/The_Croods)*, which was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Animated Feature](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Feature). This followed with an appearance in *[Movie 43](/wiki/Movie_43)*, an [anthology film](/wiki/Anthology_film) which consists of 16 short stories—she played the title role in the segment entitled "Veronica". She next starred alongside Ryan Gosling and [Sean Penn](/wiki/Sean_Penn) in Ruben Fleischer's *[Gangster Squad](/wiki/Gangster_Squad_(film))* (2013), a crime thriller set in Los Angeles during the 1940s. [A. O. Scott](/wiki/A._O._Scott) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* dismissed the film as "a hectic jumble of fedoras and zoot suits", but praised Stone's pairing with Gosling.

### Established actress (2014–2017)

In 2014, Stone reprised the role of Gwen Stacy in *[The Amazing Spider-Man 2](/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_2)*. She believed that her character did not depend on the film's protagonist, asserting: "She saves him more than he saves her. She's incredibly helpful to Spider-Man... He's the muscle, she's the brains." Her performance was well received by critics; an *Empire* reviewer commended her for standing out in the film, writing, "Stone is the [Heath Ledger](/wiki/Heath_Ledger) of this series, doing something unexpected with an easily dismissed supporting character." The role won her the Favorite Movie Actress award at the [2015 Kids' Choice Awards](/wiki/2015_Kids%27_Choice_Awards). Despite Gwen's onscreen death, Stone expressed interest in reprising the role in later films. However, due to *The Amazing Spider-Man 2*'s mixed reviews, low box office performance, and the [2014 Sony Pictures hack](/wiki/2014_Sony_Pictures_hack), all planned sequels and spinoffs were cancelled, and the franchise was rebooted once again after being integrated into the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe). Later that year, Stone took on a role in [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen)'s romantic comedy *[Magic in the Moonlight](/wiki/Magic_in_the_Moonlight)*, a modest commercial success. [A. O. Scott](/wiki/A._O._Scott) criticized her role and pairing with [Colin Firth](/wiki/Colin_Firth), describing it as "the kind of pedantic nonsense that is meant to signify superior intellect".

The black comedy *[Birdman](/wiki/Birdman_(film))*, directed by [Alejandro González Iñárritu](/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu), was Stone's final film release of 2014. Co-starring [Michael Keaton](/wiki/Michael_Keaton) and [Edward Norton](/wiki/Edward_Norton), it featured her as Sam Thomson, the [recovering-addict](/wiki/Addiction_recovery) daughter of actor [Riggan Thomson](/wiki/Riggan_Thomson) (Keaton), who becomes his assistant. Iñárritu created the character based on his experience with his daughter. *Birdman* was critically acclaimed, and was the most successful film at the [87th Academy Awards](/wiki/87th_Academy_Awards); it was nominated for nine awards, winning four, including Best Picture. [The Movie Network](/wiki/The_Movie_Network) deemed it one of Stone's best performances to date, and Robbie Collin of *The Daily Telegraph* found her to be "superb" and "tremendous" in her role, while also highlighting her monologue in the film which he believed to have been "delivered like a knitting needle to the gut". She received [Academy](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress), [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role), [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture), and [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role) nominations.

Stone at a screening of *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)* (2016); her performance in the film won her the [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress)

From November 2014 to February 2015, Stone starred in a revival of the [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) musical *[Cabaret](/wiki/Cabaret_(musical))* as [Sally Bowles](/wiki/Sally_Bowles), taking over the role from [Michelle Williams](/wiki/Michelle_Williams_(actress)). Deeming it "the most nerve-racking thing ever", Stone listened to a French radio station to mentally prepare herself for the role. [Marilyn Stasio](/wiki/Marilyn_Stasio) of *Variety* was critical of her singing abilities and found her performance to be "a bit narrow as an emotional platform, but a smart choice for her acting skills, the perfect fit for her sharp intelligence and kinetic energy." Stone's 2015 film, the romantic comedy *[Aloha](/wiki/Aloha_(2015_film))* by [Cameron Crowe](/wiki/Cameron_Crowe), was a critical and commercial failure. Her portrayal of an air force pilot, alongside [Bradley Cooper](/wiki/Bradley_Cooper), was panned by critics for its [controversial](/wiki/Aloha_(2015_film)#Whitewashing_controversy) [whitewashing](/wiki/Whitewashing_in_film) of the cast, as Stone's character was meant to be of Asian, Hawaiian, and Swedish descent. She later regretted taking part in the project, acknowledging whitewashing as a widespread problem in Hollywood. Despite the backlash, Stone was nominated for Choice Movie Actress – Comedy at the [2015 Teen Choice Awards](/wiki/2015_Teen_Choice_Awards). Also in 2015, Stone starred opposite [Joaquin Phoenix](/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix) as his character's love interest in Woody Allen's *[Irrational Man](/wiki/Irrational_Man_(film))*, which received mixed reviews. She also appeared in the music video for [Will Butler](/wiki/Will_Butler)'s single "Anna".

During her run on *Cabaret*, Stone met filmmaker [Damien Chazelle](/wiki/Damien_Chazelle), who, impressed with her performance, cast her in his musical comedy-drama *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)*. The project, which marked her third collaboration with Gosling, starred Stone as Mia Dolan, an aspiring actress living in Los Angeles. Stone borrowed several real-life experiences for her character, and in preparation, watched *[The Umbrellas of Cherbourg](/wiki/The_Umbrellas_of_Cherbourg)* and films of [Fred Astaire and Ginger Rogers](/wiki/Fred_Astaire_and_Ginger_Rogers). For the [film's soundtrack](/wiki/La_La_Land_(soundtrack)), she recorded six songs. *La La Land* served as the opening film at the [2016 Venice Film Festival](/wiki/2016_Venice_Film_Festival), where it generated critical acclaim and earned Stone the [Volpi Cup for Best Actress](/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actress). It emerged as a commercial success, with a worldwide gross of over $440 million against a production budget of $30 million. [Peter Bradshaw](/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) of *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* wrote that "Stone has never been better: superbly smart, witty, vulnerable, her huge doe eyes radiating intelligence even, or especially, when they are filling with tears." For her performance, Stone won the [Academy](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical), [SAG](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), and [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role) Award for Best Actress.

Stone's sole release of 2017 was *[Battle of the Sexes](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sexes_(2017_film))*, based on the 1973 [eponymous match](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sexes_(tennis)) between tennis players [Billie Jean King](/wiki/Billie_Jean_King) (Stone) and [Bobby Riggs](/wiki/Bobby_Riggs) (Steve Carell). In preparation, Stone met with King, watched old footage and interviews of her, worked with a dialect coach to speak in King's accent, and drank high-calorie protein shakes to gain 15 pounds (6.8 kg). The film premiered to positive reviews at the [2017 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2017_Toronto_International_Film_Festival), and certain critics considered Stone's performance to be the finest of her career. Benjamin Lee of *The Guardian* praised her for playing against type, and for being "strong" and "convincing" in the part. Even so, the film earned less than its $25 million budget. Stone received her fourth Golden Globe nomination for it, and attended the ceremony with King.

### Films with Yorgos Lanthimos and professional expansion (2018–present)

Stone collaborated with director [Yorgos Lanthimos](/wiki/Yorgos_Lanthimos) on several projects, such as *[The Favourite](/wiki/The_Favourite_(2018_film))* (2018) and *[Poor Things](/wiki/Poor_Things_(film))* (2023); she won her second [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress) for her performance in the latter.

In 2018, Stone and [Rachel Weisz](/wiki/Rachel_Weisz) played [Abigail Masham](/wiki/Abigail_Masham) and [Sarah Churchill](/wiki/Sarah_Churchill,_Duchess_of_Marlborough), respectively; two cousins fighting for the affection of [Queen Anne](/wiki/Anne,_Queen_of_Great_Britain) ([Olivia Colman](/wiki/Olivia_Colman)), in [Yorgos Lanthimos](/wiki/Yorgos_Lanthimos)'s historical comedy-drama *[The Favourite](/wiki/The_Favourite_(2018_film))*. She found it challenging to be an American among an all-British cast, and struggled with mastering her character's accent. The film premiered at the [75th Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/75th_Venice_International_Film_Festival) to critical acclaim. Michael Nordine of [IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire) praised Stone for taking on such a bold role following the success of *La La Land*, and termed the three lead actresses "a majestic triumvirate in a period piece that's as tragic as it is hilarious." Stone then executive-produced and starred in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) dark comedy miniseries *[Maniac](/wiki/Maniac_(miniseries))* (2018), directed by [Cary Joji Fukunaga](/wiki/Cary_Joji_Fukunaga). It featured Stone and Jonah Hill as two strangers whose lives are transformed due to a mysterious pharmaceutical trial. An admirer of Fukunaga's work, she agreed to the project without reading the script. Judy Berman of *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* magazine was impressed with Stone and Hill for their growth as actors since *Superbad* and noted the complexity in their performances. Stone received her fifth Golden Globe nomination and third Oscar nomination for *The Favourite*, and additionally earned SAG nominations for both *Maniac* and *The Favourite*. That same year, Stone appeared in [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney)'s music video for his song "[Who Cares](/wiki/Who_Cares_(Paul_McCartney_song))".

Stone reprised her role as Wichita in *[Zombieland: Double Tap](/wiki/Zombieland:_Double_Tap)* (2019), the sequel to 2009's *Zombieland*, which received mixed reviews and grossed $125 million worldwide. She narrated the Netflix documentary series *[The Mind, Explained](/wiki/The_Mind,_Explained)* (2019) and reprised the voice role of Eep in *[The Croods: A New Age](/wiki/The_Croods:_A_New_Age)* (2020), the sequel to 2013's *The Croods*. In 2021, Stone played [Cruella de Vil](/wiki/Cruella_de_Vil) (originated by [Glenn Close](/wiki/Glenn_Close) in the previous live-action adaptations) in [Craig Gillespie](/wiki/Craig_Gillespie)'s crime comedy *[Cruella](/wiki/Cruella_(film))*, a Disney live-action based on the 1961 animation *[One Hundred and One Dalmatians](/wiki/One_Hundred_and_One_Dalmatians)*. Starring opposite [Emma Thompson](/wiki/Emma_Thompson), Stone also served as an executive producer of the film alongside Close. The film was released in US theaters and on [Disney+ Premier Access](/wiki/Disney%2B_Premier_Access) to positive reviews and grossed $233 million worldwide against its $100 million budget. [Justin Chang](/wiki/Justin_Chang) of *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* wrote that despite the film's flawed screenplay, Stone was "wholly committed, glammed-to-the-nines"; Chang favorably compared it with her performance in *The Favourite*, adding that she "nailed every nuance as another lowly young woman turned ambitious schemer". For *Cruella*, Stone garnered another Golden Globe nomination.

In 2020, Stone and her husband, [Dave McCary](/wiki/Dave_McCary), launched the production company [Fruit Tree](/wiki/Fruit_Tree_(company)). Their first project was the independent film *[When You Finish Saving the World](/wiki/When_You_Finish_Saving_the_World_(film))*, which marked Jesse Eisenberg's feature directorial debut. The film premiered at the [2022 Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/2022_Sundance_Film_Festival), and had modest reviews. The company's next release was the comedy *[Problemista](/wiki/Problemista)*, directed by [Julio Torres](/wiki/Julio_Torres). It premiered at the 2023 [South by Southwest](/wiki/South_by_Southwest), and was initially scheduled to be released that year, but was delayed due to the [2023 SAG-AFTRA strike](/wiki/2023_SAG-AFTRA_strike). Both films were produced in collaboration with [A24](/wiki/A24).

Continuing her collaboration with Lanthimos, Stone starred in his short film *Bleat* (2022) and feature film *[Poor Things](/wiki/Poor_Things_(film))* (2023). The latter, a fantasy coming-of-age film, is based on the [novel of the same name](/wiki/Poor_Things) by [Alasdair Gray](/wiki/Alasdair_Gray). Stone also produced the film, in which she starred as Bella, a young Victorian woman who is crudely resurrected after her suicide. She found the experience of playing a character liberated of societal pressures to be "extremely freeing", and she performed nudity and several sex scenes in it. David Rooney of *The Hollywood Reporter* wrote that Stone "gorges on it in a fearless performance that traces an expansive arc most actors could only dream about", and particularly praised her ability to perform physical comedy. [Stephanie Zacharek](/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek) of *Time* termed her performance "wonderful—vital, exploratory, almost lunar in its perfect oddness". Stone next executive produced and starred in the [Showtime](/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network)) satirical comedy series *[The Curse](/wiki/The_Curse_(American_TV_series))*. She played Whitney, an influencer who hosts an [HGTV](/wiki/HGTV) show with her husband. Commenting on her achievements of the year, [BBC Culture](/wiki/BBC_Culture)'s [Caryn James](/wiki/Caryn_James) opined that "Stone has quickly moved past Hollywood stardom to claim serious artistic credentials". She received two more Golden Globe nominations for Best Actress for her performances in *Poor Things* and *The Curse*, winning for the former. Also for *Poor Things*, she won her second Academy Award and BAFTA for Best Actress in addition to a Best Picture nomination.

Stone at the [2024 New York Film Festival](/wiki/2024_New_York_Film_Festival) premiere of *[A Real Pain](/wiki/A_Real_Pain)*, where she wore a wig to disguise her shaved head for her role in *[Bugonia](/wiki/Bugonia_(film))*, wanting to keep the look a surprise.

The [2024 Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/2024_Sundance_Film_Festival) marked the release of two films produced by Stone for Fruit Tree — the horror film *[I Saw the TV Glow](/wiki/I_Saw_the_TV_Glow)* and Eisenberg's second directorial, *[A Real Pain](/wiki/A_Real_Pain)*. The latter, a comedy-drama about two mismatched [Jewish American](/wiki/Jewish_American) cousins, received critical acclaim and [several accolades](/wiki/List_of_accolades_received_by_A_Real_Pain). In her fourth collaboration with Lanthimos, Stone played three characters in his [absurdist](/wiki/Absurdist_fiction) anthology film *[Kinds of Kindness](/wiki/Kinds_of_Kindness)*, which premiered at the [2024 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2024_Cannes_Film_Festival). [Mashable](/wiki/Mashable)'s Robert Daniels considered it to be the weakest among their collaborations, and labeled her as "miscast". In 2025, Stone first appeared in [Ari Aster](/wiki/Ari_Aster)'s [contemporary Western](/wiki/Contemporary_Western) film *[Eddington](/wiki/Eddington_(film))*, which premiered at the [Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2025_Cannes_Film_Festival), and also starred [Joaquin Phoenix](/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix), [Pedro Pascal](/wiki/Pedro_Pascal), and [Austin Butler](/wiki/Austin_Butler). Ben Croll of *[TheWrap](/wiki/TheWrap)* felt that while Stone and Pascal were "no doubt game and ready to let loose", their characters "aren't given an awful lot to actually do – with both set up less as fully fledged characters than as images for [Phoenix] to pine-for or run against". Stone reunited with Lanthimos in *[Bugonia](/wiki/Bugonia_(film))*, a remake of the South Korean film *[Save the Green Planet!](/wiki/Save_the_Green_Planet!)*, playing a CEO kidnapped by two men who believe she is an alien. She shaved her head for her role in the film and wore wigs in public in the months that followed to disguise the look, wanting to keep it a surprise for the audience. Released on October 24, 2025, the film received generally positive reviews. She received [Actor](/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role), [Critics' Choice](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actress), [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy), and two [Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Awards) ([Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) and [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress)) nominations. She became the second-youngest person, and the youngest woman, in Oscar history to receive seven nominations, surpassing previous record holder [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep).

Stone and close friend [Jennifer Lawrence](/wiki/Jennifer_Lawrence) are attached to produce a movie about [The Muppets](/wiki/The_Muppets) character [Miss Piggy](/wiki/Miss_Piggy), which will be written by [Cole Escola](/wiki/Cole_Escola). She is expected to have a starring role opposite [Chris Pine](/wiki/Chris_Pine) in the [romantic comedy](/wiki/Romantic_comedy) *The Catch*, which will be directed by McCary. In addition, she will reprise her role as Cruella in a sequel to the 2021 film.

## Reception and acting style

Stone at the 2011 [San Diego Comic-Con](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con). Her hair, eyes, and husky voice have been described by the media as her trademarks.

Commenting on her performance in *The Help*, Kirk Honeycutt of *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* called her "one of our very best young actresses". *Time*'s Daniel D'Addario stated in 2015 that Stone "appears to have fairly limited options" and choosing roles in independent films by "less established directors would represent a substantive risk". Analyzing her on-screen persona, Jessica Kiang of *[IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire)* noted that Stone "usually [plays] the approachable, down-to-earth, girl-next-door type, [and] in person she demonstrates many of those qualities too, along with an absolute refusal to take herself too seriously." Biographer Karen Hollinger writes that early in her career, Stone was often labeled a "star ingénue", a perceived limitation she escaped despite not being a classically trained actress as she "crafted a brilliant career based on performative skills, careful choice of roles and distinctive personality". In a 2024 analysis of her career trajectory, *IndieWire* suggested that from her breakthrough roles to acclaimed performances in independent films, Stone is positioned as a potential new film star in Hollywood's evolving landscape.

In 2008, Stone topped *[Saturday Night Magazine](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Magazine_(U.S.))*'s Top 20 Rising Stars Under 30 and was included in a similar list compiled by [Moviefone](/wiki/Moviefone). [LoveFilm](/wiki/LoveFilm) placed her on their list of 2010 Top 20 Actresses Under 30, and her performance in *Easy A* was included in *Time*'s Top 10 Everything of 2010. She appeared in the 2013 [Celebrity 100](/wiki/Celebrity_100) list, a compilation of the 100 most powerful people in the world, as selected annually by *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)*. The magazine reported that she had earned $16 million from June 2012 to June 2013. That same year, she was ranked first in the magazine's Top 10 Best Value Stars. In 2015, *Forbes* published that she had become one of the world's highest-paid actresses with earnings of $6.5 million. The magazine ranked her the [world's highest-paid actress](/wiki/List_of_highest-paid_film_actors) two years later with annual earnings of $26 million. In 2017, she was included on *Time*'s annual list of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100).

Stone has been described as a style icon, with her hair, eyes, and husky voice listed as her trademark features. *[Vogue](/wiki/Vogue_(magazine))* credits the actress for her "sophisticated, perfectly put-together looks", writing that "her charisma, both on-screen and off-, has charmed many" and for her embrace of "Old Hollywood Glamour". Bee Shapiro of *The New York Times* called Stone a "likable ... spunky, talented, self-deprecating and slightly goofy" actress who is diverse in her fashion choices. In 2009, she was featured on *[FHM](/wiki/FHM)*'s [100 Sexiest Women in the World](/wiki/FHM%27s_100_Sexiest_Women_(UK)) and *[Maxim](/wiki/Maxim_(magazine))*'s Hot 100; the latter also placed her on the list on three other occasions—2010, 2011, and 2014. In 2011, she featured on [Victoria's Secret](/wiki/Victoria%27s_Secret)'s list of "What is Sexy?" as the Sexiest Actress. She was mentioned in other media outlet listings that year, including *[People](/wiki/People_(magazine))*'s 100 Most Beautiful Women, each of *FHM*'s and *[FHM Australia](/wiki/FHM_Australia)*'s 100 Sexiest Women in the World, and *[Men's Health](/wiki/Men%27s_Health)*'s 100 Hottest Women. She was ranked sixth on *Empire*'s list of the 100 Sexiest Movie Stars in 2013. Stone was named the best-dressed woman of 2012 by *Vogue* and was included on similar listings by *[Glamour](/wiki/Glamour_(magazine))* in 2013 and 2015, and *People* in 2014.

## Personal life

Stone moved from Los Angeles to [Greenwich Village](/wiki/Greenwich_Village), New York, in 2009. In 2016, she moved back to Los Angeles. Despite significant media attention, she refuses to publicly discuss her personal life. Concerned with living a normal life, Stone has said she dislikes receiving paparazzi attention outside her home. She has expressed her fondness for her profession, and has cited [Diane Keaton](/wiki/Diane_Keaton) as an acting influence, calling her "one of the most covered-up actresses of all time". Stone has a close relationship with her family. She says, "I am blessed with a great family and great people around me that would be able to kick me in the shins if I ever for one minute got lost up in the clouds. I've been really lucky in that sense."

Stone and [Andrew Garfield](/wiki/Andrew_Garfield) at the premiere of *[The Amazing Spider-Man 2](/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_2)* in 2014

Stone dated her *Paper Man* co-star [Kieran Culkin](/wiki/Kieran_Culkin) for two years. In 2011, she started dating her *Amazing Spider-Man* co-star Andrew Garfield for four years. Their relationship was speculated on in the media; the pair refused to speak publicly about it, though they made several appearances together. In 2014, in New York City, Stone and Garfield encouraged paparazzi to visit websites that spread awareness of causes such as [autism](/wiki/Autism). In 2015, they were reported to have broken up.

Stone met *Saturday Night Live* segment director Dave McCary on December 3, 2016, while hosting the show. They began dating the following year. On December 4, 2019, they announced their engagement. They married in a private ceremony in September 2020. In March 2021, Stone gave birth to their daughter, Louise Jean McCary. As of 2021, the family lives in [Austin, Texas](/wiki/Austin,_Texas). In 2022, Stone sold her house in [Malibu, California](/wiki/Malibu,_California), for $4.425 million, and in 2024, she sold her Los Angeles home for $4 million.

Stone has said she has [asthma](/wiki/Asthma), which she discovered after having difficulty breathing while filming *Easy A*. Her mother was diagnosed with [triple-negative breast cancer](/wiki/Triple-negative_breast_cancer) in 2008. In 2010 Stone and her mother celebrated the remission of the cancer by getting tattoos of birds' feet, designed by [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney), a reference to [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles) song "[Blackbird](/wiki/Blackbird_(Beatles_song))", which she and her mother love.

In a 2024 interview with [Terry Gross](/wiki/Terry_Gross) of [NPR](/wiki/NPR), Stone said of her Lutheran upbringing: "I very early on realized that religion didn't resonate for me...the sort of relationship I had to it was a more of a guilt and a sort of self-judgment rather than...focusing on the wonderful things that it can teach you...there's guilt around sexuality. There's guilt around your body. There's, you know, different stories about womanhood and what it means to be a woman and to be of service to a man or to not ever be jealous or not covet and, you know, all these things that are just sort of human and live in the shadow side. I went from religion into [Jungian therapy](/wiki/Jungian_therapist), obviously. So learning that being complex and complicated and female was OK, you know, that took me a while, I think, to unpack in myself." In 2025, on the subject of [God](/wiki/God_in_Christianity), she said, "If it's a He — [if He even exists](/wiki/Agnosticism) — I can't imagine He wouldn't be loving and generous, full of empathy and understanding. No offense to anyone who's religious, but that's what bothers me: that's the story we've been told for so long."

## Philanthropy

Stone appeared in a [Revlon](/wiki/Revlon) campaign that promoted breast cancer awareness. In 2011, she appeared in a collaborative video between *[Star Wars](/wiki/Star_Wars)* and [Stand Up to Cancer](/wiki/Stand_Up_to_Cancer) to raise funds for cancer research. From 2012 to 2014, she hosted the [Entertainment Industry Foundation](/wiki/Entertainment_Industry_Foundation)'s Revlon Run/Walk, which helps fight women's cancer. Following the 2016 [Pulse nightclub shooting](/wiki/Pulse_nightclub_shooting), she spoke out in support for the [LGBTQ community](/wiki/LGBTQ_community) and for gun law reforms, calling the politicians seeking reforms "heroes of equality." She is a supporter of [Planned Parenthood](/wiki/Planned_Parenthood), and attended the [2017 Academy Awards](/wiki/2017_Academy_Awards) wearing a Planned Parenthood pin on her dress.

Stone and three other celebrities were present at the 2012 [Nickelodeon HALO Awards](/wiki/Nickelodeon_HALO_Awards), a TV special that profiled four teenagers who are "Helping And Leading Others" (HALO). She attended the 2014 [Earth Hour](/wiki/Earth_Hour), a worldwide movement for the planet organized by the [World Wide Fund for Nature](/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature). In 2015, she was part of a fundraising event in support of the [Motion Picture & Television Fund](/wiki/Motion_Picture_%26_Television_Fund), which helps people in the film and television industries with limited or no resources. A [feminist](/wiki/Feminism), she collaborated with 300 women in Hollywood to set up the [Time's Up](/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization)) initiative to protect women from [sexual harassment](/wiki/Sexual_harassment) and discrimination in 2018. In 2025, Stone signed the [Film Workers for Palestine](/wiki/Film_Workers_for_Palestine) letter pledging that she would not to work with [Israeli](/wiki/Israel) film institutions implicated in "[genocide](/wiki/Gaza_genocide) and [apartheid](/wiki/Israeli_apartheid)" against [Palestinians](/wiki/Palestinians).

## Acting credits and awards

Main articles: [List of Emma Stone performances](/wiki/List_of_Emma_Stone_performances) and [List of awards and nominations received by Emma Stone](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Emma_Stone)

According to the review aggregator site Rotten Tomatoes and the box-office site [Box Office Mojo](/wiki/Box_Office_Mojo), Stone's most critically acclaimed and commercially successful films are *Superbad* (2007), *Zombieland* (2009), *Easy A* (2010), *Crazy, Stupid, Love* (2011), *The Help* (2011), *The Amazing Spider-Man* (2012), *The Amazing Spider-Man 2* (2014), *Birdman* (2014), *La La Land* (2016), *Battle of the Sexes* (2017), *The Favourite* (2018), *Cruella* (2021), and *Poor Things* (2023).

Stone greeting fans at the premiere of [*Bugonia*](/wiki/Bugonia_(film)), for which she earned her sixth and seventh [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations

Stone has been recognized by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) for the following:

- [87th Academy Awards](/wiki/87th_Academy_Awards): [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress), nomination, for *[Birdman](/wiki/Birdman_(film))* (2014)
- [89th Academy Awards](/wiki/89th_Academy_Awards): [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), win, for *[La La Land](/wiki/La_La_Land)* (2016)
- [91st Academy Awards](/wiki/91st_Academy_Awards): [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress), nomination, for *[The Favourite](/wiki/The_Favourite_(2018_film))* (2018)
- [96th Academy Awards](/wiki/96th_Academy_Awards): [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), win, for *[Poor Things](/wiki/Poor_Things_(film))* (2023)
- [96th Academy Awards](/wiki/96th_Academy_Awards): [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture), nomination, for *[Poor Things](/wiki/Poor_Things_(film))* (2023)
- [98th Academy Awards](/wiki/98th_Academy_Awards): [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), nomination, for *[Bugonia](/wiki/Bugonia_(film))* (2025)
- [98th Academy Awards](/wiki/98th_Academy_Awards): [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture), nomination, for *[Bugonia](/wiki/Bugonia_(film))* (2025)

She has also been nominated for five [British Academy Film Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards): BAFTA Rising Star Award, Best Supporting Actress for *Birdman* and *The Favourite*, and Best Actress in a Leading Role for *La La Land* and *Poor Things*, winning for the last two. Her other awards include two [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) for Best Actress in a Comedy or Musical for *La La Land* and *Poor Things*, the [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) for Outstanding Performance by a Female Actor in a Leading Role and the Volpi Cup for Best Actress at Venice Film Festival, both for *La La Land*.

# Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 2016

"[Love Yourself](/wiki/Love_Yourself)" by [Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber) came in at number one, spending a total of two nonconsecutive weeks at the top position of the *Billboard* Hot 100 during 2016. "[Sorry](/wiki/Sorry_(Justin_Bieber_song))", which also topped the weekly chart, is ranked at number two on the year-end list. Bieber is only the third artist to have two songs rank in the top-two of the Year-End Hot 100, after [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles) in 1964 and [Usher](/wiki/Usher_(musician)) in 2004.

The [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) is a chart that ranks the best-performing [singles](/wiki/Single_(music)) of the United States. Its data, published by *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* magazine and compiled by [Nielsen SoundScan](/wiki/Nielsen_SoundScan), is based collectively on each single's weekly [physical](/wiki/CD_single) and [digital](/wiki/Digital_distribution) sales, as well as [airplay](/wiki/Heavy_rotation) and [streaming](/wiki/Streaming_media). At the end of a year, *Billboard* will publish an annual list of the 100 most successful songs throughout that year on the Hot 100 chart based on the information. For 2016, the list was published on December 8, calculated with data from December 5, 2015 to November 26, 2016. The 2016 list was dominated by [Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber) and [Drake](/wiki/Drake_(musician)), who shared the top four spots, marking the first time two artists took up the top four spots since [2009](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2009) with [Lady Gaga](/wiki/Lady_Gaga) and [The Black Eyed Peas](/wiki/The_Black_Eyed_Peas).

The list is also notable for being one of five *Billboard* Year-End lists that featured 14 songs that appeared in the previous year (in this case [2015](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2015)'s) repeat onto to this list. The highest being [Adele](/wiki/Adele)'s "[Hello](/wiki/Hello_(Adele_song))", which with only three weeks being available to count in 2015's chart year, made it on to 2015's list at number 35 and repeat higher at number 7 in 2016's. Only four other year-end lists would repeat the same feat, those being [1997](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_1997), [2010](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2010), [2018](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2018) and [2022](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2022).

For the first time [in 10 years](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End_Hot_100_singles_of_2006), the year-end number-one single was #1 for five weeks or fewer.

## Year-end list

[Drake](/wiki/Drake_(musician)) has the most songs out of any artist on the list with eight songs, six of which are in the top 50. His highest-ranking song on the list "[One Dance](/wiki/One_Dance)" (at number 3) featuring [Wizkid](/wiki/Wizkid_(musician)) and [Kyla](/wiki/Kyla_(British_singer)) became his first number-one song on the *Billboard* Hot 100 as a lead artist.

American DJ duo [The Chainsmokers](/wiki/The_Chainsmokers) has three songs in the top 30. One of them is "[Closer](/wiki/Closer_(The_Chainsmokers_song))" (number 10), which not only became their first number-one song but also the longest-running number-one song of 2016.

[Rihanna](/wiki/Rihanna) has four songs in the top 30 including "[Work](/wiki/Work_(Rihanna_song))" at number 4, "[Needed Me](/wiki/Needed_Me)" at number 13, "[This Is What You Came For](/wiki/This_Is_What_You_Came_For)" at number 17 and "[Too Good](/wiki/Too_Good)" at number 29.

[Twenty One Pilots](/wiki/Twenty_One_Pilots) has three songs in the top 25 with "[Stressed Out](/wiki/Stressed_Out)" at number 5, "[Ride](/wiki/Ride_(Twenty_One_Pilots_song))" at number 20, and "[Heathens](/wiki/Heathens_(Twenty_One_Pilots_song))" at number 21.

[Adele](/wiki/Adele) had three of the Hot 100 singles, topped by "[Hello](/wiki/Hello_(Adele_song))" at number 7.

[Sia](/wiki/Sia) became the first woman over 40 to top the Hot 100 with her first number-one single "[Cheap Thrills](/wiki/Cheap_Thrills_(song))" (number 11) since [Madonna](/wiki/Madonna) topped the chart with "[Music](/wiki/Music_(Madonna_song))" in 2000.

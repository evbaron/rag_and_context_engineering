**George Alfred Christian Knudson**, [CM](/wiki/Order_of_Canada) (June 28, 1937 – January 24, 1989) was a Canadian [professional golfer](/wiki/Professional_golfer), who along with [Mike Weir](/wiki/Mike_Weir) holds the record for the Canadian with the most wins on the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour), with eight career victories.

## Early life and amateur career

Knudson was born and raised in [Winnipeg](/wiki/Winnipeg), [Manitoba](/wiki/Manitoba). He learned to play golf at the [St. Charles Country Club](/wiki/St._Charles_Country_Club). He won the 1954 and 1955 Manitoba Junior Championships. In addition, he won the 1955 Canadian Junior Championship.

## Professional career

In 1958, Knudson moved to [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto) and worked at the [Oakdale Golf & Country Club](/wiki/Oakdale_Golf_%26_Country_Club) where he received instruction and encouragement from the Club, to improve his game; the club has named one of its three nines after him. He was then able to secure some financial backing to try the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour). He won the Manitoba Open in 1958, 1959, and 1960, and the [Ontario Open](/wiki/Ontario_Open) in 1960, 1961, 1971, 1976 and 1978.

Between [1961](/wiki/1961_PGA_Tour) and [1972](/wiki/1972_PGA_Tour), he won eight tournaments on the PGA Tour. He won the [Canadian PGA Championship](/wiki/Canadian_PGA_Championship) five times, and won the [World Cup](/wiki/World_Cup_(men%27s_golf)) with [Al Balding](/wiki/Al_Balding) in 1968.

Knudson's last official PGA TOUR victory was the Kaiser Invitational in October 1972. However, Knudson nearly won again the next week at the [Sahara Invitational](/wiki/Sahara_Invitational). He carried the lead going into the final round at 15-under par after shooting 65-70-66, but a final round 76 dropped him into a T-7th at 11-under.

In seven Masters appearances, Knudson posted three top-10s, including 10th in his [1965](/wiki/1965_Masters_Tournament) debut and sixth a [year later](/wiki/1966_Masters_Tournament). Knudson's best finish in a [major championship](/wiki/Men%27s_major_golf_championships) was a tie for second in the [1969 Masters Tournament](/wiki/1969_Masters_Tournament), one shot behind champion [George Archer](/wiki/George_Archer). Knudson's birdie putt on the 72nd hole to tie Archer came up 3 inches short.

Knudson left tournament golf in the late 1970s, and started teaching golf, with success, at a facility in the Toronto area. His teaching methods have since been adopted by the Canadian PGA.

## Death

Knudson had long been a heavy smoker and was diagnosed with [lung cancer](/wiki/Lung_cancer) in 1987. He recovered well enough to compete at the 1988 [Liberty Mutual Legends of Golf](/wiki/Liberty_Mutual_Legends_of_Golf) senior tournament. Shortly thereafter, it was discovered the cancer had spread to his brain. George Knudson died in January 1989 at age 51 and was buried at [Mount Pleasant Cemetery](/wiki/Mount_Pleasant_Cemetery,_Toronto) in [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto).

## Awards and honors

- In 1969, Knudson was inducted into [Canada's Sports Hall of Fame](/wiki/Canada%27s_Sports_Hall_of_Fame).
- In 1988, Knudson was inducted into both [Royal Canadian Golf Association](/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Golf_Association) [Hall of Fame](/wiki/Canadian_Golf_Hall_of_Fame) and the [Manitoba Sports Hall of Fame and Museum](/wiki/Manitoba_Sports_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum)
- In 1988, he was also made a member of the [Order of Canada](/wiki/Order_of_Canada).
- In 1996, he was inducted into the [Ontario Sports Hall of Fame](/wiki/Ontario_Sports_Hall_of_Fame).

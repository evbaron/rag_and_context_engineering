**Vassar College** ([/ˈvæsər/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*VASS-ər*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [private](/wiki/Private_college) [liberal arts college](/wiki/Liberal_arts_colleges_in_the_United_States) in the town of [Poughkeepsie, New York](/wiki/Poughkeepsie_(town),_New_York), United States. Founded in 1861 by [Matthew Vassar](/wiki/Matthew_Vassar), it was the second degree-granting institution of higher education for women in the United States. The college became [coeducational](/wiki/Coeducational) in 1969. The college offers [BA](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degrees in more than fifty majors. Vassar College's varsity sports teams, known as the Brewers, play in the [NCAA Division III](/wiki/NCAA_Division_III) as members of the [Liberty League](/wiki/Liberty_League). As of 2023, there are close to 2,500 students.

The college is one of the historic [Seven Sisters](/wiki/Seven_Sisters_(colleges)). The Vassar campus comprises over 1,000 acres (400 ha) and more than 100 buildings. A designated [arboretum](/wiki/Arboretum), the campus features more than 200 species of trees, a native plant preserve, and a 530-acre (210 ha) [ecological preserve](/wiki/Ecological_preserve).

## History

An 1861 oil portrait of [Matthew Vassar](/wiki/Matthew_Vassar) by [Charles Loring Elliott](/wiki/Charles_Loring_Elliott)

Vassar was founded as a [women's school](/wiki/Women%27s_college) under the name "Vassar Female College" in 1861. Its first president was Milo P. Jewett, who had previously been first president of another women's school, [Judson College](/wiki/Judson_College_(Alabama)); he led a staff of ten professors and twenty-one instructors. After one year, its founder, [Matthew Vassar](/wiki/Matthew_Vassar), had the word *Female* removed from the name, prompting some local residents of [Poughkeepsie, New York](/wiki/Poughkeepsie_(town),_New_York), to quip that its founder believed it might one day admit male students. The college became [coeducational](/wiki/Coeducational) in 1969.

Vassar was the second of the [Seven Sisters](/wiki/Seven_Sisters_(colleges)) colleges, higher education schools that were strictly for women, and historically sister institutions to the all-male [Ivy League](/wiki/Ivy_League) colleges. It was chartered by its namesake, brewer [Matthew Vassar](/wiki/Matthew_Vassar), in 1861 in the [Hudson Valley](/wiki/Hudson_Valley), about 70 miles (110 km) north of [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City). The first person appointed to the Vassar faculty was [astronomer](/wiki/Astronomer) [Maria Mitchell](/wiki/Maria_Mitchell), in 1865.

The Main Building, built in 1861 by architect [James Renwick Jr.](/wiki/James_Renwick_Jr.), had the most interior space of any building in the United States, until the [United States Capitol](/wiki/United_States_Capitol) was completed in 1868.

Vassar adopted coeducation in 1969. Immediately following [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), Vassar accepted a small number of male students on the [G.I. Bill](/wiki/G.I._Bill). The formal decision to become co-ed came after its trustees declined an offer to merge with [Yale University](/wiki/Yale_University), its sibling institution, in the wave of mergers between the historically all-male colleges of the Ivy League and their Seven Sisters counterparts.

In its early years, Vassar was associated with the social elite of the Protestant establishment. [E. Digby Baltzell](/wiki/E._Digby_Baltzell) writes that "upper-class [WASP](/wiki/White_Anglo-Saxon_Protestant) families educated their children at colleges such as [Harvard](/wiki/Harvard_College), [Princeton](/wiki/Princeton_University), [Yale](/wiki/Yale_College), and Vassar." A select and elite few of Vassar's students were allowed entry into the school's secret society Delta Sigma Rho, founded in 1922. Before becoming President of the United States, [Franklin Delano Roosevelt](/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt) was a Trustee.

Approximately 2,450 students attend Vassar, and 98% live on campus. About 60% come from public high schools, and 40% come from [private schools](/wiki/Private_schools) (both independent and religious). Vassar is currently 56% women and 44% men, at national average for national liberal arts colleges. Students are taught by more than 336 faculty members, virtually all holding the doctorate degree or its equivalent. The student-faculty ratio is 8:1, average class size, 17.

In recent freshman classes, students of color constituted 32–38% of matriculants. International students from over 60 countries make up 8–10% of the student body. In May 2007, Vassar returned to a [need-blind](/wiki/Need-blind) admissions policy without regard to a student's financial status.

Vassar president [Catharine Bond Hill](/wiki/Catharine_Bond_Hill) departed in 2016. She was succeeded by [Elizabeth Howe Bradley](/wiki/Elizabeth_H._Bradley) in 2017.

The college was listed as a census-designated place (Vassar College CDP) in 2019.

## Campus

The campus is in [Poughkeepsie Town](/wiki/Poughkeepsie_(town),_New_York), adjacent to [Poughkeepsie City](/wiki/Poughkeepsie,_New_York). The area around the campus appeared as a [census-designated place](/wiki/Census-designated_place) (CDP) in the [2020 U.S. census](/wiki/2020_U.S._census), with a population of 2,472.

### Architecture

Main Hall (2026)

The [Vassar College Observatory](/wiki/Vassar_College_Observatory) is one of two National Historic Landmarks on the college's campus, along with Main Building.

Vassar's campus, also an [arboretum](/wiki/Arboretum), is 1,000 acres (400 ha) and has more than 100 buildings, ranging in style from [Collegiate Gothic](/wiki/Collegiate_Gothic_in_North_America) to [International](/wiki/International_Style_(architecture)), with several buildings of architectural interest. At the center of campus stands [Main Building](/wiki/Main_Building_(Vassar_College)), one of the best examples of [Second Empire architecture](/wiki/Second_Empire_architecture) in the United States. When it was opened, Main Building was the largest building in the U.S. in terms of floor space. It formerly housed the entire college, including classrooms, dormitories, museum, library, and dining halls. The building was designed by Smithsonian architect [James Renwick Jr.](/wiki/James_Renwick_Jr.) and was completed in 1865. It was preceded on campus by the original [observatory](/wiki/Vassar_College_Observatory). Both buildings are [National Historic Landmarks](/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark). [Rombout House](/wiki/Rombout_House) was purchased by the college in 1915 and added to the [National Register of Historic Places](/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 1982.

[Eero Saarinen](/wiki/Eero_Saarinen) made designs for several Vassar dormitories, but only one, the Emma Hartman Noyes House, was completed in 1958. Built for roughly 160 students, it was the first part of a circular construction that was to be continued in "Noyes II." The starkly modernist building's high cost and structural difficulties with the windows, however, led administrators to leave it at one. The dorm's common area is famous for its futuristic design; readings and concerts are held there regularly. The Noyes building was also the home of an all-female football team, the Noyes Nymphs, who competed against Ivy League teams in the 1960s and 1970s.

### Libraries

Further information: [Thompson Memorial Library](/wiki/Thompson_Memorial_Library)

Vassar's Thompson Library

Vassar is home to one of the largest undergraduate library collections in the U.S. The library collection today – which actually encompasses eight libraries at Vassar – contains about 1 million volumes and 7,500 serial, periodical and newspaper titles, as well as an extensive collection of microfilm and microfiche, with special collections of [Ellen Swallow Richards](/wiki/Ellen_Swallow_Richards), [Albert Einstein](/wiki/Albert_Einstein), [Mary McCarthy](/wiki/Mary_McCarthy_(author)), and [Elizabeth Bishop](/wiki/Elizabeth_Bishop). Vassar has been a Federal Depository library for selected U.S. Government documents since 1943 and currently receives approximately 25% of the titles available through the Federal Depository Program.

The interior and exterior of the Van Ingen Art Library was renovated from June 2008 – May 2009 in an effort to restore its original design and appearance. This was the library's first major renovation since its construction in 1937.

### Housing

The majority of students in their first three years live on campus in the nine [residence halls](/wiki/Residence_halls). Main House occupies the upper floors of Main Building, constructed at the college's founding. To accommodate a growing student body, [Strong House](/wiki/Strong_House_(Vassar_College)) was constructed in 1893. Strong was made all-women's housing after the college went coeducational in 1969; the house now also accepts [nonbinary](/wiki/Non-binary_gender) and other [gender-nonconforming](/wiki/Gender_nonconformity) students. Similar [Elizabethan](/wiki/Elizabethan_architecture)-style houses—[Raymond](/wiki/Raymond_House_(Vassar_College)), [Lathrop](/wiki/Lathrop_House_(Vassar_College)), and [Davison](/wiki/Davison_House)—were built from 1897 to 1902 to form a residential [quadrangle](/wiki/Quadrangle_(architecture)). A nine-story Tudor-style dormitory, [Jewett House](/wiki/Jewett_House), was constructed in 1907 on the north end of the quadrangle. The second largest dormitory, Josselyn House, was built in 1912 to Jewett's east. [Cushing House](/wiki/Cushing_House) was constructed outside of the quadrangle in 1927. In a departure from the architecture of the other dormitories, Noyes House was built in 1958 in a distinct modernist style, curved around a large [green](/wiki/Village_green).

Most fourth-year students live in groups of four or five in on-campus apartments. Vassar has three apartment complexes: the Terrace Apartments, the Town Houses, and the South Commons. A [cooperative](/wiki/Cooperative) house, Ferry House, opened in 1951 and houses 20 students. Vassar guarantees housing to all full-time students.

As part of a master plan to renovate all the dormitories, Jewett, Davison, and Josselyn were renovated from 2002 to 2011. The college opted for minor improvements to the rest of the dorms due to cost.

Vassar maintains housing for faculty; the current complex opened in 2023. The previous faculty housing facility, [Williams House](/wiki/Williams_House_(Vassar_College)), was to be demolished after 2020. School-age dependents living on the Vassar faculty complex, as well as other areas in the Vassar College CDP, are within the [Arlington Central School District](/wiki/Arlington_Central_School_District), which operates [Arlington High School](/wiki/Arlington_High_School_(New_York)).

Intangibles will and should play a role in these decisions, but that doesn't mean we shouldn't also look at the tangibles".

### Post-graduation outcomes

Over half of Vassar graduates pursue advanced study within five years of graduation, including one-fifth immediately post-graduation. Of the seniors who applied to medical school in 2017, 76% were accepted; to law school, 96% were accepted.

## Student life

### Traditions

Vassar students celebrating Founder's Day in 1935

Founder's Day is an annual campus festival at Vassar College that usually takes place in late April or early May. It started as a surprise birthday party for college founder [Matthew Vassar](/wiki/Matthew_Vassar)'s seventy-fourth birthday and evolved into an annual celebration. Originally, Founder's Day was a spontaneous event consisting of lectures, but was soon replaced with plays, pageants, and more recreational activities. Circus and fair activities followed, with the eventual addition of the modern day music events over the course of two days. More recently themes have been added, including *[Alice in Wonderland](/wiki/Alice_in_Wonderland)*, Dinosaurs, Vintage 1800s Vassar, [Nickelodeon](/wiki/Nickelodeon), [Nintendo](/wiki/Nintendo), and *[Candyland](/wiki/Candyland)*. Recent artists at Founder's Day have included [The Walkmen](/wiki/The_Walkmen), [Edan](/wiki/Edan_(musician)), [DJ /rupture](/wiki/DJ_/rupture), [Odd Nosdam](/wiki/Odd_Nosdam), [Jel](/wiki/Jel_(rapper)), [Toro y Moi](/wiki/Toro_y_Moi), and [Odesza](/wiki/Odesza).

### Extracurricular organizations

The Vastards performing in 2014

- The [Night Owls](/wiki/Night_Owls_(vocal_group)), established in the 1940s, are, as of 2017, one of the oldest extant collegiate [a cappella](/wiki/A_cappella) groups in the United States, and one of nine vocal music groups at Vassar. Other groups include the Vastards (specializing in the [music of the 2000s](/wiki/2000s_in_music)), Broadway and More (BAM; showtunes), the Accidentals (the Axies; the sole all-lower voices a cappella group at Vassar), Beauty and the Beats (focusing on music from Disney movies), Home Brewed (formerly Matthew's Minstrels, the college's first mixed-gender a cappella group), the Vassar Devils, Measure 4 Measure (both themeless groups), and AirCappella (an all-whistling ensemble). Some a cappella groups tour and compete, including the Vassar Devils, who competed in the 2015 [International Championship of Collegiate A Cappella](/wiki/International_Championship_of_Collegiate_A_Cappella).
- [The Philaletheis Society](/wiki/The_Philaletheis_Society), which was founded in 1865 as a literary society, is the oldest theater group on campus. It has now become a completely student run theater group. Others include Unbound (experimental theater), Woodshed (a troupe focused on [devised theater](/wiki/Devised_theater)), and Idlewild (an ensemble for people of marginalized genders). Britomartis, Vassar's only theater group exclusively creating devised theater, was founded in 2011. Further groups include the Future Waitstaff of America (for [musical theater](/wiki/Musical_theater)), Ebony Theatre Ensemble (focusing on Black theater), and two Shakespeare-specific troupes, Shakespeare Troupe and Merely Players. The college also hosts the [Powerhouse Summer Theater](/wiki/Powerhouse_Theater) workshop series.
- Happily Ever Laughter ("HEL") is the college's oldest continually active [sketch comedy](/wiki/Sketch_comedy) group, founded in 1993. Another comedy group, Big K!dz (formerly No Offense), which was started by two former members of an earlier group called Laughingstock (for which recognition by the student assembly was withdrawn in March 2000, as a result of a controversial sketch), was started in September 2000. Another sketch comedy group, The Limit, was started a few years later. Indecent Exposure, an all-women's troupe performing both sketch and [stand-up comedy](/wiki/Stand-up_comedy), was founded in 2004. Comedy Normative, which began in 2009, performs exclusively stand-up comedy. Vassar has a tradition of [improv comedy](/wiki/Improv_comedy) groups, which continue today.
- The Vassar Greens are Vassar's environmental group.
- Vassar College Television (VCTV) is the college's first student-run video production company.

### Campus publications

Front page of the May 20, 1922, *Miscellany News*

- *[The Miscellany News](/wiki/The_Miscellany_News)* has been the weekly paper of the college since 1866, making it one of the oldest college weeklies in the United States. It is available for free most Thursdays when school is in session
- *Squirm* "is a submissions-based magazine about sex and sexuality. Squirm seeks to create a sex-positive forum on campus for the artistic, literary, and creative exploration of sex." The magazine, published annually since 1999, typically runs around 60 pages and is only distributed to the campus community.
- *Boilerplate Magazine* is a student-run publication that calls itself an "alternative news source... that aims to publish radical pieces and creative works which address issues through a socially conscious lens." Due to its independence from collegiate funds, Boilerplate Magazine is generally more critical of the college than other student-run outlets.
- *Unscrewed* (October 1, 1976 – April 1, 1989) was a student-run consumer report on campus residential and classroom safety, local food and drug price comparison, an annual local pizza delivery survey, and long-term topics such as the college's endowment and staffing.

### Student government

In March 2016, in a 15–2 vote, the Vassar Student Association (VSA) passed a resolution calling for the support of the [Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions](/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions) movement and the boycott of Israel. In April 2016, the BDS resolution went to a school-wide referendum, where it was defeated 573–503.

### Athletics

See also: [List of college athletic programs in New York](/wiki/List_of_college_athletic_programs_in_New_York)

Vassar athletics logo

Vassar teams, known as the Brewers, compete in [Division III](/wiki/Division_III_(NCAA)) of the [NCAA](/wiki/NCAA), as a member of the [Liberty League](/wiki/Liberty_League). The nickname originates from the college's founder and namesake [Matthew Vassar](/wiki/Matthew_Vassar), whose family ran a [brewery](/wiki/Brewery) in Poughkeepsie and would later amass a sizable fortune in the industry.

In 2008, the Vassar men's volleyball team made the school's first appearance in a national championship game, beating [UC Santa Cruz](/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Cruz) 3–0 in the semifinal before falling to Springfield in the championship game.

The Butterbeer Broooers, Vassar's [Muggle Quidditch](/wiki/Muggle_Quidditch) team

In 2007, the Vassar cycling team hosted the Eastern Collegiate Cycling Championship in Poughkeepsie and New Paltz, New York. The competition included a 100-mile (160 km) road race over the Shawangunk Mountains in New Paltz as well as a criterium in Poughkeepsie just blocks from the school's campus.

In a controversial move, on November 5, 2009, the athletics department leaders decided the men's and women's [rowing](/wiki/College_rowing_(United_States)) team would transition over a two-year period from a varsity to a club sport as a cost-saving measure.

In 1940, 1941 and 1942, Vassar athletes won national [intercollegiate women's tennis championships](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Women%27s_Tennis_Championship#Singles_and_Doubles_Championships_(1922–1982)) each year in both singles ([Katharine Hubbell](/wiki/Kay_Hubbell)) and doubles (Hubbell, Carolyn "Lonny" Myers).

In 2018, the Vassar women's rugby team won the school's first team national championship, beating [Winona State](/wiki/Winona_State_University) 50–13 in the final of the USA Rugby Women's Division 2.

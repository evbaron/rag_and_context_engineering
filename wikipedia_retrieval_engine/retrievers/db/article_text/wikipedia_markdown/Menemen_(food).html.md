# Menemen (food)

## Infobox

- **Course:** Breakfast
- **Place of origin:** Turkey
- **Region or state:** Menemen, İzmir Province [ 1 ] [ 2 ]
- **Main ingredients:** Eggs, tomato, green peppers, and spices such as ground black and red pepper, salt and oregano, onion, garlic, chili powder
- **Food energy (per serving):** 120 kcal (500 kJ )
- **Similar dishes:** Shakshouka

**Menemen** is a popular traditional [Turkish](/wiki/Turkish_cuisine) dish that includes eggs, tomato, green peppers, and spices such as ground [black](/wiki/Black_pepper) and [red pepper](/wiki/Crushed_red_pepper) cooked in [olive oil](/wiki/Olive_oil). Menemen is commonly eaten for [breakfast](/wiki/Breakfast) and served with [bread](/wiki/Bread). Its name originates from a [small town](/wiki/Menemen) in [İzmir Province](/wiki/%C4%B0zmir_Province). Menemen may be made with onions, but the addition of onions is often debated and is more common when the dish is eaten as a main dish, rather than at breakfast.

Menemen is similar to the Maghrebi dish [shakshouka](/wiki/Shakshouka), widely spread and popular across [North Africa](/wiki/North_Africa) and the [Levant](/wiki/Levant), and to the Hungarian *[lecsó](/wiki/Lecs%C3%B3)* and Serbo-Croat *[sataraš](/wiki/Satara%C5%A1)* which likely have descended from Menemen during the [Ottoman imperial period](/wiki/Ottoman_Empire). Turkish [şakşuka](/wiki/%C5%9Eak%C5%9Fuka), although it shares the same etymology as [shakshouka](/wiki/Shakshouka) (from a North African Arabic dialect meaning "mixed"), is a completely different dish which does not include eggs. Menemen is also different from [çılbır](/wiki/%C3%87%C4%B1lb%C4%B1r), a classic Turkish dish made of poached eggs mixed with yoghurt instead of tomatoes.

## Preparation

The tomatoes are typically finely diced or may be grated. Grated and diced tomatoes can also be mixed together, depending on the cook's preferred texture. If onions are being used, they may be added to the pan with the green chili peppers and sautéed with heated butter or oil. [Aleppo pepper](/wiki/Aleppo_pepper) may be added. The addition of onions is often debated and is more common when menemen is not eaten at breakfast but as a main dish. Some Turkish cooks like [Saniye Anne](/wiki/Saniye_Anne_Yemekleri) insist that a proper menemen cannot be made without onions. In 2018, Turkish food critic Vedat Milor launched a Twitter poll to resolve the discussion about onion within menemen. The poll became widely popular, with 437,657 people voting. The vote proved inconclusive, as the onion faction only had a narrow victory, with 50.6% of the vote.

*[Sucuk](/wiki/Sujuk)*, a dried salami-like sausage, can be added to the pan after the peppers have softened. This adds some flavor to the oil; the sucuk can be removed from the pan before the tomatoes are added to avoid overcooking, but this is not necessary. The tomatoes become very soft and the mix not be too watery when the eggs are added. The eggs may be beaten together with salt, pepper and any desired fresh herbs or added directly to the pan. If *[pastırma](/wiki/Past%C4%B1rma)* is being used, it is added to the pan with the eggs. *Kaşar* cheese or white cheese may optionally be added on top of the eggs just before they finish cooking, along with a garnish of fresh herbs or scallions. It is usually served in the pan in which it is cooked, a double-handled cooking pan known as *[sahan](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/sahan#Turkish)*, together with fresh bread.

Some variations may include mushrooms or [minced lamb](/wiki/Minced_lamb). Different spices may be added according to taste including [cumin](/wiki/Cumin), [paprika](/wiki/Paprika), [mint](/wiki/Mentha), and [thyme](/wiki/Thyme).

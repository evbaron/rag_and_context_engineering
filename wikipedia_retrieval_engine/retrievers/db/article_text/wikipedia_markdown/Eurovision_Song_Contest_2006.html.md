The **Eurovision Song Contest 2006** was the 51st edition of the [Eurovision Song Contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest). It consisted of a semi-final on 18 May and a final on 20 May 2006, held at the [O.A.C.A. Olympic Indoor Hall](/wiki/Telekom_Center_Athens) in [Athens](/wiki/Athens), Greece, and presented by [Maria Menounos](/wiki/Maria_Menounos) and [Sakis Rouvas](/wiki/Sakis_Rouvas). It was organised by the [European Broadcasting Union](/wiki/European_Broadcasting_Union) (EBU) and host broadcaster the [Hellenic Broadcasting Corporation](/wiki/Hellenic_Broadcasting_Corporation) (ERT), who staged the event after winning the [2005 contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005) for [Greece](/wiki/Greece_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005) with the song "[My Number One](/wiki/My_Number_One)" by [Helena Paparizou](/wiki/Helena_Paparizou). Rouvas had also represented [Greece in 2004](/wiki/Greece_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004).

Broadcasters from thirty-seven countries participated in the contest. [Armenia](/wiki/Armenia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) took part for the first time. Meanwhile, [Austria](/wiki/Austria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), and [Serbia and Montenegro](/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) announced their non-participation in the contest. Serbian-Montenegrin broadcaster [Udruženje javnih radija i televizija](/wiki/Udru%C5%BEenje_javnih_radija_i_televizija) (UJRT) had intended to participate, but due to a [scandal in its national selection](/wiki/Evropesma#2006_event_and_controversy), the tensions between its members, [Radio Television of Serbia](/wiki/Radio_Television_of_Serbia) (RTS) and [Radio and Television of Montenegro](/wiki/Radio_and_Television_of_Montenegro) (RTCG), forced its withdrawal. Despite this, they did retain voting rights for the contest.

The winner was [Finland](/wiki/Finland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006) with the heavy metal-song "[Hard Rock Hallelujah](/wiki/Hard_Rock_Hallelujah)", performed by [Lordi](/wiki/Lordi) and written by lead singer [Mr. Lordi](/wiki/Mr._Lordi). This was Finland's first victory in the contest - and first top five placing - in 45 years of participation, the longest time a country had competed without a win at that point. It was also the first ever [hard rock](/wiki/Hard_rock) song to win the contest, as well as the first band to win since [1997](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1997). [Russia](/wiki/Russia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), [Romania](/wiki/Romania_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), and [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006) rounded out the top five. Bosnia and Herzegovina achieved their best result in their Eurovision history. Further down the table, Lithuania also achieved their best result to date, finishing sixth. Of the "[Big Four](/wiki/Big_Four_(Eurovision))" countries [Germany](/wiki/Germany_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006) placed the highest, tying for fourteenth with [Norway](/wiki/Norway_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006).

The contest saw the 1,000th song performed in the contest, when "[Every Song Is a Cry for Love](/wiki/Every_Song_Is_a_Cry_for_Love)" was performed by [Brian Kennedy](/wiki/Brian_Kennedy_(singer)) for [Ireland](/wiki/Ireland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006) in the semi-final.

## Location

[O.A.C.A. Olympic Indoor Hall](/wiki/Telekom_Center_Athens) – host venue of the 2006 contest

Location of host venue (red) and other contest-related sites and events (blue)

The contest took place in [Athens](/wiki/Athens), Greece, following the country's victory at the 2005 edition. It was the first time Greece hosted the contest.
The venue that was chosen as the host venue was the [O.A.C.A. Olympic Indoor Hall](/wiki/Telekom_Center_Athens), which is located in the [Athens Olympic Sports Complex](/wiki/Athens_Olympic_Sports_Complex), in the capital city of Greece. Completed in 1995, it was the largest indoor venue used at the [2004 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics) when it hosted the gymnastics events and the basketball finals and the [2004 Summer Paralympics](/wiki/2004_Summer_Paralympics) when it hosted the wheelchair basketball event.

When Greece won the 2005 contest, the Head of the Greek Delegation, Fotini Yiannoulatou, said that the [Hellenic Broadcasting Corporation](/wiki/Hellenic_Broadcasting_Corporation) (ERT) was ready to host the event in [Athens](/wiki/Athens) the next year. However, multiple cities bid to host the 2006 contest, including [Thessaloniki](/wiki/Thessaloniki) and [Patras](/wiki/Patras), the second and the third largest city in Greece, respectively. The majors of the three cities (Athens, Thessaloniki, Patras) were said that their cities were ready to host the event. The venues that were rumored for each city were: the Olympic Indoor Hall for Athens, [Pylea Sports Hall](/wiki/P.A.O.K._Sports_Arena) for Thessaloniki and [Dimitris Tofalos Arena](/wiki/Dimitris_Tofalos_Arena) for Patras.

A few days after Greece's win, the Greek public broadcaster stated that "ERT intends to hold the Eurovision Song Contest in Athens, taking into account EBU's already expressed wish for the event to be combined with the Olympic facilities and amenities that the city of Athens has to offer". Mr. Panaghiotis Psomiadis, the Prefect of Thessaloniki stated the city will fight for the hosting of the contest. As the city of Patras seemed not to be available to host the contest, at the end it was a two-horse race between Athens and Thessaloniki.

Finally, on 30 June 2005, ERT and [European Broadcasting Union](/wiki/European_Broadcasting_Union) (EBU) announced that Athens will be the host city of the 2006 contest, despite the opposition of some Greek politicians, stated that Athens already had its promotion during the 2004 Summer Olympics and that it's "another city's turn now". The joint decision of the EBU and ERT is to host the 51st Eurovision Song Contest in Athens, which has several modern Olympic venues, infrastructure and a proven ability to host events of this size.

### Other sites

The Eurovision Village was the official Eurovision Song Contest fan and sponsors' area during the events week. There, it was possible to watch performances by local artists, as well as the live shows broadcast from the main venue. Located at the [Zappeion](/wiki/Zappeion), it was open from 15 to 21 May 2006.

The EuroClub was the venue for the official after-parties and private performances by contest participants. Unlike the Eurovision Village, access to the EuroClub was restricted to accredited fans, delegates, and press. It was located at [Athens Technopolis](/wiki/Technopolis_(Gazi)), an industrial museum and a major cultural venue of the city.

The official "Welcome and Opening Ceremonies" events, where the contestants and their delegations are presented before the accredited press and fans, took place also in Zappeion on 15 May 2006 at 21:00 [EET](/wiki/Eastern_European_Time), followed by the Opening Ceremony.

All participating broadcasters in the Eurovision Song Contest must be [active members](/wiki/European_Broadcasting_Union#Members) of the EBU. On 16 January 2006, the EBU announced that the broadcasters from thirty-eight countries would participate in the 2006 contest, with the broadcaster from Austria opting not to participate due to the bad result at the previous contest, and the one from Hungary also withdrawing due to financial reasons. Armenia participated for the very first time in the history of the contest.

On 15 March 2006, the participating broadcaster from [Serbia and Montenegro](/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) announced its withdrawal, reducing the participants number from 38 to 37 and leaving a vacant spot in the final; however, the country retained its rights to vote in the contest.

Several of the performing artists had previously competed as lead artists representing the same country in past editions: [Carola](/wiki/Carola_H%C3%A4ggkvist) had represented [Sweden in 1983](/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1983) and she won the contest [in 1991](/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991); [Eddie Butler](/wiki/Eddie_Butler_(singer)) had represented [Israel in 1999](/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999) as member of [Eden](/wiki/Eden_(Israeli_band)); [Fabrizio Faniello](/wiki/Fabrizio_Faniello) had represented [Malta in 2001](/wiki/Malta_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2001); [Ich Troje](/wiki/Ich_Troje) had represented [Poland in 2003](/wiki/Poland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2003); Victor Diawara, member of [LT United](/wiki/LT_United), had represented [Lithuania in 2001](/wiki/Lithuania_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2001) as member of [Skamp](/wiki/SKAMP); and [Anna Vissi](/wiki/Anna_Vissi) in addition to having represented [Greece in 1980](/wiki/Greece_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1980) with the Epikouri, had represented [Cyprus in 1982](/wiki/Cyprus_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1982). [Sigríður Beinteinsdóttir](/wiki/Sigga), who had represented [Iceland in 1990](/wiki/Iceland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1990) as member of [Stjórnin](/wiki/Stj%C3%B3rnin), [in 1992](/wiki/Iceland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1992) as member of [Heart 2 Heart](/wiki/Heart_2_Heart_(band)), and [in 1994](/wiki/Iceland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1994), provided backing vocals for Iceland.

Additionally, [Hari Mata Hari](/wiki/Hari_Mata_Hari) were selected to represent [Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1999](/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999), but their entry was disqualified; Ireland's [Brian Kennedy](/wiki/Brian_Kennedy_(singer)) performed in *Lumen*, the interval act of the [1995 contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1995); host Sakis Rouvas had represented [Greece in 2004](/wiki/Greece_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004); and if [No Name](/wiki/No_Name_(Montenegrin_band)) had been permitted to represent [Serbia and Montenegro](/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), they would have done so for the second consecutive year.

## Format

### Visual design

The official logo of the contest remained the same from 2004 and 2005 with the country's flag in the heart being changed. The 2006 sub-logo was presented to the public through a press conference that was held on 1 November 2005, at the King George Hotel in Athens, while it was created by the design company Karamela for ERT and was apparently based on the [Phaistos Disc](/wiki/Phaistos_Disc) which is a popular symbol of [ancient Greece](/wiki/Ancient_Greece). According to ERT, it was "inspired by the wind and the sea, the golden sunlight and the glow of the sand". Following [2004](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004)'s "Under The Same Sky" and [2005](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005)'s "Awakening", the slogan for 2006 was "Feel The Rhythm". This theme was also the basis for the postcards in 2006, which emphasized Greece's historical significance as well as being a major modern tourist destination.

In addition to the graphic design, there was a theme song used for the contest composed by Nikko Patrelakis, which was featured in the intros and in-between commercial breaks, as well as besides the participating entries. The theme music package was conducted by Andreas Pylarinos, while the ERT Symphony Orchestra recorded all music used during the show.

### Broadcasting

As with the 2005 edition, the shows were broadcast in [widescreen](/wiki/Widescreen) [16:9](/wiki/16:9) format in [standard-definition](/wiki/Standard-definition_television). The shows were also filmed (but not broadcast) in [high-definition](/wiki/High-definition_television), as part of a research experiment carried by the EBU, host broadcaster ERT, the [Institute for Broadcasting Technology](/wiki/Institut_f%C3%BCr_Rundfunktechnik) in Munich, the research and development laboratories of [RAI](/wiki/RAI) and the BBC. This was done to test high-definition television and to gather "artistic and scientific knowledge" for future contests, but the HD footage was never intended to be used as part of the original broadcast. The first edition produced and broadcast in high-definition was the [subsequent contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2007) in 2007.

On 31 July 2021, the HDTV raw footage of the 2006 contest was broadcast for the first time, as part of the *[EurovisionAgain](/wiki/EurovisionAgain)* series, on the Eurovision YouTube channel.

### Stage design

ERT announced that the British company *Stage One* has been appointed to build the set for the contest. *Stage One* had designed the sets for the [Opening](/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony) and [Closing](/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics_closing_ceremony) ceremonies of the 2004 Summer Olympics. The broadcaster have announced that the concept will be rich with traditional Greek elements, paying homage to the country's history and culture. The stage for the contest was designed by Greek stage designer Elias Ledakis. He would go on to design the stage for the [Junior Eurovision Song Contest 2013](/wiki/Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013) in [Kyiv](/wiki/Kyiv), Ukraine. The stage was a replica of an ancient Greek amphitheatre.

### Postcards

As it was referred, the theme "Feel The Rhythm" was also the basis for the postcards, which emphasized Greece's historical significance as well as being a major modern tourist destination. The postcards filmed between March and April 2006. The host broadcaster ERT spent €3 million on the production of the 37 postcards. Fanis Papathanisiou of ERT said: *"An impressive, international tourism campaign is expensive as well. The Eurovision Song Contest is a perfect platform to achieve equal or even better results. That's why it is worth the investment"*. To decide what to show in the postcards, ERT held surveys in all of the participating countries, asking people what they associate Greece with.

### Voting segment

To save time in the final, the voting time lasted ten minutes and the voting process was changed: points 1–7 were shown immediately on-screen. The spokespersons only announced the countries scoring 8, 10 and 12 points. Despite this being intended to speed up proceedings, there were still problems during voting – EBU imaging over-rode presenter Menounos during a segment in the voting interval and some scoreboards loaded slowly. The Dutch spokesperson [Paul de Leeuw](/wiki/Paul_de_Leeuw) also caused problems, giving his [mobile number](/wiki/Mobile_phone) to presenter Rouvas during the Dutch results, and slowing down proceedings, also by announcing the first seven points. [Constantinos Christoforou](/wiki/Constantinos_Christoforou) (who represented [Cyprus in 1996](/wiki/Cyprus_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1996), [in 2002](/wiki/Cyprus_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2002), and [in 2005](/wiki/Cyprus_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005)) saluted from "[Nicosia](/wiki/Nicosia), the last [divided capital](/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Cyprus) in Europe"; during Cyprus' reading, the telecast displayed Switzerland by mistake. This voting process has been criticized because suspense was lost by reading only three votes instead of ten. And for the first and only time before the [Prespa agreement](/wiki/Prespa_agreement), the display for the Macedonian entry had the title spelled out in its entirety (as "Former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia") instead of being abbreviated as it has been in previous years (as "FYR Macedonia").

### Presenters

The hosts, [Maria Menounos](/wiki/Maria_Menounos) and [Sakis Rouvas](/wiki/Sakis_Rouvas).

After Greece's win, several websites claimed to know that [Alexandra Pascalidou](/wiki/Alexandra_Pascalidou) would be co-hosting the 2006 contest, together with the [Greek-French](/wiki/Greeks_in_France) journalist and entertainer [Nikos Aliagas](/wiki/Nikos_Aliagas), but these speculations were untrue.

Initially, ERT asked [Sakis Rouvas](/wiki/Sakis_Rouvas) to represent again Greece in [Athens](/wiki/Athens), an offer which he didn't accept. With the Greek broadcaster wanting Rouvas' involvement in the contest, they instead asked him to be one of the hosts of the contest, to which he agreed.

Among the names that were rumored for the female host included [Greek Canadian](/wiki/Greek_Canadians) actress [Nia Vardalos](/wiki/Nia_Vardalos), Greek social entrepreneur and philanthropist [Elizabeth Filippouli](/wiki/Elizabeth_Filippouli), [Greek American](/wiki/Greek_Americans) actress [Jennifer Aniston](/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston), and the [previous edition's](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005) winner, [Helena Paparizou](/wiki/Helena_Paparizou). After a lot of speculations, ERT announced on 7 March 2006 that Greek American television personality [Maria Menounos](/wiki/Maria_Menounos) would be the hostess of the contest.

Menounos and Rouvas also hosted the allocation draw on 21 March 2006, in order to determine the running order for the semi-final, the grand final and – for the first time in the history of the contest – the voting order. The "Welcome to the Party" opening ceremony was hosted by actress [Zeta Makrypoulia](/wiki/Zeta_Makrypoulia) and actor/screenwriter of the show, [Giorgos Kapoutzidis](/wiki/Giorgos_Kapoutzidis), while Ioanna Papanikolopoulou moderated the press conferences.

### Final

[Nana Mouskouri](/wiki/Nana_Mouskouri) appeared as a guest in the grand final.

The finalists were:

- the four automatic qualifiers [France](/wiki/France_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), [Germany](/wiki/Germany_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), [Spain](/wiki/Spain_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), and the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006);
- the top 10 countries from the [2005 final](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005#Final) (other than the automatic qualifiers);
- the top 10 countries from the 2006 semi-final.

The final was held on 20 May 2006 at 22:00 [EEST](/wiki/Eastern_European_Summer_Time) (21:00 [CEST](/wiki/Central_European_Time)). and was won by Finland. 24 countries performed and all 37 participants and Serbia (as a part of Serbia and Montenegro) voted.

The grand final opened with a ballet dance, symbolizing the birth of Greece. The Greek singer Foteini Darra performed "The Mermaid Song" (also known as *"The Song of Life"*), while the dancers and the sets mimicked the creative elements (the sea, the wind, the sun). At the end of the ballet, the presenters appeared in the air, suspended from ropes. They landed on the stage and greeted the audience. They immediately introduced the previous year's winner, Helena Paparizou, who performed her winning song, "My Number One".

The voting lines for the final were opened by the Greek singer [Nana Mouskouri](/wiki/Nana_Mouskouri), who represented [Luxembourg in 1963](/wiki/Luxembourg_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1963).

The interval act of the final featured Helena Paparizou performing her song "[Mambo!](/wiki/Mambo!_(Helena_Paparizou_song))", already a smash hit in Greece, and a contemporary ballet composed by Dimitris Papadimitriou and choreographed by Fokas Evangelinos entitled *4000 Years of Greek Song,* which traced the history of the musical culture of the host country.

Finland won with 292 points. Russia came second with 248 points, with Bosnia and Herzegovina, Romania, Sweden, Lithuania, Ukraine, Armenia, Greece and Ireland completing the top ten. Moldova, Spain, France, Israel and Malta occupied the bottom five positions.

## Detailed voting results

Televoting was used in all nations except Monaco and Albania. Monaco used a jury as the chances of getting enough votes needed to validate the votes were low. Albania used a jury since there were problems with their televote. In the semi-final, Monaco and Albania also solely used jury voting due to insufficient televoting numbers. Coincidentally, Albania and Monaco were two of the three countries that did not vote for the winning entry, the third one was Armenia.

Serbia had been allowed to vote in the two shows, despite not competing, and despite not being an independent country, but a part of Serbia and Montenegro.

### International broadcasts

- Australia – Although Australia was not itself eligible to enter, the semi-final and final were broadcast on [SBS](/wiki/Special_Broadcasting_Service), and took commentary from the BBC broadcast. As is the case each year, they were not however broadcast live due to the difference in Australian time zones. The final rated an estimated 462,000, and was ranked 21st of the broadcaster's top rating programs for the 2005/06 financial year.
- Azerbaijan – [Azerbaijan](/wiki/Azerbaijan) were willing to enter the contest but since [AzTV](/wiki/AzTV)'s application for active EBU membership was denied on 18 June 2007, they missed the contest and had to wait until they were accepted. Another Azerbaijan broadcaster, [İctimai](/wiki/%C4%B0ctimai_Television), broadcast the contest. It was a passive EBU member, and had broadcast it for the last 2 years. It was the only non-participating broadcaster this year to send its own commentators to the contest.
- Gibraltar – [Gibraltar](/wiki/Gibraltar) screened only the final on [GBC](/wiki/Gibraltar_Broadcasting_Corporation).
- Italy – Italy did not enter because [RAI](/wiki/RAI), the national broadcaster, is in strong competition with commercial TV stations and they believed that the Eurovision Song Contest would not be a popular show in Italy. They had not broadcast the contest in recent years, although an independent Italian channel for the gay community had shown it in 2003. Italy eventually rejoined in [2011](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2011), and has since enjoyed an upturn in fortunes.
- [Serbia](/wiki/Serbia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006) – Serbia and Montenegro was originally set to compete in 2006, before withdrawing after being unable to select an entry following a controversy at its national final. Despite this, Serbia's broadcaster [RTS](/wiki/Radio_Television_of_Serbia) retained broadcasting rights, and viewers in Serbia were allowed to vote in both the semi-final and the final.

### Ratings

After the contest, EBU officials stated that the overall ratings for the semi-final were 35% higher than in 2005, and for the Final had risen by 28%.

In France, average market shares reached 30.3%, up by 8% over the 2005 figure. Other countries that showed a rise in average market shares included Germany with 38% (up from 29%), United Kingdom with 37.5% (up from 36%), Spain with 36% (up from 35%), Ireland with 58% (up from 35%) and Sweden, which reached over 80% compared to 57% the year previously.

Voting revenues had also risen from the previous contest, and the official Eurovision website, www.eurovision.tv, reported visits from over 200 countries and over 98 million page views, compared with 85 million in 2005.

## Aftermath

ERT's net income from the Eurovision event amounted to €7.28 million, while the cost of the entire event reached €5.5 million, said on Thursday in a press conference the president of ERT, Christos Panagopoulos and the authorized consultant George Chouliaras, who stated: *"The allegations about the waste of money of the Greek taxpayer do not apply. The Greek people did not pay a penny for the event. It was a commercial and profitable event and the money we spent was donor money"*.

According to G. Chouliaras, the revenues that ERT had from the event were €3.63 million from national sponsors, €2.2 million from tickets and €1.45 million from the share of international sponsors, advertising revenues outside sponsorships, sms, etc.

Regarding the costs paid by ERT for the event together with the EBU, it amounted to a total of €9 million, of which €5.5 million were paid by ERT and €3.5 million by the EBU. These costs include the costs for the television production, the production of the artistic program, the technical production, the payment of contributions, the organization of the competition and any other direct costs related to the organization of Eurovision 2006. It is also noted that ERT paid for the production of 47 commercials and their promotion during the semifinals and the final €3.5 million.

### Spectacles and rewards

The president of ERT, Christos Panagopoulos, clarified, however, that the total cost does not include the shows that started in February for the advertising support of the event, for which he estimated that their cost will not exceed €1 million. He stated that in essence the net profit of ERT amounts to €745,000, which will be allocated for other cultural events.

It was also clarified that ERT did not pay anything to Anna Vissi, nor to [Nikos Karvelas](/wiki/Nikos_Karvelas), as well as did not pay for the dress of Anna Vissi. Chouliaras stressed that all the participants in the event were paid at market prices and in particular [Zeta Makrypoulia](/wiki/Zeta_Makrypoulia) and [Giorgos Kapoutzidis](/wiki/Giorgos_Kapoutzidis) received €8–10,000 per month for their four-month employment, Sakis Rouvas €50,000 and Maria Menounos €45,000.

It was also clarified that the costs of the "promotour" of Anna Vissi are included in the total cost and that from these the transfers were covered by [Olympic Airlines](/wiki/Olympic_Airlines) and the hotels, the cost of which amounted to €150,000, by the sponsors.

Regarding the future, Giorgos Chouliaras noted that "ERT should have a dynamic participation in the next Eurovision Song Contests and not devalue the institution, since it is a television product watched by 3.5 million Greeks".

The awards are divided into three categories: Artistic Award which was voted by [previous winners](/wiki/List_of_Eurovision_Song_Contest_winners) of the contest, Composer Award, and Press Award.

### Barbara Dex Award

The [Barbara Dex Award](/wiki/Barbara_Dex_Award) was a humorous fan award given to the worst dressed artist each year. Named after [Belgium's representative](/wiki/Barbara_Dex) who came last in the 1993 contest, wearing her self-designed dress, the award was handed by the fansite House of Eurovision from 1997 to 2016 and by the fansite songfestival.be from 2017 to 2021.

## Official album

Cover art of the official album

***Eurovision Song Contest: Athens 2006*** was the official compilation album of the 2006 contest, put together by the European Broadcasting Union and released by [CMC International](/wiki/CMC_International) on 28 April 2006. The album featured all 37 songs that entered in the 2006 contest, including the semi-finalists that failed to qualify into the grand final.

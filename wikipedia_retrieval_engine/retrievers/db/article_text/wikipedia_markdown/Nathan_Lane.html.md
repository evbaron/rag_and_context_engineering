**Nathan Lane** (born **Joseph Lane**; February 3, 1956) is an American actor. Known for his versatile [roles on stage and screen](/wiki/Nathan_Lane_on_screen_and_stage), he has received [numerous accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Nathan_Lane) including three [Tony Awards](/wiki/Tony_Awards), seven [Drama Desk Awards](/wiki/Drama_Desk_Awards), a [Laurence Olivier Award](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award), three [Emmy Awards](/wiki/Emmy_Awards), and a [Screen Actors Guild](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild) [Actor Award](/wiki/Actor_Award). Lane was awarded a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 2006 and was inducted into the [American Theater Hall of Fame](/wiki/American_Theater_Hall_of_Fame) in 2008. In 2010, *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* hailed Lane as being "the greatest stage entertainer of the decade".

Lane made his professional theatre debut in 1978 in an [off-Broadway](/wiki/Off-Broadway) production of *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)*. During that time he also briefly appeared as one half of the comedy team of Stack and Lane, until he was cast in the 1982 Broadway revival of [Noël Coward](/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward)'s *[Present Laughter](/wiki/Present_Laughter)* directed by and starring [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott). That led to an extensive career onstage, where he had a long friendship and fruitful collaboration with the playwright [Terrence McNally](/wiki/Terrence_McNally) which started in 1989 with the [Manhattan Theater Club](/wiki/Manhattan_Theater_Club) production of *[The Lisbon Traviata](/wiki/The_Lisbon_Traviata)*.

He has won three [Tony Awards](/wiki/Tony_Awards): for [Best Actor in a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical) for Pseudolus in [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim)'s *[A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum](/wiki/A_Funny_Thing_Happened_on_the_Way_to_the_Forum)* (1996) and Max Bialystock in [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks)' *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))* (2001), and [Best Featured Actor in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actor_in_a_Play) for [Roy Cohn](/wiki/Roy_Cohn) in [Tony Kushner](/wiki/Tony_Kushner)'s *[Angels in America](/wiki/Angels_in_America)* (2018). He was also Tony-nominated for *[Guys and Dolls](/wiki/Guys_and_Dolls)* (1992), *[The Nance](/wiki/The_Nance)* (2013), *[The Front Page](/wiki/The_Front_Page)* (2016), and *[Death of a Salesman](/wiki/Death_of_a_Salesman)* (2026). Among his 25 Broadway credits are *[The Man Who Came To Dinner](/wiki/The_Man_Who_Came_To_Dinner)* (2000), *[The Odd Couple](/wiki/The_Odd_Couple_(play))* (2005), *[Butley](/wiki/Butley_(play))* (2006), *[Waiting for Godot](/wiki/Waiting_for_Godot)* (2009), *[The Addams Family](/wiki/The_Addams_Family_(musical))* (2010), *[It's Only a Play](/wiki/It%27s_Only_a_Play)* (2014), *[Gary: A Sequel to Titus Andronicus](/wiki/Gary:_A_Sequel_to_Titus_Andronicus)* (2019), and *[Pictures from Home](/wiki/Pictures_from_Home)* (2023).

Lane has acted in films such as *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)* (1994), *[The Birdcage](/wiki/The_Birdcage)* (1996), *[Mouse Hunt](/wiki/Mouse_Hunt)* (1997), *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(2005_film))* (2005), and *[Beau Is Afraid](/wiki/Beau_Is_Afraid)* (2023). He received the [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) for his role in the [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) mystery comedy series *[Only Murders in the Building](/wiki/Only_Murders_in_the_Building)* in 2022. He has received eight Emmy nominations for his guest roles in *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)*, *[Mad About You](/wiki/Mad_About_You)*, *[Modern Family](/wiki/Modern_Family)*, *[The Good Wife](/wiki/The_Good_Wife)*, and *[Only Murders in the Building](/wiki/Only_Murders_in_the_Building)*. He portrayed [F. Lee Bailey](/wiki/F._Lee_Bailey) in the [FX](/wiki/FX_(TV_channel)) miniseries *[The People v. O. J. Simpson: American Crime Story](/wiki/The_People_v._O._J._Simpson:_American_Crime_Story)* (2016) and [Dominick Dunne](/wiki/Dominick_Dunne) in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) anthology series *[Monsters: The Lyle and Erik Menendez Story](/wiki/Monsters:_The_Lyle_and_Erik_Menendez_Story)* (2024). He has also starred in *[Penny Dreadful: City of Angels](/wiki/Penny_Dreadful:_City_of_Angels)* (2020), *[The Gilded Age](/wiki/The_Gilded_Age_(TV_series))* (2022–present), and *[Mid-Century Modern](/wiki/Mid-Century_Modern_(TV_series))* (2025).

## Early life and education

Lane was born **Joseph Lane** in [Jersey City, New Jersey](/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey) on February 3, 1956. His father Daniel Joseph Lane was a truck driver and an aspiring tenor who died in 1967 from alcoholism when Nathan was 11. His mother Nora Veronica (née Finnerty) was a housewife and secretary who suffered from [bipolar disorder](/wiki/Bipolar_disorder) and died in 2000. Nathan has two older brothers, Daniel Jr. and Robert. Their parents were [Catholics](/wiki/Catholics) and all of their grandparents were [Irish](/wiki/Irish_diaspora) immigrants. He was named Joseph after his uncle, a [Jesuit](/wiki/Jesuit) priest. Nathan attended Catholic schools in Jersey City, including Jesuit-run [St. Peter's Preparatory School](/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Preparatory_School), where he was voted Best Actor in 1974, and in 2011 received the Prep Hall of Fame Professional Achievement Award.

## Career

### 1975–1990s: Rise to prominence

Lane in Los Angeles at the 1998 [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), September 1998

Accepted to [Saint Joseph's University](/wiki/Saint_Joseph%27s_University) in [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia) on a drama scholarship, Joseph Lane was accompanied on what was supposed to be his first day there by his older brother Dan. Discovering that the scholarship would not cover enough of his expenses, he decided to leave, and work for a year to earn some money. His brother said, "I remember him saying to me, 'College is for people who don't know what they want to do.'"

Because there already was a Joseph Lane registered with [Actors' Equity](/wiki/Actors%27_Equity), he changed his name to Nathan after the character Nathan Detroit from the musical *[Guys and Dolls](/wiki/Guys_and_Dolls)*. He moved to New York City in 1975 where after a long struggle, his career began to take off, first with some brief success in the world of stand-up comedy with partner Patrick Stack and later with off-Broadway productions at [Second Stage Theatre](/wiki/Second_Stage_Theatre), the [Roundabout Theatre](/wiki/Roundabout_Theatre), and the [Manhattan Theatre Club](/wiki/Manhattan_Theatre_Club). In 1978, he appeared in a production of *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)* alongside [John Goodman](/wiki/John_Goodman) at the [Equity Library Theatre](/wiki/Equity_Library_Theatre). He made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) debut in a 1982 revival of [Noël Coward](/wiki/No%C3%ABl_Coward)'s *[Present Laughter](/wiki/Present_Laughter)* as Roland Maule (Drama Desk nomination) with [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott), [Kate Burton](/wiki/Kate_Burton_(actress)), [Dana Ivey](/wiki/Dana_Ivey), [Bette Henritze](/wiki/Bette_Henritze), [Elizabeth Hubbard](/wiki/Elizabeth_Hubbard), [Jim Piddock](/wiki/Jim_Piddock), and [Christine Lahti](/wiki/Christine_Lahti).

His second Broadway appearance was in the 1983 musical *[Merlin](/wiki/Merlin_(musical))*, starring [Chita Rivera](/wiki/Chita_Rivera) and magician [Doug Henning](/wiki/Doug_Henning). This was followed by *[Wind in the Willows](/wiki/Wind_in_the_Willows_(1985_musical))* as [Mr. Toad](/wiki/Mr._Toad), *Some Americans Abroad* at [Lincoln Center](/wiki/Lincoln_Center), and the national tour of [Neil Simon](/wiki/Neil_Simon)'s *[Broadway Bound](/wiki/Broadway_Bound)*.

Off-Broadway productions in which he appeared, include *Love* (the musical version of [Murray Schisgal](/wiki/Murray_Schisgal)'s *[Luv](/wiki/Luv_(play))*), *[Measure for Measure](/wiki/Measure_for_Measure)* directed by [Joseph Papp](/wiki/Joseph_Papp) in Central Park, for which he received the [St. Clair Bayfield Award](/wiki/St._Clair_Bayfield_Award), *The Common Pursuit*, *The Film Society*, *In a Pig's Valise*, *[She Stoops to Conquer](/wiki/She_Stoops_to_Conquer)*, *[The Merry Wives of Windsor](/wiki/The_Merry_Wives_of_Windsor)* and *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)*. He appeared at the Williamstown Theatre Festival in *[The School for Scandal](/wiki/The_School_for_Scandal)* and John Guare's *Moon Over Miami*. His association with [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim) began in 1989 with a workshop reading of *[Assassins](/wiki/Assassins_(musical))*, where he played [Samuel Byck](/wiki/Samuel_Byck), the would-be murderer of [Richard Nixon](/wiki/Richard_Nixon). Lane also appeared in the television shows *[Miami Vice](/wiki/Miami_Vice)* and *[The Days and Nights of Molly Dodd](/wiki/The_Days_and_Nights_of_Molly_Dodd)* as Bing Shalimar.

Lane had a long friendship with [Terrence McNally](/wiki/Terrence_McNally)

In 1991, Lane appeared with [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott) again in a revival of Paul Osborne's *[On Borrowed Time](/wiki/On_Borrowed_Time)* at the [Circle in the Square Theatre](/wiki/Circle_in_the_Square_Theatre) on Broadway. In 1992, he starred in the hit revival of *[Guys and Dolls](/wiki/Guys_and_Dolls)*, playing Nathan Detroit, the character who lent him his name, opposite [Peter Gallagher](/wiki/Peter_Gallagher) and [Faith Prince](/wiki/Faith_Prince). For this performance, he received his first [Tony](/wiki/Tony_Award) nomination, as well as Drama Desk and [Outer Critics Circle Awards](/wiki/Outer_Critics_Circle_Award). In 1992, he won an [Obie Award](/wiki/Obie_Award) for Sustained Excellence of Performance. His professional association with his close friend the playwright [Terrence McNally](/wiki/Terrence_McNally), whom he met in 1987, includes roles in *[The Lisbon Traviata](/wiki/The_Lisbon_Traviata)* (Drama Desk and [Lucille Lortel](/wiki/Lucille_Lortel) Awards, and Outer Critics Circle nomination), *[Bad Habits](/wiki/Bad_Habits_(play))*, *[Lips Together, Teeth Apart](/wiki/Lips_Together,_Teeth_Apart)*, *[Love! Valour! Compassion!](/wiki/Love!_Valour!_Compassion!)* ([Obie](/wiki/Obie), Drama Desk, and Outer Critics Circle Awards), *Dedication or the Stuff of Dreams*, which opened in 2005 (Drama Desk nomination), *[The Last Mile](/wiki/The_Last_Mile_(1992_film))* on PBS' *[Great Performances](/wiki/Great_Performances)*, and the film version of *[Frankie and Johnny](/wiki/Frankie_and_Johnny_(1991_film))*.

The early 1990s began a stretch of successful Broadway shows for Lane. In 1993, he portrayed [Sid Caesar](/wiki/Sid_Caesar)-like Max Prince in [Neil Simon](/wiki/Neil_Simon)'s *[Laughter on the 23rd Floor](/wiki/Laughter_on_the_23rd_Floor)*, inspired by Simon's early career writing sketches for *[Your Show of Shows](/wiki/Your_Show_of_Shows)*. In 1996, he starred in the hit revival of [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim)'s *[A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum](/wiki/A_Funny_Thing_Happened_on_the_Way_to_the_Forum)*. For his performance he won the [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical) as well as the Drama Desk and Outer Critics Circle Awards. In 1998, he appeared off Broadway in [Jon Robin Baitz](/wiki/Jon_Robin_Baitz)'s revised 1984 comedy, *Mizlansky/Zilinsky or 'Schmucks'*.

### 1994–2009: Breakthrough and acclaim

In 1994, Lane voiced [Timon](/wiki/Timon_(The_Lion_King)), the meerkat, in [Disney](/wiki/Disney)'s blockbuster animated film *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)* and reprised the role in its sequel *[The Lion King II: Simba's Pride](/wiki/The_Lion_King_II:_Simba%27s_Pride)* and midquel *[The Lion King 1½](/wiki/The_Lion_King_1%C2%BD)*. In 1995, Lane was the voice of the meerkat in the early episodes of [*Timon & Pumbaa*](/wiki/Timon_%26_Pumbaa_(TV_series)). In 1995, he played the Cowardly Lion in *[The Wizard of Oz in Concert](/wiki/The_Wizard_of_Oz_in_Concert)* at Lincoln Center to benefit the [Children's Defense Fund](/wiki/Children%27s_Defense_Fund).
The performance was originally broadcast on Turner Network Television (TNT).

Lane collaborated with [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim) in several of his projects

In 1996 Lane appeared in the film *[The Birdcage](/wiki/The_Birdcage)*, for which he received his first [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe) nomination. The film, an American remake of the classic French farce *[La Cage aux Folles](/wiki/La_Cage_aux_Folles_(play))*, was directed by [Mike Nichols](/wiki/Mike_Nichols) with a screenplay by [Elaine May](/wiki/Elaine_May), and starred [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams), Lane, and [Gene Hackman](/wiki/Gene_Hackman), and went on to be a big success. The Stephen Sondheim song "Little Dream" in *The Birdcage* was supposedly written especially for him. In 1999, he appeared with [Victor Garber](/wiki/Victor_Garber) in the workshop of the Sondheim musical *Wise Guys* (later retitled *[Road Show](/wiki/Road_Show_(musical))*). His collaboration with Sondheim would continue when Lane revised the original book for and starred in the Broadway debut of the composer's *[The Frogs](/wiki/The_Frogs_(musical))* at Lincoln Center in 2004.

Lane appeared in the 1997 dark comedy *[Mouse Hunt](/wiki/Mouse_Hunt)*, one of the first films to come out of the newly formed DreamWorks Studios, in which he co-starred with British comedian [Lee Evans](/wiki/Lee_Evans_(comedian)) and Christopher Walken. In 1999, he appeared in the [Encores!](/wiki/Encores!) concert revival of *Do Re Mi* at [City Center](/wiki/City_center_theater). That same year he also voiced the role of Snowbell in the family film *[Stuart Little](/wiki/Stuart_Little_(film))*, opposite his *[Life With Mikey](/wiki/Life_With_Mikey)* co-star [Michael J. Fox](/wiki/Michael_J._Fox).

He is known for his voice work in two Disney animated series, *[Teacher's Pet](/wiki/Teacher%27s_Pet_(TV_series))* and *[Timon & Pumbaa](/wiki/Timon_%26_Pumbaa_(TV_series))*, as well as *[George and Martha](/wiki/George_and_Martha)* on [HBO](/wiki/HBO). He received [Daytime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Awards) for his voice performances in *Teacher's Pet* and *Timon & Pumbaa*, as well as a nomination for *George and Martha*. He hosted *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* in 1997, and the [Tony Awards](/wiki/Tony_Awards) (once as host for the 50th anniversary telecast, and three times as co-host, with [Glenn Close](/wiki/Glenn_Close) and [Gregory Hines](/wiki/Gregory_Hines); [Rosie O'Donnell](/wiki/Rosie_O%27Donnell); and [Matthew Broderick](/wiki/Matthew_Broderick) respectively). From 1998 to 1999 he starred in the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) sitcom *[Encore! Encore!](/wiki/Encore!_Encore!)* alongside [Joan Plowright](/wiki/Joan_Plowright) and [Glenne Headly](/wiki/Glenne_Headly). *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* gave a very positive review to the show's debut, writing it possessed the "most accomplished, high-powered cast on television." Although the series got positive reviews it was canceled. He still won the *[People's Choice Award](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Award)* that year for Favorite New Actor in a Comedy. Lane received Emmy Award nominations for his guest appearances on *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)* and *[Mad About You](/wiki/Mad_About_You)* in 1995 and 1998, respectively.

Lane starred in [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks)' *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))* (2001)

Lane starred in the Roundabout revival of *[The Man Who Came to Dinner](/wiki/The_Man_Who_Came_to_Dinner)* as Sheridan Whiteside, with [Jean Smart](/wiki/Jean_Smart) and [Harriet Sansom Harris](/wiki/Harriet_Sansom_Harris) in 2000. Charles Isherwood of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* liked his performance, "Nathan Lane, an actor who makes virtually every role he plays seem like a role he was born to play, is the splendidly seething, delightfully acerbic center of Jerry Zaks' splashy production of the 1939 comedy". The production was taped and shown on [PBS](/wiki/PBS). That same year he starred in [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh)'s film adaptation of [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare)'s *[Love's Labour's Lost](/wiki/Love%27s_Labour%27s_Lost_(film))* (2000). He acted in the comedy *[Isn't She Great](/wiki/Isn%27t_She_Great)* (2000) opposite [Bette Midler](/wiki/Bette_Midler), the crime drama *[Trixie](/wiki/Trixie_(film))* (2000), and voiced a character in the animated science fiction film *[Titan A.E.](/wiki/Titan_A.E.)* (2000).

In 2001, he starred as [Max Bialystock](/wiki/Max_Bialystock) in the blockbuster musical version of [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks)'s *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))*. He acted alongside [Matthew Broderick](/wiki/Matthew_Broderick). Chris Jones of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* wrote "Lane's greatest contribution, though, is this performer's innate sense of pace. He's constantly propelling the show forward and giving all this nonsense a necessary sense of urgency." [Ben Brantley](/wiki/Ben_Brantley) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* praised Lane's performance describing it as his "most delicious performance". He complimented Lane's and Broderick's chemistry adding "Mr. Lane and Mr. Broderick, have the most dynamic stage chemistry since [Natasha Richardson](/wiki/Natasha_Richardson) met [Liam Neeson](/wiki/Liam_Neeson) in *[Anna Christie](/wiki/Anna_Christie)*. The role earned him his second [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical) as well as Drama Desk and Outer Critics Circle Awards.

The next year he reprised his role as Snowbell in *[Stuart Little 2](/wiki/Stuart_Little_2)* (2002). He then appeared as Vincent Crummles in a film adaptation of *[Nicholas Nickleby](/wiki/Nicholas_Nickleby)* (2002) and the cast received the Ensemble Acting award from the National Board of Review. In 2003, he starred off Broadway in *Trumbo: Red, White, and Blacklisted*. In 2004, Lane revised the libretto and portrayed [Dionysus](/wiki/Dionysus) in the revival of [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim)'s musical *[The Frogs](/wiki/The_Frogs_(musical))* which opened at the [Vivian Beaumont Theater](/wiki/Vivian_Beaumont_Theater) at [Lincoln Center](/wiki/Lincoln_Center) on [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)). That same year he replaced [Richard Dreyfuss](/wiki/Richard_Dreyfuss) in *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))* in the West End. Dreyfuss was let go just a week before the show's first preview at London's [Theatre Royal Drury Lane](/wiki/Theatre_Royal_Drury_Lane). Lane went on to win the [Olivier Award](/wiki/Olivier_Award) as Best Actor in a Musical. His performance in the [film version](/wiki/The_Producers_(2005_film)), opposite Broadway co-star [Matthew Broderick](/wiki/Matthew_Broderick) as [Leo Bloom](/wiki/Leo_Bloom), earned him his second nomination for the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy).

In 2005, Lane rejoined Broderick for a successful limited run of *[The Odd Couple](/wiki/The_Odd_Couple_(play))*. In 2006, he took on a primarily dramatic role in a revival of [Simon Gray](/wiki/Simon_Gray)'s *[Butley](/wiki/Butley_(play))*, having played the role to great success at The [Huntington Theater Company](/wiki/Huntington_Theater_Company) in Boston in 2003. He and Broderick received adjacent stars on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in a joint ceremony on January 9, 2006, and were immortalized in wax as Max and Leo at [Madame Tussauds](/wiki/Madame_Tussauds) Museum in New York City on January 16, 2009. In 2008, he played the President of the United States in the [David Mamet](/wiki/David_Mamet) political satire, *[November](/wiki/November_(play))*, directed by [Joe Mantello](/wiki/Joe_Mantello). This was followed by the critically acclaimed 2009 revival of *[Waiting for Godot](/wiki/Waiting_for_Godot)* (Outer Critics Circle nomination) in which he played [Estragon](/wiki/Estragon) opposite [Bill Irwin](/wiki/Bill_Irwin)'s [Vladimir.](/wiki/Vladimir_(Waiting_for_Godot)) He was a 2008 [American Theatre Hall of Fame](/wiki/American_Theatre_Hall_of_Fame) inductee. In the 2000s Lane also made guest appearances on *[Sex and the City](/wiki/Sex_and_the_City)*, *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, *[Absolutely Fabulous](/wiki/Absolutely_Fabulous)*, and *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)*.

### 2010–2019: Established actor

In 2009, Lane starred in the musical version of *[The Addams Family](/wiki/The_Addams_Family_(musical))* as [Gomez](/wiki/Gomez_Addams) in Chicago, a role he reprised on Broadway the following year, receiving Drama Desk and Outer Critics Circle nominations. That year he also received a Drama League Award for Distinguished Achievement in Musical Theater. Committed to starring in a revival of the [Eugene O'Neill](/wiki/Eugene_O%27Neill) play *[The Iceman Cometh](/wiki/The_Iceman_Cometh)* at Chicago's [Goodman Theatre](/wiki/Goodman_Theatre) in 2012, Lane assumed the role of Hickey, with [Brian Dennehy](/wiki/Brian_Dennehy) playing the role of Larry Slade in a production directed by the Goodman's Artistic Director, [Robert Falls](/wiki/Robert_Falls). Receiving rave reviews, it won six [Jeff Awards](/wiki/Jeff_Award), including Best Ensemble, director, and Production, and is the most successful play to date in the theater's history.

From 2010 to 2019, Lane portrayed Pepper Saltzman in the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) sitcom *[Modern Family](/wiki/Modern_Family)* for which he received three [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) nominations. From 2012 to 2014 he played Clarke Hayden in the legal series *[The Good Wife](/wiki/The_Good_Wife)* receiving a nomination for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Drama Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series). In the spring of 2013, Lane returned to Broadway in *[The Nance](/wiki/The_Nance)*, a [Lincoln Center](/wiki/Lincoln_Center) production of a new play by [Douglas Carter Beane](/wiki/Douglas_Carter_Beane) that was directed by [Jack O'Brien.](/wiki/Jack_O%27Brien_(director)) David Rooney of *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* praised his performance writing, "Lane is masterful, finding new depths in a well-worn sad clown persona" adding, "[The production] at the very least it provides a tremendous vehicle for Lane". He went on to receive Tony and Drama Desk Award nominations and won the Outer Critics Circle Award and the 2013 Drama League Award for Distinguished Performance. The play aired on PBS *[Live from Lincoln Center](/wiki/Live_from_Lincoln_Center)* in 2014.

In autumn 2014, he appeared in an all-star ensemble of Terrence McNally's revised and updated *[It's Only a Play](/wiki/It%27s_Only_a_Play)*, with [F. Murray Abraham](/wiki/F._Murray_Abraham), [Matthew Broderick](/wiki/Matthew_Broderick), [Stockard Channing](/wiki/Stockard_Channing), [Rupert Grint](/wiki/Rupert_Grint), [Megan Mullally](/wiki/Megan_Mullally), and [Micah Stock](/wiki/Micah_Stock). The show became one of the biggest hits of the season. In February 2015 he reprised the role of Hickey in the Robert Falls production of *The Iceman Cometh* to great acclaim at the [Brooklyn Academy of Music](/wiki/Brooklyn_Academy_of_Music). *[New York Post](/wiki/New_York_Post)* film critic Elizabeth Vincentelli wrote of his performance, "Lane, one of his generation's most brilliant comic actors...[hits] the sweet spot between pretend perkiness and self-loathing". He later returned to the Broadway run of *It's Only a Play*. In 2015, he received the Eugene O'Neill Theater Center Monte Cristo Award for his body of work. In March 2016, he opened the play *[White Rabbit, Red Rabbit](/wiki/White_Rabbit,_Red_Rabbit)* off Broadway.

Lane portrayed [Roy Cohn](/wiki/Roy_Cohn) in the revival of *[Angels in America](/wiki/Angels_in_America)* in 2018

Lane played [F. Lee Bailey](/wiki/F._Lee_Bailey) in *[The People v. O. J. Simpson: American Crime Story](/wiki/The_People_v._O._J._Simpson:_American_Crime_Story)*, the first season of *[American Crime Story](/wiki/American_Crime_Story)*, which premiered on the [FX](/wiki/FX_(TV_channel)) channel in February 2016. Daniel Feinberg of *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* described his performance as "understatedly Machiavellian". Emily St. Jones of *[Vox](/wiki/Vox_(website))* declared Lane as "hugely enjoyable" in the series.
It received 22 Emmy nominations and went on to win the [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) for [Outstanding Limited Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Limited_Series). In fall of 2016, he returned to Broadway to rave reviews in an all-star revival of Hecht and MacArthur's *[The Front Page](/wiki/The_Front_Page)*, directed by Jack O'Brien and produced by Scott Rudin. He played the ruthless editor Walter Burns opposite [John Slattery](/wiki/John_Slattery) as Hildy Johnson and [John Goodman](/wiki/John_Goodman) as Sheriff Hartman, for which he received a nomination for the [Tony Award for Best Featured Actor in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actor_in_a_Play). He also received nominations for Drama Desk and Outer Critics Circle Awards. During this time he also guest starred on series such as *[Difficult People](/wiki/Difficult_People)* (2016) and *[The Blacklist](/wiki/The_Blacklist)* (2018).

Next he played [Roy Cohn](/wiki/Roy_Cohn) with [Andrew Garfield](/wiki/Andrew_Garfield) as Prior Walter in the revival of *[Angels in America](/wiki/Angels_in_America)*, directed by [Marianne Elliott](/wiki/Marianne_Elliott) at the [Lyttlelton Theatre](/wiki/Lyttelton_Theatre) of the [National Theatre of Great Britain](/wiki/National_Theatre_of_Great_Britain). Lane reprised his acclaimed portrayal on Broadway at the [Neil Simon Theatre](/wiki/Neil_Simon_Theatre), and won the Tony, Drama Desk, and Outer Critics Circle Awards for Best Featured Actor in a Play. In March 2019, Lane starred in [Taylor Mac](/wiki/Taylor_Mac)'s absurdist black comedy *[Gary: A Sequel to Titus Andronicus](/wiki/Gary:_A_Sequel_to_Titus_Andronicus)* at the [Booth Theatre](/wiki/Booth_Theatre) directed by [George C. Wolfe](/wiki/George_C._Wolfe). The play received seven [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) nominations, including [Best Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Play).

### 2020–present

Lane played the role of Lewis Michener on Showtime's *[Penny Dreadful: City of Angels](/wiki/Penny_Dreadful:_City_of_Angels)* which premiered April 26, 2020, and ran for one season. He has a recurring role in the Hulu series *[Only Murders in the Building](/wiki/Only_Murders_in_the_Building)*, starring [Steve Martin](/wiki/Steve_Martin), [Martin Short](/wiki/Martin_Short), and [Selena Gomez](/wiki/Selena_Gomez), for which he received a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series). That was Lane's first Primetime Emmy Award after a record-breaking seven nominations in the guest actor categories, making him the most nominated Comedy Guest Actor in Emmy history, a record he still holds after receiving his eighth nomination in 2023 in the same category. He also plays the recurring role of [Ward McAllister](/wiki/Ward_McAllister) in the HBO period series, *[The Gilded Age](/wiki/The_Gilded_Age_(TV_series))*, written by [Julian Fellowes](/wiki/Julian_Fellowes), which received a 2024 [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) nomination for [Outstanding Ensemble in a Drama Series](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_an_Ensemble_in_a_Drama_Series) and a 2024 Emmy nomination for Best Drama. In 2023, Lane returned to the [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) stage, marking his 25th [Broadway show](/wiki/Broadway_show), in *[Pictures from Home](/wiki/Pictures_from_Home)*, a play adapted from the photo memoir by [Larry Sultan](/wiki/Larry_Sultan). Lane portrayed the father and former razor blade salesman to his son a photographer, played by [Danny Burstein](/wiki/Danny_Burstein), who's remembering his visits with his family. Lane's wife in the play was portrayed by [Zoë Wanamaker](/wiki/Zo%C3%AB_Wanamaker). The production was directed by [Bartlett Sher](/wiki/Bartlett_Sher) and was helmed at the [Studio 54](/wiki/Studio_54) theatre. The play received mixed reviews but praise for Lane's performance with Marilyn Stasio of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* writing, "Lane and Burstein are consummate pros, and there are considerable sparks of familial communication between the father and son they play with such warmth and understanding."

Also in 2023, Lane co-starred in [Ari Aster](/wiki/Ari_Aster)'s new [A24](/wiki/A24) film, *[Beau Is Afraid](/wiki/Beau_Is_Afraid)* alongside [Joaquin Phoenix](/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix), [Amy Ryan](/wiki/Amy_Ryan), and [Patti LuPone](/wiki/Patti_LuPone). Max Ceo of *[Esquire](/wiki/Esquire_(magazine))* praised Aster for the casting of Lane writing, "There's a palpable sense that the director had seasoned character actors such as Nathan Lane in his mind while writing. He milks every dad-ish 'My dude' the script hands him".

He co-starred in another A24 film, *[Dicks: The Musical](/wiki/Dicks:_The_Musical)*, directed by [Larry Charles](/wiki/Larry_Charles) and written by Aaron Jackson and Josh Sharp, with the film based on their [Upright Citizens Brigade](/wiki/Upright_Citizens_Brigade) musical stage show *Fucking Identical Twins*. Jackson and Sharp play the twins with Lane and [Megan Mullally](/wiki/Megan_Mullally) as the parents. It also features [Bowen Yang](/wiki/Bowen_Yang) and [Megan Thee Stallion](/wiki/Megan_Thee_Stallion). The film premiered at the [2023 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2023_Toronto_International_Film_Festival) to positive reviews and won the Midnight Madness People's Choice Award. Kristy Puchko of *[Mashable](/wiki/Mashable)* wrote, "[Lane] who stole scenes earlier this year as a plucky papa in another [A24](/wiki/A24) movie *Beau is Afraid*—gives his all, committing to bit after bit" adding "In a career of superb comedy, he's in top form here". He was part of the voice cast for *[Spellbound](/wiki/Spellbound_(2024_film))*, a new animated film from [Skydance](/wiki/Skydance) for [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix), with [Rachel Zegler](/wiki/Rachel_Zegler), [Nicole Kidman](/wiki/Nicole_Kidman), [Javier Bardem](/wiki/Javier_Bardem), [Jenifer Lewis](/wiki/Jenifer_Lewis) and [John Lithgow](/wiki/John_Lithgow), as well as the [Ryan Murphy](/wiki/Ryan_Murphy_(producer)) miniseries, *[Monsters: The Lyle and Erik Menendez Story](/wiki/Monsters:_The_Lyle_and_Erik_Menendez_Story)*, as [Dominick Dunne](/wiki/Dominick_Dunne). He then starred in the new [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) multi-cam comedy, *[Mid-Century Modern](/wiki/Mid-Century_Modern_(TV_series))*, created by [Max Mutchnick](/wiki/Max_Mutchnick) and [David Kohan](/wiki/David_Kohan) of *[Will & Grace](/wiki/Will_%26_Grace)* and also produced by Ryan Murphy, alongside [Matt Bomer](/wiki/Matt_Bomer), [Nathan Lee Graham](/wiki/Nathan_Lee_Graham), and [Linda Lavin](/wiki/Linda_Lavin) in her final role.

In 2026, he returned to the Broadway stage portraying [Willy Loman](/wiki/Willy_Loman) in the critically acclaimed revival of the [Arthur Miller](/wiki/Arthur_Miller) play *[Death of a Salesman](/wiki/Death_of_a_Salesman)* opposite [Laurie Metcalf](/wiki/Laurie_Metcalf), [Christopher Abbott](/wiki/Christopher_Abbott), and [Ben Ahlers](/wiki/Ben_Ahlers).

In March 2026, as part of his final appearance, Lane performed the song 'Laughing Matters' from the 1996 off-Broadway revue *[When Pigs Fly](/wiki/When_Pigs_Fly_(musical))* on *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert)*, which had been canceled by [CBS](/wiki/CBS) in July 2025. He was accompanied on piano by [Marc Shaiman](/wiki/Marc_Shaiman).

## Personal life

### Identity and marriage

Lane says that when he told his mother at age 21 that he was [gay](/wiki/Homosexuality), she said, "I'd rather you were dead." He replied, "I knew you'd understand." He joked that "once I got her head out of the oven, everything went fine." He [came out](/wiki/Came_out) publicly in 1999 after the killing of [Matthew Shepard](/wiki/Matthew_Shepard) and has been a long-time board member of and fundraiser for [Broadway Cares/Equity Fights AIDS](/wiki/Broadway_Cares/Equity_Fights_AIDS). He has been honored with the Human Rights Campaign Equality Award, the Gay & Lesbian Alliance Against Defamation Vito Russo Award, The Trevor Project Hero Award, and the Matthew Shepard Foundation Making A Difference Award for his work in the [LGBT](/wiki/LGBT) community in 2015. On November 17, 2015, he married his partner of 18 years, theater producer and writer Devlin Elliott. They live in [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan) and [East Hampton, New York](/wiki/East_Hampton,_New_York).

### Political orientation

Lane has made various critical statements about [Republican Party](/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) figures. He was an active supporter of [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) and [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) and has hosted fundraisers for the [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)).

## Acting credits

Main article: [Nathan Lane on screen and stage](/wiki/Nathan_Lane_on_screen_and_stage)

Lane has had an extensive career in film, television, and in theater. He has appeared in such films as *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)* (1994), *[The Birdcage](/wiki/The_Birdcage)* (1996), *[Mouse Hunt](/wiki/Mouse_Hunt)* (1997), *Nicholas Nickleby* (2002), and the film adaptation of the Broadway musical *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(2005_film))* (2005). He is also known for numerous guest roles including *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)*, *[Mad About You](/wiki/Mad_About_You)*, *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)*, *[Absolutely Fabulous](/wiki/Absolutely_Fabulous)*, *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, *[The Blacklist](/wiki/The_Blacklist)* and recurring roles on *[Modern Family](/wiki/Modern_Family)* and *[The Good Wife](/wiki/The_Good_Wife)*. He has received critical praise for his roles as [F. Lee Bailey](/wiki/F._Lee_Bailey) in the limited series *[The People v. O.J. Simpson](/wiki/The_People_v._O._J._Simpson:_American_Crime_Story)* (2016) and in the 2020 Showtime series *[Penny Dreadful: City of Angels](/wiki/Penny_Dreadful:_City_of_Angels)* as Det. Lewis Michener. His roles in theatre range from musical comedies, *[Guys and Dolls](/wiki/Guys_and_Dolls)* (1992), *[A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum](/wiki/A_Funny_Thing_Happened_on_the_Way_to_the_Forum)* (1996), *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))* (2001) and *[The Addams Family](/wiki/The_Addams_Family_(musical))* (2010) to dramatic roles in the work of Terrence McNally, Jon Robin Baitz, and Simon Gray as well as revivals and new plays such as *[The Odd Couple](/wiki/The_Odd_Couple_(play))* (2005), *[November](/wiki/November_(play))* (2008), *[Waiting for Godot](/wiki/Waiting_for_Godot)* (2009), *[The Nance](/wiki/The_Nance)* (2013), *[It's Only a Play](/wiki/It%27s_Only_a_Play)* (2015), *[The Iceman Cometh](/wiki/The_Iceman_Cometh)* (2015), *[The Front Page](/wiki/The_Front_Page)* (2016), *[Angels in America](/wiki/Angels_in_America)* (2018), *[Gary: A Sequel to Titus Andronicus](/wiki/Gary:_A_Sequel_to_Titus_Andronicus)* (2019), *[Pictures From Home](/wiki/Pictures_From_Home)* (2023), and *[Death of a Salesman](/wiki/Death_of_a_Salesman)* (2026).

## Awards and honors

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Nathan Lane](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Nathan_Lane)

Lane has received seven [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) nominations for his work on [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)), winning three times for *[A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum](/wiki/A_Funny_Thing_Happened_on_the_Way_to_the_Forum)* (1996), *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))* (2001), and *[Angels in America](/wiki/Angels_in_America)* (2018). Also for his work in theatre he has received seven [Drama Desk Awards](/wiki/Drama_Desk_Awards), six [Outer Critics Circle Awards](/wiki/Outer_Critics_Circle_Awards), two [Obies](/wiki/Obies), the [Lucille Lortel Award](/wiki/Lucille_Lortel_Award), the [Drama League Award for Distinguished Achievement in Musical Theatre](/wiki/Drama_League_Award_for_Distinguished_Achievement_in_Musical_Theatre), the [Drama League Award for Distinguished Performance](/wiki/Drama_League_Award_for_Distinguished_Performance) for *The Nance*, the [Theatre World John Willis Award](/wiki/Theatre_World_Award) for Lifetime Achievement in the Theater, the [Eugene O'Neill Monte Cristo Award](/wiki/Eugene_O%27Neill_Award), the [New Dramatists Career Achievement Award](/wiki/New_Dramatists), the Sir Peter Ustinov Comedy Award, and the [Laurence Olivier Award](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award). In 2024 Lane received the Stephen Sondheim Award from the [Signature Theatre Company](/wiki/Signature_Theatre_Company) and the [Harold S. Prince Award for Lifetime Achievement](/wiki/Drama_Desk_Special_Award) from the [Drama Desk Awards](/wiki/Drama_Desk_Awards).

Lane has received two [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nominations for *The Birdcage* and *The Producers*, the [National Board of Review](/wiki/National_Board_of_Review) Award for Ensemble Acting for *[Nicholas Nickleby](/wiki/Nicholas_Nickleby)*, and two [Actor Awards](/wiki/Actor_Awards) nominations for [Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Supporting Role](/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role) and for [Outstanding Performance by a Cast in a Motion Picture](/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Cast_in_a_Motion_Picture) for *The Birdcage*, winning the latter. For his work on television Lane has received eight [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nominations for guest starring roles on *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)*, *[Mad About You](/wiki/Mad_About_You)*, *[Modern Family](/wiki/Modern_Family)*, and *[The Good Wife](/wiki/The_Good_Wife)* and won for *[Only Murders in the Building](/wiki/Only_Murders_in_the_Building)*. He received two [Daytime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Awards) for his voice work in *[Timon & Pumbaa](/wiki/Timon_%26_Pumbaa_(TV_series))* and *[Teacher's Pet](/wiki/Teacher%27s_Pet_(TV_series))*, as well as a nomination for *[George and Martha](/wiki/George_and_Martha)* for HBO. He has received the [People's Choice Award](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Award) for Favorite New Actor in a Comedy and an [American Comedy Award](/wiki/American_Comedy_Award) for *The Birdcage* as well as a nomination for *Jeffrey*.

# Kevin S. Bright

## Infobox

- **Notable work:** Dream On Friends

**Kevin S. Bright** (born November 15, 1954) is an American television [executive producer](/wiki/Executive_producer) and [director](/wiki/Television_director). He is best known as the showrunner of the sitcoms *[Dream On](/wiki/Dream_On_(TV_series))* and *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*.

## Early life

Born to a [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish)-American family in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City), Bright attended the [East Side Hebrew Institute](/wiki/East_Side_Hebrew_Institute) on the [Lower East Side](/wiki/Lower_East_Side) of [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan) and graduated *[magna cum laude](/wiki/Magna_cum_laude)* from [Emerson College](/wiki/Emerson_College).

## Career

Bright started his professional career under the tutelage of his father, Jackie Bright. After graduation, he worked in New York with Joseph Cates, where he produced specials for [George Burns](/wiki/George_Burns), [Johnny Cash](/wiki/Johnny_Cash), [David Copperfield](/wiki/David_Copperfield_(illusionist)), and [Dolly Parton](/wiki/Dolly_Parton). After moving to Los Angeles in 1982, he started work in comedy programming such as *[The History of White People in America](/wiki/The_History_of_White_People_in_America)* and comedy specials starring [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams), [Martin Mull](/wiki/Martin_Mull), [Harry Shearer](/wiki/Harry_Shearer), [Paul Shaffer](/wiki/Paul_Shaffer), and [Merrill Markoe](/wiki/Merrill_Markoe).

In 1993, Bright entered a partnership with [Marta Kauffman](/wiki/Marta_Kauffman) and [David Crane](/wiki/David_Crane_(producer)) to form Bright/Kauffman/Crane Productions and began a development deal with [Warner Bros. Television](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Television) to produce the comedy series *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*. He also directed 60 episodes of the series, including the series finale. After *Friends*, he went on to executive-produce the spin-off series *[Joey](/wiki/Joey_(TV_series))* with *Friends* producers [Shana Goldberg-Meehan](/wiki/Shana_Goldberg-Meehan) and [Scott Silveri](/wiki/Scott_Silveri). *Joey* starred *Friends* actor [Matt LeBlanc](/wiki/Matt_LeBlanc) as the title character and featured [Jennifer Coolidge](/wiki/Jennifer_Coolidge), also an [Emerson College](/wiki/Emerson_College) attendee. *Joey* was cancelled on May 15, 2006, during its second season after a major ratings slump.

After *Joey*, Bright moved back to [Boston](/wiki/Boston) where he began working at his alma mater, Emerson College. Over the last four years at Emerson, he executive produced three-sketch comedy shows, *Zebro: A Laugh Show* and *Chocolate Cake City*, four original half-hour situation comedies, *Browne At Midnight*, *Saturdays*, *Ground Floor*, and *Record Cellar*, and a live multi-cam stand-up comedy special, *Die Laughing*. He also serves as an advisor to The EVVY Awards.

Bright then went on to teach a series of television production classes in the Visual Media Arts department, and helped develop the program for Emerson's new LA Center, which opened in 2013. Kevin ran a diversity workshop for high school students through Emerson College, and worked with [Perkins School for the Blind](/wiki/Perkins_School_for_the_Blind) in [Watertown, Massachusetts](/wiki/Watertown,_Massachusetts), to develop a method of teaching television production to the blind.

Bright moved back to Los Angeles in 2013, when he was appointed as Founding Director of the Emerson Los Angeles program. There, he has focused on building new programs that take full advantage of the opportunities Emerson's expanded presence in Los Angeles provides. He directed a documentary in 2007 with [Linda Feferman](/wiki/Linda_Feferman) called *Who Ordered Tax?* about his father, Jackie, who was an actor and vaudevillian performer.

In 2016, Bright served as the executive producer of the documentary *[Best and Most Beautiful Things](/wiki/Best_and_Most_Beautiful_Things)* about Michelle Smith, a woman from [Bangor, Maine](/wiki/Bangor,_Maine) who is both legally blind and autistic. Bright also directed on [CBS](/wiki/CBS) comedy *[Man with a Plan](/wiki/Man_with_a_Plan_(TV_series))* in 2019 for one episode, reuniting with *Friends* alum [Matt LeBlanc](/wiki/Matt_LeBlanc).

In 2024, Bright served as an executive producer of the documentary *My Own Normal* about [Alexander Freeman](/wiki/Alexander_Freeman_(film_director)), a filmmaker from [Newton, Massachusetts](/wiki/Newton,_Massachusetts) who has [cerebral palsy](/wiki/Cerebral_palsy), following his journey of becoming a partner and father and confronting the pain of his parents' reaction. The documentary world premiered at [Independent Film Festival Boston](/wiki/Independent_Film_Festival_Boston).

## Personal life

Bright lives with his wife, Claudia Wilsey Bright in [Saratoga Springs, New York](/wiki/Saratoga_Springs,_New_York).

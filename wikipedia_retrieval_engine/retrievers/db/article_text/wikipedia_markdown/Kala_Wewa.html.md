# Kala Wewa

## Infobox

- **Location:** Anuradhapura District, Sri Lanka
- **Type:** Reservoir
- **Primary outflows:** Transferred water to Thisa Wewa via Jaya Ganga
- **Basin countries:** Sri Lanka
- **Surface area:** 7 square miles (18.1 km²) at full capacity
- **Water volume:** 123 million cubic meters (4 billion cubic feet)
- **Shore length 1:** 40 miles (64.4 km)

**Kala Wewa** ([Sinhala](/wiki/Sinhala_language):කලා වැව), built by [King Datusena](/wiki/Dhatusena_of_Anuradhapura) in 460 [CE](/wiki/Common_Era), is a [reservoir](/wiki/Reservoir) complex consisting of two reservoirs, Kala Wewa and Balalu Wewa. It has the capacity to store 123 million cubic meters of water. The reservoir complex includes a stone made [spillway](/wiki/Spillway) and three main [sluices](/wiki/Sluice). The main central sluice, 40 [feet](/wiki/Foot_(unit)) wide, supplies water to thousands of acres of [paddy fields](/wiki/Paddy_Field) and ends at [Tissa Wewa](/wiki/Tissa_Wewa_(Anuradhapura)), in the ancient city of [Anuradhapura](/wiki/Capital_Anuradhapura), having meandered over 87 km (54 mi) at a slope of 6 inches per mile. The [hydraulic engineering](/wiki/Hydraulic_engineering) reflects the advanced technology that existed in ancient [Sri Lanka](/wiki/Sri_Lanka).

## History

This [reservoir](/wiki/Reservoir) was built by [King Dhatusena](/wiki/King_Dhatusena), who ruled the country from 454 to 473 CE.

[Tamil](/wiki/Tamil_people) invaders who arrived from [South India](/wiki/South_India) ruled the north part of the country during the period from 429 to 455 CE. King Dhatusena repelled the invaders and united the country. He wanted to rebuild the [irrigation system](/wiki/Tank_cascade_system) by constructing several [tanks](/wiki/Water_tank) and [canals](/wiki/Canals) in and around the city of [Anuradhapura](/wiki/Anuradhapura).

After the construction of Kala Wewa was completed, the king built another tank called *Balalu Wewa* ([Sinhala](/wiki/Sinhala_language): බලලු වැව) nearby and connected the two tanks together, with the resulting combined tank being the largest in [Sri Lanka](/wiki/Sri_Lanka). [King Mahinda II](/wiki/Mahinda_II_of_Anuradhapura), who ruled the country from 777 to 797 CE, expanded the tank further. Water of the tank was transferred to [Tissa Wewa](/wiki/Tissa_Wewa_(Anuradhapura)) ([Sinhala](/wiki/Sinhala_language): තිසා වැව) in [Anuradhapura](/wiki/Anuradhapura) by an ancient 54-mile (86.9 km) canal, [Yodha Ela](/wiki/Yodha_Ela_(Jaya_Ganga)) ([Sinhala](/wiki/Sinhala_language): යෝධ ඇල), with an average gradient of 6 inches per mile (9.5 cm/km) over the first 17 miles (27 km).

## Legends

King [Dhathusena](/wiki/Dhathusena) was very keen on information with regard to a spot very suitable to construct a tank to be the massive one in the history of [Sri Lanka](/wiki/Sri_Lanka). There are some [folklore](/wiki/Folklore) on how the king was able to find a place for the tank he imagined. There was a man called *Kadawara* who left his family and went to live in the jungle due to his wife's unbearable and repeated insults and disrespects towards him. After some years in the jungle he was well accustomed with wild animals and lived with a flock of deer. One day a hunter suddenly noticed this strange man living with animals in the jungle; went to the palace and told the king that it seems that this strange man lives in the forest in order to guard an unknown treasure there. King sent his army to catch him. *Kadawara* was caught and brought to the palace. When the king questioned him of the treasure, *Kadawara* revealed his true story and told real reason for his leaving the city and living in the jungle. Then king asked him of any interesting thing he had seen while living in the jungle. *Kadawa* said, "No sir, I have not seen anything interesting but in a brook somewhere in the jungle, water is being blocked by the flora called ***Kala*** that has been grown across that stream. According to this legend, it was the spot wherein the king created the **Kala** Wewa.

## Renovation

First restoration to the tank is done by [King](/wiki/King) [Parakramabahu I](/wiki/Parakramabahu_I) in the 12th century. The tank was renovated several times in the past as in the period of [British](/wiki/British_Raj) [Governor](/wiki/Governor) Sir [William Henry Gregory](/wiki/William_Henry_Gregory) (1872–1877
and Sir [Arthur Hamilton-Gordon](/wiki/Arthur_Hamilton-Gordon,_1st_Baron_Stanmore) (1883–1890). After the British rule in the country and in 1958, the tank's [bund](/wiki/Bunding) was reconstructed connecting the tank with *Balalu Wewa*.

## Size

Its [circumference](/wiki/Circumference) is 40 miles (64.4 km) and has a total area of 7 square miles (18.1 km2) at full capacity. [Length](/wiki/Length) of the [dam](/wiki/Dam) is 22,572 feet (6,879.9 m) and the [height](/wiki/Height) is 48 feet (14.6 m).

## Attraction

There is a 12 meter high standing [statue](/wiki/Statue) of [Buddha](/wiki/Buddha) created by same ruler. This statue is named after the [village](/wiki/Village) it is situated so it is called [Avukana Buddha statue](/wiki/Avukana_Buddha_statue) ([Sinhala](/wiki/Sinhala_language): අව්කන බුදු පිළිමය) and it can be seen over-looking at the [tank](/wiki/Tank) near by.

## Purpose

The [reservoir](/wiki/Reservoir) served as one of largest [irrigation tanks](/wiki/Irrigation_tanks) in ancient time. While supplying water also for the small [tanks](/wiki/Tanks) in rural areas on the way, the [canal](/wiki/Canal) Jaya Ganga carried water from Kala Wewa and stored enough water in the Thisā Wewa for the population of then [capital city](/wiki/Capital_city) of Anuradhapura.well

Being one of main storages in the [Mahaweli Irrigation Scheme](/wiki/Mahaweli_River) since 1976, the [tank](/wiki/Tank) serves to the population in the [North Central](/wiki/North_Central_Province,_Sri_Lanka) Sri Lanka. It is used for [fresh water](/wiki/Fresh_water) [fishing](/wiki/Fishing) and the [flora](/wiki/Flora), specially the [grasses](/wiki/Grasses) in its [valley](/wiki/Valley), is the main sources of [silage](/wiki/Silage) for the herds of [cattle](/wiki/Cattle) in the area.

## Route

The way to [Anuradhapura](/wiki/Anuradhapura) via [Dambulla](/wiki/Dambulla) reaches *Kekirawa* in the [Kekirawa Divisional Secretariat](/wiki/Kekirawa_Divisional_Secretariat) and from there the distance to the [tank](/wiki/Tank) is 9.7 kilometres (6 mi).

Kalawewa Railway Station

[Kalawewa Railway Station](/wiki/Kalawewa_Railway_Station) can be reached by train from [Colombo Fort](/wiki/Colombo_Fort) to [Trincomalee](/wiki/Trincomalee) or from Colombo Fort to [Batticaloa](/wiki/Batticaloa) via [Maho Junction Railway Station](/wiki/Maho_railway_station).

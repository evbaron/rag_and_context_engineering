# Mons Hansteen

**Mons Hansteen** is a mountain on the [Moon](/wiki/Moon), also known as Hansteen Alpha (α), named after [Christopher Hansteen](/wiki/Christopher_Hansteen). It is roughly triangular in shape and occupies an area about 30 km across on the western margin of [Oceanus Procellarum](/wiki/Oceanus_Procellarum), southeast of the crater [Hansteen](/wiki/Hansteen_(crater)) and north of the dark-floored crater [Billy](/wiki/Billy_(crater)). It is thought to be an extrusion of volcanic material that is younger than the crater Hansteen, with most of surface volcanic ash been deposited 3.5-3.74 billion years ago.

The Mons Hansteen belongs to rare class of non-mare moon volcanoes.

## Composition

The extruded material is significantly enriched in silica, and strongly depleted in iron and titanium oxides compared to surrounding mare terrain. Initially, it was believed its lavas are not as evolved as in [Compton–Belkovich Thorium Anomaly](/wiki/Compton%E2%80%93Belkovich_Thorium_Anomaly), but by 2004 the low thorium enrichment signal was attributed to small size of volcanic complex, making it composition compatible with highly processed silicic lavas. By 2013, a patchy deposits of magnesium [spinel](/wiki/Spinel) were detected in the central and western parts of the volcanic dome. The spinel-rich areas are associated with boulder fields, and fringed by areas showing a sign of hydration, with 4-8% of mineral by weight been hydroxylated.

The average bulk density of material in Mons Hansteen is extremely low, been equal only to 1500–2000 kg/m3.

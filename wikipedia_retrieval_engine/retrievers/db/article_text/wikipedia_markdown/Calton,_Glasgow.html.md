**Calton** ([Scottish Gaelic](/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic_language): *A' Challtainn*, [lit.](/wiki/Literal_translation) 'the hazel wood', [Scots](/wiki/Scots_language): *Caltoun*), known locally as **The Calton**, is a district in [Glasgow](/wiki/Glasgow). It is situated north of the [River Clyde](/wiki/River_Clyde), and just to the east of the city centre. Calton's most famous landmark is the [Barras](/wiki/Barras_(market)) street market and the [Barrowland Ballroom](/wiki/Barrowland_Ballroom), one of Glasgow's principal musical venues.

No official definition of Calton's boundaries exist, notionally it can be thought of as the roughly trapezoidal area bounded by the [River Clyde](/wiki/River_Clyde) to the south, Abercromby Street to the east (where it borders both [Camlachie](/wiki/Camlachie) and [Bridgeton](/wiki/Bridgeton,_Glasgow)), and the [City Union Line](/wiki/City_Union_Line) railway to the north and [High Street](/wiki/High_Street,_Glasgow)/[Saltmarket](/wiki/Saltmarket) to the west.

## History

The former Templeton's Carpet Factory in the Calton, designed to resemble the Doge's Palace in Venice. Photo taken 2004.

The area was a [Burgh of Barony](/wiki/Burgh_of_Barony) from 1817 to 1846, when it was annexed to Glasgow.

The lands of Blackfaulds, on which Calton now stands, originally formed part of the lands of the [Archbishopric](/wiki/Archbishopric) of Glasgow, but were annexed to [The Crown](/wiki/The_Crown) in 1587. In 1705 the owner, John Walkinshaw, began to [feu](/wiki/Feu_(land_tenure)) the lands of Blackfaulds (part of the [Barrowfield](/wiki/Barrowfield) estate) on which the old village of Calton was built, and in 1817 a [charter](/wiki/Charter) was granted, erecting Calton into a [Burgh](/wiki/Burgh). During the area's time as an independent burgh, there were four [Provosts](/wiki/Provost_(civil)) of Calton:

- Robert Struthers (1817–1818)
- Nathaniel Stevenson (1818–1839)
- Robert Bartholomew (1839–1843)
- William Bankier (1843–1846)

### The Calton martyrs

Main article: [Calton weavers' strike](/wiki/Calton_weavers%27_strike)

The area became known for its [weaving](/wiki/Weaving) industry. On 30 June 1787, a meeting of [Calton weavers](/wiki/Calton_weavers) was held on [Glasgow Green](/wiki/Glasgow_Green). Their [wages](/wiki/Wages) had dropped because of the increased importation of cheaper foreign [textiles](/wiki/Textiles). Most of the workers decided to take [strike action](/wiki/Strike_action), although some accepted lower wages and carried on working. The dispute came to a head on 3 September 1787: when violence erupted after some striking weavers tried to seize materials from weavers who had carried on working.

The [military](/wiki/Military) were called in and a detachment of the [39th Regiment of Foot](/wiki/39th_Regiment_of_Foot) opened fire on the demonstrators. Six of the men killed at the scene were locally called '[martyrs](/wiki/Martyr)' and some of them were buried in the Calton [Cemetery](/wiki/Cemetery) off the main London Road. The families of the men could not afford a [headstone](/wiki/Headstone) although, a century later, a memorial was raised to commemorate their actions.

## Social problems

The Calton Bar on the London Road in 2008

[Comedian](/wiki/Comedian) [Janey Godley](/wiki/Janey_Godley), in her 2005 [autobiography](/wiki/Autobiography) *Handstands in the Dark*, wrote about the 14 years she spent running a Calton [pub](/wiki/Pub), the Weavers Inn (formerly the Nationalist Bar, now the Calton Bar). Her book details life there in the 1980s and 1990s, a time when the area became notorious for [heroin](/wiki/Heroin) abuse and when [urban renewal](/wiki/Urban_renewal) began.

Calton is an area of considerable poverty and [multiple deprivation](/wiki/Scottish_Index_of_Multiple_Deprivation) regarding; "*current income, employment, health, education, skills and training, housing, geographic access and crime*." In January 2006, a report into poverty in [The Scotsman](/wiki/The_Scotsman) newspaper stated that "a child born in Calton... is three times as likely to suffer [heart disease](/wiki/Heart_disease), four times as likely to be hospitalised and ten times as likely to grow up in a workless household than a child in the city's prosperous western [suburbs](/wiki/Suburbs)". Calton has the lowest male [life expectancy](/wiki/Life_expectancy) in Scotland. A [BBC Scotland](/wiki/BBC_Scotland) news report on 13 February 2006 pointed out that, partially due to poor diet, crime, [alcohol](/wiki/Alcohol_(drug)) and [drug abuse](/wiki/Drug_abuse), life expectancy in Calton is lower than in some areas of [Iraq](/wiki/Iraq) or the [Gaza Strip](/wiki/Gaza_Strip). A news report in the 21 January 2006 edition of [The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian) newspaper gave the average lifespan of a person living in the Calton area as 53.9 years against a city average of 69 years and the Scottish average of 78. A 2008 [World Health Organization](/wiki/World_Health_Organization) report contrasted Calton's male life expectancy, reported as standing at 54, with that of nearby [Lenzie](/wiki/Lenzie), [East Dunbartonshire](/wiki/East_Dunbartonshire), at 82. Most of the affordable housing is owned by [Housing Associations](/wiki/Housing_Association) with a high percentage of [tenants](/wiki/Tenants) on [housing benefit](/wiki/Housing_benefit) (For example, Thenue Housing Association has approximately 75% of tenants on housing benefit). [The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian) writes that higher risk of dying before they are 65 (up to 30% in comparison with [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool) and [Manchester](/wiki/Manchester)) occurred because of rehousing skilled workers ([cream skimming](/wiki/Cream_skimming)) due to 1970s policy documents.

Tongland graffiti in the Calton. Photo taken 2004.

The area has experienced [sectarian](/wiki/Sectarian) tensions for generations; it is a predominantly Catholic area (due to Irish immigration), and there are also [Irish republican](/wiki/Irish_republican) supporter groups present. This is reflected, albeit much declined in modern times, in gang and sectarian related [graffiti](/wiki/Graffiti), with the main gang being the Calton Tongs. In the 1960s, the Calton was known locally as [Tongland](/wiki/Tongland,_gang) (and still is by some), prominently marked out as such by graffiti. Tongland appears in [Gillies MacKinnon](/wiki/Gillies_MacKinnon)'s 1995 movie *[Small Faces](/wiki/Small_Faces_(film))*. The gangs' power over the area and their decline in the 1970s is described in *Handstands in the Dark*.

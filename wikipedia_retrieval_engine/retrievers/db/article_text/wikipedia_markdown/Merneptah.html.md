**Merneptah** ([/ˈmɛrnɛptɑː, mərˈnɛptɑː/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English)) or **Merenptah** (reigned 13 August 1213–2 May 1203 BCE) was the fourth pharaoh of the [Nineteenth Dynasty](/wiki/Nineteenth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) of [Ancient Egypt](/wiki/Ancient_Egypt). According to contemporary historical records, he ruled Egypt for almost ten years, from 13 August 1213 until his death on 2 May 1203. He was the first royal-born pharaoh since [Tutankhamun](/wiki/Tutankhamun) of the [Eighteenth Dynasty of Egypt](/wiki/Eighteenth_Dynasty_of_Egypt).

Merneptah was the thirteenth son of [Ramesses II](/wiki/Ramesses_II), only coming to power because all of his older brothers had died, including his full brother [Khaemweset](/wiki/Khaemweset).

He was around fifty years old when he ascended to the throne. He is arguably best known for the [Merneptah Stele](/wiki/Merneptah_Stele), featuring the first known mention of the name [Israel](/wiki/Israelites). His throne name was *Ba-en-re Mery-netjeru*, which means "[Soul](/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_conception_of_the_soul#Ba_(personality)) of [Ra](/wiki/Ra), Beloved of the Gods".

## Early years

### Family

See also: [Nineteenth Dynasty of Egypt family tree](/wiki/Nineteenth_Dynasty_of_Egypt_family_tree)

Merneptah was likely the fourth child born to [Isetnofret](/wiki/Isetnofret) and Ramesses II, and his thirteenth son. He was the first royal-born pharaoh since [Tutankhamun](/wiki/Tutankhamun). He married [Isetnofret II](/wiki/Isetnofret_II), who was likely his full sister or niece, who would become [Great Royal Wife](/wiki/Great_Royal_Wife) when he was named pharaoh. They had at least two sons, Merenptah, named after his father, and [Seti II](/wiki/Seti_II). When Seti II became pharaoh, Tausret became his Great Royal Wife. She became pharaoh in her own right after the death of pharaoh [Siptah](/wiki/Siptah).

[Takhat](/wiki/Takhat), the mother of [Amenmesse](/wiki/Amenmesse), may have been a secondary queen of Merneptah, though scholars are yet to confirm this.

### King's Son

Aswan Rockstela. Top: Ramesses II, Isetnofret and Khaemwaset before [Khnum](/wiki/Khnum). Bottom left to right: Merneptah, Bintanath and Prince Ramesses

Ramesses II lived well into his nineties and was one of the oldest pharaohs in Egyptian history. He outlived many of his heirs; eventually, Merneptah would be the son to succeed him. Merneptah would have been prepared to be pharaoh through the responsibility of his government roles.

Merneptah is depicted as a prince in [Aswan](/wiki/Aswan) on a royal family stela dating to **Year 30 of Ramesses II**, and on a family stela from the Speos of Horemheb at [Silsila](/wiki/Gebel_el-Silsila) dating to **Years 33/34 of Ramesses II**, On these stelae he is accompanied by his parents and arranged by age alongside his sister Bintanath and his brothers [Ramesses](/wiki/Ramesses_(prince)) and Khaemwaset.

However, other than that, we know virtually nothing else about Merneptah before **Year 40 of Ramesses II**, when he became Overseer of the Army (General).

### Crown Prince

In **Year 55 of Ramesses II**, he was officially proclaimed [crown prince](/wiki/Crown_prince). At that point, he gained additional responsibilities by serving as Prince [Regent](/wiki/Regent) for the last twelve years of Ramesses II's life.

## Reign

### Chronology

According to one reading of contemporary historical records, Merneptah ruled Egypt for almost ten years, from late July or early August 1213 BC until his death on 2 May 1203 BC.

### New capital

Merneptah moved Egypt's administrative center from [Pi-Ramesses](/wiki/Pi-Ramesses), his father's capital, back to [Memphis](/wiki/Memphis,_Egypt), where he constructed a royal palace next to the temple of [Ptah](/wiki/Ptah). The [Penn Museum](/wiki/Penn_Museum), led by [Clarence Stanley Fisher](/wiki/Clarence_Stanley_Fisher), excavated this palace in 1915.

### Campaigns

Limestone block showing a pair of unfinished cartouches of Merenptah (Merneptah) I, 19th dynasty of Egypt, Petrie Museum of Egyptian Archaeology, London

Merneptah had to carry out several [military campaigns](/wiki/Military_campaign) during his reign. In the fifth year of his rule, he fought against the [Libyans](/wiki/Ancient_Libya), who—with the assistance of the [Sea Peoples](/wiki/Sea_Peoples)—were threatening Egypt from the west. Merneptah led a [victorious six-hour battle](/wiki/Battle_of_Perire) against a combined Libyan and Sea People force at the city of Perire, probably located on the western edge of the [Nile Delta](/wiki/Nile_Delta). His [account of this campaign](/wiki/Great_Karnak_Inscription) against the Sea Peoples and [Libu](/wiki/Libu) is described in prose on a wall beside the sixth pylon at [Karnak](/wiki/Karnak), which states:

:   [Beginning of the victory that his majesty achieved in the land of Libya] -I, Ekwesh, Teresh, [Lukka](/wiki/Lycians), Sherden, Shekelesh, Northerners coming from all lands.

Later in the inscription, Merneptah receives news of the attack:

:   ... the third season, saying: 'The wretched, fallen chief of Libya, Meryre, son of Ded, has fallen upon the country of Tehenu with his bowmen—Sherden, Shekelesh, Ekwesh, Lukka, Teresh, Taking the best of every warrior and every man of war of his country. He has brought his wife and his children—leaders of the camp, and he has reached the western boundary in the fields of Perire.'

An inscription on the Athribis Stele, now in the garden of Cairo Museum, declares "His majesty was enraged at their report, like a lion", assembled his court, and gave a rousing speech. Later he dreamed that he saw [Ptah](/wiki/Ptah) handing him a sword and saying "Take thou (it) and banish thou the fearful heart from thee." When the bowmen went forth, says the inscription, "Amun was with them as a shield." After six hours the surviving [Nine Bows](/wiki/Nine_bows) threw down their weapons, abandoned their baggage and dependents, and ran for their lives. Merneptah states that he defeated the invasion, killing 6,000 soldiers and taking 9,000 prisoners. To be sure of the numbers, among other things, he took the penises of all uncircumcised enemy dead and the hands of all the [circumcised](/wiki/Circumcised), from which history learns that the [Ekwesh](/wiki/Ekwesh) were circumcised, a fact causing some to doubt that they were [Greek people](/wiki/Greek_people).

There is also an account of the same events in the form of a poem from the [Merneptah Stele](/wiki/Merneptah_Stele), also known as the *Israel Stele*, which mentions the suppression of revolts in Canaan and makes reference to the supposed utter destruction of [Israel](/wiki/Israelites) in a campaign prior to his fifth year, in [Canaan](/wiki/Canaan): "Israel has been wiped out ... its seed is no more." This is the first recognised ancient Egyptian record of the existence of Israel—"not as a country or city, but as a tribe" or people. A newly discovered massive layer of fiery destruction confirms Merneptah's boast about his Canaanite campaign.

## Death

As soon as Merneptah died the succession apparently had become a problem, he was put in his tomb KV 8 but later removed, and his mummy has survived.

### Succession

Main article: [End of the 19th Dynasty](/wiki/End_of_the_19th_Dynasty)

Stone sarcophagus of Merneptah in [KV8](/wiki/KV8)

Mummy studies show that Merneptah died at around sixty years of age. It is inferred that he was born between the 10th and 17th years of Ramesses II’s reign, and by the time he ascended the throne, he was likely already over fifty.

Merneptah's successor, [Seti II](/wiki/Seti_II), was a son of Queen [Isetnofret](/wiki/Isetnofret_II). However, Seti II's accession to the throne was not unchallenged: a rival king named [Amenmesse](/wiki/Amenmesse), who was either another son of Merneptah by Takhat or, much less likely, of Ramesses II, seized control of [Upper Egypt](/wiki/Upper_Egypt) and [Kush](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kush) during the middle of the reign of Seti II. Only after he overcame Amenmesse, was Seti able to reassert his authority over [Thebes](/wiki/Thebes,_Egypt) in his fifth year. It is possible that before seizing Upper Egypt, Amenmesse had been known as [Messuy](/wiki/Messuy) and had been viceroy of Kush.

### Mummy

Mummy of Merneptah

Merneptah suffered from [arthritis](/wiki/Arthritis) and [atherosclerosis](/wiki/Atherosclerosis) and died as an old man after a reign that lasted for nearly a decade. He was originally buried within tomb [KV8](/wiki/KV8) in the [Valley of the Kings](/wiki/Valley_of_the_Kings), but his [mummy](/wiki/Mummy) was not found there, having been moved in ancient times. In 1898, it was located along with eighteen other mummies in the mummy cache found in the tomb of [Amenhotep II](/wiki/Amenhotep_II) ([KV35](/wiki/KV35)) by [Victor Loret](/wiki/Victor_Loret). His mummy was taken to Cairo and eventually unwrapped by [G. Elliott Smith](/wiki/Grafton_Elliot_Smith) on July 8, 1907. Smith notes that:

> The body is that of an old man and is 1 meter 714 millimeters [5'6"] in height. Merneptah was almost completely bald, only a narrow fringe of white hair (now cut so close as to be seen only with difficulty) remaining on the temples and occiput. A few short (about 2 mill) black hairs were found on the upper lip and scattered, closely clipped hairs on the cheeks and chin. The general aspect of the face recalls that of Ramesses II, but the form of the cranium and the measurements of the face much more nearly agree with those of his [grand]father, Seti the Great.

In April 2021 his mummy was moved from the [Museum of Egyptian Antiquities](/wiki/Museum_of_Egyptian_Antiquities) to the [National Museum of Egyptian Civilization](/wiki/National_Museum_of_Egyptian_Civilization) along with those of 17 other kings and 4 queens in an event termed the [Pharaohs' Golden Parade](/wiki/Pharaohs%27_Golden_Parade).

Examination of the mummy revealed that the king was a corpulent man, perhaps even [obese](/wiki/Obese). It also showed that Merneptah was tall, measuring 1.71 m, though slightly shorter than his father, who measured 1.73 m.

## Family

See also: [Nineteenth Dynasty of Egypt family tree](/wiki/Nineteenth_Dynasty_of_Egypt_family_tree)

Merenptah's [Great Royal Wife](/wiki/Great_Royal_Wife) was [Isetnofret II](/wiki/Isetnofret_II), who was his full sister or her niece as daughter of his full brother [Khaemwaset](/wiki/Khaemweset).

They had at least four children:

- Seti Merenptah, who assume the throne as [Seti II](/wiki/Seti_II);
- [Merenptah](/wiki/Merenptah_(prince)), King's son, Executive at the Head of the Two Lands, and [Generalissimo](/wiki/Generalissimo);
- Khaemwaset, King's son, depicted in Karnak Temple;
- Isetnofret, King's daughter, depicted in the Leiden ship log.

Another his possible consort was [Takhat](/wiki/Takhat). If so, Merenptah would be the father of her son [Amenmesse](/wiki/Amenmesse), Seti II's rival for the throne.

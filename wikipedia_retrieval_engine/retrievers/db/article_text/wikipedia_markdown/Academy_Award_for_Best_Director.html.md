# Academy Award for Best Director

## Infobox

- **Awarded for:** Excellence in Cinematic Direction Achievement
- **Country:** United States
- **Presented by:** Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS)
- **First award:** May 16, 1929; 96 years ago ( 1929-05-16 ) (for films released during the 1927 / 1928 film season)
- **Most recent winner:** Paul Thomas Anderson, One Battle After Another ( 2025 )
- **Most awards:** John Ford (4)
- **Most nominations:** William Wyler (12)
- **Website:** oscars.org

The **Academy Award for Best Director** (officially known as the **Academy Award of Merit for Directing**) is an award presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) (AMPAS). It is given in honor of a [film director](/wiki/Film_director) who has exhibited outstanding directing while working in the [film industry](/wiki/Film_industry).

The [1st Academy Awards](/wiki/1st_Academy_Awards) ceremony was held in 1929 with the award being split into "Dramatic" and "Comedy" categories; [Frank Borzage](/wiki/Frank_Borzage) and [Lewis Milestone](/wiki/Lewis_Milestone) won for *[7th Heaven](/wiki/7th_Heaven_(1927_film))* and *[Two Arabian Knights](/wiki/Two_Arabian_Knights)*, respectively. However, these categories were merged for all subsequent ceremonies. Nominees are determined by [single transferable vote](/wiki/Single_transferable_vote) within the directors branch of AMPAS; winners are selected by a [plurality vote](/wiki/Plurality_(voting)) from the entire eligible voting members of the academy.

For the first eleven years of the Academy Awards, directors were allowed to be nominated for multiple films in the same year. However, after the nomination of [Michael Curtiz](/wiki/Michael_Curtiz) for two films, *[Angels with Dirty Faces](/wiki/Angels_with_Dirty_Faces)* and *[Four Daughters](/wiki/Four_Daughters_(1938_film))*, at the [11th Academy Awards](/wiki/11th_Academy_Awards), the rules were revised so that an individual could only be nominated for one film at each ceremony. That rule has since been amended, although the only director who has received multiple nominations in the same year was [Steven Soderbergh](/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh) for *[Erin Brockovich](/wiki/Erin_Brockovich_(film))* and *[Traffic](/wiki/Traffic_(2000_film))* in [2000](/wiki/73rd_Academy_Awards), winning the award for the latter.

The Academy Awards for Best Director and [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) have been very closely linked throughout their history. Of the 92 films that won Best Picture and were also nominated for Best Director, 71 won the award. The award has been criticised in recent years for failing to recognise female directors. Of the 260 individual directors nominated in the history of the award, only 9 have been women, with only 2 women having been nominated more than once (compared to 101 men), and only 3 of the 77 individual winners have been women.

Since its inception, the award has been given to 75 different directors or directing teams. As of the [98th Academy Awards](/wiki/98th_Academy_Awards) ceremony, American filmmaker [Paul Thomas Anderson](/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson) is the most recent winner in this category for his work on *[One Battle After Another](/wiki/One_Battle_After_Another)*.

## Winners and nominees

In the following table, the years are listed as per Academy convention, and generally correspond to the year of film release in [Los Angeles County](/wiki/Los_Angeles_County,_California), [California](/wiki/California); the ceremonies are always held the following year. For the first five ceremonies, the eligibility period spanned twelve months from August 1 to July 31. For the 6th ceremony held in 1934, the eligibility period lasted from August 1, 1932, to December 31, 1933. Since the [7th ceremony](/wiki/7th_Academy_Awards) held in 1935, the period of eligibility became the full previous calendar year from January 1 to December 31.

[Frank Borzage](/wiki/Frank_Borzage) won twice: "Dramatic director" at the [first ceremony](/wiki/1st_Academy_Awards), for *[7th Heaven](/wiki/7th_Heaven_(1927_film))* (1927); & later, *[Bad Girl](/wiki/Bad_Girl_(1931_film))* (1931).

[Lewis Milestone](/wiki/Lewis_Milestone) won twice: "Comedy director" at the [first ceremony](/wiki/1st_Academy_Awards), for *[Two Arabian Knights](/wiki/Two_Arabian_Knights)* (1927); & later, *[All Quiet on the Western Front](/wiki/All_Quiet_on_the_Western_Front_(1930_film))* (1930).

[Frank Lloyd](/wiki/Frank_Lloyd) won twice, for *[The Divine Lady](/wiki/The_Divine_Lady)* (1929) & *[Cavalcade](/wiki/Cavalcade_(1933_film))* (1933).

[Frank Capra](/wiki/Frank_Capra) won thrice, for *[It Happened One Night](/wiki/It_Happened_One_Night)* (1934), *[Mr. Deeds Goes to Town](/wiki/Mr._Deeds_Goes_to_Town)* (1936), & *[You Can't Take It with You](/wiki/You_Can%27t_Take_It_with_You_(film))* (1938).

[John Ford](/wiki/John_Ford) won [a record four times](/wiki/List_of_superlative_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Best_Director), for: *[The Informer](/wiki/The_Informer_(1935_film))* (1935), *[The Grapes of Wrath](/wiki/The_Grapes_of_Wrath_(film))* (1940), *[How Green Was My Valley](/wiki/How_Green_Was_My_Valley_(film))* (1941), & *[The Quiet Man](/wiki/The_Quiet_Man)* (1952).

[Victor Fleming](/wiki/Victor_Fleming) won for *[Gone with the Wind](/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_(film))* (1939).

[William Wyler](/wiki/William_Wyler), with [a record twelve nominations](/wiki/List_of_superlative_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Best_Director), won thrice, for: *[Mrs. Miniver](/wiki/Mrs._Miniver)* (1942), *[The Best Years of Our Lives](/wiki/The_Best_Years_of_Our_Lives)* (1946), & *[Ben-Hur](/wiki/Ben-Hur_(1959_film))* (1959).

## Records

- [John Ford](/wiki/John_Ford) has received the most awards in this category, with four. [Frank Capra](/wiki/Frank_Capra) and [William Wyler](/wiki/William_Wyler) won three each.
  - Wyler has the most nominations, with 12—including a record four years in a row. [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) is currently second, with 10 nominations.
  - [Clarence Brown](/wiki/Clarence_Brown) has the most nominations without a win (6). [Alfred Hitchcock](/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock), [King Vidor](/wiki/King_Vidor), and [Robert Altman](/wiki/Robert_Altman) each received 5 nominations without a win.
- Four directors have won twice for films that did not win [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture): [Frank Borzage](/wiki/Frank_Borzage), [George Stevens](/wiki/George_Stevens), [Ang Lee](/wiki/Ang_Lee), and [Alfonso Cuarón](/wiki/Alfonso_Cuar%C3%B3n).
  - Of John Ford's four wins, the only film which also won Best Picture was *[How Green Was My Valley](/wiki/How_Green_Was_My_Valley_(film))* (1941).
  - Ford (1940–1941), [Joseph L. Mankiewicz](/wiki/Joseph_L._Mankiewicz) (1949–1950), and [Alejandro González Iñárritu](/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu) (2014–2015) are the only directors to have won the award in two consecutive years.
  - [Francis Ford Coppola](/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola) is the only director to be nominated for each film of a trilogy, [The Godfather trilogy](/wiki/The_Godfather_(film_series)), winning for the [second film](/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_II).
- Four directing teams have been nominated together (a total of five times, winning on three occasions): [Robert Wise](/wiki/Robert_Wise) and [Jerome Robbins](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins) for *[West Side Story](/wiki/West_Side_Story_(1961_film))* (1961, winners); [Warren Beatty](/wiki/Warren_Beatty) and [Buck Henry](/wiki/Buck_Henry) for *[Heaven Can Wait](/wiki/Heaven_Can_Wait_(1978_film))* (1978); [Joel and Ethan Coen](/wiki/Coen_brothers) for *[No Country for Old Men](/wiki/No_Country_for_Old_Men)* (2007, winners) and *[True Grit](/wiki/True_Grit_(2010_film))* (2010); and [Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert](/wiki/Daniels_(directors)) for *[Everything Everywhere All at Once](/wiki/Everything_Everywhere_All_at_Once)* (2022, winners).
  - The Coen Brothers are the only siblings to have won the award.
- Six directors won the award for their feature film debut: [Delbert Mann](/wiki/Delbert_Mann) for *[Marty](/wiki/Marty_(film))* (1955), [Jerome Robbins](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins) for *[West Side Story](/wiki/West_Side_Story_(1961_film))* (1961), [Robert Redford](/wiki/Robert_Redford) for *[Ordinary People](/wiki/Ordinary_People)* (1980), [James L. Brooks](/wiki/James_L._Brooks) for *[Terms of Endearment](/wiki/Terms_of_Endearment)* (1983), [Kevin Costner](/wiki/Kevin_Costner) for *[Dances With Wolves](/wiki/Dances_With_Wolves)* (1990), and [Sam Mendes](/wiki/Sam_Mendes) for *[American Beauty](/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film))* (1999).
  - Robbins is the only director to have won for his only career directing credit.
- [Lina Wertmüller](/wiki/Lina_Wertm%C3%BCller) was the first woman nominated in the category, for *[Seven Beauties](/wiki/Seven_Beauties)* (1976).
  - [Kathryn Bigelow](/wiki/Kathryn_Bigelow) became the first woman to win the award, for *[The Hurt Locker](/wiki/The_Hurt_Locker)* (2009).
  - [Chloé Zhao](/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Zhao) is the first woman of color to win the award, for *[Nomadland](/wiki/Nomadland)* (2020). She became the second woman nominated twice for the award for *[Hamnet](/wiki/Hamnet_(film))* (2025).
  - [Jane Campion](/wiki/Jane_Campion) is the first woman to be nominated twice for the award: *[The Piano](/wiki/The_Piano)* (1993) and *[The Power of the Dog](/wiki/The_Power_of_the_Dog_(film))* (2021)—winning for the latter.
- [John Singleton](/wiki/John_Singleton) is the first Black (and youngest) nominee for *[Boyz n the Hood](/wiki/Boyz_n_the_Hood)* (1991).
  - [Steve McQueen](/wiki/Steve_McQueen_(director)) is the first Black nominee to direct a [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) winner, for *[12 Years a Slave](/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_(film))*. [Barry Jenkins](/wiki/Barry_Jenkins) subsequently did the same three years later, with *[Moonlight](/wiki/Moonlight_(2016_film))* (2016).
- [David Lean](/wiki/David_Lean) was the first non-American to win—and twice, for *[The Bridge on the River Kwai](/wiki/The_Bridge_on_the_River_Kwai)* (1957) and *[Lawrence of Arabia](/wiki/Lawrence_of_Arabia_(film))* (1962). This did not recur for five decades with any other non-American directors, until Ang Lee, Alfonso Cuarón, and Alejandro González Iñárritu each won twice themselves.
  - Lee was the first Asian director to win the award, for *[Brokeback Mountain](/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain)* (2005). He won again for *[Life of Pi](/wiki/Life_of_Pi_(film))* (2012).
  - Cuarón was the first Mexican (and Latin American) director to win the award, for *[Gravity](/wiki/Gravity_(2013_film))* (2013). He won again for *[Roma](/wiki/Roma_(2018_film))* (2018).
- No married or ex-married couple have won the award for the same film, though [James Cameron](/wiki/James_Cameron) (1997's *[Titanic](/wiki/Titanic_(1997_film))*) and [Kathryn Bigelow](/wiki/Kathryn_Bigelow) (2008's *[The Hurt Locker](/wiki/The_Hurt_Locker)*) were the first (and so far, only) ex-married couple to both win the award.
  - It was also the first time in Oscar history that ex-married couple were [nominated against each other in competition for an award in the same year](/wiki/82nd_Academy_Awards).

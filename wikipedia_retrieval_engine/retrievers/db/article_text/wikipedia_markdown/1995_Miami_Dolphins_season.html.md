# 1995 Miami Dolphins season

## Infobox

- **Head coach:** Don Shula
- **Offensive coordinator:** Gary Stevens
- **Defensive coordinator:** Tom Olivadotti
- **Home stadium:** Joe Robbie Stadium
- **Record:** 9–7
- **Division place:** 3rd AFC East
- **Playoffs:** Lost Wild Card Playoffs (at Bills ) 22–37
- **Pro Bowlers:** QB Dan Marino LT Richmond Webb G Keith Sims LB Bryan Cox

The **1995 Miami Dolphins season** was the franchise's 30th season, 26th in the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League), and 26th and final under head coach [Don Shula](/wiki/Don_Shula). The Dolphins finished 9–7 before losing to the [Bills](/wiki/1995_Buffalo_Bills_season) in the playoffs.

Until the [2022 NFL season](/wiki/2022_Miami_Dolphins_season), this marked the last time the Dolphins finished with a top 10 Offense.

## Playoffs

### AFC Wild Card Game

**Buffalo Bills 37, Miami Dolphins 22**

| Quarter | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dolphins | 0 | 0 | 0 | 22 | **22** |
| **Bills** | 10 | 14 | 3 | 10 | **37** |

*at [Rich Stadium](/wiki/Rich_Stadium), [Orchard Park, New York](/wiki/Orchard_Park_(town),_New_York)*

- **Game time**: 12:30 p.m. [EST](/wiki/North_American_Eastern_Standard_Time_Zone)
- **Game weather**: 30 °F (−1 °C), cloudy
- **Game attendance**: 73,103
- **Referee**: [Red Cashion](/wiki/Red_Cashion)
- **TV announcers ([ABC](/wiki/Monday_Night_Football))**: [Brent Musburger](/wiki/Brent_Musburger) and [Dick Vermeil](/wiki/Dick_Vermeil)

Dolphins quarterback [Dan Marino](/wiki/Dan_Marino) completed 33 of 64 passes for 422 yards and two touchdowns, but also threw three interceptions. The Bills used an NFL-playoff record 341 rushing yards to gain a 27–0 lead by the start of the fourth quarter.

### Don Shula's retirement

The week after Miami's playoff loss to Buffalo, Shula announced his retirement. His 347 wins as a head coach, including 257 with the Dolphins, are an NFL record. Shula coached in six Super Bowls, winning two, and in [1972](/wiki/1972_Miami_Dolphins_season) he led the only unbeaten and untied season in NFL history. Shula was inducted into the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in 1997 and is considered one of the greatest coaches in NFL history.

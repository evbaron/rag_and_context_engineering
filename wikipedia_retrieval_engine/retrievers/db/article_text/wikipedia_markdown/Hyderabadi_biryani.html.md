# Hyderabadi biryani

## Infobox

- **Place of origin:** India
- **Region or state:** Hyderabad
- **Main ingredients:** Basmati rice Goat meat Beef Chicken Vegetables Spices

**Hyderabadi biryani** (also known as **Hyderabadi dum biryani**) is a style of [biryani](/wiki/Biryani) originating from [Hyderabad](/wiki/Hyderabad), [India](/wiki/India) made with [basmati rice](/wiki/Basmati) and [meat](/wiki/Meat) (mostly [goat meat](/wiki/Goat_meat)). Originating in the kitchens of the [Nizam of Hyderabad](/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad), it combines elements of [Hyderabadi](/wiki/Hyderabadi_cuisine) and [Mughlai](/wiki/Mughlai_cuisine) cuisines. Hyderabad biryani is a key dish in [Hyderabadi cuisine](/wiki/Hyderabadi_cuisine) and it is so famous that the dish is considered synonymous with the city of Hyderabad.

## History

Hyderabad was conquered by the [Mughals](/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in the 1630s, and ruled by its [Nizams](/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad). Mughlai culinary traditions joined with local traditions to create [Hyderabadi cuisine](/wiki/Hyderabadi_cuisine).  Local folklore attributes the creation of Hyderabadi biryani to the chef of the first Nizam, [Nizam-ul-Mulk, Asaf Jah I](/wiki/Nizam-ul-Mulk,_Asaf_Jah_I), in the mid-18th century, during a hunting expedition. In 1857, when the [Mughal Empire](/wiki/Mughal_Empire) [declined in Delhi](/wiki/Siege_of_Delhi), Hyderabad emerged as the center of [South Asian](/wiki/South_Asia) culture, resulting in a mix of innovations in Hyderabadi biryani.

### Origin

The exact origin of the dish is uncertain. Despite legends attributing it to the Nizam's chef, the biryani is of South Indian origin, derived from *[pilaf](/wiki/Pilaf)* varieties brought to [South Asia](/wiki/South_Asia) by [Arab](/wiki/Arab_people) traders. *Pulao* may have been an army dish in medieval India. [Armies](/wiki/Army) would prepare a one-pot dish of [rice](/wiki/Rice) with whichever meat was available. The distinction between "pulao" and "biryani" is arbitrary. Hyderabadi biryani incorporated Deccani or Telangana flavors of, perhaps in the Nizam's kitchen.

## Ingredients

- Hyderabadi biryani (on the left) served with other [Indian dishes](/wiki/Indian_dishes).
- Two biryani accompaniments: [mirchi ka salan](/wiki/Mirchi_ka_salan) and [raitha](/wiki/Raita)/[dahi chutney](/wiki/Dahi_chutney).

Base ingredients are [basmati rice](/wiki/Basmati), meat (usually [goat](/wiki/Goat_meat), but sometimes also [chicken](/wiki/Chicken_as_food) or [beef](/wiki/Beef)), [dahi](/wiki/Dahi_(curd)), [fried onion](/wiki/Fried_onion) and [ghee](/wiki/Ghee). Spices include [cinnamon](/wiki/Cinnamon), [cloves](/wiki/Clove), [cardamom](/wiki/Cardamom) (*elaichi*), [bay leaves](/wiki/Bay_leaf), [nutmeg](/wiki/Nutmeg), [papaya paste](/wiki/Papaya), [caraway](/wiki/Caraway) (shahi jeera), mace flower (javitri), [star anise](/wiki/Illicium_verum), Garam Masala, (biryani flower), [lemon](/wiki/Lemon), and [saffron](/wiki/Saffron).

Hyderabadi biryani is of two types: the *kachchi* (raw) biryani, and the *pakki* (cooked) biryani.

### Kachche-gosht ki biryani

The *kachchi* biryani is prepared with *kachchi gosht* (raw meat) marinated with spices overnight and then soaked in curd (dahi) before cooking. The meat is sandwiched between layers of fragrant basmati rice and cooked "in dum" after sealing the *handi* (vessel) with dough. This is a challenging process as it requires meticulous attention to time and temperature to avoid over- or under-cooking the meat.

### Vegetable dum biryani

The vegetable biryani is prepared with mixed vegetables in a similar manner to dum biryani with meat. It is suitable for [vegetarians](/wiki/Vegetarians).

## Accompaniments

A biryani is usually served with [dahi chutney](/wiki/Dahi_chutney) and [mirchi ka salan](/wiki/Mirchi_ka_salan). [Baghaar-e-baingan](/wiki/Baghaar-e-baingan) is a common side dish. The [salad](/wiki/Kachumber) includes [onion](/wiki/Onion), [carrot](/wiki/Carrot), [cucumber](/wiki/Cucumber), and [lemon](/wiki/Lemon) wedges.

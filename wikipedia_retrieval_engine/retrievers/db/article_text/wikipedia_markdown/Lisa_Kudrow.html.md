**Lisa Valerie Kudrow** ([/ˈkuːdroʊ/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*KOO-droh*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born July 30, 1963) is an American actress and writer. She rose to international fame for her role as [Phoebe Buffay](/wiki/Phoebe_Buffay) in the American television sitcom *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*, which aired from 1994 to 2004. The series earned her [Primetime Emmy](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), [Screen Actors Guild](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards), [Satellite](/wiki/Satellite_Awards), [American Comedy](/wiki/American_Comedy_Awards) and [TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide_Award) awards. Phoebe has since been named one of the greatest television characters of all time and is considered to be Kudrow's breakout role, spawning her successful film career.

Kudrow initially appeared in a 1989 episode of the hit sitcom *[Cheers](/wiki/Cheers)* playing a character named Emily. She also starred in several episodes of the show *[Mad About You](/wiki/Mad_About_You)* (1993) as Ursula, before auditioning and earning the role of Phoebe on *Friends*; her character on *Mad About You* was written into the *Friends* storyline as Phoebe's twin. In the late 1990s, Kudrow starred in the [cult](/wiki/Cult_film) comedy film *[Romy and Michele's High School Reunion](/wiki/Romy_and_Michele%27s_High_School_Reunion)* (1997) and followed it with an acclaimed performance in the comedy/drama *[The Opposite of Sex](/wiki/The_Opposite_of_Sex)* (1998), which won her the [New York Film Critics Circle Award for Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/New_York_Film_Critics_Circle_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress), a [Chlotrudis Award](/wiki/Chlotrudis_Award), as well as nominations for an [Independent Spirit Award for Best Supporting Female](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Female), an [American Comedy Award](/wiki/American_Comedy_Award), a [National Society of Film Critics Award](/wiki/National_Society_of_Film_Critics_Award), a [Chicago Film Critics Association Award](/wiki/Chicago_Film_Critics_Association_Award), an [Online Film Critics Society Award](/wiki/Online_Film_Critics_Society_Award), and an Online Film Critics Association Award. She created, produced, wrote, and starred in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) mockumentary series *[The Comeback](/wiki/The_Comeback_(TV_series))*, which initially lasted for one season in 2005 but was revived for two critically acclaimed seasons in 2014 and 2026, respectively. She was nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) for its first two seasons.

In 2007, Kudrow received praise for her starring role in the film *[Kabluey](/wiki/Kabluey)*, earning a [Satellite Award](/wiki/Satellite_Award) nomination, and appeared in the film *[P.S. I Love You](/wiki/P.S._I_Love_You_(film))*. She produced and starred in the [Showtime](/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network)) program *[Web Therapy](/wiki/Web_Therapy_(TV_series))* (2011–2015), which was nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Short-Format_Live-Action_Entertainment_Program). She is a producer on the [TLC](/wiki/TLC_(TV_network))/[NBC](/wiki/NBC) reality program *[Who Do You Think You Are](/wiki/Who_Do_You_Think_You_Are%3F_(U.S._TV_series))*, which has been nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Reality_Program) five times. She has also received two nominations at the [Children's and Family Emmy Awards](/wiki/Children%27s_and_Family_Emmy_Awards) for her roles in *[Better Nate Than Ever](/wiki/Better_Nate_Than_Ever_(film))* (2022) and *[Time Bandits](/wiki/Time_Bandits_(TV_series))* (2024). She played Honey the dog on the FOX animated series *[HouseBroken](/wiki/HouseBroken_(TV_series))*.

## Early life

Lisa Valerie Kudrow was born in the [Encino](/wiki/Encino,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood of [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) on July 30, 1963, the daughter of Nedra, a travel agent, and Lee Kudrow, a doctor who specialized in treating headaches. She has an older sister, Helene, and two older brothers, David and Derrick. She was raised in a middle-class [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish) family and had a [Bat Mitzvah](/wiki/Bat_Mitzvah). Some of her ancestors came from [Ilya](/wiki/Ilya,_Belarus), in what is now [Belarus](/wiki/Belarus), just east of [Vilnius](/wiki/Vilnius), [Lithuania](/wiki/Lithuania). Almost all Jews in Ilya were murdered during [the Holocaust](/wiki/The_Holocaust), including Kudrow's paternal great-grandmother. Her paternal grandmother left Belarus for [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn), where her father grew up.

Kudrow attended Portola Middle School in the [Tarzana](/wiki/Tarzana,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood of Los Angeles. She graduated from [Taft High School](/wiki/Taft_High_School_(Los_Angeles)) in the [Woodland Hills](/wiki/Woodland_Hills,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood, which was attended at the same time by rapper [Ice Cube](/wiki/Ice_Cube) and actress [Robin Wright](/wiki/Robin_Wright). She received her [A.B.](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in biology from [Vassar College](/wiki/Vassar_College) in [Poughkeepsie, New York](/wiki/Poughkeepsie_(town),_New_York), intending to become an expert on headaches like her father. While breaking into acting, she worked for her father for eight years and earned a research credit on his study on the comparative likelihood of left-handed individuals developing [cluster headaches](/wiki/Cluster_headache).

## Career

### 1989–1994: Pre-*Friends*

At the urging of comedian [Jon Lovitz](/wiki/Jon_Lovitz), who was a childhood friend of her brother, Kudrow began her comedic career as a member of [The Groundlings](/wiki/The_Groundlings), an improv and sketch comedy school in Los Angeles. She has credited [Cynthia Szigeti](/wiki/Cynthia_Szigeti), her improv teacher at The Groundlings, for changing her perspective on acting, calling her "the best thing that happened, on so many levels". Briefly, Kudrow joined with [Conan O'Brien](/wiki/Conan_O%27Brien) and director Tim Hillman in the short-lived [improv](/wiki/Improvisational_theatre) troupe [Unexpected Company](/wiki/Unexpected_Company). She was also the only regular female member of the Transformers Comedy Troupe. She played a role in an episode of the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) sitcom *[Cheers](/wiki/Cheers)*. She tried out for *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* in 1990, but [Julia Sweeney](/wiki/Julia_Sweeney) was chosen instead. She had a recurring role as Kathy Fleisher in three episodes of season one of the [Bob Newhart](/wiki/Bob_Newhart) sitcom *[Bob](/wiki/Bob_(TV_series))* ([CBS](/wiki/CBS), 1992–1993), a role she played after taking part in the series finale of Newhart's previous series *[Newhart](/wiki/Newhart)*. Prior to *Friends*, she appeared in at least two network-produced [pilots](/wiki/Television_pilot): NBC's *Just Temporary* (also known as *[Temporarily Yours](/wiki/Temporarily_Yours)*) in 1989, playing Nicole; and CBS' *Close Encounters* (also known as *Matchmaker*) in 1990, playing a [Valley girl](/wiki/Valley_girl).

Kudrow was cast as [Roz Doyle](/wiki/Roz_Doyle) in *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)*, but the role was re-cast with [Peri Gilpin](/wiki/Peri_Gilpin) during the taping of the pilot episode. In 2000, Kudrow explained that when rehearsals started, "I knew it wasn't working. I could feel it all slipping away, and I was panicking, which only made things worse." Her first recurring television role was [Ursula Buffay](/wiki/List_of_Friends_and_Joey_characters#Ursula_Buffay), the eccentric waitress on the NBC sitcom *[Mad About You](/wiki/Mad_About_You)*.

### 1994–2004: *Friends* and worldwide recognition

Kudrow, the oldest actor of the main cast, reprised the character of Ursula on the NBC sitcom *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*, in which she co-starred as [massage therapist](/wiki/Massage_therapy) [Phoebe Buffay](/wiki/Phoebe_Buffay), Ursula's twin sister. Praised for her performance, Kudrow was the first *Friends* cast member to win an [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Award) with her 1998 honor as [Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) for her starring role as Phoebe on *Friends* (NBC, 1994–2004). Kudrow received critical acclaim for playing Phoebe. According to the *[Guinness Book of World Records](/wiki/Guinness_Book_of_World_Records)* (2005), Kudrow and co-stars [Jennifer Aniston](/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston) and [Courteney Cox](/wiki/Courteney_Cox) became the highest paid TV actresses of all time, earning $1 million per episode for the ninth and tenth seasons of *Friends*. Phoebe has since been named one of the greatest television characters of all time. She played Phoebe until the show ended in 2004. The program was a massive hit and Kudrow, along with her co-stars, gained worldwide recognition. Her character, Phoebe, was especially popular. *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* voted Phoebe on *Friends* as Kudrow's best performance.

During her tenure on *Friends*, Kudrow appeared in several comedy films such as *[Romy and Michele's High School Reunion](/wiki/Romy_and_Michele%27s_High_School_Reunion)*, *[Hanging Up](/wiki/Hanging_Up)*, *[Marci X](/wiki/Marci_X)*, *[Dr. Dolittle 2](/wiki/Dr._Dolittle_2)*, *[Analyze This](/wiki/Analyze_This)* and its sequel *[Analyze That](/wiki/Analyze_That)*, and dramatic films, such as *[Wonderland](/wiki/Wonderland_(2003_film))* and *[The Opposite of Sex](/wiki/The_Opposite_of_Sex)*. She also guest-starred on numerous television series during *Friends*, including *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)*, *[Hope and Gloria](/wiki/Hope_and_Gloria)* and *[King of the Hill](/wiki/King_of_the_Hill)*, and hosted *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*.

### 2004–present: Post-*Friends*

Following *Friends*, Kudrow starred as protagonist Valerie Cherish on the experimental three-season [HBO](/wiki/HBO) series *[The Comeback](/wiki/The_Comeback_(TV_series))* (premiered June 5, 2005), about a has-been sitcom star trying for a comeback. She also served as co-creator, writer, and executive producer. Nine years after the original season, [HBO](/wiki/HBO) revived the series in 2014 for an abbreviated second season. Kudrow received two Emmy nominations for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series for her work on *The Comeback*. Her production company is 'Is or Isn't Entertainment'. Kudrow serves as the executive producer for the [U.S. version](/wiki/Who_Do_You_Think_You_Are%3F_(U.S._TV_series)) of the British television series *[Who Do You Think You Are?](/wiki/Who_Do_You_Think_You_Are%3F_(UK_TV_series))*, in which celebrities trace their [family trees](/wiki/Family_tree). The subjects of the first series included Kudrow herself, in which it was discovered her great-grandmother was murdered in the [Holocaust](/wiki/Holocaust).

Kudrow co-created an improvised comedy webseries, *[Web Therapy](/wiki/Web_Therapy)* on Lstudio.com. The improv series, which launched online in 2008, has earned several Webby nominations and one Outstanding Comedic Performance Webby for Kudrow, who plays therapist Fiona Wallice. She offers her patients three-minute sessions over iChat. In July 2011, a reformatted, half-hour version of the show premiered on Showtime, before being cancelled in 2015 after four seasons. Kudrow has guest starred on multiple television series such as *[Cougar Town](/wiki/Cougar_Town)*, *[BoJack Horseman](/wiki/BoJack_Horseman)*, *[Angie Tribeca](/wiki/Angie_Tribeca)*, *[Unbreakable Kimmy Schmidt](/wiki/Unbreakable_Kimmy_Schmidt)*, and *[Scandal](/wiki/Scandal_(American_TV_series))*.

Kudrow has appeared in multiple comedic films such as *[Happy Endings](/wiki/Happy_Endings_(film))*, *[Hotel for Dogs](/wiki/Hotel_for_Dogs_(film))*, *[Easy A](/wiki/Easy_A)*, *[Neighbors](/wiki/Neighbors_(2014_film))* and its sequel *[Neighbors 2: Sorority Rising](/wiki/Neighbors_2:_Sorority_Rising)*, *[Long Shot](/wiki/Long_Shot_(2019_film))*, and *[Booksmart](/wiki/Booksmart)*. She also appeared in the [romantic drama film](/wiki/Romantic_drama_film) *[P.S. I Love You](/wiki/P.S._I_Love_You_(film))* and co-starred in the [thriller film](/wiki/Thriller_film) *[The Girl on the Train](/wiki/The_Girl_on_the_Train_(2016_film))*. In 2020, Kudrow played [Hypatia of Alexandria](/wiki/Hypatia) in season 4 episode 12 of *[The Good Place](/wiki/The_Good_Place)* "Patty", and starred as Maggie Naird in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) comedy series, *[Space Force](/wiki/Space_Force_(TV_series))*. She reunited with her *Friends* cast mates for an HBO Max unscripted television special titled *[Friends: The Reunion](/wiki/Friends:_The_Reunion)* in May 2021. She is currently, along with [Mary McCormack](/wiki/Mary_McCormack), an executive producer of the syndicated game show, *[25 Words or Less](/wiki/25_Words_or_Less_(game_show))*; sometimes, Kudrow herself is one of the two celebrity guests playing with a contestant on the show.

In December 2024, Kudrow starred as Lydia in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) comedy-drama *[No Good Deed](/wiki/No_Good_Deed_(TV_series))*, a role for which she gained positive reviews from critics.

In 2026, Kudrow reprised her role as Valerie Cherish in the critically acclaimed third season of [The Comeback](/wiki/The_Comeback_(TV_series)) .

## Personal life

Kudrow married French advertising executive Michel Stern on May 27, 1995. They live in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California), and have a son born on May 7, 1998. Kudrow's pregnancy was written into the fourth season of *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*, with her character having triplets as a [surrogate mother](/wiki/Surrogacy) for her half-brother and his wife. In addition to her Beverly Hills home, Kudrow maintained a penthouse in [Park City, Utah](/wiki/Park_City,_Utah), which she sold in April 2017. She revealed in 2019 that she had experienced [body dysmorphic disorder](/wiki/Body_dysmorphic_disorder) while working on *Friends*.

## Awards and nominations

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Lisa Kudrow](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Lisa_Kudrow)

Kudrow has been honored with numerous accolades over her career. For her role in the sitcom *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*, she received six nominations at the [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award), winning in 1998 for [Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series), won two [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award), one [Satellite Award](/wiki/Satellite_Award), one [American Comedy](/wiki/American_Comedy_Awards) award, and one [TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide_Award) award.

In total, she has received fifteen Emmy nominations, with her most recent nomination in the 2021 [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), twelve nominations and two wins at the [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award), one win and eight nominations at the [American Comedy Awards](/wiki/American_Comedy_Awards), one [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nomination, one [Banff Rockie](/wiki/Banff_World_Media_Festival) award, one [Blockbuster Entertainment](/wiki/Blockbuster_Entertainment_Awards) award, one Chicago Film Critics Association nomination, one [Chlotrudis](/wiki/Chlotrudis_Awards) award, two [Critics' Choice Television](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Awards) nominations, one [Dorian](/wiki/Dorian_Awards) award, one [Gracie](/wiki/Gracie_Awards) award, one [Independent Spirit](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Awards) nomination, one [MTV Movie & TV](/wiki/MTV_Movie_%26_TV_Awards) nomination, one National Society of Film Critics nomination, one [New York Film Critics Circle](/wiki/New_York_Film_Critics_Circle) award, one [Nickelodeon Kids' Choice](/wiki/Nickelodeon_Kids%27_Choice_Awards) nomination, one [Online Film Critics Society](/wiki/Online_Film_Critics_Society) nomination, one [People Magazine](/wiki/People_Magazine_Awards) award, five nominations and one win at the [Satellite Awards](/wiki/Satellite_Awards), three nominations and one win at the [Teen Choice Awards](/wiki/Teen_Choice_Awards), two [Streamy](/wiki/Streamy_Awards) nominations, two nominations and one win at the [TV Guide Awards](/wiki/TV_Guide_Awards), one [Viewers for Quality Television](/wiki/Viewers_for_Quality_Television) nomination and seven nominations and four wins at the [Webby Awards](/wiki/Webby_Award).

# List of Northern Ireland international footballers

[David Healy](/wiki/David_Healy_(footballer)) scored the most goals and made the third most number of appearances for Northern Ireland.

The [Northern Ireland national football team](/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team) represents [Northern Ireland](/wiki/Northern_Ireland) in international [association football](/wiki/Association_football). It is organised by the [Irish Football Association](/wiki/Irish_Football_Association) (IFA), which was formed in 1880, prior to the [partition of Ireland](/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland). The original [Ireland national team](/wiki/Ireland_national_football_team_(1882%E2%80%931950)) was selected by the IFA and included players from all of Ireland. Following the creation of the [Irish Free State](/wiki/Irish_Free_State), the [Football Association of Ireland](/wiki/Football_Association_of_Ireland) (FAI) was set up and it picked its [own national team](/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team). Until 1950, both Irish associations picked players from the whole of the island, which resulted in there being [several 'dual internationals'](/wiki/List_of_dual_Irish_international_footballers) (33 originating from the territory of the Irish Republic and six from the territory of Northern Ireland). After complaints by the FAI against this practice being used by the IFA during [1950 FIFA World Cup qualification](/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) matches, FIFA decreed that each association should select teams based on their own part of Ireland.

Until the 1950s, the only major competition entered by Ireland/Northern Ireland was the [British Home Championship](/wiki/British_Home_Championship), which operated until 1984. The team won the competition eight times, taking the title outright on three occasions, including the last championship in 1984. The best [World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup) performance by Northern Ireland was in their first appearance in the finals, the [1958 World Cup](/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), where they reached the quarter-finals after beating [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) 2–1 in a play-off. Northern Ireland became the smallest country to have qualified for the World Cup, a record that stood until [Trinidad & Tobago](/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_national_football_team) qualified for the [2006 World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).

Northern Ireland qualified for the [1982 World Cup](/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup). [Gerry Armstrong](/wiki/Gerry_Armstrong_(footballer)) scored the goal in a shock 1–0 win against tournament hosts [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team), which helped the team progress to the second group stage by winning their first group stage. [Norman Whiteside](/wiki/Norman_Whiteside) became the youngest player ever in the World Cup finals, breaking a record set by [Pelé](/wiki/Pel%C3%A9). Northern Ireland also qualified for the [1986 World Cup](/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup), but did not progress beyond the first group stage. [Billy Bingham](/wiki/Billy_Bingham), a playing member of the 1958 squad, was manager for both of these tournaments.

Northern Ireland qualified for the [UEFA European Championship](/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) for the first time in [2016](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016). They defeated [West Germany](/wiki/Germany_national_football_team) 1–0 both home and away in [UEFA Euro 1984 qualifying](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984_qualifying) and [David Healy](/wiki/David_Healy_(footballer)) set a record for goals scored in one European qualifying section, by scoring 13 times in [UEFA Euro 2008 qualifying](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_qualifying).

## List of players

The list includes all players who were selected for Ireland (IFA) from 1882 to 1952 (including those from the territory of what became the Irish Republic, 33 of whom also played for the FAI team), and all those who played for Northern Ireland from then on. Caps/goals in unofficial wartime international matches are not included here, and any [players who appeared in them](/wiki/Category:Ireland_(IFA)_men%27s_wartime_international_footballers) but did not gain an official cap are not listed.

[Willoughby Hamilton](/wiki/Willoughby_Hamilton) played once for [Ireland](/wiki/Ireland_national_football_team_(1882%E2%80%931950)), in 1885. He was also a [tennis](/wiki/Tennis) player and won the [Wimbledon men's singles championship in 1890](/wiki/1890_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Gentlemen%27s_Singles).

The **2020–21 NBA season** was the 75th season of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA), though the 75th anniversary was not celebrated until the following season. Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_sports), the regular season was reduced to 72 games for each team, and began on December 22, 2020. The season started just 72 days after the completion of the [2020 NBA Finals](/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals), the shortest off-season in league history. The [2021 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2021_NBA_All-Star_Game) was played on March 7, at [State Farm Arena](/wiki/State_Farm_Arena) in [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta), and was won by Team LeBron, 170–150. For the first time, the NBA staged a play-in tournament for teams ranked 7th through 10th in each conference from May 18 to 21. The playoffs then ran under the standard 16-team playoff format from May 22 to July 20, 2021. Due to [COVID-19 cross-border restrictions](/wiki/Travel_during_the_COVID-19_pandemic) imposed by the [Canadian government](/wiki/Canadian_government), the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Toronto_Raptors_season) played their 2020–21 home games at [Amalie Arena](/wiki/Amalie_Arena) in [Tampa, Florida](/wiki/Tampa,_Florida).

### Retirement

- On September 8, 2020, [Marvin Williams](/wiki/Marvin_Williams) announced his retirement from the NBA. Williams played for four teams during his 15-year NBA career.
- On September 14, 2020, [Leandro Barbosa](/wiki/Leandro_Barbosa) announced his retirement from the NBA. Barbosa played 14 seasons in the NBA, winning one championship with the [Golden State Warriors](/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) in 2015.
- On October 24, 2020, [Kevin Séraphin](/wiki/Kevin_S%C3%A9raphin) announced his retirement from the NBA. Séraphin played for three teams during his seven-year NBA career.
- On November 16, 2020, [Corey Brewer](/wiki/Corey_Brewer) announced his retirement from the NBA. Brewer played for 12 years in the NBA for eight teams, winning one championship with the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) in 2011.
- On November 18, 2020, [Dorell Wright](/wiki/Dorell_Wright) announced his retirement from the NBA. Wright played for four teams during his 11-year NBA career, winning one championship with the Miami Heat in 2006.
- On November 25, 2020, [Aaron Brooks](/wiki/Aaron_Brooks_(basketball)) announced his retirement from the NBA. Brooks played for seven teams during his 13-year NBA career.
- On November 30, 2020, [Andrew Bogut](/wiki/Andrew_Bogut) announced his retirement from the NBA. Bogut played for five teams during his 14-year NBA career, winning one championship with the Golden State Warriors in 2015.
- On November 30, 2020, [Evan Turner](/wiki/Evan_Turner) announced his retirement from the NBA. Turner played 10 seasons for five teams during his time in the NBA.
- On February 4, 2021, [Lucas Nogueira](/wiki/Lucas_Nogueira) announced his retirement from the NBA. Nogueira played for the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) for his entire four-year NBA career.
- On March 1, 2021, [Joakim Noah](/wiki/Joakim_Noah) announced his retirement from the NBA. Noah played for four teams during his 13-year NBA career. He was the 2014 [Defensive Player of the Year](/wiki/NBA_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year_Award), and a two-time All-Star.
- On March 31, 2021, [Thabo Sefolosha](/wiki/Thabo_Sefolosha) announced his retirement from the NBA. Sefolosha played for five teams during his 14-year NBA career.
- On April 15, 2021, [LaMarcus Aldridge](/wiki/LaMarcus_Aldridge) announced his retirement from the NBA as a result of heart issues and an irregular heartbeat after games. Aldridge played for three teams during his 15-year NBA career, and was a seven-time All-Star.
- On May 19, 2021, [Jeremy Lin](/wiki/Jeremy_Lin) announced his retirement from the NBA. Lin played for eight teams during his nine-year NBA career, winning one championship with the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) in 2019.

### Free agency

[Free agency](/wiki/Free_agency_(sports)) negotiations were scheduled to begin on October 18, 2020, but that date was delayed. On November 9, it was announced that free agency would begin on November 20 at 6 p.m. ET, with signings permitted starting at 12 p.m. ET on November 22.

## Preseason

The [COVID-19 pandemic in North America](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_North_America), which pushed the conclusion of the previous 2019–20 season and playoffs into the fall, had delayed the start date of training camp to November 10, 2020. The preseason began on December 11 and ended on December 19.

## Regular season

The start of the 2020–21 regular season was delayed because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_North_America). The NBA initially set a target date of December 1, 2020, to start the regular season. However, NBA Commissioner [Adam Silver](/wiki/Adam_Silver) suggested further delaying the season until at least January because local health orders at each NBA city would limit fan attendance. The NBA receives 40 percent of its revenue from attendance, and thus delaying the season until it was safer to let more fans into the arenas would ease the financial pain. The NBA also contemplated organizing the schedule such that teams would have less travel, with back-to-back games in the same cities against the same opponent. [National Basketball Players Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Players_Association) executive director Michele Roberts suggested that the season might eventually have to begin within a "bubble" environment, similar to the 2020 playoffs.

On October 13, the NBA delayed the targeted start date of the regular season from December 2020 to [Martin Luther King Jr. Day](/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr._Day), January 18, 2021. Later in October, *Sports Illustrated* reported that the NBA was targeting December 22, 2020, as the first day of the season. On November 5, 2020, the National Basketball Players Association (NBPA) tentatively approved a 72-game regular season that began on December 22, 2020. The season featured a condensed schedule so that the NBA Finals could conclude by July 22, allowing NBA players to participate in the [2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics); the Olympics were postponed to 2021 because of the [COVID-19 pandemic in Japan](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Japan).

On November 17, the NBA announced that the 72-game regular season would run from December 22 through May 16. Each team would play three games against each opponent from its own conference and two games against each interconference opponent. The season would include a six-day All-Star break from March 5 to 10. While the All-Star Game (which was originally scheduled for [Indianapolis](/wiki/Indianapolis)) was initially considered unlikely, the NBA announced in February that the [2021 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2021_NBA_All-Star_Game) would be held in [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta) on March 7, 2021. The schedule was released in two parts; the first half was released in early December, while the second half was released in the latter part of the first half.

It was the latest a season had started, and with the fewest games per team, since the [2011–12 season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NBA_season). That season, each team played only 66 games starting on [Christmas Day](/wiki/Christmas_Day); this was due to the aftermath of the [2011 NBA lockout](/wiki/2011_NBA_lockout).

## Play-in tournament

Further information: [NBA play-in tournament](/wiki/NBA_play-in_tournament)

The NBA staged a [play-in tournament](/wiki/NBA_play-in_tournament) for teams ranked 7th through 10th in each conference after regular season, played from May 18 to 21, to determine in each conference 7th and 8th seeded teams making playoffs. To this end in each conference: 1) The 7th-place team hosted the 8th place team with the winner clinching the 7th seed in the playoffs. 2) The 9th-place team hosted the 10th-place team with the loser being eliminated from playoff contention. 3) The 7th-vs.-8th-place game's loser then hosted the 9th-vs.-10th-place game's winner, with the winner clinching the 8th seed and the loser being eliminated.

### Temporary relocation of the Toronto Raptors to Tampa

As the NBA's plans for the 2020–21 season began to take shape, the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) were denied permission to play home games in Toronto as the [Canadian federal government](/wiki/Canadian_federal_government) ruled that repeated cross-border trips by the Raptors and their opponents would be a major health risk due to the different levels of COVID-19 cases in the United States and Canada. This is similar to what happened to the Raptors' [Major League Baseball](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball) counterpart, the [Toronto Blue Jays](/wiki/Toronto_Blue_Jays), who were forced to play their [2020](/wiki/2020_Major_League_Baseball_season) home games in [Buffalo](/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York) and would later play their [2021](/wiki/2021_Major_League_Baseball_season) home games at their spring training home of [Dunedin, Florida](/wiki/Dunedin,_Florida).

After looking at several U.S. cities, the Raptors announced on November 20, 2020, that they would play their home games at [Amalie Arena](/wiki/Amalie_Arena) in [Tampa, Florida](/wiki/Tampa,_Florida) during the 2020–21 season. This marked the first time since the then-named [New Orleans Hornets](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_New_Orleans_Hornets_season) in 2007 that an NBA team was temporarily displaced from their home city. The team, dealing with the aftermath of [Hurricane Katrina](/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) in 2005, had played in [Oklahoma City](/wiki/Oklahoma_City) for the 2005–06 and 2006–07 seasons as the [New Orleans/Oklahoma City Hornets](/wiki/New_Orleans/Oklahoma_City_Hornets).

## Media

This was the fifth year of a nine-year deal with [ABC](/wiki/NBA_on_ABC), [ESPN](/wiki/NBA_on_ESPN), [TNT](/wiki/NBA_on_TNT) and [NBA TV](/wiki/NBA_TV).

To reduce on-site staff, ESPN and TNT leverage the home team's rightsholder as a host broadcaster for some of their games. They send a neutral "world feed" and other camera feeds to the network, which then added commentary and surrounding coverage. ESPN and TNT are also deploying additional cameras specific to their broadcasts, and ESPN may provide a supplemental on-site presence if the local broadcaster does not have enough capacity to support the host model. ESPN stated that some (roughly half) of their games, particularly marquee games exclusive to ESPN and ABC, would be produced on-site with an existing hybrid model (where some producers and graphics operators work from ESPN's studios in Bristol, Connecticut). TNT also planned to begin doing some games on-site beginning with [Martin Luther King Jr. Day](/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr._Day).

On December 26, 2020, it was announced that [Fox Sports Networks](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Networks) had acquired rights to simulcast 36 Toronto Raptors games locally in the [Tampa Bay area](/wiki/Tampa_Bay_area) through at least the first half of the season. All of the games were carried via the [Fox Sports Go](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Go) app, with selected games to also air on television via [Fox Sports Sun](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Sun) (15) and [Fox Sports Florida](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Florida) (2).

On March 31, 2021, the Fox Sports Networks rebranded as [Bally Sports](/wiki/Bally_Sports), as part of an agreement between majority-owner [Sinclair Broadcast Group](/wiki/Sinclair_Broadcast_Group) and casino operator [Bally's Corporation](/wiki/Bally%27s_Corporation).

## Notable occurrences

- On May 13, 2020, the NBA announced that [Spalding](/wiki/Spalding_(company))'s partnership with the league as official game ball supplier would end after this season. - On December 27, 2020, [Stephen Curry](/wiki/Stephen_Curry) of the [Golden State Warriors](/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) became the third player ever to reach 2,500 career 3-pointers.
- On December 29, 2020, the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) set a new record for most three-point field goals made in a game with 29 against the [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat), breaking the previous record of 27 set by the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) on April 7, 2019.
- On December 30, 2020, [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James) of the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) became the first player to reach 1,000 consecutive regular season games with at least 10 points.
- On January 1, 2021, [Carmelo Anthony](/wiki/Carmelo_Anthony) of the [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers) passed [Tim Duncan](/wiki/Tim_Duncan) for 14th on the [NBA all-time scoring list](/wiki/List_of_NBA_career_scoring_leaders).
- On January 6, 2021, the Milwaukee Bucks and [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons) took a knee for their first possessions in protest to the announcement that criminal charges would not be filed against police officers in the [Jacob Blake shooting](/wiki/Shooting_of_Jacob_Blake). The Bucks held the ball for seven seconds in reference to Blake's seven gunshots. Various teams linked arms at center court in unison during the anthem in response to the [2021 storming of the United States Capitol](/wiki/2021_storming_of_the_United_States_Capitol).
- On January 7, 2021, [Duncan Robinson](/wiki/Duncan_Robinson_(basketball)) of the [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat) became the fastest player to reach 300 career three-pointers. He made it in a span of only 95 games, surpassing both [Luka Dončić](/wiki/Luka_Don%C4%8Di%C4%87)'s and [Damian Lillard](/wiki/Damian_Lillard)'s previous record within 117 games.
- On January 8, 2021, the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) set a new franchise record for points in a single regular season game, scoring 144 points against the [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) on the road.
- On January 9, 2021, [LaMelo Ball](/wiki/LaMelo_Ball) posted a triple-double for the [Charlotte Hornets](/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) against the [Atlanta Hawks](/wiki/Atlanta_Hawks), becoming the youngest player ever to record a triple-double, at the age of 19 years and 140 days. He surpassed the previous record held by [Markelle Fultz](/wiki/Markelle_Fultz), who recorded a triple-double aged 19 years and 317 days.
- On January 16, 2021, [James Harden](/wiki/James_Harden) of the [Brooklyn Nets](/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) posted a triple-double against the [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic), becoming the seventh player to record a triple-double during his team debut.
- On January 20, 2021, [Clint Capela](/wiki/Clint_Capela) of the [Atlanta Hawks](/wiki/Atlanta_Hawks) became the first player since [Shaquille O'Neal](/wiki/Shaquille_O%27Neal) in 2004 to record 25 points, 25 rebounds and 5 blocks in a single game, doing so against the [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons).
- On January 20, 2021, [Donovan Mitchell](/wiki/Donovan_Mitchell) of the [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz) became the fastest player to reach 600 three-pointers. He did it in just 240 games, beating the previous record of 244 games set by [Buddy Hield](/wiki/Buddy_Hield) in 2019.
- On January 23, 2021, Stephen Curry of the Golden State Warriors passed [Reggie Miller](/wiki/Reggie_Miller) for second place on the [NBA all-time three-point list](/wiki/List_of_NBA_career_3-point_scoring_leaders).
- On January 29, 2021, Russell Westbrook of the Washington Wizards passed [Maurice Cheeks](/wiki/Maurice_Cheeks) for 13th place on the [NBA all-time assist list.](/wiki/List_of_NBA_career_assists_leaders)
- On January 29, 2021, [Trae Young](/wiki/Trae_Young) of the [Atlanta Hawks](/wiki/Atlanta_Hawks) became the fastest player to reach 400 career three-pointers doing it in 159 games, surpassing the previous total of 163 games set by [Damian Lillard](/wiki/Damian_Lillard).
- On February 2, 2021, Carmelo Anthony of the Portland Trail Blazers passed [Dominique Wilkins](/wiki/Dominique_Wilkins) for 13th on the [NBA all-time scoring list](/wiki/List_of_NBA_career_scoring_leaders).
- On February 4, 2021, LeBron James of the Los Angeles Lakers passed [Wilt Chamberlain](/wiki/Wilt_Chamberlain) for third place on the NBA all-time field goal list.
- On February 6, 2021, [Nikola Jokić](/wiki/Nikola_Joki%C4%87) of the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) became the first center since [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) in 1975 to record 50 points and 10 assists.
- On February 9, 2021, Carmelo Anthony of the Portland Trail Blazers passed Oscar Robertson for 12th on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On February 19, 2021, LeBron James of the Los Angeles Lakers became the third player in NBA history to reach 35,000 career points joining [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) and [Karl Malone](/wiki/Karl_Malone).
- On February 19, 2021, [Jimmy Butler](/wiki/Jimmy_Butler) and [Bam Adebayo](/wiki/Bam_Adebayo) of the [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat), in a game against the [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/Sacramento_Kings), became the first pair of teammates to each record a triple-double on multiple occasions. The duo first accomplished this feat on December 10, 2019, in a game against the [Atlanta Hawks](/wiki/Atlanta_Hawks).
- On February 19, 2021, [Jamal Murray](/wiki/Jamal_Murray) had the most points (50) without a free throw attempted.
- On February 20, 2021, [Chris Paul](/wiki/Chris_Paul) of the [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/Phoenix_Suns) passed Oscar Robertson for sixth place on the NBA all-time assist list.
- On February 23, 2021, the Denver Nuggets broke the record for fewest turnovers (1) by a team in a game.
- On March 3, 2021, [T. J. McConnell](/wiki/T._J._McConnell) of the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) set several records. The first record was most steals in a half with 9. He also became the first player since [Mookie Blaylock](/wiki/Mookie_Blaylock) to record a triple-double with steals and the sixth player overall. He did this all off the bench which is also an NBA record.
- On March 3, 2021, [Mason Plumlee](/wiki/Mason_Plumlee) and [Dennis Smith Jr.](/wiki/Dennis_Smith_Jr.) of the [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons) became the 13th time a pair of teammates each had a triple-double in a game.
- On March 11, 2021, [Duncan Robinson](/wiki/Duncan_Robinson_(basketball)) of the [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat) became the fastest player to record 400 made career three-pointers, doing it in a span of 125 games, surpassing previous record set by [Trae Young](/wiki/Trae_Young) at 159 games earlier on January 29, 2021. Robinson made this feat by scoring only nine points, all in three-pointers in a game won against the [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic). Currently, Robinson holds the record of being the fastest player ever to reach three three-pointers-made milestones—200 in 69 games, 300 in 95 games, and now 400 in 125 games.
- On March 13, 2021, Carmelo Anthony of the Portland Trail Blazers passed [Hakeem Olajuwon](/wiki/Hakeem_Olajuwon) for 11th place on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On March 27, 2021, [Gregg Popovich](/wiki/Gregg_Popovich) of the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs) became the third coach in NBA history to reach 1,300 career wins with a 120–104 win against the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/Chicago_Bulls).
- On March 29, 2021, Russell Westbrook of the Washington Wizards became the third player to record a 30–10–20 triple-double, joining Oscar Robertson and [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson). He also became the first player to record a triple-double with 35 [points](/wiki/Point_(basketball)) and 20 [assists](/wiki/Assist_(basketball)), with his 35 points, 21 assists and 14 rebounds.
- On April 3, 2021, the Utah Jazz had the most three-point field goals made (18) by a team in a half.
- On April 10, 2021, the [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) had the youngest starting lineup in NBA history with an average age of 20 years, 8 months and 6 days.
- On April 12, 2021, Stephen Curry passed Wilt Chamberlain as the Golden State Warriors' all-time scoring leader.
- On April 30, the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) overcame a 32-point deficit and beat the San Antonio Spurs, which was the third-biggest comeback ever. They were led by [Jayson Tatum](/wiki/Jayson_Tatum) who had 60 points.
- On May 1, 2021, the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) defeated the [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) 152–95. The 57-point victory is the largest road win in NBA history.
- On May 1, 2021, [Duncan Robinson](/wiki/Duncan_Robinson_(basketball)) of the [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat) passed [Luka Dončić](/wiki/Luka_Don%C4%8Di%C4%87) as the fastest player to reach 500 three-pointers. He made this feat in 152 games, in a 124–107 victory over the [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers), 35 games earlier than Dončić's record of within 187 games.
- On May 3, 2021, Carmelo Anthony of the Portland Trail Blazers passed [Elvin Hayes](/wiki/Elvin_Hayes) for 10th place on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On May 3, 2021, Russell Westbrook of the Washington Wizards clinched a fourth season averaging a triple-double. He also recorded the third-ever 20-rebound, 20-assist game, as well as the third-ever 10–20–20 game, with 14 points, 21 rebounds and 24 assists against the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers). This was his second time reaching each feat. He also tied the record for most assists (24) in a triple-double.
- On May 8, 2021, Russell Westbrook of the Washington Wizards tied Oscar Robertson for [most career triple-doubles](/wiki/Double-double#Triple-doubles_in_the_NBA), with a career total of 181. Two days later on May 10, Westbrook passed Robertson, with a career total of 182.
- On June 1, 2021, Damian Lillard of the Portland Trail Blazers broke an NBA playoff record with 12 made three-pointers, including 2 threes to force overtime and double-overtime as part of a 55-point performance during a 147–140 loss to the Denver Nuggets in game 5 of the first round.
- On June 8, 2021, Nikola Jokić became the first Denver Nuggets player, the first Serbian native, and the lowest-drafted player to be named NBA MVP; he is the first center since [Shaquille O'Neal](/wiki/Shaquille_O%27Neal) in 2000 to win NBA MVP. Jokić and his fellow Serbian countryman [Vasilije Micić](/wiki/Vasilije_Mici%C4%87) ([Anadolu Efes](/wiki/Anadolu_Efes_S.K.), [Turkey](/wiki/Basketbol_S%C3%BCper_Ligi)) became the first-ever pair of players from the same country to be awarded both NBA MVP and [EuroLeague MVP](/wiki/EuroLeague_MVP) honors in the same season.
- [Mitchell Robinson](/wiki/Mitchell_Robinson) set the record for the highest two-point field goal percentage in a season at 74.19%.
- Utah Jazz set the record for the most three-point field goals made per game (16.7) in a season.
- Los Angeles Clippers set the record for the highest free-throw percentage averaged (83.9%) in a season.
- Portland Trail Blazers set the record for the lowest average turnovers per game (11.1) in a season.

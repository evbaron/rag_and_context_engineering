# WrestleMania 13

**WrestleMania 13** was a 1997 [professional wrestling](/wiki/Professional_wrestling) [pay-per-view](/wiki/Pay-per-view) (PPV) [event](/wiki/List_of_WWE_pay-per-view_events) produced by the [World Wrestling Federation](/wiki/WWE) (WWF, now WWE). It was the 13th annual [WrestleMania](/wiki/WrestleMania) and was [presented](/wiki/Sponsor_(commercial)) by [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation_(console)). The event was held on March 23, 1997, at the [Rosemont Horizon](/wiki/Allstate_Arena) in the [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago) suburb of [Rosemont, Illinois](/wiki/Rosemont,_Illinois). This was the second WrestleMania held at this venue after the middle portion of [WrestleMania 2](/wiki/WrestleMania_2) in 1986. It would also be the final WrestleMania with [Vince McMahon](/wiki/Vince_McMahon) on commentary.

Eight matches were held at the event, including one on the *[Free for All](/wiki/WWF_Free_for_All)* pre-show. The [main event](/wiki/Card_(sports)#Main_event) was a [no disqualification match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#No_Disqualification_match/No_Holds_Barred_match) between [The Undertaker](/wiki/The_Undertaker) and [Sycho Sid](/wiki/Sid_Eudy) for the [WWF Championship](/wiki/WWE_Championship), which Undertaker won following interference from [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart). The main matches on the [undercard](/wiki/Card_(sports)#Undercard) were Bret Hart versus ["Stone Cold" Steve Austin](/wiki/%22Stone_Cold%22_Steve_Austin) in a [No Disqualification Submission match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Variations_of_singles_matches) with [Ken Shamrock](/wiki/Ken_Shamrock)  as the [special guest referee](/wiki/Special_guest_referee), [Legion of Doom](/wiki/The_Road_Warriors) and [Ahmed Johnson](/wiki/Ahmed_Johnson) versus [Nation of Domination](/wiki/Nation_of_Domination) in a [Chicago Street Fight](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Street_Fight), and [Rocky Maivia](/wiki/Rocky_Maivia) versus [The Sultan](/wiki/Rikishi_(wrestler)) for the [WWF Intercontinental Championship](/wiki/WWE_Intercontinental_Championship).

The event was attended by 18,197 who paid a total of $837,150 in admission fees, and drew a 0.77 buy rate. The event as a whole received mixed to negative reviews. However, the submission match between Bret Hart and "Stone Cold" Steve Austin was highly praised, being called one of the greatest matches in wrestling history, as well as being cited as the beginning of the [Attitude Era](/wiki/Attitude_Era) (due to the intense match style and the storyline [double turn](/wiki/Double_turn) between the pair); the event elevated "Stone Cold" Steve Austin's public popularity to become one of the very top WWF superstars. This match was the inaugural "Immortal Moment" recipient of the [WWE Hall of Fame](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame) in [2025](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame_(2025)).

## Production

### Background

The event was held at the [Rosemont Horizon](/wiki/Allstate_Arena) in the [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago) suburb of [Rosemont, Illinois](/wiki/Rosemont,_Illinois).

[WrestleMania](/wiki/WrestleMania) is considered the [World Wrestling Federation](/wiki/World_Wrestling_Federation)'s (WWF, now WWE) flagship [professional wrestling](/wiki/Professional_wrestling) [pay-per-view](/wiki/Pay-per-view) (PPV) event, having first been held in [1985](/wiki/WrestleMania_I). It has become the longest-running professional wrestling event in history and is held annually between mid-March to mid-April. It was the first of the WWF's original four pay-per-views, which includes [Royal Rumble](/wiki/Royal_Rumble), [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam), and [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series), which were dubbed the "Big Four", and was considered one of the "Big Five" PPVs, along with [King of the Ring](/wiki/King_of_the_Ring). WrestleMania 13 was scheduled to be held on March 23, 1997, at the [Rosemont Horizon](/wiki/Rosemont_Horizon) in the [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago) suburb of [Rosemont, Illinois](/wiki/Rosemont,_Illinois). The event was [presented](/wiki/Sponsor_(commercial)) by [PlayStation](/wiki/PlayStation). This was the second WrestleMania in the [Chicago metropolitan area](/wiki/Chicago_metropolitan_area), after [WrestleMania 2](/wiki/WrestleMania_2) in 1986.

### Storylines

The main [feud](/wiki/Feud_(professional_wrestling)) heading into WrestleMania 13 was between [The Undertaker](/wiki/The_Undertaker) and [Sycho Sid](/wiki/Sid_Eudy), with the two battling over the [WWF Championship](/wiki/WWE_Championship). At [In Your House 13](/wiki/In_Your_House_13:_Final_Four) in February, [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) last eliminated The Undertaker in the [Final Four match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Basic_elimination_matches) to win the vacant WWF Championship. Hart's reign, however, lasted only one day as he lost the title the next night on *[Monday Night Raw](/wiki/WWE_Raw)* to Sycho Sid after interference by [Stone Cold Steve Austin](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin), one of the other participants in the Final Four match (the other was [Vader](/wiki/Big_Van_Vader), who had no further involvement in the ongoing storyline). Due to being the runner-up to the title at Final Four, Undertaker was named the number one contender and was [booked](/wiki/Glossary_of_professional_wrestling_terms#Book) to challenge Sycho Sid for the title at WrestleMania. However, on the March 17 edition of *Raw Is War*, Sid defended the title against Hart in a [steel cage match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Cages), with the added stipulation that the winner would defend the title in their respective match at WrestleMania. During the match, both Undertaker and Austin interfered. Undertaker came out to help Sid because he wanted to wrestle Sid for the title, while Austin helped Hart because he wanted to turn his scheduled [submission match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Variations_of_singles_matches) with Hart into a title match. Sid would ultimately win the match and retain his title. As a result, Sid vs. Undertaker remained the [main event](/wiki/Card_(sports)#Main_event) of WrestleMania.

[Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) faced [Stone Cold Steve Austin](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin) in a [Submission Match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Variations_of_singles_matches) at WrestleMania

The second main match on the card was [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) versus [Stone Cold Steve Austin](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin) in a [Submission match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Variations_of_singles_matches). Hart and Austin's rivalry began after Austin won the [1996 King of the Ring tournament](/wiki/King_of_the_Ring#1996) and began taunting Hart, who was taking a hiatus from wrestling at the time. Austin began frequently insulting Hart in his speeches, in the hopes that Hart would accept his challenge to a match. Hart officially returned in October and accepted Austin's challenge, with the two facing each other at [Survivor Series 1996](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(1996)), where Hart defeated Austin. Their rivalry continued as Hart and Austin were the final two participants in the [1997 Royal Rumble match](/wiki/Royal_Rumble_(1997)). Hart had originally eliminated Austin from the match, but Austin's elimination was considered unofficial because the [referees](/wiki/Referee_(professional_wrestling)) did not see it, as they were busy attending to a brawl between eliminated wrestlers [Mankind](/wiki/Mick_Foley) and [Terry Funk](/wiki/Terry_Funk). Austin would eventually eliminate Hart from the match, thus becoming the official winner, albeit controversially. As a result, both Hart and Austin were named as participants in a [Four Corners Elimination match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Basic_elimination_matches) for the vacant [WWF Championship](/wiki/WWE_Championship) at [In Your House: Final Four](/wiki/In_Your_House_13:_Final_Four), which Hart won. The next night on *Monday Night Raw*, however, Austin cost Hart the WWF Championship against Sycho Sid when Hart had applied the [Sharpshooter](/wiki/Sharpshooter_(professional_wrestling)) on Sid, allowing Austin to nail Hart with a [steel chair](/wiki/Foreign_object_(professional_wrestling)), followed by Sid [powerbombing](/wiki/Powerbomb) Hart for the win. Hart and Austin were then booked to wrestle in a submission match at WrestleMania. Along the way, Hart was granted a shot at the WWF Championship against Sycho Sid in a [steel cage match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Cages) on the March 17 edition of *Raw Is War*, with the winner defending the title against their respective opponent at WrestleMania 13. Hart almost had the match won, until Undertaker interfered and helped Sid in getting the victory.

One of the main [undercard](/wiki/Card_(sports)#Undercard) matches was a [Chicago Street Fight](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Street_Fight) featuring [Ahmed Johnson](/wiki/Ahmed_Johnson) and [Legion of Doom](/wiki/The_Road_Warriors) ([Hawk](/wiki/Road_Warrior_Hawk) and [Animal](/wiki/Road_Warrior_Animal)) against [Nation of Domination](/wiki/Nation_of_Domination) ([Faarooq](/wiki/Ron_Simmons), [Crush](/wiki/Brian_Adams_(wrestler)) and [Savio Vega](/wiki/Savio_Vega)). Johnson was attacked by a newcomer named [Faarooq Asad](/wiki/Ron_Simmons), a [Roman](/wiki/Rome) gladiator on the July 22, 1996 edition of *Monday Night Raw* during a match for the [WWF Tag Team Championship](/wiki/World_Tag_Team_Championship_(WWE)). Ahmed was scheduled to defend his [Intercontinental Championship](/wiki/WWE_Intercontinental_Championship) against Faarooq at [SummerSlam 1996](/wiki/SummerSlam_(1996)), but [legitimate](/wiki/Legit_(professional_wrestling)) kidney problems forced him to vacate the Intercontinental Championship. In [storyline](/wiki/Glossary_of_professional_wrestling_terms#Angle), it was explained that Faarooq's attack had caused Ahmed's kidney problem. Ahmed returned from his injury in early 1997 and continued his feud with Faarooq, who had since formed The [Nation of Domination](/wiki/Nation_of_Domination) in Ahmed's absence. Ahmed then joined forces with the Legion of Doom and the trio were scheduled to wrestle the Nation of Domination in a Chicago Street Fight at WrestleMania 13.

Before the event aired live on [pay-per-view](/wiki/Pay-per-view) (PPV), [Billy Gunn](/wiki/Monty_Sopp) faced [Flash Funk](/wiki/Flash_Funk) at [Free for All](/wiki/WWE_Free_for_All). Gunn defeated Funk by pinning him following a [tornado DDT](/wiki/Tornado_DDT).

The first match that aired on television was a [Four-Way Tag Team Elimination Match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_tag_team_match_types#Elimination_tag_team_matches). The match featured [The Headbangers](/wiki/The_Headbangers) ([Mosh](/wiki/Mosh_(wrestler)) and [Thrasher](/wiki/Thrasher_(wrestler))), [The Godwinns](/wiki/The_Godwinns) ([Henry O. Godwinn](/wiki/Mark_Canterbury) and [Phineas I. Godwinn](/wiki/Dennis_Knight)), [The New Blackjacks](/wiki/The_Blackjacks#The_New_Blackjacks) ([Blackjack Windham](/wiki/Barry_Windham) and [Blackjack Bradshaw](/wiki/John_Layfield)) and the team of [Doug Furnas and Phil LaFon](/wiki/The_Can-Am_Express). The first elimination came when Bradshaw attacked the referee and got [disqualified](/wiki/Professional_wrestling#Disqualification), which led to The Blackjacks being eliminated. Later, Doug Furnas was counted-out, leading to the second elimination. This left The Headbangers and The Godwinns as the remaining teams. The match came to an end when Thrasher hit Phineas with a [Cannonball Senton](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_aerial_techniques#Senton) and won the match via pinfall. As a result of winning the match, The Headbangers earned a shot at the [WWF Tag Team Championship](/wiki/World_Tag_Team_Championship_(WWE)).

The second match pitted [Rocky Maivia](/wiki/Dwayne_Johnson) against [The Sultan](/wiki/Rikishi_(wrestler)) for Maivia's [WWF Intercontinental Championship](/wiki/WWE_Intercontinental_Championship). [The Honky Tonk Man](/wiki/The_Honky_Tonk_Man) would also provide commentary for the match. Sultan gained an early advantage in the match due to his size and power, but Maivia used his speed and quickness to push the big man outside the ring. Sultan then overpowered Maivia with [slams](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_throws) and [punches](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_attacks#Punch), but was unable to pin him. Maivia then gained the advantage on Sultan by nailing him with a [belly to belly suplex](/wiki/Suplex#Belly-to-belly_suplex), [Maivia Hurricane](/wiki/DDT_(professional_wrestling)#Float-over_DDT) and a [flying crossbody](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_aerial_techniques#Plancha) before [pinning](/wiki/Pin_(professional_wrestling)) Sultan with a [roll-up](/wiki/Pin_(professional_wrestling)#Roll-up) to win the match and retain the title. After the match, Sultan and his [managers](/wiki/Manager_(professional_wrestling)), [Bob Backlund](/wiki/Bob_Backlund) and [The Iron Sheik](/wiki/The_Iron_Sheik) attacked Maivia before his father, [Rocky Johnson](/wiki/Rocky_Johnson), made the save.

The third match was between [Hunter Hearst Helmsley](/wiki/Triple_H) and [Goldust](/wiki/Goldust). Goldust dominated the early portion of the match, but Helmsley began using [submission maneuvers](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_holds) to turn the tide. Eventually Goldust attempted his [Curtain Call](/wiki/Reverse_DDT) finisher, until Helmsley's [bodyguard](/wiki/Glossary_of_professional_wrestling_terms#Enforcer) [Chyna](/wiki/Chyna) manhandled Goldust's wife [Marlena](/wiki/Terri_Runnels) to distract him. Helmsley took advantage and hit Goldust with a [Pedigree](/wiki/Facebuster#Double_underhook_facebuster), enabling him to pin Goldust for the win.

The fourth match was a [tag team](/wiki/Tag_team) match for the [WWF Tag Team Championship](/wiki/World_Tag_Team_Championship_(WWE)), as [Owen Hart and The British Bulldog](/wiki/Owen_Hart_and_The_British_Bulldog) defended the titles against [Mankind](/wiki/Mick_Foley) and [Vader](/wiki/Big_Van_Vader). Vader made easy work of Owen in the beginning of the match. Eventually, both men would tag out, bringing Bulldog and Mankind into the match. Eventually, Bulldog applied a [sleeper hold](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_holds#Sleeper_hold) on Mankind which caused both of them to fall to the outside, where they continued to brawl. While the [referee](/wiki/Referee_(professional_wrestling)) was distracted, Vader took advantage and hit Bulldog with [Paul Bearer](/wiki/Paul_Bearer)'s urn before dragging him into the ring. Vader and Mankind then both took turns and continued to attack Bulldog. Owen tried to save his partner, but Vader used his strength while Mankind used his cheating tactics to overwhelm Owen. Mankind then trapped Bulldog to the outside and applied the mandible claw on him. Both men were [counted out](/wiki/Professional_wrestling#Countout) as the match ended in a draw. As a result, Hart and Bulldog retained the championship.

[Ken Shamrock](/wiki/Ken_Shamrock) was the [guest referee](/wiki/Referee_(professional_wrestling)#Special_guest_referees) for the [submission match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Variations_of_singles_matches) between [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) and [Stone Cold Steve Austin](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin).

The fifth match was a [No Disqualification Submission match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Variations_of_singles_matches) between Bret Hart and Stone Cold Steve Austin. [UFC](/wiki/Ultimate_Fighting_Championship) fighter [Ken Shamrock](/wiki/Ken_Shamrock) was named as the [special guest referee](/wiki/Referee_(professional_wrestling)#Special_guest_referees) for this match. During Hart's entrance, Austin attacked him and the two brawled from the ring to the outside. Hart then tossed Austin into the steel ring post, while Austin drove Hart onto the steel barrier. The two men then began fighting in the crowd, where both men hit each other with several [foreign objects](/wiki/Glossary_of_professional_wrestling_terms#Foreign_object). Shamrock followed the two and brought them back towards the ring where Austin attempted to use steel steps on Hart, but Hart stopped him with a kick to the midsection. As the action finally began in the ring, Hart focused on Austin's injured leg. Eventually, Hart busted Austin open with an Irish whip into the steel barricade and Austin's head began to bleed profusely. Hart then tried to use a steel chair to further injure Austin's leg, but Austin countered Hart and began nailing him repeatedly with the chair. Eventually, Hart hit Austin in the head with the ring bell, causing Austin to bleed further. He then applied a [Sharpshooter](/wiki/Sharpshooter_(professional_wrestling)) on Austin who did not submit and tried to resist, but eventually passed out from the pain and from loss of blood. Shamrock then awarded the match to Hart, but Hart continued to attack Austin which led to a double-turn as the fans turned on Hart and began cheering for Austin. Shamrock then pulled Hart off of Austin and executed a waistlock takedown, before physically challenging him to a fight. Hart declined to fight Shamrock and left the ring to a chorus of boos. Austin, meanwhile, after regaining consciousness, hit a [Stunner](/wiki/Stunner_(professional_wrestling)) on a referee when he tried to help Austin out, then slowly limped away to backstage, while the crowd loudly chanted his name.

The sixth match of the event was a [Chicago Street Fight](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#Street_Fight) between the [Legion of Doom](/wiki/The_Road_Warriors) ([Hawk](/wiki/Road_Warrior_Hawk) and [Animal](/wiki/Road_Warrior_Animal)) and [Ahmed Johnson](/wiki/Ahmed_Johnson) and [Nation of Domination](/wiki/Nation_of_Domination) ([Faarooq](/wiki/Ron_Simmons), [Crush](/wiki/Brian_Adams_(wrestler)) and [Savio Vega](/wiki/Savio_Vega)). The match saw both teams use numerous foreign objects while brawling inside and outside the ring. Finally, Animal hit Crush with a [2x4](/wiki/Dimensional_lumber) and pinned him to win the match for his team.

### Main event

[The Undertaker](/wiki/The_Undertaker), who won the [WWF World Heavyweight Championship](/wiki/WWE_Championship) from [Sycho Sid](/wiki/Sid_Eudy)

The [main event](/wiki/Card_(sports)#Main_event) was a [No Disqualification Match](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_match_types#No_Disqualification_match/No_Holds_Barred_match) between [The Undertaker](/wiki/The_Undertaker) and reigning champion [Sycho Sid](/wiki/Sid_Eudy) for the [WWF Championship](/wiki/WWE_Championship). [Shawn Michaels](/wiki/Shawn_Michaels) also provided commentary for the match. Before the match could begin, [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) came out and insulted Undertaker, Michaels and particularly Sid, while claimed that he had been repeatedly screwed out of the title. Sid eventually [powerbombed](/wiki/Powerbomb) Hart before Undertaker took advantage and began attacking the champion from behind as the match finally began. At one point, Undertaker went for [Old School](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_aerial_techniques#Arm_twist_ropewalk_chop), but Sid snatched him out of mid-air into a [bearhug](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_holds#Bear_hug). Sid then attacked Undertaker with various maneuvers and even attacked him with television monitors, before applying a [camel clutch](/wiki/Professional_wrestling_holds#Camel_clutch) on Undertaker. Sid had the advantage in the match until Bret Hart came back and attacked Sid with a steel chair. Sid would recover and attempted his signature powerbomb on Undertaker, but Hart returned once again and distracted Sid, which allowed Undertaker to hit Sid with the [Tombstone](/wiki/Piledriver_(professional_wrestling)#Kneeling_reverse_piledriver) and pin him to win the match and the WWF Championship.

## Reception

The event was attended by 18,197 who paid a total of $837,150 in admission fees and drew a 0.77 buy-rate.

In 2011, Marc Elusive of *411Mania* gave the event an overall score of 7.0 out of 10.0 and noted that "The Attitude Era began here..." and that the main event was "a very boring match". John Canton of *TJR Wrestling* gave the event an overall score of 4 out of 10 and said that the show was "poor" and noted that the main event "sucked for the first 15 minutes, but the ending was okay".

Despite the lackluster reviews towards the event, the submission match between Bret Hart and "Stone Cold" Steve Austin was highly praised. In 2007, it was placed #1 on [IGN](/wiki/IGN)'s list of Top 20 Matches in WrestleMania History, and described as a match that "launched an era". Thomas Golianopoulos of *Complex Sports* also ranked it at number 1 in his list of the 50 Greatest Matches in WrestleMania History, citing the match's six factors of storyline, innovation, psychology, finish, post-match angle, and fallout. Elusive of *411Mania* described the match as "outstanding" and "that helped propel Steve Austin into the stratosphere and become the star of the late 90s and the early 00s", while also noting the double-turn after the match. John Canton called the match "wrestling perfection". It received a [five-star rating](/wiki/List_of_professional_wrestling_matches_rated_5_or_more_stars_by_Dave_Meltzer) from [Dave Meltzer](/wiki/Dave_Meltzer) and was also voted Match of the Year (1997) by readers of his *Wrestling Observer Newsletter* publication. *[Pro Wrestling Illustrated](/wiki/Pro_Wrestling_Illustrated)* readers named it Match of the Year (1997). The submission match would also be the last time a WrestleMania match would receive a five-star rating until [WrestleMania 39](/wiki/WrestleMania_39) in 2023 when [Kevin Owens and Sami Zayn](/wiki/Kevin_Owens_and_Sami_Zayn) vs. [The Usos](/wiki/The_Usos) ([Jey Uso](/wiki/Jey_Uso) and [Jimmy Uso](/wiki/Jimmy_Uso)) on Night 1 and [Gunther](/wiki/Gunther_(wrestler)) vs. [Sheamus](/wiki/Sheamus) vs. [Drew McIntyre](/wiki/Drew_McIntyre) on Night 2 both received a five-star rating. Hart would call it his favourite match, labeling it "a real masterpiece". [Ken Shamrock](/wiki/Ken_Shamrock) would call it "one of the greatest matches in wrestling history", and [Jim Ross](/wiki/Jim_Ross) called it the greatest match he had called at WrestleMania and the most "well-executed" match he had seen. The match would later be inducted as the inaugural "Immortal Moment" recipient of the [WWE Hall of Fame](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame) in [2025](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame_(2025)).

## Aftermath

"Stone Cold" Steve Austin and Bret Hart would continue their rivalry after WrestleMania, however Austin was now a [babyface](/wiki/Face_(professional_wrestling)) and his popularity would start to increase considerably while Hart was now a [heel](/wiki/Heel_(professional_wrestling)). The next night on *Raw* following WrestleMania, Hart cut a promo where he apologized to his Canadian fans, saying that he felt like he let them down by not actually getting Austin to submit, but Hart then cemented his heel turn by refusing to apologize to the American fans, chastising them for cheering Austin, even though Hart had won the match, while claiming that American fans didn't know what "true heroes" were. Austin would then interrupt Hart and argue that the fans cheered Austin not because he lost the match, but because Austin had refused to quit. Hart would eventually recruit [British Bulldog](/wiki/Davey_Boy_Smith), [Owen Hart](/wiki/Owen_Hart), [Brian Pillman](/wiki/Brian_Pillman), and former [tag team](/wiki/Tag_team) partner [Jim Neidhart](/wiki/Jim_Neidhart) to reform [The Hart Foundation](/wiki/The_Hart_Foundation), where Hart would continue to criticize American fans and claim that Canadian fans represented the proper "family values" that the Hart Foundation stood for. At [In Your House 14: Revenge of the 'Taker](/wiki/In_Your_House_14:_Revenge_of_the_%27Taker) in April, Hart and Austin once again had a match in which Austin won by [disqualification](/wiki/Professional_wrestling#Disqualification) after interference from the rest of the Foundation. Their [feud](/wiki/Feud_(professional_wrestling)) would continue until [In Your House 16: Canadian Stampede](/wiki/In_Your_House_16:_Canadian_Stampede) in July, where the [Hart Foundation](/wiki/The_Hart_Foundation) (working as faces in Canada) defeated the American team of Austin, [Ken Shamrock](/wiki/Ken_Shamrock), [Legion of Doom](/wiki/The_Road_Warriors) ([Hawk](/wiki/Road_Warrior_Hawk) and [Animal](/wiki/Road_Warrior_Animal)) and [Goldust](/wiki/Goldust).

[The Undertaker](/wiki/The_Undertaker) received a [push](/wiki/Push_(professional_wrestling)) after he won his second WWF Championship at WrestleMania 13. His reign lasted a total of [133 days](/wiki/List_of_WWE_Champions) which included battles with [Mankind](/wiki/Mick_Foley), [Steve Austin](/wiki/Stone_Cold_Steve_Austin), [Faarooq](/wiki/Ron_Simmons) and [Vader](/wiki/Big_Van_Vader). At [SummerSlam 1997](/wiki/SummerSlam_(1997)), Undertaker's long reign finally ended when he lost the title to [Bret Hart](/wiki/Bret_Hart) when [special guest referee](/wiki/Referee_(professional_wrestling)#Special_guest_referees) [Shawn Michaels](/wiki/Shawn_Michaels) accidentally struck Undertaker with a steel chair, which was intended for Hart.

After losing the WWF Championship to The Undertaker at WrestleMania, Sycho Sid's push began to diminish greatly and after [King of the Ring](/wiki/King_of_the_Ring_(1997)), Sid would quietly disappear from WWF programming. In reality, Sid would leave the WWF to focus on recovering from a lingering neck injury. After making a few appearances for [Extreme Championship Wrestling](/wiki/Extreme_Championship_Wrestling) in early 1999, Sid would eventually return to rival promotion [World Championship Wrestling](/wiki/World_Championship_Wrestling) (WCW) in mid-1999 and continue to wrestle as Sid Vicious until he suffered a career-ending leg injury at [WCW Sin](/wiki/WCW_Sin) on January 14, 2001.

The event's Hart/Austin match would be inducted into the [WWE Hall of Fame](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame) as part of the [class of 2025](/wiki/WWE_Hall_of_Fame_(2025)), receiving the WWE Hall of Fame's inaugural "Immortal Moment" award.

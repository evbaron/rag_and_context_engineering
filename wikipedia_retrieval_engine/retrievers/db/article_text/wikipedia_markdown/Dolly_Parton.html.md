**Dolly Rebecca Parton** (born January 19, 1946) is an American singer, songwriter, actress, philanthropist, and businesswoman. After achieving success as a songwriter for other artists, Parton's debut album, *[Hello, I'm Dolly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_I%27m_Dolly)*, was released in 1967, commencing a career spanning 60 years and [50 studio albums](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton_albums_discography). Referred to as the "[Queen of Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorific_nicknames_in_popular_music#P)", Parton is one of the most-honored female country performers in history and has received [various accolades](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Dolly_Parton), including eleven [Grammy Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Awards) and three [Emmy Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards), as well as nominations for two [Academy Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards) including a humanitarian honorary Oscar win in 2025, six [Golden Globe Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and a [Tony Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award).

Parton has sold more than 100 million records worldwide, making her one of the [best-selling music artists ever](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists). Her music includes [Recording Industry Association of America](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA)-certified gold, platinum and multi-platinum awards. She has had 25 [singles](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_(music)) reach No. 1 on the *[Billboard](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* country music charts, a record for a female artist (tied with [Reba McEntire](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire)). She has 44 career Top 10 country albums, a record for any artist and she has 110 career-charted singles over the past 40 years. Her 49th solo studio album, *[Rockstar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_(Dolly_Parton_album))* (2023), became her highest-charting [*Billboard* 200](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) album, peaking at number three. Parton has composed over 3,000 songs, including "[I Will Always Love You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You)" (a two-time U.S. country chart-topper and an international hit for [Whitney Houston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston)), "[Jolene](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene)", "[Coat of Many Colors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song))" and "[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song))". As an actress, she has starred in the films *[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film))* (1980) and *[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Little_Whorehouse_in_Texas_(film))* (1982), for each of which she earned [Best Actress](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical) [Golden Globe](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe) nominations, as well as *[Rhinestone](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film))* (1984), *[Steel Magnolias](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Magnolias)* (1989), *[Straight Talk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Talk)* (1992), and *[Joyful Noise](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyful_Noise_(film))* (2012).

She was honored with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 1984, the [National Medal of Arts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts) in 2004, the [Kennedy Center Honors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors) in 2006, the [Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Lifetime_Achievement_Award) in 2011 and the [Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Hersholt_Humanitarian_Award) in 2025. In 1986, Parton was inducted into the [Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame). In 2021, she was included on the [*Time* 100](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100), *[Time](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine))*'s annual list of the 100 most influential people in the world. She was ranked at No. 27 on *[Rolling Stone](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*'s 2023 list of the 200 Greatest Singers of All Time. In 2025, it was announced that Parton would be the recipient of the 2026 [Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Hersholt_Humanitarian_Award) (an honorary Oscar).

Outside of her work in the music and film industries, Parton co-owns [The Dollywood Company](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dollywood_Company), which manages a number of entertainment venues including the [Dollywood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood) theme park, the [Splash Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood%27s_Splash_Country) water park and a number of [dinner theater](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_theater) venues such as The Dolly Parton Stampede and Pirates Voyage. She has founded a number of charitable and philanthropic organizations, chief among them being the [Dollywood Foundation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood_Foundation), who manage a number of projects to bring education and poverty relief to [East Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee), where she was raised.

## Early life

Dolly Rebecca Parton was born on January 19, 1946, in a one-room cabin on the banks of the [Little Pigeon River](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Pigeon_River_(Tennessee)) in [Pittman Center, Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittman_Center,_Tennessee).
She is the fourth of twelve children born to Avie Lee Caroline ([née](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Owens; 1923–2003) and Robert Lee Parton Sr. (1921–2000). Parton's middle name comes from her maternal great-great-grandmother Rebecca (née Dunn) Whitted.

Parton's father, known as "Lee", worked in the mountains of [East Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee), first as a [sharecropper](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharecropper) and later tending his own small [tobacco](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco) farm and acreage. He also worked construction jobs to supplement the farm's small income. Despite her father's [illiteracy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiteracy), Parton has often commented that he was one of the smartest people she has ever known with regard to business and making a profit.

Parton's mother cared for their large family. Her eleven pregnancies (the tenth being twins) in twenty years made her a mother of twelve by age thirty-five. Parton attributes her musical abilities to the influence of her mother; often in poor health, she still managed to keep house and entertain her children with [Smoky Mountain](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains) [folklore](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) and ancient [ballads](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad). Having [Welsh](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people) ancestors, Avie Lee knew many old ballads that [immigrants](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrants) from the British Isles brought to southern [Appalachia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia) in the 18th and 19th century. Avie Lee's father, Jake Owens, was a [Pentecostal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal) preacher and Parton and her siblings all attended church regularly.

Parton has long credited her father for her business savvy and her mother's family for her musical abilities.

When Parton was a young girl, her family moved from the Pittman Center area to a two-room cabin and farm on nearby Locust Ridge. Most of her cherished memories of youth happened there. A replica of the Locust Ridge cabin is displayed at Parton's namesake theme park [Dollywood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood). The farm acreage and surrounding woodland inspired her to write the song "[My Tennessee Mountain Home](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Tennessee_Mountain_Home_(song))" in the 1970s. Years after the farm was sold, Parton bought it back in the late 1980s. Her brother Bobby helped with building restoration and new construction.

Parton has described her family as being "dirt poor". Parton's father paid [missionary](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) Dr. Robert F. Thomas with a sack of cornmeal for delivering her. Later in life, Parton wrote a song about Dr. Thomas. She also outlined her family's poverty in her early songs "[Coat of Many Colors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song))" and "[In the Good Old Days (When Times Were Bad)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Good_Old_Days_(When_Times_Were_Bad))".

For six or seven years, Parton and her family lived in their one-bedroom cabin on their small [subsistence farm](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture) on Locust Ridge. This was a predominantly Pentecostal area located north of the [Greenbrier Valley of the Great Smoky Mountains](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenbrier_(Great_Smoky_Mountains)).
Music played an important role in her early life. She was brought up in the [Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_God_(Cleveland,_Tennessee)), in a congregation her grandfather, Jake Robert Owens, pastored. Her earliest public performances were in the church, beginning at age six. At seven, she started playing a homemade guitar. When she was eight, her uncle bought Dolly her first real guitar.

The Parton family was well-fed despite their poverty, and her 2024 cookbook, *Good Lookin' Cookin'* (co-written with her sister [Rachel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Parton_George)), recalls numerous family meals.

After graduating from [Sevier County High School](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevier_County_High_School) in 1964, Parton moved to [Nashville](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville) the next day.

## Music career

### 1956–1966: Early work and songwriting

Parton began performing as a child, singing on local radio and television programs in the [East Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Tennessee) area. By 10 years old, she was appearing on *[The Cas Walker Show](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cas_Walker#Radio_and_television)* on both [WIVK Radio](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WIVK-FM) and [WBIR-TV](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBIR-TV) in [Knoxville](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville,_Tennessee), Tennessee. At 13, she recorded the single "Puppy Love" on a small Louisiana label, [Goldband Records](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldband_Records), and appeared at the [Grand Ole Opry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry), where she first met [Johnny Cash](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash), who encouraged her to follow her own instincts regarding her career.

*[Billboard](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* advertisement, September 4, 1965

Parton's initial success came as a songwriter, having signed with [Combine Publishing](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combine_Music) shortly after her arrival. Along with her frequent songwriting partner, uncle [Bill Owens](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Owens_(songwriter)), she wrote several charting singles during this time, including two Top 10 hits for [Bill Phillips](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Phillips_(singer)): "[Put It Off Until Tomorrow](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put_It_Off_Until_Tomorrow)" and "The Company You Keep" (1966). She also wrote [Skeeter Davis](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeeter_Davis)'s number 11 hit "[Fuel to the Flame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_to_the_Flame)" (1967). Her songs were recorded by many other artists during this period, including [Kitty Wells](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Wells) and [Hank Williams Jr.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams_Jr.) At 19, She signed with [Monument Records](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Records) in 1965, where she was initially pitched as a [bubblegum pop](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubblegum_pop) singer. She released a string of singles, but the only one that charted, "Happy, Happy Birthday Baby", did not crack the [*Billboard* Hot 100](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100). Although she expressed a desire to record country material, Monument resisted, thinking her unusually high [soprano](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soprano) voice was not suited to the genre.

After her uncredited composition of "Put It Off Until Tomorrow" with Bill Phillips went to number six on the country chart in 1966, the label relented and allowed her to record country. Her first country single, "Dumb Blonde", composed by [Curly Putman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curly_Putman), reached number 24 on the country chart in 1967, followed by "Something Fishy", which went to number 17. The two songs appeared on her first full-length album, *[Hello, I'm Dolly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_I%27m_Dolly)*.

### 1967–1975: Country music success

Porter Wagoner and Dolly Parton in 1969

In 1967, musician and country music entertainer [Porter Wagoner](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_Wagoner) invited Parton to join *The Porter Wagoner Show*, offering her a regular spot on his weekly [syndicated](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_syndication) television program and in his road show. As documented in her 1994 autobiography, much of Wagoner's audience was initially unhappy that Parton had replaced previous performer [Norma Jean](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norma_Jean_(singer)), who had left the show, sometimes chanting loudly for Norma Jean from the audience. With Wagoner's assistance however, Parton was eventually accepted. Wagoner convinced his label, [RCA Victor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Victor), to sign her. RCA decided to protect their investment by releasing her first single as a [duet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duet) with Wagoner. That song, a remake of [Tom Paxton](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Paxton)'s "[The Last Thing on My Mind](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Thing_on_My_Mind)", released in late 1967, reached the country Top 10 in January 1968, launching a six-year streak of virtually uninterrupted Top 10 singles for the pair.

Parton's first solo single for RCA Victor, "[Just Because I'm a Woman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Because_I%27m_a_Woman_(1968_album)#Album_information)", was released in the summer of 1968 and was a moderate chart hit, reaching number 17. For the next two years, none of her solo efforts – even "[In the Good Old Days (When Times Were Bad)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Good_Old_Days_(When_Times_Were_Bad))", which later became a standard – were as successful as her duets with Wagoner. The duo was named [Vocal Group of the Year](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association_Awards) in 1968 by the [Country Music Association](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association), but Parton's solo records were continually ignored. Wagoner had a significant financial stake in her future; as of 1969, he was her co-producer and owned nearly half of Owe-Par, the publishing company Parton had founded with Bill Owens.

By 1970, both Parton and Wagoner had grown frustrated by her lack of solo chart success. Wagoner persuaded Parton to record [Jimmie Rodgers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Rodgers_(country_singer))' "[Mule Skinner Blues](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mule_Skinner_Blues)", a [gimmick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimmick) that worked. The record shot to number three, followed closely by her first number-one single, "[Joshua](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_(song))" in February 1971. For the next two years, she had numerous solo hits in addition to her duets, including her signature song, "[Coat of Many Colors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song))" (number four, 1971). Further Top 20 singles included "The Right Combination" and "Burning the Midnight Oil" (both duets with Wagoner, 1971); "Lost Forever in Your Kiss" (with Wagoner), "[Touch Your Woman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_Your_Woman)" (1972), "[My Tennessee Mountain Home](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Tennessee_Mountain_Home)" and "Travelin' Man" (1973).

Although her solo singles and the Wagoner duets were successful, her biggest hit of this period was "[Jolene](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene)". Released in late 1973, the song topped the country chart in February 1974 and reached the lower regions of the Hot 100. It also eventually charted in the U.K., reaching number seven in 1976, representing Parton's first U.K. success. Parton, who had always envisioned a solo career, made the decision to leave Wagoner's show and she stopped appearing in mid-1974. The pair performed their last duet concert in April 1974, although they remained affiliated, with Wagoner helping to produce her records through 1975. Their final release as a duo was 1975's *[Say Forever You'll Be Mine](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Say_Forever_You%27ll_Be_Mine)*.

In 1974, her song, "[I Will Always Love You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You)", written about her professional break from Wagoner, went to number one on the country chart. Around the same time, [Elvis Presley](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley) indicated that he wanted to record the song. Parton was interested until Presley's manager, [Colonel Tom Parker](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Tom_Parker), told her that it was standard procedure for the songwriter to sign over half of the publishing rights to any song recorded by Presley. Parton refused. That decision has been credited with helping to make her many millions of dollars in royalties from the song over the years. Parton had three solo singles reach number one on the country chart in 1974 ("Jolene", "I Will Always Love You" and "[Love Is Like a Butterfly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Is_Like_a_Butterfly_(song))"), as well as the duet with Porter Wagoner, "[Please Don't Stop Loving Me](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Don%27t_Stop_Loving_Me)". Parton again topped the singles chart in 1975 with "[The Bargain Store](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bargain_Store)". In a 2019 episode of the [Sky Arts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Arts) music series *Brian Johnson: A Life on the Road*, Parton described finding old cassette tapes later on in her career and discovered that she had composed both "Jolene" and "I Will Always Love You" in the same songwriting session. As she told Johnson, "Buddy, that was a good night!"

### 1976–1986: A broader audience

Parton in 1977

Between 1974 and 1980, Parton had a series of country hits, with eight singles reaching number one. Her influence on pop culture is reflected by the many performers covering her songs, including mainstream and crossover artists such as [Olivia Newton-John](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Newton-John), [Emmylou Harris](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris) and [Linda Ronstadt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt).

Parton began to embark on a high-profile crossover campaign, attempting to aim her music in a more mainstream direction and increase her visibility outside of the confines of country music. In 1976, she began working closely with [Sandy Gallin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Gallin), who served as her personal manager for the next 25 years. With her 1976 album *[All I Can Do](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_I_Can_Do_(album))*, which she co-produced with Porter Wagoner, Parton began taking more of an active role in production and began specifically aiming her music in a more mainstream, pop direction. Her first entirely self-produced effort, *[New Harvest...First Gathering](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Harvest...First_Gathering)* (1977), highlighted her pop sensibilities, both in terms of choice of songs and production: the album contained covers of the pop and R&B classics "[My Girl](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Girl_(The_Temptations_song))" and "[Higher and Higher](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/(Your_Love_Keeps_Lifting_Me)_Higher_and_Higher)". Though the album was well received and topped the U.S. country albums chart, neither it nor its single "[Light of a Clear Blue Morning](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_of_a_Clear_Blue_Morning)" made much of an impression on the pop charts.

After *New Harvest*'s disappointing crossover performance, Parton turned to high-profile pop producer [Gary Klein](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Klein_(producer)) for her next album. The result, 1977's *[Here You Come Again](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_You_Come_Again)*, became her first million-seller, topping the country album chart and reaching number 20 on the pop chart. The [Barry Mann](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Mann)-[Cynthia Weil](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Weil)-penned [title track](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_You_Come_Again_(song)) topped the country singles chart and became Parton's first Top 10 single on the pop chart (no. 3). A second single, the double A-sided "[Two Doors Down](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Doors_Down_(Dolly_Parton_song))"/"[It's All Wrong, But It's All Right](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_All_Wrong,_But_It%27s_All_Right)" topped the country chart and crossed over to the pop Top 20. For the remainder of the 1970s and into the early 1980s, many of her subsequent singles moved up on both charts simultaneously. Her albums during this period were developed specifically for pop-[crossover](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_(music)) success.

With [Carol Burnett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Burnett), 1979

In 1978, Parton won a [Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Country_Vocal_Performance) for her *Here You Come Again* album. She continued to have hits with "[Heartbreaker](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_(Dolly_Parton_song))" (1978), "[Baby I'm Burning](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_I%27m_Burning)" (1979) and "[You're the Only One](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You%27re_the_Only_One_(Dolly_Parton_song))" (1979), all of which charted in the pop [Top 40](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_40) and topped the country chart. "[Sweet Summer Lovin'](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Summer_Lovin%27)" (1979) became the first Parton single in two years to not top the country chart, although it did reach the Top 10. During this period, her visibility continued to increase with multiple television appearances. A highly publicized candid interview on a *[Barbara Walters Special](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Walters_Special)* in 1977, timed to coincide with *Here You Come Again*'s release, was followed by appearances in 1978 on [Cher](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher)'s ABC [television special](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher..._Special) and her own joint special with [Carol Burnett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Burnett) on CBS, *[Dolly & Carol in Nashville](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_%26_Carol_in_Nashville)*.

Parton served as one of three co-hosts (along with [Roy Clark](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Clark) and [Glen Campbell](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Campbell)) on the CBS special *Fifty Years of Country Music*. In 1979, Parton hosted the [NBC](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) special *The Seventies: An Explosion of Country Music*, performed live at the [Ford Theatre](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Theatre) in [Washington, D.C.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) and whose audience included President [Jimmy Carter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter). Her commercial success grew in 1980, with three consecutive country chart number-one hits: the [Donna Summer](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Summer)-written "[Starting Over Again](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_Over_Again_(Donna_Summer_song))", "[Old Flames Can't Hold a Candle to You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Flames_Can%27t_Hold_a_Candle_to_You)" and "[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song))", which topped the country and pop charts in early 1981. She had another Top 10 single that year with "Making Plans" from the 1980 [album with Porter Wagoner](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_%26_Dolly), released as part of a lawsuit settlement between the pair.

Dolly Parton holding a baby in [Honolulu, Hawaii](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu), 1983

The theme song to the 1980 feature film *[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film))*, in which she starred with [Jane Fonda](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Fonda) and [Lily Tomlin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Tomlin), not only reached number one on the country chart, but also on the pop and the [adult-contemporary](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_contemporary_music) charts in February 1981, giving her a triple number-one hit. Parton became one of the few female country singers to have a number-one single on the country and pop charts simultaneously. It also received a nomination for an [Academy Award for Best Original Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song). Her singles continued to appear consistently in the country Top 10. Between 1981 and 1985, she had twelve Top 10 hits, with half of them hitting number one. She continued to make inroads on the pop charts as well. A re-recorded version of "I Will Always Love You", from the feature film *[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Little_Whorehouse_in_Texas_(film))* (1982) scraped the Top 50 that year and her duet with [Kenny Rogers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Rogers), "[Islands in the Stream](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_in_the_Stream_(song))" (written by the [Bee Gees](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Gees) and produced by [Barry Gibb](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Gibb)), spent two weeks at number one in 1983.

In the mid-1980s, her record sales were still relatively strong, with songs like "[Save the Last Dance for Me](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Last_Dance_for_Me)", "[Tennessee Homesick Blues](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Homesick_Blues)", "God Won't Get You" (1984), "[Real Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Love_(Dolly_Parton_and_Kenny_Rogers_song))" (another duet with Kenny Rogers), "[Don't Call It Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_Call_It_Love_(song)#Dolly_Parton_cover)" (1985) and "[Think About Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_About_Love_(song))" (1986) all reaching the country Top 10. "[Tennessee Homesick Blues](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Homesick_Blues)", "[Think About Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_About_Love_(song))" and "[Real Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Love_(Dolly_Parton_and_Kenny_Rogers_song))" reached number one on the country chart and became modest crossover hits. Despite this success, RCA Records did not renew her contract after it expired in 1986 and she signed with [Columbia Records](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records) in 1987.

### 1987–2005: Country and bluegrass period

Along with [Emmylou Harris](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris) and [Linda Ronstadt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt), Parton released *[Trio](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trio_(1987_album))* (1987) to critical acclaim. The album revitalized Parton's music career, spending five weeks at number one on *Billboard's* Country Albums chart and also reached the Top 10 on *Billboard*'s Top 200 Albums chart. It sold several million copies and produced four Top 10 country hits, including [Phil Spector](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Spector)'s "[To Know Him Is to Love Him](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Know_Him_Is_to_Love_Him)", which went to number one. *Trio* won the [Grammy Award for Best Country Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Performance_by_a_Duo_or_Group_with_Vocal) and was nominated for a [Grammy Award for Album of the Year](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year). After a further attempt at pop success with the album *[Rainbow](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_(Dolly_Parton_album))* (1987), which included the single "[The River Unbroken](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_River_Unbroken)", it was not well-received commercially, causing Parton to focus on recording country material. *[White Limozeen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Limozeen)* (1989) produced two number one hits in "[Why'd You Come in Here Lookin' Like That](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why%27d_You_Come_in_Here_Lookin%27_Like_That)" and "[Yellow Roses](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Roses_(Dolly_Parton_song))". Although Parton's career appeared to be revived, it was actually just a brief revival before contemporary country music came in the early 1990s and moved most veteran artists off the charts.

Dolly Parton at a recording session c. 1989

A duet with [Ricky Van Shelton](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Van_Shelton), "[Rockin' Years](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockin%27_Years)" (1991) reached number one, though Parton's greatest commercial fortune of the decade came when Whitney Houston recorded "I Will Always Love You" for the soundtrack of the feature film *[The Bodyguard](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bodyguard_(1992_film))* (1992). Both the single and the album were massively successful. Parton's soundtrack album from the 1992 film, *[Straight Talk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Talk)*, however, was less successful. But her 1993 album *[Slow Dancing with the Moon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Dancing_with_the_Moon)* won critical acclaim and did well on the charts, reaching number four on the country albums chart and number 16 on the *Billboard* 200 album chart. It would also become Platinum certified. She recorded "The Day I Fall in Love" as a duet with [James Ingram](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ingram) for the feature film *[Beethoven's 2nd](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beethoven%27s_2nd_(film))* (1993). The songwriters Ingram, [Carole Bayer Sager](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carole_Bayer_Sager) and [Clif Magness](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clif_Magness) were nominated for an Academy Award for Best Original Song and Parton and Ingram performed the song at the awards telecast. Similar to her earlier collaborative album with Emmy-Lou Harris and Linda Ronstadt, Parton released *[Honky Tonk Angels](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honky_Tonk_Angels)* in the fall of 1993 with [Loretta Lynn](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynn) and [Tammy Wynette](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammy_Wynette). It was certified as [a gold album](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oro_album) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) and helped revive both Wynette and Lynn's careers. Also in 1994, Parton contributed the song "You Gotta Be My Baby" to the AIDS benefit album *[Red Hot + Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_%2B_Country)* produced by the [Red Hot Organization](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hot_Organization). A live acoustic album, *[Heartsongs: Live from Home](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartsongs:_Live_from_Home)*, featuring stripped-down versions of some of her hits, as well as some traditional songs, was released in late 1994.

Parton's recorded music during the mid-to-late-1990s remained steady and somewhat eclectic. Her 1995 re-recording of "I Will Always Love You", performed as a duet with [Vince Gill](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Gill) from her album *[Something Special](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Something_Special_(Dolly_Parton_album)),* won the Country Music Association's [Vocal Event of the Year Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Association_Awards#Awards_by_year). The following year, *[Treasures](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasures_(Dolly_Parton_album))*, an album of covers of 1960s/70s hits was released and featured a diverse collection of material, including songs by [Mac Davis](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_Davis), [Pete Seeger](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Seeger), [Kris Kristofferson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris_Kristofferson), [Cat Stevens](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_Stevens) and [Neil Young](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young). Her recording of Stevens' "[Peace Train](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Train)" was later re-mixed and released as a dance single, reaching *Billboard's* dance singles chart. Her 1998 country-rock album *[Hungry Again](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_Again)* was made up entirely of her own compositions. Although neither of the album's two singles, "(Why Don't More Women Sing) Honky Tonk Songs" and "Salt in my Tears", charted, videos for both songs received significant airplay on [CMT](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Television). A second and more contemporary collaboration with Harris and Ronstadt, *[Trio II](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trio_II)*, was released in early 1999. Its cover of Neil Young's song "[After the Gold Rush](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_the_Gold_Rush_(song))" won a [Grammy Award for Best Country Collaboration with Vocals](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Collaboration_with_Vocals). Parton also was inducted into the [Country Music Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame) in 1999.

Parton recorded a series of [bluegrass](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music)-inspired albums, beginning with *[The Grass Is Blue](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grass_Is_Blue)* (1999), winning a [Grammy Award for Best Bluegrass Album](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Bluegrass_Album); and *[Little Sparrow](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Sparrow)* (2001), with its cover of [Collective Soul](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Soul)'s "[Shine](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shine_(Collective_Soul_song))" winning a [Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Country_Vocal_Performance). The third, *[Halos & Horns](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halos_%26_Horns)* (2002) included a bluegrass version of the [Led Zeppelin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin) song "[Stairway to Heaven](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairway_to_Heaven)". In 2005, she released *[Those Were The Days](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Those_Were_the_Days_(Dolly_Parton_album))* consisting of her interpretations of hits from the [folk-rock](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk-rock) era of the late 1960s and early 1970s, including "[Imagine](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagine_(John_Lennon_song))", "[Where Do the Children Play?](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Do_the_Children_Play%3F)", "[Crimson and Clover](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson_and_Clover)" and "[Where Have All the Flowers Gone?](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Have_All_the_Flowers_Gone%3F)"

### 2005–2020: Touring and holiday album

[![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Dolly_Parton_Wildhorse_Saloon_Nashville_2-2-09.webm/250px-seek%3D24-Dolly_Parton_Wildhorse_Saloon_Nashville_2-2-09.webm.jpg)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/77/Dolly_Parton_Wildhorse_Saloon_Nashville_2-2-09.webm/Dolly_Parton_Wildhorse_Saloon_Nashville_2-2-09.webm.480p.vp9.webm)

Dolly Parton introducing [Coat of Many Colors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_Many_Colors_(song)) in 2009

Parton at the [Grand Ole Opry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry) in 2005

Parton earned her second Academy Award nomination for Best Original Song for "[Travelin' Thru](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamerica_(soundtrack))", which she wrote specifically for the feature film *[Transamerica](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamerica_(film))*. (2005) Due to the song's (and film's) acceptance of a [transgender](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender) woman, Parton received death threats. She returned to number one on the country chart later in 2005 by lending her distinctive harmonies to the [Brad Paisley](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Paisley) ballad, "[When I Get Where I'm Going](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_I_Get_Where_I%27m_Going)". In September 2007, Parton released her first single from her own record company, Dolly Records, titled, "[Better Get to Livin'](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Get_to_Livin%27)", which eventually peaked at number 48 on *Billboard*'s [Hot Country Songs](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) chart. It was followed by the studio album *[Backwoods Barbie](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backwoods_Barbie)*, which was released on February 26, 2008, and reached number two on the country chart. The album's debut at number 17 on the all-genre *Billboard* 200 albums chart was the highest in her career. *Backwoods Barbie* produced four additional singles, including the title track, written as part of her score for *[9 to 5: The Musical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(musical))*, an adaptation of her feature film. After the death of [Michael Jackson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jackson), whom Parton knew personally, she released a video in which she sombrely told of her feelings on Jackson and his death.

Parton receiving the [Liseberg Applause Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liseberg_Applause_Award) 2010

On October 27, 2009, Parton released a four-CD box set, *Dolly*, which featured 99 songs, spanning most of her career. She released her second live DVD and album, *Live From London* in October 2009, filmed during her sold-out 2008 concerts at London's [The O2 Arena](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O2_Arena). On August 10, 2010, with longtime friend [Billy Ray Cyrus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Ray_Cyrus), Parton released the album *[Brother Clyde](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brother_Clyde_(album))*. Parton is featured on "The Right Time", which she co-wrote with Cyrus and Morris Joseph Tancredi. On January 6, 2011, Parton announced that her new album would be titled *[Better Day](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Day_(album))*. In February 2011, she announced that she would embark on the [Better Day World Tour](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Better_Day_World_Tour) on July 17, 2011, with shows in northern Europe and the U.S. The album's lead-off single, "Together You and I", was released on May 23, 2011, and *Better Day* was released on June 28, 2011. In 2011, Parton voiced the character Dolly Gnome in the animated film *[Gnomeo & Juliet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomeo_%26_Juliet)*. On February 11, 2012, after the sudden death of [Whitney Houston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston), Parton stated, "Mine is only one of the millions of hearts broken over the death of Whitney Houston. I will always be grateful and in awe of the wonderful performance she did on my song and I can truly say from the bottom of my heart, 'Whitney, I will always love you. You will be missed.'"

In 2013, Parton joined [Lulu Roman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulu_Roman) for a re-recording of "I Will Always Love You" for Roman's album, *At Last*. In 2013, Parton and Kenny Rogers reunited for the title song of his album *[You Can't Make Old Friends](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can%27t_Make_Old_Friends_(album))*. For their performance, they were nominated at the [2014 Grammy Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56th_Annual_Grammy_Awards) for [Grammy Award for Best Country Duo/Group Performance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Duo/Group_Performance). In 2014, Parton embarked on the [Blue Smoke World Tour](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Smoke_World_Tour) in support of her 42nd studio album, *[Blue Smoke](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Smoke_(album))*. The album was first released in Australia and New Zealand on January 31 to coincide with tour dates there in February and reached the Top 10 in both countries. It was released in the United States on May 13 and debuted at number six on the *Billboard* 200 chart, making it her first Top 10 album and her highest-charting solo album ever; it also reached the number two on the U.S. country chart. The album was released in Europe on June 9 and reached number two on the UK album chart. On June 29, 2014, Parton performed for the first time at the UK [Glastonbury Festival](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival), singing songs such as "Jolene", "9 to 5" and "Coat of Many Colors" to a crowd of more than 180,000. On March 6, 2016, Parton announced that she would be embarking on a tour in support of her new album, *[Pure & Simple](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_%26_Simple_(Dolly_Parton_album))*. The tour was one of Parton's biggest tours within the United States in more than 25 years. 64 dates were planned in the United States and Canada, visiting the most requested markets missed on previous tours.

Parton performing at the [Lanxess Arena](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanxess_Arena) in [Cologne, Germany](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne,_Germany), 2014

In the fall of 2016 she released "Jolene" as a single with the *a cappella* group [Pentatonix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatonix) and performed on *[The Voice](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voice_(U.S._season_11)#Week_4:_Top_10_(November_28_and_29))* with Pentatonix and Miley Cyrus in November 2016. Also in 2016, Parton was one of 30 artists to perform on "[Forever Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_Country)", a mash-up of the songs, "[Take Me Home, Country Roads](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_Me_Home,_Country_Roads)", "[On the Road Again](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Road_Again_(Willie_Nelson_song))" and her own "I Will Always Love You". The song celebrates fifty years of the [CMA Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMA_Awards). At the ceremony itself, Parton was honored with the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award, which was presented by [Lily Tomlin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Tomlin) and preceded by a tribute featuring [Jennifer Nettles](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Nettles), [Pentatonix](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatonix), [Reba McEntire](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire), [Kacey Musgraves](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kacey_Musgraves), [Carrie Underwood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood) and [Martina McBride](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_McBride). In 2017, Parton appeared on *[Rainbow](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_(Kesha_album))*, the third studio album by [Kesha](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesha) performing a duet of "[Old Flames Can't Hold a Candle to You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Flames_Can%27t_Hold_a_Candle_to_You)". The track had been co-written by Kesha's mother [Pebe Sebert](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pebe_Sebert). It was previously a hit for Parton and was included on her 1980 album *[Dolly, Dolly, Dolly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly,_Dolly,_Dolly)*. She also co-wrote and provided featuring vocals on the song "Rainbowland" on *[Younger Now](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younger_Now)*, the sixth album by her goddaughter [Miley Cyrus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus).

In July 2019, Parton made an unannounced appearance at the [Newport Folk Festival](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Folk_Festival) in [Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island) and performed several songs accompanied by [the Highwomen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Highwomen) and [Linda Perry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Perry). In 2019, Parton collaborated with Christian alternative rock duet [For King and Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_King_%26_Country_(band)) and released a version of their hit "[God Only Knows](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Only_Knows_(For_King_%26_Country_song))". She followed this by recording a duet with Christian music artist [Zach Williams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Williams_(musician)) in the song "[There Was Jesus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Was_Jesus)".

In 2020, Parton received worldwide attention after posting four pictures, in which she showed how she would present herself on social media platforms [LinkedIn](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn), [Facebook](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook), [Instagram](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram) and [Twitter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter). The original post on Instagram went viral after celebrities posted their own versions of the so-called Dolly Parton challenge on social media. On April 10, 2020, Parton re-released 93 songs from six of her classic albums: *Little Sparrow*, *Halos & Horns*, *For God and Country*, *Better Day*, *Those Were The Days* and *Live and Well*. On May 27, 2020, Parton released a brand new song called "[When Life Is Good Again](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Life_Is_Good_Again)". This song was released to help keep the spirits up of those affected by the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic. She also released a music video for "When Life Is Good Again", which premiered on [*Time* 100](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100) talks on May 28, 2020.

In October 2020, Parton was featured on the single "Pink" alongside [Monica](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_(singer)), [Jordin Sparks](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordin_Sparks), [Sara Evans](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Evans) and [Rita Wilson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Wilson). The single was released in aid of [Breast Cancer Research](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_Cancer_Research). Parton released *[A Holly Dolly Christmas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Holly_Dolly_Christmas)* in October 2020. On December 6, [CBS](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) aired a Christmas special, "A Holly Dolly Christmas", where Parton performed songs from her album.

### 2022–present: *Rockstar* and Las Vegas residency

[![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Dionne_Warwick_%26_Dolly_Parton_-_Peace_Like_A_River_%28Official_Music_Video%29.webm/250px--Dionne_Warwick_%26_Dolly_Parton_-_Peace_Like_A_River_%28Official_Music_Video%29.webm.jpg)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ef/Dionne_Warwick_%26_Dolly_Parton_-_Peace_Like_A_River_%28Official_Music_Video%29.webm/Dionne_Warwick_%26_Dolly_Parton_-_Peace_Like_A_River_%28Official_Music_Video%29.webm.480p.vp9.webm)

Parton performing "Peace Like a River" with [Dionne Warwick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionne_Warwick).

In 2021, a duet version of the song "[Does He Love You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Does_He_Love_You)" with singer [Reba McEntire](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire) was released.

In early 2022, Parton was nominated for induction into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame). Parton initially declined the nomination believing that the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame was "for the people in [rock music](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music)", but after learning that this was not the case, Parton said she would accept her induction if she were chosen for the honor. In May, her induction was announced and finally on November 5, 2022, she was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. In October 2022, Parton stated in an interview that she would no longer tour, but would continue to play live shows occasionally. On December 31, 2022, Parton co-hosted NBC's New Year's special *[Miley's New Year's Eve Party](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley%27s_New_Year%27s_Eve_Party).*

On January 17, 2023, Parton announced she would release her first rock album, titled *[Rockstar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockstar_(Dolly_Parton_album))*, later that year, during an interview on *The View*. The lead single "World on Fire" was released on May 11, 2023, and it went on to peak at number one a week later. The album was released on November 17, 2023, and features collaborations with [Paul McCartney](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney), [Ringo Starr](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringo_Starr), [Sting](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_(musician)), [Elton John](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John), [Sheryl Crow](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheryl_Crow), [Miley Cyrus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus) and [Lizzo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzo), amongst others. The album received generally positive reviews from critics and debuted at number three on the [*Billboard* 200](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200), becoming Parton's highest-charting solo studio album as well as topping the [Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Country_Albums) and [Rock Albums](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Rock_Albums) charts.

The soundtrack single "Gonna Be You" from the movie *[80 for Brady](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80_for_Brady)* was released January 20, 2023. The song was written by [Diane Warren](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Warren) and performed by Dolly Parton, [Belinda Carlisle](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda_Carlisle), [Cyndi Lauper](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyndi_Lauper), [Debbie Harry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debbie_Harry) and [Gloria Estefan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Estefan). The official music video shows Parton, Carlisle, Lauper and Estefan performing while wearing football jerseys similar to the ones worn by the women in the film, interspersed with clips from the film.

On February 14, 2025, Parton featured on [Sabrina Carpenter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrina_Carpenter)'s song "[Please Please Please](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Please_Please_Please_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song))" on the deluxe edition of her album *[Short n' Sweet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_n%27_Sweet)* and its accompanying music video.

On March 7, 2025, Parton released the single "[If You Hadn't Been There](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_You_Hadn%27t_Been_There)", as a tribute to her husband, who had died a week before. In the United Kingdom, the single peaked at number twenty-one on the sales and downloads chart components.

On June 23, 2025, it was announced that Parton would be embarking on a six-date [Las Vegas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas) residency, *Dolly: Live in Las Vegas*, at [Caesars Palace](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Palace). The run was set for December 2025, but Parton postponed her residency until September 2026 due to health procedures she would be undergoing. In May 2026, she canceled the residency altogether due to the treatment.

On January 16, 2026, in anticipation of her 80th birthday, Dolly Parton released a new live version of the song "[Light of a Clear Blue Morning](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_of_a_Clear_Blue_Morning)" featuring several celebrity guests: [Queen Latifah](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Latifah), [Lainey Wilson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lainey_Wilson), [Miley Cyrus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus), [Reba McEntire](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire), [David Foster](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Foster).

On March 13, 2026, Parton made her first major appearance since the health scare to give the keynote address at [Dollywood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood) during the 41st anniversary celebration.

## Public image

> The way I look and the way I looked then was a country girl's idea of glam, just like I wrote in my "Backwoods Barbie" song. People wanted me to change, they thought I looked cheap. But I patterned my look after the town tramp. Everybody said, "She's trash." And in my little girl mind, I thought, "Well, that's what I'm going to be when I grow up." It was really like a look I was after. I wasn't a natural beauty. So, I just like to look the way I look. I'm so outgoing inside in my personality, that I need the way I look to match all of that.

— Dolly Parton, 2022

Parton had turned down several offers to pose nude for *[Playboy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy)* magazine, but did appear on the cover of the October 1978 issue wearing a [Playboy bunny](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy_bunny) outfit, complete with ears. The issue featured Lawrence Grobel's extensive and candid interview with Parton, representing one of her earliest high-profile interviews with the mainstream press. The association of breasts with Parton's public image is illustrated in the naming of [Dolly the sheep](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_(sheep)) after her, since the sheep was cloned from a cell taken from an adult ewe's [mammary gland](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammary_gland). In [Mobile, Alabama](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama), the [General W.K. Wilson Jr. Bridge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_W.K._Wilson_Jr._Bridge) is commonly called "the Dolly Parton Bridge" due to its arches resembling her bust. The thickened appearance of the turret frontal armor of the [T-72](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72)A [main battle tank](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_battle_tank) led to the unofficial Army nickname "Dolly Parton" and later the [T-72](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72)BIs got the "Super Dolly Parton" nickname.

Parton is known for having undergone considerable plastic surgery. On a 2003 episode of *[The Oprah Winfrey Show](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oprah_Winfrey_Show)*, Winfrey asked what kind of [cosmetic surgery](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmetic_surgery) Parton had undergone. Parton replied that cosmetic surgery was imperative in keeping with her famous image.
Parton has repeatedly joked about her physical image and surgeries, saying, "It takes a lot of money to look this cheap." Her breasts have garnered her mentions in several songs, including "Dolly Parton's Hits" by [Bobby Braddock](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Braddock), "[Marty Feldman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Feldman) Eyes" by [Bruce Baum](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Baum) (a parody of "[Bette Davis Eyes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Davis_Eyes)"), "No Show Jones" by [George Jones](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Jones) and [Merle Haggard](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merle_Haggard) and "[Make Me Proud](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_Me_Proud)" by [Drake](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_(rapper)), featuring [Nicki Minaj](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicki_Minaj). When asked about future plastic surgeries, she famously said, "If I see something sagging, bagging or dragging, I'll get it nipped, tucked or sucked." Parton's feminine escapism is acknowledged in her words, "Womanhood was a difficult thing to get a grip on in those hills, unless you were a man." Parton said in 2012 that she had entered a Dolly Parton drag queen lookalike contest and lost.

## Artistry

### Influences

Parton, though influenced by big-name stars, often credits much of her inspiration to her family and community. In her 2020 book, *Songteller: My Life in Lyrics*, Parton wrote of her mother, "So it was just natural for my mom to always be singing. My mother had that old-timey voice and she used to sing all these songs that were brought over from the Old World. They were English, Irish, Welsh, folk songs where people tell stories." Parton calls her mother's voice "haunting". "Lord you would feel it", she wrote. Her biggest influence however was her Aunt Dorothy Jo: "People often ask me who my influences were, they think I'm going to say some big names and there were a few 'stars' I was impressed with. But my hero was my aunt Dorothy Jo, Mama's baby sister. She was not only an evangelist, she played banjo, she played guitar and she wrote some great songs." Fellow singers also had an impact on Parton, describing [George Jones](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Jones) as her "all time favorite singer", as well as her love for other artists such as [Kitty Wells](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Wells), [Roy Acuff](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Acuff) and [Rose Maddox](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Maddox).

### Musicianship

Though unable to read sheet music, Parton can play many instruments, including: the [dulcimer](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulcimer), [autoharp](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoharp), [banjo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo), [guitar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar), [electric guitar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_guitar), [fiddle](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddle), [piano](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano), [recorder](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorder_(musical_instrument)) and the [saxophone](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone). Reflecting on her multi-instrumental abilities, Parton said, "I play some of everything. I ain't that good at none of it, but I try to sell it. I really try to lay into it." Parton has also used her fingernails as an instrument, most evident on her 1980 song "[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(Dolly_Parton_song))", which she derived the beat from clacking her nails together while backstage on the set of the film *[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film))*.

## Other ventures

In 1998, *Nashville Business* ranked her the wealthiest country music star. As of 2017, her net worth is estimated at $500 million.

### Songwriting

Parton is a prolific songwriter, writing country music songs with strong elements of [folk music](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) and her upbringing in humble mountain surroundings, as well as reflecting her family's Christian background. Her songs "Coat of Many Colors", "I Will Always Love You" and "Jolene", among others, have become classics. On November 4, 2003, Parton was honored as a [BMI Icon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Music_Incorporated) at the 2003 BMI Country Awards.
Parton has earned over 35 BMI Pop and Country Awards. In 2001, she was inducted into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame). In a 2009 interview on [CNN](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN)'s *[Larry King Live](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_King_Live)*, she said she had written "at least 3,000" songs, having written seriously since the age of seven. Parton also said she writes something every day, be it a song or an idea.

Parton's songwriting has been featured prominently in several films. In addition to the title song for *9 to 5*, she also recorded a second version of "I Will Always Love You" for *The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas* (1982). The second version was a number one country hit and also reached number 53 on the pop charts.
"I Will Always Love You" has been covered by many country artists, including Linda Ronstadt on *[Prisoner In Disguise](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_In_Disguise)* (1975), Kenny Rogers on *[Vote for Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_for_Love)* (1996) and [LeAnn Rimes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeAnn_Rimes) on *[Unchained Melody: The Early Years](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unchained_Melody:_The_Early_Years)* (1997). Whitney Houston also performed it on *The Bodyguard* soundtrack and her version became a best-selling hit both written and performed by a female vocalist, with worldwide sales of over twelve million copies. In addition, the song has been translated into Italian and performed by the Welsh opera singer [Katherine Jenkins](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Jenkins).

As a songwriter, Parton has twice been nominated for an [Academy Award for Best Original Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) for "9 to 5" and "[Travelin' Thru](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamerica_(soundtrack))" (2005) from the film *[Transamerica](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamerica_(film))*. "Travelin' Thru" won Best Original Song at the 2005 Phoenix Film Critics Society Awards. It was also nominated for both the 2005 [Golden Globe Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) for [Best Original Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) and the 2005 [Broadcast Film Critics Association Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association_Award) (also known as the Critics' Choice Awards) for [Best Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Film_Critics_Association_Award_for_Best_Song). A cover of "Love Is Like A Butterfly" by [Clare Torry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Torry) was used as the theme music for the British TV show *[Butterflies](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterflies_(TV_series))*.

### Stage musicals

#### *9 to 5: The Musical*

Main article: [*9 to 5* (musical)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(musical))

Parton wrote the score (and Patricia Resnick the book) for *[9 to 5: The Musical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5:_The_Musical)*, a [musical-theater](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theater) [adaptation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatrical_adaptation) of Parton's feature film *9 to 5* (1980). The musical ran at the [Ahmanson Theatre](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmanson_Theatre), Los Angeles, in late 2008. It opened on Broadway at the [Marquis Theatre](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_Theatre) in New York on April 30, 2009, to mixed reviews.
The title track of her 2008 album *Backwoods Barbie* was written for the musical's character Doralee. Although her score (as well as the musical debut of actress [Allison Janney](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allison_Janney)) was praised, the show struggled, closing on September 6, 2009, after 24 previews and 148 performances. Parton received nominations for [Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Music](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_Desk_Award_for_Outstanding_Music) and [Drama Desk Award for Outstanding Lyrics](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_Desk_Award_for_Outstanding_Lyrics) as well as a nomination for [Tony Award for Best Original Score](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Original_Score). Developing the musical was not a quick process. According to the [public-radio](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_broadcasting) program *[Studio 360](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_360)* (October 29, 2005), in October 2005 Parton was in the midst of composing songs for a Broadway musical theater adaptation of the film. In late June 2007, *9 to 5: The Musical* was read for industry presentations. The readings starred [Megan Hilty](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Hilty), Allison Janney, [Stephanie J. Block](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_J._Block), [Bebe Neuwirth](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebe_Neuwirth) and [Marc Kudisch](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Kudisch). [Ambassador Theatre Group](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Theatre_Group) announced a 2012 UK tour for *Dolly Parton's 9 to 5: The Musical*, commencing at [Manchester Opera House](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Opera_House), on October 12, 2012.

#### *Dolly: A True Original Musical*

Main article: [Dolly: A True Original Musical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly:_A_True_Original_Musical)

In June 2024, Parton announced an [autobiographical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobiographical) [musical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theatre) about her life and career initially titled *Hello, I'm Dolly* (named after her debut album and also a play on *[Hello, Dolly!](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_Dolly!_(musical))*), with a goal of opening on [Broadway](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre) in 2026 with direction by [Bartlett Sher](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartlett_Sher). The musical, with a co-written [book](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_(musical_theater)) by Parton and Maria S. Schlatter, features original songs as well as her more well-known hits. Parton also revealed that she has been working on the musical for the last decade. The title was promoted as *Dolly: An Original Musical* on December 6, coinciding with a nationwide casting call for actresses to play Parton through different stages of her life, using the hashtag #SearchForDolly on social media video posts. The show opened as *[Dolly: A True Original Musical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly:_A_True_Original_Musical)* at the [Belmont University](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_University) Fisher Center for the Performing Arts in Nashville in July 2025.

### The Dollywood Company

Entrance to Dollywood in Pigeon Forge

Parton invested much of her earnings into business ventures in her native East Tennessee, notably [Pigeon Forge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_Forge,_Tennessee). She is a co-owner of [The Dollywood Company](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschend_Family_Entertainment_Corporation#The_Dollywood_Company), which operates the theme park [Dollywood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood) (a former [Silver Dollar City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Dollar_City)), a [dinner theater](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner_theater), [Dolly Parton's Stampede](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton%27s_Stampede), the [waterpark](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterpark) [Dollywood's Splash Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood%27s_Splash_Country) and the Dream More Resort and Spa, all in Pigeon Forge. With 3.1 million visitors in 2024, Dollywood is among the 20 most popular theme park in the United States, and the fifth most popular theme park that operates seasonally. The Dolly Parton's Stampede business has venues in [Branson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branson,_Missouri), Missouri and [Myrtle Beach](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Beach,_South_Carolina), South Carolina. In 2011, the Myrtle Beach Stampede location was redesigned under the name Pirates Voyage, and the show later expanded to Tennessee and Florida locations. A former location in [Orlando](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida), Florida, closed in January 2008 after the land and building were sold to a developer.

Parton has been honored with proclamations of "Dolly Parton Day" multiple times. In 1970, her hometown [Sevierville, Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville,_Tennessee), declared one day to be Dolly Parton Day. In 2011, the [South Carolina General Assembly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_General_Assembly) declared June 3, 2011, as Dolly Parton Day, in connection with the grand opening of Pirates Voyage in Myrtle Beach. In 2026, the State of Tennessee declared her 80th birthday to be Dolly Parton Day in the state.

On January 19, 2012, Parton's 66th birthday, Gaylord Opryland and Dollywood announced plans to open a $50 million water and snow park, a family-friendly destination in Nashville that is open all year. On September 29, 2012, Parton officially withdrew her support for the Nashville park due to the restructuring of Gaylord Entertainment Company after its merger with Marriott International.
On June 12, 2015, it was announced that the Dollywood Company had purchased the Lumberjack Feud Dinner Show in Pigeon Forge. The show, which opened in June 2011, was owned and operated by Rob Scheer until the close of the 2015 season. The new, renovated show by the Dollywood Company opened in 2016.

### Dolly Parton's SongTeller Hotel and Dolly's Life of Many Colors Museum

Parton announced in October 2025 that she would be opening her new SongTeller Hotel in downtown Nashville in partnership with the firm Herschend. With the hotel, Parton will also be opening her new Dolly's Life of Many Colors Museum, which claims to be the "largest exhibit celebrating her life anywhere to date." Advance tickets for the museum's opening in June 2026 went on sale on October 29, 2025.

Parton was a co-owner of Sandollar Productions, with [Sandy Gallin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Gallin), her former manager. A film and television [production company](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_company), it produced the documentary *[Common Threads: Stories from the Quilt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Threads:_Stories_from_the_Quilt)* (1989), which won an [Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature); the television series *[Babes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babes_(TV_series))* (1990–91), *[Buffy the Vampire Slayer](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffy_the_Vampire_Slayer_(TV_series))* (1997–2003), and *[Angel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_(1999_TV_series))* (1999-2004); and the feature films *[Father of the Bride](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_of_the_Bride_(1991_film))* (1991), *[Father of the Bride: Part II](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_of_the_Bride_II)* (1995) *[Straight Talk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Talk)* (1992) (in which Parton starred) and *[Sabrina](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrina_(1995_film))* (1995), among other shows. In a 2009 interview, singer [Connie Francis](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connie_Francis) revealed that Parton had been contacting her for years in an attempt to film the singer's life story. Francis turned down Parton's offers, as she was already in negotiations with singer [Gloria Estefan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Estefan) to produce the film, a collaboration now ended. After the retirement of her partner, Sandy Gallin, Parton briefly operated Dolly Parton's Southern Light Productions and in 2015 she announced her new production company would be called Dixie Pixie Productions and produce the movies-of-week in development with NBC Television and [Magnolia Hill Productions](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia_Hill_Productions).

### Acting

#### Breakthrough

In addition to her performing appearances on *[The Porter Wagoner Show](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Porter_Wagoner_Show)* in the 1960s and into the 1970s, her two self-titled television variety shows in the [1970s](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly!) and [1980s](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_(1987_TV_series)) and on *American Idol* in 2008 and other guest appearances, Parton has had television roles. In 1979, she received an Emmy award nomination as "Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Variety Program" for her guest appearance in a [Cher](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher) special. During the mid-1970s, Parton wanted to expand her audience base. Although her first attempt, the television variety show *Dolly!* (1976–77), had high ratings, it lasted only one season, with Parton requesting to be released from her contract because of the stress it was causing on her [vocal cords](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_cords). (She later tried a second television variety show, also titled *Dolly* (1987–88); it too lasted only one season).

In her first feature film, Parton portrayed a secretary in a leading role with [Jane Fonda](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Fonda) and [Lily Tomlin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Tomlin) in the comedy film *[9 to 5](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9_to_5_(film))* (1980). The movie highlights discrimination against women in the workplace and created awareness of the National Association of Working Women (9–5). She received nominations for a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Musical or Comedy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Musical_or_Comedy) and a [Golden Globe Award for New Star of the Year – Actress](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_New_Star_of_the_Year_%E2%80%93_Actress). Parton wrote and recorded the film's title song. It received nominations for an [Academy Award for Best Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Song) and a [Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Song). Released as a single, the song won both the [Grammy Award for Best Female Country Vocal Performance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Female_Country_Vocal_Performance) and the [Grammy Award for Best Country Song](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Song). It also reached no. 1 on the Hot 100 chart and it was no. 78 on the "[AFI's 100 Years...100 Songs](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI%27s_100_Years...100_Songs)" list released by the [American Film Institute](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute) in 2004. *9 to 5* became a major box office success, grossing over $3.9 million its opening weekend and over $103 million worldwide. Parton was named Top Female Box Office Star by the *[Motion Picture Herald](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Herald)* in both 1981 and 1982 due to the film's success.

In late 1981, Parton began filming her second film, the [musical film](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_film) *[The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_Little_Whorehouse_in_Texas_(film))* (1982). The film earned her a second nomination for a Golden Globe Award for Best Actress – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy. The film was greeted with positive critical reviews and became a commercial success, earning over $69 million worldwide. After a two-year hiatus from films, Parton was teamed with [Sylvester Stallone](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) for *[Rhinestone](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone_(film))* (1984), a comedy film about a country music star's efforts to mold an unknown into a music sensation. The film was a critical and financial failure, making just over $21 million on a $28 million budget.

#### Continued roles

In 1989, Parton returned to film acting in *[Steel Magnolias](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Magnolias)* (1989), based on the play *[Steel Magnolias](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Magnolias_(play))* by [Robert Harling](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Harling_(writer)). The film was popular with critics and audiences, grossing over $95 million in the U.S. Parton starred in the [television movies](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_movie) *[A Smoky Mountain Christmas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Smoky_Mountain_Christmas)* (1986), *[Wild Texas Wind](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Texas_Wind)* (1991), *[Unlikely Angel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unlikely_Angel)* (1996), portraying an angel sent back to earth after a deadly car crash and *[Blue Valley Songbird](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Valley_Songbird)* (1999), where her character lives through her music. She starred with [James Woods](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Woods) in *[Straight Talk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_Talk)* (1992), which received mixed reviews and grossed a mild $21 million at the box office.

Parton's 1987 variety show *[Dolly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_(1987_TV_series))* lasted only one season. She made a [cameo appearance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_appearance) as herself in *[The Beverly Hillbillies](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hillbillies_(film))* (1993), an adaptation of the long-running TV sitcom *[The Beverly Hillbillies](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hillbillies)* (1962–1971). Parton has done [voice work](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_acting) for animation for television series, playing herself in *[Alvin and the Chipmunks](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_and_the_Chipmunks_(1983_TV_series))* (episode "Urban Chipmunk", 1983) and the character Katrina Eloise "Murph" Murphy (Ms. Frizzle's first cousin) in *[The Magic School Bus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_School_Bus)* (episode "The Family Holiday Special", 1994). She also has guest-starred in several sitcoms, including a 1990 episode of *[Designing Women](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designing_Women)* (episode "The First Day of the Last Decade of the Entire Twentieth Century") as herself, the guardian movie star of Charlene's baby. She made a guest appearance on *[Reba](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_(TV_series))* (episode "Reba's Rules of Real Estate") portraying a [real-estate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) agency owner and on *[The Simpsons](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons)* (episode "[Sunday, Cruddy Sunday](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday,_Cruddy_Sunday)", 1999). She appeared as herself in 2000 on the Halloween episode of [Bette Midler](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Midler)'s short-lived sitcom *[Bette](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_(TV_series))* and on episode 14 of *[Babes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babes_(TV_Series))* (produced by Sandollar Productions, Parton and [Sandy Gallin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Gallin)'s joint production company). She made cameo appearances on the [Disney Channel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Channel) as "Aunt Dolly", visiting Hannah and her family in fellow Tennessean and real-life [goddaughter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddaughter) [Miley Cyrus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus)'s series *[Hannah Montana](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Montana)* (episodes "Good Golly, Miss Dolly", 2006, "I Will Always Loathe You", 2007 and "Kiss It All Goodbye", 2010). She was nominated for Outstanding Guest Actress in a Comedy Series.

Parton appeared as an overprotective mother in the comedy *[Frank McKlusky, C.I.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_McKlusky,_C.I.)*. (2002) She made a cameo appearance in the comedy film *[Miss Congeniality 2: Armed and Fabulous](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Congeniality_2:_Armed_and_Fabulous)*, starring [Sandra Bullock](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock). ## Personal life

### Family

Parton is the fourth of twelve children born to Avie Lee Caroline (née Owens; 1923–2003) and Robert Lee Parton Sr. (known as Lee; 1921–2000). Her siblings are Willadeene, David Wilburn (1942–2024), Coy Denver, Robert Lee "Bobby," [Stella Mae](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Parton), Cassie Nan, [Randle Huston "Randy"](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Parton) (1953–2021), Larry Gerald (1955), twins Frieda Estelle and Floyd Estel (1957–2018), and [Rachel Ann.](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Parton_George)

### Faith

Parton is a committed [Christian](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians), which has influenced many of her musical releases.

She talked about her liberal approach to faith in the January 2024 issue of *New Humanist* magazine. "I wouldn't even say I'm religious, though I grew up with that background. But I have a lot of faith in myself and I've been so blessed to have been around great people my whole life, my Uncle Bill and my family being supportive and all the people I met along the way."

### Carl Dean

Dolly Parton arrived in [Nashville](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee) on a Saturday morning, the day after graduating from [Sevier County High School](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevier_County_High_School). After starting her laundry inside the Wishy Washy Laundromat, Parton got a cold drink and walked outside. Carl Thomas Dean (July 20, 1942 – March 3, 2025), a Nashville native, pulled up along the sidewalk where Parton was standing. Dean mentioned Parton might get sunburned because of the style of shirt she was wearing. They began a conversation. Two years later, they were married in [Ringgold, Georgia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringgold,_Georgia), on May 30, 1966. Although Parton does not use Dean's surname professionally, she has stated that her passport reads "Dolly Parton Dean" and she sometimes uses Dean when signing contracts.

Dean, who ran an [asphalt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphalt_concrete) road and driveway-paving business in Nashville for decades, always shunned publicity and rarely accompanied his wife to public events. According to Parton, he only saw her perform once. However, she would also state in interviews that even though it appeared they spent little time together, it was because nobody saw him publicly. Dean would often visit Dollywood by himself and tour the park unrecognized.

Parton has commented on Dean's romantic side, saying that he did spontaneous things to surprise her and sometimes even wrote poems for her. In 2011, she said, "We're really very proud of our marriage. It's the first for both of us. And the last." On May 6, 2016, Parton announced that she and Dean would renew their vows in honor of their 50th wedding anniversary later in the month.

Dean and Parton moved into their long-term home on 75 acres in [Brentwood, Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood,_Tennessee), in 1972. However, they retained ownership of their prior home in [Antioch, Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch,_Tennessee), for several years. Since the 2010s, many [clickbait](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clickbait) articles and videos claim Dean and Parton purchased their home in Brentwood in 1999. This is not correct. Parton's uncle, Dot Watson, and Dean constructed the home in 1970 and 1971. Parton's brothers Denver and [Randy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Parton) also worked on the structure. Several years later, they added a [guesthouse](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guesthouse) to the property. Dean and Parton lived at their home together for over 52 years until Dean's death.

While Parton and Dean never had children, they helped raise several of her younger siblings in Nashville, leading her nieces and nephews (and great nieces and nephews as well) to refer to them as "Uncle Peepaw" and "Aunt Granny"; the latter moniker later lent its name to one of Parton's [Dollywood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood) restaurants. Parton is also the godmother of singer-songwriter and actress [Miley Cyrus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus).

On March 3, 2025, Parton announced on her social media that Dean had died in Nashville at the age of 82.

## Philanthropy

Since the mid-1980s, Parton has supported many charitable efforts, particularly in the area of literacy, primarily through her [Dollywood Foundation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollywood_Foundation). Her literacy program, Dolly Parton's Imagination Library, which is a part of the Dollywood Foundation, was founded in honor of her father, who never learned to read or write. It mails one book per month to each enrolled child from the time of their birth until they enter kindergarten. Over 1600 local communities provide the Imagination Library to almost 850,000 children each month across the U.S., Canada, the UK, Australia and the Republic of Ireland. In February 2018, she donated her 100 millionth free book, a copy of Parton's children's picture book *Coat of Many Colors*, to the [Library of Congress](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) in Washington, D.C. and was honored by the Library on account of the "charity sending out its 100 millionth book".

In February 2025, Indiana governor [Mike Braun](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Braun) declined to continue a state 50% match for Parton's Imagination Library. Dollywood Foundation President Jeff Conyers said, "We are hopeful that Governor Braun and the Indiana Legislature will continue this vital investment by restoring the state's funding match for local Imagination Library programs." Braun would soon afterwards task his wife Maureen with finding ways to keep the Imagination Library's Indiana chapter alive.

For her work in literacy, Parton has received awards including the [Association of American Publishers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Publishers) Honors Award (2000), [Good Housekeeping Seal of Approval](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Housekeeping_Seal_of_Approval) (2001) (the first time the seal had been awarded to a person), [American Association of School Administrators](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_School_Administrators) – Galaxy Award (2002), National State Teachers of the Year – Chasing Rainbows Award (2002) and Parents as Teachers National Center – Child and Family Advocacy Award (2003).

On May 8, 2009, Parton gave the [commencement speech](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commencement_speech) at the graduation ceremony for the [University of Tennessee, Knoxville](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee,_Knoxville)'s College of Arts and Sciences. During the ceremony, she received an honorary [Doctor of Humane Letters](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Humane_Letters) from the university. It was only the second honorary degree given by the university and in presenting the degree, the university's [Chancellor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_(education)), [Jimmy Cheek](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Cheek), said, "Because of her career not just as a musician and entertainer, but for her role as a cultural ambassador, philanthropist and lifelong advocate for education, it is fitting that she be honored with an honorary degree from the flagship educational institution of her home state."

In 2006, Parton published a cookbook, *Dolly's Dixie Fixin's: Love, Laughter and Lots of Good Food*.

The Dollywood Foundation, funded from Parton's profits, has brought jobs and tax revenues to a previously depressed region. Parton has also worked to raise money for other causes, including the [American Red Cross](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Red_Cross) and [HIV/AIDS](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS)-related charities.

In December 2006, Parton pledged $500,000 toward a proposed $90 million hospital and cancer center to be constructed in [Sevierville](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevierville) in the name of Robert F. Thomas, the physician who delivered her. She announced a benefit concert to raise additional funds for the project. The concert played to about 8,000 people. That same year, Parton and [Emmylou Harris](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris) allowed use of their music in a [PETA](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_for_the_Ethical_Treatment_of_Animals) ad campaign that encouraged pet owners to keep their dogs indoors rather than chained outside.

With Tennessee Senator [Bob Corker](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Corker) at the rededication ceremony for the [Great Smoky Mountains National Park](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Smoky_Mountains_National_Park) in September 2009

In 2003, her efforts to preserve the [bald eagle](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle) through the [American Eagle Foundation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Eagle_Foundation)'s sanctuary at Dollywood earned her the Partnership Award from the [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Fish_and_Wildlife_Service). Parton received the [Woodrow Wilson Award for Public Service](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_Awards) from the [Woodrow Wilson International Center for Scholars](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_International_Center_for_Scholars) of the [Smithsonian Institution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution) at a ceremony in Nashville on November 8, 2007.

In response to the [2016 Great Smoky Mountains wildfires](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Great_Smoky_Mountains_wildfires), Parton was one of the country music artists who participated in a telethon to raise money for victims of the fires. This was held in Nashville on December 9. In addition, Parton hosted her own telethon for the victims on December 13 and reportedly raised around $9 million. Her fund, the "My People Fund", provided $1,000 a month for six months to over 900 families affected by the wildfires, culminating with $5,000 to each home in the final month due to increased fundraising, totaling $10,000 per family. In 2018, the FBI honored Parton for her wildfire aid, awarding her the 2018 Director's Community Leadership Award at a ceremony at [FBI Headquarters](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_Headquarters). The honor was bestowed by Director Christopher Wray and was accepted on Parton's behalf by David Dotson, the CEO of the Dollywood Foundation.

The impact of the fund's financial relief for the 2016 wildfire victims was studied by University of Tennessee College of Social Work professor Stacia West, who examined the impact of cash transfers on poverty alleviation. West surveyed 100 recipients of the emergency relief funds in April 2017 on topics including housing, financial impact, physical and emotional health and sources of support, with a follow-up survey conducted in December 2017. West found that the "My People Fund", in tandem with traditional disaster response, gave families the ability to make decisions that were most beneficial to them and concluded that unconditional cash support may be more beneficial for disaster relief than conditional financial support. The report cited the impact of the monthly financial disbursements from the "My People Fund" on residents' emergency savings: "Following the monthly disbursements of unconditional cash assistance, participants were able to return to baseline financial stability reported prior to the wildfire and improve their ability to set aside savings for hypothetical future emergencies."

Parton has been a generous donor to [Vanderbilt University Medical Center](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University_Medical_Center) (VUMC). Among her gifts was a contribution to the Monroe Carell Jr. Children's Hospital at Vanderbilt Pediatric Cancer Program in honor of a friend, [Naji Abumrad](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naji_Abumrad) and her niece, Hannah Dennison, who was successfully treated for [leukemia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia) as a child at the Children's Hospital.

In the aftermath of 2024's [Hurricane Helene](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Helene), Parton announced a donation of $2 million to relief efforts, $1 million personally and another $1 million through her various businesses and the Dollywood Foundation.

### LGBTQ+ rights

Though often politically neutral, Parton is known for her long history of openly supporting the [gay community](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay) and publicly came out in support of [same-sex marriage](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage) in 2009. She also voiced her support for the [trans community](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender) after a series of [anti-trans measures](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Tennessee#Gender_identity_and_expression) came into law in Tennessee in 2023.

### Moderna COVID-19 vaccine

In response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), Parton donated $1 million towards research at [Vanderbilt University Medical Center](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University_Medical_Center) and encouraged those who can afford it to make similar donations. She said "I'm a very proud girl today to know I had anything at all to do with something that's going to help us through this crazy pandemic." Her donation funded the critical early stages of development of the [Moderna](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderna) vaccine. In March 2021, Parton was [vaccinated](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine) against [COVID-19](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019) at Vanderbilt University. She labeled social media accounts of the occasion "Dolly gets a dose of her own medicine." Parton strongly encouraged everyone to get vaccinated when eligible and performed a song celebrating her vaccination, set to the tune of her song "[Jolene](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jolene)".

## Awards and honors

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Dolly Parton](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Dolly_Parton)

President [George W. Bush](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) and First Lady [Laura Bush](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bush), with the [John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) honorees in the [Blue Room](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Room_(White_House)) of the [White House](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House) during a 2006 reception. From left: singer-songwriter [William "Smokey" Robinson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_%22Smokey%22_Robinson); composer [Andrew Lloyd Webber](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lloyd_Webber); Dolly Parton; film director [Steven Spielberg](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg); and conductor [Zubin Mehta](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubin_Mehta).

Dolly Parton is one of the most-honored female country performers in history. The Record Industry Association of America has certified 25 of her single or album releases as either Gold Record, Platinum Record or Multi-Platinum Record. She has had 26 songs reach no. 1 on the *Billboard* country charts, a record for a female artist. She has 42 career Top 10 country albums, a record for any artist and 110 career-charted singles over the past 40 years. As of 2012 she had written more than 3,000 songs and sold more than 100 million records, making her one of the best-selling female artists ever. As of 2021, she had appeared on the country music charts in each of seven decades, the most of any artist.

Dolly Parton has earned 11 Grammy Awards (including her 2011 Lifetime Achievement Grammy) and a total of 55 Grammy Award nominations, the third-most nominations of any female artist in the history of the prestigious awards.

At the [American Music Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Music_Award), she has won three awards out of 18 nominations. At the Country Music Association, she has won ten awards out of 42 nominations. At the [Academy of Country Music](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music), she has won seven awards and 39 nominations. She is one of only six female artists (including [Reba McEntire](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reba_McEntire), [Barbara Mandrell](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Mandrell), [Shania Twain](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shania_Twain), [Loretta Lynn](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynn) and [Taylor Swift](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift)), to win the Country Music Association's highest honor, Entertainer of the Year (1978). She also has been nominated for two [Academy Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Awards) and a [Tony Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Award). She was nominated for an [Emmy Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award) for her appearance in a 1978 [Cher](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cher) television special. She was awarded a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) for her music in 1984, located at 6712 Hollywood Boulevard in [Hollywood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_California), California; a star on the Nashville [StarWalk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarWalk) for Grammy winners; and a bronze sculpture on the courthouse lawn in Sevierville. She has called that statue of herself in her hometown "the greatest honor", because it came from the people who knew her. Parton was inducted into the Grand Ole Opry in 1969 and in 1986 was named one of *[Ms. Magazine](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ms._Magazine)*'s Women of the Year. In 1986, she was inducted into the [Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame).

In 1999, Parton received country music's highest honor, an induction into the Country Music Hall of Fame. She received an honorary [doctorate degree](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate_degree) from [Carson-Newman College](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson-Newman_College) ([Jefferson City, Tennessee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City,_Tennessee)) in 1990. This was followed by induction into the [National Academy of Popular Music](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Popular_Music)/[Songwriters Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame) in 2001. In 2002, she ranked no. 4 in [CMT](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Music_Television)'s 40 Greatest Women of Country Music.

Parton's star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame)

Parton was honored in 2003 with a tribute album called *Just Because I'm a Woman: Songs of Dolly Parton*. The artists who recorded versions of Parton's songs included [Melissa Etheridge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Etheridge) ("I Will Always Love You"), [Alison Krauss](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Krauss) ("9 to 5"), [Shania Twain](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shania_Twain) ("Coat of Many Colors"), [Meshell Ndegeocello](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshell_Ndegeocello) ("Two Doors Down"), [Norah Jones](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Jones) ("The Grass is Blue") and [Sinéad O'Connor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sin%C3%A9ad_O%27Connor) ("Dagger Through the Heart"). Parton herself contributed a re-recording of the title song, originally the title song for her first RCA album in 1968. Parton was awarded the [Living Legend Medal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress_Living_Legend) by the U.S. [Library of Congress](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) on April 14, 2004, for her contributions to the cultural heritage of the United States. She is also the focus of a Library of Congress collection exploring the influences of country music on her life and career. The collection contains images, articles, sheet music and more.

In 2005, she was honored with the [National Medal of Arts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts), the highest honor given by the U.S. government for excellence in the arts. The award is presented by the [U.S. President](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States). On December 3, 2006, Parton received the [Kennedy Center Honors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honors) from the [John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) for her lifetime of contributions to the arts. During the show, some of country music's biggest names came to show their admiration. [Carrie Underwood](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Underwood) performed "Islands in the Stream" with Rogers, Parton's original duet partner. Krauss performed "Jolene" and duetted "Coat of Many Colors" with Twain. McEntire and [Reese Witherspoon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reese_Witherspoon) also came to pay tribute. On November 16, 2010, Parton accepted the Liseberg Applause Award, the theme park industry's most prestigious honor, on behalf of Dollywood theme park during a ceremony held at IAAPA Attractions Expo 2010 in [Orlando, Florida](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida).

In 2015, a newly discovered species of [lichen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen) found growing in the southern Appalachians was named *[Japewiella dollypartoniana](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japewiella_dollypartoniana)* in honor of Parton's music and her efforts to bring national and global attention to that region. In 2018, Parton received a second star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame), inducted alongside [Linda Ronstadt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt) and [Emmylou Harris](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmylou_Harris) in recognition of their work as a trio. Parton was also recognized in the [Guinness World Records](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) 2018 Edition for holding records for the Most Decades with a Top 20 hit on Billboard's Hot Country Songs Chart and Most Hits on Billboard's Hot Country Songs Chart by a Female Artist. In 2020, Parton received a Grammy award for her collaboration with [For King & Country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_King_%26_Country_(band)) on their song, "God Only Knows". In 2021, she was included on the [*Time* 100](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100), *[Time](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_(magazine))*'s annual list of the 100 most influential people in the world. *[The New York Times](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* called her among the three of America's Most Beloved Divas (alongside [Patti LaBelle](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_LaBelle) and [Barbra Streisand](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand)).

During the Trump presidency, Parton turned down the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) twice due to her husband's illness and the ongoing pandemic. Parton turned down the Presidential Medal of Freedom a third time during the Biden presidency to avoid the appearance of politics. In response to a 2021 proposal by the Tennessee legislature to erect a statue of Parton, she released a statement asking the legislature to remove the bill from consideration, saying "Given all that is going on in the world, I don't think putting me on a pedestal is appropriate at this time."

In late 2022, Parton received a $100-million [Courage and Civility Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courage_and_Civility_Award) from the founder of Amazon, [Jeff Bezos](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos). According to Bezos, the award was given to Parton because of her charity work focused on improving children's literacy around the world.

In 2023, Parton was awarded [American Library Association Honorary Membership](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Library_Association_Honorary_Membership).

Parton is a [Kentucky Colonel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Colonel), awarded to her by the [governor of Kentucky](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Kentucky).

Asteroid (10731) Dollyparton, the [former](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_designation_in_astronomy) 1998 BL3, was named in her honor in 2022.

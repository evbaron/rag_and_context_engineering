The **1984–85 NHL season** was the [68th season](/wiki/List_of_NHL_seasons) of the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League). The [Edmonton Oilers](/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) won their second straight [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup) by beating the [Philadelphia Flyers](/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) four games to one in the final series.

## League business

### Entry draft

The [1984 NHL entry draft](/wiki/1984_NHL_entry_draft) was held on June 9, at the [Montreal Forum](/wiki/Montreal_Forum) in [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec). [Mario Lemieux](/wiki/Mario_Lemieux) was selected first overall by the [Pittsburgh Penguins](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins).

### Ice officials begin wearing helmets

Referee [Andy Van Hellemond](/wiki/Andy_Van_Hellemond) becomes the first on ice official in league history to wear a helmet. Soon, several officials would follow his lead and wear helmets before it became mandatory for all officials for the [2006–07 season](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_NHL_season).

## Regular season

The Philadelphia Flyers had the best record in the NHL, four points ahead of second place Edmonton Oilers. Flyers goaltender [Pelle Lindbergh](/wiki/Pelle_Lindbergh) went on to become the first European to win the [Vezina Trophy](/wiki/Vezina_Trophy). Oilers' star [Wayne Gretzky](/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky) once again won the [Art Ross Trophy](/wiki/Art_Ross_Trophy) by reaching the 200 point plateau for the third time in four years. He also set a new record for assists in a season with 135 and won his sixth straight [Hart Memorial Trophy](/wiki/Hart_Memorial_Trophy). [Mario Lemieux](/wiki/Mario_Lemieux) made his NHL debut by scoring 100 points and winning the [Calder Trophy](/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy) for rookie of the year. On October 26, 1984, [Paul Coffey](/wiki/Paul_Coffey) of the Edmonton Oilers would be the last defenceman in the 20th century to score four goals in one game. It occurred in a game versus the Detroit Red Wings.

The last two players active in the 1960s, [Butch Goring](/wiki/Butch_Goring) and [Brad Park](/wiki/Brad_Park), retired after the playoffs. Goring was the last active, playing his last playoff game three days after Park's last game.

## Playoffs

Main article: [1985 Stanley Cup playoffs](/wiki/1985_Stanley_Cup_playoffs)

### Bracket

The top four teams in each division qualified for the playoffs. In the division semifinals, the fourth [seeded](/wiki/Seed_(sports)) team in each division played against the division winner from their division. The other series matched the second and third place teams from the divisions. The two winning teams from each division's semifinals then met in the division finals. The two division winners of each conference then played in the conference finals. The two conference winners then advanced to the Stanley Cup Finals.

In the division semifinals, teams competed in a [best-of-five](/wiki/Best-of-five) series. In the other three rounds, teams competed in a [best-of-seven](/wiki/Best-of-seven) series (scores in the bracket indicate the number of games won in each series).

## Broadcasting

This was the first season in more than a decade that [CBC](/wiki/CBC_Television) was not the lone Canadian national broadcaster. While [Molson](/wiki/Molson) continued to present *[Hockey Night in Canada](/wiki/Hockey_Night_in_Canada)* on Saturday nights, rival brewery [Carling O'Keefe](/wiki/Carling_O%27Keefe) began airing Friday night games on [CTV](/wiki/NHL_on_CTV). The two networks also split the playoffs and finals. CTV had previously aired *HNIC*-produced telecasts in the 1960s.

This was the third and final season of the league's U.S. national broadcast rights deal with [USA](/wiki/NHL_on_USA), covering a slate of regular season games and selected playoff games. [ESPN](/wiki/NHL_on_ESPN) then signed a three-year agreement with the league after bidding about twice as much as USA had been paying. USA would not televise the NHL again until after the network was acquired by [NBCUniversal](/wiki/NBCUniversal) in the early 2000s, airing selected playoff games as part of [NBC Sports](/wiki/NHL_on_NBC)' overall NHL coverage between 2015 and 2021.

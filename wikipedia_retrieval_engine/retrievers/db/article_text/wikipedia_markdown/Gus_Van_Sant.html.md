**Gus Green Van Sant Jr.** (born July 24, 1952) is an American filmmaker, photographer, painter, and musician. He has earned acclaim as an [independent](/wiki/Independent_film) filmmaker, and is considered to be one of the most prominent [auteurs](/wiki/Auteur) of the [new queer cinema](/wiki/New_queer_cinema) movement. His films typically deal with themes of marginalized [subcultures](/wiki/Subculture), especially within [gay culture](/wiki/Gay_culture).

His early career was devoted to directing television commercials in the [Pacific Northwest](/wiki/Pacific_Northwest). He made his feature-length directorial debut film *[Mala Noche](/wiki/Mala_Noche)* (1985). He earned acclaim for a string of independent films such as the crime drama *[Drugstore Cowboy](/wiki/Drugstore_Cowboy)* (1989), the adventure film *[My Own Private Idaho](/wiki/My_Own_Private_Idaho)* (1991), and the [black comedy](/wiki/Black_comedy) *[To Die For](/wiki/To_Die_For)* (1995). He earned [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) nominations for the drama *[Good Will Hunting](/wiki/Good_Will_Hunting)* (1997), and the biographical film *[Milk](/wiki/Milk_(2008_American_film))* (2008), both of which also received [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) nominations.

Van Sant directed the [psychological drama](/wiki/Psychological_drama) *[Elephant](/wiki/Elephant_(2003_film))* (2003), a film based on the [Columbine High School massacre](/wiki/Columbine_High_School_massacre), for which he won the [Palme d'Or](/wiki/Palme_d%27Or) and [Best Director Award](/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Director) at the [Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2003_Cannes_Film_Festival). The reactions to Van Sant's creative output have since been mixed. He received positive reviews for *[Finding Forrester](/wiki/Finding_Forrester)* (2000), *[Paranoid Park](/wiki/Paranoid_Park_(film))* (2007), and *[Don't Worry, He Won't Get Far on Foot](/wiki/Don%27t_Worry,_He_Won%27t_Get_Far_on_Foot)* (2018) while receiving negative receptions for his films *[Even Cowgirls Get the Blues](/wiki/Even_Cowgirls_Get_the_Blues_(film))* (1993), *[Psycho](/wiki/Psycho_(1998_film))* (1998), and *[The Sea of Trees](/wiki/The_Sea_of_Trees)* (2015). His films *[Last Days](/wiki/Last_Days_(2005_film))* (2005) and *[Promised Land](/wiki/Promised_Land_(2012_film))* (2012) received mixed reviews.

Also known for his work on television, he has directed and executive produced the [political drama](/wiki/Political_drama) series *[Boss](/wiki/Boss_(TV_series))* (2011), the [docudrama](/wiki/Docudrama) miniseries *[When We Rise](/wiki/When_We_Rise)* (2018), and the [anthology series](/wiki/Anthology_series) *[Feud: Capote vs. The Swans](/wiki/Feud:_Capote_vs._The_Swans)* (2024). Van Sant has written screenplays for several of his earlier works, and has also published the novel, *[Pink](/wiki/Pink_(Van_Sant_novel))*, and a book of his photography, *108 Portraits*, He has also released two musical albums.

## Early life and education

Van Sant was born and raised in [Louisville, Kentucky](/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), the son of Betty (née Seay) and Gus Green Van Sant Sr., a clothing manufacturer and traveling salesman who rapidly worked his way into middle class prosperity, holding executive marketing positions that included being president of the [White Stag Manufacturing Company](/wiki/White_Stag_(clothing))'s apparel operation. As a result of his father's job, the family moved continually during his childhood.

Van Sant's paternal family is of partial Dutch origin; the name "Van Sant" is derived from the Dutch name "[*Van Zandt*](/wiki/Van_(Dutch))". The earliest Van Zandt arrived in the [New Netherland](/wiki/New_Netherland) area in the early 17th century, around what is now [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City).

Gus Van Sant is an alumnus of [Darien High School](/wiki/Darien_High_School) in [Darien, Connecticut](/wiki/Darien,_Connecticut), and [The Catlin Gabel School](/wiki/The_Catlin_Gabel_School) in [Portland, Oregon](/wiki/Portland,_Oregon). One constant in the director's early years was his interest in visual arts (namely, painting and [Super-8](/wiki/Super-8) filmmaking); while still in school he began making semi-autobiographical shorts costing between 30 and 50 dollars. Van Sant's artistic leanings took him to the [Rhode Island School of Design](/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) in 1970, where his introduction to various avant-garde directors inspired him to change his major from painting to cinema.

## Career

### 1982–1989: Early career

After spending time in Europe, Van Sant went to [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) in 1976. He secured a job as a production assistant to filmmaker [Ken Shapiro](/wiki/Ken_Shapiro), with whom he developed a few ideas, none of which came to fruition. In 1981, Van Sant made *Alice in Hollywood*, a film about a naïve young actress who goes to Hollywood and abandons her ideals. It was never released. During this period, Van Sant began to spend time observing the denizens of the more down-and-out sections of [Hollywood Boulevard](/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard). He became fascinated by the existence of this marginalized section of L.A.'s population, especially in context with the more ordinary, prosperous world that surrounded them. Van Sant would repeatedly focus his work on those existing on society's fringes, making his feature film [directorial debut](/wiki/Directorial_debut) *[Mala Noche](/wiki/Mala_Noche)*.

It was made two years after Van Sant went to New York to work in an advertising agency. He saved $20,000 during his tenure there, enabling him to finance the majority of his tale of doomed love between a gay liquor store clerk and a Mexican immigrant. The film, which was taken from Portland street writer [Walt Curtis](/wiki/Walt_Curtis)' semi-autobiographical novella, featured some of the director's hallmarks, notably an unfulfilled romanticism, a dry sense of the absurd, and the refusal to treat homosexuality as something deserving of judgment. Unlike many gay filmmakers, Van Sant—who had long been openly gay—declined to use same-sex relationships as fodder for overtly political statements, although such relationships would frequently appear in his films.

Shot in black-and-white, the film earned Van Sant almost overnight acclaim on the festival circuit, with the *Los Angeles Times* naming it the year's best [independent film](/wiki/Independent_film). The film's success attracted Hollywood interest, and Van Sant was briefly courted by [Universal](/wiki/Universal_Pictures); the courtship ended after Van Sant pitched a series of project ideas (including what would become *[Drugstore Cowboy](/wiki/Drugstore_Cowboy)* and *[My Own Private Idaho](/wiki/My_Own_Private_Idaho)*) that the studio declined to take interest in.

Van Sant returned to Portland, Oregon, where he set up house and began giving life to the ideas rejected by Universal. He directed *Drugstore Cowboy* about four drug addicts robbing pharmacies to support their habit. The film met with great critical success and revived the career of [Matt Dillon](/wiki/Matt_Dillon).

### 1990–1995: Indie and arthouse success

*Drugstore Cowboy'*s exploration of the lives of those living on society's outer fringes, as well as its Portland setting, were mirrored in Van Sant's next effort, the similarly acclaimed *[My Own Private Idaho](/wiki/My_Own_Private_Idaho)* (1991). Only with the success of *Cowboy* was Van Sant now given license to make *Idaho* (a film he had originally pitched that was knocked back several times because the studios deemed the script 'too risky'). [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema) now gave Van Sant the green light, and he went on a mission to get the *Idaho* script into the hands of [River Phoenix](/wiki/River_Phoenix) and [Keanu Reeves](/wiki/Keanu_Reeves), his preferred choice for the two young leads. After months of struggle with agents and managers over the content of the script, Van Sant finally secured Phoenix and Reeves, who played the roles of Mike Waters and Scott Favor, respectively.

Centering on the dealings of two male hustlers (played by Phoenix and Reeves), the film was a compelling examination of unrequited love, alienation and the concept of family (a concept Van Sant repeatedly explores in his films). The film won him an [Independent Spirit Award](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award) for his screenplay (he had won the same award for his *[Drugstore Cowboy](/wiki/Drugstore_Cowboy)* screenplay), as well as greater prestige. The film gained River Phoenix best actor honors at the [Venice Film Festival](/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival) among others. It helped Reeves—previously best known for his work in the *[Bill and Ted](/wiki/Bill_and_Ted)* movies—to get the critical respect that had eluded him.

Van Sant's next film, a 1993 [adaptation](/wiki/Even_Cowgirls_Get_the_Blues_(film)) of [Tom Robbins](/wiki/Tom_Robbins)' *[Even Cowgirls Get the Blues](/wiki/Even_Cowgirls_Get_the_Blues_(novel))*, was an excessive flop, both commercially and critically. Featuring an unusually large budget (for Van Sant, at least) of $8.5 million and a large, eclectic cast including [Uma Thurman](/wiki/Uma_Thurman), [John Hurt](/wiki/John_Hurt), Keanu Reeves and a newcomer in the form of River Phoenix's younger sister [Rain](/wiki/Rain_Phoenix) (at Phoenix's suggestion), the film was worked and then reworked, but the finished product nonetheless resulted in something approaching a significant disaster.

Van Sant's 1995 film *[To Die For](/wiki/To_Die_For)* helped to restore his luster. An adaptation of [Joyce Maynard](/wiki/Joyce_Maynard)'s novel, the [black comedy](/wiki/Black_comedy) starred [Nicole Kidman](/wiki/Nicole_Kidman) as a murderously ambitious weather girl; it also stars [Matt Dillon](/wiki/Matt_Dillon) as her hapless husband and, the third Phoenix sibling in as many projects, [Joaquin Phoenix](/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix), as her equally hapless lover (River had died of a drug overdose a year and half earlier). It was Van Sant's first effort for a major studio ([Columbia](/wiki/Columbia_Pictures)), and its success paved the way for further projects of the director's choosing. The same year, he served as executive producer for [Larry Clark](/wiki/Larry_Clark)'s *[Kids](/wiki/Kids_(film))*; it was a fitting assignment, due to both the film's subject matter and the fact that Clark's photographs of junkies had served as reference points for Van Sant's *Drugstore Cowboy*.

### 1997–2003: Mainstream breakout

In 1997, Van Sant gained mainstream recognition and critical acclaim thanks to *[Good Will Hunting](/wiki/Good_Will_Hunting)*, which was written by [Matt Damon](/wiki/Matt_Damon) and [Ben Affleck](/wiki/Ben_Affleck). The film, about a troubled, blue-collar mathematical genius, was a huge critical and commercial success. It was nominated for nine [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Award), including Best Director for Van Sant. It won two, including Best Screenplay for Damon and Affleck, and Best Supporting Actor Oscar for [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams), who, in his acceptance speech, referred to Van Sant as "being so subtle you're almost subliminal." Van Sant, Damon and Affleck parodied themselves and the film's success in [Kevin Smith](/wiki/Kevin_Smith)'s *[Jay and Silent Bob Strike Back](/wiki/Jay_and_Silent_Bob_Strike_Back).*

Van Sant received the opportunity to [remake](/wiki/Psycho_(1998_film)) [Alfred Hitchcock](/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock)'s classic *[Psycho](/wiki/Psycho_(1960_film))*. As opposed to reinterpreting the 1960 film, Van Sant opted to recreate the film shot-for-shot, in color, with a cast of young Hollywood A-listers. His decision was met with equal parts curiosity, skepticism, and derision from industry insiders and outsiders alike, and the finished result met with a similar reception. It starred [Anne Heche](/wiki/Anne_Heche), [Vince Vaughn](/wiki/Vince_Vaughn) and [Julianne Moore](/wiki/Julianne_Moore), and met with a negative critical reception and did [poorly at the box office](/wiki/Box-office_bomb).

In 2000, Van Sant directed *[Finding Forrester](/wiki/Finding_Forrester)*, about a high-school student ([Rob Brown](/wiki/Rob_Brown_(actor))) from [the Bronx](/wiki/The_Bronx) unlikely becoming a friend of a crusty, reclusive author ([Sean Connery](/wiki/Sean_Connery)). Critical response was generally positive and became a box office success. In addition to directing, he devoted considerable energy to releasing two albums and publishing a novel, *[Pink](/wiki/Pink_(Van_Sant_novel))*, which was a thinly veiled exploration of his grief over River Phoenix's death.

### 2003–present: Return to arthouse cinema

Van Sant and [Joaquin Phoenix](/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix) at the press conference of *[Don't Worry, He Won't Get Far on Foot](/wiki/Don%27t_Worry,_He_Won%27t_Get_Far_on_Foot)* (Berlin Film Festival 2018)

Van Sant traveled to the deserts of [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina), [Utah](/wiki/Utah), and [Death Valley](/wiki/Death_Valley) for the production of 2002's *[Gerry](/wiki/Gerry_(2002_film))*, a loosely devised, largely improvised feature in which stars Matt Damon and [Casey Affleck](/wiki/Casey_Affleck)—both playing characters named Gerry—wander through the desert, discussing *[Wheel of Fortune](/wiki/Wheel_of_Fortune_(American_game_show))*, video games, and nothing in particular. The film premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival).

It took *Gerry* over a year to make it to theaters, in which time Van Sant began production on his next film, *[Elephant](/wiki/Elephant_(2003_film))*. Approached by [HBO](/wiki/HBO) and producer [Diane Keaton](/wiki/Diane_Keaton) to craft a fictional film based on the 1999 [Columbine High School massacre](/wiki/Columbine_High_School_massacre), the director chose to shoot in his hometown of Portland, employing dozens of untrained, teen actors. Melding improvisational long takes like those in *Gerry* with [Harris Savides](/wiki/Harris_Savides)' fluid camerawork, the film was influenced by Alan Clarke's 1989 film of the same name (see *[Elephant](/wiki/Elephant_(1989_film))*). The finished film provoked strong reactions from audiences at the [2003 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2003_Cannes_Film_Festival). At the [Cannes](/wiki/Cannes) festival, the jury awarded *Elephant* with their top prize, the [Palme d'Or](/wiki/Palme_d%27Or), and Van Sant with his first Best Director statue from the festival.

In 2005, Van Sant released *[Last Days](/wiki/Last_Days_(2005_film))*, the final component of what he refers to as his "Death Trilogy", (the other parts being *Gerry* and *Elephant*). It is a fictionalized account of what happened to [Nirvana](/wiki/Nirvana_(band)) frontman [Kurt Cobain](/wiki/Kurt_Cobain) in the days leading up to his death. In 2006, Van Sant began work on *[Paranoid Park](/wiki/Paranoid_Park_(film))* based on the book by [Blake Nelson](/wiki/Blake_Nelson), about a skateboarding teenager who accidentally causes someone's death. The film was released in Europe in February 2008. He also directed the "Le Marais" segment of the omnibus film *[Paris, je t'aime](/wiki/Paris,_je_t%27aime)*.

Released in 2008, Van Sant's *[Milk](/wiki/Milk_(2008_American_film))* is a biopic of openly gay San Francisco politician [Harvey Milk](/wiki/Harvey_Milk), who was assassinated in 1978. The film received eight [Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations at the [81st Academy Awards](/wiki/81st_Academy_Awards), including [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture), winning two for [Best Actor in a Leading Role](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) for [Sean Penn](/wiki/Sean_Penn), who starred as Milk, and [Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay) for writer [Dustin Lance Black](/wiki/Dustin_Lance_Black). Van Sant was nominated for [Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director). Van Sant later stated that his experience with Sean Penn on the film was "amazing". His 2011 film *[Restless](/wiki/Restless_(2011_film))* was screened in the [Un Certain Regard](/wiki/Un_Certain_Regard) section at the [2011 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2011_Cannes_Film_Festival), and starred Henry Hopper and [Mia Wasikowska](/wiki/Mia_Wasikowska).

Van Sant's film, *[Promised Land](/wiki/Promised_Land_(2012_film))*, was released on December 28, 2012. The film stars [Frances McDormand](/wiki/Frances_McDormand), Matt Damon, and John Krasinski—the latter two co-wrote the screenplay based on a story by Dave Eggers. Filmed in April 2012, the production company, Focus Features, selected the release date so that the film is eligible to qualify for awards consideration. After *Promised Land*, Van Sant directed a film titled *[Sea of Trees](/wiki/Sea_of_Trees_(film))*, which starred [Matthew McConaughey](/wiki/Matthew_McConaughey) and [Ken Watanabe](/wiki/Ken_Watanabe). The film tells the story of a man who travels to the infamous [Aokigahara](/wiki/Aokigahara) suicide forest in Japan to kill himself, only to encounter another man wishing to kill himself as well, with whom he then embarks on a "spiritual journey". The film was selected to compete for the [Palme d'Or](/wiki/Palme_d%27Or) at the [2015 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2015_Cannes_Film_Festival) but was met with harsh critical reception at the Cannes, being booed and laughed at.

In December 2016, it was announced Van Sant would direct *[Don't Worry, He Won't Get Far on Foot](/wiki/Don%27t_Worry,_He_Won%27t_Get_Far_on_Foot)*, a biopic about cartoonist [John Callahan](/wiki/John_Callahan_(cartoonist)), starring [Joaquin Phoenix](/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix), [Rooney Mara](/wiki/Rooney_Mara), [Jonah Hill](/wiki/Jonah_Hill), [Jack Black](/wiki/Jack_Black), and [Mark Webber](/wiki/Mark_Webber_(actor)). Principal photography began in March 2017.

In December 2024, he was reported to be directing *[Dead Man's Wire](/wiki/Dead_Man%27s_Wire)* starring [Bill Skarsgård](/wiki/Bill_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) and [Dacre Montgomery](/wiki/Dacre_Montgomery), and produced by Elevated Films and Balcony 9 Productions.

### Other work

Van Sant released two musical albums: *Gus Van Sant* and *18 Songs About Golf*. Van Sant played himself in episodes of the IFC series *[Portlandia](/wiki/Portlandia)* and the HBO series *[Entourage](/wiki/Entourage_(American_TV_series))*.

Van Sant was credited for all photography, paintings and art direction on the [Red Hot Chili Peppers](/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers)' album *[Blood Sugar Sex Magik](/wiki/Blood_Sugar_Sex_Magik)*, and directed the video for "[Under the Bridge](/wiki/Under_the_Bridge)". He directed the pilot for the Starz television program *[Boss](/wiki/Boss_(TV_series))*, starring [Kelsey Grammer](/wiki/Kelsey_Grammer). Van Sant went onto *The [Bret Easton Ellis](/wiki/Bret_Easton_Ellis) Podcast* in 2014 to discuss filmmaking, writing, film history and their collaborations that never got made (*The Golden Suicides*) and the one that did (*[The Canyons](/wiki/The_Canyons_(film))*).

## Personal life

Van Sant is [gay](/wiki/Gay_men) and lives in the [Los Feliz](/wiki/Los_Feliz,_Los_Angeles) neighborhood of [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles).

## Archive

The moving image collection of Gus Van Sant is held at the [Academy Film Archive](/wiki/Academy_Film_Archive). The archive has preserved many of Van Sant's short films, including *The Happy Organ*, *Ken Death Gets Out of Jail*, *Five Ways to Kill Yourself*, and others.

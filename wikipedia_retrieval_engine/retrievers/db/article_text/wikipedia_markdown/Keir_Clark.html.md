**William Keir Clark** (May 30, 1910 – November 28, 2010) was a [Canadian](/wiki/Canadians) merchant and political figure in [Prince Edward Island](/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island). After serving as mayor of [Montague](/wiki/Montague,_Prince_Edward_Island) in 1941 and 1942, he represented [3rd Kings](/wiki/3rd_Kings) in the [Legislative Assembly of Prince Edward Island](/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Prince_Edward_Island) from 1947 to 1959 and [4th Kings](/wiki/4th_Kings) from 1966 to 1970 as a [Liberal](/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Liberal_Party). In 1970, he sat as an Independent Liberal.

He was born in [Mount Stewart, Prince Edward Island](/wiki/Mount_Stewart,_Prince_Edward_Island), the son of [Russell Charles Clark](/wiki/Russell_C._Clark) and Marion J. McKay, and was educated at [Prince of Wales College](/wiki/Prince_of_Wales_College) and [Dalhousie University](/wiki/Dalhousie_University). In 1940, Clark married Anna I. McLaren. He served as mayor of [Montague](/wiki/Montague,_Prince_Edward_Island). Clark served in the province's Executive Council as Minister of Education from 1953 to 1959, as Provincial Treasurer from 1954 to 1955 and as Minister of Health and Municipal Affairs from 1966 to 1969. He resigned from cabinet in 1969, saying that he did not support all aspects of his government's development plan.

He was defeated when he ran for reelection in 1959. In 2008 he became P.E.I.'s oldest living former politician, and he [turned 100](/wiki/Centenarian) in 2010. Clark died in Eldon, Prince Edward Island on November 28, 2010.

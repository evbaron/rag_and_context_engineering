**Capital One Arena** is an [indoor arena](/wiki/Indoor_arena) in [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) Located in the [Chinatown](/wiki/Chinatown,_Washington,_D.C.) section of the larger [Penn Quarter](/wiki/Penn_Quarter) neighborhood, the arena sits atop the [Gallery Place](/wiki/Gallery_Place_station) rapid transit station of the [Washington Metro](/wiki/Washington_Metro). The arena was opened on December 2, 1997, as **MCI Center**, but renamed to **Verizon Center** in 2006 when [MCI](/wiki/MCI_Inc.) was acquired by [Verizon Communications](/wiki/Verizon_Communications). The name was changed to Capital One Arena in 2017 when [Capital One](/wiki/Capital_One) bank, headquartered in nearby [Tysons, Virginia](/wiki/Tysons,_Virginia), purchased the naming rights.

Owned and operated by [Monumental Sports & Entertainment](/wiki/Monumental_Sports_%26_Entertainment), it is the home arena of the [Washington Capitals](/wiki/Washington_Capitals) of the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) (NHL), the [Washington Wizards](/wiki/Washington_Wizards) of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA), and the [Georgetown University men's basketball team](/wiki/Georgetown_Hoyas_men%27s_basketball). It was also home to the [Washington Mystics](/wiki/Washington_Mystics) of the [Women's National Basketball Association](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) (WNBA) from 1998 to 2018, after which they moved to the [CareFirst Arena](/wiki/CareFirst_Arena) in [southeast Washington](/wiki/Southeast_(Washington,_D.C.)) for the 2019 season. The arena project was a commercial success for its backers.

The development of the arena has contributed to the [gentrification](/wiki/Gentrification_in_the_United_States) of the surrounding area, the displacement of most of its [Asian-American](/wiki/Asian-American) residents (the local [Chinese-American](/wiki/Chinese-American) population, which numbered over 3,000 before the arena's construction, was a mere 300 in 2023), and the replacement of most of the small businesses and restaurants that served the Asian-American community by national chains.

## History

The block where the arena was built, between 6th and 7th and F and G Streets, historically held a mix of residences and small businesses. By the 1960s, it was suffering from [urban decay](/wiki/Urban_decay), like much of the eastern end of [Downtown Washington](/wiki/Downtown_(Washington,_D.C.)). In 1973, while the [Gallery Place](/wiki/Gallery_Place_station) Metro station was being developed below it, the District government bought the land in hopes of redeveloping it. Capital Landmark Associates was selected in 1979 to develop the site with a planned mixed-use complex including retail, offices, apartments, and a hotel. Most of the remaining buildings on the site were demolished in 1985. The project languished for many years but never materialized, and was finally canceled in 1992.

Before the arena's opening, the Capitals and the Wizards (then known as the Washington Bullets) played at [USAir Arena](/wiki/Capital_Centre_(Landover,_Maryland)) in the Washington suburb of [Landover, Maryland](/wiki/Landover,_Maryland). The teams experienced subpar attendance because the location was inconvenient for both Washington and Baltimore residents, and their arena, though only 20 years old, was not up to the standards of other NBA and NHL venues. In December 1993, [Abe Pollin](/wiki/Abe_Pollin), the owner of both teams, began studying options to move the teams to a new arena to be built with public financing, with possible locations including Baltimore, downtown Washington, and [Laurel, Maryland](/wiki/Laurel,_Maryland).

The arena in January 2006, then known as MCI Center

A group of Washington business leaders brokered a deal between Pollin and the District government to build an arena at the Gallery Place site, with the District paying for the $150 million project, which was envisioned to have shopping, food, and exhibitors for daily use even when there was no arena event. The [D.C. Council](/wiki/D.C._Council) approved a special tax on businesses to finance the deal. However, a competing proposal soon emerged, when [Robert Johnson](/wiki/Robert_L._Johnson), head of [Black Entertainment Television](/wiki/BET), offered to build the arena with mostly private financing. With the arena deal facing criticism amid the [District's budget crisis](/wiki/District_of_Columbia_Financial_Control_Board), Pollin eventually agreed to privately fund the construction of the building, which ultimately came to $200 million (US$375 million in 2024 dollars). The District would pay for other costs, including purchasing the portion of the land it did not already own, preparing the site, and expanding the Metro station; these eventually amounted to $79 million (US$141 million in 2024 dollars). The District leased the land to Pollin at a below-market rate of $300,000 per year.

A [naming rights](/wiki/Naming_rights) deal was struck with [MCI Communications](/wiki/MCI_Communications) to name the arena as the **MCI Center**. The [groundbreaking ceremony](/wiki/Groundbreaking_ceremony) for the project was held in October 1995. On December 2, 1997, the arena held its first event, a game between the Wizards and the [Seattle SuperSonics](/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics), with President [Bill Clinton](/wiki/Bill_Clinton) in attendance. The arena had a 25,000-square-foot (2,300 m2) [Discovery Channel Store](/wiki/Discovery_Channel_Store) from 1998 to 2001 and the MCI National Sports Gallery, an interactive sports museum with interactive games, memorabilia, and the American Sportscasters Hall of Fame inside from 1998 to 2000 or 2001 which was repurposed for office space. Clinton toured the gallery before the game, playing the museum games. A block of F Street NW between 6th and 7th Street NW outside the arena was declared Fun Street, complete with signage. This block later was declared Abe Pollin Way in 2007. The arena was noted for building spectator seats vertically rather than out, creating better views for all attending albeit with limited leg room in the upper levels, as well as spacious quarters for players and coaches with advanced competitive research technology. The arena concourse featured multimedia arenaNet stations where fans could check scores, watch highlights, and send digital postcards over email. These replaced an abandoned idea to have smart seats with televisions and technology that was scrapped due to technological challenges. Arena technology was powered by a virtual LAN software and switching technology called ArenaNET from [Cabletron Systems](/wiki/Cabletron_Systems).

In 1999, a group led by technology executive [Ted Leonsis](/wiki/Ted_Leonsis) bought a 36% stake in Pollin's holdings, including the MCI Center, as well as full ownership of the Capitals. The Leonsis group increased its stake to 44% in 2000.

Capital One Arena, then the Verizon Center, in August 2008

In January 2006, [Verizon Communications](/wiki/Verizon_Communications) purchased MCI and the arena's name was changed accordingly to **Verizon Center**. VIDA Fitness opened its first location in the arena that same year. The following year, in 2007, the "first true indoor high-definition [LED](/wiki/LED) [scoreboard](/wiki/Scoreboard)" was installed in the arena. In May 2024, VIDA Fitness announced that they would close their Gallery Place location. Gallery Place, a 14-screen movie theater, opened at Capital One Arena in 2004.

In June 2010, following Pollin's death in November 2009, the Leonsis group, newly organized as [Monumental Sports & Entertainment](/wiki/Monumental_Sports_%26_Entertainment), bought out Pollin's interests, gaining full ownership of the arena and the Wizards.

A report emerged in May 2015 that Verizon would not renew its naming rights to the Verizon Center when its agreement with Monumental was to end in 2018. In the same week, it was announced that [Etihad Airways](/wiki/Etihad_Airways) signed a deal to become the official airline of the arena, sparking speculation that Etihad might be the leading contender to assume naming rights in 2017. However, on August 9, 2017, it was announced that the bank [Capital One](/wiki/Capital_One) had purchased the rights, renaming the venue Capital One Arena. In October 2024, it was revealed that Etihad Airways is no longer the official airline for Capital One Arena as [United Airlines](/wiki/United_Airlines) (which operates a hub at [Dulles International Airport](/wiki/Washington_Dulles_International_Airport)) was chosen as the new official airline.

Panorama of the Capital One Arena before the Toronto Maple Leafs @ Washington Capitals matchup on [Black Friday](/wiki/Black_Friday_(shopping)) 2025.

In 2019 and 2020, Monumental Sports undertook a $30 million renovation of the arena. This included completely replacing the arena's seating, improving the concourse, and altering many of the arena's dining options. A new, larger overhead video board was also added as well as a new SkyRing video screen that goes around the top of the arena.

In July 2020, bookmaker [William Hill](/wiki/William_Hill_(bookmaker)) opened a [sportsbook](/wiki/Sportsbook) at the arena, following the 2018 legalization of sports betting in Washington. It was the first brick-and-mortar sportsbook in the District, and the first to open at a professional sports venue in the United States.

On June 23, 2023, *[The Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* reported that Monumental Sports was considering moving the Capitals and Wizards to a new arena in the [National Landing](/wiki/National_Landing) area of [Arlington](/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia) in [Northern Virginia](/wiki/Northern_Virginia) if the Washington, D.C. government did not invest in upgrades to the arena and surrounding area. Despite this, the article outlined that the city intended on continuing its dialogue with the company to keep both teams in Capital One Arena. Monumental later pivoted to a part of the National Landing area lying in a different Northern Virginia jurisdiction, announcing on December 13, 2023, that it planned to build the new arena in the [Potomac Yard](/wiki/Potomac_Yard) area of [Alexandria](/wiki/Alexandria,_Virginia). Under the revised plan, Capital One Arena would have remained in use as a concert and event venue during the NBA and NHL seasons, and also become the permanent home of Monumental's [Women's National Basketball Association](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) (WNBA) team, the [Washington Mystics](/wiki/Washington_Mystics). In March 2024, after officials in Alexandria announced that the $2 billion entertainment and sports complex plans were scrapped, Washington, D.C. mayor [Muriel Bowser](/wiki/Muriel_Bowser) announced that she had signed a deal with both teams' majority owner, Ted Leonsis, to keep the Capitals and Wizards in the District "at least until 2050." The deal will see a massive renovation to the arena which will be completed by 2028 in six phases, changing the exterior and interior that will include new technology and improved fan experiences.

Students from [Middletown High School](/wiki/Middletown_High_School_(Ohio)) in Ohio march at an indoor Presidential Inauguration parade event in Washington on Monday, January 20, 2025.

Capital One Arena is undergoing a multi-year $850 million renovation that will completely alter the exterior and parts of the interior of the arena. $515 million is coming from the District of Columbia government, while the rest is being paid for by [Monumental Sports and Entertainment](/wiki/Monumental_Sports_and_Entertainment), which owns the teams. The renovation is expected to be completed in time for the start of the 2027-2028 NHL and NBA seasons.

The indoor parade of the [second inauguration of Donald Trump](/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) took place in the arena on January 20, 2025.

## Sports

### Ice hockey

The Capitals playing the [Pittsburgh Penguins](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) in May 2017 during the [2017 Stanley Cup playoffs](/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) at Capital One Arena, then known as the Verizon Center

The arena has been home to the Capitals [NHL](/wiki/NHL) team since its opening. As a result, numerous memorable moments in franchise history have occurred in the arena. The arena hosted games three and four of the [1998 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/1998_Stanley_Cup_Final), when the Capitals lost to the [Detroit Red Wings](/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) in four games. The Red Wings hoisted the namesake [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup) in the arena on June 16, 1998, after winning game four by a score of 4–1. On April 5, 2008, the Capitals won the Southeast Division in the last game of the regular season, after beating the [Florida Panthers](/wiki/Florida_Panthers) 3–1. Game 2 of the 2009 Eastern Conference semifinals between the Capitals and the [Pittsburgh Penguins](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins), played on May 4, 2009, saw dueling [hat tricks](/wiki/Hat-trick_(ice_hockey)) from Ovechkin and rival [Sidney Crosby](/wiki/Sidney_Crosby) of the Pittsburgh Penguins, culminating in a 4–3 victory for Washington thanks to an additional goal from [David Steckel](/wiki/David_Steckel). The arena also hosted games three and four of the [2018 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_Final). The Capitals won both games and then went on to win game five in [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas) to capture the Stanley Cup for the first major sports championship for a Washington, D.C. team since the [1991 Washington Redskins](/wiki/1991_Washington_Redskins_season). The Capitals had their Stanley Cup banner installation ceremony in the arena before their first game of the next season, which took place on October 3, 2018.

On April 4, 2025, with [Wayne Gretzky](/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky) and commissioner [Gary Bettman](/wiki/Gary_Bettman) in attendance, Ovechkin scored goals 893 and 894 in a 5-3 Capitals win over the [Chicago Blackhawks](/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks). This tied him with Gretzky for the all-time goals record.

#### Non-NHL hockey events

The arena hosted the 2009 "Frozen Four", the final round of the [2009 NCAA Division I men's ice hockey tournament](/wiki/2009_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_ice_hockey_tournament) [Boston University](/wiki/Boston_University_Terriers_men%27s_ice_hockey) took [Miami OH](/wiki/Miami_RedHawks_men%27s_ice_hockey) in overtime 4–3.

The inaugural 2024 Capital Hockey Classic took place on December 12, 2024, This event marked the first full-fledged college hockey event since the [2009 D1 Finals](/wiki/2009_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_ice_hockey_tournament). It featured military and club collegiate ice hockey teams.

The Capital Hockey Classic aimed to capture attention, especially since it occurred just two days before the [Army-Navy game](/wiki/Army%E2%80%93Navy_Game) at the nearby [Northwest Stadium](/wiki/Northwest_Stadium). The event included two matches: the first game featured [Army](/wiki/Army_Black_Knights_men%27s_ice_hockey) vs [Navy](/wiki/Navy_Midshipmen) Club hockey at 5PM, followed by the Army Black Knights vs. the [Penn State Nittany Lions](/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_men%27s_ice_hockey) [D1](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I) match at 7PM.

On January 18, 2026, the [Professional Women's Hockey League](/wiki/Professional_Women%27s_Hockey_League) (PWHL) played their first game in Washington, D.C. between the [Montreal Victoire](/wiki/Montreal_Victoire) and [New York Sirens](/wiki/New_York_Sirens). The Sirens won 2–1 against the Victoire in front of 17,228 fans, setting a then-attendance record for women's hockey in the United States.

### Basketball

A ticket from a 2006 game at the then-MCI Center between the Wizards and [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons)

The arena has been home to the Wizards [NBA](/wiki/NBA) team since its opening and was home to the [Washington Mystics](/wiki/Washington_Mystics) [WNBA](/wiki/WNBA) team from 1998 to 2018, before the Mystics moved to [a new, smaller arena](/wiki/CareFirst_Arena) in the [Congress Heights](/wiki/Congress_Heights) area of [southeast](/wiki/Southeast_(Washington,_D.C.)) Washington. In 2024, the Mystics relocated their June 7 and September 19 games against the [Indiana Fever](/wiki/Indiana_Fever) to Capital One Arena, citing the demand of tickets as a result of the rising popularity of [Caitlin Clark](/wiki/Caitlin_Clark). The [Georgetown Hoyas men's basketball](/wiki/Georgetown_Hoyas_men%27s_basketball) team has also played there since the arena's opening. The arena has hosted three basketball all star games: the [2001 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2001_NBA_All-Star_Game) and the [2002](/wiki/2002_WNBA_All-Star_Game) and [2007 WNBA All-Star Games](/wiki/2007_WNBA_All-Star_Game). The arena has been home to many playoff games, but has yet to host an [NBA Finals](/wiki/NBA_Finals).

The arena has hosted games for the [NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament) several times. It hosted first- and second-round games in 1998, 2002, 2008 and 2011, and hosted the regional finals (sweet sixteen and elite eight) in 2006, 2013, 2019, and 2026. Most notably the [2005–06 George Mason Patriots men's basketball team](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_George_Mason_Patriots_men%27s_basketball_team) from nearby [Fairfax, Virginia](/wiki/Fairfax,_Virginia) advanced to the Final Four in the arena. The arena also hosted the [Atlantic 10 men's basketball tournament](/wiki/Atlantic_10_men%27s_basketball_tournament) in 2018 and 2022. It hosted the [ACC men's basketball tournament](/wiki/ACC_men%27s_basketball_tournament) in 2005, 2016, and 2024. In 2017 the arena hosted the [Big Ten men's basketball tournament](/wiki/2017_Big_Ten_men%27s_basketball_tournament).

The [Harlem Globetrotters](/wiki/Harlem_Globetrotters) play in the arena on an annual basis, often against the [Washington Generals](/wiki/Washington_Generals), a team named in honor of President [Dwight Eisenhower](/wiki/Dwight_Eisenhower).

### Combat sports

[Mike Tyson](/wiki/Mike_Tyson)'s final non-exhibition fight ([Mike Tyson vs. Kevin McBride](/wiki/Mike_Tyson_vs._Kevin_McBride)) was held at the arena on June 11, 2005. On October 1, 2011, [UFC Live: Cruz vs. Johnson](/wiki/UFC_Live:_Cruz_vs._Johnson) was held at the arena. On December 7, 2019, [UFC on ESPN: Overeem vs. Rozenstruik](/wiki/UFC_on_ESPN:_Overeem_vs._Rozenstruik) was held at the arena.

### Arena football

In 2017, the [Washington Valor](/wiki/Washington_Valor) began play at the arena for their inaugural season in the [Arena Football League](/wiki/Arena_Football_League). The Valor folded in 2019 and the arena has not hosted an Arena League game since.

### Gymnastics and figure skating

The arena hosted the [2003 World Figure Skating Championships](/wiki/2003_World_Figure_Skating_Championships) and the 2016 Kellogg's Tour of Gymnastics Champions.

### Professional wrestling

The arena has hosted various [professional wrestling](/wiki/Professional_wrestling) events; it hosted the final four editions of [WCW](/wiki/WCW)'s [Starrcade](/wiki/Starrcade) pay-per-view, followed by [WWE](/wiki/WWE)'s [Backlash](/wiki/WWE_Backlash) in [2000](/wiki/Backlash_(2000)), [SummerSlam](/wiki/SummerSlam) in [2005](/wiki/SummerSlam_(2005)), [Cyber Sunday](/wiki/WWE_Cyber_Sunday) in [2007](/wiki/Cyber_Sunday_(2007)), [Survivor Series](/wiki/Survivor_Series) in [2009](/wiki/Survivor_Series_(2009)), [Capitol Punishment](/wiki/WWE_Capitol_Punishment) in 2011, and [Battleground](/wiki/WWE_Battleground) in [2016](/wiki/Battleground_(2016)). On December 13, 2025, the Capital One Arena hosted WWE's [Saturday Night's Main Event XLII](/wiki/Saturday_Night%27s_Main_Event_XLII), featuring [John Cena](/wiki/John_Cena)'s final match in his 26-year wrestling career.

## Criticism

### Gentrification

When the arena opened, there was concern that it would lead to the displacement of Chinese businesses and culture in the area that is the city's [Chinatown](/wiki/Chinatown,_Washington,_D.C.). The surrounding area has indeed been dramatically [gentrified](/wiki/Gentrification), and most of the Chinese residents and businesses who lived and operated in the neighborhood when the arena first opened have been displaced because of the spike in real estate prices. 2011 estimates hold that the number of Chinese in the neighborhood is down to around 400 to 500. The Chinese-owned restaurants and businesses in the Chinatown area are largely gone and there has not been a full-service Chinese grocery in the neighborhood since 2005.

A [similar stadium project](/wiki/76_Place_at_Market_East) proposed for [Philadelphia's Chinatown](/wiki/Chinatown,_Philadelphia) sparked comparisons in 2023 to the Capital One Arena, and caused significant community backlash prior to its cancellation.

### Ice quality issues

In December 2007, then-Capitals captain [Chris Clark](/wiki/Chris_Clark_(ice_hockey)) stated that he believed the arena had the worst ice in the NHL. "There's a lot of ruts in the ice. It's soft. It's wet half the time. I could see a lot of injuries coming from the ice there. It could cost [players] their jobs... Even guys on other teams say the same thing. When we're facing off, they say, 'How do you guys play on this?'" Capitals owner [Ted Leonsis](/wiki/Ted_Leonsis) addressed this criticism directly. The ice quality issue has been persistent both since the opening of the facility and with the Capitals franchise in general. During playoff games, the arena installs additional portable refrigeration units outside the arena to aid the ice conditions during the warm and humid summer months.

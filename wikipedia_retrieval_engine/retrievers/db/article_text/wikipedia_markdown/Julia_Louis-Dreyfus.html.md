This article is about the American actress. For the French actress, see [Julie Dreyfus](/wiki/Julie_Dreyfus).

**Julia Scarlett Elizabeth Louis-Dreyfus** ([/ˌluːi ˈdraɪfəs/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*LOO-ee DRY-fəs*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born January 13, 1961) is an American actress, comedian, and producer. She is known for her roles in a string of successful comedy series and several comedy films. She has received [numerous accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Julia_Louis-Dreyfus) including 11 [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award), and nine [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards).

The daughter of French billionaire [Gérard Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Louis-Dreyfus), she entered comedy as a performer with the [Practical Theatre Company](/wiki/The_Practical_Theatre_Company) and was a cast member on the [sketch comedy](/wiki/Sketch_comedy) series *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* from 1982 to 1985. Her breakthrough came playing [Elaine Benes](/wiki/Elaine_Benes) on the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) [sitcom](/wiki/Sitcom) *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* (1990–1998), one of the most successful sitcoms in television history. She earned further acclaim for her roles as [Christine Campbell](/wiki/Christine_Campbell_(character)) on *[The New Adventures of Old Christine](/wiki/The_New_Adventures_of_Old_Christine)* (2006–2010) and [Selina Meyer](/wiki/Selina_Meyer) on *[Veep](/wiki/Veep)* (2012–2019), and has guest starred on shows such as *[Arrested Development](/wiki/Arrested_Development)*, *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, and *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)*.

Louis-Dreyfus has had leading film roles in the comedy-dramas *[Enough Said](/wiki/Enough_Said)* (2013), *[Downhill](/wiki/Downhill_(2020_film))* (2020), *[You Hurt My Feelings](/wiki/You_Hurt_My_Feelings_(2023_film))* (2023), and *[Tuesday](/wiki/Tuesday_(2023_film))* (2023), with supporting film roles in comedy films such as *[Hannah and Her Sisters](/wiki/Hannah_and_Her_Sisters)* (1986), *[National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation](/wiki/National_Lampoon%27s_Christmas_Vacation)* (1989), *[Deconstructing Harry](/wiki/Deconstructing_Harry)* (1997), and *[You People](/wiki/You_People)* (2023). Her voice acting work includes roles in the [Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation) animated films *[A Bug's Life](/wiki/A_Bug%27s_Life)* (1998), *[Planes](/wiki/Planes_(film))* (2013), and *[Onward](/wiki/Onward_(film))* (2020). She has played [Valentina Allegra de Fontaine](/wiki/Valentina_Allegra_de_Fontaine_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe)) in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) since 2021.

Louis-Dreyfus is one of the most awarded actors in television history, winning more Emmy and Screen Actors Guild Awards than any other performer. She received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 2010 and was inducted into the [Television Academy Hall of Fame](/wiki/Television_Academy_Hall_of_Fame) in 2014. She was named as one of *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* magazine's [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100) in 2016. She also received the [Mark Twain Prize for American Humor](/wiki/Mark_Twain_Prize_for_American_Humor) in 2018 and the [National Medal of Arts](/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts) in 2021.

## Early life

Julia Scarlett Elizabeth Louis-Dreyfus was born in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) on January 13, 1961. Her mother, Judith ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) LeFever), is an American writer and [special needs educator](/wiki/Special_needs_education). Her father, [Gérard Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Louis-Dreyfus) (1932–2016), was a French billionaire who served as chairman of the [Louis Dreyfus Company](/wiki/Louis_Dreyfus_Company). Her paternal grandfather, [Pierre Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Pierre_Louis-Dreyfus) (1908–2011), was president of the [Louis Dreyfus Group](/wiki/Louis_Dreyfus_Group) commodities and shipping conglomerate. He came from a family of [Alsatian Jews](/wiki/Alsatian_Jews), and served as a [cavalry](/wiki/Cavalry) officer and member of the [French Resistance](/wiki/French_Resistance) during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). Louis-Dreyfus is the great-great-granddaughter of French businessman [Léopold Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_Louis-Dreyfus) (1833–1915), founder of the Louis Dreyfus Group, which members of her family still control. She is the fifth cousin four times removed of [Alfred Dreyfus](/wiki/Alfred_Dreyfus) (1859–1935) of the infamous [Dreyfus affair](/wiki/Dreyfus_affair). [Robert Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Robert_Louis-Dreyfus) (1946–2009), her father's second cousin, was the CEO of [Adidas](/wiki/Adidas) and owner of the soccer team [Olympique de Marseille](/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille). Julia's paternal grandmother was the American-born daughter of a Brazilian father and a Mexican mother.

In 1962, a year after her birth, Louis-Dreyfus's parents divorced. She has said that she first noticed her penchant for comedy after sticking raisins up her nose at the age of three, which first made her mother laugh but then led to an emergency hospital visit. After moving to [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), when Louis-Dreyfus was four, her mother married L. Thompson Bowles, dean of the [George Washington University Medical School](/wiki/George_Washington_University_Medical_School); Louis-Dreyfus gained a half-sister, [Lauren Bowles](/wiki/Lauren_Bowles), also an actress. Due to her stepfather's work with [Project HOPE](/wiki/Project_HOPE), she spent her childhood in several U.S. states and countries such as [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia), [Sri Lanka](/wiki/Sri_Lanka), and [Tunisia](/wiki/Tunisia). In 1979, she graduated from the all-girls [Holton-Arms School](/wiki/Holton-Arms_School) in [Bethesda, Maryland](/wiki/Bethesda,_Maryland). She later said of the school, "There were things I did in school that, had there been boys in the classroom, I would have been less motivated to do. For instance, I was president of the honor society."

Louis-Dreyfus matriculated at [Northwestern University](/wiki/Northwestern_University) in [Evanston, Illinois](/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois) in 1979, where she was a member of the [Delta Gamma](/wiki/Delta_Gamma) sorority. She studied theatre and performed in the [Mee-Ow Show](/wiki/Mee-Ow_Show), a student-run improv and sketch comedy revue, before dropping out during her junior year to take a job at *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*. In 2007, she received an honorary doctor of arts degree from Northwestern University.

## Career

### 1980s: Early career and *Saturday Night Live*

Louis-Dreyfus as a part of The Practical Theatre Company's "Golden 50th Anniversary Jubilee" in 1982, alongside castmates [Brad Hall](/wiki/Brad_Hall), [Gary Kroeger](/wiki/Gary_Kroeger) and Paul Barrosse

As part of her comedic training, Louis-Dreyfus appeared in [The Second City](/wiki/The_Second_City), one of the best-known improvisational theatre groups. It was her performance with [The Practical Theatre Company](/wiki/The_Practical_Theatre_Company) at their "Golden 50th Anniversary Jubilee" that led to her being asked to join the cast of [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* at the age of 21.

Louis-Dreyfus subsequently became a cast member on *Saturday Night Live* from 1982 to 1985, the youngest female cast member in the history of the program at that time. It was during her third and final year on *SNL* that she met writer [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David) during his only year on the show. David later co-created *Seinfeld*. Louis-Dreyfus has commented that her casting on *SNL* was a "[Cinderella](/wiki/Cinderella)-getting-to-go-to-the-ball kind of experience"; however, she has also admitted that at times it was often quite tense, stating that she "didn't know how to navigate the waters of show business in general and specifically doing a live sketch-comedy show".

**Recurring characters on *Saturday Night Live***

- April May June, a [televangelist](/wiki/Televangelist)
- Becky, El Dorko's ([Gary Kroeger](/wiki/Gary_Kroeger)) date
- Consuela, Chi Chi's friend and co-host of *Let's Watch TV*
- Darla in *SNL*'s parody of *[The Little Rascals](/wiki/The_Little_Rascals)*
- Weather Woman, a superhero who controls the weather
- Patti Lynn Hunnsucker, a teenage correspondent on [Weekend Update](/wiki/Weekend_Update)

Following her 1985 departure from *SNL*, Louis-Dreyfus appeared in several films, including *[Hannah and Her Sisters](/wiki/Hannah_and_Her_Sisters)* (1986) by [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen), *[Soul Man](/wiki/Soul_Man_(film))* (1986), and *[National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation](/wiki/National_Lampoon%27s_Christmas_Vacation)* (1989), in which she starred alongside fellow *SNL* alumnus [Chevy Chase](/wiki/Chevy_Chase). In 1987, Louis-Dreyfus appeared in the NBC sitcom pilot *[The Art of Being Nick](/wiki/The_Art_of_Being_Nick)*, an intended spin-off from *[Family Ties](/wiki/Family_Ties)* starring [Scott Valentine](/wiki/Scott_Valentine_(actor)). When the pilot did not make it to series, Louis-Dreyfus was retained by producer [Gary David Goldberg](/wiki/Gary_David_Goldberg) for a role on his new sitcom *[Day by Day](/wiki/Day_by_Day_(American_TV_series))*, as the sarcastic and materialistic neighbor, Eileen Swift. Premiering in early 1988, *Day by Day* aired for two seasons on NBC before being cancelled.

### 1990s: *Seinfeld* and widespread recognition

Louis-Dreyfus at the [47th Emmy Awards ceremony](/wiki/47th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) in September 1995

In the early 1990s, Louis-Dreyfus became famous for the role of [Elaine Benes](/wiki/Elaine_Benes) on NBC's *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)*. She played the role for nine seasons, appearing in all but three episodes. One of the episodes that she did not appear in was the pilot episode, "[The Seinfeld Chronicles](/wiki/The_Seinfeld_Chronicles)", because her character was not initially intended to be a part of the series. It was only after the first episode that [NBC](/wiki/NBC) executives felt the show was too male-centric and demanded that creators [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David) and [Jerry Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld) add a woman to the cast. It was revealed in the commentary on the DVD package that the addition of a female character was the condition for commissioning the show. Louis-Dreyfus won the role over several other actresses who also eventually enjoyed TV success, including [Patricia Heaton](/wiki/Patricia_Heaton) and [Megan Mullally](/wiki/Megan_Mullally). On the "Notes About Nothing" featurette on the [DVD](/wiki/DVD) package, Seinfeld says that Louis-Dreyfus's ability to eat a peanut [M&M](/wiki/M%26M%27s) without breaking the peanut described her: "She cracks you up without breaking your nuts."

Louis-Dreyfus garnered critical acclaim for her performance on the series, and she was a regular winner and nominee at television award shows throughout the 1990s. Her performance earned her two [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nominations, winning once in 1994, nine [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) nominations, winning one in 1995 and two in both 1997 and 1998, and seven [American Comedy Awards](/wiki/American_Comedy_Awards), winning five times in 1993, 1994, 1995, 1997 and 1998. In 1996, she won the [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series, an award she was nominated for on seven occasions from 1992 to 1998. After receiving the award, Louis-Dreyfus said the win was a "shocker", and that after being in both positions, it was "much better to win than to lose."

In 1998, Jerry Seinfeld decided to end the series after nine seasons. The [series finale](/wiki/The_Finale_(Seinfeld)) aired on May 14 and was one of the most-watched TV events in history, with over 76 million viewers tuning in. During her time on *Seinfeld,* she appeared in several films, including the comedy films *[Fathers' Day](/wiki/Fathers%27_Day_(1997_film))* (1997), opposite [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams) and [Billy Crystal](/wiki/Billy_Crystal), and [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen)'s *[Deconstructing Harry](/wiki/Deconstructing_Harry)* (1997). Following a voice role in the highly successful [Pixar](/wiki/Pixar) film *[A Bug's Life](/wiki/A_Bug%27s_Life)* (1998), Louis-Dreyfus lent her voice as [Snake](/wiki/Snake_(The_Simpsons))'s girlfriend Gloria in *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)* episode "[A Hunka Hunka Burns in Love](/wiki/A_Hunka_Hunka_Burns_in_Love)".

### 2000s: Post-*Seinfeld* and *The New Adventures of Old Christine*

In 2001, she made several special guest appearances on *Seinfeld* co-creator [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David)'s show *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, playing herself fictionally trying to break the "curse" by planning to star in a show in which she would play an actress affected by a *Seinfeld*-like curse.

After several years away from a regular TV job, Louis-Dreyfus began a new single-camera sitcom, *[Watching Ellie](/wiki/Watching_Ellie)*, which premiered on NBC in February 2002. The series was created by husband [Brad Hall](/wiki/Brad_Hall) and co-starred [Steve Carell](/wiki/Steve_Carell) and Louis-Dreyfus's half-sister [Lauren Bowles](/wiki/Lauren_Bowles). The initial premise of the show was to present viewers with a "slice of life" from the goings-on and happenings of the life of Ellie Riggs, a Southern California jazz singer. The first season included a 22-minute countdown kept digitally in the lower left-hand corner of the screen, which many critics panned, claiming it was useless and "did nothing for the show." Overall, the show received mixed reviews but debuted strongly with over 16 million viewers tuning in for the series premiere, and maintained an average audience of about 10 million viewers per week.

When the series returned for a second season in the spring of 2003, it suffered a decline in viewership, averaging around eight million viewers per week. The show had undergone a drastic stylistic change between the production of seasons one and two. The first season was filmed in the single-camera format, but the second season was presented as a traditional multicamera sitcom filmed in front of a live studio audience. With dwindling viewership and failing to retain the numbers from its *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)* lead-in, the series was cancelled by NBC in May 2003.

Following NBC's cancellation of *Watching Ellie*, the media began circulating rumors of a so-called "[*Seinfeld* curse](/wiki/Seinfeld_curse)", which claimed that none of the former *Seinfeld* actors could ever achieve success again in the television industry. Louis-Dreyfus dismissed the rumor as "a made-up thing by the media", while *Seinfeld* co-creator Larry David asserted that the curse was "completely idiotic." Louis-Dreyfus was interested in the role of [Susan Mayer](/wiki/Susan_Mayer) on *[Desperate Housewives](/wiki/Desperate_Housewives)*, the role that ultimately went to [Teri Hatcher](/wiki/Teri_Hatcher). Instead, Louis-Dreyfus scored a recurring guest role as Maggie Lizer, the deceitful prosecutor and love interest of [Michael Bluth](/wiki/Michael_Bluth) on the Emmy-winning comedy *[Arrested Development](/wiki/Arrested_Development)*, from 2004 to 2005.

Louis-Dreyfus representing her role from *[The New Adventures of Old Christine](/wiki/The_New_Adventures_of_Old_Christine)* at the [Museum of Television & Radio](/wiki/Museum_of_Television_%26_Radio) in April 2007

In 2005, Louis-Dreyfus was cast in the title role of a new CBS sitcom, *[The New Adventures of Old Christine](/wiki/The_New_Adventures_of_Old_Christine)*. The series and its concept were created by writer and producer of *[Will & Grace](/wiki/Will_%26_Grace)*, Kari Lizer. The series told the story of Christine Campbell, a single mother who manages to maintain a fantastic relationship with her ex-husband while running a women's gym. The series debuted on CBS in March 2006 to an audience of 15 million and was initially a ratings winner for the network.

Louis-Dreyfus received considerable critical acclaim for her performance on the show, with Brian Lowry of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* stating that Louis-Dreyfus broke the so-called "*Seinfeld* curse [...] with one of the best conventional half-hours to come along in a while." Alessandra Stanley from *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* asserted that Louis-Dreyfus's performance on the series proved she is "one of the funniest women on network television." Louis-Dreyfus also earned the 2006 [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) for her performance in the first season. Referring to the [curse](/wiki/The_Seinfeld_Curse), she stated in her acceptance speech, "I'm not somebody who really believes in curses, but curse this, baby!" Throughout the course of the series, she received five consecutive [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Award) nominations, three consecutive [Satellite Award](/wiki/Satellite_Award) nominations, two [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) nominations, and a nomination for a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award). In 2007, she also received two nominations for a [People's Choice Award](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Award) due to her return to popularity, thanks to the success of *Old Christine*.

In May 2006, Louis-Dreyfus hosted an episode of *Saturday Night Live*, becoming the first female former cast member to return to the show as a host. In the episode, she appeared with her *Seinfeld* co-stars [Jason Alexander](/wiki/Jason_Alexander) and [Jerry Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld) in her opening monologue, parodying the so-called "*Seinfeld* curse". After a successful reception of her 2006 episode, Louis-Dreyfus again hosted *SNL* on March 17, 2007, and April 17, 2016. Louis-Dreyfus reprised her role as Gloria in two *Simpsons* episodes: 2007's "[I Don't Wanna Know Why the Caged Bird Sings](/wiki/I_Don%27t_Wanna_Know_Why_the_Caged_Bird_Sings)" and 2008's "[Sex, Pies and Idiot Scrapes](/wiki/Sex,_Pies_and_Idiot_Scrapes)". In the fall of 2009, she appeared with the rest of the cast of *Seinfeld* in four episodes of the seventh season of [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David)'s sitcom *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*. The reunion shows received much media attention, and the episode received strong ratings for the series.

In 2009, Louis-Dreyfus was granted the honorary award for Legacy of Laughter at the [TV Land Awards](/wiki/TV_Land_Awards). Previous winners had included [Lucille Ball](/wiki/Lucille_Ball) and [Mike Myers](/wiki/Mike_Myers). She was presented with the award by friend [Amy Poehler](/wiki/Amy_Poehler). The following year, Louis-Dreyfus received the 2,407th star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) on May 4, 2010, for her remarkable contribution to the broadcast television industry as both an actress and a comedian. Originally, the star was set with Louis-Dreyfus's name spelled incorrectly. It was missing both the 'o' and the hyphen in her last name. The star was corrected and the misspelled portion was removed and presented to her. Celebrity guests at the event included past and current colleagues from throughout her career, including [Clark Gregg](/wiki/Clark_Gregg), Larry David, [Eric McCormack](/wiki/Eric_McCormack), and Jason Alexander.

### 2010s: *Veep* and acclaim

Louis-Dreyfus at the unveiling of her star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in May 2010

*Old Christine* was cancelled by CBS on May 18, 2010, after 5 years. Discussions were held with ABC to revive the show but the show was never revived. In the spring of 2010, Louis-Dreyfus guest-starred several times in the third season of the web series *[Web Therapy](/wiki/Web_Therapy)*, starring [Lisa Kudrow](/wiki/Lisa_Kudrow). Louis-Dreyfus played the sister of the main character, Fiona Wallice, who gives her therapy online. When the series made the transition to cable television on the [Showtime](/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network)) network, Louis-Dreyfus's appearance from the web series was included in the second season, airing in July 2012. In fall 2010, Louis-Dreyfus made a guest appearance on the live episode of *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)*, playing [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey)'s role of [Liz Lemon](/wiki/Liz_Lemon) in the cutaway shots. Louis-Dreyfus was among several *Saturday Night Live* alumni appearing in the episode, including [Rachel Dratch](/wiki/Rachel_Dratch), [Bill Hader](/wiki/Bill_Hader), and regulars [Tracy Morgan](/wiki/Tracy_Morgan) and Fey herself. Louis-Dreyfus also starred in a "Women of SNL" special on November 1, 2010, on NBC.

In May and June 2011, Louis-Dreyfus teamed up with husband Brad Hall for her first short film, *[Picture Paris](/wiki/Picture_Paris)*. This was the first time the couple had collaborated since their early-2000s NBC comedy *Watching Ellie*. Hall wrote and directed the film, while Louis-Dreyfus played the lead role of an ordinary woman with an extraordinary obsession with the city of Paris. The film premiered on January 29, 2012, at the Santa Barbara International Film Festival, and has received considerable critical acclaim. It made its television premiere on HBO on December 17, 2012.

Louis-Dreyfus with then Vice President [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) in April 2013

In early 2011, HBO confirmed that Louis-Dreyfus had been cast in the lead role of U.S. Vice President Selina Meyer in a new satirical comedy series titled *[Veep](/wiki/Veep)*. The series was commissioned for a first season of eight episodes. In addition to her starring role, Louis-Dreyfus would also be a producer. In preparation for her role, Louis-Dreyfus spoke with [Al Gore](/wiki/Al_Gore) and another former vice president, senators, speechwriters, chiefs of staffs of various offices, and schedulers. Louis-Dreyfus commended HBO for allowing the cast and crew to engage in a "protracted pre-production process", which included a six-week rehearsal period before filming began.

The first season was filmed in the fall of 2011, in [Baltimore](/wiki/Baltimore), and the series premiered on April 22, 2012. The premiere episode was met with high praise from critics, particularly for Louis-Dreyfus's performance. *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* asserted the character of Selina Meyer was her "best post-Seinfeld role" to date and claimed she gives "an Emmy-worthy effort", while the *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* contended the series demonstrates she is "one of the medium's great comediennes." Following the success of the first season, Louis-Dreyfus was named by the *[Huffington Post](/wiki/Huffington_Post)* as one of the funniest people of 2012, asserting that she is the "most magnetic and naturally funny woman on TV since Mary Tyler Moore."

Louis-Dreyfus after receiving her third [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) for *[Veep](/wiki/Veep)* in August 2014

For her performance on *Veep*, Louis-Dreyfus received several accolades, most notably seven nominations for the [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) for [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) between 2012 and 2019, winning the award six times. These Emmy wins for *Veep*, following previous wins for *Seinfeld* and *The New Adventures of Old Christine*, resulted in her becoming the only woman to win an acting award for three separate comedy series. Her sixth win in 2016 surpassed the record previously held by [Mary Tyler Moore](/wiki/Mary_Tyler_Moore) and [Candice Bergen](/wiki/Candice_Bergen) for the most wins in that category. In 2017, her sixth consecutive win, and eighth acting win, overall made her the performer with the most Emmys for the same role in the same series, surpassing [Candice Bergen](/wiki/Candice_Bergen) and [Don Knotts](/wiki/Don_Knotts), and put her in a tie with [Cloris Leachman](/wiki/Cloris_Leachman) for the most Emmys ever won by a performer. She was also nominated as one of the producers for *Veep* in the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Comedy_Series) category for all seven seasons, winning the award in 2015, 2016 and 2017 for the fourth, fifth and sixth seasons respectively. Louis-Dreyfus also received five [Critics' Choice Television Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) nominations, winning twice in 2013 and 2014, ten [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) nominations, winning twice in 2014 and 2017, and five [Television Critics Association Award](/wiki/TCA_Award_for_Individual_Achievement_in_Comedy) nominations, winning once in 2014. Her performance additionally garnered her five [Satellite Award](/wiki/Satellite_Award) nominations and five consecutive [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nominations.

Louis-Dreyfus with her colleague [Timothy Simons](/wiki/Timothy_Simons) accepting the [Peabody Award](/wiki/Peabody_Award) for *[Veep](/wiki/Veep)* from [Al Franken](/wiki/Al_Franken) in May 2017

Louis-Dreyfus lent her voice to the 2013 animated film *[Planes](/wiki/Planes_(film))*, in the role of Rochelle. To date, the film has grossed well over $200 million at the box office worldwide. She also starred in the film *[Enough Said](/wiki/Enough_Said)*, directed by [Nicole Holofcener](/wiki/Nicole_Holofcener), which was released on September 18, 2013. This marked her debut as a lead actress in a full-length feature film. The film garnered rave reviews from film critics, ranking among the best-reviewed films of 2013. The website [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) gives the film a score of 96% based on 152 reviews, many of them praising Louis-Dreyfus's performance. She received several Best Actress nominations including for the [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and the [Critics' Choice Movie Awards](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Awards). Another review aggregation website, Metacritic, gave the film a score of 78 out of 100, based on 44 critics, signifying "generally favorable reviews".

Since December 2014, Louis-Dreyfus has appeared in a series of television commercials for [Old Navy](/wiki/Old_Navy). In 2015 she acted in the [Comedy Central](/wiki/Comedy_Central) sketch series *[Inside Amy Schumer](/wiki/Inside_Amy_Schumer)* alongside [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey) and [Patricia Arquette](/wiki/Patricia_Arquette), playing a version of themselves giving advice on aging to [Amy Schumer](/wiki/Amy_Schumer). Dreyfus said of the experience "I started to feel unbelievably paranoid that I was making fun of myself and wondering, was this really happening to me? Like, how meta is this moment in my life? I started to have a kind of soul-searching crisis in the middle of the day. And I didn't know [the other women] well enough to bring it up, so I was just trying to be a good sport even though I was dying a little bit on the inside." On April 16, 2016, she returned to *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* serving as host for the third time with musical guest [Nick Jonas](/wiki/Nick_Jonas). During the episode's cold open, she reprised her role of Elaine Benes from *Seinfeld*.

### 2020s: Professional expansion

Louis-Dreyfus with [Gladys Knight](/wiki/Gladys_Knight) and [Mindy Kaling](/wiki/Mindy_Kaling) in the [White House](/wiki/White_House) in March 2023.

In 2020, Louis-Dreyfus headlined the comedy-drama *[Downhill](/wiki/Downhill_(2020_film))*, opposite [Will Ferrell](/wiki/Will_Ferrell). The film premiered at the [2020 Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/2020_Sundance_Film_Festival) and was theatrically released on February 14. Next, she voiced a suburban elf mother, Laurel Lightfoot, in [Pixar](/wiki/Pixar)'s *[Onward](/wiki/Onward_(film))* opposite [Tom Holland](/wiki/Tom_Holland) and [Chris Pratt](/wiki/Chris_Pratt). The film was released on March 6, 2020. In January 2020, Louis-Dreyfus signed a multi-year deal with [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B). Under the deal, she will develop new projects for Apple TV+ as both an executive producer and star. The following year Louis-Dreyfus appeared in the [Disney+](/wiki/Disney%2B) series *[The Falcon and the Winter Soldier](/wiki/The_Falcon_and_the_Winter_Soldier)* (2021) as [Valentina Allegra de Fontaine](/wiki/Valentina_Allegra_de_Fontaine_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe)), which is set in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, though she was originally intended to debut in the film *[Black Widow](/wiki/Black_Widow_(2021_film))* (where she appears in the post credit scene). She reprised the role in *[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever](/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever)* (2022), and also appears in the film *[Thunderbolts\*](/wiki/Thunderbolts*)* (2025). In 2022 she was a guest on the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) show *[My Next Guest Needs No Introduction with David Letterman](/wiki/My_Next_Guest_Needs_No_Introduction_with_David_Letterman)*.

In 2023 she reunited with [Nicole Holofcener](/wiki/Nicole_Holofcener) starring in the [A24](/wiki/A24) independent comedy film *[You Hurt My Feelings](/wiki/You_Hurt_My_Feelings_(2023_film))*. Dreyfus produced the film and acted alongside [Tobias Menzies](/wiki/Tobias_Menzies), [Michaela Watkins](/wiki/Michaela_Watkins), [Arian Moayed](/wiki/Arian_Moayed) and [Jeannie Berlin](/wiki/Jeannie_Berlin). The film premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival) to positive reviews. [Peter Bradshaw](/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) praised her performance writing, "Louis-Dreyfus is such a superb comic performer that it is interesting seeing her take on something low-key". That same year she portrayed a liberal Jewish mother in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) romantic comedy *[You People](/wiki/You_People)* (2023). She also starred in the A24 film *[Tuesday](/wiki/Tuesday_(2023_film))*, directed by Daina Oniunas-Pusić, which premiered at the 2023 [Telluride Film Festival](/wiki/Telluride_Film_Festival).

In 2023, Louis-Dreyfus became host of the podcast *[Wiser Than Me](/wiki/Wiser_Than_Me)*. On the show, she interviews women older than her on their lived experience and earned wisdom. Guests have included [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda), [Carol Burnett](/wiki/Carol_Burnett), [Isabel Allende](/wiki/Isabel_Allende) and [Amy Tan](/wiki/Amy_Tan). The show, produced by [Lemonada Media](/wiki/Lemonada_Media), was named Apple's Best Podcast of the Year in 2023. Among her season two guests, Louis-Dreyfus interviews [Billie Jean King](/wiki/Billie_Jean_King), [Patti Smith](/wiki/Patti_Smith), and [Julie Andrews](/wiki/Julie_Andrews).

In 2024, Apple announced that [Wiser Than Me](/wiki/Wiser_Than_Me) was the 3rd most shared show in the [United States](/wiki/United_States) on [Apple Podcasts](/wiki/Apple_Podcasts) in 2024.

## Artistry and reception

Louis-Dreyfus said she respects "women who are not afraid of making themselves look bad or foolish to get a laugh" and cites her acting idols as [Lucille Ball](/wiki/Lucille_Ball), [Mary Tyler Moore](/wiki/Mary_Tyler_Moore), [Madeline Kahn](/wiki/Madeline_Kahn), [Teri Garr](/wiki/Teri_Garr), [Valerie Harper](/wiki/Valerie_Harper), and [Cloris Leachman](/wiki/Cloris_Leachman). The actress [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey) said that Louis-Dreyfus inspired her character [Liz Lemon](/wiki/Liz_Lemon) on the NBC comedy series *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)*.

Louis-Dreyfus is widely regarded as one of the finest comedic actresses of her generation. Jake Coyle of the *[Lubbock Avalanche-Journal](/wiki/Lubbock_Avalanche-Journal)* said "Few comediennes have both her gift for physical comedy... and vocal precision". According to the journalist [Molly Ball](/wiki/Molly_Ball), Louis-Dreyfus has played mostly "funny, self-centered women who are compelling despite often being ill-behaved." Louis-Dreyfus said she had turned playing unlikeable people into a career. Ball said: "She has also left an indelible cultural mark, expanding the possibilities for women in comedy–and maybe in politics and public life as well."

## Personal life

Louis-Dreyfus was raised [Catholic](/wiki/Catholic) but moved towards [agnosticism](/wiki/Agnosticism); she said she had no "traditional religious affiliation".

### Marriage and family

Louis-Dreyfus and her husband [Brad Hall](/wiki/Brad_Hall) at the 2012 [Tribeca Film Festival](/wiki/Tribeca_Film_Festival)

Louis-Dreyfus's maternal half-sister, [Lauren Bowles](/wiki/Lauren_Bowles), is also an actress. She also had two paternal half-sisters, Phoebe and Emma. Emma died in August 2018.

While at Northwestern University, Louis-Dreyfus met her future husband, the *Saturday Night Live* comedian [Brad Hall](/wiki/Brad_Hall). They married in 1987 and have two sons. Their older son, Henry Hall, is a singer-songwriter who has performed on *The Tonight Show*. Their younger son, [Charlie Hall](/wiki/Charlie_Hall_(actor,_born_1997)), is an actor. Her first pregnancy ended in a miscarriage.

### Health

On September 28, 2017, Louis-Dreyfus announced that she had been diagnosed with [breast cancer](/wiki/Breast_cancer), which she discovered the day after winning a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) for her role in *Veep*. She said: "One in eight women get breast cancer. Today, I'm the one. The good news is that I have the most glorious group of supportive and caring family and friends, and fantastic insurance through my union. The bad news is that not all women are so lucky, so let's fight all cancers and make [universal healthcare](/wiki/Universal_healthcare) a reality." On October 18, 2018, she announced on an episode of *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)* that she was cancer-free.

### Activism and politics

Louis-Dreyfus supported [Al Gore](/wiki/Al_Gore)'s 2000 U.S. presidential bid and [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s bid for the presidency in both 2008 and 2012. She appeared in a video that urged Obama to reject the proposal of the [Keystone XL pipeline](/wiki/Keystone_XL_pipeline), arguing that if the pipeline ever were to leak, it would cause massive pollution across the U.S. She has voiced her concern for several environmental issues and has raised millions for [Heal the Bay](/wiki/Heal_the_Bay), the [Natural Resources Defense Council](/wiki/Natural_Resources_Defense_Council), and the [Trust for Public Land](/wiki/Trust_for_Public_Land). She also worked for the successful passage of Proposition O, which allocated $500 million for cleaning up the Los Angeles [water supply](/wiki/Water_supply).

In October 2010, before the [U.S. Senate election in California](/wiki/United_States_Senate_election_in_California,_2010), Louis-Dreyfus starred in a humorous [Barbara Boxer](/wiki/Barbara_Boxer) ad regarding [energy policy](/wiki/Energy_policy_of_the_United_States). During the [2016 Democratic National Convention](/wiki/2016_Democratic_National_Convention), she supported [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in that year's [presidential election](/wiki/U.S._presidential_election,_2016). In her acceptance speech at the 2017 [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards), she denounced President [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump)'s [executive order, referred to as the "Muslim ban"](/wiki/Executive_Order_13769), as "un-American" and said, "My father fled religious persecution in [Nazi-occupied France](/wiki/Nazi-occupied_France)."

Louis-Dreyfus [emceed](/wiki/Emcee) the final night of the [2020 Democratic National Convention](/wiki/2020_Democratic_National_Convention), endorsing [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden). She has also published information regarding voting by mail and urged all Americans to vote. Louis-Dreyfus endorsed Representative [Karen Bass](/wiki/Karen_Bass) in the [2022 Los Angeles mayoral election](/wiki/2022_Los_Angeles_mayoral_election), in various social media posts. In 2024, she supported Vice President Kamala Harris for president in the general election.

## Awards and nominations

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Julia_Louis-Dreyfus)

Julia Louis-Dreyfus has won the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) seven times: once for her role on *[The New Adventures of Old Christine](/wiki/The_New_Adventures_of_Old_Christine)* (2006) and six consecutive awards for playing [Selina Meyer](/wiki/Selina_Meyer) on *[Veep](/wiki/Veep)* (2012–17), as well as [Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) once for *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* (1996).

As of 2017, she holds the record for the most Primetime Emmy awards as an actor for the same role and is tied with fellow Northwestern University alum [Cloris Leachman](/wiki/Cloris_Leachman) for the most acting Primetime Emmy awards (with eight). She has also been nominated for nine [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), winning one for Best Supporting Actress in a Series, Miniseries, or Television Film for her role as [Elaine Benes](/wiki/Elaine_Benes) on Seinfeld (1995).

She has also been nominated for twenty-one [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards) and has won five for individual performance (nine altogether) for her work on *Seinfeld* (1997–98) and *Veep* (2014, 2017–18). In 2016, she won the Crossover Talent award at the 4th Annual American Reality Television Awards.

In 2018, she was the twentieth recipient of the [Mark Twain Prize for American Humor](/wiki/Mark_Twain_Prize_for_American_Humor).

In 2023, the podcast she hosts, *Wiser Than Me*, won Apple's Best Podcast of the Year.

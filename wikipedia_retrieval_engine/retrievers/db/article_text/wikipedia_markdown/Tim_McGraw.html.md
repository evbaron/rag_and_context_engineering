**Samuel Timothy McGraw** (born May 1, 1967) is an American [country](/wiki/Country_music) singer and actor. He has released 17 studio albums (11 for [Curb Records](/wiki/Curb_Records), five for [Big Machine Records](/wiki/Big_Machine_Records) and one for [Arista Nashville](/wiki/Arista_Nashville)). 10 of those albums have reached number one on the [Top Country Albums](/wiki/Top_Country_Albums) charts, with his 1994 breakthrough album *[Not a Moment Too Soon](/wiki/Not_a_Moment_Too_Soon)* being the top country album of 1994. In total, McGraw's albums have produced 65 singles, 25 of which have reached number one on the [Hot Country Songs](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) or [Country Airplay](/wiki/Country_Airplay) charts.

Three of these singles – "[It's Your Love](/wiki/It%27s_Your_Love)", "[Just to See You Smile](/wiki/Just_to_See_You_Smile)", and "[Live Like You Were Dying](/wiki/Live_Like_You_Were_Dying_(song))" – were respectively the top country songs of 1997, 1998, and 2004 according to [*Billboard* Year-End](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End). He has also won three [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards), 14 [Academy of Country Music](/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music) awards, 11 [Country Music Association](/wiki/Country_Music_Association) (CMA) awards, 10 [American Music Awards](/wiki/American_Music_Awards), and three [People's Choice Awards](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards). His [Soul2Soul II Tour](/wiki/Soul2Soul_II_Tour), which was done in partnership with his wife, [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill), is one of the highest-grossing tours in country music history, and one of the top five among all genres of music. He has sold more than 80 million records worldwide, making him one of the [best-selling music artists of all time](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists).

McGraw has ventured into acting, with supporting roles in *[The Blind Side](/wiki/The_Blind_Side_(film))*, *[Friday Night Lights](/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_(film))*, *[The Kingdom](/wiki/The_Kingdom_(2007_film))*, *[Tomorrowland](/wiki/Tomorrowland_(film))*, *[Four Christmases](/wiki/Four_Christmases)*, flashback scenes in 2 episodes of *[Yellowstone](/wiki/Yellowstone_(American_TV_series))*, and *[The Shack](/wiki/The_Shack_(2017_film))* as well as lead roles in *[Flicka](/wiki/Flicka)* (2006), *[Country Strong](/wiki/Country_Strong)* (2010), and *[1883](/wiki/1883_(TV_series))* (2021). He was a minority owner of the [Arena Football League](/wiki/Arena_Football_League_(1987%E2%80%932008))'s [Nashville Kats](/wiki/Nashville_Kats). McGraw has been married to singer Faith Hill since 1996 and is the eldest son of former [Major League Baseball](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball) (MLB) pitcher [Tug McGraw](/wiki/Tug_McGraw).

McGraw was [inducted](/wiki/List_of_Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame_inductees) into the [Country Music Hall of Fame](/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum) in 2026.

## Early life and education

Samuel Timothy McGraw was born in [Delhi, Louisiana](/wiki/Delhi,_Louisiana), the only child of Elizabeth "Betty" Ann D'Agostino, a waitress from [Jacksonville, Florida](/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida), and [Frank Edwin "Tug" McGraw Jr.](/wiki/Tug_McGraw), a [pitcher](/wiki/Pitcher) for the minor league [Jacksonville Suns](/wiki/Jacksonville_Suns) and future star pitcher for the [New York Mets](/wiki/New_York_Mets) and the [Philadelphia Phillies](/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies). In 1966, D'Agostino was a student at [Terry Parker High School](/wiki/Terry_Parker_High_School). She lived in the same apartment building as Tug McGraw, who was playing baseball for Jacksonville. When she became pregnant with McGraw as a teen, D'Agostino's parents sent her to Louisiana to live with relatives. Through his father, McGraw has two half-brothers, Mark and Matthew, and a half-sister named Cari. He also has two younger half-sisters, Tracey and Sandra, through his mother's marriage to Horace Smith.

[Start, Louisiana](/wiki/Start,_Louisiana), welcome sign notes that McGraw once resided there.

McGraw grew up believing Smith was his father, and used this stepfather's surname until meeting Tug. At age 11, McGraw discovered his birth certificate while searching in his mother's closet whilst looking for money to buy sweets. Following the discovery, he learned from her who his biological father was and she took him to meet the elder McGraw for the first time. Tug McGraw denied the parentage for seven years until Tim was 18 years old. After that time, the two formed a relationship and remained close until the former baseball star died in 2004.

As a child, McGraw played competitive sports, including baseball, even before the knowledge of who his father was and his professional baseball career. McGraw was also a member of the [FFA](/wiki/National_FFA_Organization) in high school. Following high school graduation, he attended [Northeast Louisiana University](/wiki/University_of_Louisiana_at_Monroe) on a baseball scholarship, where he was a [pre-law](/wiki/Pre-law) major and pledged as a member of [Pi Kappa Alpha](/wiki/Pi_Kappa_Alpha) fraternity. A knee injury sustained while playing baseball for the college prevented him from pursuing a professional career in sports.

While in college, McGraw learned to play guitar and would frequently perform and sing for money. He has claimed his roommates often hid the guitar because he was so bad. McGraw followed his mother when she returned to [Jacksonville, Florida](/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida), in 1987. After the move, he attended [Florida Community College at Jacksonville](/wiki/Florida_Community_College_at_Jacksonville) for one term, and occasionally sat in with local bands. In 1989, on the day his hero [Keith Whitley](/wiki/Keith_Whitley) died, McGraw dropped out of college to head to [Nashville](/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee) and pursue a musical career.

## Music career

### 1990s

#### *Tim McGraw*

Tim McGraw's star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame)

McGraw came to the attention of [Curb Records](/wiki/Curb_Records) in 1990. After cutting a demo single, McGraw gave a copy to his father. A man who was friends with Curb Records executives heard the demo while driving with Tug one day and recommended that Curb contact the young singer. Several weeks later, he was able to play his tape for Curb executives, after which they signed him to a recording contract. McGraw made his debut with the single "What Room Was the Holiday In", which was released on March 29, 1991, and did not enter the *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* [Hot Country Songs](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) chart upon its release. In a 2001 retrospective on McGraw's career in *Billboard*, a former program director for Nashville station [WSM-FM](/wiki/WSM-FM) said that he added the song to the station's playlist because it showed "undeniable promise", while another former program director at [WXTU](/wiki/WXTU) in [Philadelphia, Pennsylvania](/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania), recalled that McGraw's debut single was "terrible" but that he booked the singer to make an appearance at the station due to his father's fame.

Two years later in April 1993, McGraw released his debut album *[Tim McGraw](/wiki/Tim_McGraw_(album))*, which was commercially unsuccessful and did not sell well. This is his only studio album not to achieve a [music recording sales certification](/wiki/Music_recording_sales_certification) or to enter the [Top Country Albums](/wiki/Top_Country_Albums) charts. Three more singles were released from *Tim McGraw*: "[Welcome to the Club](/wiki/Welcome_to_the_Club_(Tim_McGraw_song))", "[Memory Lane](/wiki/Memory_Lane_(Tim_McGraw_song))", and "[Two Steppin' Mind](/wiki/Two_Steppin%27_Mind)". None made country Top 40 and the album itself did not chart. Both "Memory Lane" and "Tears in the Rain", another cut from the album, were co-written by [Joe Diffie](/wiki/Joe_Diffie). "Memory Lane" had originally appeared on [Keith Palmer](/wiki/Keith_Palmer_(singer))'s self-titled 1991 debut album.
McGraw later appeared in his friend and future collaborator [Tracy Lawrence](/wiki/Tracy_Lawrence)'s music video for "[My Second Home](/wiki/My_Second_Home)" in 1993.

#### *Not a Moment Too Soon*

McGraw's second album, entitled *[Not a Moment Too Soon](/wiki/Not_a_Moment_Too_Soon)*, was much more successful than his self-titled debut, and it was the best-selling country album of 1994. Its first single, "[Indian Outlaw](/wiki/Indian_Outlaw)", sparked controversy, as critics argued that it presented [Native Americans](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) in a patronizing way. Some radio stations even chose not to play it. However, the controversy helped spur sales, and the song became McGraw's first Top 10 entry on the U.S. country charts after getting as high as number 8. The song also peaked at number 15 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100).

The album's second single, "[Don't Take the Girl](/wiki/Don%27t_Take_the_Girl)", became McGraw's first number one on the U.S. country charts, in addition to peaking at number 17 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. The song also "helped cement his image as a ruggedly good-looking guy with a sensitive side." By year's end, the third single from the album, "[Down on the Farm](/wiki/Down_on_the_Farm_(Tim_McGraw_song))" peaked at number 2; after that, the album's fourth single, also its [title track](/wiki/Not_a_Moment_Too_Soon_(song)), became the singer's second number one song in early 1995. The fifth and final single "[Refried Dreams](/wiki/Refried_Dreams)" reached number 5. The album sold over 6 million copies, topping the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) and Top Country Album charts. On the strength of this success, McGraw won [Academy of Country Music](/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music) awards for Album of the Year and Top New Male Vocalist in 1994. *Billboard* named *Not a Moment Too Soon* as the top country album of 1994 on [Billboard Year-End](/wiki/Billboard_Year-End).

#### *All I Want*

McGraw's third studio album, *[All I Want](/wiki/All_I_Want_(Tim_McGraw_album))*, was released in 1995. Just like its predecessor, this album debuted at No. 1 on the country charts. The album even sold over 2 million copies in the United States and reached the Top 5 on the *Billboard* 200. The album's first single, "[I Like It, I Love It](/wiki/I_Like_It,_I_Love_It)", became McGraw's third number one on the American country charts and it also peaked at number 25 on the *Billboard* Hot 100. This song also eventually became the goal song for the [Nashville Predators](/wiki/Nashville_Predators) once they began their [inaugural season](/wiki/1998-99_NHL_season) and since then has been left unchanged. The album's next two singles, "[Can't Be Really Gone](/wiki/Can%27t_Be_Really_Gone)" and "[All I Want Is a Life](/wiki/All_I_Want_Is_a_Life)" (its partial title track) both made the top 5 at numbers 2 and 5, respectively. The fourth single, "[She Never Lets It Go to Her Heart](/wiki/She_Never_Lets_It_Go_to_Her_Heart)", gave McGraw his fourth number one on the U.S. country charts in 1996. Finishing off the singles was "[Maybe We Should Just Sleep on It](/wiki/Maybe_We_Should_Just_Sleep_on_It)", which peaked at number 4.

In 1996, McGraw headlined the most successful country tour of the year, The Spontaneous Combustion Tour, with [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill) as his supporting act. Hill broke off her engagement to her former producer [Scott Hendricks](/wiki/Scott_Hendricks) so that she and McGraw could start dating each other; they then married on October 6, 1996.

#### *Everywhere*

The singer's fourth album, *[Everywhere](/wiki/Everywhere_(Tim_McGraw_album))* was released in 1997. It topped the country charts as well and reached No. 2 on the *Billboard* 200, selling 4 million copies. The album spawned six singles. Four of those singles - "[It's Your Love](/wiki/It%27s_Your_Love)" (a duet with [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill)), the [title track](/wiki/Everywhere_(Tim_McGraw_song)), "[Where the Green Grass Grows](/wiki/Where_the_Green_Grass_Grows)", and "[Just to See You Smile](/wiki/Just_to_See_You_Smile)" - reached number one on the country charts. The [Country Music Association](/wiki/Country_Music_Association) awarded *Everywhere* its Album of the Year award for 1997. At the 40th Grammy Awards, "It's Your Love" received two [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) nominations for Best Country Collaboration With Vocals and Best Country Song. Both "It's Your Love" and "Just to See You Smile" were the number one country songs of 1997 and 1998 according to *Billboard* Year-End charts; "Just to See You Smile" also set a record for the longest run on the country charts at the time, at 42 weeks. The album's other two singles, "[One of These Days](/wiki/One_of_These_Days_(Marcus_Hummon_song))" and "[For a Little While](/wiki/For_a_Little_While)" both peaked at number 2.

#### *A Place in the Sun*

McGraw's fifth album, *[A Place in the Sun](/wiki/A_Place_in_the_Sun_(Tim_McGraw_album))*, continued his streak in 1999, debuting atop both the US country and pop album charts and selling 3 million copies. Over 251,000 of those copies were sold during its first week, making this the singer's first number 1 opener on the *Billboard* 200. It produced another four number one hits on the U.S. country charts with "[Please Remember Me](/wiki/Please_Remember_Me)", "[Something Like That](/wiki/Something_Like_That)", "[My Best Friend](/wiki/My_Best_Friend_(Tim_McGraw_song))", and "[My Next Thirty Years](/wiki/My_Next_Thirty_Years)". "[Some Things Never Change](/wiki/Some_Things_Never_Change_(Tim_McGraw_song))" peaked at number 7 on the charts. McGraw also contributed a song for the Grammy-winning tribute album to [Bob Wills](/wiki/Bob_Wills) entitled *Ride With Bob*. A cover of "Milk Cow Blues", this song was recorded as a duet with [Asleep at the Wheel](/wiki/Asleep_at_the_Wheel), whom he had met while performing together at the George Strait Country Music Festival.

McGraw recorded two more duets with his wife in the late-1990s, both of which appeared on her albums. "[Just to Hear You Say That You Love Me](/wiki/Just_to_Hear_You_Say_That_You_Love_Me)", from her multi-platinum-certified 1998 album *[Faith](/wiki/Faith_(Faith_Hill_album))*, reached the Top 5 of the US country charts. Her follow-up album, 1999's *[Breathe](/wiki/Breathe_(Faith_Hill_album))*, featured "[Let's Make Love](/wiki/Let%27s_Make_Love_(song))", which won a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for [Best Country Vocal Collaboration](/wiki/Best_Country_Vocal_Collaboration) in 2000.

### 2000s

McGraw performing for the United States Air Force in 2003

#### *Greatest Hits*

In 2000, McGraw released his first *[Greatest Hits](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_(Tim_McGraw_album))* album, which topped the country albums charts for nine weeks and sold nearly 6 million copies, making this one of the biggest-selling albums in the modern country market. In the latter half of the year, he and Hill went out on the [Soul2Soul Tour](/wiki/Soul2Soul_Tour), playing to sellout crowds in 64 venues, including [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden). The tour was one of the top tours of any genre in the U.S. It was also the leading country tour of 2000. While in [Buffalo, New York](/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), McGraw and [Kenny Chesney](/wiki/Kenny_Chesney) became involved in a scuffle with police officers after Chesney attempted to ride a police horse. McGraw came to Chesney's aid after police officers nearby believed the horse was being stolen and tried to arrest him. The two were arrested and charged with assault but were later cleared. During a concert with the George Strait Country Music Festival several weeks later, Hill, dressed as a police officer, made an unscheduled appearance at the end of McGraw's set and led him off the stage.

#### *Set This Circus Down*

McGraw's sixth studio album, *[Set This Circus Down](/wiki/Set_This_Circus_Down)*, was released in April 2001. It even spawned four number one hits on the country charts as well, this time with "[Grown Men Don't Cry](/wiki/Grown_Men_Don%27t_Cry)", "[Angry All the Time](/wiki/Angry_All_the_Time)" (with Faith Hill), "[The Cowboy in Me](/wiki/The_Cowboy_in_Me)", and "[Unbroken](/wiki/Unbroken_(Tim_McGraw_song))". The singer provided harmony vocals for [Jo Dee Messina](/wiki/Jo_Dee_Messina)'s song "[Bring On the Rain](/wiki/Bring_On_the_Rain)", which he also produced. That song topped the country charts. Hungry for more of his music, fans downloaded a version of his performance of the song "Things Change" from his appearance at the [Country Music Association](/wiki/Country_Music_Association) Awards Show. The song was played extensively on radio, becoming the first country song to appear on the charts from a fully downloaded version.

#### *Tim McGraw and the Dancehall Doctors*

In 2002, McGraw bucked country music traditions by recording his seventh studio album *[Tim McGraw and the Dancehall Doctors](/wiki/Tim_McGraw_and_the_Dancehall_Doctors)* with his tour band The Dancehall Doctors. Unlike rock music—where it is commonplace for touring bands to provide the music on albums recorded by the artist they support, country albums are typically recorded with [session musicians](/wiki/Session_musician). McGraw chose to use his own touring band, in order to recognize their part in
his success, and to capture some of the feel of a real band.

All of the Dancehall Doctors have worked with McGraw since at least 1996. Their lineup includes:

- Darran Smith – lead guitar, acoustic guitar
- Bob Minner – rhythm guitar, acoustic guitar, banjo, mandolin
- Denny Hemingson – steel guitar, electric, baritone, and slide guitars, dobro
- John Marcus – bass guitar
- Dean Brown – fiddle, mandolin
- Jeff McMahon – piano, organ, synthesizer, keyboards ... etc.
- Billy Mason – drums
- David Dunkley – percussion

The album debuted at No. 2 on the country albums charts, Its fourth and fifth singles "[Real Good Man](/wiki/Real_Good_Man)" and "[Watch the Wind Blow By](/wiki/Watch_the_Wind_Blow_By)" both climbed to number one on the U.S. *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* [Hot Country Songs](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) chart. "[She's My Kind of Rain](/wiki/She%27s_My_Kind_of_Rain)" peaked at No. 2 in 2003, and "[Red Rag Top](/wiki/Red_Rag_Top)" reached number 5. The album also features a cover of [Elton John](/wiki/Elton_John)'s early-1970s classic "[Tiny Dancer](/wiki/Tiny_Dancer)," as well as duets with [Kim Carnes](/wiki/Kim_Carnes) on "Comfort Me" (a response to the [September 11, 2001 attacks](/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks)) and [Don Henley](/wiki/Don_Henley) and [Timothy B. Schmit](/wiki/Timothy_B._Schmit) of [the Eagles](/wiki/The_Eagles) on "Illegal." "[She's My Kind of Rain](/wiki/She%27s_My_Kind_of_Rain)" also received a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) nomination for Best Country Vocal Performance-Male at the [46th Grammy Awards](/wiki/46th_Grammy_Awards).

#### *Live Like You Were Dying*

His eighth album, 2004's *[Live Like You Were Dying](/wiki/Live_Like_You_Were_Dying)*, continued the singer's record of commercial success. The album's first single and its [title track](/wiki/Live_Like_You_Were_Dying_(song)) was dedicated to his father [Tug McGraw](/wiki/Tug_McGraw), who died of a brain tumor earlier in the year, was an ode to living life fully and in the moment. The second single "[Back When](/wiki/Back_When)" was a [paean](/wiki/Paean) to an easy nostalgia. In December 2019, McGraw spoke on stage at the annual End Well Symposium about why he wrote "Live Like You Were Dying" and his struggles with caregiving for his dying father. "Live Like You Were Dying" spent seven non-consecutive weeks at No. 1 on *Billboard* and went on to become the top country song of 2004 on the *Billboard* Year-End charts. It also became one of the most awarded records by winning ACM Single and Song of the Year, CMA Single and Song of the Year, and a Grammy. "Back When" went to number one on the country charts as well. The album produced three more singles with "[Drugs or Jesus](/wiki/Drugs_or_Jesus)", "[Do You Want Fries with That](/wiki/Do_You_Want_Fries_with_That)", and "[My Old Friend](/wiki/My_Old_Friend)". "Drugs or Jesus" became McGraw's first single since 1993 to not ever reach within the Top 10 on the U.S. country charts, while "Do You Want Fries with That" and "My Old Friend" peaked at numbers 5 and 6, respectively.

In late 2004, his unlikely [duet](/wiki/Duet_(music)) with hip hop artist [Nelly](/wiki/Nelly) on "[Over and Over](/wiki/Over_and_Over_(Nelly_song))" became a [crossover](/wiki/Crossover_music) hit, spending 10 weeks atop the top 40 chart. "Over and Over" brought McGraw a success he had never previously experienced on [contemporary hit radio](/wiki/Contemporary_hit_radio) or R&B radio, and brought both artists success neither had previously experienced in the [hot adult contemporary](/wiki/Hot_adult_contemporary) market. The song also spent a week at the top of the charts in the United Kingdom, becoming McGraw's first hit single in Britain and Nelly's third number one hit in the country after "[Dilemma](/wiki/Dilemma_(Nelly_song))" and "[My Place](/wiki/My_Place_(Nelly_song))". "Over and Over" also reached the top of the charts in Australia, New Zealand, and the Republic of Ireland, and the top 10 in Austria, Canada, Denmark, Germany, Romania, and Switzerland.

Throughout the 2005 [NFL](/wiki/NFL) season, McGraw sang an alternate version of "I Like It, I Love It" every week during the season. The alternate lyrics, which changed each week, would make reference to plays during Sunday's games, and the song would be played alongside video highlights during halftime on *[Monday Night Football](/wiki/Monday_Night_Football)*. Later in the year, McGraw became a minority owner of the [Arena Football League](/wiki/Arena_Football_League_(1987%E2%80%932008))'s [Nashville Kats](/wiki/Nashville_Kats) when majority owner [Bud Adams](/wiki/Bud_Adams) (owner of the NFL's [Tennessee Titans](/wiki/Tennessee_Titans)) was awarded the expansion franchise.

#### *Let It Go*

McGraw performing during the [Soul2Soul Tour](/wiki/Soul2Soul_Tour), July 2006 photo by T. Scott/Sisters Photography

In April 2006, McGraw and Hill began their 73-concert 55-city [Soul2Soul II Tour](/wiki/Soul2Soul_II_Tour), again to strong commercial acceptance. The tour grossed roughly $89 million and sold approximately 1.1 million tickets, making it the top-grossing tour in the history of country music. It was also named "Major Tour of the Year" by *[Pollstar](/wiki/Pollstar)*, beating out such heavyweights as [Madonna](/wiki/Madonna) and the Rolling Stones. In a special gesture, the couple donated all of the profits from their performance in [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans,_Louisiana) to Hurricane Katrina relief. McGraw, along with [Kenny Chesney](/wiki/Kenny_Chesney), contributed to a version of [Tracy Lawrence](/wiki/Tracy_Lawrence)'s song "[Find Out Who Your Friends Are](/wiki/Find_Out_Who_Your_Friends_Are)", which can be found on Lawrence's album *[For the Love](/wiki/For_the_Love)*. Although the official single version features only Lawrence's vocals, many stations have opted to play the version with McGraw and Chesney instead.

McGraw released his eleventh studio album, *[Let It Go](/wiki/Let_It_Go_(Tim_McGraw_album))*, on March 27, 2007. The album's first single, "[Last Dollar (Fly Away)](/wiki/Last_Dollar_(Fly_Away))", peaked at number one on the [Hot Country Songs](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) chart. This marked McGraw's first No. 1 single since "Back When" in late 2004. The album debuted at No. 1 on both the *Billboard* 200 and Top Country Album charts, marking his fourth No. 1 album on the 200 charts and his ninth overall. His daughters can be heard singing the chorus during the last few seconds of the song on the video. During the [Academy of Country Music](/wiki/Academy_of_Country_Music) awards show on May 15, 2007, McGraw performed a song titled "[If You're Reading This](/wiki/If_You%27re_Reading_This)", which he co-wrote with [The Warren Brothers](/wiki/The_Warren_Brothers). Several radio stations began to play the live recording of the song; as a result, it entered the U.S. *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* [Hot Country Songs](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) chart at No. 35. McGraw also produced [the debut album](/wiki/Halfway_to_Hazard_(album)) of country music duo [Halfway to Hazard](/wiki/Halfway_to_Hazard). The duo's first single, "Daisy", peaked at No. 39 on the country charts in the summer of 2007. In the summer of 2007, McGraw and Hill toured together once again in the [Soul2Soul 2007](/wiki/Soul2Soul_2007) tour.

In the edition of January 18, 2008 of the *USA Today* newspaper, McGraw was stated to be featured on the [Def Leppard](/wiki/Def_Leppard) album *[Songs from the Sparkle Lounge](/wiki/Songs_from_the_Sparkle_Lounge)*, having also co-written the first single, "Nine Lives", with Def Leppard band members [Joe Elliott](/wiki/Joe_Elliott), [Phil Collen](/wiki/Phil_Collen), and [Rick Savage](/wiki/Rick_Savage). The unusual pairing goes back to 2006 when McGraw joined Def Leppard onstage for the song "[Pour Some Sugar On Me](/wiki/Pour_Some_Sugar_On_Me)", and then collaborated on the song "Nine Lives" afterward. The album was released on April 25, 2008. At the 2007 50th Annual Grammy Awards, McGraw received 5 nominations including Best Country Album (for *Let It Go*), Best Country Song (both for "[If You're Reading This](/wiki/If_You%27re_Reading_This)" and "[I Need You](/wiki/I_Need_You_(Tim_McGraw_and_Faith_Hill_song))"), Best Country Collaboration with Vocals (with "I Need You"), and Best Male Country Vocal Performance (with "If You're Reading This"). In May 2008, he hit the road with the Live Your Voice tour. The mainly-outdoor arena concert tour was his first solo outing in nearly three years. Also in May 2008, he debuted a new song in his follow-up to *Let It Go* at the Stagecoach Music Festival in Indio, California. In July 2008, the album's sixth single and its title track, "[Let It Go](/wiki/Let_It_Go_(Tim_McGraw_song))", was released to country radio. Following that, a seventh single, "[Nothin' to Die For](/wiki/Nothin%27_to_Die_For)", entered the Country charts at No. 57 in late December. McGraw released his third greatest-hits package, *[Greatest Hits 3](/wiki/Greatest_Hits_3_(Tim_McGraw_album))* on October 7, 2008. The album features 12 tracks. McGraw was set to debut a new song at the 2009 ACM Awards, but then canceled his performance; he was replaced by [Blake Shelton](/wiki/Blake_Shelton), who sang "[She Wouldn't Be Gone](/wiki/She_Wouldn%27t_Be_Gone)".

#### *Southern Voice*

Tim McGraw with [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill) at the 2009 [American Music Awards](/wiki/American_Music_Awards)

McGraw's twelfth studio album, *[Southern Voice](/wiki/Southern_Voice_(album))*, was released October 20, 2009, and led by the single "[It's A Business Doing Pleasure With You](/wiki/It%27s_A_Business_Doing_Pleasure_With_You)", which was shipped to radio outlets in late June 2009.
*Southern Voice* was argued to be McGraw's last album for Curb Records, following the dispute over releasing his third Greatest Hits collection back in October 2008 without his permission. McGraw did not approve of the release. On November 30, 2010, Curb Records released his fourth greatest hits compilation, *[Number One Hits](/wiki/Number_One_Hits_(Tim_McGraw_album))*.

### 2010s

#### *Emotional Traffic* and Curb Records lawsuit

On January 2, 2011, McGraw announced plans for his Emotional Traffic Tour featuring opening acts [Luke Bryan](/wiki/Luke_Bryan) and [The Band Perry](/wiki/The_Band_Perry).
[Sirius XM](/wiki/Sirius_XM) announced on March 30, 2011, that they would be launching Tim McGraw radio, a commercial-free music channel devoted to McGraw's music, and featuring an in-depth interview with McGraw as well. As of fall 2010, McGraw had finished work on the album *[Emotional Traffic](/wiki/Emotional_Traffic)*, his last album with Curb Records. On May 13, 2011, Curb Records filed a breach-of-contract suit against McGraw. The label alleged that McGraw recorded tracks for his *Emotional Traffic* album too early prior to its delivery to the label. Several days later, McGraw filed a counter suit against the label seeking advance payment and recording-fund reimbursement, unspecified damages, and a jury trial. A trial was scheduled to begin in July 2012. In November 2011, a judge granted McGraw permission to record music for another label, ending his relationship with Curb Records that began in 1990. A few hours after the ruling, Curb released "[Better Than I Used to Be](/wiki/Better_Than_I_Used_to_Be_(Sammy_Kershaw_song)#Tim_McGraw_version)", the first single from *Emotional Traffic*. The album was released on January 24, 2012.

#### *Two Lanes of Freedom*

In December 2011, McGraw released his first Christmas single, "Christmas All Over the World", on his own label StyleSonic Records. On May 21, 2012, however, he signed with [Big Machine Records](/wiki/Big_Machine_Records).
McGraw's debut album for Big Machine, entitled *[Two Lanes of Freedom](/wiki/Two_Lanes_of_Freedom)*, was released on February 5, 2013. It debuted at number 2 on the charts by selling 108,000 copies. The album includes the singles "[Truck Yeah](/wiki/Truck_Yeah)", "[One of Those Nights](/wiki/One_of_Those_Nights_(Tim_McGraw_song))", "[Highway Don't Care](/wiki/Highway_Don%27t_Care)" (a duet with [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift) which also features [Keith Urban](/wiki/Keith_Urban) on lead guitar), and "[Southern Girl](/wiki/Southern_Girl_(song))". McGraw performed at the [C2C: Country to Country](/wiki/C2C:_Country_to_Country) festival in [London](/wiki/London) on March 16, 2013.

#### *Love Story* and *Sundown Heaven Town*

McGraw released a single titled "[Lookin' for That Girl](/wiki/Lookin%27_for_That_Girl)" in January 2014 as the lead-off single to his second album for Big Machine. It was followed immediately by the announcement of the [Sundown Heaven Town Tour](/wiki/Sundown_Heaven_Town_Tour). The album, titled *[Sundown Heaven Town](/wiki/Sundown_Heaven_Town)*, was released on September 16, 2014. Four months into its run, "Lookin' for That Girl" was withdrawn as a single and replaced with "Meanwhile, Back at Mama's", which features backing vocals from Hill. "[Shotgun Rider](/wiki/Shotgun_Rider)" became the album's third single and a number 1 [Country Airplay](/wiki/Country_Airplay) hit by the end of 2014. Following it was "[Diamond Rings and Old Barstools](/wiki/Diamond_Rings_and_Old_Barstools)", a duet with Catherine Dunn. McGraw's eighth greatest hits album, *Love Story*, is a compilation of his twelve biggest love songs and two previously unreleased recordings. It was released exclusively through Walmart on February 4, 2014, by Curb Records.

#### *Damn Country Music*

On August 10, 2015, McGraw released a new single to digital retailers, titled "[Top of the World](/wiki/Top_of_the_World_(Tim_McGraw_song))", which was later released to radio on August 17, 2015, as the lead single to his third studio album for [Big Machine Records](/wiki/Big_Machine_Records). On September 17, McGraw announced that the album was titled *[Damn Country Music](/wiki/Damn_Country_Music)*, with a release date scheduled for November 6. The album's second single, "[Humble and Kind](/wiki/Humble_and_Kind)", released to country radio on February 1, 2016, and went on to reach number 1 on the [Billboard Hot Country](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_Country_Singles_and_Tracks) charts. McGraw was selected as one of 30 artists to perform on "*Forever Country*", a mash-up track of [Take Me Home, Country Roads](/wiki/Take_Me_Home,_Country_Roads), [On the Road Again](/wiki/On_the_Road_Again_(Willie_Nelson_song)) and [I Will Always Love You](/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You) which celebrates 50 years of the [CMA Awards](/wiki/CMA_Awards). The album's third single, "[How I'll Always Be](/wiki/How_I%27ll_Always_Be)" released to country radio on July 11, 2016. It reached number 3 on the Country Airplay in January 2017.

#### *The Rest of Our Life*

On October 4, 2016, during a show at the Ryman Auditorium, McGraw and Hill announced that they would be going back on the road together again on the [Soul2Soul World Tour](/wiki/Soul2Soul:_The_World_Tour). The tour began on April 7, 2017, in [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans) and will continue into 2018, incorporating the [C2C: Country to Country](/wiki/C2C:_Country_to_Country) festival held in the [UK](/wiki/United_Kingdom) and Ireland throughout March 2018.

Before the commencement of the tour, it was reported that McGraw, alongside Hill, had signed a new deal with [Sony Music Nashville](/wiki/Sony_Music_Nashville). The signing also indicated the release of a duet album between the couple, and that multiple solo recordings would be produced. The new record label signing also preceded the release of "[Speak to a Girl](/wiki/Speak_to_a_Girl)", the lead single from the duet album, *[The Rest of Our Life](/wiki/The_Rest_of_Our_Life)*, which was released on November 17, 2017. The release of the album coincided with the opening of an exhibit at the [Country Music Hall of Fame and Museum](/wiki/Country_Music_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum) titled *Mississippi Woman, Louisiana Man*, which celebrates the careers of both McGraw and Hill.

McGraw released two major best-selling books in 2019, with the first co-written with "Pulitzer Prize" winner [Jon Meacham](/wiki/Jon_Meacham) which reached No. 2 on the New York Times Bestsellers list. Songs of America: Patriotism, Protest, and the Music That Made a Nation. New York: Random House. 2019. [ISBN](/wiki/ISBN_(identifier)) [978-0593132951](/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-0593132951). His second book, "Grit and Grace", also reached No. 2 on the New York Times Bestsellers list.

### 2020s

#### *Here on Earth*

In February 2020, McGraw rejoined Big Machine Records, which he was previously signed to from 2011 to 2017. He released a new album, *[Here on Earth](/wiki/Here_on_Earth_(Tim_McGraw_album))*, on August 21, 2020. The "Here on Earth Tour" was cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States). In January 2021, McGraw released the single "[Undivided](/wiki/Undivided_(Tim_McGraw_and_Tyler_Hubbard_song))" with [Tyler Hubbard](/wiki/Tyler_Hubbard) of [Florida Georgia Line](/wiki/Florida_Georgia_Line), which was included on a deluxe edition of *Here on Earth* released later that year. On October 9, 2021, McGraw confronted a heckler at a performance at the Nugget Events Center in [Sparks, Nevada](/wiki/Sparks,_Nevada), after he forgot the words to his song "Just to See You Smile." After removing the heckler, he explained that he had been filming a movie and had a lot on his mind, and asked for the audience's help in remembering the words.

#### *Standing Room Only* and *Poet's Resumé*

On August 25, 2023, McGraw released his sixteenth studio album, *[Standing Room Only](/wiki/Standing_Room_Only_(Tim_McGraw_album))*. The title track was released as the album's lead single on March 10, 2023. It reached number 2 on the U.S. [Country Airplay](/wiki/Country_Airplay) chart. Three months after releasing *Standing Room Only*, McGraw surprise-released a six-track [EP](/wiki/Extended_play), *Poet's Resumé* on November 21, 2023.

## Production career

McGraw has occasionally served as a [record producer](/wiki/Record_producer) in collaboration with [Byron Gallimore](/wiki/Byron_Gallimore), who has co-produced all of his albums. The two co-produced [Jo Dee Messina](/wiki/Jo_Dee_Messina)'s [self-titled debut](/wiki/Jo_Dee_Messina_(album)), as well as her next two albums, *[I'm Alright](/wiki/I%27m_Alright_(Jo_Dee_Messina_album))* and *[Burn](/wiki/Burn_(Jo_Dee_Messina_album))*. McGraw and Gallimore also produced the only album released by [The Clark Family Experience](/wiki/The_Clark_Family_Experience) in 2000, and [Halfway to Hazard](/wiki/Halfway_to_Hazard)'s 2007 [self-titled debut album](/wiki/Halfway_to_Hazard_(album)).

## Acting career

McGraw's first acting appearance came in a 1997 episode of *[The Jeff Foxworthy Show](/wiki/The_Jeff_Foxworthy_Show)*, where he played Foxworthy's rival. In 2004, McGraw played a sheriff in [Rick Schroder](/wiki/Rick_Schroder)'s independent release *[Black Cloud](/wiki/Black_Cloud_(film))*. Later in the same year, McGraw received critical acclaim as the overbearing father of [running back](/wiki/Running_back) "Donald Billingsley" in the major studio Texas high school football drama *[Friday Night Lights](/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_(film))*. The *[Dallas Observer](/wiki/Dallas_Observer)* said the role was "played with unexpected ferocity by country singer Tim McGraw". The movie went on to gross over $60 million worldwide at the box office, and sold millions in the DVD market. Most recently, it was named one of the Top 50 High School Movies of All Time (No. 37) by *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)*.

McGraw's first lead role was in the 2006 film *[Flicka](/wiki/Flicka)*, which was released in theaters October 20, 2006. In the remake of the classic book *[My Friend Flicka](/wiki/My_Friend_Flicka)*, McGraw played the father, Rob, costarring with [Alison Lohman](/wiki/Alison_Lohman) and [Maria Bello](/wiki/Maria_Bello). The family-friendly movie debuted in the top 10 list and has grossed over $25 million at the box office. McGraw again achieved critical acclaim for his acting. Shortly before *Flicka* opened, McGraw received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame). His star is located at 6901 Hollywood Boulevard near stars honoring [Julie Andrews](/wiki/Julie_Andrews), [William Shatner](/wiki/William_Shatner), and the late [Greta Garbo](/wiki/Greta_Garbo). One of his *Flicka* co-stars, [Alison Lohman](/wiki/Alison_Lohman), attended the ceremony that included comments from [Billy Bob Thornton](/wiki/Billy_Bob_Thornton), McGraw's co-star in the film *[Friday Night Lights](/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_(film))*. In addition to acting in *Flicka*, McGraw served as executive producer of the soundtrack album, which was released by his record label, StyleSonic Records, in association with Curb Records and Fox 2000 films. It featured the closing credit song "[My Little Girl](/wiki/My_Little_Girl_(Tim_McGraw_song))", one of the first two songs that McGraw recorded that he also co-wrote (the other being "I've Got Friends That Do", both of which were included on *Greatest Hits Vol. 2*). The song was nominated by the Broadcast Film Critics for "Best Song" in a film, and the movie was nominated in the category "Best Family Film (Live Action)". The movie proved to be another success in the DVD market, and has sold over a million copies, debuting at No. 3 on the DVD sales chart.

McGraw also had a small part in the Michael Mann–produced 2007 film *[The Kingdom](/wiki/The_Kingdom_(2007_film))*, reuniting him with *Friday Night Lights* director [Peter Berg](/wiki/Peter_Berg). McGraw played a bitter, angered widower whose wife was killed in the terrorist attack that is the centerpiece of the movie. On November 22, 2008, McGraw made his first appearance on *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*. He also played "Dallas McVie" in *[Four Christmases](/wiki/Four_Christmases)*. McGraw appeared in the 2009 film *[The Blind Side](/wiki/The_Blind_Side_(film))* as [Sean Tuohy](/wiki/Sean_Tuohy), husband of [Sandra Bullock](/wiki/Sandra_Bullock)'s character, [Leigh Anne Tuohy](/wiki/Leigh_Anne_Tuohy). *The Blind Side* is based on the true story of [Michael Oher](/wiki/Michael_Oher), a homeless African-American youngster from a broken home, taken in and adopted by the Tuohys, a well-to-do white family who help him fulfill his potential. He is among the stars of *[Dirty Girl](/wiki/Dirty_Girl_(2010_film))*, a film that premiered on September 12, 2010, at the Toronto Film Festival, along with [Juno Temple](/wiki/Juno_Temple), [Milla Jovovich](/wiki/Milla_Jovovich), [William H. Macy](/wiki/William_H._Macy) and [Dwight Yoakam](/wiki/Dwight_Yoakam). Also in 2010, McGraw starred in *[Country Strong](/wiki/Country_Strong)* as James Canter, the husband and manager of the fictional country singer Kelly Canter (portrayed by [Gwyneth Paltrow](/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow)). In addition to his appearance in the film, McGraw's song "Me and Tennessee", a duet with Paltrow, was played during the closing credits and appears on the film's [soundtrack](/wiki/Country_Strong_(soundtrack)). In 2015, McGraw appeared in [Brad Bird](/wiki/Brad_Bird)'s *[Tomorrowland](/wiki/Tomorrowland_(film))* as Eddie Newton, a NASA engineer, and Casey Newton's (played by [Britt Robertson](/wiki/Britt_Robertson)) father.

*[1883](/wiki/1883_(TV_series))*, a spinoff prequel to [Taylor Sheridan](/wiki/Taylor_Sheridan)'s [Yellowstone](/wiki/Yellowstone_(American_TV_series)) television series was released in late 2021 and stars McGraw, [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill), and [Sam Elliott](/wiki/Sam_Elliott). The success of the miniseries led to McGraw's involvement in stunt director Mike Watson's Western feature film, "The Unbelievers," as an actor & producer.

McGraw was set to headline & produce a bull riding drama series for [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix), until he dropped out as the lead due to his surgery.

## Charitable efforts

In 1994, when McGraw first reached fame, he established the annual Swampstock event. It began as a charity softball game to raise money for hometown little league programs; the event now includes a celebrity softball game and a multi-artist concert that attracts over 11,000 fans per year. The combined events have funded new [Little League](/wiki/Little_League) parks and equipment, and have established college scholarship funds for students in the northeast Louisiana area.

From 1996 to 1999, McGraw hosted an annual New Year's Eve concert in Nashville with special guests including [Jeff Foxworthy](/wiki/Jeff_Foxworthy), the [Dixie Chicks](/wiki/Dixie_Chicks), and [Martina McBride](/wiki/Martina_McBride). The 1997 show raised over $100,000 for the Country Music Foundation Hall of Fame and Museum. Beginning in 1999, McGraw would pick select cities on each tour, and the night before he was scheduled to perform, would choose a local club and host a quickly-organized show. This tour-within-a-tour became known as "The Bread and Water Tour", and all proceeds from the show would go to a charity from that community.

McGraw designed a charity T-shirt sold through Angelwear to benefit MusiCares. MusiCares supports musicians in times of need. His charity focuses particularly on health issues. The Tim McGraw Foundation raises funds to enhance the quality of life of children and adults with brain tumors. He supports the Muhammad Ali Parkinson Center, the David Foster Foundation, which helps families of children in need of organ transplants, and Musicians on Call, which brings music to hospital patients' bedsides. He also performs during dinners and auctions to benefit children with disabilities. Hill and McGraw gave the Navy-Marine Corps Relief Society $375,000 (the entire gate receipts from one of their concerts) to assist the families of 17 sailors following the terrorist attack on [USS *Cole*](/wiki/USS_Cole_(DDG-67)), the guided-missile destroyer that suffered significant damage in the Gulf of Aden, Yemen on October 12, 2000.

In the days immediately following [Hurricane Katrina](/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina), McGraw who was raised in [Mississippi](/wiki/Mississippi), joined groups taking supplies to [Gulfport, Mississippi](/wiki/Gulfport,_Mississippi). The two also hosted several charity concerts to benefit those who were displaced by the storm. Later in the year, the couple established the Neighbor's Keeper Foundation, which provides funding for community charities to assist with basic humanitarian services, in the event of a natural disaster, or for desperate personal circumstances.

McGraw is also a member of the [American Red Cross](/wiki/American_Red_Cross) National Celebrity Cabinet, to which various celebrities donate their time, skills, and fame, to help the Red Cross highlight important initiatives and response efforts.

McGraw has helped out with charity events held by [Green Bay Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) quarterback [Brett Favre](/wiki/Brett_Favre). The [Brett Favre](/wiki/Brett_Favre) Fourward Foundation has featured McGraw performing concerts during dinners and auctions that benefit children with disabilities in Wisconsin and Mississippi. One instance is recorded on Favre's official website.

On July 12, 2007, it was made public that McGraw while in [Grand Rapids, Michigan](/wiki/Grand_Rapids,_Michigan) for a performance, donated $5,000 to Kailey Kozminski, the 3-year-old daughter of Officer Robert Kozminski, a Grand Rapids police officer who was killed on July 8, 2007, while responding to a domestic disturbance.

In April 2023, McGraw surprised rising country star Brandon Davis, whom he previously brought on tour with him in 2022, with a new guitar after all of his gear was stolen earlier in the month.

## Personal politics

Tim McGraw poses for a sailor at [Ponte Vedra Beach, Florida](/wiki/Ponte_Vedra_Beach,_Florida) on May 5, 2010, before performing at the [Tournament Players Club at Sawgrass](/wiki/TPC_at_Sawgrass) military appreciation day.

In a 2006 interview with *[Esquire](/wiki/Esquire_(magazine))* magazine, McGraw stated he would like to run for public office as a [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) candidate, possibly for [United States Senate](/wiki/United_States_Senate) or [Governor of Tennessee](/wiki/Governor_of_Tennessee)—his home state. In the same interview, he praised [Bill Clinton](/wiki/Bill_Clinton).

In a 2008 interview with *[People](/wiki/People_Magazine)* magazine, McGraw referred to himself as a "[Blue Dog Democrat](/wiki/Blue_Dog_Coalition)" and stated that he and his family support [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama).

In January 2021, McGraw performed on the *Celebrating America* inaugural special, a primetime virtual concert celebrating the [inauguration of Joe Biden](/wiki/Inauguration_of_Joe_Biden).

## Personal life

McGraw married fellow country singer [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill) in 1996, and they have three daughters.

In 2000, [Kenny Chesney](/wiki/Kenny_Chesney) and McGraw became involved in a scuffle with police officers in [Buffalo, New York](/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), after Chesney was riding a State Police horse and refused to get off the horse. McGraw came to Chesney's aid after police officers nearby believed the horse was being stolen. The two were arrested and charged, Chesney for disorderly conduct and McGraw for assault, but were acquitted in 2001.

McGraw holds a [private pilot license](/wiki/Private_pilot_license) and owns a single-engine [Cirrus SR22](/wiki/Cirrus_SR22).

In 2015, *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* estimated McGraw's annual income at $38 million.

McGraw is the godfather to the son of [Garrett Hedlund](/wiki/Garrett_Hedlund) and [Emma Roberts](/wiki/Emma_Roberts). He and Hedlund became friends after co-starring in *[Friday Night Lights](/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_(film))* and *[Country Strong](/wiki/Country_Strong)*.

In acknowledgment of his grandfather's Italian heritage, McGraw was honored by the National Italian American Foundation (NIAF) in 2004, receiving the NIAF Special Achievement Award in Music during the Foundation's 29th Anniversary Gala.

McGraw's [Bracco Italiano](/wiki/Bracco_Italiano) named Lepshi won Best of Breed at the 2023 [Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show](/wiki/Westminster_Kennel_Club_Dog_Show). The McGraw-Hill family also has two other Bracco Italianos, Caesar and Stromboli.

# List of Nobel laureates in Physics

Front side (obverse) of the Nobel Prize Medal for Physics presented to [Edward Victor Appleton](/wiki/Edward_Victor_Appleton) in 1947

The [Nobel Prize in Physics](/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics) ([Swedish](/wiki/Swedish_language): *Nobelpriset i fysik*) is awarded annually by the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) to scientists in the various fields of [physics](/wiki/Physics). It is one of the five [Nobel Prizes](/wiki/Nobel_Prize) established by the 1895 [will](/wiki/Will_and_testament) of [Alfred Nobel](/wiki/Alfred_Nobel) (who died in 1896), awarded for outstanding contributions in physics. As dictated by Nobel's will, the award is administered by the [Nobel Foundation](/wiki/Nobel_Foundation) and awarded by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences. The award is presented in [Stockholm](/wiki/Stockholm) at an annual ceremony on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death. Each recipient receives a medal, a diploma and a monetary award prize that has varied throughout the years.

## Statistics

The Nobel Prize in Physics has been awarded to 229 individuals as of 2025. The first prize in physics was [awarded in 1901](/wiki/1901_Nobel_Prize_in_Physics) to [Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen](/wiki/Wilhelm_R%C3%B6ntgen), of Germany, who received 150,782 [SEK](/wiki/Swedish_krona). [John Bardeen](/wiki/John_Bardeen) is the only laureate to win the prize twice—in 1956 and 1972.

[William Lawrence Bragg](/wiki/Lawrence_Bragg) was the youngest Nobel laureate in physics; he won the prize in 1915 at the age of 25. He was also the youngest laureate for any Nobel prize until 2014 (when [Malala Yousafzai](/wiki/Malala_Yousafzai) won the Nobel Peace Prize at the age of 17). The oldest Nobel Prize laureate in physics was [Arthur Ashkin](/wiki/Arthur_Ashkin) who was 96 years old when he was awarded the prize in 2018.

Only five women have won the prize: [Marie Curie](/wiki/Marie_Curie) (1903), [Maria Goeppert-Mayer](/wiki/Maria_Goeppert-Mayer) (1963), [Donna Strickland](/wiki/Donna_Strickland) (2018), [Andrea Ghez](/wiki/Andrea_Ghez) (2020), and [Anne L'Huillier](/wiki/Anne_L%27Huillier) (2023). Before L'Huillier, each woman only ever received a quarter share of the prize, although Marie Curie did receive an [unshared Nobel prize in chemistry in 1911](/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Chemistry). In 2023, L'Huillier received a one-third share.

There have been six years for which the Nobel Prize in Physics was not awarded (1916, 1931, 1934, 1940–1942). There were also nine years for which the Nobel Prize in Physics was delayed for one year:

- The 1914 prize awarded to [Max von Laue](/wiki/Max_von_Laue) was announced only in November 1915.
- The Prize was not awarded in 1917, as the Nobel Committee for Physics decided that none of that year's nominations met the necessary criteria, but was awarded to [Charles Glover Barkla](/wiki/Charles_Glover_Barkla) in 1918 and counted as the 1917 prize.
- This precedent was followed for the 1918 prize awarded to [Max Planck](/wiki/Max_Planck) in 1919,
- the 1921 prize awarded to [Albert Einstein](/wiki/Albert_Einstein) in 1922,
- the 1924 prize awarded to [Manne Siegbahn](/wiki/Manne_Siegbahn) in 1925,
- the 1925 prize awarded to [James Franck](/wiki/James_Franck) and [Gustav Hertz](/wiki/Gustav_Hertz) in 1926,
- the 1928 prize awarded to [Owen Richardson](/wiki/Owen_Richardson) in 1929,
- the 1932 prize awarded to [Werner Heisenberg](/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg) in 1933, and
- the 1943 prize awarded to [Otto Stern](/wiki/Otto_Stern) in 1944.

A 2020 study reported that half of the Nobel Prizes for science awarded between 1995 and 2017 are clustered in few disciplines. [Particle physics](/wiki/Particle_physics) (14%), [atomic physics](/wiki/Atomic_physics) (10.9%), and 3 non-physics disciplines dominate the prize in recent decades, followed by [semiconductor physics](/wiki/Semiconductor_physics) and [magnetics](/wiki/Magnetics).

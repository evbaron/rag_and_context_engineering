**Sir Peter Cook** [RA](/wiki/Royal_Academician) (born 22 October 1936) is an English [architect](/wiki/Architecture), lecturer and writer on architectural subjects. He was a founder of [Archigram](/wiki/Archigram), and was knighted in 2007 by [Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II) for his services to architecture and teaching. He is also a [Royal Academician](/wiki/Royal_Academy) and a Commandeur de l'[Ordre des Arts et des Lettres](/wiki/Ordre_des_Arts_et_des_Lettres) of the French Republic. His achievements with [Archigram](/wiki/Archigram) were recognised by the [Royal Institute of British Architects](/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_British_Architects) in 2004, when the group was awarded the [Royal Gold Medal](/wiki/Royal_Gold_Medal).

## Early life and education

Cook was born in [Southend-on-Sea](/wiki/Southend-on-Sea), [Essex](/wiki/Essex). His mother aspired to a career as an artist, while his father served in the UK as an army officer. Cook studied architecture at [Bournemouth College of Art](/wiki/Arts_University_Bournemouth) from 1953–58. He then entered the [Architectural Association School of Architecture](/wiki/Architectural_Association_School_of_Architecture) in London, graduating in 1960.

## Career

Cook was a director of London's [Institute of Contemporary Arts](/wiki/Institute_of_Contemporary_Arts) (1970–1972), chair of architecture at [The Bartlett School of Architecture](/wiki/The_Bartlett) at [University College London](/wiki/University_College_London) (1990–2006), and has been the director of Art Net in London and curator of the British Pavilion at the [Venice Architecture Biennale](/wiki/Venice_Architecture_Biennale). He continues to curate, organise and exhibit around the world: in Seoul, LA, Cyprus, the [Centre Georges Pompidou](/wiki/Centre_Georges_Pompidou), [Design Museum](/wiki/Design_Museum), [Louisiana Museum of Modern Art](/wiki/Louisiana_Museum_of_Modern_Art), as well as in castles, sheds and garages.

He is a Senior Fellow of the [Royal College of Art](/wiki/Royal_College_of_Art), London. Cook's professorships include those of the [Royal Academy](/wiki/Royal_Academy), University College London and the Hochschule für Bildende Künste ([Städelschule](/wiki/St%C3%A4delschule)) in [Frankfurt](/wiki/Frankfurt)-Main, Germany.

Cook has built in Osaka, Nagoya, Berlin and Madrid. However, it was the construction of his arts building in 2003, the [Kunsthaus Graz](/wiki/Kunsthaus_Graz) (also known as 'The Friendly Alien') in [Graz](/wiki/Graz), [Austria](/wiki/Austria), with [Colin Fournier](/wiki/Colin_Fournier), that brought his work to a wider public.

Cook was awarded a knighthood in Elizabeth II's [2007 Birthday Honours List](/wiki/2007_Birthday_Honours), for services to architecture.

In 2013, he completed the [Vienna University of Economics and Business](/wiki/Vienna_University_of_Economics_and_Business)'s new law faculty  and Australia's newest school of architecture, the Abedian School of Architecture at [Bond University](/wiki/Bond_University) on the Gold Coast.

His first building in the UK, a new drawing studio at the [Arts University Bournemouth](/wiki/Arts_University_Bournemouth) was opened by [Zaha Hadid](/wiki/Zaha_Hadid) in March 2016. He also built the Innovation Studio at the [Arts University Bournemouth](/wiki/Arts_University_Bournemouth), which was opened by [Odile Decq](/wiki/Odile_Decq) in 2021.

In 2025, Cook  built a Play Pavilion at [the Serpentine](/wiki/The_Serpentine) London in collaboration with [LEGO](/wiki/Lego). It opened on World Play Day on the 11th June.

**Isère** ([US](/wiki/American_English): [/iːˈzɛər/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*ee-ZAIR*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); French: [[izɛʁ]](/wiki/Help:IPA/French) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:LL-Q150_(fra)-GrandCelinien-Is%C3%A8re.wav); [Arpitan](/wiki/Arpitan_language): *Isera*; [Occitan](/wiki/Occitan_language): *Isèra*, Occitan pronunciation: [[iˈsɛɾa]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Occitan)) is a landlocked [department](/wiki/Departments_of_France) in the southeastern French [region](/wiki/Regions_of_France) of [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](/wiki/Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes). Named after the river [Isère](/wiki/Is%C3%A8re_(river)), it had a population of 1,298,990 in 2023. Its [prefecture](/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) is [Grenoble](/wiki/Grenoble). It borders [Rhône](/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_(department)) to the northwest, [Ain](/wiki/Ain) to the north, [Savoie](/wiki/Savoie) to the east, [Hautes-Alpes](/wiki/Hautes-Alpes) to the south, [Drôme](/wiki/Dr%C3%B4me) and [Ardèche](/wiki/Ard%C3%A8che) to the southwest and [Loire](/wiki/Loire_(department)) to the west.

## History

Isère is one of the original 83 departments created during the [French Revolution](/wiki/French_Revolution) on 4 March 1790. It was established from the main part of the [former province](/wiki/Provinces_of_France) of [Dauphiné](/wiki/Dauphin%C3%A9). Its area was reduced twice, in 1852 and again in 1967, on both occasions losing territory to the department of [Rhône](/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_(department)).

The [Château de Vizille](/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Vizille), which was the seat of the [Assembly of Vizille](/wiki/Assembly_of_Vizille) that followed the 1788 [Day of the Tiles](/wiki/Day_of_the_Tiles) in Grenoble, now houses the [Musée de la Révolution française](/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_de_la_R%C3%A9volution_fran%C3%A7aise).

In 1852 in response to rapid urban development around the edge of [Lyon](/wiki/Lyon), the (hitherto Isère) communes of [Bron](/wiki/Bron), [Vaulx-en-Velin](/wiki/Vaulx-en-Velin), [Vénissieux](/wiki/V%C3%A9nissieux) and [Villeurbanne](/wiki/Villeurbanne) were transferred to [Rhône](/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_(department)). In 1967 the redrawing of local government borders led to the creation of the [Urban Community of Lyon](/wiki/Urban_Community_of_Lyon) (more recently known simply as Greater Lyon or Grand Lyon). At that time intercommunal groupings of this nature were not permitted to straddle departmental frontiers, and accordingly 23 more Isère communes (along with six communes from [Ain](/wiki/Ain)) found themselves transferred to Rhône. The affected Isère communes were [Chaponnay](/wiki/Chaponnay), [Chassieu](/wiki/Chassieu), [Communay](/wiki/Communay), [Corbas](/wiki/Corbas), [Décines-Charpieu](/wiki/D%C3%A9cines-Charpieu), [Feyzin](/wiki/Feyzin), [Genas](/wiki/Genas), [Jonage](/wiki/Jonage), [Jons](/wiki/Jons), [Marennes](/wiki/Marennes,_Rh%C3%B4ne), [Meyzieu](/wiki/Meyzieu), [Mions](/wiki/Mions), [Pusignan](/wiki/Pusignan), [Saint-Bonnet-de-Mure](/wiki/Saint-Bonnet-de-Mure), [Saint-Laurent-de-Mure](/wiki/Saint-Laurent-de-Mure), [Saint-Pierre-de-Chandieu](/wiki/Saint-Pierre-de-Chandieu), [Saint-Priest](/wiki/Saint-Priest,_Rh%C3%B4ne), [Saint-Symphorien-d'Ozon](/wiki/Saint-Symphorien-d%27Ozon), [Sérézin-du-Rhône](/wiki/S%C3%A9r%C3%A9zin-du-Rh%C3%B4ne), [Simandres](/wiki/Simandres), [Solaize](/wiki/Solaize), [Ternay](/wiki/Ternay,_Rh%C3%B4ne) and [Toussieu](/wiki/Toussieu).

Most recently, on 1 April 1971, [Colombier-Saugnieu](/wiki/Colombier-Saugnieu) was transferred to Rhône. Banners appeared in the commune's three little villages at the time proclaiming *[Dauphinois](/wiki/Dauphin%C3%A9) toujours* ("Always Dauphinois").

## Geography

Isère includes a part of the [French Alps](/wiki/French_Alps). The highest point in the department is the subpeak Pic Lory at 4,088 metres (13,412 feet), subsidiary to the 4,102 metres (13,458 feet) [Barre des Écrins](/wiki/Barre_des_%C3%89crins) in the adjoining [Hautes-Alpes](/wiki/Hautes-Alpes) department. The summit of [La Meije](/wiki/La_Meije) at 3,988 metres (13,084 feet) is also well known. The [Vercors Plateau](/wiki/Vercors_Plateau) aesthetically dominates the western part of the department.

## Transport

The department is served by [Alpes–Isère Airport](/wiki/Alpes%E2%80%93Is%C3%A8re_Airport) which provides flights to some European destinations. However, other airports such as [Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport](/wiki/Lyon%E2%80%93Saint-Exup%C3%A9ry_Airport) and [Geneva Airport](/wiki/Geneva_Airport) are also used by air travellers from the department as both airports provide more domestic and international destinations.

## Culture

The [Grande Chartreuse](/wiki/Grande_Chartreuse)

The [Grande Chartreuse](/wiki/Grande_Chartreuse) is the mother abbey of the [Carthusian](/wiki/Carthusians) order. It is located 22 km (14 mi) north of Grenoble.

As early as the 13th century, residents of the north and central parts of Isère spoke a dialect of the [Franco-Provençal language](/wiki/Franco-Proven%C3%A7al_language) called [Dauphinois](/wiki/Franco-Proven%C3%A7al_language#Dialects), while those in the Southern parts spoke the [Vivaro-Alpine](/wiki/Vivaro-Alpine_dialect) dialect of [Occitan](/wiki/Occitan_language). Both continued to be spoken in rural areas of Isère into the 20th century.

## Tourism

Isère features many [ski resorts](/wiki/Ski_resort), including the [Alpe d'Huez](/wiki/Alpe_d%27Huez), [Les Deux Alpes](/wiki/Les_Deux_Alpes), the [1968 Winter Olympics](/wiki/1968_Winter_Olympics) resorts of [Chamrousse](/wiki/Chamrousse), [Villard de Lans](/wiki/Villard_de_Lans), [Autrans](/wiki/Autrans). Other popular resorts include [Les 7 Laux](/wiki/Les_7_Laux), [Méaudre](/wiki/M%C3%A9audre), [Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse](/wiki/Saint-Pierre-de-Chartreuse), [Alpe du Grand Serre](/wiki/Alpe_du_Grand_Serre) and [Gresse-en-Vercors](/wiki/Gresse-en-Vercors). At the department level, Isère is the third-largest ski and winter destination in France, after [Savoie](/wiki/Savoie) and [Haute-Savoie](/wiki/Haute-Savoie). It also hosts [Coupe Icare](/wiki/Coupe_Icare), an annual festival of free flight, such as [paragliding](/wiki/Paragliding) and [hang-gliding](/wiki/Hang-gliding), held at the world-renowned paragliding site at [Lumbin](/wiki/Lumbin).

Grenoble has a dozen museums, including its most famous, established in 1798, the [Museum of Grenoble](/wiki/Museum_of_Grenoble). The [European Synchrotron Radiation Facility](/wiki/European_Synchrotron_Radiation_Facility) (ESRF), an international research facility in Grenoble, is also open to visitors.

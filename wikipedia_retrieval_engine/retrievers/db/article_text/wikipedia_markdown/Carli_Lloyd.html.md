**Carli Anne Hollins** ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Lloyd**; born July 16, 1982) is an American former professional [soccer](/wiki/Association_football) player. She won the [2015](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) and [2019](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) editions of the [FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) with the [United States](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team), finished second at the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), and scored the gold medal-winning goals in the finals of the [2008 Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament) and the [2012 Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_-_Women%27s_tournament). She also won the bronze medal with the U.S. at the [2020 Olympics](/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics). She was named [FIFA Player of the Year](/wiki/FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year) in 2015 and [2016](/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Football_Awards_2016).

Lloyd made [316 appearances](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team#Player_records) for the U.S. national team, placing her second in [caps](/wiki/Cap_(sport)). She ranks [third](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team#Player_records) in goals and [fifth](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team#Player_records) in assists for the team. During the United States' 5–2 win over [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team) in the [2015 Women's World Cup Final](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Final), Lloyd became the first player in history to score three goals in a Women's World Cup final and the second player of any gender to score a [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick) in a World Cup Final. At the conclusion of the tournament, she received the [Golden Ball](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup#Awards), the Silver Boot and the Best Goal awards.

During her club career, Lloyd played for the [Chicago Red Stars](/wiki/Chicago_Red_Stars), Sky Blue FC and the [Atlanta Beat](/wiki/Atlanta_Beat_(WPS)) in the [Women's Professional Soccer](/wiki/Women%27s_Professional_Soccer) (WPS) league. In the [National Women's Soccer League](/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League) (NWSL), she won the league championship with the [Western New York Flash](/wiki/Western_New_York_Flash), and also played for the [Houston Dash](/wiki/Houston_Dash) and [Gotham FC](/wiki/Gotham_FC). In 2019, Lloyd was the highest paid female soccer player in the world. Her memoir, *When Nobody Was Watching*, was published in 2016.

## Early life

Born to Stephen and Pamela Lloyd, Carli was raised in [Delran Township](/wiki/Delran_Township,_New_Jersey), a suburban community in [New Jersey](/wiki/New_Jersey). She has a brother, Stephen, and a sister, Ashley. She began playing soccer at age five. She attended the opening U.S. match of the [1999 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), which inspired her to play for the national team.

## High school career

Lloyd attended [Delran High School](/wiki/Delran_High_School) from 1997 to 2001, where she played soccer under the tutelage of Rudy Klobach. As a high school athlete, she was known for her exceptional ball control and skill at distributing the ball from the midfield. During her senior year, she scored 26 goals and served eight assists while captaining her team to an 18–3 record. *[The Philadelphia Inquirer](/wiki/The_Philadelphia_Inquirer)* named her Girls' High School Player of the Year in 1999 and 2000. She was named to the Star-Ledger All-State First Team twice and received 1999 and 2000 [Parade All-American](/wiki/Parade_(magazine)) honors. In 2000, she was named the *[Courier-Post](/wiki/Courier-Post)* Player of the Year and the South Jersey Soccer Coaches Association (SJSCA) Midfielder of the Year. While in high school, Lloyd also played for the semi-professional [W-League](/wiki/USL_W-League_(1995%E2%80%932015)) teams [Central Jersey Splash](/wiki/USL_W-League_(1995%E2%80%932015)) (1999), New Brunswick Power (2000), and [South Jersey Banshees](/wiki/South_Jersey_Banshees) (2001).

## College career

Lloyd attended [Rutgers University](/wiki/Rutgers_University) from 2001 to 2005, and played for the [Scarlet Knights](/wiki/Rutgers_Scarlet_Knights) women's soccer team under head coach Glenn Crooks. During her [freshman](/wiki/Freshman) season, she started every match and was the team's leading scorer with 15 goals. She was named to [Soccer America](/wiki/Soccer_America)'s All-Freshman Team and was the first Rutgers player to earn Big East Rookie of the Year honors. As a [sophomore](/wiki/Sophomore), Lloyd was the team's leading scorer for the second consecutive season, with 12 goals and seven assists. The same year, she was a finalist for the [Hermann Trophy](/wiki/Hermann_Trophy), widely considered the highest accolade for collegiate soccer players. During her third season with the Scarlet Knights, she scored 13 goals and served 2 assists, and was named a Big East Academic All-Star.

In the summer of 2004—just prior to her senior year—Lloyd played one match for the semi-professional [W-League](/wiki/USL_W-League_(1995%E2%80%932015)) team [New Jersey Wildcats](/wiki/New_Jersey_Wildcats). As a senior at Rutgers, she scored 10 goals and served one assist. She was named the 2004 Big East Midfielder of the Year, and was the first Rutgers athlete to be named First-Team All-[Big East](/wiki/Big_East_Conference_(1979%E2%80%932013)) for four consecutive years. Lloyd ended her college career as the school's all-time leader in points (117), goals (50), and shots. She graduated with a [bachelor's degree](/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree) in Exercise Science and Sport Studies. In 2013, she was inducted into the Rutgers Hall of Distinguished Alumni.

## Club career

### 2009–2011: Women's Professional Soccer

In 2008, Lloyd signed with the [Chicago Red Stars](/wiki/Chicago_Red_Stars) of the newly-launched [Women's Professional Soccer](/wiki/Women%27s_Professional_Soccer) league. During the league's [inaugural season](/wiki/2009_Women%27s_Professional_Soccer_season), she scored two goals: one during her team's 4–0 win over the [Boston Breakers](/wiki/Boston_Breakers) on April 25, and the other during a 3–1 victory over the [Los Angeles Sol](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Sol) on August 2. The Red Stars finished the season in sixth place in the league, with a 5–10–5 record. Lloyd was a free agent for the upcoming [2010 season](/wiki/2010_Women%27s_Professional_Soccer_season); she signed with her home state club, [Sky Blue FC](/wiki/NJ/NY_Gotham_FC), winner of the 2009 WPS championship. In April 2010, Lloyd slipped and broke her ankle during a match against the Red Stars. The injury kept her off the field until September, when she returned for two games.

In December 2010, Lloyd signed with the expansion team [Atlanta Beat](/wiki/Atlanta_Beat_(WPS)) for the [2011 season](/wiki/2011_WPS_season). The team's head coach [James Galanis](/wiki/James_Galanis) described Lloyd as a "fantastic midfielder", and said he expected her to bring professionalism to the team. The Beat finished their first season in last place with a 1–13–4 record, with Lloyd scoring two goals in ten appearances.

### 2013–2014: Western New York Flash

In January 2013, Lloyd joined the [Western New York Flash](/wiki/Western_New_York_Flash) in the new [National Women's Soccer League](/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League) as part of the [NWSL Player Allocation](/wiki/NWSL_Player_Allocation). After recovering from a shoulder injury suffered earlier in the year, Lloyd made her debut for the Flash on May 12, during the team's 2–1 win over [FC Kansas City](/wiki/FC_Kansas_City). She scored her first goal for the Flash in a match against her former club, [Sky Blue FC](/wiki/Sky_Blue_FC), as the Flash won 3–0. During a match against the [Washington Spirit](/wiki/Washington_Spirit) on June 28, Lloyd scored a [hat trick](/wiki/Hat_trick) as the Flash achieved a 4–0 victory. She was named NWSL Player of the Week for her performance.

Lloyd finished the [2013 season](/wiki/2013_Western_New_York_Flash_season) with 10 goals, the third-highest goal total in the league. The Flash finished first in league play during the regular season with a 10–4–8 record, and advanced to the playoffs. During the Flash's semifinal match against Sky Blue, Lloyd scored two goals to secure a 2–0 win. The Flash were defeated 2–0 by the [Portland Thorns FC](/wiki/Portland_Thorns_FC) in the championship final.

### 2015–2017: Houston Dash and Manchester City

On October 16, 2014, Lloyd was traded to the [Houston Dash](/wiki/Houston_Dash) in exchange for [Becky Edwards](/wiki/Becky_Edwards_(soccer)), [Whitney Engen](/wiki/Whitney_Engen) and a third-round pick in the [2016 NWSL College Draft](/wiki/2016_NWSL_College_Draft).

In February 2017, Lloyd joined the English club [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_W.F.C.) on loan, for the [FA WSL Spring Series](/wiki/FA_WSL_Spring_Series). Manchester finished second in the Spring Series and won the [2016–17 FA Women's Cup](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_FA_Women%27s_Cup). Lloyd scored a goal in [the final](/wiki/2017_FA_Women%27s_Cup_Final), a 4–1 victory over [Birmingham City](/wiki/Birmingham_City_L.F.C.). Lloyd's final appearance for Manchester came on May 21, when she was [ejected](/wiki/Fouls_and_misconduct_(association_football)#Red_card_(dismissal)) from the game for elbowing an opposing player in the face. The three-match ban she received for violent conduct covered the remainder of her loan period.

### 2018–2021: Sky Blue FC (Gotham FC)

In January 2018, Lloyd was traded to [Sky Blue FC](/wiki/Gotham_FC), whose name would be changed to NJ/NY Gotham FC in 2021. Lloyd scored 4 goals in 18 appearances for the team in 2018, including the only goal in a 1–0 victory over the [Orlando Pride](/wiki/Orlando_Pride), which was Sky Blue's sole win of the season. She was named to the 2018 NWSL Second XI.

## International career

### Youth national team

Lloyd represented the United States on the [under-21 team](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_under-23_soccer_team) from 2002 to 2005. She played at the Nordic Cup four times, winning consecutive titles from 2002 to 2005 in Finland, Denmark, Iceland, and Sweden respectively. During the first round of the 2003 Nordic Cup, she served the assist in her team's 1–0 win against Denmark. At the 2004 edition, she scored two goals and served one assist. At the 2005 edition, she scored three times, including one goal during the championship match against Norway.

### Senior national team

Lloyd made her first appearance for the [United States women's national soccer team](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team) on July 10, 2005, against [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine_women%27s_national_football_team). She scored her first international goal on October 1, 2006, against [Taiwan](/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_women%27s_national_football_team). She first made the starting line-up at the 2006 [Algarve Cup](/wiki/Algarve_Cup), when she started in a group match against [Denmark](/wiki/Denmark_women%27s_national_football_team) and in the final against [Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_football_team).

#### 2007: Algarve Cup and FIFA Women's World Cup

After scoring once in her first 24 matches with the national team, Lloyd scored four goals at the [2007 Algarve Cup](/wiki/Algarve_Cup), held in March in Portugal. As the tournament's top scorer, she was named Most Valuable Player. In August, she registered her first two-goal game for the national team during a 6–1 win against [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_women%27s_national_football_team). In September, Lloyd played in her first [Women's World Cup](/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), held in China. The U.S. team finished third in the tournament, with Lloyd starting in three of the five games in which she played. During 2007, Lloyd started in 13 of her 23 matches. She ranked third on the team in scoring, with nine goals and three assists.

#### 2008 Summer Olympics in China

During the championship match of the [CONCACAF Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](/wiki/CONCACAF_Women%27s_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament), Lloyd scored one goal as the U.S. tied with Canada, eventually defeating them in a [penalty shootout](/wiki/Penalty_shoot-out_(association_football)). Before Lloyd left for China to play in the [2008 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), the mayor of [Delran Township](/wiki/Delran_Township,_New_Jersey)—Lloyd's hometown—gave her a key to the city and announced that several soccer fields would be named after her. During the group stage of the Olympics, Lloyd scored the only goal in her team's 1–0 defeat of [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team). In the final against [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil_women%27s_national_football_team), she scored the game-winning goal in [extra time](/wiki/Extra_time_(association_football)) to secure a 1–0 victory and the gold medal for the United States.

Lloyd was named the [2008 U.S. Soccer Athlete of the Year](/wiki/U.S._Soccer_Athlete_of_the_Year) along with [Tim Howard](/wiki/Tim_Howard). She was on the starting lineup in all 35 games in which she played in 2008, tying for the team lead in matches started during the year. Her 2,781 minutes on the pitch for the U.S. ranked third on the team in minutes played. Her nine goals and nine assists resulted in her best scoring year yet on the national team.

#### 2009–2010: Injury recovery, 100th cap and World Cup qualifying

In 2009, the U.S. national team competed in eight games, of which Lloyd was on the starting lineup in five. At the [2010 Algarve Cup](/wiki/2010_Algarve_Cup), Lloyd scored the game-opening goal in the final helping the U.S. clinch the championship title after defeating [Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_football_team) 3–2.

Although she suffered a broken ankle in the fourth game of the [2010 WPS Season](/wiki/2010_Women%27s_Professional_Soccer_season) while playing for [Sky Blue FC](/wiki/Sky_Blue_FC), she played in 15 matches for the United States in 2010, starting 14. Lloyd started all five games at the [2010 CONCACAF Women's World Cup Qualifying Tournament](/wiki/2010_CONCACAF_Women%27s_Gold_Cup), scoring two goals, including the United States' lone goal during the championship match. She ended the tournament with five assists and was named the Player of the Match three times during the tournament. After the U.S. finished third at the tournament, they traveled to Italy to vie for a place at the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in the [UEFA-CONCACAF play-off](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_qualification_(UEFA%E2%80%93CONCACAF_play-off)) against [Italy](/wiki/Italy_women%27s_national_football_team). Playing every minute of the series, Lloyd scored three goals with five assists during the series. She earned her 100th career cap during the second leg of the series.

#### 2011 FIFA Women's World Cup

2011 saw the U.S. team making preparations for the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) and training starting with the [Four Nations Tournament](/wiki/Four_Nations_Tournament_(women%27s_football)). Lloyd scored the lone goal for the U.S. in the opening match loss to Sweden. In the championship match, the U.S. defeated Canada 2–0 with Lloyd scoring the first goal and being named Player of the Match.

At the [2011 Algarve Cup](/wiki/2011_Algarve_Cup), Lloyd scored three goals including the first goal in the championship match; subsequently named best goal for the tournament. She was named player of the match for the 2nd time in the tournament. The team won the cup, making it their eighth title win.

At the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup), Lloyd scored the final goal in a 3–0 win against [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia_women%27s_national_football_team) for her first World Cup goal. Throughout the tournament, she tallied an assist, a goal, and one successful [penalty kick](/wiki/Penalty_kick_(association_football)) in the shootout against Brazil to send the U.S. to the semi-finals vs. [France](/wiki/France_women%27s_national_football_team). In the [World Cup final](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Final), after finishing the game tied 2–2 the U.S. went on to penalty kicks with [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team). Along with two teammates who failed to convert their penalty kicks, Lloyd mishit the ball over the crossbar. The U.S. won the silver medal at the tournament.

#### 2012 Summer Olympics in London

Lloyd celebrates with her teammates after scoring a goal at the 2012 Summer Olympics.

The national team opened 2012 with the [Olympic Qualifiers](/wiki/2012_CONCACAF_Women%27s_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament) in [Vancouver](/wiki/Vancouver), British Columbia, Canada. The U.S. was placed in Group B with the [Dominican Republic](/wiki/Dominican_Republic_women%27s_national_football_team), [Guatemala](/wiki/Guatemala_women%27s_national_football_team), and [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_women%27s_national_football_team). In the first match, the United States routed the Dominican Republic by a score of 14–0 with Lloyd tallying one goal and one assist. In the second match, the U.S. again defeated [Guatemala](/wiki/Guatemala_women%27s_national_football_team) 13–0 with Lloyd again finding the back of the net and providing an assist.

The game to win the group and thus play the second place team from Group A occurred between the U.S. and [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_women%27s_national_football_team). The U.S. was previously defeated 2–1 by Mexico during the [2010 CONCACAF Women's Gold Cup](/wiki/2010_CONCACAF_Women%27s_Gold_Cup). This time, the U.S. beat Mexico 4–0 with Lloyd netting her first career [hat trick](/wiki/Hat_trick). She was subsequently named player of the match.

Lloyd and teammate [Hope Solo](/wiki/Hope_Solo) after the 2012 Summer Olympics final

In the semi-final, the U.S. faced [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_women%27s_national_football_team). During the second half, the U.S. scored two goals, the second coming from Lloyd. The U.S. beat Costa Rica 3–0 with Lloyd named player of the match for the second game in a row. During the final match against [Canada](/wiki/Canada_women%27s_national_soccer_team), the U.S. defeated Canada at home 4–0 to move on to the Olympics as the [CONCACAF](/wiki/CONCACAF) champions. Lloyd finished the tournament with six goals and three assists and tied for the team lead in goals scored.

At the [2012 Summer Olympics in London](/wiki/Football_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), Lloyd scored the go-ahead goal in the 56th minute of the team's opening match against [France](/wiki/France_women%27s_national_football_team), to boost the U.S. to a 3–2 lead; the match ended with a final score of 4–2. She scored her second goal of the tournament against [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia_women%27s_national_football_team) during the group stage.

During the Olympic gold medal match against [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team), played at London's [Wembley Stadium](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium), Lloyd scored both American goals in the team's 2–1 victory. Her four goals in the tournament tied for the second highest on the U.S. squad. She is the only player (of either gender) in history to score the game-winning goal in two separate Olympic gold medal matches; her first occurred during the [Beijing 2008 final](/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament#Gold_medal_match) against Brazil.

After scoring her 46th international goal in the 13th minute of a friendly against [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_women%27s_national_football_team) in October 2013, Lloyd became the highest-scoring midfielder in the team's history, passing [Julie Foudy](/wiki/Julie_Foudy), who finished her career with 45 goals.

#### 2015 FIFA Women's World Cup

Lloyd celebrates the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) win at the ticker tape parade in New York City, July 2015

In April 2015, Lloyd was named by head coach [Jill Ellis](/wiki/Jill_Ellis) to the 23-player roster for the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in Canada. Lloyd captained the team during four of the team's matches, including the quarterfinal against China, in which she scored the winning goal on her 200th cap, semi-final against Germany and final against Japan, and scored six goals during the tournament, finishing the tournament on a four-game goalscoring streak that spanned the knockout stage and that culminated in a [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick) in the game's opening 16 minutes during the [final](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Final) against Japan. The last goal was hailed by [Reuters](/wiki/Reuters) as "one of the most remarkable goals ever witnessed in a Women's World Cup", and featured Lloyd catching Japanese goalkeeper [Ayumi Kaihori](/wiki/Ayumi_Kaihori) off her line and chipping her virtually from the halfway line.

For her efforts in leading the United States to a record third World Cup title, and first since 1999, she won the Golden Ball as the best player of the tournament. While Lloyd's six goals were enough to match [Célia Šašić](/wiki/C%C3%A9lia_%C5%A0a%C5%A1i%C4%87) as the tournament's top scorer, Šašić won the Golden Boot for playing fewer minutes and Lloyd was awarded the Silver Boot. Lloyd also became the first woman to score a hat-trick in a World Cup final and the first player, male or female, to do so since [Geoff Hurst](/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) did so for England against West Germany at Wembley in [1966](/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_Final). Only one player has since replicated the feat – [Kylian Mbappé](/wiki/Kylian_Mbapp%C3%A9) for France against Argentina in Lusail, Qatar in [2022](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Final). Furthermore, her third goal earned her a nomination for the [FIFA Puskás Award](/wiki/FIFA_Pusk%C3%A1s_Award), FIFA's annual award for Goal of the Year. Finally, Lloyd is the only player to have scored a hat-trick in normal time of a World Cup final; Hurst scored two goals in extra time and Mbappé one.

#### 2016 Rio de Janeiro Olympics

Lloyd during a match against Sweden at the 2016 Summer Olympics in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

Lloyd scored the first goal for the U.S. at the 2016 Summer Olympics in [Rio de Janeiro](/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro), Brazil. Her goal helped lift the U.S. to a 2–0 win over New Zealand in the group stage.

#### 2019 FIFA Women's World Cup

Lloyd scored in her first two games of the [2019 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup); one against [Thailand](/wiki/Thailand_women%27s_national_football_team) and two against [Chile](/wiki/Chile_women%27s_national_football_team). Hence, she became the first player to score in six straight Women's World Cup matches.

#### Reaching her 300th cap, 2019–2021

On April 10, 2021, Lloyd earned her 300th cap in a friendly match against Sweden to become the third player to achieve that feat after [Kristine Lilly](/wiki/Kristine_Lilly) and [Christie Pearce](/wiki/Christie_Pearce). On June 14, 2021, she became the oldest player at 38 years and 332 days to score for the United States in a 4–0 win over [Jamaica](/wiki/Jamaica_women%27s_national_football_team) at the [BBVA Stadium](/wiki/BBVA_Stadium), and thereby beating the record previously held by Kristine Lilly (38 years, 264 days).

#### 2020 Tokyo Olympics and retirement

On August 5, 2021, she scored twice in a 4–3 win over [Australia](/wiki/Australia_women%27s_national_soccer_team) in the bronze medal match of the [2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), to set the U.S. women's record for most Olympic goals, 10 goals in total, surpassing Abby Wambach. Following the Olympics, Lloyd announced she would be retiring from the national team in 2021.

In early 2022, she partnered with Teqball USA and one of her retirement plans is to make [Teqball](/wiki/Teqball) an Olympic sport by 2028. She also developed CL10 Soccer Clinic, a program to help people improve their soccer skills. In October 2022, she co-conducted the draw for the [2023 Women's World Cup](/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup) in Australia and New Zealand held at the [Aotea Centre](/wiki/Aotea_Centre) in Auckland. She also co-conducted the draw for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup) in Qatar, held at the Doha Exhibition and Convention Center, in April 2022.

#### Criticism of USWNT post-retirement

Since retiring, Lloyd has made numerous critical comments regarding the national team, saying she "hated" playing for the team in the final years of her career. Lloyd has said that the culture became "toxic" after the 2015 World Cup victory, alleging that players became more focused on building their brands than winning as a team. During the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup, Lloyd criticized the team for celebrating after finishing runners-up in their group, avoiding elimination after being close to losing to Portugal in their last group match, saying, "Players are smiling. They're laughing. It's not good enough." Lloyd's comments created controversy and drew pushback from both current players and former teammates.

Later in 2023, she said U.S. Soccer never wanted her to be the star of the team. “That’s just the way the world works as far as showcasing players," Lloyd said. "They’re not always highlighting the best players. They’re oftentimes highlighting the most marketable players.”

## Style of play

Although she was criticised for being inconsistent at the beginning of career, and for losing possession too easily, Lloyd later developed into one of the best players in the world, and is highly regarded for her outstanding determination, mental strength, and work-ethic. A tenacious, energetic, and hard-working player, she also is known for her control, technique, and passing accuracy, and is capable of aiding her team both defensively and offensively, due to her stamina, strength, and tackling, as well as her ability to get into good attacking positions, and either score goals or create chances for teammates. These abilities, coupled with her tactical versatility, enabled her to be deployed in several [midfield](/wiki/Midfielder) positions; although she began her career in the centre, as a [defensive midfielder](/wiki/Defensive_Midfielder), she was most comfortable when moved to a more advanced role, as an [attacking midfielder](/wiki/Attacking_midfielders) behind the forwards. Lloyd earned a reputation as a "clutch player", due to her tendency to score decisive goals. A powerful striker of the ball, she was capable of scoring from any position on the pitch, and could finish well both with her head and with her feet inside the area.

## Personal life

Lloyd and husband Brian Hollins after a Phillies game

Lloyd lives with her husband, golfer Brian Hollins, in [Medford, New Jersey](/wiki/Medford,_New_Jersey). They married on November 4, 2016, in Puerto Morelos, Mexico. On May 1, 2024, the couple announced that they were expecting their first child in October 2024. On October 18, 2024, she gave birth to a girl. Her memoir, *When Nobody Was Watching*, was published in 2016.

## In popular culture

### Endorsements

Lloyd has had an [endorsement](/wiki/Testimonial) deal with [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) for several years. In 2011, she was the focus of a promotional feature for the sports company entitled, *Pressure Makes Us: Carli Lloyd*. Following the 2015 FIFA Women's World Cup, Lloyd starred in a commercial for [Xfinity](/wiki/Xfinity) and signed an endorsement deal with [Visa](/wiki/Visa_Inc.). In August 2015, she co-starred in a Nike commercial called *Snow Day* also featuring [Rob Gronkowski](/wiki/Rob_Gronkowski) and teammate [Sydney Leroux](/wiki/Sydney_Leroux). In April 2016, she was named brand ambassador for [Lifeway Foods](/wiki/Lifeway_Foods) and appeared in a television commercial for [Heineken](/wiki/Heineken) the same year. She has a partnership with [Whole Foods Market](/wiki/Whole_Foods_Market) and appeared in a television commercial for [United Airlines](/wiki/United_Airlines). In June 2016, she joined [Michael Phelps](/wiki/Michael_Phelps) in partnership deals with [Krave Jerky](/wiki/Krave_Jerky). She also has endorsement deals with [Beats by Dre](/wiki/Beats_by_Dre), [Kind](/wiki/Kind_(company)), and [NJM Insurance](/wiki/NJM_Insurance).

### Magazines, television, and video games

Lloyd has been featured in *[Glamour](/wiki/Glamour_(magazine))*, *[Shape](/wiki/Shape_(magazine))*, and *[Sports Illustrated](/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)* magazines. She was on the covers of Howler Magazine and Sports Illustrated.

In 2012, Lloyd appeared in an ESPN feature called *Title IX is Mine: USWNT*. She has been interviewed on numerous television shows including: *[Good Morning America](/wiki/Good_Morning_America)*, *[The Daily Show with Jon Stewart](/wiki/The_Daily_Show_with_Jon_Stewart)*, *[Live with Kelly and Michael](/wiki/Live_with_Kelly_and_Michael)*, *[The Today Show](/wiki/The_Today_Show)*, *[Late Night with Seth Meyers](/wiki/Late_Night_with_Seth_Meyers)*, *[Fox & Friends](/wiki/Fox_%26_Friends)*, *[Late Late Show with James Corden](/wiki/Late_Late_Show_with_James_Corden)*. Lloyd also participated in the 2023 reality show *[Special Forces: World's Toughest Test](/wiki/Special_Forces:_World%27s_Toughest_Test)*. She joined [Soccer on Fox Sports](/wiki/Soccer_on_Fox_Sports) as a studio analyst in 2022.

Lloyd was featured along with her national teammates in the [EA Sports' FIFA video game series](/wiki/FIFA_(video_game_series)) in *[FIFA 16](/wiki/FIFA_16)*, the first time women players were included in the game. In September 2015, she was ranked by EA Sports as the No. 1 women's player in the game.

### Ticker tape parade and White House honor

Following the United States' win at the 2015 FIFA Women's World Cup, Lloyd and her teammates became the first women's sports team to be honored with a [ticker tape parade](/wiki/Ticker_tape_parade) in New York City. Each player received a key to the city from Mayor [Bill de Blasio](/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio). In October of the same year, the team was honored by [President Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) at the [White House](/wiki/White_House).

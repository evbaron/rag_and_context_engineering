Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States), the rest of the season was played at the [ESPN Wide World of Sports Complex](/wiki/ESPN_Wide_World_of_Sports_Complex) in [Bay Lake, Florida](/wiki/Bay_Lake,_Florida), including the [NBA playoffs](/wiki/2020_NBA_playoffs) and [Finals](/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals).

The **2019–20 NBA season** was the 74th season of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA). The regular season began on October 22, 2019, and originally was scheduled to end on April 15, 2020. The [2020 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2020_NBA_All-Star_Game) was played on February 16, at the [United Center](/wiki/United_Center) in [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago), and was won by Team LeBron, 157–155. The playoffs were originally scheduled to begin on April 18, and end with the NBA Finals in June.

On January 26, the season took an abrupt delicate turn when [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) legend [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant), who was less than four years into retirement from the league, died in [a helicopter crash in Calabasas, California](/wiki/2020_Calabasas_helicopter_crash), along with his 13-year-old daughter [Gianna](/wiki/Gianna_Bryant) and seven others. A number of tributes and memorials were issued, and the [All-Star MVP Award](/wiki/NBA_All-Star_Game_Most_Valuable_Player) was renamed in Bryant's honor.

[The season was suspended](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2019%E2%80%9320_NBA_season) on March 11 as a result of the [Utah Jazz](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Utah_Jazz_season)–[Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Oklahoma_City_Thunder_season) game being postponed due to [Rudy Gobert](/wiki/Rudy_Gobert) testing positive for [COVID-19](/wiki/COVID-19) at the onset of the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_sports). At the time of the suspension, teams had played between 63 and 67 games.

On June 4, the NBA Board of Governors approved a plan to restart the season on July 30, and the [National Basketball Players Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Players_Association) approved this plan the next day. Under the plan, 22 teams played eight additional regular season games to determine playoff seeding, and 16 teams entered a [conventional postseason tournament](/wiki/2020_NBA_playoffs). All of these games took place in the [NBA Bubble](/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble), an isolation zone specifically created for NBA operations at the [ESPN Wide World of Sports Complex](/wiki/ESPN_Wide_World_of_Sports_Complex) at [Walt Disney World](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World) in [Bay Lake, Florida](/wiki/Bay_Lake,_Florida).

On August 26, the season was suspended for a second time by a [wildcat strike](/wiki/Wildcat_strike_action), to protest the [shooting of Jacob Blake](/wiki/Shooting_of_Jacob_Blake#Sports) and police brutality, during the playoffs. Play resumed three days later on August 29. The Finals ended on October 11, 2020, 355 days after the October 22, 2019, regular season opening day, and 377 days after the first pre-season games on September 30, 2019. In the [NBA Finals](/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals), the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) defeated the [Miami Heat](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Miami_Heat_season), 4 games to 2, to win their 17th NBA championship. This was the longest season in NBA history.

### Retirement

- On March 1, 2019, [Channing Frye](/wiki/Channing_Frye) announced his retirement from the NBA. Frye played 13 seasons in the NBA, winning one championship with the [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers) in 2016.
- On April 9, 2019, [Dirk Nowitzki](/wiki/Dirk_Nowitzki) announced his retirement from the NBA. Nowitzki played all his 21 seasons with the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) franchise, winning one championship and Finals MVP with the Mavericks in 2011, and also led them to a Finals appearance in [2006](/wiki/2006_NBA_Finals).
- On June 10, 2019, [Tony Parker](/wiki/Tony_Parker) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played 18 seasons in the NBA and was a four-time NBA champion and Finals MVP in 2007 with the San Antonio Spurs.
- On June 28, 2019, [Darren Collison](/wiki/Darren_Collison) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played for five franchises during his 10-year NBA career.
- On August 29, 2019, [Zaza Pachulia](/wiki/Zaza_Pachulia) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played for six franchises during his 16-year career. He won two championships with the [Golden State Warriors](/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors).
- On September 13, 2019, [Shaun Livingston](/wiki/Shaun_Livingston) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played for ten franchises during his 15-year NBA career. He won three championships with the Golden State Warriors.
- On October 17, 2019, [Luol Deng](/wiki/Luol_Deng) signed a ceremonial one-day contract with the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/Chicago_Bulls) and officially retired as a Bull after playing 15 seasons. He was a two-time All-Star with the Bulls.
- On November 4, 2019, [José Calderón](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Calder%C3%B3n_(basketball)) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played for seven franchises during his 14-year NBA career.
- On December 28, 2019, [Zach Randolph](/wiki/Zach_Randolph) announced his retirement from the NBA, Randolph played for five franchises during his 17-year NBA career. He was a two-time All-Star with the Memphis Grizzlies.
- On February 16, 2020, [Marcin Gortat](/wiki/Marcin_Gortat) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played for four franchises during his 12-year NBA career.
- On April 14, 2020, [Trevor Booker](/wiki/Trevor_Booker) announced his retirement from the NBA. He played for five franchises during his eight-year NBA career.
- On June 25, 2020, [Vince Carter](/wiki/Vince_Carter) announced his retirement from the NBA. Carter played for eight franchises during a record 22-year career in the NBA; he is the only player to play in four different decades and was the last active player to have been drafted and played in the 1990s.

### Free agency

[Free agency](/wiki/Free_agency_(sports)) negotiations began on June 30, 2019, at 6 p.m. ET, unlike July 1 from previous seasons. Players officially signed after the [July moratorium](/wiki/July_moratorium) on July 6 at 12 p.m. ET.

#### Off-season

- On April 11, 2019, the [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers) and [Larry Drew](/wiki/Larry_Drew) parted ways after Drew's contract expired after the 2018–19 season.
- On April 11, 2019, the [Memphis Grizzlies](/wiki/Memphis_Grizzlies) fired [J. B. Bickerstaff](/wiki/J._B._Bickerstaff) after nearly two seasons.
- On April 11, 2019, the [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) fired [Dave Joerger](/wiki/Dave_Joerger) after three seasons. The team missed the playoffs for the thirteenth straight year.
- On April 12, 2019, the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and head coach [Luke Walton](/wiki/Luke_Walton) parted ways after three seasons.
- On April 14, 2019, the Sacramento Kings hired Luke Walton as their new head coach.
- On April 22, 2019, the [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/Phoenix_Suns) fired [Igor Kokoškov](/wiki/Igor_Koko%C5%A1kov) after one season. The team missed the playoffs for the ninth straight year.
- On May 3, 2019, the Phoenix Suns hired [Monty Williams](/wiki/Monty_Williams) as their new head coach.
- On May 13, 2019, the Cleveland Cavaliers hired [John Beilein](/wiki/John_Beilein) as their new head coach.
- On May 13, 2019, the Los Angeles Lakers hired [Frank Vogel](/wiki/Frank_Vogel) as their new head coach.
- On June 11, 2019, the Memphis Grizzlies hired [Taylor Jenkins](/wiki/Taylor_Jenkins) as their new head coach.

#### In-season

- On December 6, 2019, the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks) fired head coach [David Fizdale](/wiki/David_Fizdale) after a 4–18 start to the season and named [Mike Miller](/wiki/Mike_Miller_(basketball,_born_1964)) as interim head coach.
- On February 19, 2020, the Cleveland Cavaliers named [J. B. Bickerstaff](/wiki/J._B._Bickerstaff) the new head coach of the team after John Beilein resigned from the position.
- On March 7, 2020, the [Brooklyn Nets](/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) and head coach [Kenny Atkinson](/wiki/Kenny_Atkinson) mutually agreed to part ways. [Jacque Vaughn](/wiki/Jacque_Vaughn) was named interim head coach.

## Preseason

The preseason began on September 30 and ended on October 18.

### International games

The [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) and the [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) played two preseason games at the [NSCI Dome](/wiki/NSCI_Dome) in [Mumbai](/wiki/Mumbai), India on October 4 and 5, 2019.

The [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) and the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) played two preseason games at the [Saitama Super Arena](/wiki/Saitama_Super_Arena) in [Saitama City](/wiki/Saitama_(city)), Japan on October 8 and 10, 2019.

The [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and the [Brooklyn Nets](/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) played two preseason games in [China](/wiki/China), in [Shanghai](/wiki/Shanghai) on October 10 and in [Shenzhen](/wiki/Shenzhen) on October 12.

The [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers) and the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) played one preseason game in [Canada](/wiki/Canada), at [Rogers Arena](/wiki/Rogers_Arena) in [Vancouver](/wiki/Vancouver) on October 17.

## Regular season

The 2019–20 schedule was released on August 12, 2019, and the regular season began on October 22, 2019.

On March 11, 2020, the season was [suspended](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2019%E2%80%9320_NBA_season) due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). The reformatted regular season, with 8 more games scheduled for 22 qualified teams going to the [NBA Bubble](/wiki/NBA_Bubble) in Orlando, was released on June 26. The regular season resumed play within the bubble on July 30.

### International games

The [Charlotte Hornets](/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) and the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) played at the [AccorHotels Arena](/wiki/AccorHotels_Arena) in [Paris](/wiki/Paris), [France](/wiki/France) on January 24, 2020, marking the first NBA regular season game in France.

On June 7, 2019, the NBA announced that the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks), [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons), [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/Phoenix_Suns), and [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs) would play at [Mexico City Arena](/wiki/Mexico_City_Arena) in [Mexico City](/wiki/Mexico_City), marking the first time that four NBA teams would play in Mexico City in one regular season. The Mavericks and Pistons played on December 12, 2019, and the Spurs and Suns played on December 14, 2019.

## Play-in tournament

Main article: [NBA Play-In Tournament](/wiki/NBA_Play-In_Tournament)

Under the plan to restart the season in the [NBA Bubble](/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble), a possible best-of-three play-in series for the final seed in each conference would be held if the ninth seed finished the regular season within four games of the eighth seed. The eighth seed would start with a *de facto* 1–0 lead, meaning that it would need just one win to advance, while the ninth seed must win two in a row.

### Eastern Conference

Because eighth-place Orlando (33–40) finished 7+1⁄2 games ahead ninth-place Washington (25–47), the Magic automatically advanced to the main rounds of the playoffs as the 8th seed in the Eastern Conference, with no play-in contests being necessary there.

### Western Conference

Because eighth-place Portland (35–39) only finished one-half games ahead of ninth-place Memphis (34–39), a play-in was required to determine the 8th seed in the Western Conference.

## Death of Kobe Bryant

Main article: [2020 Calabasas helicopter crash](/wiki/2020_Calabasas_helicopter_crash)

On January 26, 2020, less than four weeks after commissioner emeritus [David Stern](/wiki/David_Stern) died from a brain hemorrhage at age 77 on [New Year's Day](/wiki/New_Year%27s_Day), recently retired [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) legend [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) and his 13-year-old daughter [Gianna](/wiki/Gianna_Bryant) were tragically killed when the [Sikorsky S-76B](/wiki/Sikorsky_S-76B) helicopter that they were on board crashed into a hillside in the city of [Calabasas, California](/wiki/Calabasas,_California), around 30 mi (48 km) northwest of [Downtown Los Angeles](/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), while en route from [John Wayne Airport](/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport) to [Camarillo Airport](/wiki/Camarillo_Airport). Bryant was 41, the accident left behind no survivors and alongside the Bryants were seven others: baseball coach [John Altobelli](/wiki/John_Altobelli), his wife Keri, their 14-year-old daughter Alyssa, Sarah Chester, her 13-year-old daughter Payton, basketball coach Christina Mauser, and the pilot, Ara Zobayan. All occupants were killed on impact and on January 28, Bryant's identity was officially confirmed using fingerprints. The following day, the [Los Angeles County Department of Medical Examiner-Coroner](/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Department_of_Medical_Examiner-Coroner) stated that the official cause of death for him and the eight others on the helicopter was [blunt force trauma](/wiki/Blunt_force_trauma). A year later on February 9, 2021, the cause of the crash was officially determined to be Zobayan experiencing [spatial disorientation](/wiki/Spatial_disorientation) due to heavy fog in the Los Angeles area that morning.

Makeshift memorial at [Staples Center](/wiki/Staples_Center) on the day of the crash

There were eight NBA games that were scheduled to be played on the day of the accident, the first of which being between the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) and [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets), as Pepsi Center (now [Ball Arena](/wiki/Ball_Arena)) was the first NBA arena to acknowledge Bryant's death with the Nuggets holding a brief tribute prior to tip-off and concluding the memorial with the message "Rest in Peace, Mamba". Due to that game tipping off just minutes after the news was revealed for the first time, that game went on as scheduled as Denver defeated Houston, 117–110. There were reportedly conversations held among league authorities on whether or not to cancel any of the other games that day. However, each match ultimately went on as planned, partly due to many feeling that Bryant himself would have wanted the games to go on. Of the remaining seven games played that day, beginning with the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) at [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs) game, every matchup featured each side intentionally taking either an 8-second violation or a 24-second shot clock violation on their very first possession to start the game, serving as a homage to Bryant's number 8 jersey, which he wore from 1996 to 2006, and his number 24 jersey, which he donned from 2006 to 2016. These violation tributes were initially proposed by Spurs head coach [Gregg Popovich](/wiki/Gregg_Popovich) and the rest of the league subsequently followed suit throughout the rest of the Sunday and the following several days. Notably, [Kyrie Irving](/wiki/Kyrie_Irving), who at the time was a member of the [Brooklyn Nets](/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets), excused himself and left [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden) prior to tip-off against the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks), upon learning of Bryant's death. Irving, who considered Bryant to be a mentor, often trained with Kobe and was and continues to be very close with the Bryant family.

On January 30, the first game at [Staples Center](/wiki/Staples_Center) since the crash was played, as the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Clippers_season) held a tribute to Bryant, his daughter, and the seven other victims of the helicopter crash prior to their home game against the [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Sacramento_Kings_season). Although decor representing the Lakers is typically covered during Clippers home games, both of Bryant’s retired Lakers jerseys were visible in the rafters during a tribute video narrated by [Palmdale, California](/wiki/Palmdale,_California) native [Paul George](/wiki/Paul_George).

> "Kobe is a brother to me. From the time I was in high school watching him from afar, to getting in this league at 18 and watching him up close, all the battles that we had throughout my career, the one thing that we always shared was that determination to just want to win, to just want to be great. The fact that I'm here now means so much to me. I want to continue, along with my teammates, his legacy. Not only for this year, but for as long as we can play this game of basketball that we love, because that's what [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) would want. So in the words of Kobe Bryant, 'Mamba out'. But in the words of us, 'not forgotten'. Live on, brother."

— [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James)' tribute to Kobe Bryant prior to the Lakers' first game since the tragedy against the [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers), five days after [his death](/wiki/2020_Calabasas_helicopter_crash) (January 31, 2020)

The previous evening on January 25, [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James) passed Bryant for third place on the [NBA's all-time scoring list](/wiki/List_of_NBA_career_scoring_leaders) during a road loss to the [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Philadelphia_76ers_season) in Bryant's hometown. James would eventually surpass [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) to stand alone as the NBA's all-time leading scorer three years later on February 7, 2023. The [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) learned of Bryant's death while flying back to Los Angeles from Philadelphia one day following their loss to the 76ers, leaving everyone on board the flight in absolute shock and their January 28 meeting with the crosstown rival [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers) would be postponed the following day. This marked the first time an NBA game was postponed for any reason since nearly seven years earlier when the [2013 Boston Marathon bombing](/wiki/Boston_Marathon_bombing) led to the postponement of a Celtics game. The Lakers did not play a game in the wake of Bryant's death until January 31 when they hosted the [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers) on ESPN. Prior to the commencement of the game, the Lakers conducted a 24-minute long, heavily detailed tribute to Bryant, his daughter and the seven other victims. This tribute began with [Usher](/wiki/Usher_(musician)) performing "[Amazing Grace](/wiki/Amazing_Grace)" and was capped off by a rendition of [The Star-Spangled Banner](/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner) by [Boyz II Men](/wiki/Boyz_II_Men) before being concluded with a speech from James, who initially had a monologue prepared, but quickly tossed his index card onto the hardwood after saying "Laker Nation man, I would be selling y'all short if I read off this shit, so I'm going to go straight from the heart", as he proceeded to address a sorrowful [Staples Center](/wiki/Staples_Center) crowd with an emotional, improvised oration, ending his speech with "...so in the words of Kobe Bryant: 'Mamba out', but in the words of us: 'not forgotten', live on brother". Following the eulogy, each member of the Lakers' starting five would be introduced with Bryant's credentials. Portland won 127–119, in what turned out to be the second most watched NBA broadcast ever in ESPN's history, averaging 4.41 million viewers. The first ever head-to-head meeting between [Shaquille O'Neal](/wiki/Shaquille_O%27Neal) and [Yao Ming](/wiki/Yao_Ming) in 2003 was the only one with more viewers. Beginning with the game against Portland, the Lakers added a black logo featuring Bryant’s initials to the baseline of their home floor, and a matching commemorative patch to their jerseys. The team also added a number 8 decal to the immediate left of the scorer's table and a number 24 decal to the right of it. The on-court logos remained in place for home games at Staples Center for about six weeks, until the [COVID-19 suspension](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2019%E2%80%9320_NBA_season) on March 11. The jersey patch continued to be worn for the rest of the season, including through the [Orlando bubble](/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble) with the [2020 NBA playoffs](/wiki/2020_NBA_playoffs) and [Finals](/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals).

The NBA also renamed the NBA All Star Game MVP Award to the [NBA All-Star Game Kobe Bryant Most Valuable Player award](/wiki/NBA_All-Star_Game_Kobe_Bryant_Most_Valuable_Player_award), with [Kawhi Leonard](/wiki/Kawhi_Leonard) of the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers) being the inaugural recipient, who a few days after the crash revealed that he and Bryant shared the same pilot, Ara Zobayan, who also lost his life in the accident. The [2020 NBA All Star Game](/wiki/2020_NBA_All_Star_Game) saw every player on Team LeBron wear the jersey number two for Gianna and every player on Team [Giannis](/wiki/Giannis_Antetokounmpo) wear the jersey number 24 for Kobe. The All Star Game also temporarily changed to an Echelon-style format starting in 2020 with the leading score at the end of the third quarter plus 24 (Bryant's jersey number from 2006 to 2016) acting as the target score. This format would be scrapped however in 2023, with [that year's game](/wiki/2023_NBA_All-Star_Game) in [Salt Lake City](/wiki/Delta_Center) being the final one under this format. The postponed Lakers-Clippers game was originally planned to be made up on April 9, however due to the [suspension of the season on March 11](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2019%E2%80%9320_NBA_season), it was made up at the very beginning of the NBA restart on July 30 with the Lakers emerging victorious 103–101.

On February 7, Bryant and his daughter were buried in a private funeral in [Pacific View Memorial Park](/wiki/Pacific_View_Memorial_Park) in the [Corona del Mar](/wiki/Corona_del_Mar,_Newport_Beach) neighborhood of [Newport Beach, California](/wiki/Newport_Beach,_California). A public memorial service was held on February 24 (2/24, marking both Kobe's and Gianna's jersey numbers) at the Staples Center (now [Crypto.com Arena](/wiki/Crypto.com_Arena)) with [Jimmy Kimmel](/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel) hosting. Speakers at the service included Bryant's widowed wife [Vanessa](/wiki/Vanessa_Bryant), [Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan), and [Shaquille O'Neal](/wiki/Shaquille_O%27Neal), along with [Phoenix Mercury](/wiki/Phoenix_Mercury) guard [Diana Taurasi](/wiki/Diana_Taurasi) and [Geno Auriemma](/wiki/Geno_Auriemma), Taurasi's coach at [Connecticut](/wiki/Connecticut_Huskies_women%27s_basketball), where Gianna had been aspiring to play.

On October 11, 2020, the Lakers defeated the [Miami Heat](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Miami_Heat_season) in Game 6 of the [2020 NBA Finals](/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals) to capture their 17th championship in franchise history and would dedicate the victory and their season to Bryant.

In his first year of eligibility, Bryant was named a finalist for the [Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame), just less than three weeks after his death, before being posthumously inducted in April 2020. His formal induction was delayed until 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States). In October 2021, Bryant was honored as one of the league's greatest players of all time by being named to the [NBA 75th Anniversary Team](/wiki/NBA_75th_Anniversary_Team). During the [2022 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2022_NBA_All-Star_Game) in [Cleveland](/wiki/Rocket_Mortgage_FieldHouse), he was one of fifteen members of that honorary team to be posthumously lionized during a halftime ceremony dedicated to that 75th Anniversary Team, with the mention of Bryant inducing one of, if not the loudest crowd reaction of the commemoration.

## Suspension of season and restart

Main article: [Suspension of the 2019–20 NBA season](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2019%E2%80%9320_NBA_season)

On March 11, 2020, the game between the [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz) and [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) was abruptly postponed shortly before tipoff after it was discovered that the Jazz's player [Rudy Gobert](/wiki/Rudy_Gobert) tested positive for [COVID-19](/wiki/COVID-19). That same evening, the game between the [New Orleans Pelicans](/wiki/New_Orleans_Pelicans) and [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) was also postponed last minute once it was discovered that a referee for the game, Courtney Kirkland, had worked a [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz) game just two days prior. Kirkland later tested negative for COVID-19. The NBA then suspended the remainder of the 2019–20 season "until further notice" following the completion of games already underway. This was the first time a regular season had been interrupted since the [2011 NBA lockout](/wiki/2011_NBA_lockout). The following day, Gobert's teammate [Donovan Mitchell](/wiki/Donovan_Mitchell) also tested positive. On March 14, [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons) forward [Christian Wood](/wiki/Christian_Wood) became the third NBA player to test positive for the virus, and the first outside of the Jazz. On March 17, four [Brooklyn Nets](/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) players, including superstar [Kevin Durant](/wiki/Kevin_Durant), tested positive for the virus. On March 19, two players for the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers), as well as [Marcus Smart](/wiki/Marcus_Smart) of the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics), tested positive for COVID-19. All players recovered and were cleared by local health officials.

On May 23, it was announced that the NBA were in negotiations with [The Walt Disney Company](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company) about the possibility to finish the season at [Walt Disney World](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World)'s [ESPN Wide World of Sports Complex](/wiki/ESPN_Wide_World_of_Sports_Complex) in Orlando. On May 29, NBA Commissioner [Adam Silver](/wiki/Adam_Silver) and the league office informed Board of Governors that July 31 was the target date for a season return.

On June 4, the NBA Board of Governors approved a plan to restart the season on July 31 in Orlando. Under this plan, 13 Western Conference teams and nine Eastern Conference teams, all clubs within six games of a playoff spot, would play eight regular-season "seeding" games. [Play-in games](/wiki/NBA_Play-In_Tournament) for the eighth seed in each conference would then be held if the ninth seed finishes the regular season within four games of the eighth seed. This proposal was then approved by members of the [National Basketball Players Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Players_Association) on June 5.

## Players boycott

See also: [2020 American athlete strikes](/wiki/2020_American_athlete_strikes)

On August 26, 2020, the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) chose not to play in game 5 of the playoff series against the [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic) to protest the police [shooting of Jacob Blake](/wiki/Shooting_of_Jacob_Blake). The NBA and the [National Basketball Players Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Players_Association) announced that, in light of the Milwaukee Bucks striking for their game, the following games of the day would be postponed. The [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) had also discussed striking for their second-round playoff series with the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) in frustration with a lack of social or legislative change after the [murder of George Floyd](/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd) and as a result of Blake's shooting before the Bucks' strike a few days later.

## Medical protocol for season restart

Main article: [2020 NBA Bubble](/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble)

On June 16, 2020, the NBA released a medical protocol to be used during the season restart in the [NBA Bubble](/wiki/NBA_Bubble) at Walt Disney World to ensure the health and safety of players, coaches, officials, and staff. This included regular testing for [COVID-19](/wiki/COVID-19) prior to and throughout the season restart, wearing a face covering or mask, and [social distancing](/wiki/Social_distancing) to prevent an outbreak of COVID-19 from occurring. Players and coaches who were deemed "high-risk individuals" by their team, or players who had already suffered season-ending injuries prior to season suspension, were not permitted to play and also did not lose any salary. Any player who was medically cleared could also decline to participate, but as a result lost their corresponding paychecks.

Phase 1 of the plan ran from June 16 to 22, consisting of players traveling back to the home cities of their respective teams. In Phase 2 from June 23 to 30, [COVID-19 tests](/wiki/COVID-19_testing) began being administered to players every other day. In Phase 3 from July 1 to 11, mandatory individual workouts were conducted at team facilities, but group workouts were prohibited.

Phase 4 ran from July 7 to 21, consisting of the teams traveling to Disney World and conducting practices. Any player who tested positive in the previous phases could not travel until he was medically cleared to do so. Once teams arrive in Orlando, players and staff were isolated in their rooms, requiring to pass two [polymerase chain reaction](/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction) (PCR) tests 24 hours apart before being let out of this quarantine. They were still regularly tested for COVID-19 afterwards. A player who tested positive would be isolated and re-tested in case of a false positive; if COVID-19 was definitely confirmed, he would be quarantined for at least 14 days to recover.

Players and staff were not permitted into another's room, nor were they be able to socialize with players on other teams staying at a different hotel building. They had access to food and recreational activities within their hotel's bubble, but they had to wear masks indoors except when eating. Anybody who left the bubble without prior approval had to be quarantined for at least 10 days.

During Phase 5 from July 22 to 29, teams played three scrimmages against the other teams staying at the same hotel. During Phase 6, when the regular-season seeding games and playoffs were under way and teams began to be eliminated from contention, players and staff on those clubs had to pass one final COVID-19 test before they could leave Disney World.

The NBA also set up an anonymous hotline for players to report protocol violations while in the bubble.

## Arenas

- This was the [Golden State Warriors](/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors)' first season at the new [Chase Center](/wiki/Chase_Center) in [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco) after playing at [Oracle Arena](/wiki/Oracle_Arena) in [Oakland](/wiki/Oakland,_California) from 1971 to 2019. The Warriors played their first game there in a preseason game against the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) on October 5, 2019. They played their first regular season game there against the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers) on October 24, 2019, where they lost 141–122.
- The [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers)' home arena, formerly known as Quicken Loans Arena, was renamed [Rocket Mortgage FieldHouse](/wiki/Rocket_Mortgage_FieldHouse) on April 9, 2019.

## Media

This was the fourth year of a nine-year deal with [ABC](/wiki/NBA_on_ABC), [ESPN](/wiki/NBA_on_ESPN), [TNT](/wiki/NBA_on_TNT) and [NBA TV](/wiki/NBA_TV).

Under an agreement with the [U.S. Department of Justice](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice) regarding [Disney's acquisition of 21st Century Fox](/wiki/Proposed_acquisition_of_21st_Century_Fox_by_Disney), the [Fox Sports Regional Networks](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Networks) were required to be sold off to third parties by June 18, 2019. Fox also invoked a clause to give [Yankee Global Enterprises](/wiki/Yankee_Global_Enterprises) the rights to buy their stake back in the [YES Network](/wiki/YES_Network), which aired the local broadcasts to the NBA's Brooklyn Nets. Including YES, the Fox Sports Regional Networks held the local TV rights to a combined total of 44 NHL, NBA, and MLB teams. On March 8, YES was sold to a consortium including Yankee Global Enterprises, [Amazon](/wiki/Amazon_(company)), and [Sinclair Broadcast Group](/wiki/Sinclair_Broadcast_Group) for $3.5 billion. Then on May 3, Sinclair and [Entertainment Studios](/wiki/Entertainment_Studios) agreed to purchase the rest of the Fox Sports Regional Networks. The networks continued to temporarily use the Fox Sports name under a transitional license agreement while Sinclair explored re-branding options.

- On December 10, 2019, Vince Carter became the fifth player in NBA history to play at least 1,500 games.
- On December 14, 2019, [James Harden](/wiki/James_Harden) became the first player to record back-to-back 50-point games with more than 10 three-pointers
- On December 28, 2019, [Jrue Holiday](/wiki/Jrue_Holiday) of the [New Orleans Pelicans](/wiki/New_Orleans_Pelicans), in addition to [Justin Holiday](/wiki/Justin_Holiday) and [Aaron Holiday](/wiki/Aaron_Holiday) of the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers), became the first three brothers to play in the same NBA game.
- On December 29, 2019, LeBron James became the first player in NBA history to record at least 30,000 points, 9,000 rebounds and 9,000 assists.

For the remainder of the season, all thirty teams wore a black stripe of fabric on the left side of their jerseys in memory of Stern.
- On January 4, 2020, Vince Carter became the only player in NBA history to have played in four different [decades](/wiki/Decades).
- On January 13, 2020, [Shai Gilgeous-Alexander](/wiki/Shai_Gilgeous-Alexander) of the [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) became the youngest player in NBA history to record a 20-rebound triple-double at 21 years and 185 days old.
- On January 15, 2020, [Chandler Parsons](/wiki/Chandler_Parsons) of the Atlanta Hawks was hit by a drunk driver, suffering potentially career-ending injuries.
- On January 17, 2020, Carmelo Anthony of the Portland Trail Blazers became the 18th player in NBA history to reach 26,000 points.
- On January 20, 2020, [Russell Westbrook](/wiki/Russell_Westbrook) of the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) became the second player to record a triple-double against all 30 NBA teams.
- On January 22, 2020, Vince Carter moved past [Alex English](/wiki/Alex_English) for 19th on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On January 25, 2020, LeBron James moved past Kobe Bryant for 3rd on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On January 26, 2020, a day after being passed by LeBron James for 3rd on the NBA's all-time scoring list, [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) and his daughter Gianna [died in a helicopter crash](/wiki/2020_Calabasas_helicopter_crash) in [Calabasas, California](/wiki/Calabasas,_California). During the first minute of each game for this day (outside of the first matchup of the day between the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Denver_Nuggets_season) and [Houston Rockets](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Houston_Rockets_season), which tipped off just minutes after news of the tragedy had broken), players paid tribute by dribbling through the 24-second shot clock violation and the 8-second backcourt violation, referencing the two numbers Bryant wore during his career. In addition to this, the [2020 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2020_NBA_All-Star_Game) was played with jersey numbers 24 and 2, the latter to pay tribute to Gianna.
- On January 27, the NBA announced that they would postpone the January 28 game between the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) and [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Clippers_season) at [Staples Center](/wiki/Staples_Center) out of respect to Bryant, marking the first time an NBA game was postponed for any reason since the [2013 Boston Marathon bombing](/wiki/2013_Boston_Marathon_attack) led to the postponement of a Celtics game. The game was later originally rescheduled for April 9, but was eventually played as the first game of the NBA restart on July 30 following the suspension of play due to the [coronavirus pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).
- On January 29, 2020, Carmelo Anthony moved past [Kevin Garnett](/wiki/Kevin_Garnett) for 17th on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On January 31, 2020, Vince Carter moved up to third place in the NBA all-time games played list with 1,523 passing [Dirk Nowitzki](/wiki/Dirk_Nowitzki) in the process.
- On February 23, 2020, the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) clinched the earliest playoff berth with their 137–134 win over the [Washington Wizards](/wiki/Washington_Wizards).
- On March 1, 2020, [Shake Milton](/wiki/Shake_Milton) tied the record for most consecutive 3-point field goals made (13) over a span of three games; [Brent Price](/wiki/Brent_Price) and [Terry Mills](/wiki/Terry_Mills_(basketball)) did as well
- On March 11, 2020, the league [suspended the season indefinitely](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2019%E2%80%9320_NBA_season) after [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz) [center](/wiki/Center_(basketball)) [Rudy Gobert](/wiki/Rudy_Gobert) tested positive for [COVID-19](/wiki/COVID-19), just hours after the [WHO](/wiki/WHO) declared the disease a [pandemic](/wiki/Pandemic) on the same day. Gobert's positive test causes a massive ripple effect, causing a shutdown of the vast majority of the sports world within five days. Vince Carter played in what became his final game, hitting a three-pointer as his final shot in a 136–131 overtime loss to the New York Knicks.
- On June 4, the NBA Board of Governors approved 29–1 (with the lone dissenter being the [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers)) resuming the 2019–20 season in Orlando, Florida at Walt Disney World, after prior consideration of [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas) and [Houston](/wiki/Houston) as potential spots.
- On June 16, 2020, the NBA released a medical protocol to be used during the season restart in the bubble to ensure the health and safety of players, coaches, officials, and staff.
- On June 25, 2020, Vince Carter officially announced his retirement after 22 seasons and four separate decades in the NBA. He is the only player to accomplish both these feats.
- On July 30, 2020, the regular season resumed in the [NBA bubble](/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble).
- On August 8, 2020, Luka Dončić recorded his 17th [triple-double](/wiki/Triple-double) of the season, clinching his spot as the youngest player to ever lead the NBA in triple-doubles in a season.
- On August 10, 2020, Carmelo Anthony moved past [John Havlicek](/wiki/John_Havlicek) and [Paul Pierce](/wiki/Paul_Pierce), respectively, for 16th and 15th on the NBA all-time scoring list.
- On August 13, 2020, the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs) were eliminated from playoff contention, ending an NBA record-tying 22-year streak.
- On August 17, 2020, [Donovan Mitchell](/wiki/Donovan_Mitchell) scored 57 points, the third highest in NBA single-game playoff history, against the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) in game 1 of their playoff series.
- On August 23, 2020, Luka Dončić's performance in game 4 of the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks) playoff series against the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers), including 43 points, 17 rebounds, 13 assists, and a game-winning buzzer beater, broke a number of records. He became the youngest player to hit a game-winning buzzer beater in the playoffs, one of only three players to make 40-plus points, 15-plus rebounds, and 10-plus assists in a playoff game, one of only five players to make 40-plus points including a buzzer beater in a playoff game, first Dallas Maverick to have a triple-double in the playoffs, among others.
- On August 23, 2020, Donovan Mitchell scored 51 points and [Jamal Murray](/wiki/Jamal_Murray) scored 50 points in a playoff game between the [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz) and the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets), the first time in NBA history that two players scored 50 points in a playoff game.
- On August 26, 2020, the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) decided to boycott Game 5 of their First Round series against the [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic) in order to protest the [shooting of Jacob Blake](/wiki/Shooting_of_Jacob_Blake) a few days prior. Similar decisions by the [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder), [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets), [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers), and [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) came shortly after.
- On September 15, 2020, the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) defeated the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers) in the Western Conference semifinals after initially trailing in the series 3–1. This marked the first time that a team overcame consecutive 3–1 series deficits in a single playoff run, as the Nuggets had previously overcome a 3–1 series deficit to win their first round series against the [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz).
- On October 11, 2020, [Talen Horton-Tucker](/wiki/Talen_Horton-Tucker) became the second-youngest NBA player to win an [NBA championship](/wiki/NBA_championship) after the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) beat the [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat) 106–93 to win the [2020 NBA Finals](/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals).
- [Tony Snell](/wiki/Tony_Snell_(basketball)) had the first [50-50-100](/wiki/50-40-90_club) season (min. 100 attempts) in NBA history
- [James Harden](/wiki/James_Harden) became the third player, after [Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan) and [Allen Iverson](/wiki/Allen_Iverson), to lead the league in total points and total steals in the same season

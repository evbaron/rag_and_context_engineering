# 1983 NBA Finals

The **1983 NBA World Championship Series**, also known as **Showdown '83**, was the championship round of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA)'s [1982–83 season](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_NBA_season), and the culmination of the [season's playoffs](/wiki/1983_NBA_Playoffs). It was the last NBA Championship Series completed before June 1. The [Eastern Conference](/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Philadelphia_76ers_season) swept the [Western Conference](/wiki/Western_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) to win their third NBA championship. After their previous [four final meetings](/wiki/List_of_NBA_champions#Frequent_matchups), the 76ers returned the favor.

76ers center [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone) was named the [NBA Finals Most Valuable Player](/wiki/Bill_Russell_NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) (MVP). This, along with the [1989 NBA Finals](/wiki/1989_NBA_Finals), were the only two NBA championships of the 1980s not to be won by either the Lakers or the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics); every NBA Finals of that decade featured either the Lakers or Celtics, and sometimes both ([1984](/wiki/1984_NBA_Finals), [1985](/wiki/1985_NBA_Finals), [1987](/wiki/1987_NBA_Finals)). Coincidentally, the Lakers were also swept in the 1989 NBA Finals, that time by the [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons).

## Background

### Philadelphia 76ers

Main article: [1982–83 Philadelphia 76ers season](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Philadelphia_76ers_season)

The 76ers lost their first two Finals meetings with the Lakers in [1980](/wiki/1980_NBA_Finals) and [1982](/wiki/1982_NBA_Finals). While [Julius Erving](/wiki/Julius_Erving) played superbly in both series, their frontcourt of [Darryl Dawkins](/wiki/Darryl_Dawkins), [Caldwell Jones](/wiki/Caldwell_Jones), and [Bobby Jones](/wiki/Bobby_Jones_(basketball,_born_1951)) couldn't neutralize [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar). So in the off-season, the 76ers acquired [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone) from the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Houston_Rockets_season) in an effort to counter Abdul-Jabbar, in addition to providing some offense and rebounding to the team. They parted ways with Caldwell Jones, Dawkins, and [Lionel Hollins](/wiki/Lionel_Hollins) before the season, while giving greater responsibility to high-scoring guard [Andrew Toney](/wiki/Andrew_Toney) and backup playmaker [Clint Richardson](/wiki/Clint_Richardson), and adding forwards [Clemon Johnson](/wiki/Clemon_Johnson) and rookie [Marc Iavaroni](/wiki/Marc_Iavaroni).

Malone's acquisition paid dividends, as the 76ers won 65 games in the [1982–83 NBA season](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_NBA_season). Prior to the [playoffs](/wiki/1983_NBA_Playoffs), Malone predicted the team would win in four games in each of the three rounds, ending it with the statement *Fo, Fo, Fo*. The 76ers would steamroll through the playoffs, sweeping the [New York Knicks](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_New_York_Knicks_season) 4–0 in the conference semifinals, before overcoming the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Milwaukee_Bucks_season) in five games.

### Los Angeles Lakers

Main article: [1982–83 Los Angeles Lakers season](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season)

The Lakers earned the top pick of the [1982 NBA draft](/wiki/1982_NBA_draft), becoming the only defending champion to earn the top overall pick in the same season. This was because [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season) owner [Ted Stepien](/wiki/Ted_Stepien) traded their first round pick and [Butch Lee](/wiki/Butch_Lee) to the Lakers for [Don Ford](/wiki/Don_Ford) and draft pick [Chad Kinch](/wiki/Chad_Kinch) [three years earlier](/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_NBA_season). Because the Cavaliers earned the worst record at 15–67, they would have earned the top overall pick via a coin toss with the 17–65 [San Diego Clippers](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_San_Diego_Clippers_season). Instead, the Lakers would earn the top pick and ultimately selected future [Hall of Famer](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame) [James Worthy](/wiki/James_Worthy) first overall.

The Lakers won 58 games the next season. Worthy was a strong contender for [Rookie of the Year](/wiki/NBA_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) when he broke his leg late in the season, therefore missing the rest of the season and the playoffs. Despite Worthy's absence, Magic Johnson, already a top-level guard, and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, 35 and still in his prime, led the Lakers back to the Finals. They beat the [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Portland_Trail_Blazers_season) 4–1 and the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_San_Antonio_Spurs_season) 4–2 in the second and third playoff rounds, respectively.

[Julius Erving](/wiki/Julius_Erving)'s famous “Rock The Baby” cradle [dunk](/wiki/Posterized) on [Michael Cooper](/wiki/Michael_Cooper) occurred during the Lakers versus 76ers matchup on January 5, 1983 at [The Spectrum](/wiki/Spectrum_(arena)). The dunk put the 76ers up by 4 with about a minute and a half left in the game. The Sixers won the game, 122–120, in overtime, sweeping the regular season series versus the Lakers, which is what also occurred during the 1983 Finals.

The final piece of the [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers)' championship puzzle was completed before the 1982–83 season when they acquired center [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone) from the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets). They went on to capture their third NBA championship as they won 65 games, and stormed through the [playoffs](/wiki/1983_NBA_Playoffs), first sweeping the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks), and then beating the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) in five games. They finally finished it off with a four-game sweep of the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers), who had defeated them the season before.

Said head coach [Billy Cunningham](/wiki/Billy_Cunningham), *"The difference from last year was Moses."* Malone was named MVP of the 1983 Finals, as well as league MVP for the third time in his career. The 76ers completed one of the most dominating playoff runs in league history with a 12–1 mark after league and NBA Finals MVP Moses promised *"Fo', fo', fo"* (as in *"four, four, four"*—four wins to win round 1, four wins to win round 2, etc.), but it actually wound up as *"Fo', fi', fo."* (four, five, four). The 76ers were also led by [Julius Erving](/wiki/Julius_Erving), [Maurice Cheeks](/wiki/Maurice_Cheeks), [Andrew Toney](/wiki/Andrew_Toney), and [Bobby Jones](/wiki/Bobby_Jones_(basketball,_born_1951)).

With 59 seconds to go in Game 4, it was Erving who made a three-point play to hold the lead for good, crushing the Lakers in a four-game sweep and ending the last NBA Finals to end before June.

Until the [Phillies](/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies) won the [2008 World Series](/wiki/2008_World_Series) over the [Tampa Bay Rays](/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays) 4–1, this was the last time a team from Philadelphia in the [Big Four leagues](/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) won their league's championship.

## Television coverage

The 1983 NBA Finals was broadcast by [CBS](/wiki/NBA_on_CBS). [Dick Stockton](/wiki/Dick_Stockton) and [Bill Russell](/wiki/Bill_Russell) were the commentators and [Brent Musburger](/wiki/Brent_Musburger) was the host, with [Kevin Loughery](/wiki/Kevin_Loughery) as a pre-game, halftime and post-game analyst. It also introduced a new theme music (composed by Allyson Bellink) for the [CBS Sports](/wiki/CBS_Sports) coverage of the NBA, used an introduction of the NBA arenas (similar to the [Boston Garden](/wiki/Boston_Garden)) until the [1989 playoffs](/wiki/1989_NBA_Playoffs) and later revived the second theme beginning in the [1989 Finals](/wiki/1989_NBA_Finals).

Russell departed CBS following the series, and was replaced by former Celtics teammate [Tom Heinsohn](/wiki/Tom_Heinsohn). Russell would later resurface as a color analyst on [TBS](/wiki/NBA_on_TBS) until 1987.

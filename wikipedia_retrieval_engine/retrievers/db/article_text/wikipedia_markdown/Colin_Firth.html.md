**Colin Andrew Firth** (born 10 September 1960) is an English actor and producer. He is the recipient of [a number of awards and honours](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Colin_Firth), including an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award), two [British Academy Film Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award), and a [Volpi Cup](/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actor) as well as nominations for two [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards) and three [British Academy Television Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Awards). In 2011, Firth was appointed a [CBE](/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) for his services to drama, and appeared in *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* magazine's [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100).

Identified in the mid-1980s with the "[Brit Pack](/wiki/Brit_Pack_(actors))" of rising young British actors, he had leading roles in *[A Month in the Country](/wiki/A_Month_in_the_Country_(film))* (1987), *[Tumbledown](/wiki/Tumbledown)* (1988) and *[Valmont](/wiki/Valmont_(film))* (1989). His portrayal of [Mr. Darcy](/wiki/Mr._Darcy) in the [1995 television adaptation](/wiki/Pride_and_Prejudice_(1995_TV_series)) of [Jane Austen](/wiki/Jane_Austen)'s *[Pride and Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_and_Prejudice)* led to widespread attention and roles in more prominent films such as *[The English Patient](/wiki/The_English_Patient_(film))* (1996), *[Shakespeare in Love](/wiki/Shakespeare_in_Love)* (1998), *[The Importance of Being Earnest](/wiki/The_Importance_of_Being_Earnest_(2002_film))* (2002), and *[Love Actually](/wiki/Love_Actually)* (2003). He starred as Mark Darcy in the [*Bridget Jones* film series](/wiki/Bridget_Jones_(film_series)) (2001–2025), and also acted in the musical comedy *[Mamma Mia!](/wiki/Mamma_Mia!_(film))* (2008) and [its 2018 sequel](/wiki/Mamma_Mia!_Here_We_Go_Again!).

Firth won the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) for his portrayal of [King George VI](/wiki/King_George_VI) in the historical drama *[The King's Speech](/wiki/The_King%27s_Speech)* (2010). He was previously Oscar-nominated for playing a grieving gay man in the romantic drama *[A Single Man](/wiki/A_Single_Man)* (2009), which earned him the [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor) and the [Volpi Cup for Best Actor](/wiki/Volpi_Cup_for_Best_Actor). He subsequently played [secret agent](/wiki/Secret_agent) [Harry Hart](/wiki/Agent_Galahad) in *[Kingsman: The Secret Service](/wiki/Kingsman:_The_Secret_Service)* (2014) and [its 2017 sequel](/wiki/Kingsman:_The_Golden_Circle). He also performed in films such as *[Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy](/wiki/Tinker_Tailor_Soldier_Spy_(film))* (2011), *[Mary Poppins Returns](/wiki/Mary_Poppins_Returns)* (2018), *[1917](/wiki/1917_(2019_film))* (2019), *[The Secret Garden](/wiki/The_Secret_Garden_(2020_film))*, *[Supernova](/wiki/Supernova_(2020_film))* (both 2020), and *[Operation Mincemeat](/wiki/Operation_Mincemeat_(film))* (2021). For his roles on television, he received [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nominations for his portrayals of Dr. [Wilhelm Stuckart](/wiki/Wilhelm_Stuckart) in the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) film *[Conspiracy](/wiki/Conspiracy_(2001_film))* (2001), and [Michael Peterson](/wiki/Michael_Peterson_trial) in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) limited series *[The Staircase](/wiki/The_Staircase_(American_miniseries))* (2022).

In 2012, he founded the production company Raindog Films, under which he served as a producer for *[Eye in the Sky](/wiki/Eye_in_the_Sky_(2015_film))* (2015) and *[Loving](/wiki/Loving_(2016_film))* (2016). His films have grossed more than $3 billion from 42 releases worldwide. Firth has campaigned for the rights of Indigenous people and is a member of [Survival International](/wiki/Survival_International). He has also campaigned on issues of asylum seekers, refugees' rights and the environment. He commissioned and co-authored a scientific paper on a study of the differences in brain structure between people of differing political orientations.

## Early life and education

Colin Andrew Firth was born on 10 September 1960 in the village of [Grayshott, Hampshire](/wiki/Grayshott), to parents who were academics and teachers. His mother, Shirley Jean (*née* Rolles), was a [comparative religion](/wiki/Comparative_religion) lecturer at King Alfred's College (now the [University of Winchester](/wiki/University_of_Winchester)); and his father, David Norman Lewis Firth, was a history lecturer at King Alfred's and education officer for the [Nigerian government](/wiki/Nigerian_government). Firth is the eldest of three children; his sister [Kate](/wiki/Kate_Firth) is an actress and voice coach, and his brother [Jonathan](/wiki/Jonathan_Firth) is also an actor. His maternal grandparents were [Congregationalist](/wiki/Congregationalist) ministers and his paternal grandfather was an [Anglican](/wiki/Anglican) priest. They did overseas [missionary](/wiki/Missionary) work, and both of his parents were born and spent part of their childhoods in [India](/wiki/India).

As a child, Firth frequently travelled due to his parents' work, spending some years in [Nigeria](/wiki/Nigeria). He also lived in [St. Louis](/wiki/St._Louis), [Missouri](/wiki/Missouri), when he was 11, which he has described as "a difficult time". On returning to England, he attended the Montgomery of Alamein Secondary School (now [Kings' School, Winchester](/wiki/Kings%27_School,_Winchester)), which at the time was a state [comprehensive school](/wiki/Comprehensive_school) in [Winchester, Hampshire](/wiki/Winchester,_Hampshire). He was still an outsider and the target of [bullying](/wiki/Bullying). To counter this, he adopted the local working-class Hampshire accent and copied his schoolmates' lack of interest in schoolwork.

Firth began attending drama workshops at age 10, and by 14 had decided to be a professional actor. Until [further education](/wiki/Further_education), he was not academically inclined, later saying in an interview, "I didn't like school. I just thought it was boring and mediocre and nothing they taught me seemed to be of any interest at all." However, at [Barton Peveril Sixth Form College](/wiki/Barton_Peveril_Sixth_Form_College) in [Eastleigh](/wiki/Eastleigh), he was imbued with a love of [English literature](/wiki/English_literature) by an enthusiastic teacher, Penny Edwards, and has said that his two years there were among the happiest of his life.

After his [sixth form](/wiki/Sixth_form) years, Firth moved to London and joined the [National Youth Theatre](/wiki/National_Youth_Theatre), where he made many contacts and got a job in the [wardrobe department](/wiki/Wardrobe_supervisor) at the [National Theatre](/wiki/TreeHouse_School). He subsequently studied at [Drama Centre London](/wiki/Drama_Centre_London).

## Career

### 1983–1995: Early work and breakthrough

Firth played [Hamlet](/wiki/Prince_Hamlet) in the Drama Centre end-of-year production, and in 1984, Firth made his film debut as Tommy Judd, Guy Bennett's [straight](/wiki/Heterosexuality), [Marxist](/wiki/Marxist) school friend in the [screen adaptation of the play Another Country](/wiki/Another_Country_(1984_film)) (with [Rupert Everett](/wiki/Rupert_Everett) as Guy Bennett). It was the start of a longstanding public feud between Firth and Everett, which was eventually resolved. He starred with Sir [Laurence Olivier](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier) in *[Lost Empires](/wiki/Lost_Empires)* (1986), a TV adaptation of [J. B. Priestley](/wiki/J._B._Priestley)'s novel.

In 1987, Firth and other up-and-coming British actors such as [Tim Roth](/wiki/Tim_Roth), [Bruce Payne](/wiki/Bruce_Payne) and [Paul McGann](/wiki/Paul_McGann) were dubbed the '[Brit Pack](/wiki/Brit_Pack_(actors))'. That year, he appeared with [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh) in the film version of [J. L. Carr](/wiki/J._L._Carr)'s *[A Month in the Country](/wiki/A_Month_in_the_Country_(film))*. Sheila Johnston observed a theme in his early work of playing those traumatised by war. He portrayed real-life British soldier [Robert Lawrence](/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(British_Army_officer)) [MC](/wiki/Military_Cross) in the 1988 BBC dramatisation *[Tumbledown](/wiki/Tumbledown)*. Lawrence was severely injured at the [Battle of Mount Tumbledown](/wiki/Battle_of_Mount_Tumbledown) during the [Falklands War](/wiki/Falklands_War), and the film details his struggles to adjust to his disability whilst confronted with indifference from the government and public. It attracted controversy at the time, with criticism coming from left and right sides of the political spectrum. Despite this, the performance brought Firth a Royal TV Society Best Actor Award, and a nomination for the 1989 [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA) [Television Award](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Awards). In 1989, he played the title role in [Miloš Forman](/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Forman)'s *[Valmont](/wiki/Valmont_(film))*, based on *[Les Liaisons dangereuses](/wiki/Les_Liaisons_dangereuses)*. Released just a year after *[Dangerous Liaisons](/wiki/Dangerous_Liaisons)*, it did not make a big impact in comparison. That year he also played a paranoid, socially awkward character in the [Argentinian](/wiki/Argentinian) psychological thriller *[Apartment Zero](/wiki/Apartment_Zero)*.

Firth finally became a British household name through his role as the aloof, haughty aristocrat [Mr. Darcy](/wiki/Mr._Darcy) in the 1995 [BBC](/wiki/BBC) television adaptation of [Jane Austen](/wiki/Jane_Austen)'s *[Pride and Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_and_Prejudice_(1995_TV_series))*. Producer Sue Birtwistle's first choice for the part, he was eventually persuaded to take it despite his unfamiliarity with Austen's writing. He and co-star [Jennifer Ehle](/wiki/Jennifer_Ehle) began a romantic relationship during the filming, which received media attention only after their separation. Sheila Johnston wrote that Firth's approach to the part "lent Darcy complex shades of coldness, even caddishness, in the early episodes". The series was an international success and unexpectedly elevated Firth to stardom—in some part due to a scene not from the novel, where he emerges from a lake swim in a wet shirt. Although he did not mind being recognised as "a romantic idol as a Darcy with smouldering sex appeal" in a role that "officially turned him into a heart-throb", he expressed the wish not to be associated with *Pride and Prejudice* forever. He was, therefore, reluctant to accept similar roles and risk becoming [typecast](/wiki/Typecast).

### 1996–2008: Romance and ensemble films

For a time, it did seem as if Mr. Darcy would overshadow the rest of Firth's career, and there were humorous allusions to the role in his next five movies. The most notable was his casting as the love interest [Mark Darcy](/wiki/Mark_Darcy) in the [film adaptation](/wiki/Bridget_Jones%27s_Diary) of *[Bridget Jones's Diary](/wiki/Bridget_Jones%27s_Diary_(novel))*, itself a modern-day retelling of *Pride and Prejudice*. Firth accepted the part as he saw it as an opportunity to lampoon his Mr. Darcy character. The film was very successful and critically well-liked. A [2004 sequel](/wiki/Bridget_Jones:_The_Edge_of_Reason_(film)) was mostly panned by critics but still financially successful.

Prior to this, Firth had a significant supporting role in *[The English Patient](/wiki/The_English_Patient_(film))* (1996) as the husband of [Kristin Scott Thomas](/wiki/Kristin_Scott_Thomas)'s character, whose jealousy of her adultery leads to tragedy. That year he also played the husband of the character of Kristin's sister, [Serena Scott Thomas](/wiki/Serena_Scott_Thomas), in the television miniseries *[Nostromo](/wiki/Nostromo_(TV_series))*. Of the two he said "Serena was a much more faithful wife."

He next played the lead role as a school teacher and obsessed [Arsenal F.C.](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) [football](/wiki/Association_football) fan in the romantic fictional adaptation *[Fever Pitch](/wiki/Fever_Pitch_(1997_film))* (1997) of [Nick Hornby](/wiki/Nick_Hornby)’s million-selling autobiographical essay *[Fever Pitch: A Fan's Life](/wiki/Fever_Pitch:_A_Fan%27s_Life)*.

He had parts in light [romantic](/wiki/Romance_film) [period pieces](/wiki/Costume_drama) such as *[Shakespeare in Love](/wiki/Shakespeare_in_Love)* (1998), *[Relative Values](/wiki/Relative_Values_(film))* (2000) and *[The Importance of Being Earnest](/wiki/The_Importance_of_Being_Earnest_(2002_film))* (2002). He appeared in several television productions, including *Donovan Quick* (an updated version of *[Don Quixote](/wiki/Don_Quixote)*) (1999), and had a more serious role as Dr. [Wilhelm Stuckart](/wiki/Wilhelm_Stuckart) in *[Conspiracy](/wiki/Conspiracy_(2001_film))* (2001), concerning the Nazi [Wannsee Conference](/wiki/Wannsee_Conference), for which he was nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award).

Firth featured in the ensemble all-star cast of [Richard Curtis](/wiki/Richard_Curtis)'s *[Love Actually](/wiki/Love_Actually)* (2003), another financial success which divided critics. He was also given solo billing as the romantic lead in *[Hope Springs](/wiki/Hope_Springs_(2003_film))*, but it received very poor reviews and made little box-office impact. He starred as [Amanda Bynes](/wiki/Amanda_Bynes)' character's father in the 2003 teen comedy *[What A Girl Wants](/wiki/What_a_Girl_Wants_(film))*, which was based on the play *[The Reluctant Debutante](/wiki/The_Reluctant_Debutante_(play))*. He played painter [Johannes Vermeer](/wiki/Johannes_Vermeer) opposite [Scarlett Johansson](/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson) in the 2003 release *[Girl with a Pearl Earring](/wiki/Girl_with_a_Pearl_Earring_(film))*; some critics praised the film's subtlety and sumptuous visuals, whilst others found it almost restrained, tedious and bereft of emotion. Nevertheless, it received mostly favourable reviews, was moderately successful and earned several awards and nominations.

Firth at the premiere of *[Nanny McPhee](/wiki/Nanny_McPhee)* in 2005

In 2005 Firth appeared in *[Nanny McPhee](/wiki/Nanny_McPhee)* with [Emma Thompson](/wiki/Emma_Thompson), in which he plays a struggling widowed father, it was a rare venture for him into the [fantasy](/wiki/Fantasy_film) genre. He also appeared in *[Where the Truth Lies](/wiki/Where_the_Truth_Lies)*, a return to some of his darker, more intense early roles, that included a notorious scene featuring a [bisexual](/wiki/Bisexual) [orgy](/wiki/Orgy). Sheila Johnston wrote that it "confounded his fans", but nonetheless that his character "draws knowingly on that suave, cultivated persona", which could be traced from Mr. Darcy. Other films from this time included *[Then She Found Me](/wiki/Then_She_Found_Me)* (2007) with [Helen Hunt](/wiki/Helen_Hunt) and *[The Last Legion](/wiki/The_Last_Legion)* (2007) with [Aishwarya Rai](/wiki/Aishwarya_Rai).

In 2008, he played the adult [Blake Morrison](/wiki/Blake_Morrison) reminiscing on his difficult relationship with his ailing father in the film adaptation of Morrison's memoir, *[And When Did You Last See Your Father?](/wiki/And_When_Did_You_Last_See_Your_Father%3F)* It received generally favorable reviews. [Peter Bradshaw](/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) of *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* gave it four out of five stars. [Manohla Dargis](/wiki/Manohla_Dargis) in *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* said: "It's a pleasure to watch Mr. Firth–a supremely controlled actor who makes each developing fissure visible–show the adult Blake coming to terms with his contradictory feelings, letting the love and the hurt pour out of him." [Philip French](/wiki/Philip_French) of *[The Observer](/wiki/The_Observer)* wrote that Firth "[does] quiet agonising to perfection." However, Derek Elley of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* called the film "an unashamed tearjerker that's all wrapping and no center." While he conceded that it was "undeniably effective at a gut level despite its dramatic shortcomings", he added, "Things aren't helped any by Firth's dour perf, as his Blake comes across as a self-centered whiner, a latter-day Me Generation figure who's obsessed with finding problems when there really aren't any."

The film adaptation of *[Mamma Mia!](/wiki/Mamma_Mia!_(film))* (2008) was Firth's first foray into [musicals](/wiki/Musical_film). He described the experience as "a bit nerve-wracking" but believed he got off lightly by being tasked with one of the less demanding songs, *[Our Last Summer](/wiki/Our_Last_Summer)*. *Mamma Mia* became the highest grossing British-made film of all time, taking in over $600 million worldwide. Like *Love Actually*, it polarised critics, with supporters such as *Empire* calling it "cute, clean, camp fun, full of sunshine, and toe tappers", whereas Peter Bradshaw in *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* said the film gave him a "need to vomit". Carrie Rickey in *[The Philadelphia Inquirer](/wiki/The_Philadelphia_Inquirer)* described Firth's performance as "the embodiment of forced mirth." That year, Firth also starred in *[Easy Virtue](/wiki/Easy_Virtue_(2008_film))*, which screened at the Rome Film Festival to excellent reviews. He starred in *[Genova](/wiki/Genova_(2008_film))*, which premiered at the [2008 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2008_Toronto_International_Film_Festival).
In 2009 he appeared in *[A Christmas Carol](/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol_(2009_film))*, an adaptation of [Charles Dickens](/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' novel, using the [performance capture](/wiki/Performance_capture) procedure, playing Scrooge's optimistic nephew Fred, with [Jim Carrey](/wiki/Jim_Carrey), [Gary Oldman](/wiki/Gary_Oldman), [Robin Wright Penn](/wiki/Robin_Wright), [Bob Hoskins](/wiki/Bob_Hoskins) and [Cary Elwes](/wiki/Cary_Elwes).

### 2009–2011: Critical success

Firth with [Helena Bonham Carter](/wiki/Helena_Bonham_Carter) on the set of *[The King's Speech](/wiki/The_King%27s_Speech)* in 2009, his most critically acclaimed role to date

At the [66th Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/66th_Venice_International_Film_Festival) in 2009, Firth received the [Volpi Cup](/wiki/Volpi_Cup) for Best Actor for his role in [Tom Ford](/wiki/Tom_Ford)'s directorial debut *[A Single Man](/wiki/A_Single_Man),* as a college professor grappling with solitude after the death of his longtime partner. His performance earned him career-best reviews and Academy Award, Golden Globe, Screen Actors' Guild, BAFTA, and BFCA nominations; he won the BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role in February 2010.

Firth starred in the 2010 film *[The King's Speech](/wiki/The_King%27s_Speech)* as [Prince Albert, Duke of York/King George VI](/wiki/George_VI), detailing his efforts to overcome his speech impediment while becoming monarch of the United Kingdom at the end of 1936. At the [Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival) (TIFF), the film received a standing ovation. The TIFF release of *The King's Speech* fell on Firth's 50th birthday and was called the "best 50th birthday gift". On 16 January 2011, he won a Golden Globe for his performance in *The King's Speech* in the category of [Best Performance by an Actor in a Motion Picture – Drama](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama). The [Screen Actors Guild](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild) recognised him with the award for [Best Male Actor](/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) on 30 January 2011.
In February 2011, he won his second consecutive [Best Actor Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) at the [2011 BAFTA awards](/wiki/64th_British_Academy_Film_Awards), and received an [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) in on 27 February 2011. The film grossed $414,211,549 worldwide.

Firth appeared as senior British secret agent [Bill Haydon](/wiki/Bill_Haydon) in the 2011 adaptation of the [John le Carré](/wiki/John_le_Carr%C3%A9) novel *[Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy](/wiki/Tinker_Tailor_Soldier_Spy_(film))*, directed by [Tomas Alfredson](/wiki/Tomas_Alfredson) and co-starring [Gary Oldman](/wiki/Gary_Oldman), [Benedict Cumberbatch](/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch), [Tom Hardy](/wiki/Tom_Hardy), [Mark Strong](/wiki/Mark_Strong) and [John Hurt](/wiki/John_Hurt). It gathered mostly excellent reviews. *The Independent* described Firth's performance as "suavely arrogant" and praised the film. Deborah Young in *The Hollywood Reporter* thought Firth got "all the best dialogue", which he delivered "sardonically". Leslie Felperin in *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* wrote that all the actors brought their "A game" and Firth was in "particularly [choleric](/wiki/Choleric), amusing form."

### 2012–present: Established actor

Firth receiving a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 2011

In May 2011, Firth began filming *[Gambit](/wiki/Gambit_(2012_film))* — a remake of [Ronald Neame](/wiki/Ronald_Neame)'s [1960s crime caper](/wiki/Gambit_(1966_film)), in the part originally played by [Michael Caine](/wiki/Michael_Caine). It was released in the UK in November 2012 and was a financial and critical failure. *[Empire](/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine))*'s [Kim Newman](/wiki/Kim_Newman) wrote, "Firth starts out homaging Caine with his [horn-rimmed](/wiki/Horn-rimmed_glasses) cool but soon defaults to his usual repressed British cold mode", whilst *Time Out London* called his a "likeable performance", although criticised the film overall. Stephen Dalton in *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* said, "To his credit, Firth keeps his performance grounded in downbeat realism while all around are wildly mugging in desperate pursuit of thin, forced laughs. In 2012, Firth co-founded Raindog Films with British music industry executive and entrepreneur [Ged Doherty](/wiki/Ged_Doherty). Its first feature, *[Eye in the Sky](/wiki/Eye_in_the_Sky_(2015_film))*, for which Firth was co-producer, was released in April 2016.

In May 2013, it was announced that Firth had signed to co-star with [Emma Stone](/wiki/Emma_Stone) in [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen)'s romantic comedy *[Magic in the Moonlight](/wiki/Magic_in_the_Moonlight)*, set in the 1920s and shot on the [French Riviera](/wiki/French_Riviera). In 2014, he did his first turn as [Harry Hart / Agent Galahad](/wiki/Agent_Galahad) in the spy action film *[Kingsman: The Secret Service](/wiki/Kingsman:_The_Secret_Service)*, which grossed $414.4 million against an $81 million budget. Firth had been announced to voice [Paddington Bear](/wiki/Paddington_Bear) for the film *[Paddington](/wiki/Paddington_(film))*; however, he announced his withdrawal on 17 June 2014, saying: "It's been bittersweet to see this delightful creature take shape and come to the sad realization that he simply doesn't have my voice".

In June 2015, he began filming the story of amateur yachtsman [Donald Crowhurst](/wiki/Donald_Crowhurst) in *[The Mercy](/wiki/The_Mercy)*, alongside [Rachel Weisz](/wiki/Rachel_Weisz), [David Thewlis](/wiki/David_Thewlis) and [Jonathan Bailey](/wiki/Jonathan_Bailey). In 2016, Firth reprised his popular role as Mark Darcy in *[Bridget Jones's Baby](/wiki/Bridget_Jones%27s_Baby)*, which fared much better with audiences and critics than the second in the series (*Bridget Jones: Edge of Reason*). He portrayed American book editor [Max Perkins](/wiki/Max_Perkins) in *[Genius](/wiki/Genius_(2016_film))*, co-starring [Jude Law](/wiki/Jude_Law) as author [Thomas Wolfe](/wiki/Thomas_Wolfe) and based on [A. Scott Berg](/wiki/A._Scott_Berg)'s biography *Max Perkins: Editor of Genius*. In 2016 he began filming for [Rupert Everett](/wiki/Rupert_Everett)'s directorial debut *[The Happy Prince](/wiki/The_Happy_Prince_(2018_film))*, a biopic of [Oscar Wilde](/wiki/Oscar_Wilde), playing Wilde's friend [Reginald "Reggie" Turner](/wiki/Reginald_Turner).

In 2017, he reprised his role as Jamie from 2003's *[Love Actually](/wiki/Love_Actually)* in the television short film *[Red Nose Day Actually](/wiki/Red_Nose_Day_Actually)*, by original writer and director [Richard Curtis](/wiki/Richard_Curtis). Also that year, Firth returned as [Harry Hart / Agent Galahad](/wiki/Agent_Galahad) in the sequel *[Kingsman: The Golden Circle](/wiki/Kingsman:_The_Golden_Circle)*. In 2018, Firth reprised his role of Harry Bright in the sequel to *[Mamma Mia!](/wiki/Mamma_Mia!_(film))*, *[Mamma Mia! Here We Go Again](/wiki/Mamma_Mia!_Here_We_Go_Again)*. That year, he also appeared as William Weatherall Wilkins in the musical fantasy film *[Mary Poppins Returns](/wiki/Mary_Poppins_Returns)*, starring [Emily Blunt](/wiki/Emily_Blunt) in the title role. He also played British naval commander [David Russell](/wiki/David_Russell_(Royal_Navy_officer)) in [Thomas Vinterberg](/wiki/Thomas_Vinterberg)'s *[Kursk](/wiki/Kursk_(film))*, a film about the true story of the [2000 Kursk submarine disaster](/wiki/Kursk_submarine_disaster), in which he starred alongside [Matthias Schoenaerts](/wiki/Matthias_Schoenaerts). In 2019, he had a cameo as British General Erinmore in [Sam Mendes](/wiki/Sam_Mendes)'s World War I film *[1917](/wiki/1917_(2019_film))*. Set in 1947 England, Firth starred with [Julie Walters](/wiki/Julie_Walters) in *[The Secret Garden](/wiki/The_Secret_Garden_(2020_film))* and later in 2020 with [Stanley Tucci](/wiki/Stanley_Tucci) in *[Supernova](/wiki/Supernova_(2020_film))*. In 2021, he starred in romantic drama film *[Mothering Sunday](/wiki/Mothering_Sunday_(film))* directed by Eva Husson. In December 2021, he was cast in Sam Mendes's drama film *[Empire of Light](/wiki/Empire_of_Light)*, starring [Olivia Colman](/wiki/Olivia_Colman). He was seen as Ewen Montagu in *[Operation Mincemeat](/wiki/Operation_Mincemeat_(film))*, in April 2022. Firth returned to television in May 2022 starring as Michael Peterson in the HBO production *[The Staircase](/wiki/The_Staircase_(American_miniseries))*. In 2025, Firth starred in the drama series *[Lockerbie: A Search for Truth](/wiki/Lockerbie:_A_Search_for_Truth)*, portraying [Jim Swire](/wiki/Jim_Swire) whose daughter died on [Pan Am Flight 103](/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_103) while in flight over the Scottish town of [Lockerbie](/wiki/Lockerbie) in December 1988.

## Writing

Firth's first published work, "The Department of Nothing", appeared in *[Speaking with the Angel](/wiki/Speaking_with_the_Angel)* (2000), a collection of short stories edited by [Nick Hornby](/wiki/Nick_Hornby) and published to benefit the [TreeHouse Trust](/wiki/TreeHouse_School) to aid autistic children. He met Hornby during the filming of the original *[Fever Pitch](/wiki/Fever_Pitch_(1997_film))*. He contributed to the book *We Are One: A Celebration of Tribal Peoples* (2009), which explores the cultures, diversity and challenges of Indigenous peoples around the world. It features contributions from many Western writers, including [Laurens van der Post](/wiki/Laurens_van_der_Post), [Noam Chomsky](/wiki/Noam_Chomsky), [Claude Lévi-Strauss](/wiki/Claude_L%C3%A9vi-Strauss); and from Indigenous people such as [Davi Kopenawa Yanomami](/wiki/Davi_Kopenawa_Yanomami) and [Roy Sesana](/wiki/Roy_Sesana). Profits from the book's sale benefit the Indigenous rights organisation [Survival International](/wiki/Survival_International). Firth was an executive producer for the film *[In Prison My Whole Life](/wiki/In_Prison_My_Whole_Life)*, featuring Noam Chomsky and [Angela Davis](/wiki/Angela_Davis). It was selected to the 2007 London Film Festival and the 2008 Sundance Film Festival.

In December 2010, Firth was guest editor on [BBC Radio 4](/wiki/BBC_Radio_4)'s *[Today](/wiki/Today_(BBC_Radio_4))* programme, where he commissioned research to scan the brains of volunteers (mostly university students) to see if there were structural differences that might account for political leanings. The resulting academic paper listed him as an author, along with two [University College London](/wiki/University_College_London) researchers and the science reporter of the BBC Radio 4 *Today* programme. For his contribution, professor [John Jost](/wiki/John_Jost) called Firth a 'scientific ambassador' in the field of political neuroscience. The study suggested that conservatives had more development in the [amygdala](/wiki/Amygdala), and liberals in the [anterior cingulate cortex](/wiki/Anterior_cingulate_cortex).

In 2012, Firth's audiobook recording of [Graham Greene](/wiki/Graham_Greene)'s *[The End of the Affair](/wiki/The_End_of_the_Affair)* was released at [Audible.com](/wiki/Audible.com) and was declared [Audiobook of the Year](/wiki/Audie_Award_for_Audiobook_of_the_Year) at the 2013 [Audie Awards](/wiki/Audie_Awards).

## Activism

Firth with [Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) (and [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama)) at [Winfield House](/wiki/Winfield_House) in London in 2011

Firth has been a longstanding supporter of [Survival International](/wiki/Survival_International), a [non-governmental organisation](/wiki/Non-governmental_organisation) that advocates for the rights of tribal peoples. Speaking in 2001, he said, "My interest in tribal peoples goes back many years ... and I have supported [Survival] ever since." In 2003, during the promotion of *[Love Actually](/wiki/Love_Actually)*, he spoke in defence of the Indigenous people of [Botswana](/wiki/Botswana), condemning the Botswana government's eviction of the [Gana and Gwi people](/wiki/Gana_and_Gwi_people) (San) from the [Central Kalahari Game Reserve](/wiki/Central_Kalahari_Game_Reserve). He said of San, "These people are not the remnants of a past era who need to be brought up to date. Those who are able to continue to live on the land that is rightfully theirs are facing the 21st century with a confidence that many of us in the so-called developed world can only envy." He has also backed a Survival International campaign to press the Brazilian government to take more decisive action in defence of [Awá-Guajá people](/wiki/Aw%C3%A1-Guaj%C3%A1_people), whose land and livelihood is critically threatened by the actions of loggers.

As a supporter of the [Refugee Council](/wiki/Refugee_Council), Firth was involved in a campaign to stop the [deportation](/wiki/Deportation) of a group of 42 Congolese [asylum seekers](/wiki/Asylum_seeker), expressing concerns in open letters to *The Independent* and *The Guardian* that they faced being murdered on their return to the [Democratic Republic of Congo](/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Congo). Firth said: "To me, it's just basic civilisation to help people. I find this incredibly painful to see how we dismiss the most desperate people in our society. It's easily done. It plays to the tabloids, to the [Middle-England](/wiki/Middle_England) [xenophobes](/wiki/Xenophobes). It just makes me furious. And all from a government we once had such high hopes for." Four of the asylum seekers were given last-minute reprieves from deportation.

Firth, along with other celebrities, has been involved in the [Oxfam](/wiki/Oxfam) global campaign [Make Trade Fair](/wiki/Make_Trade_Fair), focusing on trade practices considered especially unfair to third-world producers, including dumping, high import tariffs, and labour rights. He and some collaborators opened Eco, an eco-friendly shop in West London, which offers fair-trade and eco-friendly goods, and expert advice on making spaces more energy efficient. In October 2009, at the [London Film Festival](/wiki/London_Film_Festival), he launched a film and political activism website, Brightwide (since decommissioned), with his wife Livia.

During the [2010 general election](/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election), Firth announced his support for the [Liberal Democrats](/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_(UK)), having previously been a [Labour](/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK)) supporter, citing asylum and refugees' rights as key reasons for the change. In December 2010, he publicly dropped his support of the Liberal Democrats, citing their U-turn on [tuition fees](/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_student_protests), and said that he was currently unaffiliated. He appeared in literature supporting changing the British electoral system from [first-past-the-post](/wiki/First-past-the-post) to [alternative vote](/wiki/Alternative_vote) for electing members of parliament to the [House of Commons](/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom), in the unsuccessful [Alternative Vote referendum](/wiki/2011_United_Kingdom_Alternative_Vote_referendum) in 2011.

In 2009, Firth joined the [10:10](/wiki/10:10) project, supporting the movement calling for people to reduce their carbon footprints. In 2010, he endorsed "Roots & Shoots", an education programme in the UK run by the [Jane Goodall Institute](/wiki/Jane_Goodall_Institute#United_Kingdom).

## Personal life

Firth with Livia Giuggioli in 2011

In 1989, Firth began a relationship with [Meg Tilly](/wiki/Meg_Tilly), his co-star in *Valmont*. Their son, William Joseph Firth, was born in 1990. William is now also an actor, appearing with his father in *[Bridget Jones's Baby](/wiki/Bridget_Jones%27s_Baby)* in 2016. The family moved to the [Lower Mainland](/wiki/Lower_Mainland) of [British Columbia](/wiki/British_Columbia), Canada but Firth and Tilly ultimately broke up in 1994. During the filming of *Pride and Prejudice*, Firth and co-star [Jennifer Ehle](/wiki/Jennifer_Ehle) began a romantic relationship, which received media attention only after their separation.

In 1997, Firth married Italian activist [Livia Giuggioli](/wiki/Livia_Giuggioli_Firth). They have two sons, Luca and Matteo. Firth speaks fluent Italian. The family divided their time between [Wandsworth](/wiki/Wandsworth), in London, and [Umbria](/wiki/Umbria), Italy. They announced their separation in 2019. They had gone through a private separation several years prior, but had reconciled.

Firth was a vocal opponent of the [Brexit](/wiki/Brexit) initiative for the United Kingdom to leave the European Union. Following the referendum's passage, and the ensuing uncertainty over rights of non-EU citizens, he applied for "dual citizenship (British and Italian)" in 2017 to "have the same passports as his wife and children". The Italian [Ministry of the Interior](/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_(Italy)) announced Firth's application had been approved on 22 September 2017. Firth stated regarding the decision that "I have a passionate love of Italy" but "will always be extremely British (you only have to look at or listen to me)."

In 2011, after winning the Academy Award for his portrayal of King George VI in *The King's Speech*, Firth suggested that he may be a [republican](/wiki/Republicanism_in_the_United_Kingdom) (anti-monarchist) in a [CNN](/wiki/CNN) interview with [Piers Morgan](/wiki/Piers_Morgan), saying that voting was "one of his favourite things" and that unelected institutions were "a problem for him".

## Acting credits and accolades

Main articles: [List of Colin Firth performances](/wiki/List_of_Colin_Firth_performances) and [List of awards and nominations received by Colin Firth](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Colin_Firth)

Wax statue of Firth at [Madame Tussauds](/wiki/Madame_Tussauds), London

Firth has received numerous awards, including an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor), an [Actor Award](/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), a [Critics' Choice Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actor), and a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama), all for Best Actor, for his performance as [King George VI](/wiki/King_George_VI) in [Tom Hooper](/wiki/Tom_Hooper)'s historical drama *[The King's Speech](/wiki/The_King%27s_Speech)* (2010).

He received an [honorary doctorate](/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) on 19 October 2007 from the [University of Winchester](/wiki/University_of_Winchester). On 13 January 2011, he was presented with the 2,429th star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame. In April 2011, *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* included him in its list of the World's 100 Most Influential People. He was made a [Freeman](/wiki/Freedom_of_the_City#Freedom_of_the_City_of_London) of the [City of London](/wiki/City_of_London) on 8 March 2012, and was awarded an [honorary fellowship](/wiki/Honorary_fellowship) by the [University of the Arts London](/wiki/University_of_the_Arts_London) in 2012.

Firth was appointed [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (CBE) in the [2011 Birthday Honours](/wiki/2011_Birthday_Honours) for services to drama.

This article is about the American actor. For other people named Jason Alexander, see [Jason Alexander (disambiguation)](/wiki/Jason_Alexander_(disambiguation)).

**Jason Alexander** (born **Jay Scott Greenspan**; September 23, 1959) is an American actor and comedian. Over the course of his career, he has received an [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Award) and a [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) as well as nominations for four [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards). He gained stardom for his role as [George Costanza](/wiki/George_Costanza) in the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) [sitcom](/wiki/Sitcom) *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* (1989–1998), for which he won a [Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) and was nominated for seven consecutive [Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) and four [Golden Globe Awards for Best Supporting Actor in Television](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film).

Alexander made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) debut originating the role of Joe in [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim)'s *[Merrily We Roll Along](/wiki/Merrily_We_Roll_Along_(musical))* in 1981. He remained active on Broadway acting in the musicals *[The Rink](/wiki/The_Rink_(musical))* in 1984 and the [Neil Simon](/wiki/Neil_Simon) play *[Broadway Bound](/wiki/Broadway_Bound)* in 1986. He then starred in *[Jerome Robbins' Broadway](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins%27_Broadway)* in 1989, for which he won the [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical). His subsequent theatre roles include the touring production of *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))* from 2003 to 2004 and replacing [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David) in the original 2015 Broadway production of *[Fish in the Dark](/wiki/Fish_in_the_Dark)*. He has also directed several plays such as the original 2023 Broadway production of *[The Cottage](/wiki/The_Cottage_(play))*.

His film roles include *[Pretty Woman](/wiki/Pretty_Woman)* (1990), *[Jacob's Ladder](/wiki/Jacob%27s_Ladder_(1990_film))* (1990), *[Coneheads](/wiki/Coneheads_(film))* (1993), *[The Paper](/wiki/The_Paper_(film))* (1994), *[Dunston Checks In](/wiki/Dunston_Checks_In)* (1996), *[Shallow Hal](/wiki/Shallow_Hal)* (2001), and *[Hachi: A Dog's Tale](/wiki/Hachi:_A_Dog%27s_Tale)* (2009). He voiced the [gargoyle](/wiki/Gargoyle) Hugo in the Disney film *[The Hunchback of Notre Dame](/wiki/The_Hunchback_of_Notre_Dame_(1996_film))* (1996) and the titular role in *[Duckman](/wiki/Duckman)* (1994–1997). He was the narrator for the documentary film *[Sideshow: Alive on the Inside](/wiki/Sideshow:_Alive_on_the_Inside)* (1999). For his role in *[Dream On](/wiki/Dream_On_(TV_series))* (1994) he was nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series).

## Early life and education

Alexander as a senior at [Livingston High School](/wiki/Livingston_High_School_(New_Jersey)) in 1977

Alexander was born on September 23, 1959, in [Newark, New Jersey](/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey) to a [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish) family, the son of Ruth Minnie (née Simon), a nurse and health care administrator, and Alexander B. Greenspan, an accounting manager. Alexander later borrowed his father's first name to create his stage name, Jason Alexander.

Alexander grew up in [Maplewood](/wiki/Maplewood,_New_Jersey) and [Livingston, New Jersey](/wiki/Livingston,_New_Jersey), and is a 1977 graduate of [Livingston High School](/wiki/Livingston_High_School_(New_Jersey)). Interested in magic from an early age, he initially hoped to be a magician, but while attending a magic camp was told that his hands were too small for card magic. He became interested in theater, eventually realizing, "Wait a minute—the whole thing's an illusion. Nothing up there is real" and that theater itself was "a magic trick". He then decided to pursue a theater career.

After high school, he studied theater at [Boston University](/wiki/Boston_University). He wanted to pursue classical acting, but a professor redirected him toward comedy after noticing his physique, remarking, "I know your heart and soul are [Hamlet](/wiki/Hamlet), but you will never play Hamlet." Alexander left Boston University without a degree after his third year to take a full-time acting job in New York City. The university awarded him an honorary degree in 1995.

## Career

### 1980–1999: Broadway debut and *Seinfeld*

Alexander began his acting career on the New York stage and is an accomplished singer and dancer. Alexander made his film debut in 1981 in the summer camp [slasher film](/wiki/Slasher_film) *[The Burning](/wiki/The_Burning_(1981_film))*. On [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) he appeared in [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim)'s *[Merrily We Roll Along](/wiki/Merrily_We_Roll_Along_(musical))* in 1981, [Kander](/wiki/John_Kander) & [Ebb](/wiki/Fred_Ebb)'s *[The Rink](/wiki/The_Rink_(musical))* in 1984, [Neil Simon](/wiki/Neil_Simon)'s *[Broadway Bound](/wiki/Broadway_Bound)* in 1986, *Accomplice* in 1990, and *[Jerome Robbins' Broadway](/wiki/Jerome_Robbins%27_Broadway)* in 1989, for which he garnered the 1989 [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical). [Frank Rich](/wiki/Frank_Rich) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* wrote of his performance "Jason Alexander, the evening's delightful narrator, accomplishes the seemingly impossible: he banishes the memory of [Zero Mostel](/wiki/Zero_Mostel) from the role of [Pseudolus](/wiki/Pseudolus) in *[A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to the Forum](/wiki/A_Funny_Thing_Happened_on_the_Way_to_the_Forum)*".

In addition to his roles as an insensitive, money-hungry lawyer in *[Pretty Woman](/wiki/Pretty_Woman)* in 1990, Alexander has appeared in *[Jacob's Ladder](/wiki/Jacob%27s_Ladder_(1990_film))* in 1990, *[Coneheads](/wiki/Coneheads_(film))* in 1993, *[The Paper](/wiki/The_Paper_(film))* and *[North](/wiki/North_(1994_film))* in 1994, *[The Last Supper](/wiki/The_Last_Supper_(1995_film))* in 1995, *[Dunston Checks In](/wiki/Dunston_Checks_In)* in 1996, *[Love! Valour! Compassion!](/wiki/Love!_Valour!_Compassion!_(film))* in 1997, *[Denial](/wiki/Denial_(1998_film))* in 1998, and *[Love and Action in Chicago](/wiki/Love_and_Action_in_Chicago_(film))* in 1999. Alexander starred in several commercials during the 1980s. Among them were commercials for [Hershey's Kiss](/wiki/Hershey%27s_Kiss); Delta Gold [potato chips](/wiki/Potato_chips); [Miller Lite](/wiki/Miller_Lite) beer; [McDonald's](/wiki/McDonald%27s) [McDLT](/wiki/McDLT) hamburger; [Pabst Blue Ribbon](/wiki/Pabst_Blue_Ribbon) beer; [Levi's](/wiki/Levi%27s) 501 jeans; [Sony Watchman](/wiki/Sony_Watchman) TV; and [Western Union](/wiki/Western_Union) wire transfer. Before *Seinfeld*, Alexander appeared in commercials for John Deere and McDonald's and in the short-lived [CBS](/wiki/CBS) sitcom *[Everything's Relative](/wiki/Everything%27s_Relative_(1987_TV_series))* (1987).

Alexander is best known as one of the key cast members of the award-winning television sitcom *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)*, where he played the bumbling [George Costanza](/wiki/George_Costanza) ([Jerry Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld)'s character's best friend since childhood). He was nominated for seven [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards) and four [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) for the role, but did not win any, mainly due to his co-star [Michael Richards](/wiki/Michael_Richards) winning for his role as [Cosmo Kramer](/wiki/Cosmo_Kramer). He did, however, win the 1995 [Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award).

Concurrently with his *Seinfeld* role, he had a part in the ABC sitcom *[Dinosaurs](/wiki/Dinosaurs_(TV_series))* as Al "Sexual" Harris (who frequently engaged in [sexual harassment](/wiki/Sexual_harassment)) as well as other characters from 1991 to 1994. For his role in an episode of *[Dream On](/wiki/Dream_On_(TV_series))*, Alexander was nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) in 1994. Alexander voiced the lead character in the animated series *[Duckman](/wiki/Duckman)* (1994–1997) and voiced [Catbert](/wiki/Catbert), the evil director of human resources, in the short-lived animated series *[Dilbert](/wiki/Dilbert_(TV_series))* from 1999 to 2000, based on the then-popular comic strip. In January 1995, he did a commercial for [Rold Gold](/wiki/Rold_Gold) pretzels to be broadcast during the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl). The commercial depicts him with *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)* dog Eddie jumping out of an airplane with a parachute over the stadium. After the commercial, the audience is brought back to a supposedly live feed of the playing field hearing startled sports commentators as Alexander and the dog land in the field to wild applause.

Alexander appeared in the 1995 TV version of the [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) musical *[Bye Bye Birdie](/wiki/Bye_Bye_Birdie_(1995_film))*, as Conrad Birdie's agent, Albert Peterson. He guest-starred in episode 8 of the 1996 variety show *[Muppets Tonight](/wiki/Muppets_Tonight)*. He voiced the [gargoyle](/wiki/Gargoyle) Hugo in [Disney](/wiki/Disney)'s 1996 [animated film](/wiki/Animated_film) *[The Hunchback of Notre Dame](/wiki/The_Hunchback_of_Notre_Dame_(1996_film))* and its direct-to-video sequel, *[The Hunchback of Notre Dame II](/wiki/The_Hunchback_of_Notre_Dame_II)*. Alexander voiced the character [Abis Mal](/wiki/List_of_Aladdin_characters#Abis_Mal) in the 1994 film *[The Return of Jafar](/wiki/The_Return_of_Jafar)* and the 1994-1995 [TV series](/wiki/Aladdin_(animated_TV_series)) based on the 1992 film *[Aladdin](/wiki/Aladdin_(1992_Disney_film))*.

In 1997, he appeared in *[Cinderella](/wiki/Cinderella_(1997_film))*, a remake of *Rodgers and Hammerstein's Cinderella,* alongside [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston), [Brandy Norwood](/wiki/Brandy_Norwood), and [Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg). His other Disney voice work includes *[House of Mouse](/wiki/House_of_Mouse)* in 2001 and the 2012 video game *[Kingdom Hearts 3D: Dream Drop Distance](/wiki/Kingdom_Hearts_3D:_Dream_Drop_Distance)*. He has dabbled in directing, starting with 1996's *[For Better or Worse](/wiki/For_Better_or_Worse_(film))* and 1999's *[Just Looking](/wiki/Just_Looking)*. In 1999, Alexander presided over the *New York Friars Club Roast* event honoring [Jerry Stiller](/wiki/Jerry_Stiller), who played his father on *Seinfeld*; it featured appearances by [Kevin James](/wiki/Kevin_James) and [Patton Oswalt](/wiki/Patton_Oswalt), both Stiller's costars on *[The King of Queens](/wiki/The_King_of_Queens)*. Alexander appeared in the 1999 *[Star Trek: Voyager](/wiki/Star_Trek:_Voyager)* episode "[Think Tank](/wiki/Think_Tank_(Star_Trek:_Voyager))" as Kurros, a genius alien trying to get [Seven of Nine](/wiki/Seven_of_Nine) to serve on his ship.

In 1999, he provided narration for the documentary film *[Sideshow: Alive on the Inside](/wiki/Sideshow:_Alive_on_the_Inside).*

### 2000–2009: Solo-lead sitcoms and return to theater

Despite a successful career in film and stage, Alexander did not repeat his *Seinfeld*-level of success in television. 2001 marked his appearance as inept womanizer Mauricio in *[Shallow Hal](/wiki/Shallow_Hal)* and his first post-*Seinfeld* return to prime-time television: the heavily promoted but short-lived [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) sitcom *[Bob Patterson](/wiki/Bob_Patterson_(TV_series))*, which was canceled after five episodes. Alexander partially blames the show's failure on the country's mood after [9/11](/wiki/September_11_attacks). Alexander made cameo appearances as himself in 2001 in the second season of *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*, and he appeared in the show's seventh season with his three principal *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* co-stars. He was featured in the *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)* 2001 episode "[The One Where Rosita Dies](/wiki/The_One_Where_Rosita_Dies)" as Earl, a suicidal [supply manager](/wiki/Procurement#Roles_in_procurement). [Phoebe](/wiki/Phoebe_Buffay) calls him trying to sell him [toner](/wiki/Toner_(printing)), learns about his problem, and tries to persuade him not to commit suicide. This is referenced in an episode of *[Malcolm in the Middle](/wiki/Malcolm_in_the_Middle)* where Alexander appears as Leonard, a neurotic and critical loner. He describes himself as "free" and says he makes money "selling toner over the phone". Later in the episode, he is repeatedly harassed by a man named George.

He appeared in "[One Night at Mercy](/wiki/One_Night_at_Mercy)", the first episode of the short-lived [2002 revival of *The Twilight Zone*](/wiki/The_Twilight_Zone_(2002_TV_series)), playing [Death](/wiki/Personifications_of_death). He played the toymaker [A.C. Gilbert](/wiki/A.C._Gilbert) in the 2002 film *[The Man Who Saved Christmas](/wiki/The_Man_Who_Saved_Christmas_(film))*. He appeared in [Kentucky Fried Chicken](/wiki/Kentucky_Fried_Chicken) (KFC) commercials in 2002, including one with [Barry Bonds](/wiki/Barry_Bonds) of the [San Francisco Giants](/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) and another with [Trista Rehn](/wiki/Trista_Rehn) of *[The Bachelorette](/wiki/The_Bachelorette_(American_TV_series))*. It was rumored that he quit doing these commercials due to KFC suppliers and [slaughterhouses](/wiki/Slaughterhouse)' alleged [cruelty to animals](/wiki/Cruelty_to_animals), but he denied that in an interview with *[Adweek](/wiki/Adweek)*, saying, "That's [PETA](/wiki/PETA) bullcrap. I loved working for KFC. I was targeted by PETA to broker something between them. I think KFC really stepped up to the plate; unfortunately PETA did not." In 2007, Alexander appeared in a commercial for the [ASPCA](/wiki/ASPCA) that aired on cable TV stations. In 2018, Alexander portrayed [Colonel Sanders](/wiki/Colonel_Sanders) in commercials for [KFC](/wiki/Kentucky_Fried_Chicken), reprising his role from the 2002 campaign.

In 2003, he was cast opposite [Martin Short](/wiki/Martin_Short) in the Los Angeles production of [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks)'s *[The Producers](/wiki/The_Producers_(musical))*. He appeared with [Kelsey Grammer](/wiki/Kelsey_Grammer) in the 2004 [musical adaptation](/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol_(2004_film)) of [Charles Dickens](/wiki/Charles_Dickens)'s *[A Christmas Carol](/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol)*, as [Jacob Marley](/wiki/Jacob_Marley). He voiced Lil' Lightning in *[101 Dalmatians II: Patch's London Adventure](/wiki/101_Dalmatians_II:_Patch%27s_London_Adventure)* (2003), which went on to become one of his most signature roles. Alexander's second chance as a TV series lead, the [CBS](/wiki/CBS) sitcom *[Listen Up](/wiki/Listen_Up_(TV_series))* (2004–05), also fell short of a second season. Alexander was the principal executive producer of the series, based very loosely on the life of the popular sports-media personality [Tony Kornheiser](/wiki/Tony_Kornheiser). He performed on the *[Family Guy: Live in Vegas](/wiki/Family_Guy:_Live_in_Vegas)* 2005 album. Alexander continued to appear in live stage shows, including [Barbra Streisand](/wiki/Barbra_Streisand)'s memorable birthday party in 2005 for Stephen Sondheim at the Hollywood Bowl, where he performed selections from *[Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street](/wiki/Sweeney_Todd:_The_Demon_Barber_of_Fleet_Street)* with [Angela Lansbury](/wiki/Angela_Lansbury). He featured in the 2005 *[Monk](/wiki/Monk_(TV_series))* episode "[Mr. Monk and the Other Detective](/wiki/Mr._Monk_and_the_Other_Detective)" as Monk's rival, [Marty Eels](/wiki/Marty_Eels).

Alexander in 2006

In a 2006 appearance on *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)*, Alexander demonstrated several self-defense techniques. Also that year, he hosted the [PBS](/wiki/PBS) "A Capitol Fourth" celebrations in Washington, D.C., singing, dancing, and playing tuned drums. Alexander was featured as a recurring cast member in the second season of *[Everybody Hates Chris](/wiki/Everybody_Hates_Chris)*. He hosted the [Comedy Central Roast of William Shatner](/wiki/Comedy_Central_Roast_of_William_Shatner). He was the artistic director of Reprise Theatre Company in Los Angeles from 2007 until it went defunct in 2013, where he previously directed *[Sunday in the Park with George](/wiki/Sunday_in_the_Park_with_George)*, and directed its 2007 revival of *[Damn Yankees](/wiki/Damn_Yankees)*. In 2007, Alexander was a guest star in the third episode of the [improv comedy](/wiki/Improv_comedy) series *[Thank God You're Here](/wiki/Thank_God_You%27re_Here_(US_TV_series))*.

He has been a frequent guest and panelist on [Bill Maher](/wiki/Bill_Maher)'s *[Politically Incorrect](/wiki/Politically_Incorrect)* in 1995, 1997 and 2000 and *[Real Time](/wiki/Real_Time_with_Bill_Maher)* in 2006, 2009 and 2012; *[Hollywood Squares](/wiki/Hollywood_Squares)* in 1999, 2002 and 2004; the *[Late Late Show](/wiki/The_Late_Late_Show_(CBS_TV_series))* in 2003, 2012, 2014 and 2015, with [Craig Kilborn](/wiki/Craig_Kilborn), [Craig Ferguson](/wiki/Craig_Ferguson), and [James Corden](/wiki/James_Corden); *[Late Show with David Letterman](/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman)* in 1989, 2000, and 2002; *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert)* in 2015; and *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)* in 2015. In 2008, Alexander guest-starred in the [season four](/wiki/List_of_Criminal_Minds_episodes_(season_4)) episode "Masterpiece" of the [CBS](/wiki/CBS) show *[Criminal Minds](/wiki/Criminal_Minds)* as Professor Rothschild, a well-educated [serial killer](/wiki/Serial_killer) obsessed with the [Fibonacci sequence](/wiki/Fibonacci_number) who sends the team into a race against time to save his last victims. He returned in the same season to direct the episode "Conflicted", featuring the actor [Jackson Rathbone](/wiki/Jackson_Rathbone).

Alexander hosted the LOL Sudbury opening night gala in [Sudbury](/wiki/Greater_Sudbury), Ontario, Canada in 2008, which was simulcast throughout Canada at 60 Cineplex theaters, a first for any comedy festival. He has lent his voice to several episodes of the *[Twilight Zone Radio Dramas](/wiki/The_Twilight_Zone_(radio_series))*. In 2008 and again in 2009, Alexander fronted *Jason Alexander's Comedy Spectacular*, a routine exclusive to Australia. The show consists of stand-up and improvisation and incorporates Alexander's musical talent. He is backed up by several well-known Australian comedians. His first time performing a similar show of this nature was in 2006's *Jason Alexander's Comedy Christmas*. In February/March 2010, Alexander starred in his show, *The Donny Clay Experience*, at the [Planet Hollywood Resort](/wiki/Planet_Hollywood_Resort) in [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley), [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada). Donny Clay, whom he has portrayed in a tour of the United States and Orillia, Ontario, is a [self-help](/wiki/Self-help) guru in a similar mold to his *[Bob Patterson](/wiki/Bob_Patterson_(TV_series))* character. In 2009, he played [Joseph](/wiki/Joseph_(son_of_Jacob)) in the [Thomas Nelson](/wiki/Thomas_Nelson_(publisher)) audio Bible production *The Word of Promise*. The project featured a large ensemble of actors, including [Jim Caviezel](/wiki/Jim_Caviezel), [Lou Gossett Jr.](/wiki/Lou_Gossett_Jr.), [John Rhys-Davies](/wiki/John_Rhys-Davies), [Jon Voight](/wiki/Jon_Voight), [Gary Sinise](/wiki/Gary_Sinise), [Christopher McDonald](/wiki/Christopher_McDonald), [Marisa Tomei](/wiki/Marisa_Tomei), and [John Schneider](/wiki/John_Schneider_(screen_actor)). In 2009, Alexander had a small role in the film *[Hachi: A Dog's Tale](/wiki/Hachi:_A_Dog%27s_Tale)* as a train station manager.

### 2010–present

Alexander with [Alan F. Horn](/wiki/Alan_F._Horn) in May 2010

He starred as [Cosmo](/wiki/Cosmo_and_Wanda) in the 2011 live action film *[A Fairly Odd Movie: Grow Up, Timmy Turner!](/wiki/A_Fairly_Odd_Movie:_Grow_Up,_Timmy_Turner!)*. In 2011, Alexander was the guest star in an episode of *[Harry's Law](/wiki/Harry%27s_Law)*, playing a high school teacher bringing a wrongful dismissal suit. In 2015, he replaced [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David) as the lead in David's [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) play *[Fish in the Dark](/wiki/Fish_in_the_Dark).* He co-starred opposite [Sherie Rene Scott](/wiki/Sherie_Rene_Scott) in the 2017 world premiere of [John Patrick Shanley](/wiki/John_Patrick_Shanley)'s *The Portuguese Kid* at the [Manhattan Theatre Club](/wiki/Manhattan_Theatre_Club). In 2018, Alexander played Olix the bartender in *[The Orville](/wiki/The_Orville)*. The same year, he portrayed Gene Lundy, a drama teacher, on two episodes of *[Young Sheldon](/wiki/Young_Sheldon)*. In 2020, 2021 and 2022, he reprised the role of Gene Lundy on one episode.

In 2019, Alexander appeared on *[The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel](/wiki/The_Marvelous_Mrs._Maisel)* as Asher Friedman, a blacklisted Broadway playwright who is an old friend of Midge Maisel's father Abe Weissman. He won the [Daytime Emmy Award](/wiki/Daytime_Emmy_Award) for Outstanding Original Song for "The Bad Guys?" on *Brainwashed By Toons* (2020). In 2020, Alexander hosted the *[Saturday Night Seder](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Seder)*, an online Passover Seder that featured many celebrities and benefited the [CDC Foundation](/wiki/CDC_Foundation).

From February 2023 he co-presented *Really? No, Really?*, a weekly podcast in which he, co-host Peter Tilden, and their guests attempt to find answers "to life’s most baffling, intriguing, confusing and annoying questions". In July 2023, he made his Broadway directing debut with Sandy Rustin's comedy *[The Cottage](/wiki/The_Cottage_(play))*. The cast includes [Eric McCormack](/wiki/Eric_McCormack), [Laura Bell Bundy](/wiki/Laura_Bell_Bundy) and [Lilli Cooper](/wiki/Lilli_Cooper). In 2023, on the December 21 primetime CBS special *Dick Van Dyke: 98 Years of Magic*, Alexander performed two songs in conjunction with dance-performers and closed the show with a spoken tribute to Van Dyke.

## Personal life

Alexander has been married to Daena E. Title, cousin of director [Stacy Title](/wiki/Stacy_Title), since May 31, 1982. They have two sons, Gabriel and Noah.

Alexander performed a [mentalism](/wiki/Mentalism) and magic act at [The Magic Castle](/wiki/The_Magic_Castle) in Hollywood, California, from April 24 to 30, 2006, and he was later named The Academy of Magical Arts Parlor Magician of the Year for this act. He won the academy's Junior Achievement Award in 1989.

### Charity

Alexander was the national spokesman for the [Scleroderma](/wiki/Scleroderma) Foundation, a leading organization dedicated to raising awareness of the disease and assisting those who are afflicted. In summer 2005, he appeared with [Lee Iacocca](/wiki/Lee_Iacocca) in ads for [DaimlerChrysler](/wiki/DaimlerChrysler). Iacocca did the ads as part of a way to raise money for [Denise Faustman](/wiki/Denise_Faustman)'s research on [autoimmunity](/wiki/Autoimmunity). Iacocca and Alexander both have loved ones whose lives have been adversely affected by autoimmunity.

Alexander competed on televised poker shows and in various tournaments. He appeared twice on [Bravo's](/wiki/Bravo_(US_TV_channel)) *[Celebrity Poker Showdown](/wiki/Celebrity_Poker_Showdown)*, winning the final table of the 8th season. Alexander won the $500,000 prize for the charity of his choice, The [United Way of America](/wiki/United_Way_of_America), to help benefit the New Orleans area. Alexander played in the [2007 World Series of Poker](/wiki/2007_World_Series_of_Poker) main event, but he was eliminated on the second day. He returned in 2009, making it to day 3 of the event and finishing in the top 30% of the field. Alexander has appeared on NBC's *[Poker After Dark](/wiki/Poker_After_Dark)* in the "Celebrities and Mentors" episode, finishing in 6th place after being eliminated by professional poker player [Gavin Smith](/wiki/Gavin_Smith_(poker_player)). He signed with [PokerStars](/wiki/PokerStars), where he plays under the screen name "J. Alexander". In 2021, Alexander competed in a virtual National Poker Tournament, hosted by the [Children's Tumor Foundation](/wiki/Children%27s_Tumor_Foundation), to raise money for [Neurofibromatosis](/wiki/Neurofibromatosis) research.

### Political views

Alexander has been a prominent public supporter of the [OneVoice](/wiki/OneVoice_Movement) initiative, which seeks out opinions from moderate [Israelis](/wiki/Israelis) and [Palestinians](/wiki/Palestinian_people) who want to achieve a mutual peace agreement, through what it states is the silent majority of Israelis and Palestinians. On *[Real Time with Bill Maher](/wiki/Real_Time_with_Bill_Maher)*, he said he had visited [Israel](/wiki/Israel) many times and spoke about progress toward peace he had observed.

On December 6, 2012 Alexander emceed his third gala event for non-profit group [Friends of the Israel Defense Forces](/wiki/Friends_of_the_Israel_Defense_Forces), a charity supporting the [Israel Defense Forces](/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces). Musician [Stevie Wonder](/wiki/Stevie_Wonder) canceled his performance at the event following a recommendation from the UN, and a public petition from [Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions](/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions) (BDS) requesting that he not attend the event. Alexander, who was interviewed at the event by Jewish News Syndicate, stated he supported the [OneVoice Movement](/wiki/OneVoice_Movement), and acknowledged an occupation of Palestine and called for a two-state solution, which was later criticized by Roz Rothstein, CEO of the pro-Israel education group StandWithUs. When asked about the IDF, Alexander stated that they were humane and noble, stating "some of the finest, most humane, most admirable, most noble-serving soldiers that I've ever seen" and that "there can never be any doubt that I am also an advocate for Israel". When asked about why he had repeatedly emceed the event, Alexander stated, "They don't understand first of all what [FIDF] is, and they see it as just a blanket support for Israeli military, and they don't understand how I can say that I'm an advocate for both sides. And given the fact that there are eyes on this event tonight, I thought it was important to get up and say, 'This is why I can advocate for this group and I can advocate for Israel, and I'm not blind to the fact that we're in conflict, and I hold everyone equally accountable, and I hold everyone equally to my heart.'" On November 6, 2015 he again emceed an event at [The Beverly Hilton Hotel](/wiki/The_Beverly_Hilton) in California, which raised $31 million for "provid[ing] educational, cultural and recreational programs and facilities for IDF soldiers."

Alexander is a supporter of the [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)). Alexander supports [same-sex marriage](/wiki/Same-sex_marriage) and an [assault weapons ban](/wiki/Assault_weapons_ban_(USA)). In 2020, he campaigned for the [Texas Democrats](/wiki/Texas_Democratic_Party) with former *Seinfeld* colleagues [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus) and Larry David. He endorsed [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) in [2012](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election) and [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) in [2020](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election). Alexander has been an outspoken critic of the Trump administration and he has ridiculed [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump) over his dancing. He has called [Republican Party](/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) senator [Ted Cruz](/wiki/Ted_Cruz) a jerk from the "jerk store" in reference to a joke from *Seinfeld*.

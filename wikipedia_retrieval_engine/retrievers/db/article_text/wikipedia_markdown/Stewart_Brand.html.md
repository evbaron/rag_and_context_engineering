**Stewart Brand** (born December 14, 1938) is an American project developer and writer, best known as the co-founder and editor of the *[Whole Earth Catalog](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Catalog)*. Other organizations he has founded include [the WELL](/wiki/The_WELL), the [Global Business Network](/wiki/Global_Business_Network), and the [Long Now Foundation](/wiki/Long_Now_Foundation). He is the author of several books, most recently *[Whole Earth Discipline: An Ecopragmatist Manifesto](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Discipline)* and *Maintenance: Of Everything*.

## Life

Brand was born in [Rockford, Illinois](/wiki/Rockford,_Illinois), and attended [Phillips Exeter Academy](/wiki/Phillips_Exeter_Academy) in New Hampshire. He studied [biology](/wiki/Biology) at [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University) under [Paul R. Ehrlich](/wiki/Paul_R._Ehrlich), graduating in 1960. As a soldier in the [U.S. Army](/wiki/U.S._Army), he was a [parachutist](/wiki/Parachutist) and taught [infantry](/wiki/Infantry) skills; he later expressed the view that his experience in the military had fostered his competence in organizing.

A civilian again in 1962, he moved back to California, where he has mostly lived since. He studied design at [San Francisco Art Institute](/wiki/San_Francisco_Art_Institute), photography at [San Francisco State College](/wiki/San_Francisco_State_College), and participated in a scientific study of then-legal [LSD](/wiki/LSD) with [Myron Stolaroff](/wiki/Myron_Stolaroff)'s International Foundation for Advanced Study, in [Menlo Park, California](/wiki/Menlo_Park,_California).

Brand became acquainted with [Richard Raymond](/wiki/Richard_Raymond_(publisher)), a land-use and community-development expert turned entrepreneur, who had established a nonprofit society, the [Portola Institute](/wiki/Portola_Institute) in Menlo Park. Raymond had become a consultant to the [Warm Springs Indian Reservation](/wiki/Warm_Springs_Indian_Reservation) and recommended Brand for a job there as a photographer.

In 1966, Brand married the [mathematician](/wiki/Mathematician) Lois Jennings, a half-[Odawa](/wiki/Odawa) [Native American](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas). The couple divorced in 1973.

Brand met Ryan Phelan when she applied for a job to work at *Whole Earth Catalog*, and the two married in 1983. The couple lives on *Mirene*, a 64-foot (20 m)-long working [tugboat](/wiki/Tugboat). Built in 1912, the boat is moored in a former shipyard in [Sausalito, California](/wiki/Sausalito,_California). He works in *Mary Heartline*, a grounded fishing boat about 100 yards (90 metres) away. One of his favorite items is a table on which [Otis Redding](/wiki/Otis_Redding) is said to have written "[(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay](/wiki/(Sittin%27_On)_The_Dock_of_the_Bay)", a furnishing that Brand acquired from an antiques dealer in Sausalito.

## USCO and Merry Pranksters

By the mid-1960s, Brand became associated with New York multimedia group [USCO](/wiki/USCO) and Bay Area author [Ken Kesey](/wiki/Ken_Kesey) and his [Merry Pranksters](/wiki/Merry_Pranksters). Brand co-produced the [1966 Trips Festival](/wiki/Acid_Tests#Trips_Festival), an early effort blending rock music and [light shows](/wiki/Liquid_light_shows), with Kesey and [Ramón Sender Barayón](/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Sender_Baray%C3%B3n). The Trips Festival was among the first [Grateful Dead](/wiki/Grateful_Dead) performances in San Francisco. An estimated 10,000 [hippies](/wiki/Hippies) attended, and [Haight-Ashbury](/wiki/Haight-Ashbury) soon emerged as the epicenter of an emerging counterculture, with the [Summer of Love](/wiki/Summer_of_Love) in 1967. [Tom Wolfe](/wiki/Tom_Wolfe) includes Brand in his 1968 book *[The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test](/wiki/The_Electric_Kool-Aid_Acid_Test)*.

## NASA images of Earth

*[Earthrise](/wiki/Earthrise)*, by [William Anders](/wiki/William_Anders), [Apollo 8](/wiki/Apollo_8), 1968

See also: [The Blue Marble](/wiki/The_Blue_Marble)

In 1966, while on an [LSD](/wiki/Lysergic_acid_diethylamide) trip on the roof of his house in [North Beach, San Francisco](/wiki/North_Beach,_San_Francisco), Brand became convinced that seeing an image of the whole Earth would change how we think about the planet and ourselves. He then campaigned to have [NASA](/wiki/NASA) release the then-rumored satellite image of the entire Earth as seen from space. He sold and distributed buttons for 25 cents each, asking, "Why haven't we seen a photograph of the whole Earth yet?" During this campaign, Brand met [Richard Buckminster Fuller](/wiki/Richard_Buckminster_Fuller), who offered to help Brand with his projects.

In 1967, the [ATS-3](/wiki/ATS-3) satellite took the photo Brand had envisioned. He thought the image of our planet would be a powerful symbol and featured it on the first (fall 1968) edition of the *[Whole Earth Catalog](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Catalog)*. Later in 1968, NASA astronaut [Bill Anders](/wiki/Bill_Anders) took an Earth photo, *[Earthrise](/wiki/Earthrise)*, from Moon orbit, which became the front image of the spring 1969 edition of the *Catalog*. 1970 saw the first celebration of [Earth Day](/wiki/Earth_Day).

## Douglas Engelbart

In late 1968, Brand assisted electrical engineer [Douglas Engelbart](/wiki/Douglas_Engelbart) with [the Mother of All Demos](/wiki/The_Mother_of_All_Demos), a presentation of many revolutionary computer technologies (including [hypertext](/wiki/Hypertext), email, and the [mouse](/wiki/Computer_mouse)) to the [Fall Joint Computer Conference](/wiki/Fall_Joint_Computer_Conference) in San Francisco.

Brand surmised that given the necessary consciousness, information, and tools, human beings could reshape the world they had made (and were making) for themselves into something environmentally and socially sustainable.

## *Whole Earth Catalog*

During the late 1960s and early 1970s, about 10 million Americans were involved in living [communally](/wiki/Intentional_community). In 1968, using the most basic approaches to [typesetting](/wiki/Typesetting) and page layout, Brand and his colleagues created issue number one of the *[Whole Earth Catalog](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Catalog)*, with the subtitle "access to tools".  The Portola Institute provided financial support for Brand's brainchild. Until 1980, editions of the *Whole Earth Catalog* were published by the Portola Institute. Brand and his wife, Lois, traveled to communes in a 1963 Dodge truck known as the *Whole Earth Truck Store*, which (as a division of the Portola Institute) moved to a storefront in [Menlo Park, California](/wiki/Menlo_Park,_California).

The first oversized *Catalog*, and its successors from the 1970s to the last issue (in 1994), included a wide assortment of things that could serve as useful "tools": books, maps, garden implements, specialized clothing, carpenters' and [masons](/wiki/Brick_mason)' tools, forestry gear, tents, welding equipment, professional journals, early synthesizers, and personal computers. It also comprised ideas, stories, examples of successful projects, mentors like [Buckminster Fuller](/wiki/Buckminster_Fuller), and the experiences of many pioneers. The catalog anticipated social media by reflecting its readers' contemporary experiences in the world. Brand invited "reviews" (written in the form of a letter to a friend) of the best of these items from experts in specific fields or from ordinary people with experience. The catalog described where these things could be located or purchased, if they could be, or how they could be created. Its publication coincided with the wave of social and cultural experimentation, convention-breaking, and "[do it yourself](/wiki/Do_it_yourself)" attitude associated with the [counterculture](/wiki/Counterculture_of_the_1960s).

The *Whole Earth Catalog* had widespread influence within the rural [back-to-the-land movement](/wiki/Back-to-the-land_movement) of the 1970s, and the communities movement within many cities throughout the United States, Canada, and Australia. The 1972 edition sold 1.5 million copies, winning the first U.S. [National Book Award](/wiki/National_Book_Award) in the [Contemporary Affairs](/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Current) category.

[Steve Jobs](/wiki/Steve_Jobs) ended his 2005 commencement address at [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University) by acknowledging both Stewart Brand and the *Whole Earth Catalog*, quoting its farewell message: "Stay hungry. Stay foolish".

## *CoEvolution Quarterly*

Brand with [passenger pigeon](/wiki/Passenger_pigeon) [study skins](/wiki/Study_skins)

To continue his work and also to publish full-length articles on specific topics in the natural sciences and invention, in numerous areas of the arts and the [social sciences](/wiki/Social_sciences), and on the contemporary scene in general, Brand founded *[CoEvolution Quarterly](/wiki/CoEvolution_Quarterly)* in 1974, aimed primarily at educated laypeople. Brand never better revealed his opinions and reason for hope than when he ran, in *CoEvolution Quarterly* #4, a transcription of technology historian [Lewis Mumford](/wiki/Lewis_Mumford)'s talk "The Next Transformation of Man", in which he stated that "man has still within him sufficient resources to alter the direction of modern civilization, for we then need no longer regard man as the passive victim of his own irreversible technological development".

The content of *CoEvolution Quarterly* often included [futurism](/wiki/Futurism) or risqué topics. Besides giving space to unknown writers with something to say, Brand presented articles by many respected authors and thinkers, including Mumford, [Howard T. Odum](/wiki/Howard_T._Odum), [Witold Rybczynski](/wiki/Witold_Rybczynski), [Karl Hess](/wiki/Karl_Hess), [Orville Schell](/wiki/Orville_Schell), [Ivan Illich](/wiki/Ivan_Illich), [Wendell Berry](/wiki/Wendell_Berry), [Ursula K. Le Guin](/wiki/Ursula_K._Le_Guin), [Gregory Bateson](/wiki/Gregory_Bateson), [Amory Lovins](/wiki/Amory_Lovins), [Hazel Henderson](/wiki/Hazel_Henderson), [Gary Snyder](/wiki/Gary_Snyder), [Lynn Margulis](/wiki/Lynn_Margulis), [Eric Drexler](/wiki/Eric_Drexler), [Gerard K. O'Neill](/wiki/Gerard_K._O%27Neill), [Peter Calthorpe](/wiki/Peter_Calthorpe), [Sim Van der Ryn](/wiki/Sim_Van_der_Ryn), [Paul Hawken](/wiki/Paul_Hawken), [John Todd](/wiki/John_Todd_(Canadian_biologist)), [Kevin Kelly](/wiki/Kevin_Kelly_(editor)), and [Donella Meadows](/wiki/Donella_Meadows). In the ensuing years, Brand authored and edited a number of books on topics as diverse as computer-based media, the life history of buildings, and ideas about [space colonies](/wiki/Space_colonization).

In 1984, Brand founded the *[Whole Earth Software Review](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Software_Catalog_and_Review)*, a supplement to the *Whole Earth Software Catalog*. It was merged with *CoEvolution Quarterly* to form the *[Whole Earth Review](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Review)* in 1985.

## California government

From 1977 to 1979, Brand served as "special advisor" to the administration of California Governor [Jerry Brown](/wiki/Jerry_Brown).

## The WELL

In 1985, Brand and [Larry Brilliant](/wiki/Larry_Brilliant) founded [the WELL](/wiki/The_WELL) ("Whole Earth 'Lectronic Link"), a prototypical, wide-ranging online community for informed participants the world over. The WELL won the 1990 Best Online Publication Award from the [Computer Press Association](/wiki/Computer_Press_Association).

## All Species Foundation

In 2000, Brand helped launch the [All Species Foundation](/wiki/All_Species_Foundation), which aimed to catalog all species of life on Earth. The project ceased functioning in 2007, transferring its mission to the *[Encyclopedia of Life](/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Life)*.

## Global Business Network

Brand speaking at the Frontiers Conference, 2010

Brand in 2020

During 1986, Brand was a visiting scientist at the [MIT Media Lab](/wiki/MIT_Media_Lab). Soon after, he became a private-conference organizer for such corporations as [Royal Dutch Shell](/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell), [Volvo](/wiki/Volvo), and [AT&T](/wiki/AT%26T_Corporation). In 1988, he became a co‑founder of the [Global Business Network](/wiki/Global_Business_Network), which became involved with the evolution and application of [scenario thinking](/wiki/Scenario_thinking), planning, and complementary strategic tools. For fourteen years, Brand was on the board of the [Santa Fe Institute](/wiki/Santa_Fe_Institute) (founded in 1984), an organization devoted to "fostering a [multidisciplinary](/wiki/Academic_discipline#Multidisciplinary) scientific research community pursuing frontier science". He has also continued to promote the preservation of tracts of wilderness.

## *Whole Earth Discipline*

The *Whole Earth Catalog* implied an ideal of human [progress](/wiki/Progress_(history)) that depended on decentralized, personal, and liberating technological development—so‑called "soft technology". However, in 2005, Brand criticized aspects of the international environmental ideology he had helped to develop. He wrote an article called "Environmental Heresies" in the May 2005 issue of the [MIT](/wiki/MIT) *[Technology Review](/wiki/Technology_Review)*, in which he described what he considered necessary changes to environmentalism. He suggested, among other things, that environmentalists embrace [nuclear power](/wiki/Nuclear_power) and [genetically modified organisms](/wiki/Genetically_modified_organisms) as technologies with more promise than risk.

Brand later developed these ideas into a book and published *[Whole Earth Discipline: An Ecopragmatist Manifesto](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Discipline)* in 2009. The book examines how urbanization, nuclear power, [genetic engineering](/wiki/Genetic_engineering), [geoengineering](/wiki/Climate_engineering), and wildlife restoration can be used as powerful tools in humanity's ongoing fight against [global warming](/wiki/Global_warming).

In a 2019 interview, Brand described his perspective as "post-libertarian", indicating that at the time when the *Whole Earth Catalog* was being written, he did not fully understand the significance of the role of government in the development of technology and engineering. In his environmental position, he self-describes as an "eco-pragmatist".

## Long Now Foundation

Brand is co‑chair and president of the board of directors of the [Long Now Foundation](/wiki/Long_Now_Foundation) and chairs the foundation's [Seminars About Long-term Thinking](/wiki/Seminars_About_Long-term_Thinking). This series on long-term thinking has presented a range of speakers, including [Brian Eno](/wiki/Brian_Eno), [Neal Stephenson](/wiki/Neal_Stephenson), [Vernor Vinge](/wiki/Vernor_Vinge), [Philip Rosedale](/wiki/Philip_Rosedale), [Jimmy Wales](/wiki/Jimmy_Wales), [Kevin Kelly](/wiki/Kevin_Kelly_(editor)), [Clay Shirky](/wiki/Clay_Shirky), [Ray Kurzweil](/wiki/Ray_Kurzweil), [Bruce Sterling](/wiki/Bruce_Sterling), and [Cory Doctorow](/wiki/Cory_Doctorow). The Long Now Foundation has worked with [Jeff Bezos](/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) to build the [10,000 Year Clock](/wiki/10,000_Year_Clock).

Brand is the subject of the 2021 documentary film *[We Are As Gods](/wiki/We_Are_As_Gods_(film))*.

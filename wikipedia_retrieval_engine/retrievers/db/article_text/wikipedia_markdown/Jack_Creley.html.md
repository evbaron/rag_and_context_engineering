# Jack Creley

## Infobox

- **Born:** Jack Craig Creley ( 1926-03-06 ) March 6, 1926 Chicago, Illinois, United States
- **Died:** March 10, 2004 (2004-03-10) (aged 78) Toronto, Ontario, Canada
- **Occupation:** Actor

**Jack Creley** (March 6, 1926 – March 10, 2004) was an American-born Canadian actor. Although most prominently a stage actor, he also had film and television roles.

## Background

Creley was born in Chicago, Illinois, on March 6, 1926. During the [Great Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression), his family moved to California, where he acted in amateur theatre as a teenager, until he was old enough to enlist in the [United States Army](/wiki/United_States_Army) late in [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). He was shot in the shoulder during the [Battle of Okinawa](/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa), and spent the rest of his life telling the story that he knew he was destined to become an actor when he realized he was responding to the injury like a character in a [John Wayne](/wiki/John_Wayne) film.

After the end of the war, he went to New York City to study acting under [Erwin Piscator](/wiki/Erwin_Piscator) at the [Dramatic Workshop](/wiki/Dramatic_Workshop), where he was a classmate of [Harry Belafonte](/wiki/Harry_Belafonte), [Tony Curtis](/wiki/Tony_Curtis) and [Rod Steiger](/wiki/Rod_Steiger). He moved to [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal) in 1951 to take a job with the Mountain Theatre Company, and remained there until 1954, when he moved to [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto). Soon after moving to Toronto, he met David Smith, who would be his partner for the remainder of his life.

## Acting career

In Toronto, he acted on stage, including frequent performances at the [Stratford Festival](/wiki/Stratford_Festival), and often appeared in [CBC Television](/wiki/CBC_Television) anthology series, including *Scope*, *Playbill*, *CBC Summer Theatre*, *[Encounter](/wiki/Encounter_(1960_TV_series))*, *Folio*, *Startime*, *[Horizon](/wiki/Horizon_(Canadian_TV_series))*, *[Playdate](/wiki/Playdate_(CBC_TV_series))* and several *[Wayne and Shuster](/wiki/Wayne_and_Shuster)* sketches, as well as performing in [cabaret](/wiki/Cabaret) shows.

In 1956, Creley starred in the Canadian production of *[Salad Days](/wiki/Salad_Days_(musical))*, and in 1958 he starred in the Canadian production of *[Visit to a Small Planet](/wiki/Visit_to_a_Small_Planet)*. In 1960, he played two roles at the Stratford Festival, as Lord Capulet in *[Romeo and Juliet](/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet)* and as King Philip in *[King John](/wiki/King_John_(play))*.

Creley had his first major film role in 1961, in the Western film *[The Canadians](/wiki/The_Canadians_(1961_film))*. After completing a run as Holofernes in a Stratford Festival production of *[Love's Labour's Lost](/wiki/Love%27s_Labour%27s_Lost)* that summer, he returned to New York to appear in a [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) production of *[A Man for All Seasons](/wiki/A_Man_for_All_Seasons_(play))* as Cardinal Wolsey. After completing his run in New York, he appeared in a Stratford Festival production of *[The Gondoliers](/wiki/The_Gondoliers)*, and then went to London to appear alongside [Corinne Conley](/wiki/Corinne_Conley), [Dave Broadfoot](/wiki/Dave_Broadfoot), [Eric House](/wiki/Eric_House) and [Eric Christmas](/wiki/Eric_Christmas) in the musical revue *Clap Hands* at the [Hammersmith Theatre](/wiki/Hammersmith_Theatre). Following the end of that show's run, most of the cast returned to Canada, although Creley remained in London to take a role as Mr. Staines in the film *[Dr. Strangelove](/wiki/Dr._Strangelove)*.

After returning to Canada, he appeared in productions of [Edward Albee](/wiki/Edward_Albee)'s *[The Zoo Story](/wiki/The_Zoo_Story)* and [Anton Chekhov](/wiki/Anton_Chekhov)'s *Summer in the Country*. During this time, he also became a popular [voice-over](/wiki/Voice-over) artist for television commercials, recording at least 18 commercial spots in 1965 and 1966. He directed a musical revue, *The Decline and Fall of the Entire World As Seen Through the Eyes of Cole Porter*, in 1965 and 1966, and when he stepped in for several shows in the absence of lead performer [Louis Negin](/wiki/Louis_Negin), it was his first time singing on stage since the end of *Clap Hands* in 1963.

In 1966, he appeared as [Wilfrid Laurier](/wiki/Wilfrid_Laurier) in the CBC Television miniseries *[Reluctant Nation](/wiki/Reluctant_Nation)*, and in 1969 he appeared in the television series *[Strange Paradise](/wiki/Strange_Paradise)* as Laslo Thaxton.

In 1970, he starred in the musical [Oh, Coward!](/wiki/Oh,_Coward!) at Theatre in the Dell, alongside [Patricia Collins](/wiki/Patricia_Collins_(actress)) and [Gordon Thomson](/wiki/Gordon_Thomson_(actor)). Charles Pope from the [Toronto Star](/wiki/Toronto_Star) called his performance "magnificent".

In 1972 he had his second and final Broadway role, appearing in a production of *There's One in Every Marriage* as Roubillon, and in 1974 he had his last role at Stratford in a production of *[The Imaginary Invalid](/wiki/The_Imaginary_Invalid)*. In this era, he began to appear more often in film and television roles, most notably in the film *[Videodrome](/wiki/Videodrome)* and the television sitcom *[Snow Job](/wiki/Snow_Job_(TV_series))*. His final role was a guest appearance on *[E.N.G.](/wiki/E.N.G.)* in 1990.

## Personal life

Creley and Smith shared ownership of an antique store, The Green Dolphin, beginning in 1955, and later of a clothing store, Mr. Smith. They also had a widespread reputation among actors as being excellent hosts of parties; performers such as [Vivien Leigh](/wiki/Vivien_Leigh), [Sean Connery](/wiki/Sean_Connery), [Richard Burton](/wiki/Richard_Burton), [Bea Arthur](/wiki/Bea_Arthur), and [Billy Dee Williams](/wiki/Billy_Dee_Williams) were frequent houseguests of the couple.

Late in life, Creley suffered two strokes, and began to develop [aphasia](/wiki/Aphasia). He died on March 10, 2004, in Toronto.

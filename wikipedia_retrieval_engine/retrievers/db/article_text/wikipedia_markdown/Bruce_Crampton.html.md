**Bruce Crampton** (born 28 September 1935) is an Australian professional [golfer](/wiki/Golf).

## Early life and amateur career

In 1935, Crampton was born in [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney), [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales). He attended [Kogarah High School](/wiki/Kogarah_High_School) from 1948 to 1950.

In August 1953, Crampton reached final of the [New South Wales Amateur Championship](/wiki/New_South_Wales_Amateur_Championship), losing 5&4 to [Harry Berwick](/wiki/Harry_Berwick). As an amateur, he also had success at professional tournaments. In October 1953, at the age of 18, he led the [Lakes Open](/wiki/Lakes_Open) after two rounds. According to [Norman Von Nida](/wiki/Norman_Von_Nida), he was "probably" the first amateur in roughly 15 years, since [Jim Ferrier](/wiki/Jim_Ferrier), "to lead a big tournament after two rounds." In 1953, Von Nida also stated: "Crampton proves my prediction that he would become one of the greatest players Australia has produced."

## Professional career

In 1953, Crampton turned professional, becoming an assistant to Billy McWilliam at Beverley Park in [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney). His decision to turn professional came soon after he had been left out of the Australian amateur team to tour Britain in 1954 and play in the [Commonwealth Tournament](/wiki/Commonwealth_Tournament) at St Andrews.

Crampton won the [Vardon Trophy](/wiki/Vardon_Trophy) for the player with the lowest stroke average on the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour) in 1973 and 1975. He had 14 career wins on the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour) between 1961 and 1975 and was runner up in four [major championships](/wiki/Men%27s_major_golf_championships) – one [Masters](/wiki/Masters_Tournament), one [U.S. Open](/wiki/U.S._Open_(golf)), and two [PGA Championships](/wiki/PGA_Championship) – all to [Jack Nicklaus](/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus). He was ranked among the top five golfers in the world in both 1972 and 1973, according to [Mark McCormack's world golf rankings](/wiki/Mark_McCormack%27s_world_golf_rankings). His other regular career victories included the [Australian Open](/wiki/Australian_Open_(golf)), [New Zealand PGA Championship](/wiki/New_Zealand_PGA_Championship), Far East Open and the [Philippine Open](/wiki/Philippine_Open_(golf)). As a senior, he won 20 times on the [Champions Tour](/wiki/Champions_Tour), and topped the money list in 1986.

## Awards and honors

- Crampton is an [Officer of the Order of Australia](/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_Australia) (AO).
- Crampton is also a [Member of the Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (MPE).
- In 1973 and 1975, Crampton earned the [Vardon Trophy](/wiki/Vardon_Trophy), given by the [PGA of America](/wiki/PGA_of_America) to the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour)'s lowest scorer of the year.
- In 1973, the New South Wales PGA bestowed upon him their Player of the Year award.
- He is also a member of the New South Wales Hall of Champion.
- In 1986, Crampton won the money list for the [Senior PGA Tour](/wiki/Senior_PGA_Tour).
- In 1992, he was inducted into the Texas Golf Hall of Fame.
- In 2001, Crampton was also inducted into the [Sport Australia Hall of Fame](/wiki/Sport_Australia_Hall_of_Fame).

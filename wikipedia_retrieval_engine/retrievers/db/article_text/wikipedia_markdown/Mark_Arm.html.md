**Mark Arm** (born **Mark Thomas McLaughlin**; February 21, 1962) is an American singer and songwriter, best known as the vocalist for the [grunge](/wiki/Grunge) band [Mudhoney](/wiki/Mudhoney). His former group, [Green River](/wiki/Green_River_(band)), was one of the first grunge bands, along with [Malfunkshun](/wiki/Malfunkshun), [Soundgarden](/wiki/Soundgarden), [Skin Yard](/wiki/Skin_Yard), the [U-Men](/wiki/U-Men), and others. He is also the manager of the [Sub Pop](/wiki/Sub_Pop) warehouse and previously worked at [Fantagraphics Books](/wiki/Fantagraphics_Books).

## Early life

Arm was born February 21, 1962, at [Vandenberg Air Force Base](/wiki/Vandenberg_Air_Force_Base) in [California](/wiki/California), and was raised in [Kirkland, Washington](/wiki/Kirkland,_Washington). As a child, he was a member of [Boy Scouts of America](/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America). He graduated from [Bellevue Christian High School](/wiki/Bellevue_Christian_School) in [Bellevue, Washington](/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington).

In 1985, Arm earned an English degree with an emphasis in creative writing from the [University of Washington](/wiki/University_of_Washington).

## Early career

Arm first entered the [Seattle](/wiki/Seattle) rock scene in 1980, when he formed a band while still in high school, called "Mr. Epp and the Calculations" with singer Jo Smitty, and Peter Wick, who wrote and recorded their first song,"The Pigeon in the Fountain bed," which caused local radio DJ Stephen Rabow to declare them, "The worst band in the world." The band played its first show in 1981, opening for the band Student Nurse. In 1982 they released a 7-inch EP entitled "Mohawk Man", produced by Johnny Rubato (of Rubato Records, a local used record shop for more than 30 years). The next year they added a second guitarist, [Steve Turner](/wiki/Steve_Turner_(guitarist)), and released a [cassette](/wiki/Cassette_tape) described on the lyric sheet as a "combination of art and [hardcore](/wiki/Hardcore_punk)." The cassette featured one side of live recordings and a [flip-side](/wiki/A-side_and_B-side) of studio recordings and experimental sounds; a sonic amalgam described in a 1984 issue of *Revenge Against Boredom* '[zine](/wiki/Zine) (produced by notable [skateboarding](/wiki/Skateboarding) historian Jocko Weyland), as "45 or so minutes of Pillow Fights, Jokes, some songs, preachers' garbled talking. And you get to find out what you get if you give up your personality."

Mr. Epp and the Calculations also played with [Ten Minute Warning](/wiki/Ten_Minute_Warning) and [the Dead Kennedys](/wiki/The_Dead_Kennedys) at the Eagles Auditorium in April.

Mark Arm of Green River at their reunion concert in 2008 in Seattle.

After Mr. Epp and the Calculations ended, Mark Arm and Steve Turner (who had become close friends) joined the band [Limp Richerds](/wiki/Limp_Richerds) for a few weeks. Afterward, Arm and Turner took on future [Pearl Jam](/wiki/Pearl_Jam) members [Jeff Ament](/wiki/Jeff_Ament) and [Stone Gossard](/wiki/Stone_Gossard), as well as [Alex Vincent](/wiki/Alex_Vincent_(drummer)), to form the band [Green River](/wiki/Green_River_(band)). Green River released two EPs and a full-length album before disbanding. Steve Turner left the band to finish college, and Arm was forced to find a new band again. After Turner returned from schooling, they resumed their Green River side project, The Thrown Ups.

## Mudhoney

Arm and Turner took on drummer [Dan Peters](/wiki/Dan_Peters), and bassist [Matt Lukin](/wiki/Matt_Lukin), formerly of [Melvins](/wiki/Melvins). The new band renamed themselves Mudhoney. In 1988, [Sub Pop](/wiki/Sub_Pop) released Mudhoney's first single, "[Touch Me I'm Sick](/wiki/Touch_Me_I%27m_Sick)". After extensive touring and an EP album, Mudhoney released [their self-titled full length debut](/wiki/Mudhoney_(album)) in 1989. Their next album, *[Every Good Boy Deserves Fudge](/wiki/Every_Good_Boy_Deserves_Fudge)* came out soon after, just before the explosion of grunge spearheaded by [Nirvana](/wiki/Nirvana_(band))'s seminal *[Nevermind](/wiki/Nevermind)*. At the time, Sub Pop, their record label, was "on the verge of bankruptcy, having trouble paying its flagship band, severely delaying the release of the album to July 1991." In 1992, they signed to a major record label, [Reprise](/wiki/Reprise_Records) and released *[Piece of Cake](/wiki/Piece_of_Cake_(album))*. The album did not sell well, due to a combination of the band's uncompromising sound and an oversaturation of the genre; according to Stephen Turner, the album references "how easily things had come to them...the songs were kinda half-baked... and Mark wasn't at his best."

Although they never achieved the fame of some of their contemporaries, Arm and Mudhoney have made significant contributions to grunge music. Mudhoney is one of the few grunge bands that continue to release albums; in 2002 they released *[Since We've Become Translucent](/wiki/Since_We%27ve_Become_Translucent)*, *[Under a Billion Suns](/wiki/Under_a_Billion_Suns)* in 2005, *[The Lucky Ones](/wiki/The_Lucky_Ones_(Mudhoney_album))* followed in May 2008, *[Vanishing Point](/wiki/Vanishing_Point_(Mudhoney_album))* came in April 2013, *[Digital Garbage](/wiki/Digital_Garbage)* came out in September 2018 and, most recently, *[Plastic Eternity](/wiki/Plastic_Eternity)* in 2023. All of these releases have been on the [Sub Pop](/wiki/Sub_Pop) record label.

## Solo and side projects

Arm released "The Freewheelin' Mark Arm", a solo single in 1990.

He was a singer and guitarist for the group [Bloodloss](/wiki/Bloodloss) and singer for the Seattle [supergroup](/wiki/Supergroup_(music)) The Monkeywrench. Monkeywrench members include Arm, Turner, Tim Kerr (Lord Hi Fixers, [Big Boys](/wiki/Big_Boys_(band)), Poison 13), Tom Price ([Gas Huffer](/wiki/Gas_Huffer)) and Martin Bland (Bloodloss). He has also made guest appearances on several albums, most notably on [Alice in Chains](/wiki/Alice_in_Chains)' 1992 EP *[Sap](/wiki/Sap_(EP))*.

In 1998, he made an appearance on the motion picture soundtrack for the film *[Velvet Goldmine](/wiki/Velvet_Goldmine)* with [Ron Asheton](/wiki/Ron_Asheton), [Mike Watt](/wiki/Mike_Watt), [Thurston Moore](/wiki/Thurston_Moore), and [Steve Shelley](/wiki/Steve_Shelley) under the name [Wylde Ratttz](/wiki/Wylde_Ratttz).

In 1999, he recorded the vocals for the song "I Need Somebody", a cover of the song by [The Stooges](/wiki/The_Stooges), featured on [Nebula](/wiki/Nebula_(band))'s first album, *[To the Center](/wiki/To_the_Center)*.

In 2000, Arm, Turner, Peters, [Scott McCaughey](/wiki/Scott_McCaughey), Tom Price and Bill Henderson recorded the album "The New Original Sonic Sound" under the band name The New Strychnines. they recorded a compilation of 16 songs by the legendary mid-1960's Seattle garage band [The Sonics](/wiki/The_Sonics). The album was released by Book Records.

In 2004, he toured with [MC5](/wiki/MC5), standing in for the late [Rob Tyner](/wiki/Rob_Tyner) on vocals.

In 2013, he contributed vocals on a cover version of [The Scientists](/wiki/The_Scientists)' "Set It on Fire" for the [Melvins](/wiki/Melvins)' album *[Everybody Loves Sausages](/wiki/Everybody_Loves_Sausages)*.

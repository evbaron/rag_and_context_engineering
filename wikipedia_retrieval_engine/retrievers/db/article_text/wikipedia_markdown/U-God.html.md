**Lamont Jody Hawkins** (born November 10, 1970), known by his stage name **U-God** (short for Universal God Allah), is an American [rapper](/wiki/Hip-hop) and member of the [hip hop](/wiki/Hip-hop_culture) collective [Wu-Tang Clan](/wiki/Wu-Tang_Clan). He has been with the group since its inception, and is known for his deep voice and rhythmic flow that can alternate between gruff and smooth.

## Early life

Hawkins was born in [Brownsville](/wiki/Brownsville,_Brooklyn), [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn), on November 10, 1970. He moved to [Staten Island](/wiki/Staten_Island) as a youth. He was originally a beatboxer for [Cappadonna](/wiki/Cappadonna), and was friends with future members [Method Man](/wiki/Method_Man), [Inspectah Deck](/wiki/Inspectah_Deck) and childhood friend of [Raekwon](/wiki/Raekwon). Sometime before the members united, U-God was mentored in rap by Cappadonna. He soon became friends with [RZA](/wiki/RZA) and [Ghostface Killah](/wiki/Ghostface_Killah), and he began rhyming under the alias Golden Arms, based on the Kung-Fu movie *[Kid with the Golden Arm](/wiki/Kid_with_the_Golden_Arm)*. Later on he changed his name to U-God (which is short for his [Five-Percent Nation](/wiki/Five-Percent_Nation) righteous name "Universal God Allah").

## Career

### *Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)* / *Wu-Tang Forever*

U-God was convicted on the grounds of [firearm](/wiki/Illegal_weapons_trade) and [drug possession](/wiki/Drug_possession) charges on April 17, 1992, and was paroled in January 1993. His incarceration prevented him from attending the group's debut album *[Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers)](/wiki/Enter_the_Wu-Tang_(36_Chambers))* recording sessions for the most part, his input on the seminal LP consisting of only a short [bridge](/wiki/Bridge_(music)) on the group's debut single "[Protect Ya Neck](/wiki/Protect_Ya_Neck)" as well as the fan-favorite opening verse of "Da Mystery of Chessboxin'". Nevertheless, after his release, he quickly became known to fans for his rugged flow and bass-like voice on Wu tracks such as "Winter Warz", "Knuckleheadz", "Investigative Reports", and "Black Jesus". He was featured heavily on the group's second album *[Wu-Tang Forever](/wiki/Wu-Tang_Forever)*, on which he was one of only four of the group to get a solo track – "Black Shampoo" (the others being [Inspectah Deck](/wiki/Inspectah_Deck) with "The City", [RZA](/wiki/RZA) with "Sunshower" and [Ol' Dirty Bastard](/wiki/Ol%27_Dirty_Bastard) with "Dog Shit").

### *Golden Arms Redemption*

U-God was the eighth member of the group to record a solo album, releasing *[Golden Arms Redemption](/wiki/Golden_Arms_Redemption)* in 1999 on Priority Records, which displayed a wide variety of sounds from gritty [blaxploitation](/wiki/Blaxploitation) [funk](/wiki/Funk) to urgent string sections, and featured guest appearances from several Wu-Tang members and affiliates. It had two singles in "Dat's Gangsta" and "Bizarre". The song "Rumble" was used as the main song for the video game *[Wu-Tang: Shaolin Style](/wiki/Wu-Tang:_Shaolin_Style)*. Bizarre debuted #7 on the *Billboard* [Hot Rap Singles](/wiki/Hot_Rap_Singles), but soon after Priority Records was on the verge of shutting down. Still, the album managed to go Gold in sales. Based on the initial success, U-God was able to open his own label, Suppa Nigga Productions. He released his second album *[Mr. Xcitement](/wiki/Mr._Xcitement)* in 2005.

### *Dopium* and *The Keynote Speaker*

U-God performing in Atlanta, 2007

In 2009, U-God released the album *[Dopium](/wiki/Dopium)* and had the lead single "Wu-Tang" featuring [Method Man](/wiki/Method_Man). The album featured guest appearances by [Sheek Louch](/wiki/Sheek_Louch), [Jim Jones](/wiki/Jim_Jones_(rapper)), [Raekwon](/wiki/Raekwon), [Ghostface Killah](/wiki/Ghostface_Killah), [GZA](/wiki/GZA), [Cappadonna](/wiki/Cappadonna), [Killah Priest](/wiki/Killah_Priest) and Scotty Wotty with production by Bloody Beat Roots, Felix Cartel and [Large Professor](/wiki/Large_Professor). In 2013 U-God announced a new album *[The Keynote Speaker](/wiki/The_Keynote_Speaker)* with production by [RZA](/wiki/RZA) who also served as the albums executive producer. The album was released on July 23, 2013, by RZA's [Soul Temple Records](/wiki/Soul_Temple_Records) with guest appearances from [Styles P](/wiki/Styles_P), [Kool Keith](/wiki/Kool_Keith), [Method Man](/wiki/Method_Man), RZA, [GZA](/wiki/GZA) and [Inspectah Deck](/wiki/Inspectah_Deck).

### *Venom* and autobiographical book

On December 13, 2016, U-God released through Babygrande Records' [SoundCloud](/wiki/SoundCloud) account a song "Venom" and announced that his new album, also called *[Venom](/wiki/Venom_(U-God_album))*, would be released soon. On September 25, 2017, he added a [tweet](/wiki/Twitter) in which he announced that he had finished his [autobiography](/wiki/Autobiography) entitled *[Raw: My Journey Into The Wu-Tang](/wiki/Raw:_My_Journey_Into_The_Wu-Tang)* and it would be released along with his new album in March 2018. On February 2, 2018, he released a free mixtape *Bring Back God II* through [DatPiff](/wiki/DatPiff) platform. *Venom* was released on March 30, 2018 and debuted at #34 on the U.S. Rap Albums Chart.

## Personal life

Around the time of *Wu-Tang Forever*, his two-year-old son Dontae (now a hip-hop artist as iNTeLL) was struck by a stray bullet while on his way to a birthday party, suffering permanent damage to his kidneys and hands, an ordeal U-God documented on the Wu-Tang Clan track "A Better Tomorrow".

**Demetrio Albertini** [Ufficiale OMRI](/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Italian_Republic) (born 23 August 1971) is an Italian [football](/wiki/Association_football) administrator and former player who is the sporting director of [Parma](/wiki/Parma_FC). He was preciously the vice-president of the [Italian Football Federation](/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) (FIGC).

As a player, he is widely considered one of the legends of the [AC Milan](/wiki/AC_Milan) side of the 1990s, and a fundamental player for the [Italy national team](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) of the same period. He spent most of his career with Milan of Italy's [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A), winning many trophies, including five Serie A titles and two [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League) titles with the club. He also played his final season for [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona), winning the [Spanish League](/wiki/La_Liga) before retiring that year.

A vital member of the [Italy national team](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team), Albertini was part of the squads that competed at the [World Cups](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup) of [1994](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1998](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), as well as the [1996](/wiki/Euro_1996) and [2000](/wiki/Euro_2000) [European Championships](/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship), reaching the finals of the 1994 World Cup and Euro 2000.

## Club career

A young Albertini during his time with [Padova](/wiki/Calcio_Padova)

Albertini, born in [Besana in Brianza](/wiki/Besana_in_Brianza), [province of Monza e Brianza](/wiki/Province_of_Monza_e_Brianza) near Milan, emerged as a product of [AC Milan's youth system](/wiki/AC_Milan_Primavera), and went on to spend 14 highly successful years with the senior club after debuting in [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A) as a 17-year-old during the [1988–89 season](/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_Serie_A) under [Arrigo Sacchi](/wiki/Arrigo_Sacchi), on 15 January 1989, in a 4–0 home win over [Como](/wiki/Como_Calcio). He spent part of the [1990–91](/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_Serie_B) season on loan at [Padova Calcio](/wiki/Padova_Calcio) in [Serie B](/wiki/Serie_B), collecting 28 appearances and 5 goals, in order to gain experience, and was subsequently awarded a prize by [Diadora](/wiki/Diadora) as one of the most promising young Italian stars. After a successful season with Padova, he soon established himself in the starting lineup of the [AC Milan](/wiki/AC_Milan) senior side during the [1991–92 season](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Serie_A) under [Fabio Capello](/wiki/Fabio_Capello), wearing the number 4 shirt, and helping Milan to win the title undefeated that season; he would go on to make almost 300 [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A) appearances for the club (293 in total, scoring 21 goals), and 406 total career appearances for Milan, scoring 28 goals in all competitions.

Albertini celebrates winning the [1991–92 Serie A](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Serie_A) title with [AC Milan](/wiki/AC_Milan)

Albertini won many titles during his years at Milan, and claimed three successive Serie A titles in [1992](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Serie_A), [1993](/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Serie_A) and [1994](/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Serie_A), and he also managed to capture two further *scudetti* in [1996](/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_Serie_A) and [1999](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Serie_A). In addition, he made 41 Champions League appearances, helping the Rossoneri to reach three consecutive finals between [1993](/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_UEFA_Champions_League) and [1995](/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_UEFA_Champions_League), lifting the trophy in [1994](/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_UEFA_Champions_League). He also won two [UEFA Super Cups](/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup), three [Italian Super Cups](/wiki/Supercoppa_Italiana), and an [Intercontinental Cup](/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_(1960%E2%80%932004)) during his time at the club. Albertini remained at Milan until [2002](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Serie_A), when his manager and former mentor [Carlo Ancelotti](/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti) preferred to play the emerging [Andrea Pirlo](/wiki/Andrea_Pirlo) in his position. During his time at the club, he managed 28 goals in 406 appearances; he also scored a personal record of 8 goals during the [1996–97](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_Serie_A) season.

After leaving Milan, Albertini bounced around different teams. He spent the [2002–03](/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_La_Liga) season on loan to [Atlético Madrid](/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid), scoring 2 goals in 28 caps for the Spanish club. He was eventually traded to [Lazio](/wiki/SS_Lazio) in exchange for [Giuseppe Pancaro](/wiki/Giuseppe_Pancaro) during the [2003–04](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_Serie_A) season, with great bitterness, where he finally won the [Coppa Italia](/wiki/2004_Coppa_Italia_final) which had eluded him at Milan, scoring 2 goals in 23 appearances for the club. He started the [2004–05](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_Serie_A) season with [Atalanta](/wiki/Atalanta_BC), playing 14 matches and scoring a goal on his debut, before transferring to [FC Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) in January, where he joined his former midfield mentor, now manager [Frank Rijkaard](/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard), and was able to capture [La Liga](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_La_Liga) during the final season of his career, with five appearances.

## International career

For the [Italy national team](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team), Albertini has been capped 79 times between 1991 and 2002, scoring 3 goals. He made his debut on 21 December 1991, at the age of 20, in a 2–0 win against [Cyprus](/wiki/Cyprus_national_football_team) in Foggia. In 1992, he competed with the national squad at the [1992 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics) in Barcelona, and he won the [1992 UEFA European Under-21 Championship](/wiki/1992_UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship) with the [Italian Under-21 side](/wiki/Italy_national_under-21_football_team). He played for his country at the [1994](/wiki/Football_World_Cup_1994) and [1998 World Cups](/wiki/Football_World_Cup_1998), [Euro 96](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996), and [Euro 2000](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000). Although he was still an Italy regular at the time, he was unable to participate in the [2002 World Cup](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) due to an injury to his Achille's tendon a few months before the competition. His final international appearance came in a 2–1 away win over [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) in Leeds, in an international friendly match in March 2002, with Albertini coming on during the second half. Albertini captained Italy on 6 occasions.

### World Cup 1994

Albertini's first major tournament for Italy came at the [1994 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) in the USA, under manager [Arrigo Sacchi](/wiki/Arrigo_Sacchi). Alongside Milan teammate [Roberto Donadoni](/wiki/Roberto_Donadoni), as well as [Dino Baggio](/wiki/Dino_Baggio), Albertini formed the "engine room" of the Italian midfield throughout the World Cup. In the last group game against [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team), Albertini assisted a crucial goal for [Daniele Massaro](/wiki/Daniele_Massaro), which allowed Italy to qualify for the second round as the best third placed team. In the semifinal against [Bulgaria](/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team), Albertini gave a dominant performance, taking several shots on goal and even hitting the post. Albertini also created several chances during the match, dictating the tempo of his team's play; he notably helped to set up [Roberto Baggio](/wiki/Roberto_Baggio)'s second goal of the match, with a lobbed throughball, which allowed Italy to progress to the final with a 2–1 victory. In the [final](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_final) against [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil_national_football_team), a balanced, scoreless game after extra-time led to a penalty shoot-out; Albertini scored his penalty, but his effort did not prove to be sufficient, as his teammates [Franco Baresi](/wiki/Franco_Baresi), [Daniele Massaro](/wiki/Daniele_Massaro), and [Roberto Baggio](/wiki/Roberto_Baggio) missed their penalties.

### Euro 1996

Italy went to the [European championship in England](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996) and many saw Sacchi's team as the key contender for the title along with [Germany](/wiki/Germany_national_football_team), and saw Albertini as Italy's key player, wearing the [number 10 shirt](/wiki/Squad_number_(association_football)). However, the tournament ended in frustration for the Italians, as they suffered a first-round elimination. Arrigo Sacchi's team selection for the second group game against [Czech Republic](/wiki/Czech_Republic_national_football_team) was based on the presumption that, after the opening 2–1 victory over Russia and in the light of the upcoming clash with Germany, Italy could afford playing without a series of key players. Italy lost 2–1 to the eventual runners-up Czech Republic, however, and then were knocked out of the tournament following a 0–0 draw with future champions Germany in their final group game, a match the "Azurri" dominated thanks to Albertini's presence in midfield, with [Gianfranco Zola](/wiki/Gianfranco_Zola) missing a potentially match-winning penalty. [Sacchi](/wiki/Arrigo_Sacchi) in one of his interviews admitted the early departure was due to his mistakes and the 1996 squad was his best Italy team, even better than the one that got the second place in USA 1994.

### World Cup 1998

Albertini played a key role in helping Italy to qualify for the 1998 World Cup: during the first leg of the [1998 World Cup qualification play-off](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(UEFA)#Play-offs) against [Russia](/wiki/Russia_national_football_team), in Moscow, on 29 October 1997, he set up [Christian Vieri](/wiki/Christian_Vieri)'s goal in a 1–1 draw; he also set up [Pierluigi Casiraghi](/wiki/Pierluigi_Casiraghi)'s goal in the second leg in Naples, which allowed Italy to qualify for the World Cup 2–1 on aggregate.

At the [1998 World Cup](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), Albertini's presence was not as central or explicit as it was in the previous major tournaments, but [Cesare Maldini](/wiki/Cesare_Maldini) relied on him as one of the team's key central midfielders and creative players. Out of the games that Italy played in France, Albertini was not involved only when Italy faced [Austria](/wiki/Austria_national_football_team) in the final match of the group stage. In the quarter-final clash, a [Zidane](/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane)-led [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) managed to overcome the Italian masters of insurmountable defence only on penalties after a goalless draw, during which Albertini's surprising failure from the spot did induce the tides to turn against la "Squadra Azzurra". Ironically, the midfielder could have been the creator of Italy's golden goal; during extra-time, his superb delivery into the area to [Roberto Baggio](/wiki/Roberto_Baggio), from a lobbed pass, left the latter alone in front of French keeper [Fabien Barthez](/wiki/Fabien_Barthez), but the volley went just inches wide. Having won the most difficult match of the tournament, France went on to claim the nation's first [World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup) title on home soil.

### Euro 2000

Albertini's Euro 2000 Italy jersey located in the Football Museum in Florence

In [Dino Zoff](/wiki/Dino_Zoff)'s formation at [Euro 2000](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), Albertini was the unquestionable leader of Italy's midfield, starting alongside [Di Biagio](/wiki/Luigi_Di_Biagio), behind either [Stefano Fiore](/wiki/Stefano_Fiore) or [Francesco Totti](/wiki/Francesco_Totti). His pace-setting and creative role in creating chances and controlling the Italian midfield was paramount to Italy's successful run in the tournament, as he provided two assists for his team (one for Totti's goal against [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team), and the other for [Inzaghi](/wiki/Filippo_Inzaghi)'s goal against [Romania](/wiki/Romania_national_football_team)), finishing the tournament as his country's top creator, although the Italians ultimately missed out on the trophy. The azzurri progressed to the [final](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000_final) undefeated, winning all three of their group matches against [Turkey](/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team), co-hosts Belgium, and [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team). Italy went on to defeat Romania 2–0 in the quarterfinal, and overcame co-hosts [the Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in the semi-final on penalties after a 0–0 draw following extra time. Italy eventually lost out once again to the [1998 World Champions](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) 2–1 in the final, on a golden goal in extra-time. Albertini was chosen to be part of the [Team of the Tournament](/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship_Teams_of_the_Tournament#Belgium_and_Netherlands_2000) due to his performances throughout the Cup.

### Missed World Cup 2002 and retirement

Despite being a key part of [Giovanni Trapattoni](/wiki/Giovanni_Trapattoni)'s Italy squad, Albertini missed the [2002 World Cup](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) due to an injury sustained on 14 April 2002, less than two months before the tournament, in the away [Serie A](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Serie_A) game for Milan against Juventus, effectively putting an end to his international career at the age of 30. Coincidentally, this game also turned out to be his last for AC Milan as well.

## After retirement

On 5 December 2005, Albertini announced his retirement from professional football and expressed his desire to one day become a full-time football manager. On 15 March 2006, a Milan vs Barcelona celebration match was organised in Albertini's honour, featuring great footballing names from both past and present (such as [Marco van Basten](/wiki/Marco_van_Basten), [Ruud Gullit](/wiki/Ruud_Gullit), [Frank Rijkaard](/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard), and [Franco Baresi](/wiki/Franco_Baresi)). Milan won the match 3–2 at the [San Siro](/wiki/San_Siro), with Albertini scoring the first goal from a textbook swerving free kick. Following the match, a visibly emotional Albertini was given a standing ovation from the fans.

In 2006, Albertini was involved in a project to create a Footballing Academy in his name, “Scuola calcio Demetrio Albertini", in Selvino (Bg), which took place in Milan and Lecchese, involving over 1000 young players.

### Sporting director with FIGC

On 18 May 2006, following the Italian football *[Calciopoli](/wiki/Calciopoli)* scandal involving [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus) and [Luciano Moggi](/wiki/Luciano_Moggi), among others, which led to the resignation of [Franco Carraro](/wiki/Franco_Carraro) from the [Italian Football Federation](/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) presidency and the appointment of a temporary commissioner, [Guido Rossi](/wiki/Guido_Rossi_(politician)), by the National Olympic Committee, Albertini was named vice-commissioner of the [Italian Football Federation](/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) (FIGC).

On 19 September, following the resignation of Rossi, who was meanwhile appointed as chairman of [Telecom Italia](/wiki/Telecom_Italia), Albertini also announced his resignation. He was later appointed vice-president of the FIGC in 2007, under Giancarlo Abete, and was re-elected in 2013.

On 27 July 2014, Albertini nominated himself for the position of the president of the FIGC, but ultimately lost controversially to his former co-vice-president [Carlo Tavecchio](/wiki/Carlo_Tavecchio) on 11 August, despite having the support of the A.I.C. (the [Italian Footballers' Association](/wiki/Italian_Footballers%27_Association)).

### Parma board member

Following the bankruptcy of [Parma FC](/wiki/Parma_FC), Demetrio Albertini was appointed to the club's board as a football advisor for the club’s administrators.

### Researcher for Football Manager

Albertini is a researcher for [Football Manager](/wiki/Football_Manager), the football management simulation game franchise.

## Style of play

Albertini was a complete, experienced and composed midfielder. His key strengths as a player were his mentality, his excellent vision, tactical knowledge, ability to read the game, and ball control, but above all, his brilliant passing range, which made him a key member of the Milan and the Italy national sides of the 90s and early 2000s; he was also known for his powerful and accurate shots from distance, which also enabled him to contribute to his team's offensive play with goals as well as [assists](/wiki/Assist_(association_football)). Few players were able to replicate Albertini's precise [long passing](/wiki/Long_ball) and powerful distance shooting adeptness, and many have likened his abilities in these areas to those of [Ronald Koeman](/wiki/Ronald_Koeman). Albertini was also an accurate [penalty kick](/wiki/Penalty_kick_(association_football)) and [set piece](/wiki/Set_piece_(football)) taker, who could [curl](/wiki/Curl_(association_football)) the ball well, but also kick with power, and was known for often striking the ball without a run-up during dead ball situations. Although he was fundamentally a hardworking, intelligent, and creative [central midfielder](/wiki/Central_midfielder) or [deep-lying playmaker](/wiki/Deep-lying_playmaker) with excellent technical ability, he was able to complete himself tactically and improve upon the defensive and offensive aspect of his game play throughout his career, demonstrating his adeptness as a ball-winner; his wide range of skills made him a versatile player, allowed him to play in several other [midfield](/wiki/Midfielder) positions, including out on the [wing](/wiki/Midfielder#Winger), due to his [crossing](/wiki/Cross_(association_football)) ability.

In the Milan side and Italy side, he was seen as the heir to [Carlo Ancelotti](/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti), and later also as the predecessor to [Andrea Pirlo](/wiki/Andrea_Pirlo) as the [playmaking](/wiki/Playmaker) pivot of the teams' midfield, due to his ability to create goalscoring opportunities or control the game in midfield and set the tempo of his team's play with his distribution, and he was often regarded as the "creative brain" and "metronome" of his teams. Many football experts draw parallels between Albertini and Pirlo, the midfield ace of European and world football, who emerged as his heir in Italian football, both for Milan and for the Italy national side. Like Albertini, Pirlo is a deep-lying playmaker who also possesses excellent technique, ball skills, vision and passing range, and who is also a set-piece specialist and a goal threat from distance. In addition to his footballing skills, Albertini was also known for his correct behaviour on the pitch, and was seen as a symbol and leader for both his club and national sides.

## Personal life

Albertini is a practising Roman Catholic. He married Uriana Capone in 1996 in Oria, Italy. He has a son named Guillermo who is born in Den Bosch, Holland.

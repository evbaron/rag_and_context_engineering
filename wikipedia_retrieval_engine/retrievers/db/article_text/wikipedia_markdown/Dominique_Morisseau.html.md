# Dominique Morisseau

- **Born:** ( 1978-03-13 ) March 13, 1978 (age 48) Detroit, Michigan, U.S.

- **Education:** University of Michigan, Ann Arbor ( BFA )

**Dominique Morisseau** (born March 13, 1978) is an American playwright and actress from [Detroit, Michigan](/wiki/Detroit). She has written more than nine plays, three of which are part of a cycle titled *The Detroit Project.* She received a [MacArthur Fellowship](/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program) (also known as the 'Genius Grant') in 2018.

## Early life

Morisseau was born and grew up in Detroit, Michigan, with her mother and father. Her mother's family is from Mississippi and her father's family is from [Haiti](/wiki/Haiti). Morisseau attended Bates Academy (a selective pre-K through 8 public school in Detroit) and [Cass Technical High School](/wiki/Cass_Technical_High_School). [[1]](https://www.freep.com/story/entertainment/arts/2022/06/13/tony-awards-cass-tech-alums-michael-r-jackson-dominique-morisseau/7609401001/) She attended the [University of Michigan](/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in [Ann Arbor](/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan), where she received her BFA in Acting in 2000.

There she met J. Keys, who is also from Michigan. They married in 2013. Keys was born in Detroit but grew up in Southfield, Michigan, a nearby suburb of the city. He works as a music industry promoter, emcee and hip hop musician.

## Career

### Acting

Morisseau began her performance career as a live poetry speaker, primarily in her hometown community of Harmonie Park in Detroit. After graduating from college, she continued acting and worked with several organizations. At the [Lark Play Development Center](/wiki/Lark_Play_Development_Center), she worked as an actor in a workshop production of *[The Mountaintop](/wiki/The_Mountaintop)* by [Katori Hall](/wiki/Katori_Hall), developing the role of Camae. In 2013, in a production at the [Actors Theatre of Louisville](/wiki/Actors_Theatre_of_Louisville), she reprised the role of Camae. She continues acting but has said that she would not act in any of her plays' premieres.

### Writing

Morisseau began writing plays in college. She has said that the lack of suitable roles at the University of Michigan drove her to write plays and create the roles she wanted to perform. She wrote *The Blackness Blues: Time to Change the Tune, A Sister's Story* at this time.

After college, in 2012 and 2013, she received a Playwrights of New York (PoNY) fellowship at the [Lark Play Development Center](/wiki/Lark_Play_Development_Center). She has also worked as a Teaching Artist with [City University of New York](/wiki/City_University_of_New_York)'s Creative Arts Team.

Morisseau has said that music plays a huge part in her work and often informs the work that she is writing. "It's a resource and clue to my work, and music plays a unifier among cultural barriers."

Morisseau was on the list of Top 20 Most Produced Playwrights in America in 2015–16, with 10 productions of her plays nationwide.

Morisseau is a story editor for the television series [*Shameless*](/wiki/Shameless_(U.S._TV_series)) on [Showtime](/wiki/Showtime_(TV_network)) and is also credited as a co-producer.

She wrote the book for the [jukebox musical](/wiki/Jukebox_musical) *[Ain't Too Proud – The Life and Times of the Temptations](/wiki/Ain%27t_Too_Proud_(musical))*, which was originally directed by [Des McAnuff](/wiki/Des_McAnuff). The musical opened on Broadway, marking her Broadway debut as a writer, at the [Imperial Theatre](/wiki/Imperial_Theatre) in March 2019. It played pre-Broadway engagements at the Berkeley Repertory Theatre (2017), the [Ahmanson Theatre](/wiki/Ahmanson_Theatre) in Los Angeles (August to September 2018), and the [Kennedy Center](/wiki/Kennedy_Center) (July 2018).  She received a nomination for a [Tony Award for Best Book of a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Book_of_a_Musical), the third Black woman to do so.

## Work

### *The Detroit Project*

Morisseau has written a three-play cycle, titled *The Detroit Project.* The three plays (in order) are:

#### *Detroit '67*

This play "explores an explosive and decisive moment in a great American city. The play's compelling characters struggle with racial tension and economic instability." It was developed and workshopped at [The Public Theater](/wiki/The_Public_Theater) in New York. *Detroit '67* eventually was featured at the [Classical Theatre of Harlem](/wiki/Classical_Theatre_of_Harlem) with the [National Black Theatre](/wiki/National_Black_Theatre). It was nominated for eight [AUDELCO](/wiki/AUDELCO) Theatre Awards and received the 2014 Edward M. Kennedy Prize for Drama Inspired by American History.

#### *Paradise Blue*

Former musician Blue decides to sell his beloved jazz club in order to live out his dreams. He is left with the moral dilemma of leaving his partner, Pumpkin, and his loyal jazz band behind. Morisseau developed this play first at [Williamstown Theatre Festival](/wiki/Williamstown_Theatre_Festival), where it eventually had its world premiere in July 2015. *Paradise Blue* continued its development at the [McCarter Theatre](/wiki/McCarter_Theatre), [New York Theatre Workshop](/wiki/New_York_Theatre_Workshop), [The Public Theater](/wiki/The_Public_Theater), and the [Signature Theatre Company](/wiki/Signature_Theatre_Company_(New_York_City)). For this play, Morisseau received the L. Arnold Weissberger Award in 2012.

#### *Skeleton Crew*

Main article: [Skeleton Crew (play)](/wiki/Skeleton_Crew_(play))

The final play in the cycle revolves around a group of auto-plant workers grappling with the likely possibility of foreclosure and impending unemployment. *[Skeleton Crew](/wiki/Skeleton_Crew_(play))* received a developmental production at the [Lark Play Development Center](/wiki/Lark_Play_Development_Center). Directed by [Ruben Santiago-Hudson](/wiki/Ruben_Santiago-Hudson), this play had its world premiere at the Off-Broadway [Linda Gross Theater](/wiki/Linda_Gross_Theater) with the [Atlantic Theater Company](/wiki/Atlantic_Theater_Company) in May 2016.

Morisseau won the 2016 [Obie Award](/wiki/Obie_Award) Special Citation for Collaboration, along with director Santiago-Hudson and the Atlantic Theater Company for *Skeleton Crew*. The play won the Edgerton Foundation New Play Award in 2015. *Skeleton Crew* opened on Broadway in January 2022. It was nominated for the Tony Award for Best Play.

## Awards

Morisseau received a [MacArthur Fellowship](/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program) (also known as the 'Genius Grant') fin 2018, which included a stipend of $625,000. She is one of 25 fellows in the 2018 Class.

Morisseau was named an Honoree for the [Jane Chambers](/wiki/Jane_Chambers) Playwriting Award, which recognizes plays and performance texts created by women that present a feminist perspective and contain significant opportunities for female performers.

She is a two-time award winner of the [NAACP](/wiki/National_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Colored_People) Image Award, which celebrates the outstanding achievements and performances of people of color in the arts, as well as those individuals or groups who promote social justice through their creative endeavors.

- Primus Prize by the [American Theatre Critics Association](/wiki/American_Theatre_Critics_Association) (honoree) for *Follow Me to Nellie's* in 2012
- Stavis Playwriting Award
- [University of Michigan](/wiki/University_of_Michigan): Emerging Leader Award
- [City of Detroit](/wiki/Detroit): Spirit of Detroit Award
- Morisseau, along with [Ruben Santiago-Hudson](/wiki/Ruben_Santiago-Hudson), received the [Apple Award](https://theatreanddance.wayne.edu/events/appleaward) from the [Department of Theatre and Dance](https://theatreanddance.wayne.edu) at [Wayne State University](/wiki/Wayne_State_University) in 2022 for Outsdanding Contributions to Theatre in the USA. The Apple Award was endowed by the [Nederlander](/wiki/Nederlander_Organization) Family in memory of Sarah Applebaum Nederlander.
- Edward M. Kennedy Prize for Drama, 2014, for her play *Detroit '67*
- Steinberg Playwright Award, 2015
- [OBIE Award](/wiki/Obie_Award) for "Special Citation: Collaboration" for her and Ruben Santiago-Hudson (director) for *Skeleton Crew* at Atlantic Theater Company, 2016
- [Windham Campbell Literature Prize](/wiki/Windham%E2%80%93Campbell_Literature_Prizes) for drama, 2023.

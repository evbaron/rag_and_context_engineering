**Maine** ([/meɪn/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:En-Maine-pronunciation.ogg) [*MAYN*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [state](/wiki/U.S._state) in the [New England](/wiki/New_England) region of the [United States](/wiki/United_States), and the northeasternmost state in the [contiguous United States](/wiki/Contiguous_United_States). It borders [New Hampshire](/wiki/New_Hampshire) to the west, the [Gulf of Maine](/wiki/Gulf_of_Maine) to the southeast, and the [Canadian provinces](/wiki/Provinces_and_territories_of_Canada) of [New Brunswick](/wiki/New_Brunswick) and [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec) to the northeast and northwest, and shares a maritime border with [Nova Scotia](/wiki/Nova_Scotia). It is the only state to border only one other state (New Hampshire). Maine is the largest state in New England by total area, almost as large as the combined area of the remaining five states. Of the [50 U.S. states](/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States), it is the [12th-smallest by area](/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area), the [9th-least populous](/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population), the [13th-least densely populated](/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density), and the most rural. As of 2024, Maine's population stood at a Census-estimated 1,400,000, the state's highest-ever population estimate. Maine's capital is [Augusta](/wiki/Augusta,_Maine), while its most populous city is [Portland](/wiki/Portland,_Maine).

The territory of Maine has been inhabited by [Indigenous populations](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Northeastern_Woodlands) for about 12,000 years, after the glaciers retreated during the [last ice age](/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). At the time of European arrival, several [Algonquian](/wiki/Algonquian_languages)-speaking nations governed the area and these nations are now known as the [Wabanaki Confederacy](/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy). The first European settlement in the area was by the French in 1604 on [Saint Croix Island](/wiki/Saint_Croix_Island,_Maine), founded by [Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons](/wiki/Pierre_Dugua,_Sieur_de_Mons). The first English settlement was the short-lived [Popham Colony](/wiki/Popham_Colony), established by the [Plymouth Company](/wiki/Plymouth_Company) in 1607. A number of Irish and English settlements were established along the coast of Maine in the 1620s, although the rugged climate and conflict with the local [Indigenous people](/wiki/Indigenous_people) and the French caused many to fail. Maine experienced significant warfare during the 17th century. As Maine entered the 18th century, only a half dozen European settlements had survived.

[Loyalist](/wiki/Loyalist_(American_Revolution)) and [Patriot](/wiki/Patriot_(American_Revolution)) forces contended for Maine's territory during the [American Revolution](/wiki/American_Revolution). During the [War of 1812](/wiki/War_of_1812), the largely undefended eastern region of Maine was occupied by British forces with the goal of annexing it to [Canada](/wiki/British_North_America) via the [Colony of New Ireland](/wiki/New_Ireland_(Maine)), but returned to the United States following failed British offensives on the northern border, mid-Atlantic and south which produced a [peace treaty](/wiki/Treaty_of_Ghent) that restored the pre-war boundaries. Maine was part of the [Commonwealth of Massachusetts](/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Massachusetts) until 1820 when it voted to secede from Massachusetts to become a separate state. On March 15, 1820, under the [Missouri Compromise](/wiki/Missouri_Compromise), Maine was [admitted to the Union](/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union) as the 23rd state.

Today, Maine is known for its jagged, rocky [Atlantic Ocean](/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean) and bay-shore coastlines, mountains, heavily [forested](/wiki/Forest) interior, and its cuisine, particularly [wild lowbush blueberries](/wiki/Vaccinium_angustifolium) and [seafood](/wiki/Seafood) such as [lobster](/wiki/American_lobster) and [clams](/wiki/Clam). Coastal and [Down East Maine](/wiki/Down_East_Maine) have emerged as important centers for the [creative economy](/wiki/Creative_economy), especially in [the vicinity of Portland](/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area,_Maine), which has also brought [gentrification](/wiki/Gentrification) to the city and its metropolitan area.

## History

The earliest known inhabitants of the territory that is now Maine were Algonquian-speaking Wabanaki peoples, including the [Passamaquoddy](/wiki/Passamaquoddy), [Maliseet](/wiki/Maliseet), [Penobscot](/wiki/Penobscot_people), [Androscoggin](/wiki/Androscoggin_people), and Kennebec. During the later [King Philip's War](/wiki/King_Philip%27s_War), many of these peoples would merge in one form or another to become the [Wabanaki Confederacy](/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy), aiding the [Wampanoag](/wiki/Wampanoag) of [Massachusetts](/wiki/Massachusetts) and the [Mahican](/wiki/Mahican) of [New York](/wiki/New_York_(state)). Afterwards, many of these people were driven from their natural territories, but most of Maine's tribes continued, unchanged, until the [American Revolution](/wiki/American_Revolution). Before this point, however, most of these people were considered separate nations. Many had adapted to living in permanent, [Iroquois](/wiki/Iroquois)-inspired settlements, while those along the coast tended to move from summer villages to winter villages on a yearly cycle. They would usually winter inland and head to the coasts by summer.

[European contact](/wiki/European_exploration_of_North_America) with what is now called Maine may have started around 1000 CE when [Vikings](/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_North_America) are believed to have interacted with the native [Penobscot](/wiki/Penobscot) in present-day [Hancock County](/wiki/Hancock_County,_Maine), most likely through trade. If confirmed, this would make Maine the site of the earliest European appearance in the entire US. About 200 years earlier, from the settlements in [Iceland](/wiki/Iceland) and [Greenland](/wiki/Greenland), the Norse [first identified America](/wiki/Norse_colonization_of_the_Americas) and [attempted to settle](/wiki/L%27Anse_aux_Meadows) areas such as [Newfoundland](/wiki/Newfoundland), but failed to establish a permanent settlement. Archeological evidence suggests that [Vikings in Greenland](/wiki/Vikings_in_Greenland) returned to North America for several [centuries](/wiki/Century) after the initial discovery to trade and collect timber, with the most relevant evidence being the [Maine Penny](/wiki/Maine_penny), an 11th-century Norwegian coin found at a Native American dig site in 1954.

The first European confirmed settlement in modern-day Maine was in 1604 on [Saint Croix Island](/wiki/Saint_Croix_Island,_Maine), led by French explorer [Pierre Dugua, Sieur de Mons](/wiki/Pierre_Dugua,_Sieur_de_Mons). His party included [Samuel de Champlain](/wiki/Samuel_de_Champlain), noted as an explorer. The French named the entire area [Acadia](/wiki/Acadia), including the portion that later became the state of Maine. (It is possible that they named the region Maine after the [French province](/wiki/Maine_(province)); however, debate exists as to the origin of the name of the state.) The [Plymouth Company](/wiki/Plymouth_Company) established the first English settlement in Maine at the [Popham Colony](/wiki/Popham_Colony) in 1607, the same year as the settlement at [Jamestown, Virginia](/wiki/Jamestown,_Virginia). The Popham colonists returned to [Britain](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) after 14 months.

The French established two [Jesuit](/wiki/Jesuit) missions: one on [Penobscot Bay](/wiki/Penobscot_Bay) in 1609, and the other on [Mount Desert Island](/wiki/Mount_Desert_Island) in 1613. The same year, [Claude de La Tour](/wiki/Claude_de_Saint-%C3%89tienne_de_la_Tour) established [Castine](/wiki/Castine,_Maine). In 1625, [Charles de Saint-Étienne de la Tour](/wiki/Charles_de_Saint-%C3%89tienne_de_la_Tour) erected [Fort Pentagouet](/wiki/Fort_Pentagouet) to protect Castine. The coastal areas of eastern Maine first became the [Province of Maine](/wiki/Province_of_Maine) in a 1622 land patent. The part of western Maine north of the [Kennebec River](/wiki/Kennebec_River) was more sparsely settled and was known in the 17th century as the [Territory of Sagadahock](/wiki/Territory_of_Sagadahock). A second settlement was attempted in 1623 by English explorer and naval Captain [Christopher Levett](/wiki/Christopher_Levett) at a place called [York](/wiki/York,_Maine), where he had been granted 6,000 acres (24 km2) by [King Charles I](/wiki/Charles_I_of_England) of England. It also failed.

The 1622 patent of the Province of Maine was split at the [Piscataqua River](/wiki/Piscataqua_River) into the [Province of New Hampshire](/wiki/Province_of_New_Hampshire) to the south and [New Somersetshire](/wiki/New_Somersetshire) to the north. A disputed 1630 patent split off the area around present-day [Saco](/wiki/Saco,_Maine) as [Lygonia](/wiki/Lygonia). Justifying its actions with a 1652 geographic survey that showed an overlapping patent, the [Massachusetts Bay Colony](/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) had seized New Somersetshire and Lygonia by force by 1658. The [Territory of Sagadahock](/wiki/Territory_of_Sagadahock) between the [Kennebec River](/wiki/Kennebec_River) and [St. Croix River](/wiki/St._Croix_River_(Maine%E2%80%93New_Brunswick)) notionally became [Cornwall County, Province of New York](/wiki/Cornwall_County,_Province_of_New_York) under a 1664 grant from [Charles II of England](/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) to his [brother James](/wiki/James_II_of_England), at the time the [Duke of York](/wiki/Duke_of_York). Some of this land was claimed by [New France](/wiki/New_France) as part of [Acadia](/wiki/Acadia). All of the English settlements in the Massachusetts Bay Colony and the Province of New York became part of the [Dominion of New England](/wiki/Dominion_of_New_England) in 1686. All of present-day Maine was unified as [York County, Massachusetts](/wiki/York_County,_Massachusetts) under a 1691 royal patent for the [Province of Massachusetts Bay](/wiki/Province_of_Massachusetts_Bay).

Central Maine was formerly inhabited by the [Androscoggin tribe](/wiki/Androscoggin_tribe) of the [Abenaki nation](/wiki/Abenaki), also known as Arosaguntacook. They were driven out of the area in 1690 during [King William's War](/wiki/King_William%27s_War). They were relocated to [St. Francis](/wiki/Saint-Fran%C3%A7ois-du-Lac,_Quebec), Canada, which was destroyed by [Rogers' Rangers](/wiki/Rogers%27_Rangers) in 1759, and is now [Odanak](/wiki/Odanak). The other Abenaki tribes suffered several severe defeats, particularly during [Dummer's War](/wiki/Dummer%27s_War), with the capture of [Norridgewock](/wiki/Norridgewock) in 1724 and the defeat of the [Pequawket](/wiki/Pequawket) in 1725, which significantly reduced their numbers. They finally withdrew to [Canada](/wiki/Canada), where they were settled at [Bécancour](/wiki/B%C3%A9cancour,_Quebec) and [Sillery](/wiki/Sillery,_Quebec_City), and later at St. Francis, along with other refugee tribes from the south.

Maine was much fought over by the [French](/wiki/French_colonial_empire), English, and allied natives during the 17th and 18th centuries. These natives conducted raids against settlers and each other, taking captives for ransom or, in some cases, kidnapped for adoption by native tribes. A notable example was the early 1692 [Abenaki](/wiki/Abenaki) [raid on York](/wiki/Raid_on_York_(1692)), where about 100 English settlers were killed and another estimated 80 taken hostage. The [Abenaki](/wiki/Abenaki) took captives taken during raids of [Massachusetts](/wiki/Massachusetts) in [Queen Anne's War](/wiki/Queen_Anne%27s_War) of the early 1700s to [Kahnewake](/wiki/Kahnewake), a Catholic [Mohawk](/wiki/Mohawk_nation) village near [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), where some were adopted and others ransomed.

After the British defeated the French in Acadia in the 1740s, the territory from the [Penobscot River](/wiki/Penobscot_River) east fell under the nominal authority of the [Province of Nova Scotia](/wiki/Nova_Scotia), and together with present-day New Brunswick formed the Nova Scotia county of [Sunbury](/wiki/Sunbury_County,_Nova_Scotia), with its court of general sessions at Campobello. American and British forces contended for Maine's territory during the American Revolution and the War of 1812, with the British occupying eastern Maine in both conflicts via the [Colony of New Ireland](/wiki/New_Ireland_(Maine)). The territory of Maine was confirmed as part of Massachusetts when the United States was formed following the [Treaty of Paris](/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783)) ending the revolution, although the final border with [British North America](/wiki/British_North_America) was not established until the [Webster–Ashburton Treaty](/wiki/Webster%E2%80%93Ashburton_Treaty) of 1842.

Maine was physically separate from the rest of Massachusetts. Longstanding disagreements over land speculation and settlements led to Maine residents and their allies in Massachusetts proper forcing an 1807 vote in the Massachusetts Assembly on permitting Maine to secede; the vote failed. Secessionist sentiment in Maine was stoked during the [War of 1812](/wiki/War_of_1812) when Massachusetts pro-British merchants opposed the war and refused to defend Maine from British invaders. In 1819, Massachusetts agreed to permit secession, sanctioned by voters of the rapidly growing region the following year.

### Statehood and Missouri Compromise

Formal secession from Massachusetts and admission of Maine as the 23rd state occurred on March 15, 1820, as part of the [Missouri Compromise](/wiki/Missouri_Compromise), which geographically restricted the spread of [slavery](/wiki/Slavery) and enabled the admission to statehood of [Missouri](/wiki/Missouri) the following year, keeping a balance between [slave](/wiki/Slave_state) and free states.

Maine's original state capital was Portland, Maine's largest city, until it was moved to the more central Augusta in 1832. The principal office of the [Maine Supreme Judicial Court](/wiki/Maine_Supreme_Judicial_Court) remains in Portland.

The [20th Maine Volunteer Infantry Regiment](/wiki/20th_Maine_Volunteer_Infantry_Regiment), under the command of Colonel [Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain](/wiki/Joshua_Lawrence_Chamberlain), prevented the [Union Army](/wiki/Union_Army) from being flanked at [Little Round Top](/wiki/Little_Round_Top) by the [Confederate Army](/wiki/Confederate_Army) during the [Battle of Gettysburg](/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg).

Four [U.S. Navy](/wiki/United_States_Navy) ships have been named [USS *Maine*](/wiki/USS_Maine), most famously the [armored cruiser](/wiki/Armored_cruiser) [USS *Maine* (ACR-1)](/wiki/USS_Maine_(1890)), whose sinking by an explosion on February 15, 1898, precipitated the [Spanish–American War](/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War).

### Political history

Many notable politicians have shaped the history of Maine, including many notable [senators](/wiki/List_of_United_States_senators_from_Maine). During the 19th century, [Hannibal Hamlin](/wiki/Hannibal_Hamlin), who served as Senator and briefly Governor, was the running mate of Republican, [Abraham Lincoln](/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln), and served one term as vice-president from 1861 to 1865 before being replaced by [Andrew Johnson](/wiki/Andrew_Johnson) of [Tennessee](/wiki/Tennessee), while Senator and Congressman, [James G. Blaine](/wiki/James_G._Blaine), nicknamed the " Plumed Knight ", served many top positions such as [Speaker of the House](/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) and [Secretary of State](/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State). He was the major Republican candidate in the [1884 United States presidential election](/wiki/1884_United_States_presidential_election), losing narrowly to [Grover Cleveland](/wiki/Grover_Cleveland). In the 20th century, Republican, [Margaret Chase Smith](/wiki/Margaret_Chase_Smith) made history as the first woman to serve in both the [House](/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) and [Senate](/wiki/United_States_Senate), when elected in 1948. In 1950, she delivered her famed [Declaration of Conscience](/wiki/Declaration_of_Conscience) speech, that defended civil liberties in the wake of [McCarthyism](/wiki/McCarthyism), while Democrat [Edmund Muskie](/wiki/Edmund_Muskie), who served as Governor and Senator, was the running mate of vice-president incumbent, [Hubert Humphrey](/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey) in [1968 United States presidential election](/wiki/1968_United_States_presidential_election) and briefly served as [Secretary of State](/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State) in 1980 under [Jimmy Carter](/wiki/Jimmy_Carter).

A map of Maine showing its famed jagged coast

To the south and east is the [Gulf of Maine](/wiki/Gulf_of_Maine), and to the west is the state of [New Hampshire](/wiki/New_Hampshire). The Canadian province of [New Brunswick](/wiki/New_Brunswick) is to the north and northeast, and the province of [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec) is to the northwest. Maine is the northernmost and largest state in New England, accounting for almost half of the region's entire land area. Maine is the only state to border exactly one other American state. Approximately half the area of Maine lies on each side of the [45th parallel north](/wiki/45th_parallel_north) in [latitude](/wiki/Latitude).

Maine is the easternmost state in the [contiguous United States](/wiki/Contiguous_United_States) both in its extreme points and its geographic center. The town of [Lubec](/wiki/Lubec,_Maine) is the easternmost organized settlement in the United States. Its Quoddy Head Lighthouse is also the closest place in the United States to Africa and Europe. [Estcourt Station](/wiki/Estcourt_Station,_Maine) is Maine's northernmost point, as well as the northernmost point in New England. (For more information see [extreme points of the United States](/wiki/Extreme_points_of_the_United_States))

Maine's [Moosehead Lake](/wiki/Moosehead_Lake) is the largest lake wholly in New England, since [Lake Champlain](/wiki/Lake_Champlain) is located between [Vermont](/wiki/Vermont), [New York](/wiki/New_York_(state)), and [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec). A number of other Maine lakes, such as [South Twin Lake](/wiki/South_Twin_Lake_(Maine)), are described by [Thoreau](/wiki/Thoreau) in *The Maine Woods* (1864). [Mount Katahdin](/wiki/Mount_Katahdin) is the northern terminus of the [Appalachian Trail](/wiki/Appalachian_Trail), which extends southerly to [Springer Mountain](/wiki/Springer_Mountain), [Georgia](/wiki/Georgia_(U.S._state)), and the southern terminus of the new [International Appalachian Trail](/wiki/International_Appalachian_Trail) which, when complete, will run to [Belle Isle](/wiki/Belle_Isle_(Newfoundland_and_Labrador)), [Newfoundland and Labrador](/wiki/Newfoundland_and_Labrador).

[Machias Seal Island](/wiki/Machias_Seal_Island) and [North Rock](/wiki/North_Rock), off the state's Downeast coast, are claimed by both [Canada](/wiki/Canada) and the Maine town of [Cutler](/wiki/Cutler,_Maine), and are within one of [four areas between the two countries whose sovereignty is still in dispute](/wiki/List_of_areas_disputed_by_Canada_and_the_United_States), but it is the only one of the disputed areas containing land. Also in this easternmost area in the [Bay of Fundy](/wiki/Bay_of_Fundy) is the [Old Sow](/wiki/Old_Sow), the largest [tidal](/wiki/Tide) [whirlpool](/wiki/Whirlpool) in the [Western Hemisphere](/wiki/Western_Hemisphere).

Maine is the least densely populated state east of the [Mississippi River](/wiki/Mississippi_River). It is called the [Pine Tree State](/wiki/List_of_U.S._state_and_territory_nicknames) due to its largest distribution and presence of [pine](/wiki/Pine), including *[Pinus strobus](/wiki/Pinus_strobus)* and *[Pinus resinosa](/wiki/Pinus_resinosa)*. Over 80% of its total area is forested or unclaimed, [the most forest cover of any U.S. state](/wiki/Forest_cover_by_state_and_territory_in_the_United_States). In the wooded areas of the interior lies much uninhabited land, some of which does not have formal political organization into local units (a rarity in New England). The [Northwest Aroostook](/wiki/Northwest_Aroostook,_Maine) [unorganized territory](/wiki/Unincorporated_area#U.S._Census_Bureau) in the northern part of the state, for example, has an area of 2,668 square miles (6,910 km2) and a population of 10, or one person for every 267 square miles (690 km2).

Maine is in the [temperate broadleaf and mixed forests](/wiki/Temperate_broadleaf_and_mixed_forests) [biome](/wiki/Biome). The land near the southern and central Atlantic coast is covered by the mixed [oaks](/wiki/Quercus) of the [Northeastern coastal forests](/wiki/Northeastern_coastal_forests). The remainder of the state, including the [North Woods](/wiki/North_Maine_Woods), is covered by the [New England–Acadian forests](/wiki/New_England%E2%80%93Acadian_forests).

Maine has almost 230 miles (400 km) of ocean coastline (and 3,500 miles (5,600 km) of tidal coastline). [West Quoddy Head](/wiki/West_Quoddy_Head) in Lubec is the easternmost point of land in the 48 contiguous states. Along the famous rock-bound coast of Maine are lighthouses, beaches, fishing villages, and thousands of offshore islands, including the [Isles of Shoals](/wiki/Isles_of_Shoals) which straddle the New Hampshire border. There are jagged rocks and cliffs and many bays and inlets. Inland are lakes, rivers, forests, and mountains. This visual contrast of forested slopes sweeping down to the sea has been summed up by American poet [Edna St. Vincent Millay](/wiki/Edna_St._Vincent_Millay) of [Rockland](/wiki/Rockland,_Maine) and [Camden](/wiki/Camden,_Maine), in "Renascence":

The Maine coast and [Portland Head Light](/wiki/Portland_Head_Light)

Rocky shoreline in [Acadia National Park](/wiki/Acadia_National_Park)

> All I could see from where I stood
> Was three long mountains and a wood;
> I turned and looked the other way,
> And saw three islands in a bay.

— Edna St. Vincent Millay, Renascence

Geologists describe this type of landscape as a "drowned coast", where a rising sea level has invaded former land features, creating bays out of valleys and islands out of mountain tops. A rise in land elevation due to the melting of heavy glacier ice caused a slight rebounding effect of underlying rock; this land rise, however, was not enough to eliminate all the effect of the rising sea level and its invasion of former land features.

Much of Maine's geomorphology was created by extended glacial activity at the end of the [last ice age](/wiki/Quaternary_glaciation). Prominent glacial features include [Somes Sound](/wiki/Somes_Sound) and Bubble Rock, both part of Acadia National Park on Mount Desert Island. Carved by glaciers, Somes Sound reaches depths of 175 feet (50 m). The extreme depth and steep drop-off allow large ships to navigate almost the entire length of the sound. These features also have made it attractive for boat builders, such as the prestigious [Hinckley Yachts](/wiki/Hinckley_Yachts).

Bubble Rock, a [glacial erratic](/wiki/Glacial_erratic), is a large boulder perched on the edge of Bubble Mountain in [Acadia National Park](/wiki/Acadia_National_Park). By analyzing the type of granite, geologists discovered that glaciers carried Bubble Rock to its present location from near [Lucerne](/wiki/Lucerne,_Maine), 30 miles (48 km) away. The [Iapetus Suture](/wiki/Iapetus_Suture) runs through the north and west of the state, being underlain by the ancient [Laurentian terrane](/wiki/Laurentia), and the south and east underlain by the [Avalonian terrane](/wiki/Avalonia).

Acadia National Park is the only national park in New England. Areas under the protection and management of the [National Park Service](/wiki/National_Park_Service) include:

- [Acadia National Park](/wiki/Acadia_National_Park) near [Bar Harbor](/wiki/Bar_Harbor,_Maine)
- [Appalachian National Scenic Trail](/wiki/Appalachian_Trail)
- Maine Acadian Culture in [St. John Valley](/wiki/Saint_John_River_(Bay_of_Fundy))
- [Roosevelt Campobello International Park](/wiki/Roosevelt_Campobello_International_Park) on [Campobello Island](/wiki/Campobello_Island) in [New Brunswick](/wiki/New_Brunswick), Canada, operated by both the U.S. and Canada, just across the [Franklin Delano Roosevelt Bridge](/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt_Bridge) from Lubec
- [Saint Croix Island International Historic Site](/wiki/Saint_Croix_Island_International_Historic_Site) at [Calais](/wiki/Calais,_Maine)
- [Katahdin Woods and Waters National Monument](/wiki/Katahdin_Woods_and_Waters_National_Monument)

### Climate

See also: [Climate change in Maine](/wiki/Climate_change_in_Maine)

Autumn in the [Hundred-Mile Wilderness](/wiki/Hundred-Mile_Wilderness)

[Köppen climate types](/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Maine, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](/wiki/Climatological_normal)

Winter in [Bangor](/wiki/Bangor,_Maine)

Maine has a humid continental climate ([Köppen climate classification](/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb*), with warm and sometimes humid summers, and long, cold and very snowy winters. Winters are especially severe in the northern and western parts of Maine, while coastal areas are moderated slightly by the [Atlantic Ocean](/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean), resulting in marginally milder winters and cooler summers than inland regions. Daytime highs are generally in the 75–85 °F (24–29 °C) range throughout the state in July, with overnight lows in the high 50s °F (around 15 °C). January temperatures range from highs near 30 °F (−1 °C) on the southern coast to overnight lows averaging below 0 °F (−18 °C) in the far north.

The state's record high temperature is 105 °F (41 °C), set in July 1911, at North Bridgton.
Precipitation in Maine is evenly distributed year-round, but with a slight summer maximum in northern/northwestern Maine and a slight late-fall or early-winter maximum along the coast due to "[nor'easters](/wiki/Nor%27easter)" or intense cold-season rain and snowstorms. In coastal Maine, the late spring and summer months are usually driest—a rarity across the Eastern United States. Maine has fewer days of thunderstorms than any other state east of the [Rockies](/wiki/Rocky_Mountains), with most of the state averaging fewer than twenty days of [thunderstorms](/wiki/Thunderstorm) a year. [Tornadoes](/wiki/Tornado) are rare in Maine, with the state averaging two per year, although this number is increasing. Most severe thunderstorms and tornadoes occur in the southwestern interior portion of the state, where summer temperatures are often the warmest and the atmosphere is thus more unstable compared to northern and coastal areas. Maine rarely sees the direct landfall of [tropical cyclones](/wiki/Tropical_cyclone), as they tend to recurve out to sea or are rapidly weakening by the time they reach the cooler waters of Maine.

In January 2009, a new record low temperature for the state was set at [Big Black River](/wiki/Big_Black_River_(Saint_John_River)) of −50 °F (−46 °C), tying the New England record.

Annual precipitation varies from 35.8 in (909 mm) in [Presque Isle](/wiki/Presque_Isle,_Maine) to 56.7 in (1,441 mm) in Acadia National Park.

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Fauna of Maine](/wiki/Fauna_of_Maine)

Maine exhibits a diverse range of flora and fauna across its varied landscapes, including forests, coastline, and wetlands. Forested areas consist primarily of coniferous and deciduous trees, such as [balsam fir](/wiki/Abies_balsamea), [sugar maple](/wiki/Acer_saccharum), and its state tree, the [Eastern white pine](/wiki/Pinus_strobus). Coastal regions are characterized by hardy [sea milkwort](/wiki/Lysimachia_maritima), [sea-blight](/wiki/Suaeda), [bayberry](/wiki/Myrica), and the invasive [rugosa rose](/wiki/Rosa_rugosa).

Maine's terrestrial fauna comprises mammals such as [moose](/wiki/Moose), [black bears](/wiki/American_black_bear), and [white-tailed deer](/wiki/White-tailed_deer), along with smaller species like [red squirrels](/wiki/Red_squirrel), [snowshoe hares](/wiki/Snowshoe_hare), and [raccoons](/wiki/Raccoon). Maine has the largest populations of moose and black bears in the contiguous United States. Avian diversity is evident with migratory birds like [piping plovers](/wiki/Piping_plover), [American oystercatcher](/wiki/American_oystercatcher), and [northern harrier](/wiki/Northern_harrier), as well as resident species like [black-capped chickadees](/wiki/Black-capped_chickadee), [blue jays](/wiki/Jay), and [barred owls](/wiki/Barred_owl). Wetlands provide habitat for amphibians such as [spotted salamanders](/wiki/Spotted_salamander), [wood frogs](/wiki/Wood_frog), and toads. Freshwater habitats support fish species like [brook trout](/wiki/Brook_trout), [landlocked salmon](/wiki/Landlocked_salmon), and multiple [gamefish](/wiki/Game_fish). The rivers of central and eastern Maine are home to the US' last remaining wild populations of [Atlantic salmon](/wiki/Atlantic_salmon) (*Salmo salar*). Marine life in offshore waters includes [Atlantic puffins](/wiki/Atlantic_puffin), [harbor seals](/wiki/Harbor_seal), [minke whales](/wiki/Minke_whale), and [lobster](/wiki/American_lobster). Maine's abundance of lobster makes the state the largest producer of lobster in the United States.

The [U.S. Census Bureau](/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) estimates that the population of Maine was 1,344,212 on July 1, 2019, a 1.19% increase since the [2010 United States census](/wiki/2010_United_States_Census). At the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), 1,362,359 people lived in the state. The state's population density is 41.3 people per square mile, making it the [least densely populated state](/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density) east of the [Mississippi River](/wiki/Mississippi_River). As of 2010, Maine was also the most rural state in the Union, with only 38.7% of the state's population living within urban areas. As explained in detail under "Geography", there are large tracts of uninhabited land in some remote parts of the interior of the state, particularly in the [North Maine Woods](/wiki/North_Maine_Woods).

The mean population center of Maine is located in [Kennebec County](/wiki/Kennebec_County,_Maine), just east of Augusta. The [Greater Portland metropolitan area](/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area,_Maine) is the most densely populated with nearly 40% of Maine's population. This area spans three counties and includes many farms and wooded areas; the 2016 population of Portland proper was 66,937.

Maine has experienced a very slow rate of population growth since the 1990 census; its rate of growth (0.57%) since the 2010 census ranks 45th of the 50 states. In 2021 and 2022, however, Maine had the highest proportion of arriving residents to departing residents of any state in the country, with 1.8 arrivals for every departure. The modest population growth in the state has been concentrated in the southern coastal counties; with more diverse populations slowly moving into these areas of the state. However, the northern, more rural areas of the state have experienced a slight decline in population from 2010 to 2016.

As of 2020, Maine has the highest population age 65 or older in the United States.

According to the [2010 census](/wiki/2010_United_States_census), Maine has the highest percentage of non-Hispanic [White](/wiki/White_American) of any state, at 94.4% of the total population. In 2011, 89.0% of all births in the state were to non-Hispanic White parents. Maine also has the second-highest residential senior population.

According to [HUD](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 4,411 [homeless](/wiki/Homelessness_in_Maine) people in Maine.

Today there are four [federally recognized](/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes_by_state) tribes in Maine, including the [Mi'kmaq Nation](/wiki/Mi%27kmaq_Nation). In 2020, 7,885 identified as being Native American alone, and 25,617 did in combination with one or more other races.

### Language

Maine does not have an official language, but the most widely spoken language in the state is English. The 2010 census reported 92.91% of Maine residents aged five and older spoke only English at home. French-speakers are the state's chief linguistic minority; census figures show that Maine has the highest percentage of people speaking French at home of any state: 3.93% of Maine households are French-speaking, compared with 3.45% (including Cajun and [Creole](/wiki/Louisiana_Creole)) in [Louisiana](/wiki/Louisiana), which is the second highest state. Spanish is the third-most-common language in Maine, after English and French.

In a 2022 study by the Public Religion Research Institute, 63% of the population were Christian, and 30% were religiously unaffiliated. Among the non-Christian population in 2022, 1% were [Unitarian Universalist](/wiki/Unitarian_Universalism), 5% [Jewish](/wiki/Jews), and 1% [New Ager](/wiki/New_Age).

According to the [Association of Religion Data Archives](/wiki/Association_of_Religion_Data_Archives) in 2020, with Christianity as the dominant faith, the largest denominations by number of adherents were Catholicism (219,233 members), non-denominational Protestantism (45,364), and United Methodists (19,686). According to the same study, there were an estimated 16,894 [Muslims](/wiki/Islam_in_the_United_States) in the state.

## Economy

See also: [Maine locations by per capita income](/wiki/Maine_locations_by_per_capita_income) and [List of power stations in Maine](/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Maine)

Total employment (May 2024):

- 674,900

Total employer establishments (2021):

- 42,519

In 2025, Maine's total [gross state product](/wiki/Gross_state_product) was $102.8 billion and the state's [per capita personal income](/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income_in_the_United_States) was $71,662. Its median gross income was $69,543. As of May 2025, Maine's [unemployment rate](/wiki/Unemployment) is 3.4%. As of January 2025, Maine's [minimum wage](/wiki/Minimum_wage) is $14.65. In 2025, [small businesses](/wiki/Small_business) made up 99.2% of Maine's businesses, and employed 54.5% of the state's workforce.

Maine's industrial outputs consist chiefly of paper, lumber and wood products, electronic equipment, leather products, food products, textiles, and bio-technology. Naval shipbuilding and construction remain key as well, with [Bath Iron Works](/wiki/Bath_Iron_Works) in Bath and [Portsmouth Naval Shipyard](/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Shipyard) in Kittery.

Brunswick Landing, formerly [Naval Air Station Brunswick](/wiki/Naval_Air_Station_Brunswick), is also in Maine. Formerly a large support base for the U.S. Navy, the [BRAC](/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure) campaign initiated the Naval Air Station's closing, despite a government-funded effort to upgrade its facilities. The former base has since been changed into a civilian business park, as well as a new satellite campus for [Southern Maine Community College](/wiki/Southern_Maine_Community_College).

Historically, Maine ports played a key role in national transportation. Beginning around 1880, Portland's rail link and [ice-free port](/wiki/Ice-free_port) made it Canada's principal winter port, until the aggressive development of [Halifax](/wiki/Halifax_Regional_Municipality,_Nova_Scotia), Nova Scotia in the mid-20th century. In 2013, 12,039,600 [short tons](/wiki/Short_tons) passed into and out of Portland by sea, which places it 45th of U.S. water ports. [Portland International Jetport](/wiki/Portland_International_Jetport) has been expanded, providing the state with increased air traffic from carriers such as [JetBlue](/wiki/JetBlue) and [Southwest Airlines](/wiki/Southwest_Airlines).

Maine has very few large companies that maintain headquarters in the state, and that number has fallen due to consolidations and mergers, particularly in the [pulp and paper industry](/wiki/Pulp_and_paper_industry). Some of the larger companies that do maintain headquarters in Maine include [Covetrus](/wiki/Covetrus) in Portland; [Fairchild Semiconductor](/wiki/Fairchild_Semiconductor) in South Portland; [IDEXX Laboratories](/wiki/IDEXX_Laboratories) in Westbrook; [Hannaford Bros. Co.](/wiki/Hannaford_Bros._Co.) in Scarborough; [L.L.Bean](/wiki/L.L.Bean) in [Freeport](/wiki/Freeport,_Maine); and [Puritan Medical Products](/wiki/Puritan_Medical_Products) in [Guilford](/wiki/Guilford,_Maine). Western Maine aquifers and springs are a source of bottled water for companies like [Poland Spring](/wiki/Poland_Spring).

### Tourism

Tourism and outdoor recreation play a major and increasingly important role in Maine's economy. In 2023, 15,267,000 visitors spent more than $9 billion in Maine. In 2024, Maine's outdoor recreation industry generated $3.9 billion and employed more than 32,000 people. An estimated 14% of the housing stock in Maine is used as vacation homes. In the late 19th century, artist colonies developed in [Ogunquit](/wiki/Ogunquit,_Maine) and on [Monhegan](/wiki/Monhegan,_Maine). [Acadia National Park](/wiki/Acadia_National_Park) became part of the national park system in 1929. The park is one of the most popular national parks in the United States. It attracts more than 4 million visitors each year. In 2024, visitors spent $475 million in the [Bar Harbor](/wiki/Bar_Harbor,_Maine) area where the park is located. The flagship [L.L. Bean](/wiki/L.L.Bean) store in [Freeport](/wiki/Freeport,_Maine) attracts more than 3 million visitors each year.

Most visitors arrive in the spring, summer, and fall, and southern Maine's coastal beaches, including those in [Old Orchard Beach](/wiki/Old_Orchard_Beach,_Maine), [Kittery](/wiki/Kittery,_Maine), [York](/wiki/York,_Maine), [Wells](/wiki/Wells,_Maine), and [Kennebunk](/wiki/Kennebunk,_Maine), are top destinations. In 2023, Maine's beaches attracted almost 4 million visitors who spent $2.5 billion. Visitors also visit interior Maine which has 6,000 lakes and ponds and many beaches, youth [summer camps](/wiki/Summer_camp), lakeside homes, cottages, and lake-related businesses. In 2024, a study determined that Maine's lakes and ponds have an estimated total net value of $14.2 billion per year.

Visitors also visit Maine in the winter. Between December 2024 and April 2025, more than 3 million out-of-state visitors came to Maine. Many arrive for the Thanksgiving and Christmas holidays, visiting towns including [Ogunquit](/wiki/Ogunquit,_Maine), which hosts a Christmas by the Sea festival, [Kennebunkport](/wiki/Kennebunkport,_Maine), which hosts [Christmas Prelude](/wiki/Christmas_Prelude), [Camden](/wiki/Camden,_Maine), which hosts a [Christmas by the Sea](/wiki/Camden,_Maine#Christmas_by_the_Sea) festival, and [Portland](/wiki/Portland,_Maine), where the [Portland Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/Portland_Symphony_Orchestra) hosts the annual [Magic of Christmas](/wiki/Portland_Symphony_Orchestra#Magic_of_Christmas) concert series and the [Maine Narrow Gauge Railroad](/wiki/Maine_Narrow_Gauge_Railroad_Museum) runs *[The Polar Express](/wiki/Maine_Narrow_Gauge_Railroad_Museum#The_Polar_Express_train)* Christmas train. At least eight [Hallmark Christmas movies](/wiki/Hallmark_Christmas_movies) have been set in Maine. Visitors also arrive to experience Maine's winter outdoor recreation, including Nordic and alpine skiing. During the 2022–2023 winter season, 1,372,128 out-of-state skiers visited Maine ski resorts, including [Sugarloaf](/wiki/Sugarloaf_(ski_resort)), [Sunday River](/wiki/Sunday_River_(ski_resort)), [Saddleback](/wiki/Saddleback_Maine), [Big Moose Mountain](/wiki/Big_Moose_Mountain_Ski_Area), [Black Mountain of Maine](/wiki/Black_Mountain_of_Maine), [Mount Abram](/wiki/Ski_Mount_Abram), and [Pleasant Mountain](/wiki/Pleasant_Mountain_Ski_Area).

Summer visitors and tourism began before the Civil War. After the Civil War, the number of visitors increased significantly. This was when a growing urban middle class wanted to leave cities in the summer to seek Maine's coast, lakes, woods, and mountains. Maine's fresh air, pure water, and local food were additional attractions.

Many notable persons have drawn attention to Maine's natural attractions. In 1846, [Henry David Thoreau](/wiki/Henry_David_Thoreau) came to Maine to climb [Katahdin](/wiki/Mount_Katahdin) and came back two more times to explore other trails and waterways. His book *The Maine Woods* was published in 1864 recounting his journeys in Maine. In 1849, [Elizabeth Oakes Smith](/wiki/Elizabeth_Oakes_Smith) climbed Katahdin and wrote about her experience. As early as 1878, [Theodore Roosevelt](/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) visited Maine. Roosevelt explored what is now designated the [Katahdin Woods and Waters National Monument](/wiki/Katahdin_Woods_and_Waters_National_Monument) with legendary [Maine guide](/wiki/Maine_Guide) William Wingate Sewall and stayed at the [William Sewall House](/wiki/William_Sewall_House). American businessman [William Henry Vanderbilt](/wiki/William_Henry_Vanderbilt) first vacationed with his family in Bar Harbor in the early 1880s. In 1910, businessman [John D. Rockefeller Jr.](/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller_Jr.) purchased a summer home in [Seal Harbor](/wiki/Mount_Desert,_Maine) and later spent $3.5 million developing the [Carriage Roads](/wiki/Acadia_National_Park_carriage_paths,_bridges_and_gatehouses) of what is now Acadia National Park. In 1997, TV personality and businesswomen [Martha Stewart](/wiki/Martha_Stewart) purchased Skylands in Seal Harbor, featuring the estate regularly in national media outlets. The state is a popular destination for [hiking](/wiki/Hiking), [snowmobiling](/wiki/Snowmobile), [skiing](/wiki/Skiing), boating, [camping](/wiki/Camping), fishing, and hunting, among other activities.

### Agriculture

Wild low-bush blueberries are only produced commercially in Maine.

Agriculture plays a significant role in Maine's economy. In 2020, Maine's 7,600 farms produced $2.1 billion in direct sales. Maine is home to the [Maine Organic Farmers and Gardeners Association](/wiki/Maine_Organic_Farmers_and_Gardeners_Association) and had 535 [certified organic](/wiki/Organic_certification) farms in 2019 that generated $60 million in direct sales.

Vegetable farming was the most valuable farming sector in Maine with $231,900,000 in sales in 2020. In 2020 dairy farming had $154,500,000 in direct sales, greenhouse and nursery production had $72,300,000 in direct sales, and fruit farming had $63,900,000 in direct sales. Maine's grain industry is undergoing a revival. In 2020, Maine farms had $26,300,000 in direct sales of grains and oilseeds, and the processing of grains and oilseeds generated an additional $89,800,000 in direct sales. Maine is a significant U.S.-producer of [maple syrup](/wiki/Maple_syrup), generating $56 million in 2025. Maine has approximately 500 licensed maple syrup producers who produced 549,000 gallons of maple syrup from almost 1.8 million taps in 2025.

Maine's [agricultural](/wiki/Agriculture) outputs include mixed vegetables, potatoes, wild blueberries, apples, grains, [maple syrup](/wiki/Maple_syrup), [maple sugar](/wiki/Maple_sugar), poultry, eggs, dairy products, and seaweed. [Aroostook County](/wiki/Aroostook_County,_Maine) is known for its [potato](/wiki/Potato) crops. Potatoes make the state $166,672,000 a year. Maine is the top U.S. producer of [low-bush blueberries](/wiki/Vaccinium_angustifolium). Preliminary data from the [USDA](/wiki/USDA) for 2012 also indicate Maine was the largest blueberry producer of the major blueberry producing states, with a total production of 91,100,000 lbs. This data includes both low (wild) and [high-bush (cultivated) blueberries](/wiki/Vaccinium_corymbosum). In 2017, the production of Maine's seaweed industry was estimated at $20 million per year. In 2022, Maine seaweed farmers harvested over 1 million pounds of seaweed, which was predicted to rise to 4.1 million pounds by 2025. Sixty percent of American seaweed comes from Maine.

### Commercial fishing

Lobstering in Portland

[Commercial fishing](/wiki/Commercial_fishing), once a mainstay of the state's economy, maintains a presence, particularly [lobstering](/wiki/Lobster_fishing) and [groundfishing](/wiki/Groundfishing). In 2025, Maine's commercial harvesters earned $619 million. While lobster is the main seafood focus for Maine, the harvest of oysters are on the rise. In 2015, 14% of the Northeast's total oyster supply came from Maine. The shrimp industry of Maine is on a government-mandated hold. With an ever-decreasing Northern shrimp population, Maine fishermen are no longer allowed to catch and sell shrimp. The hold began in 2014, but a pilot program allowing limited shrimp fishing began in 2025.

### Christmas tree farms

Since the early 20th century, Maine has been among the top producers of [Christmas trees](/wiki/Christmas_tree) and Christmas wreaths annually. Its 370 Christmas tree farms produce roughly 100,000 trees each year. The Christmas tree industry in Maine generates an estimated $19 million in direct economic impact annually and supports 800 jobs. On average, it takes Maine farmers seven to eight years to grow a Christmas tree to a sellable size. Many of Maine's Christmas tree farms are small, family-run operations that offer choose-your-own trees or sell trees at roadside stands. Ninety percent of Christmas trees grown in Maine are [balsam firs](/wiki/Abies_balsamea), the only fir tree native to Maine. As early as 1860, Maine Christmas tree farmers Thomas W. Jackson Jr. and his son Herbert A. Jackson of Portland were selling Christmas trees in New York City. The trees ranged in height from four feet to 25 feet tall.

### Shipbuilding

[Bath Iron Works](/wiki/Bath_Iron_Works) naval shipbuilding

Maine has a long-standing tradition of being home to many shipbuilding companies, such as [Bath Iron Works](/wiki/Bath_Iron_Works) and the [Portsmouth Naval Shipyard](/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Shipyard). In the 18th and 19th centuries, Maine was home to many shipyards that produced wooden sailing ships. The main function of these ships was to transport either cargos or passengers overseas. One of these yards was located in [Pennellville Historic District](/wiki/Pennellville_Historic_District) in what is now [Brunswick, Maine](/wiki/Brunswick,_Maine). This yard, owned by the Pennell family, was typical of the many family-owned shipbuilding companies of the time period. Other such examples of shipbuilding families were the Skolfields and the Morses. During the 18th and 19th centuries, wooden shipbuilding of this sort made up a sizable portion of the economy.

### Creative economy & the arts

Maine has developed a burgeoning [creative economy](/wiki/Creative_economy), most notably centered in the [Greater Portland](/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area,_Maine) vicinity. In 2022, Portland's nonprofit arts and culture industry generated $86 million in economic activity, according to a survey by [Americans for the Arts](/wiki/Americans_for_the_Arts). The foundation of Maine's creative economy is its long tradition of fine arts. It also includes craftsmen, historic sites, museums, and cultural attractions.

### Taxation

Further information: [State tax levels in the United States](/wiki/State_tax_levels_in_the_United_States)

Maine has an [income tax](/wiki/Income_tax) structure containing two brackets, 6.5 and 7.95 percent of personal income. Before July 2013, Maine had four brackets: 2, 4.5, 7, and 8.5 percent. Maine's general [sales tax](/wiki/Sales_tax) rate is 5.5 percent. The state also levies charges of nine percent on lodging and prepared food and ten percent on short-term auto rentals. Commercial sellers of blueberries, a Maine staple, must keep records of their transactions and pay the state 1.5 cents per pound ($1.50 per 100 pounds) of the fruit sold each season. All [real](/wiki/Real_property) and tangible [personal property](/wiki/Personal_property) located in the state of Maine is taxable unless specifically exempted by statute. The administration of property taxes is handled by the local assessor in incorporated cities and towns, while property taxes in the unorganized territories are handled by the State Tax Assessor.

### Airports

[Portland International Jetport](/wiki/Portland_International_Jetport)

Maine receives [passenger jet](/wiki/Jet_airliner) service at its two largest airports, the [Portland International Jetport](/wiki/Portland_International_Jetport) in Portland, and the [Bangor International Airport](/wiki/Bangor_International_Airport) in Bangor. Both are served daily by many major [airlines](/wiki/Airline) to destinations such as New York, [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta), and [Orlando](/wiki/Orlando,_Florida). [Essential Air Service](/wiki/Essential_Air_Service) also subsidizes service to a number of smaller airports in Maine, bringing small [turboprop](/wiki/Turboprop) aircraft to regional airports such as the [Augusta State Airport](/wiki/Augusta_State_Airport), [Hancock County-Bar Harbor Airport](/wiki/Hancock_County-Bar_Harbor_Airport), [Knox County Regional Airport](/wiki/Knox_County_Regional_Airport), and the [Northern Maine Regional Airport at Presque Isle](/wiki/Northern_Maine_Regional_Airport_at_Presque_Isle). Many smaller airports are scattered throughout Maine, serving only [general aviation](/wiki/General_aviation) traffic. The [Eastport Municipal Airport](/wiki/Eastport_Municipal_Airport), for example, is a city-owned public-use airport with 1,200 general aviation aircraft operations each year from single-engine and ultralight aircraft.

### Shipping

#### Cargo

The International Marine Terminal in [Portland](/wiki/Portland,_Maine) provides shipping container transport. In 2021 an estimated 36,700 shipping containers moved through the terminal. In 2017, a total of 17,515 shipping containers were transported. The Icelandic shipping company [Eimskip](/wiki/Eimskip) opened its United States headquarters in Portland in 2013. Its ships stop in Portland once a week in a route that includes Atlantic Canada and Iceland with connections to northern Europe and Asia. In 2015, the terminal moved 10,500 containers. The Maine Port Authority in 2016 began a $15.5 million expansion and improvement of the terminal. The Maine Port Authority leased the International Marine Terminal from the city of Portland in 2009.

## Law and government

See also: [List of governors of Maine](/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Maine), [List of United States representatives from Maine](/wiki/List_of_United_States_representatives_from_Maine), [List of United States senators from Maine](/wiki/List_of_United_States_senators_from_Maine), and [Electoral reform in Maine](/wiki/Electoral_reform_in_Maine)

The [Maine Constitution](/wiki/Maine_Constitution) structures Maine's state government, composed of three co-equal branches—the executive, legislative, and judicial branches. The state of Maine also has three Constitutional Officers (the Secretary of State, the State Treasurer, and the State Attorney General) and one Statutory Officer (the State Auditor).

The [legislative branch](/wiki/Legislative_branch) is the [Maine Legislature](/wiki/Maine_Legislature), a bicameral body composed of the [Maine House of Representatives](/wiki/Maine_House_of_Representatives), with 151 members, and the [Maine Senate](/wiki/Maine_Senate), with 35 members. The Legislature is charged with introducing and passing laws.

The [executive branch](/wiki/Executive_branch) is headed by the [Governor of Maine](/wiki/Governor_of_Maine) (currently [Janet Mills](/wiki/Janet_Mills)). The Governor is elected every four years; no individual may serve more than two consecutive terms in this office. The current [attorney general of Maine](/wiki/Attorney_general_of_Maine) is [Aaron Frey](/wiki/Aaron_Frey). As with other [state legislatures](/wiki/State_legislature_(United_States)), the Maine Legislature can by a two-thirds majority vote from both the House and Senate override a gubernatorial veto. Maine is one of seven states that do not have a lieutenant governor.

Maine.gov logo

The highest court in the state's [judicial branch](/wiki/Judicial_branch) is the [Maine Supreme Judicial Court](/wiki/Maine_Supreme_Judicial_Court). The lower courts are the District Court, [Superior Court](/wiki/Maine_Superior_Court) and Probate Court. All judges except for probate judges serve full-time, are nominated by the Governor, and confirmed by the Legislature for terms of seven years. Probate judges serve part-time and are elected by the voters of each county for four-year terms.

In a 2020 study, Maine was ranked as the 14th easiest state for citizens to vote in. In 2012, Maine became one of the first U.S. states to establish [marriage rights for same-sex couples](/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Maine).

### Politics

Main article: [Politics of Maine](/wiki/Politics_of_Maine)

Maine politics are dynamic in nature, with parties loosely hung together, governors often winning by pluralities rather than majorities, and significant turnover both in members and parties in [legislative districts](/wiki/Electoral_district). In his 2010 article "Maine's Paradoxical Politics", Kenneth Palmer suggests that "Maine's political leaders find themselves as [centrists](/wiki/Centrism), primarily because they want to find practical solutions to difficult problems."

The results of the elections are often [varied](/wiki/Bipartisan). Maine is currently considered to be moderately [blue](/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states) and a secondary battleground state, with unusually high support for [independent candidates](/wiki/Independent_candidates). The [Republican Party](/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) has won Maine in 11 out of the past 20 presidential elections, and the governorship has been won by [Democrats](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) and independents three times each, and Republicans four times, since 1974.

Maine uses [ranked-choice voting](/wiki/Ranked-choice_voting_in_the_United_States) in primary elections for state and federal offices, as well as in general elections for federal offices. Ranked choice voting was adopted by voters in a [2016 referendum](/wiki/2016_Maine_Question_5).

## Municipalities

### Organized municipalities

Main article: [List of municipalities in Maine](/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Maine)

An organized municipality has a form of elected local government which administers and provides local services, keeps records, collects licensing fees, and can pass locally binding [ordinances](/wiki/Municipal_ordinance), among other responsibilities of self-government. The governmental format of most organized towns and [plantations](/wiki/Plantation_(Maine)) is the town meeting, while the format of most cities is the council-manager form. As of 2022 the organized municipalities of Maine consist of 23 [cities](/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Maine), 430 [towns](/wiki/List_of_towns_in_Maine), and 30 [plantations](/wiki/List_of_plantations_in_Maine). Collectively these 483 organized municipalities cover less than half of the state's territory. Maine also has three Reservations: [Indian Island](/wiki/Penobscot_Indian_Island_Reservation), Indian Township Reservation, and Pleasant Point Indian Reservation.

- The largest municipality in Maine, by population, is the city of Portland (pop. 68,408).
- The smallest city by population is Eastport (pop. 1,288).
- The largest town by population is Brunswick (pop. 21,756).
- The smallest town by population is [Frye Island](/wiki/Frye_Island,_Maine), a resort town which reported zero year-round population in the 2000 Census; one plantation, [Glenwood Plantation](/wiki/Glenwood_Plantation,_Maine), also reported a permanent population of zero.
- In the 2000 census, the smallest town aside from Frye Island was [Centerville](/wiki/Centerville,_Maine) with a population of 26, but since that census, Centerville voted to disincorporate and therefore is no longer a town. The next smallest town with a population listed in that census is [Beddington](/wiki/Beddington,_Maine) (pop. 60 at the 2020 census).
- The largest municipality by land area is the town of [Allagash](/wiki/Allagash,_Maine), at 128 square miles (332 km2).
- The smallest municipality by land area is [Monhegan Island](/wiki/Monhegan,_Maine), at 0.86 square miles (2.2 km2). The smallest municipality by area that is not an island is [Randolph](/wiki/Randolph,_Maine), at 2.23 square miles (6 km2).

### Unorganized territory

Main article: [List of unorganized territories in Maine](/wiki/List_of_unorganized_territories_in_Maine)

[Unorganized territory](/wiki/Unincorporated_area#U.S._Census_Bureau) (UT) has no local government. Administration, services, licensing, and ordinances are handled by the state government as well as by respective county governments who have townships within each county's bounds. The unorganized territory of Maine consists of more than 400 townships (in Maine, towns are incorporated, townships are unincorporated), plus many coastal islands that do not lie within any municipal bounds. The UT land area is slightly over half the entire area of the State of Maine. Year-round residents in the UT number approximately 9,000 (about 1.3% of the state's total population), with many more people staying there only seasonally. Only four of [Maine's sixteen counties](/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Maine) (Androscoggin, Cumberland, Waldo and York) are entirely incorporated, although a few others are nearly so, and most of the unincorporated area is in the vast and sparsely populated [Great North Woods of Maine](/wiki/Maine_North_Woods).

## Education

Further information: [List of colleges and universities in Maine](/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Maine), [Education in Maine](/wiki/Education_in_Maine), [List of high schools in Maine](/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Maine), and [List of school districts in Maine](/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Maine)

The [University of Maine](/wiki/University_of_Maine) is the state's only research university.

There are thirty institutions of [higher learning](/wiki/Higher_education) in Maine. These institutions include the [University of Maine](/wiki/University_of_Maine), which is the oldest, largest and only [research university](/wiki/University) in the state. UMaine was founded in 1865 and is the state's only [land grant](/wiki/Land_grant) and [sea grant](/wiki/Sea_grant) college. The University of Maine is located in the town of [Orono](/wiki/Orono,_Maine) and is the flagship of Maine. There are also branch campuses in [Augusta](/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Augusta), [Farmington](/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Farmington), [Fort Kent](/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Fort_Kent), [Machias](/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Machias), and [Presque Isle](/wiki/University_of_Maine_at_Presque_Isle).

[Colby](/wiki/Colby_College), [Bates](/wiki/Bates_College), and [Bowdoin](/wiki/Bowdoin_College) (pictured) Colleges form the [Colby-Bates-Bowdoin Consortium](/wiki/Colby-Bates-Bowdoin_Consortium)

[Bowdoin College](/wiki/Bowdoin_College) is a liberal arts college founded in 1794 in Brunswick, making it the oldest institution of higher learning in the state. [Colby College](/wiki/Colby_College) in Waterville was founded in 1813 making it the second oldest college in Maine. [Bates College](/wiki/Bates_College) in Lewiston was founded in 1855 making it the third oldest institution in the state and the oldest [coeducational](/wiki/Mixed-sex_education) college in New England. The three colleges collectively form the [Colby-Bates-Bowdoin Consortium](/wiki/Colby-Bates-Bowdoin_Consortium) and are ranked among the best colleges in the United States; often placing in the top 10% of all liberal arts colleges.

Maine's per-student public expenditure for elementary and secondary schools was 21st in the nation in 2012, at $12,344.

The collegiate system of Maine also includes numerous [baccalaureate](/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education) colleges such as: the [Maine Maritime Academy](/wiki/Maine_Maritime_Academy) (MMA), [College of the Atlantic](/wiki/College_of_the_Atlantic), [Unity Environmental University](/wiki/Unity_Environmental_University), and [Thomas College](/wiki/Thomas_College). There is only one medical school in the state, ([University of New England](/wiki/University_of_New_England_(United_States))'s [College of Osteopathic Medicine](/wiki/University_of_New_England_College_of_Osteopathic_Medicine)) and only one law school (The [University of Maine School of Law](/wiki/University_of_Maine_School_of_Law)). There is one art school in the state, [Maine College of Art](/wiki/Maine_College_of_Art_%26_Design), along with a private graduate school, [Institute for Doctoral Studies in the Visual Arts](/wiki/Institute_for_Doctoral_Studies_in_the_Visual_Arts), which offers a [Doctor of Philosophy](/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) to visual artists.

The [Maine Community College System](/wiki/Maine_Community_College_System), founded in 1985 also serves "to provide associate degree, diploma and certificate programs directed at the educational, career and technical needs of the State's citizens and the workforce needs of the State's employers." This system includes [Southern Maine Community College](/wiki/Southern_Maine_Community_College) (SMCC), [York County Community College](/wiki/York_County_Community_College) (YCCC), [Central Maine Community College](/wiki/Central_Maine_Community_College) (CMCC), [Eastern Maine Community College](/wiki/Eastern_Maine_Community_College) (EMCC), [Kennebec Valley Community College](/wiki/Kennebec_Valley_Community_College) (KVCC), [Northern Maine Community College](/wiki/Northern_Maine_Community_College) (NMCC), and [Washington County Community College](/wiki/Washington_County_Community_College) (WCCC).

Private schools in Maine are funded independently of the state and its furthered domains. Private schools are less common than public schools. A large number of private elementary schools with under 20 students exist, but most private high schools in Maine can be described as "semi-private".

Maine also has Vocational Schools, such as the [Biddeford Regional Center of Technology](/wiki/Biddeford_Regional_Center_of_Technology) and Sanford Regional Technical Center that teach trades such as welding, construction and vehicle repair to students.

## Culture

### Agriculture

Farms and farming constitute a large part of Maine's history, heritage, and culture. Family farms, homesteads, and backyard gardens are common features of the Maine landscape. Vegetable farming is the largest sector of Maine farming. As a result, Maine is known for its locally-grown food sold in markets and prepared at restaurants. Maine residents ranked number three, after Vermont and Oregon, for access to local food as measured by the high per capita ratio of farmers markets and consumer supported agriculture programs.

Historically, Maine was a center of agriculture before it achieved statehood. Prior to colonization, [Wabanaki](/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy) nations farmed large crops of corn and other produce in southern Maine. Maine was a center of grain production in the 1800s, until grain production moved westward. However, in the early 2000s the local food movement spurred renewed interested in locally grown grains. In 2007, the Kneading Conference was founded. In, 2012, the [Skowhegan](/wiki/Skowhegan,_Maine) grist mill Maine Grains opened. The revival of grain farming and milling in Maine has led to the creation of other businesses, including bakeries and malthouses.

Maine has many vegetable farms and other small, diversified farms. In the 1960s and 1970s, the book "[Living the Good Life](/wiki/Living_the_Good_Life)" by [Helen Nearing](/wiki/Helen_Nearing) and [Scott Nearing](/wiki/Scott_Nearing) caused many young people to move to Maine and engage in small-scale farming and homesteading. These [back-to-the-land](/wiki/Back-to-the-land_movement) migrants increased the population of some counties.

### Festivals

Maine has multiple fairs and festivals that are held annually. These events include [La Kermesse](/wiki/La_Kermesse_Franco-Americaine_Festival), a celebration of the state's [French](/wiki/France) and [French Canadian](/wiki/French_Canadians) heritage, the [Acadia Night Sky Festival](/wiki/Acadia_Night_Sky_Festival), a festival of the starlit sky, the [Great Falls Balloon Festival](/wiki/Great_Falls_Balloon_Festival), a festival of hot air balloons, the [Christmas Prelude](/wiki/Christmas_Prelude) festival in Kennebunkport, and a number of [Portland Food Festivals](/wiki/Portland,_Maine#Food_festivals). Maine is home to 25 agricultural fairs that include the [Fryeburg Fair](/wiki/Fryeburg_Fair), the [Cumberland Fair](/wiki/Cumberland_Fair), the [Union Fair](/wiki/Union_Fair), and the [Common Ground Country Fair](/wiki/Common_Ground_Country_Fair). There are a number of [Old Home Days festivals](/wiki/Old_Home_Week), including the [Yarmouth Clam Festival](/wiki/Yarmouth_Clam_Festival).

### Food

Along with the growth of the local food movement over the last several decades, Maine has received national recognition for its food and restaurant scene. [Portland](/wiki/Portland,_Maine) was named *[Bon Appetit](/wiki/Bon_App%C3%A9tit)* magazine's Restaurant City of the Year in 2018. In 2018, HealthIQ.com named Maine the 3rd most vegan-friendly state. [Biddeford](/wiki/Biddeford,_Maine) was selected by [Food & Wine](/wiki/Food_%26_Wine) in 2022 as one of America's next great food cities.

Maine food shares many ingredients with [Wabanaki cuisine](/wiki/Wabanaki_Confederacy#Cuisine), including corn, beans, squash, wild blueberries, maple syrup, fish, and seafood. By 1902, the [Maine Italian sandwich](/wiki/Maine_Italian_sandwich) had been invented in Portland. Sandwich shops across Maine serve the sandwiches. Baked beans are a common dish in Maine, served at community suppers where the beans are sometimes cooked underground in a bean hole. In New England, Maine baked beans are one of two well-known regional styles of baked beans, the other being [Boston baked beans](/wiki/Boston_baked_beans). Maine baked beans use thicker skinned, native bean varieties such like Marafax, soldier, and yellow-eye beans. From 1913 until 2021, baked beans were canned on the Portland waterfront at the [B&M Baked Beans factory](/wiki/B%26M_Baked_Beans_factory).

### Fashion

Maine people have a distinctive fashion that is closely associated with [Bean boots](/wiki/Bean_boots), [L.L. Bean](/wiki/L.L.Bean) clothing, flannel shirts, rugged pants, and outerwear. Maine fashion is casual and influenced by coastal living, outdoor recreation, and demanding outdoor occupations, such as logging and lobstering. In addition to Bean boots, other Maine fashion staples include [Carhartt](/wiki/Carhartt), muck boots, and [long johns](/wiki/Long_johns). Maine fashion is regarded as understated and associated with [preppy](/wiki/Preppy) clothing and nautical clothing. Other items associated with Maine fashion are [tourmaline](/wiki/Tourmaline) jewelry, Liberty Graphics T-shirts, and G.H. Bass [Weejuns](/wiki/Weejuns). Maine, particularly Portland, is home to a small number of fashion designers. The [Maine College of Art & Design](/wiki/Maine_College_of_Art_%26_Design) has a fashion and textile design program of study.

### Accent and terminology

Main article: [Maine accent](/wiki/Maine_accent)

The traditional Maine accent is spoken in parts of Maine, especially along the "[Down East](/wiki/Down_East)" and ["Midcoast" seaside regions](/wiki/Mid_Coast). Maine maintains some vernacular and terminology that is unique in comparison to the rest of the country. Some of these include:

- "Ayuh" - An affirmative response, like "Yes".
- "Can't get there from here" - Place requires complicated instructions to reach.
- "Down cellah" - In the basement.
- "Downeast" - The coastline of Washington and Hancock counties, including Bar Harbor.
- "From away" - A non-native person of Maine.
- "Hard tellin' not knowin'" - Don't know.
- "Puckerbrush" - Dense vegetation likely to scratch your legs.
- "Upta camp" - Going to a more out-of-the-way, rustic place. Popularized by [Bob Marley](/wiki/Bob_Marley_(comedian)) after his special of the same name.

## People from Maine

Main article: [List of people from Maine](/wiki/List_of_people_from_Maine)

Citizens of Maine are often known as Mainers. The term Downeaster may be applied to residents of the northeast coast of the state. The term Mainiac is considered by some to be derogatory, but is embraced with pride by others, and is used for a variety of organizations and for events such as the YMCA Mainiac Sprint Triathlon & Duathlon.

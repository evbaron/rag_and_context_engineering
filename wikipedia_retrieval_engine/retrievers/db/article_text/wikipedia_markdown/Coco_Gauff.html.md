**Cori Dionne** "**Coco**" **Gauff** ([/ˈɡɔːf/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*GAWF*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born March 13, 2004) is an American professional [tennis](/wiki/Tennis) player. She has a career-high ranking of world No. 2 in singles and of [world No. 1](/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_doubles_tennis_players) in doubles by the [WTA](/wiki/Women%27s_Tennis_Association). Gauff has won 11 career singles titles, including two [majors](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments) at the [2023 US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) and [2025 French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), as well as the [2024 WTA Finals](/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles). She has also won 10 doubles titles, including the [2024 French Open](/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles), partnering with [Kateřina Siniaková](/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1).

Gauff made her [WTA Tour](/wiki/WTA_Tour) debut in 2019 at age 15. At that year's [Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), she became the youngest player in the tournament's history to qualify for the main draw, where she defeated [Venus Williams](/wiki/Venus_Williams) and reached the fourth round. Gauff won her first WTA Tour singles title at the [2019 Linz Open](/wiki/2019_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz_%E2%80%93_Singles). She reached her first major singles final at the [2022 French Open](/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles). In 2023, Gauff won her first major singles title at the US Open, followed by the WTA Finals title the following year. In 2025, she won a second major singles title at the French Open.

In doubles, Gauff has reached two major finals, finishing runner-up with [Caty McNally](/wiki/Caty_McNally) at the [2021 US Open](/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) and prevailing with Siniaková at the 2024 French Open. She reached the No. 1 ranking in partnership with [Jessica Pegula](/wiki/Jessica_Pegula).

## Early life

Gauff was born at West Boca Medical Center in [Boca Raton, Florida](/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida), on March 13, 2004, to Candi ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Odom) and Corey Gauff, both from [Delray Beach](/wiki/Delray_Beach,_Florida). She has two younger brothers. Her father played college basketball at [Georgia State University](/wiki/Georgia_State_University) and later worked as a health care executive. Her mother was a track-and-field athlete at [Florida State University](/wiki/Florida_State_University) and worked as an educator. Gauff lived her early years in Atlanta. She began playing tennis at age six. When she was seven, her family moved back to Delray Beach to have better training opportunities. She worked with Gerard Loglo at the New Generation Tennis Academy starting from the age of eight.

Gauff recalled, "I wasn't much of a team person. I loved tennis. I was so-so about it in the beginning because when I was younger I didn't want to practice at all. I just wanted to play with my friends. When I turned eight, that was when I played '[Little Mo](/wiki/Maureen_Connolly)' [tournament] and after that I decided to do that for the rest of my life."

Gauff's parents gave up their careers to focus on training their daughter. Her father later became her primary coach, while her mother oversaw her [homeschooling](/wiki/Homeschooling). Her father had limited experience playing tennis growing up. At the age of 10, Gauff began to train at the [Mouratoglou Tennis Academy](/wiki/Mouratoglou_Tennis_Academy) in France run by [Patrick Mouratoglou](/wiki/Patrick_Mouratoglou), longtime coach of [Serena Williams](/wiki/Serena_Williams). Mouratoglou commented, "I'll always remember the first time I saw Coco. She came over to the Mouratoglou Academy in 2014 to try out and she impressed me with her determination, athleticism and fighting spirit.... When she looks at you and tells you she will be number one, you can only believe it." He helped sponsor Gauff through his Champ'Seed foundation, which he created to provide funding for talented juniors who did not have the financial resources to afford high-level training.

Gauff won the USTA Clay Court National 12-and-under title at the age of 10 years and three months to become the youngest champion in the tournament's history.

## Juniors

### French Open singles and US Open doubles champion

Gauff is a former world No. 1 junior. She entered the prestigious [Les Petits As](/wiki/Les_Petits_As) 14-and-under tournament in 2016 at age 12 and made it to the semifinals. Gauff began playing on the [ITF Junior Circuit](/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit) at the age of 13, skipping directly to the highest-level Grade A and Grade 1 tournaments. She finished runner-up to [Jaimee Fourlis](/wiki/Jaimee_Fourlis) in her third career event, the Grade 1 Prince George's County Junior Tennis Championships in Maryland. At her next event, Gauff made her junior [Grand Slam](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)) tournament debut at the [2017 US Open](/wiki/2017_US_Open_(tennis)) and finished runner-up to [Amanda Anisimova](/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova). Gauff did not drop a set before the final in either tournament. She became the youngest girls' singles finalist in US Open history at 13 years, 181 days old.

After beginning 2018 with a semifinal at the Grade 1 Traralgon Junior International in Australia, Gauff lost her opening round match at the [Australian Open](/wiki/2018_Australian_Open). She did not enter another tournament in singles until the French, where she won her first career junior Grand Slam tournament title at the [2018 French Open](/wiki/2018_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls%27_singles). She did not drop a set until the final, where she came from behind to defeat [Caty McNally](/wiki/Caty_McNally) in three sets. With the title, Gauff became the fifth youngest girls' singles champion in French Open history at 14 years, 89 days old. A month later, following another final win against McNally at the Grade 1 Junior International Roehampton, she became the No. 1 junior in the world.

Gauff reached the quarterfinals in singles at the final two majors of the year. She fared better in doubles at both tournaments, reaching the semifinals at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2018_Wimbledon) with partner [María Lourdes Carlé](/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Lourdes_Carl%C3%A9) and winning her first junior Grand Slam tournament doubles title at the [2018 US Open](/wiki/2018_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls%27_doubles) with McNally. Gauff and McNally defeated compatriots [Hailey Baptiste](/wiki/Hailey_Baptiste) and [Dalayna Hewitt](/wiki/Dalayna_Hewitt) in the final, in straight sets. In September 2018, Gauff represented the United States at the [Junior Fed Cup](/wiki/Junior_Davis_Cup_and_Junior_Fed_Cup) with [Alexa Noel](/wiki/Alexa_Noel) and Connie Ma. The team reached the final against Ukraine. After Gauff won her singles rubber and Noel lost hers, Gauff and Noel won the Junior Fed Cup by defeating Lyubov Kostenko and Dasha Lopatetskaya 11–9 in a match tiebreak. Gauff finished the year with another Grade A title in singles at the [Orange Bowl](/wiki/Orange_Bowl_(tennis)). She ended the season ranked world No. 2 behind [Clara Burel](/wiki/Clara_Burel).

## Professional

### 2018–19: First titles, top 100

Gauff made her debut on the [ITF Women's Circuit](/wiki/ITF_Women%27s_Circuit) in May 2018 at the age of 14 as a qualifier in the 25k event at [Osprey](/wiki/Osprey,_Florida), where she won her first professional match. She received a wildcard into qualifying at the [US Open](/wiki/2018_US_Open_(tennis)), but lost her opening match five months after turning 14 years old. In her first 2019 tournament, she finished runner-up in doubles at the 100k [Midland Tennis Classic](/wiki/2019_Dow_Tennis_Classic) alongside [Ann Li](/wiki/Ann_Li_(tennis)). Two weeks later, Gauff played her next event at the $25k level in [Surprise](/wiki/Surprise,_Arizona) and reached the finals in both singles and doubles. She finished runner-up in singles and won her first WTA Tour title in doubles alongside [Paige Hourigan](/wiki/Paige_Hourigan). In March, at the [Miami Open](/wiki/2019_Miami_Open), she recorded her first WTA Tour match win against [Caty McNally](/wiki/Caty_McNally).

Gauff lost the second round of qualifying at the [French Open](/wiki/2019_French_Open). At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships), she defeated [Aliona Bolsova](/wiki/Aliona_Bolsova) and [Greet Minnen](/wiki/Greet_Minnen). Gauff became the youngest player to reach the main draw at Wimbledon by qualifying in the Open Era at the age of 15 years and three months. In her main-draw debut, she upset five-time Wimbledon champion [Venus Williams](/wiki/Venus_Williams) in straight sets. Gauff won over [Magdaléna Rybáriková](/wiki/Magdal%C3%A9na_Ryb%C3%A1rikov%C3%A1) and No. 60 [Polona Hercog](/wiki/Polona_Hercog), saving two match points against Hercog. The hype surrounding Gauff's first-round match win led to her third rounder moving to [Centre Court](/wiki/Centre_Court). She was eliminated with a fourth-round loss to eventual champion [Simona Halep](/wiki/Simona_Halep). All four of Gauff's matches were most-watched matches on [ESPN](/wiki/ESPN) on their respective days during the first week of coverage. With this performance, she rose to world No. 141.

Gauff played in one [US Open Series](/wiki/US_Open_Series) tournament at the [Washington Open](/wiki/2019_Citi_Open), where she qualified for the main draw but lost in the first round. She entered the doubles event with McNally and defeated [Fanny Stollár](/wiki/Fanny_Stoll%C3%A1r) and [Maria Sanchez](/wiki/Maria_Sanchez_(tennis)) in the final for their first career WTA title in their first joint WTA. At the [US Open](/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff wild-carded into the singles and doubles main draws. She continued her success in major singles with two three-set wins over [Anastasia Potapova](/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) and [Tímea Babos](/wiki/T%C3%ADmea_Babos), both on [Louis Armstrong](/wiki/Louis_Armstrong_Stadium). She was defeated in the third round by world No. 1 and defending champion, [Naomi Osaka](/wiki/Naomi_Osaka). In doubles, Gauff and McNally won two matches, including an upset over ninth seeds [Nicole Melichar](/wiki/Nicole_Melichar) and [Květa Peschke](/wiki/Kv%C4%9Bta_Peschke). They lost in the third round to eventual runners-up [Ashleigh Barty](/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty) and [Victoria Azarenka](/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka). Although Gauff lost in qualifying at the [Linz Open](/wiki/2019_Upper_Austria_Ladies_Linz), she entered the main draw as a [lucky loser](/wiki/Lucky_loser) and won the title, notably upsetting top seed [Kiki Bertens](/wiki/Kiki_Bertens) in the quarterfinals for her first top-10 victory. She defeated [Jeļena Ostapenko](/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko) in the final to become the youngest WTA player, at 15 years, 214 days old, to win a singles title since 2004. With this title as well as a semifinal in doubles with McNally, Gauff made her top-100 debuts in both the WTA singles and doubles rankings. Gauff and McNally ended their year with a second WTA doubles title at the [Luxembourg Open](/wiki/2019_BGL_Luxembourg_Open) over [Kaitlyn Christian](/wiki/Kaitlyn_Christian) and [Alexa Guarachi](/wiki/Alexa_Guarachi).

### 2020: Australian Open fourth round

Starting 2020 at the [Auckland Open](/wiki/2020_ASB_Classic) ranked No. 67 in the world, Gauff defeated [Viktória Kužmová](/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Ku%C5%BEmov%C3%A1) before losing to [Laura Siegemund](/wiki/Laura_Siegemund) in the second round. Playing doubles with McNally, Gauff reached the semifinals.

At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff defeated Venus Williams in straight sets in the first round and [Sorana Cîrstea](/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea) in the second round, making it three straight majors where she reached the third round. She defeated defending champion Osaka in the third, becoming the youngest player to defeat a top-5 player since [Jennifer Capriati](/wiki/Jennifer_Capriati) beat [Gabriela Sabatini](/wiki/Gabriela_Sabatini) at the [1991 US Open](/wiki/1991_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles). In the fourth round, she lost to the eventual champion, [Sofia Kenin](/wiki/Sofia_Kenin). In doubles, Gauff and McNally recorded their best result in a major championship to date, reaching the quarterfinals before falling to second seeds and eventual champions, [Kristina Mladenovic](/wiki/Kristina_Mladenovic) and Tímea Babos.

Gauff beat two top-50 players at the [Lexington Challenger](/wiki/Top_Seed_Open), before losing in straight sets to world No. 49, [Jennifer Brady](/wiki/Jennifer_Brady). At the Western and Southern Open, played in New York, Gauff lost in the first round to world No. 21, [Maria Sakkari](/wiki/Maria_Sakkari). At the [US Open](/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff was defeated in the first round by [Anastasija Sevastova](/wiki/Anastasija_Sevastova).

Gauff, ranked 53, beat world No. 34, [Ons Jabeur](/wiki/Ons_Jabeur), in the first round of the [Italian Open](/wiki/Italian_Open_(tennis)) before losing to two-time major champion [Garbiñe Muguruza](/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza). At the [French Open](/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff defeated the ninth seed and world No. 13, [Johanna Konta](/wiki/Johanna_Konta), in the first round, but went on to lose to eventual quarterfinalist [Martina Trevisan](/wiki/Martina_Trevisan) in a second-round match in which Gauff hit 19 double faults. At the [Ostrava Open](/wiki/Ostrava_Open), she qualified for the main draw and was defeated by world No. 12, [Aryna Sabalenka](/wiki/Aryna_Sabalenka), in the second round.

### 2021: French Open quarterfinals, top 20

Gauff at the [2021 French Open](/wiki/2021_French_Open)

Starting the year ranked No. 48, in the [Abu Dhabi Open](/wiki/Abu_Dhabi_Open), she beat Norwegian [Ulrikke Eikeri](/wiki/Ulrikke_Eikeri) before falling in the next round to Maria Sakkari. At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), in singles, Gauff again beat Teichmann in the first round, but fell in the round of 64 to the fifth-seeded [Elina Svitolina](/wiki/Elina_Svitolina), in straight sets. In doubles, she and McNally failed to Demi Schuurs and Nicole Melichar in the quarterfinals.

At Adelaide, she fell to second-seeded [Belinda Bencic](/wiki/Belinda_Bencic). In doubles, she partnered with Canadian [Sharon Fichman](/wiki/Sharon_Fichman), and they bowed out in the first round to [Duan Yingying](/wiki/Duan_Yingying) and [Zheng Saisai](/wiki/Zheng_Saisai). This run brought her to a then-career-high of No. 38 in singles. After the [Dubai Championships](/wiki/Dubai_Tennis_Championships), she reached a career high of No. 35 in singles.

In the [Miami Open](/wiki/2021_Miami_Open), she played her first WTA 1000 event while seeded (as the 31st). In May, Gauff reached the first semifinal at a WTA 1000 in her career at the [Italian Open](/wiki/2021_Italian_Open_(tennis)) due to then-No. 1, Barty's retiring with a right arm injury in their quarterfinal match. As a result, she entered top 30 for the first time. She then lost to the eventual champion, [Iga Świątek](/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek).

Gauff won her second singles and third doubles (with McNally) titles at the [Emilia-Romagna Open](/wiki/2021_Emilia-Romagna_Open) in Parma. She became the youngest player to win both the singles and doubles titles at an event since [Maria Sharapova](/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) won both titles at the [2004 Birmingham Classic](/wiki/2004_DFS_Classic). Gauff thus rose to new career-high rankings of world No. 25 in singles and No. 41 in doubles. She became the youngest American to make her top-25 debut in nearly 23 years (since Serena Williams, June 8, 1998).

Seeded 24th at the [French Open](/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) (her first time being seeded at a major), she beat [Aleksandra Krunić](/wiki/Aleksandra_Kruni%C4%87) and [Wang Qiang](/wiki/Wang_Qiang_(tennis)) in straight sets, received a walkover when leading one set to love against 13th seed and Australian Open runner-up [Jennifer Brady](/wiki/Jennifer_Brady), and beat 25th seed [Ons Jabeur](/wiki/Ons_Jabeur) in just 53 minutes to reach her first major quarterfinal. As a result, she became the youngest female player (17 years, three months) to reach a major quarterfinal since [Nicole Vaidišová](/wiki/Nicole_Vaidi%C5%A1ov%C3%A1) at the 2006 French Open, the youngest American to reach a quarterfinal at the French Open since [Jennifer Capriati](/wiki/Jennifer_Capriati) in 1993 and the youngest American to reach the quarterfinals of any major tournament since [Venus Williams](/wiki/Venus_Williams) reached the 1997 US Open final. Subsequently, Gauff was eliminated after losing in straight sets to the eventual champion, unseeded [Barbora Krejčíková](/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1). As a result, she reached a new career-high of No. 23.

At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff reached the fourth round for a second consecutive time defeating [Elena Vesnina](/wiki/Elena_Vesnina) in straight sets in 70 minutes, and [Kaja Juvan](/wiki/Kaja_Juvan) in straight sets in the third round. Gauff lost her next match to [Angelique Kerber](/wiki/Angelique_Kerber) in straight sets, eliminating her from the tournament. She also reached the third round in doubles with Caty McNally and as a result entered the top 40 in the doubles rankings at No. 38 on 12 July.

At 17 years old, she was selected for the [2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) in Tokyo, becoming the second youngest American player after Jennifer Capriati competed at 16 in 1992, and the youngest Olympic tennis player of any gender since [Mario Ančić](/wiki/Mario_An%C4%8Di%C4%87) in 2000. However, she tested positive for COVID-19 and was forced to withdraw.

At the [Cincinnati Open](/wiki/2021_Western_%26_Southern_Open), Gauff reached the second round and lost to second seed, and world No. 2, Naomi Osaka.

At the [US Open](/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff beat [Magda Linette](/wiki/Magda_Linette) in the first round, before falling to [Sloane Stephens](/wiki/Sloane_Stephens) in the next. In the women's doubles, Gauff and McNally stormed into their first major semifinal without dropping a set and in the finals, they lost to [Sam Stosur](/wiki/Samantha_Stosur) and [Zhang Shuai](/wiki/Zhang_Shuai).

### 2022: French Open final, singles top 5, doubles No. 1

Seeded 18th at the [Australian Open](/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff lost in the first round against Wang Qiang in straight sets.

In February, Gauff reached the quarterfinals at the [Qatar Open](/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) by defeating [Shelby Rogers](/wiki/Shelby_Rogers), Caroline Garcia and third seed [Paula Badosa](/wiki/Paula_Badosa). In the quarterfinals, Gauff lost to sixth seed Maria Sakkari. In [doubles](/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Doubles), Gauff paired with [Jessica Pegula](/wiki/Jessica_Pegula) to win her first WTA 1000 doubles title, beating third-seeded pair of [Veronika Kudermetova](/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova) and [Elise Mertens](/wiki/Elise_Mertens) in the final. With the win, she climbed to a career-high No. 10 in the doubles rankings on 28 February 2022.

Gauff reached her first major singles final at the [French Open](/wiki/2022_French_Open), defeating [Rebecca Marino](/wiki/Rebecca_Marino), [Alison Van Uytvanck](/wiki/Alison_Van_Uytvanck), Kaia Kanepi, 31st seed Elise Mertens, Sloane Stephens, and Martina Trevisan, before losing to Iga Świątek in straight sets. She reached the final in doubles with Jessica Pegula where they were defeated by Caroline Garcia and Kristina Mladenovic. As a result, she secured a new career-high of world No. 13 in singles and the top 5 in doubles.

After winning her first two matches at [Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the 11th seed against unseeded Romanians [Elena-Gabriela Ruse](/wiki/Elena-Gabriela_Ruse) and [Mihaela Buzărnescu](/wiki/Mihaela_Buz%C4%83rnescu), Gauff lost in the third round to 20th seed [Amanda Anisimova](/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova), in three sets. As a result, she reached a new career-high ranking of world No. 11 on 11 July 2022.

Seeded sixth at the [Silicon Valley Classic](/wiki/2022_Silicon_Valley_Classic), she reached the quarterfinals defeating [Anhelina Kalinina](/wiki/Anhelina_Kalinina) in the first round, and next Naomi Osaka who saved seven match points. In her quarterfinal match, she struggled with her serve and lost in straight sets to Paula Badosa.

At the [Canadian Open](/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open), she became the youngest player to reach back-to-back quarterfinals in Canada since Jennifer Capriati in 1990 and 1991. She beat sixth seed Aryna Sabalenka, a day after ousting Wimbledon champion [Elena Rybakina](/wiki/Elena_Rybakina), winning both matches in a third-set tiebreak. She lost to eventual champion Simona Halep, in straight sets. Seeded third in doubles at the same tournament she reached the semifinals with Pegula defeating fifth seeds [Desirae Krawczyk](/wiki/Desirae_Krawczyk) and [Demi Schuurs](/wiki/Demi_Schuurs). Next they defeated [Madison Keys](/wiki/Madison_Keys)/[Sania Mirza](/wiki/Sania_Mirza) in the semifinals and Nicole Melichar/[Ellen Perez](/wiki/Ellen_Perez) in the final to win their second WTA 1000 title together. As a result, Gauff became the No. 1 doubles player in the world.

At the [US Open](/wiki/2022_US_Open_(tennis)), she reached the quarterfinals of this major for the first time with wins over 20th seed Madison Keys and Zhang Shuai becoming the youngest American woman to achieve this feat since 2009, when [Melanie Oudin](/wiki/Melanie_Oudin) was 17. As a result, she guaranteed herself a top 10 debut in the singles rankings at world No. 8 on 12 September 2022, after the tournament. Subsequently, Gauff was defeated by Caroline Garcia, in straight sets. Seeded second in doubles, Gauff and partner Pegula were defeated in the first round by [Leylah Fernandez](/wiki/Leylah_Fernandez) and [Daria Saville](/wiki/Daria_Saville).

In October, Gauff became the youngest player in singles since [Maria Sharapova](/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) in 2005 to qualify for the year-end [WTA Finals](/wiki/WTA_Finals). She and partner Jessica Pegula also both qualified for the doubles championships. Gauff and Pegula were the first Americans since Serena and Venus Williams in 2009 to qualify for both the singles and doubles year-end championships.

### 2023: US Open singles champion, world No. 3

Main article: [2023 Coco Gauff tennis season](/wiki/2023_Coco_Gauff_tennis_season)

Gauff started her 2023 season at the [Auckland Open](/wiki/WTA_Auckland_Open), where she defeated [Rebeka Masarova](/wiki/Rebeka_Masarova) in the final in straight sets. At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff advanced to the fourth round, where she lost to Jeļena Ostapenko in straight sets. At the [Qatar Ladies Open](/wiki/2023_Qatar_Total_Open), Gauff reached the quarterfinals after defeating two-time champion [Petra Kvitová](/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1) in the second round. At the same tournament in doubles, Gauff and Jessica Pegula defended their title, defeating [Lyudmyla Kichenok](/wiki/Lyudmyla_Kichenok) and Jeļena Ostapenko in a three-set match. At [Dubai](/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), Gauff reached the semifinals by defeating Madison Keys in the quarterfinals, before losing to Iga Świątek. At [Indian Wells](/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open), Gauff lost in the quarterfinals to second seed and eventual runner-up, Aryna Sabalenka. In [Miami](/wiki/2023_Miami_Open), Gauff lost against 27th seed [Anastasia Potapova](/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) in the third round. In doubles at the same tournament, Gauff won her fifth overall and third WTA 1000 team title with her partner Jessica Pegula. They became the first all-American duo to win the Miami Open doubles title in 22 years, defeating Leylah Fernandez and [Taylor Townsend](/wiki/Taylor_Townsend) in the final.

In August, Gauff won the [Washington Open](/wiki/Washington_Open_(tennis)) title, beating Maria Sakkari in the final. It was Gauff's first [WTA 500](/wiki/WTA_500) singles title, and her biggest singles title to date. She became the first teenager to win the tournament. Gauff won her first [WTA 1000](/wiki/WTA_1000_tournaments) title at the [2023 Cincinnati Open](/wiki/2023_Western_%26_Southern_Open), defeating world No. 1, Iga Świątek, in the semifinals and world No. 10, [Karolína Muchová](/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Muchov%C3%A1), in the final.

In September, Gauff won the [US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), her first major singles title, beating world No. 2, Aryna Sabalenka, in three sets and becoming the first American teenager to win the US Open since Serena Williams in 1999. As a result, she reached world No. 3 in the rankings on 11 September 2023 and was nominated at the [Laureus World Sports Awards](/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Awards) for Breakthrough of the Year.

Gauff and Pegula returned to the world No. 1 doubles ranking on 23 October 2023, after qualifying for the [2023 WTA Finals](/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals) as a pair and also individually, becoming the first players to qualify in both disciplines in back-to-back years since [Sara Errani](/wiki/Sara_Errani) in 2012–13.

### 2024: WTA Finals singles & Major doubles titles, world No. 2

Main article: [2024 Coco Gauff tennis season](/wiki/2024_Coco_Gauff_tennis_season)

At the [Italian Open](/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_(tennis)) she reached the semifinal for the second time at this tournament and second at the WTA 1000 level for the season defeating Magdalena Fręch, Jaqueline Cristian, Paula Badosa and seventh seed [Zheng Qinwen](/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen). With the win, she passed [Caroline Wozniacki](/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki) for the most WTA 1000 wins before turning 21. She lost to world No. 1 Iga Świątek, in straight sets. At the same tournament, partnering [Erin Routliffe](/wiki/Erin_Routliffe), she reached a consecutive final in doubles.

At the [2024 French Open](/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) with new partner [Katerina Siniaková](/wiki/Katerina_Siniakov%C3%A1), she lifted her first major tournament doubles trophy defeating 11th seeds Sara Errani and [Jasmine Paolini](/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini) in the final. In singles, also at the French Open, she reached the semifinals and was defeated by eventual champion, Iga Świątek, but despite the loss reached a new career-high ranking of world No. 2 in singles on 10 June 2024.

At the [Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships), Gauff went out in the fourth round to [Emma Navarro](/wiki/Emma_Navarro). Gauff was chosen by her Olympic teammates to be the female flag-bearer for the United States at the opening ceremony of the [2024 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in Paris, alongside [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James). She became the youngest athlete to be so honored. In singles, she lost in the third round to [Donna Vekić](/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87) in straight sets.

In October, Gauff won the 2024 [China Open](/wiki/China_Open_(tennis)) title, beating Karolína Muchová in the final. It was Gauff's second WTA 1000 singles title. She became the first player to win each of her seven WTA hardcourt finals in the [Open era](/wiki/Open_era) and the second American to win the championship, following [Serena Williams](/wiki/Serena_Williams)' title runs in 2004 and 2013.

On November 9, Gauff became the [2024 WTA Finals](/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) singles champion, becoming the youngest player to win the year-end championships since Maria Sharapova in 2004 and the first American to win the finals since Serena Williams in 2014. She beat Iga Świątek, the No. 2 seed and defending champion of the WTA Finals in [Riyadh](/wiki/Riyadh). She then defeated Sabalenka in the semifinals and Zheng Qinwen in the finals to capture her first year-end singles championship title.

### 2025: United Cup, French Open and Wuhan titles

Main article: [2025 Coco Gauff tennis season](/wiki/2025_Coco_Gauff_tennis_season)

To begin the [2025 season](/wiki/2025_WTA_Tour), Gauff participated in the [United Cup](/wiki/2025_United_Cup), where the United States team won the title against Poland. In the final, she defeated world No. 2, Iga Świątek, in straight sets. Gauff was undefeated across the tournament.

At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), she advanced to the quarterfinals where she was defeated by Paula Badosa, in straight sets. After the Australian Open, Gauff lost both of her opening matches in the Middle East at the [Qatar Open](/wiki/2025_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open) and the [Dubai Championships](/wiki/2025_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), totaling three consecutive losses. She then reached the 4th round at both the [Indian Wells Open](/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open) and the [Miami Open](/wiki/2025_Miami_Open).

During the clay season, Gauff began to rediscover her form. She reached the quarterfinals of the [Stuttgart Open](/wiki/2025_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix), losing to Jasmine Paolini. At the [Madrid Open](/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open), she reached her first final at the tournament, losing to world No. 1, Aryna Sabalenka. Next at the [Italian Open](/wiki/2025_Italian_Open), she again reached the final, falling to Jasmine Paolini. At the [French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff became the youngest woman to reach the finals of the three biggest clay court tournaments (Madrid Open, Italian Open, and French Open) in the same year. She went on to defeat Aryna Sabalenka in the final to win her first French Open title and her second major title. It was the first French Open singles triumph for an athlete from the United States since Serena Williams in 2015.

At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff lost her first-round match to [Dayana Yastremska](/wiki/Dayana_Yastremska), in straight sets.

In October, Gauff won the 2025 [Wuhan Open](/wiki/Wuhan_Open) title, beating compatriot Jessica Pegula in the final. It was Gauff's third WTA 1000 singles title. She became the first American to win the title since [Venus Williams](/wiki/Venus_Williams) in 2015 and the first player to win her first nine hardcourt finals.

### 2026: Miami final

Gauff's 2026 season started in Australia at the [United Cup](/wiki/2026_United_Cup), where she helped the defending US team advance to the semifinals to face Poland. In the semifinals, Gauff defeated Iga Swiatek in straight sets, however, [Team United States](/wiki/United_States_at_the_United_Cup) eventually fell to Poland 2–1.

At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Gauff advanced to the quarterfinals where she was defeated by [Elina Svitolina](/wiki/Elina_Svitolina) in straight sets.

Gauff moved on to the [2026 WTA Qatar Open](/wiki/2026_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where she lost her opening round to [Elisabetta Cocciaretto](/wiki/Elisabetta_Cocciaretto), a Lucky Loser, in straight sets. After that, Gauff entered the [2026 Dubai Open](/wiki/2026_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the third seed. She defeated [Anna Kalinskaya](/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya), Elise Mertens, and [Alexandra Eala](/wiki/Alexandra_Eala) on her way to the semifinals before falling to Elina Svitolina 6–4, 6–7(13), 6–4 in just over three hours.

Gauff then entered [Indian Wells](/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the fourth seed, where she faced Eala again in the third round, and she was eliminated after retiring due to an arm injury. However, she would go on to play in [Miami](/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), where she won her second round rematch against Cocciaretto in three sets. Gauff then defeated compatriot [Alycia Parks](/wiki/Alycia_Parks) in three sets to advance to the fourth round. Despite dropping the second set, Gauff would defeat [Sorana Cirstea](/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea) in three sets to reach the quarterfinals for the first time in her career. Next, she defeated 12th seed [Belinda Bencic](/wiki/Belinda_Bencic). In the semifinals, she defeated [Karolina Muchová](/wiki/Karolina_Muchov%C3%A1) to reach her first final at her home WTA 1000 event. Despite battling back to take the second set, Coco Gauff would eventually lose the final against Aryna Sabalenka in three sets.

## Rivalries

### Iga Świątek

Gauff and [Iga Świątek](/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek) have met 16 times since 2021, with Świątek controlling the head-to-head 11–5. Gauff lost to Świątek in her first [major](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)) final at the [2022 French Open](/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) championships. They met again in the [2023 French Open](/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) quarterfinal, and the [2024 French Open](/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) semifinal, with Świątek winning each time. Gauff has won their last four meetings, including the [2024 WTA Finals](/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) and most recently at the [2026 United Cup](/wiki/2026_United_Cup).

### Aryna Sabalenka

Gauff and [Aryna Sabalenka](/wiki/Aryna_Sabalenka) have developed a noteworthy rivalry, competing in multiple Grand Slam finals. Their contrasting demeanors and styles of play shine throughout in their matches. The two have met 12 times since 2020, with Sabalenka having the edge in their head-to-head at 7–6. Gauff won her first major title by defeating Sabalenka in the [2023 US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final. Sabalenka would then defeat Gauff on her way to defending her title in the [2024 Australian Open](/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) semifinal. Gauff defeated Sabalenka in the [2024 WTA Finals](/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) semifinal on her way to winning the tournament. Gauff would then win her second [major](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)) title at the [2025 French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) by defeating Sabalenka in three sets. After a close first set, Sabalenka avenged her French Open loss by knocking Gauff out of the [2025 WTA Finals](/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) where Gauff was the defending champion in straight sets. Gauff and Sabalenka would face off in the [2026 Miami](/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final, where Sabalenka defeated Gauff in three sets.

### Jasmine Paolini

Gauff and [Jasmine Paolini](/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini) have met seven times since 2021, with the head-to-head tied at 4–3 in favor of Gauff. Their rivalry intensified during the 2025 season, when they faced each other five times across major WTA events.
Paolini defeated Gauff in consecutive clay-court meetings at the 2025 Stuttgart Open and the [2025 Italian Open](/wiki/2025_Italian_Open), both in straight sets. Gauff later responded by defeating Paolini in the quarterfinals of the [2025 Wuhan Open](/wiki/2025_Wuhan_Open) and again at the [2025 WTA Finals](/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals).

## Playing style and coaching

Hard courts and clay are Gauff's favorite surfaces. Although many analysts would describe her as an "opportunistic counterpuncher," she describes herself as having a highly aggressive playing style and a powerful serve, while on the defensive side she uses her athleticism and speed to remain in points.

From July 2023 to September 2024 her coach was [Brad Gilbert](/wiki/Brad_Gilbert). Gilbert encouraged Gauff to put high, heavy topspin on her ball and to chase down balls in every corner of the court. In September 2024 Gauff hired coach Matt Daly, who has focused on improving her serve and forehand with a grip on her racket that is closer to the [eastern backhand style](/wiki/Grip_(tennis)) rather than the more frequently used [continental style](/wiki/Grip_(tennis)). On August 20, 2025, Gauff replaced Daly with Gavin MacMillan, a biomechanics specialist who helped rescue world No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka's serve.

## Endorsements

Gauff uses a [Head](/wiki/Head_(company)) Boom MP 2022 racket with 16 main and 19 cross [strings](/wiki/Strings_(tennis)). She wears [New Balance](/wiki/New_Balance) clothing and tennis shoes. In October 2018, Gauff signed her first multi-year sponsorship contract, with New Balance. At the [2021 French Open](/wiki/2021_French_Open), Gauff wore a New Balance outfit of bold mismatched color splotches to contrast with the all-white ensemble of doubles partner Venus Williams. Since 2019, she has been a [Rolex](/wiki/Rolex) Testimonee and frequently wears an Oyster Perpetual.In March 2019, Gauff announced a multi-year sponsorship agreement with Italian food company [Barilla](/wiki/Barilla_Group), which also sponsors [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer). In January 2023, Gauff was announced as a brand ambassador for advisory CPA firm Baker Tilly US, LLP. In January 2024 at the Australian Open, Gauff debuted a new New Balance pair of shoes with geographical coordinates specially inscribed on the soles. She revealed in a post-match interview that the coordinates (*26 28 0.75" N 80 5' 1.4" W)* pointed to public tennis courts in [Delray Beach](/wiki/Delray_Beach,_Florida) where she grew up and trained.

In 2025, Gauff became the face of a collaboration between [Miu Miu](/wiki/Miu_Miu) and New Balance, blending luxury fashion with performance wear in a campaign that highlighted her crossover appeal.

She is a Rolex ambassador

## Personal life

Gauff in 2022

Gauff is a [Christian](/wiki/Christianity). Since she was eight years old, she has prayed with her father before every match that she and her opponent would be safe. After winning the Cincinnati Open in August 2023, she said: "... I'd like to thank my Lord and Savior, Jesus Christ. I spent a lot of nights alone, crying trying to figure it out. I still have a lot to figure out, but I thank Him for covering me." After winning her first major title in September 2023, she said: "... I don't pray for results, I just ask that I get the strength to give it my all and whatever happens happens. I'm so blessed in this life."

Gauff's tennis idols are Serena and Venus Williams. "Serena Williams has always been my idol...and Venus," she has said. "They are the reason why I wanted to pick up a tennis racquet." Gauff first met Serena when she won the Little Mo national tournament at the age of eight, and later met her again to film a commercial for [Delta Air Lines](/wiki/Delta_Air_Lines) and at the Mouratoglou Academy. After defeating Venus Williams at Wimbledon in 2019, Gauff expressed her respect when they shook hands at the net. "I was just telling her thank you for everything she's done for the sport," Gauff said. "She's been an inspiration for many people. I was just really telling her thank you."

Gauff stated in 2020 that she had experienced [depression](/wiki/Depression_(mood)) and stress related to her sporting career, though her parents clarified that she was not diagnosed with depression in the clinical sense, and had not sought medical attention relating to her psychological well-being.

Gauff is a fan of [anime](/wiki/Anime), including *[My Hero Academia](/wiki/My_Hero_Academia)*.

Gauff is a fan of popular YouTubers and streamers [Ludwig Ahgren](/wiki/Ludwig_Ahgren), [Valkyrae](/wiki/Valkyrae), and [Kai Cenat](/wiki/Kai_Cenat).

In July 2023, Gauff and actress [Storm Reid](/wiki/Storm_Reid) helped fund a new playground and purple-painted tennis courts in East Atlanta's [Brownwood Park](/wiki/Brownwood_Park).

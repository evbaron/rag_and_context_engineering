***Digimon Adventure*** ([Japanese](/wiki/Japanese_language): デジモンアドベンチャー, [Hepburn](/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Dejimon Adobenchā*), also known as ***Digimon: Digital Monsters Season 1*** in English-speaking territories, is a 1999 Japanese [anime](/wiki/Anime) television series produced by [Toei Animation](/wiki/Toei_Animation) in cooperation with WiZ, [Bandai](/wiki/Bandai) and [Fuji Television](/wiki/Fuji_Television). It is the first anime series in the [*Digimon* media franchise](/wiki/Digimon), based on the [Digital Monster](/wiki/Digital_Monster_(virtual_pet)) virtual pet released in 1997.

*Digimon Adventure* began production 1.5 months after the [film of the same name](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_(film)) was being produced, and it began broadcast in Japan a day after the film's theatrical release on March 7, 1999, airing until its conclusion on March 26, 2000. It was then followed up with the 2000 film *[Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure:_Our_War_Game!)*. Both films were adapted and released in North America as *[Digimon: The Movie](/wiki/Digimon:_The_Movie)* on October 6, 2000.

With the success of *Digimon Adventure*, a sequel television series, *[Digimon Adventure 02](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_02)*, was broadcast from 2000 to 2001. For the series' 15th anniversary, a six-part film series titled *[Digimon Adventure tri.](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_tri.)* was released between 2015 and 2018, and a final film titled *[Digimon Adventure: Last Evolution Kizuna](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure:_Last_Evolution_Kizuna)* was released in 2020. In 2020, the series was [rebooted](/wiki/Reboot_(fiction)) and released under the title *[Digimon Adventure](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_(2020_TV_series))*.

## Plot

See also: [List of Digimon Adventure characters](/wiki/List_of_Digimon_Adventure_characters)

On August 1, 1999, seven children from [Odaiba](/wiki/Odaiba), Tokyo, Japan, are transported into the Digital World by [Digivices](/wiki/Digivice) that appeared before them at summer camp, where they befriend several Digimon (Digital Monsters). The children's Digivices allow their partner Digimon to Digivolve into stronger forms and combat enemies. As the children explore to find a way home, they learn that they are "DigiDestined", children chosen to save the Digital World. During their adventure, the DigiDestined are hunted by Devimon, who uses black gears to corrupt various Digimon into attacking the group. After defeating Devimon, the DigiDestined are contacted by Gennai and instructed to reach the Server Continent to retrieve artifacts called Crests, allowing their Digimon partners to Digivolve into their Ultimate forms. During this time, they are targeted by Etemon.

After Etemon is defeated, the DigiDestined learn from Gennai that there is an eighth DigiDestined child, who Myotismon is entering the real world to kill. The DigiDestined follow after Myotismon to the real world. After discovering that the eighth child is Tai's younger sister, Kari, and that Myotismon’s lieutenant Gatomon is her Digimon partner, the DigiDestined are able to defeat Myotismon. However, the boundaries between the real world and Digital World are intersecting, forcing them to return to the Digital World.

The DigiDestined face the Dark Masters, a quartet of mega-level Digimon who each took control of a part of the Digital World in their absence. In the midst of their battles, they learn that they were chosen to save the real and Digital Worlds from intersecting four years ago. Tension leads to infighting within the group and causes them to temporarily separate. After reflecting, the DigiDestined reunite to defeat the last Dark Master, and confront Apocalymon who attempts to destroy both worlds. Apocalymon destroys their Crests, but the DigiDestined realize the power of their Crests was inside them all along and manage to defeat him. With the Digital World restored, Tai and his friends leave their Digimon partners behind and return to their normal lives.

## Development

In 1999, a short film based on the virtual pets called *Digimon Adventure* was released. However, shortly after the film's storyboard was completed in 1998, producers at [Toei Animation](/wiki/Toei_Animation) were requested to turn it into a television series.

The DigiDestined's character designs were created by Katsuyoshi Nakatsuru. The character names are based on kanji related to luck.

## Media

### Anime

Main article: [List of *Digimon Adventure* (1999 TV series) episodes](/wiki/List_of_Digimon_Adventure_(1999_TV_series)_episodes)

*Digimon Adventure* was produced by [Toei Animation](/wiki/Toei_Animation) and ran for 54 episodes on [Fuji TV](/wiki/Fuji_TV) between March 7, 1999, and March 26, 2000. The main opening theme for all episodes aired in Japan is "[Butter-Fly](/wiki/Butter-Fly)" by [Kōji Wada](/wiki/K%C5%8Dji_Wada), which peaked at #47 on the [Oricon](/wiki/Oricon) Weekly Singles Chart. "I Wish" by [AiM](/wiki/Ai_Maeda_(voice_actress)), Mimi's voice actress, is used as the ending theme from episodes 1–26, while "Keep On", AiM's fifth single, served as the ending theme from episodes 27–54. The series also uses three insert songs: "Brave Heart" by [Ayumi Miyazaki](/wiki/Ayumi_Miyazaki) as the Digivolution theme, "Seven" by Kōji Wada, and "Yūki o Tsubasa ni Shite" (勇気を翼にして) by [Toshiko Fujita](/wiki/Toshiko_Fujita), Tai's voice actress, as Tai's theme song. On August 1, 2014, during the series' 15th anniversary, a [Blu-ray Disc](/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc) box of the original series was announced and set for release in Japan on March 15, 2015.

[Saban Entertainment](/wiki/Saban_Entertainment) licensed the series in North America and produced an English-language version under the title *Digimon: Digital Monsters*. It premiered on [Fox Kids Network](/wiki/Fox_Kids) on August 14, 1999. The series was also broadcast in Canada on [YTV](/wiki/YTV_(Canadian_TV_channel)). The English version featured an original soundtrack and made changes to character names, as well as edits pertaining to certain aspects such as violence to make the series more suitable for younger audiences. [Wendee Lee](/wiki/Wendee_Lee), [Michael Sorich](/wiki/Michael_Sorich) and [David Walsh](/wiki/David_Walsh_(actor)) became the voice directors. The original soundtrack of the show was replaced by music composed by Udi Harpaz and [Shuki Levy](/wiki/Shuki_Levy), which recycled several music soundtracks from *[Starcom: The U.S. Space Force](/wiki/Starcom:_The_U.S._Space_Force)*, *[Masked Rider](/wiki/Masked_Rider_(TV_series))* and *[Spider-Man: The Animated Series](/wiki/Spider-Man_(1994_TV_series))*. The opening theme for all episodes is "Digimon Theme" by [Paul Gordon](/wiki/Paul_Gordon_(musician)). "Hey Digimon" by Gordon, an insert song featured in the show, and was released on the [original soundtrack](/wiki/Digimon:_The_Movie#Soundtrack) of *[Digimon: The Movie](/wiki/Digimon:_The_Movie)* along with "Digimon Theme".

The English dub series was released on DVD by [Twentieth Century Fox](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox) (Saban's parent company) in 2000 and by [Buena Vista Home Entertainment](/wiki/Buena_Vista_Home_Entertainment) in 2002. A complete DVD boxset of the English dub was released by [New Video](/wiki/New_Video) Group on October 9, 2012 in the U.S and was released by [Madman Entertainment](/wiki/Madman_Entertainment) on June 18, 2014 in Australia. On March 14, 2022, [Discotek Media](/wiki/Discotek_Media) announced a Blu-ray collection. The English dub version, *Digimon: Digital Monsters Season 1*, was released on December 27, 2022, while the original Japanese version, *Digimon Adventure*, was released on July 25, 2023.

### Films

Main article: [List of Digimon films](/wiki/List_of_Digimon_films)

Several short films based on the series were released in theaters in Japan. *[Digimon Adventure](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_(film))* was originally released on March 6, 1999. The story focuses on Tai and Kari finding a Digi-egg from their computer, which hatches and quickly Digivolves, culminating in a battle. The film grossed [¥](/wiki/Japanese_yen)650 million.

*[Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure:_Our_War_Game!)* was originally released on March 4, 2000. In the film, the DigiDestined find a virus Digimon who Digivolves into who infects the Internet. The film introduces DNA Digivolution. The film grossed [¥](/wiki/Japanese_yen)2.166 billion. *Our War Game!* later served as the inspiration for director [Mamoru Hosoda](/wiki/Mamoru_Hosoda)'s film *[Summer Wars](/wiki/Summer_Wars)*.

The two short films were combined with *[Digimon Adventure 02: Part 1: Digimon Hurricane Landing!! / Part 2: Supreme Evolution!! The Golden Digimentals](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_02#Films)* and was released as *[Digimon: The Movie](/wiki/Digimon:_The_Movie)* in North America on October 6, 2000. *Digimon: The Movie* was altered from the original script to remove "culturally awkward" Japanese elements and introduced jokes suitable for a North American audience. Originally, scriptwriter [Jeff Nimoy](/wiki/Jeff_Nimoy) wanted to combine *Digimon Adventure* and *Our War Game!* while releasing *Digimon Hurricane Landing / Supreme Evolution!! The Golden Digimentals* as a direct-to-television movie, but the idea was overruled. In order to connect the film's stories, the script was rewritten.

*Digimon Adventure 3D: Digimon Grand Prix!* (デジモンアドベンチャー3D デジモングランプリ!, *Dejimon Adobenchā: Dejimon Guran Puri*), a [stereoscopic 3D](/wiki/Stereoscopic_3D) short film, was shown at Toei Animation Festival on October 3, 2009 and was later included on a set of DVD works released on February 21, 2010.

### Video games

See also: [List of Digimon video games](/wiki/List_of_Digimon_video_games) and [Digimon Adventure (video game)](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_(video_game))

Characters and Digimon from *Adventure* appear throughout many video games based on the franchise, such as *[Digimon Rumble Arena](/wiki/Digimon_Rumble_Arena)*.

An [RPG](/wiki/Role-playing_game) based on the original storyline of *Adventure* developed by [Prope](/wiki/Prope) and published by [Namco Bandai Games](/wiki/Namco_Bandai_Games), also title *[Digimon Adventure](/wiki/Digimon_Adventure_(video_game))*, was released for the [PlayStation Portable](/wiki/PlayStation_Portable) on January 17, 2013, part of the line-up of video games of the 15th-anniversary celebration of the franchise. The game covers the entire series as well as the second Japanese film, *Bokura no War Game*, and sees the return of all the main voice actors. The game also features original story elements and an unlockable dungeon mode featuring sequel protagonists in the franchise.

### Other media

Other media adaptions include a [manhua](/wiki/Manhua) released in 2000, drawn by [Yu Yuen-wong](/wiki/Yu_Yuen-wong), and its sequel. The manhua was originally published in [Hong Kong](/wiki/Hong_Kong), by Rightman. In total it was published in five volumes. [Tokyopop](/wiki/Tokyopop) published the series in English in the United States, while [Chuang Yi](/wiki/Chuang_Yi) did so in Singapore.

There is a second manhua, titled 數碼寶貝, written by Lu Shui-shi (呂水世), published by [Ching Win](/wiki/Ching_Win).

A North American 12 issue adaption of the first arc of the show was published by Dark Horse Comics. A novelization was written by the Digimon Adventure screenwriter, Hiro Masaki and series director [Hiroyuki Kakudō](/wiki/Hiroyuki_Kakud%C5%8D). The light novels were separated into three parts. Four Drama CDs were also released between 1999 and 2003.

## Reception

On its initial release, the series found a rather large success in the United States. When it was first released in North America, the series was seen as an attempt to imitate the success of [Nintendo](/wiki/Nintendo)'s *[Pokémon](/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon)* franchise. *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* magazine named *Digimon* as the "Worst *Pokémon*/Net Crossbreeding Attempt" in 2000. However, praise was later directed towards the realistic interactions and development of its child characters compared to those of the contemporary *Pokémon* anime, as well as the improved English dubbing and complex science fiction and social themes introduced in later seasons.

Despite the criticism, it placed first at the start of the May 2000 [Nielsen ratings](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings) [sweeps](/wiki/Sweeps), surpassing *[Pokémon: Adventures on the Orange Islands](/wiki/List_of_Pok%C3%A9mon:_Adventures_on_the_Orange_Islands_episodes)* among viewers aged 2–11 and 6–11. Retailers and businesses such as snack food company [Jel Sert](/wiki/Jel_Sert) and toy store chain [Toys "R" Us](/wiki/Toys_%22R%22_Us) capitalized on the popularity of the series by licensing it for promotion with their own products. [Web search engine](/wiki/Web_search_engine) [Lycos](/wiki/Lycos) listed *Digimon* as the number five [fad](/wiki/Fads_and_trends) of 2000, and it ranked 35th on the list of the year's top searches.

In [TV Asahi](/wiki/TV_Asahi)'s poll of the Top 100 Anime, *Digimon* came in 54th. On [Anime News Network](/wiki/Anime_News_Network), Luke Carroll gave the *Digimon: Digital Monsters - Collection 2* DVD an overall grade of D+.

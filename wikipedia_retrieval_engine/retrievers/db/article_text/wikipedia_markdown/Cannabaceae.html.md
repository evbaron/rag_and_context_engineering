# Cannabaceae

Not to be confused with [Cannaceae](/wiki/Cannaceae).

**Cannabaceae** is a small [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) of [flowering plants](/wiki/Flowering_plant), known as the **hemp family**. As now [circumscribed](/wiki/Circumscription_(taxonomy)), the family includes about 170 species grouped in about 11 genera, including *[Cannabis](/wiki/Cannabis)* (hemp), *[Humulus](/wiki/Humulus)* ([hops](/wiki/Hops)) and *[Celtis](/wiki/Celtis)* (hackberries). *Celtis* is by far the largest genus, containing about 100 species.

Cannabaceae is a member of the [Rosales](/wiki/Rosales). Members of the family are erect or climbing plants with petalless flowers and dry, one-seeded fruits. Hemp (*Cannabis*) and hop (*Humulus*) are the most economically important species.

Other than a shared evolutionary origin, members of the family have few common characteristics; some are trees (e.g. *Celtis*), others are [herbaceous plants](/wiki/Herbaceous_plant) (e.g. *Cannabis*).

## Description

Members of this family can be trees (e.g. *[Celtis](/wiki/Celtis)*), erect herbs (e.g. *[Cannabis](/wiki/Cannabis)*), or twining herbs (e.g. *[Humulus](/wiki/Humulus)*).

Leaves are often more or less palmately lobed or palmately compound and always bear [stipules](/wiki/Stipule). [Cystoliths](/wiki/Cystolith) are always present and some members of this family possess [laticifers](/wiki/Laticifer).

Cannabaceae are often [dioecious](/wiki/Dioecious) (distinct male and female plants). The flowers are [actinomorphic](/wiki/Actinomorphic) (radially symmetrical) and not ornate, as these plants are [pollinated by the wind](/wiki/Wind_pollination). As an adaptation to this kind of pollination, the [calyx](/wiki/Calyx_(botany)) and [corolla](/wiki/Corolla_(flower)) are radically reduced to only vestigial remnants found as an adherent perianth coating the seed. A reduced and monophyllous cuplike perigonal bract, properly known as the bracteole, immediately surrounds and protects the seed and is often misnamed as a "calyx". Flowers are grouped to form [cymes](/wiki/Cyme_(botany)). In the dioecious plants the male inflorescences are long and look like [panicles](/wiki/Panicle), while the female ones are shorter and bear fewer flowers. The [pistil](/wiki/Pistil) is made of two connate [carpels](/wiki/Carpel), the usually superior ovary is unilocular; there is no fixed number of [stamens](/wiki/Stamen).

The fruit can be an [achene](/wiki/Achene) or a [drupe](/wiki/Drupe).

## Taxonomy

### Classification

Classification systems developed prior to the 1990s, such as those of Cronquist (1981) and Dahlgren (1989), typically recognized the order [Urticales](/wiki/Urticales), which included the families Cannabaceae, Cecropiaceae, Celtidaceae, Moraceae, Ulmaceae and Urticaceae, as then circumscribed. Molecular data from 1990s onwards showed that these families were actually embedded within the order Rosales, so that from the first classification by the [Angiosperm Phylogeny Group](/wiki/Angiosperm_Phylogeny_Group) in 1998, they were placed in an expanded Rosales, forming a group which has been called 'urticalean rosids'.

### Phylogeny

Cannabaceae likely originated in East Asia during the Late [Cretaceous](/wiki/Cretaceous). The oldest known pollen typical of members of Cannabaceae is from the Late Cretaceous ([Turonian](/wiki/Turonian) ~94–90 million years ago) of [Sarawak](/wiki/Sarawak), Borneo. Fossils show Cannabaceae were widely distributed in the Northern Hemisphere during the early Cenozoic, though their distribution shifted towards tropical regions in the later Cenozoic due to changing climates.

## Uses

[Carbon dating](/wiki/Carbon_dating) has revealed that these plants may have been used for ritual/medicinal purposes in Xinjiang, China as early as 494 B.C.

*[Humulus lupulus](/wiki/Humulus_lupulus)*, the common hop, has been the predominant bittering agent of [beer](/wiki/Beer) for hundreds of years. The flowers' resins are responsible for beer's [bitterness](/wiki/Bitter_(taste)) and their ability to extend shelf life due to some [antimicrobial](/wiki/Antimicrobial) qualities. The young shoots can be used as a vegetable.

Some plants in the genus *Cannabis* are cultivated as [hemp](/wiki/Hemp) for the production of fiber, as a source of cheap [oil](/wiki/Hemp_oil), for their nutritious seeds, or their edible leaves. Others are cultivated for medical or recreational use as [dried flowers](/wiki/Cannabis_(drug)), [extracts](/wiki/Hashish), or [infused food products](/wiki/Cannabis_edible). Induced [parthenocarpy](/wiki/Parthenocarpy) in pistilate flowers, and [selective breeding](/wiki/Cannabis_strains) are used to produce either higher or lower yields of [tetrahydrocannabinol](/wiki/Tetrahydrocannabinol) (THC), other [cannabinoids](/wiki/Cannabinoid), as well as [terpenes](/wiki/Terpenes) with desired flavors or aromas, such as blueberry, strawberry, or even citrus.

Many trees in the genus *Celtis* are grown for landscaping and ornamental purposes, and the bark of [Pteroceltis](/wiki/Pteroceltis) is used to produce high-end Chinese paper.

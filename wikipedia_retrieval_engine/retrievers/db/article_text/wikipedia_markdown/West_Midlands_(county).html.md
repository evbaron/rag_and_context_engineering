**West Midlands** is a [metropolitan](/wiki/Metropolitan_county) and [ceremonial county](/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) in the larger [West Midlands](/wiki/West_Midlands_(region)) region of England. It is bordered by [Staffordshire](/wiki/Staffordshire) to the north and west, [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire) to the south, and projects into [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire) to the east. The largest settlement is the city of [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), it also contains the cities of [Coventry](/wiki/Coventry) and [Wolverhampton](/wiki/Wolverhampton).

The county has an area of 902 km2 (348 sq mi) and is almost entirely urban, with an estimated population of 3,036,605 in 2024; this made it the 2nd [most populous county](/wiki/List_of_ceremonial_counties_of_England) in England after [Greater London](/wiki/Greater_London). Birmingham is in the centre of the county, with [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull) and the city of [Coventry](/wiki/Coventry) to the east and [West Bromwich](/wiki/West_Bromwich) and the city of [Wolverhampton](/wiki/Wolverhampton) to the west. [Sutton Coldfield](/wiki/Sutton_Coldfield) lies to the north. Most of the settlements in the centre and west of the county belong to the [West Midlands conurbation](/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation), which is separated from Coventry by [a rural area](/wiki/Meriden_Gap) around the town of [Balsall Common](/wiki/Balsall_Common). For [local government](/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes the West Midlands comprises seven [metropolitan boroughs](/wiki/Metropolitan_borough): Birmingham, Coventry, [Dudley](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Dudley), [Sandwell](/wiki/Sandwell), [Solihull](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Solihull), [Walsall](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Walsall), and Wolverhampton. Their councils collaborate through the [West Midlands Combined Authority](/wiki/West_Midlands_Combined_Authority). The county was historically part of [Shropshire](/wiki/Shropshire_(Detached)), Staffordshire, Warwickshire, and Worcestershire.

The west of the county encloses the valley of the [River Tame](/wiki/River_Tame,_West_Midlands) and its tributaries; the highest point of the surrounding area is [Turners Hill](/wiki/Turners_Hill,_West_Midlands), at 271 metres (889 ft). West Midlands contains the [Sutton Park](/wiki/Sutton_Park,_West_Midlands) [Site of Special Scientific Interest](/wiki/List_of_Sites_of_Special_Scientific_Interest_in_the_West_Midlands), which has an area of 970 hectares (2,400 acres) and is one of the largest urban parks in Europe. The area between Solihull and Coventry is part of the [Forest of Arden](/wiki/Forest_of_Arden), and the rivers [Sowe](/wiki/River_Sowe) and [Sherbourne](/wiki/River_Sherbourne) flow through Coventry.

## Status

The metropolitan county exists in law, as a geographical frame of reference, and as a [ceremonial county](/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England). As such it has a [Lord Lieutenant](/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_the_West_Midlands) and a [High Sheriff](/wiki/High_Sheriff). Between 1974 and 1986, the [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council) was the administrative body covering the county; this was abolished on 31 March 1986, and the constituent metropolitan boroughs effectively became [unitary authorities](/wiki/Unitary_authority). A new administrative body for the county (and some of the district surrounding it as Non-Constituent members), the [West Midlands Combined Authority](/wiki/West_Midlands_Combined_Authority), was created in June 2016. Since May 2017, the authority has been headed by a directly elected [Mayor of the West Midlands](/wiki/Mayor_of_the_West_Midlands), a position currently held by [Richard Parker](/wiki/Richard_Parker_(mayor)) of the [Labour and Co-operative Parties](/wiki/Labour_and_Co-operative_Party). Other county-wide bodies include the [West Midlands Police](/wiki/West_Midlands_Police), the [West Midlands Fire Service](/wiki/West_Midlands_Fire_Service) and [Transport for West Midlands](/wiki/Transport_for_West_Midlands).

The county is sometimes described as the "West Midlands [metropolitan area](/wiki/Metropolitan_area)" or the "[West Midlands conurbation](/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation)" or "Greater Birmingham", although these have different, less clearly defined, boundaries. The main [conurbation](/wiki/Conurbation) or urban area does not include Coventry, for example. The name "West Midlands" is also used for the much larger [West Midlands region](/wiki/West_Midlands_(region)), which sometimes causes confusion. Geographically the county is on the eastern side of the region, the western side comprising [Shropshire](/wiki/Shropshire) and [Herefordshire](/wiki/Herefordshire) and the southern side comprising [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire) and [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire).

## History

Main article: [History of the West Midlands](/wiki/History_of_the_West_Midlands)

See also: [History of Birmingham](/wiki/History_of_Birmingham) and [History of Coventry](/wiki/History_of_Coventry)

Although the modern county has only existed since 1974, the settlements of the West Midlands have long been important centres of commerce and industry as well as developing a good local infrastructure. Coventry was one of England's most important cities during the [Middle Ages](/wiki/Middle_Ages), with its prosperity built upon wool and cloth manufacture. Birmingham and Wolverhampton have a tradition of industry dating back to the 16th century, when small metal-working industries developed. Birmingham was known for its manufacture of [small arms](/wiki/Small_arms), whereas Wolverhampton became a centre of lock manufacture and brass working. The coal and iron ore deposits of the [Black Country](/wiki/Black_Country) area provided a ready source of raw materials. The area grew rapidly during the [Industrial Revolution](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), and by the 20th century had grown into one large conurbation. Coventry was slower to develop, but by the early 20th century it had become an important centre of bicycle and car manufacture.

1966 saw a substantial reform in the local government of the area as the patchwork of [county boroughs](/wiki/County_Borough) with [municipal boroughs](/wiki/Municipal_borough) and [urban district councils](/wiki/Urban_district_(Great_Britain_and_Ireland)) in between was replaced by a core of county boroughs covering a contiguous area, roughly as follows:

- [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), which remained substantially unaltered
- [Dudley](/wiki/Dudley), which absorbed all [Brierley Hill](/wiki/Brierley_Hill), most of [Coseley](/wiki/Coseley) and [Sedgley](/wiki/Sedgley), and part of [Amblecote](/wiki/Amblecote), [Tipton](/wiki/Tipton) and [Rowley Regis](/wiki/Rowley_Regis)
- [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull), which remained substantially unaltered
- [Walsall](/wiki/Walsall), which absorbed all [Darlaston](/wiki/Darlaston), most of [Willenhall](/wiki/Willenhall), and parts of [Wednesbury](/wiki/Wednesbury), [Coseley](/wiki/Coseley), [Wednesfield](/wiki/Wednesfield) and [Bilston](/wiki/Bilston)
- [Warley](/wiki/County_Borough_of_Warley), which was created by amalgamating most of [Smethwick](/wiki/Smethwick), [Oldbury](/wiki/Oldbury,_West_Midlands) and [Rowley Regis](/wiki/Rowley_Regis), and parts of Dudley, Tipton, West Bromwich and [Halesowen](/wiki/Halesowen)
- [West Bromwich](/wiki/West_Bromwich), which absorbed most of Wednesbury and Tipton, and parts of [Bilston](/wiki/Bilston), Oldbury, [Smethwick](/wiki/Smethwick) and Walsall
- [Wolverhampton](/wiki/Wolverhampton), which absorbed most of [Bilston](/wiki/Bilston), [Wednesfield](/wiki/Wednesfield) and [Tettenhall](/wiki/Tettenhall), and parts of Sedgley, Coseley and Willenhall

Near the area, three other towns remained separate ([Halesowen](/wiki/Halesowen), [Stourbridge](/wiki/Stourbridge) and [Sutton Coldfield](/wiki/Sutton_Coldfield)), while [Aldridge](/wiki/Aldridge) and [Brownhills](/wiki/Brownhills) joined to form a single unit, called [Aldridge-Brownhills](/wiki/Aldridge-Brownhills_Urban_District). In the same year, a single [West Midlands Constabulary](/wiki/West_Midlands_Constabulary) was formed for the Black Country county boroughs, whilst Birmingham retained its [Birmingham City Police](/wiki/Birmingham_City_Police) and Solihull continued being policed by the [Warwickshire Constabulary](/wiki/Warwickshire_Constabulary). The [West Midlands Passenger Transport Authority](/wiki/Transport_for_West_Midlands) was established in 1968.

### County creation

In 1974, the [Local Government Act 1972](/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972) came into effect, creating the metropolitan county of West Midlands. This area was based on the seven county boroughs and the other non-county boroughs and urban districts around the fringe of the conurbation.

The new area consisted of seven new metropolitan boroughs, with [Aldridge-Brownhills](/wiki/Aldridge-Brownhills_Urban_District) added to [Walsall](/wiki/Walsall); Halesowen and Stourbridge to Dudley and Sutton Coldfield to Birmingham. A new borough of [Sandwell](/wiki/Sandwell) was formed by the merger of West Bromwich and Warley. The actual designation of Warley itself was abolished and the three towns of Smethwick, Oldbury and Rowley Regis reinstated as component parts of Sandwell, although these areas formed the Warley postal district. Solihull took in much of the suburban fringe to the east of Birmingham, including the former villages of [Chelmsley Wood](/wiki/Chelmsley_Wood) and [Castle Bromwich](/wiki/Castle_Bromwich), also [Birmingham Airport](/wiki/Birmingham_Airport), and the area of countryside between Solihull and Coventry, whilst Coventry itself received only small changes and Wolverhampton was unaltered. This led to (apart from in the east, with Coventry and the Meriden Gap) quite a tightly defined metropolitan border, excluding such places as [Burntwood](/wiki/Burntwood), [Bromsgrove](/wiki/Bromsgrove), [Cannock](/wiki/Cannock), [Kidderminster](/wiki/Kidderminster), [Lichfield](/wiki/Lichfield) and [Wombourne](/wiki/Wombourne) which had been considered for inclusion in the West Midlands metropolitan area by the [Redcliffe-Maud Report](/wiki/Redcliffe-Maud_Report).

The 1974 reform created the [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council) that covered the entire area and dealt with strategic issues. A new [West Midlands Police](/wiki/West_Midlands_Police) service was formed covering the entire area, with the West Midlands Constabulary and Birmingham City Police abolished, and also taking over responsibility from the county forces.

West Midlands was also established as a new [ceremonial county](/wiki/Ceremonial_county), with the offices of [Lord Lieutenant](/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_the_West_Midlands) and [High Sheriff](/wiki/High_Sheriff_of_the_West_Midlands) created. Its constituent components had previously been, for ceremonial purposes, under the equivalent offices of [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire) ([Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) [CB](/wiki/County_borough), [Coventry](/wiki/Coventry) CB, [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull) CB, [Sutton Coldfield](/wiki/Sutton_Coldfield) [MB](/wiki/Municipal_borough) and [Meriden](/wiki/Meriden_Rural_District) [RD](/wiki/Rural_district)), [Staffordshire](/wiki/Staffordshire) ([Wolverhampton](/wiki/Wolverhampton) CB, [Walsall](/wiki/Walsall) CB, [West Bromwich](/wiki/West_Bromwich) CB, [Dudley](/wiki/Dudley) CB and Aldridge-Brownhills [UD](/wiki/Urban_district_(Great_Britain_and_Ireland))) and [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire) ([Warley](/wiki/County_Borough_of_Warley) CB, [Stourbridge](/wiki/Stourbridge) MB and [Halesowen](/wiki/Halesowen) MB).

### West Midlands County Council

Main article: [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council)

The arms of the [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council), depicted here, became redundant with the abolition of the council in 1986 (though similar arms are used by the [West Midlands Fire Service](/wiki/West_Midlands_Fire_Service)).

Between 1974 and 1986, the county had a two-tier system of local government, and the seven districts shared power with the [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council). However, the [Local Government Act 1985](/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1985) abolished the metropolitan county councils, and the West Midlands County Council ceased to exist in 1986. Most of its functions were devolved to the West Midland boroughs, which effectively became [unitary authorities](/wiki/Unitary_authority), with responsibility for most local authority functions.

Following the abolition of the county council, some county-wide bodies continued to exist, which were administered by various joint-boards of the seven districts, among these were the [West Midlands Police](/wiki/West_Midlands_Police), the [West Midlands Fire Service](/wiki/West_Midlands_Fire_Service) and the [West Midlands Passenger Transport Executive](/wiki/West_Midlands_Passenger_Transport_Executive).

### Boundary changes

In 1994, the western/southern shores of [Chasewater](/wiki/Chasewater), plus the adjacent Jeffreys Swag, were transferred from the [Metropolitan Borough of Walsall](/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Walsall) to the [District of Lichfield](/wiki/Lichfield_(district)), Staffordshire. Further boundary changes came into effect in 1995, when part of the [Hereford and Worcester](/wiki/Hereford_and_Worcester) parish of [Frankley](/wiki/Frankley) (including the south-west part of [Bartley Reservoir](/wiki/Bartley_Reservoir)) was transferred to Birmingham and became part of the county.

### West Midlands Combined Authority

Main article: [West Midlands Combined Authority](/wiki/West_Midlands_Combined_Authority)

On 17 June 2016, a new administrative body, the [West Midlands Combined Authority](/wiki/West_Midlands_Combined_Authority) was created for the county, under the [Local Democracy, Economic Development and Construction Act 2009](/wiki/Local_Democracy,_Economic_Development_and_Construction_Act_2009), which created several other [combined authorities](/wiki/Combined_authority) in England. The new body has powers over transport, economic development, skills and planning. A new directly elected position of [Mayor of the West Midlands](/wiki/Mayor_of_the_West_Midlands) was created in 2017 to chair the new body. The first [Mayoral election](/wiki/2017_West_Midlands_mayoral_election) was held in May 2017, and the position was won by [Andy Street](/wiki/Andy_Street) of the [Conservative Party](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)).

## Geography

See also: [List of places in West Midlands (county)](/wiki/List_of_places_in_West_Midlands_(county)), [List of settlements in West Midlands (county) by population](/wiki/List_of_settlements_in_West_Midlands_(county)_by_population), [List of areas in Birmingham](/wiki/List_of_areas_in_Birmingham), [List of areas in Dudley](/wiki/List_of_areas_in_Dudley), [List of areas in Sandwell](/wiki/List_of_areas_in_Sandwell), [List of areas in Walsall](/wiki/List_of_areas_in_Walsall), [List of areas in Wolverhampton](/wiki/List_of_areas_in_Wolverhampton), and [West Midlands Green Belt](/wiki/West_Midlands_Green_Belt)

Map of West Midlands, showing urban areas in grey and metropolitan district boundaries

Population density map

The West Midlands is a landlocked county that borders the counties of [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire) to the east, [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire) to the south, and [Staffordshire](/wiki/Staffordshire) to the north and west.

The West Midlands County is one of the most heavily urbanised counties in the UK. [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), [Wolverhampton](/wiki/Wolverhampton), the [Black Country](/wiki/Black_Country) and [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull) together form the [third most populous conurbation in the United Kingdom](/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_United_Kingdom) with a combined population of around 2.44 million. However, the West Midlands is not entirely urban; [Coventry](/wiki/Coventry) is separated from the [West Midlands conurbation](/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation) by a stretch of [green belt](/wiki/Green_belt_(UK)) land approximately 13 miles (21 km) across, known as the "[Meriden Gap](/wiki/Meriden_Gap)", which retains a strongly rural character. A smaller piece of green belt between Birmingham, Walsall and West Bromwich includes [Barr Beacon](/wiki/Barr_Beacon) and the [Sandwell Valley](/wiki/Sandwell_Valley).

The highest point in the West Midlands is [Turners Hill](/wiki/Turners_Hill,_West_Midlands), with an elevation of 271 m (889 ft). The hill is a [Site of Special Scientific Interest](/wiki/Site_of_Special_Scientific_Interest). [Barr Beacon](/wiki/Barr_Beacon) is another hill in the West Midlands, located on the border of Birmingham and Walsall, with a height of 227 metres (745 ft).

There are 23 [Sites of Special Scientific Interest](/wiki/List_of_Sites_of_Special_Scientific_Interest_in_the_West_Midlands) in the county. One of these SSSIs is [Sutton Park](/wiki/Sutton_Park,_West_Midlands) in Sutton Coldfield, which has an area of 970 hectares (2,400 acres). As a result, it is one of the largest urban parks in Europe, and the largest outside of a capital city in Europe. The park also has [national nature reserve](/wiki/National_nature_reserve_(United_Kingdom)) status.

There are numerous rivers that pass through the county, including the [River Tame](/wiki/River_Tame,_West_Midlands). The [river basin](/wiki/River_basin) is the most urbanised basin in the United Kingdom, with approximately 42% of the basin being urbanised. The River Tame is fed by the [River Rea](/wiki/River_Rea), [River Anker](/wiki/River_Anker), and the [River Blythe](/wiki/River_Blythe), which in turn is fed by the [River Cole](/wiki/River_Cole,_West_Midlands). The [River Sowe](/wiki/River_Sowe) and [River Sherbourne](/wiki/River_Sherbourne) both flow through Coventry. The [River Stour](/wiki/River_Stour,_Worcestershire) flows through the west of the West Midlands county.

Like other [metropolitan counties](/wiki/Metropolitan_county), the West Midlands is divided into [districts](/wiki/Districts_of_England) called [metropolitan boroughs](/wiki/Metropolitan_borough). There are seven boroughs in the West Midlands, six of which are named after the largest settlement in their administrative area. The West Midlands is unusual amongst the metropolitan counties in that three of its boroughs have [city status](/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom); Coventry is a city by [ancient prescriptive usage](/wiki/Time_immemorial), Birmingham was granted city status in 1889, and Wolverhampton in 2000 as a "Millennium City".

## Education

The West Midlands contains ten universities, seven of which are in Birmingham:

- [Aston University](/wiki/Aston_University)
- [University of Birmingham](/wiki/University_of_Birmingham)
- [Birmingham City University](/wiki/Birmingham_City_University)
- [University College Birmingham](/wiki/University_College_Birmingham)
- [BPP University](/wiki/BPP_University)
- [University of Law](/wiki/University_of_Law)
- [Birmingham Newman University](/wiki/Birmingham_Newman_University)

Both [Coventry University](/wiki/Coventry_University) and the [University of Warwick](/wiki/University_of_Warwick) are in Coventry whilst [University of Wolverhampton](/wiki/University_of_Wolverhampton) is located in Wolverhampton with campuses in [Telford](/wiki/Telford) and [Walsall](/wiki/Walsall).

Each of the local authorities has at least one further education college for students aged over 16, and since September 1992 all of the local authorities have operated traditional 5–7 infant, 7–11 junior, and 11–16/18 secondary schools for students in compulsory education. This followed the demise of 5–8 first, 8–12 middle and 12–16/18 secondary schools in the Sutton Coldfield area.

For 18 years before September 1990, Dudley had operated 5–8 first, 8–12 middle, and 12–16/18 secondary schools before then, while Halesowen (September 1972 until July 1982) and Aldridge-Brownhills (September 1972 until July 1986) had both operated 5–9 first, 9–13 middle and 13–16/18 secondary schools.

Many local authorities still have sixth form facilities in secondary schools, though sixth form facilities had been axed by most secondary schools in Dudley since the early 1990s (and in Halesowen in 1982) as the local authorities changed direction towards further education colleges.

All secondary state education in Dudley and Sandwell is mixed comprehensive, although there are a small number of single sex and grammar schools existing in parts of Birmingham, Solihull, Wolverhampton and Walsall.

In August 2009, Matthew Boulton College and Sutton Coldfield College merged to become [Birmingham Metropolitan College](/wiki/Birmingham_Metropolitan_College), one of the largest further and higher education institutions in the country.

The [Birmingham Panthers](/wiki/Birmingham_Panthers) basketball team replaced the [Birmingham Bullets](/wiki/Birmingham_Bullets) and are currently based at a facility provided by the [University of Wolverhampton](/wiki/University_of_Wolverhampton) in Walsall.

The West Midlands has its own [Quidditch](/wiki/Quidditch) team, West Midlands Revolution (after its part in the [Industrial Revolution](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution)), which won the Quidditch Premier League in 2017.

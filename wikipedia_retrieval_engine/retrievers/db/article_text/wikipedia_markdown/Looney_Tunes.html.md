***Looney Tunes*** is an American [media franchise](/wiki/Media_franchise) owned by [Warner Bros. Entertainment](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Entertainment). The franchise began as a series of animated short films that originally ran from 1930 to 1969, alongside its spin-off series *[Merrie Melodies](/wiki/Merrie_Melodies)*, produced by [Warner Bros. Cartoons](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Cartoons) during the [golden age of American animation](/wiki/Golden_age_of_American_animation). Following a revival in the late 1970s, new shorts were released theatrically as recently as 2014. The two series introduced a large [cast of characters](/wiki/List_of_Looney_Tunes_and_Merrie_Melodies_characters), including [Bugs Bunny](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny), [Daffy Duck](/wiki/Daffy_Duck), [Elmer Fudd](/wiki/Elmer_Fudd) and [Porky Pig](/wiki/Porky_Pig). The term *Looney Tunes* has since been expanded to also refer to the characters themselves.

*Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* were initially produced by [Leon Schlesinger](/wiki/Leon_Schlesinger) and animators [Hugh Harman](/wiki/Hugh_Harman) and [Rudolf Ising](/wiki/Rudolf_Ising) from 1930 to 1933. Schlesinger assumed full production from 1933 until he sold his studio to Warner Bros. in 1944, after which it was renamed Warner Bros. Cartoons. The *Looney Tunes* title was inspired by that of [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney)'s *[Silly Symphonies](/wiki/Silly_Symphonies)*. The shorts initially showcased musical compositions owned by Warner's music publishing interests through the adventures of such characters as [Bosko](/wiki/Bosko), [Foxy](/wiki/Foxy_(Merrie_Melodies)) and [Buddy](/wiki/Buddy_(Looney_Tunes)). However, the shorts gained a higher profile upon the debuts of directors [Tex Avery](/wiki/Tex_Avery), [Friz Freleng](/wiki/Friz_Freleng), [Chuck Jones](/wiki/Chuck_Jones), [Bob Clampett](/wiki/Bob_Clampett), and [Robert McKimson](/wiki/Robert_McKimson), and [voice actor](/wiki/Voice_acting) [Mel Blanc](/wiki/Mel_Blanc) later in the decade. Porky Pig and Daffy Duck became the featured *Looney Tunes* characters, while *Merrie Melodies* featured one-shot cartoons and minor recurring characters.

After Bugs Bunny became popular in the *Merrie Melodies* shorts of the early 1940s, *Looney Tunes* moved from [black-and-white](/wiki/Black-and-white) to color production between 1942 and 1943 (*Merrie Melodies* having already been in color since 1934). The two series gradually lost their distinctions, and shorts were assigned to each series arbitrarily. From 1942 to 1964, *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* were the most popular animated shorts in movie theaters.

*Looney Tunes* has become one of the [highest-grossing media franchises](/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_media_franchises) of all time, spawning several television series, feature films, comic books, music albums, video games, and amusement park rides. Many of the characters have made and continue to make cameo appearances in television shows, films, and other media. Bugs Bunny, in particular, is regarded as a [cultural icon](/wiki/Cultural_icon) as well as the mascot of [Warner Bros. Pictures](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures), and has a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame). Many *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* films are ranked among the greatest animated cartoons of all time, and five of them have won [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Short_Film). In 2013, [TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide) counted *Looney Tunes* as the third greatest television cartoon series of all time, behind *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)* and *[The Flintstones](/wiki/The_Flintstones)*, the latter of which also featured the voice talents of Mel Blanc and [Bea Benaderet](/wiki/Bea_Benaderet).

*Looney Tunes* and *[Merrie Melodies](/wiki/Merrie_Melodies)* were so named as a reference to Disney's *[Silly Symphonies](/wiki/Silly_Symphonies)* and were initially developed to showcase tracks from Warner Bros.' extensive music library; the title of the first *Looney Tunes* short, *[Sinkin' in the Bathtub](/wiki/Sinkin%27_in_the_Bathtub)* (1930), is a pun on *[Singin' in the Bathtub](/wiki/Singin%27_in_the_Bathtub)*. Between 1934 and 1943, *Merrie Melodies* were produced in color and *Looney Tunes* in black-and-white. After 1943, both series were produced in color and became virtually indistinguishable, varying only in their opening theme music and titles. Both series made use of the various Warner Bros. characters. By 1937, the theme music for *Looney Tunes* was "[The Merry-Go-Round Broke Down](/wiki/The_Merry-Go-Round_Broke_Down)" by [Cliff Friend](/wiki/Cliff_Friend) and [Dave Franklin](/wiki/Dave_Franklin), and the theme music for *Merrie Melodies* was an adaptation of "[Merrily We Roll Along](/wiki/Merrily_We_Roll_Along_(song))" by [Charles Tobias](/wiki/Charles_Tobias), Murray Mencher and [Eddie Cantor](/wiki/Eddie_Cantor).

### 1930–1933: Harman and Ising era

In 1929, to compete against [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney)'s [*Mickey Mouse*](/wiki/Mickey_Mouse_(film_series)) series, Warner Bros. became interested in developing a series of animated shorts to promote their music. They had recently acquired [Brunswick Records](/wiki/Brunswick_Records) along with four music publishers for US$28 million (equivalent to $540 million in 2025) and were eager to promote this material for the sales of sheet music and phonograph records. Warner made a deal with [Leon Schlesinger](/wiki/Leon_Schlesinger) to produce cartoons for them. Schlesinger hired [Rudolf Ising](/wiki/Rudolf_Ising) and [Hugh Harman](/wiki/Hugh_Harman) to produce a series of cartoons, impressed by their 1929 pilot film, *[Bosko, The Talk-Ink Kid](/wiki/Bosko,_The_Talk-Ink_Kid)*. The first *Looney Tunes* short was *[Sinkin' in the Bathtub](/wiki/Sinkin%27_in_the_Bathtub)* starring [Bosko](/wiki/Bosko), which was released in 1930.

### 1933–1936: Leon Schlesinger Productions

When Harman and Ising left Warner Bros. in 1933 over a budget dispute with Schlesinger, they took with them all the rights of the characters they had created. A new character called [Buddy](/wiki/Buddy_(Looney_Tunes)) became the only star of the *Looney Tunes* series for a couple of years, but quickly became unpopular.

New directors including [Tex Avery](/wiki/Tex_Avery), [Friz Freleng](/wiki/Friz_Freleng) and [Bob Clampett](/wiki/Bob_Clampett) were brought in or promoted to work with animators in the Schlesinger studio, with Avery's unit housed in a bungalow which the animators dubbed "[Termite Terrace](/wiki/Termite_Terrace)." In 1935, [Porky Pig](/wiki/Porky_Pig), the first major *Looney Tunes* star, made his debut in the *Merrie Melodies* cartoon *[I Haven't Got a Hat](/wiki/I_Haven%27t_Got_a_Hat)*, directed by Friz Freleng, featuring an ensemble of characters headed by [Beans the Cat](/wiki/Beans_(Looney_Tunes)). However, after Avery's *Looney Tune* debut *[Gold Diggers of '49](/wiki/Gold_Diggers_of_%2749)* it was Porky who emerged as the star instead of Beans. The ensemble characters of *I Haven't Got a Hat*, such as Oliver Owl and the twin dogs Ham and Ex, were quickly dropped. Beans would be phased out as well by mid-1936, leaving Porky as the only star of the Schlesinger studio.

### 1936–1944: More star characters and switch to color

The debuts of other memorable *Looney Tunes* stars soon followed: [Daffy Duck](/wiki/Daffy_Duck) in *[Porky's Duck Hunt](/wiki/Porky%27s_Duck_Hunt)* (1937), [Elmer Fudd](/wiki/Elmer_Fudd) in the *Merrie Melodies* short *[Elmer's Candid Camera](/wiki/Elmer%27s_Candid_Camera)* (1940), [Bugs Bunny](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny) in the *Merrie Melodies* short *[A Wild Hare](/wiki/A_Wild_Hare)* (1940), and [Tweety](/wiki/Tweety) in the *Merrie Melodies* short *[A Tale of Two Kitties](/wiki/A_Tale_of_Two_Kitties)* (1942).

Bugs initially starred in the color *Merrie Melodies* shorts following the success of 1940's *A Wild Hare*, and formally joined the *Looney Tunes* series with the release of *[Buckaroo Bugs](/wiki/Buckaroo_Bugs)* in 1944.

Schlesinger began to phase in the production of color *Looney Tunes* with the 1942 cartoon *[The Hep Cat](/wiki/The_Hep_Cat)*. The final black-and-white *Looney Tunes* short was *[Puss n' Booty](/wiki/Puss_n%27_Booty)* in 1943, directed by [Frank Tashlin](/wiki/Frank_Tashlin). The inspiration for the changeover was Warner's decision to re-release only the color cartoons in the *[Blue Ribbon Classics](/wiki/Blue_Ribbon_Classic)* series of *Merrie Melodies*. A brief cameo at the end of Tashlin's 1943 cartoon *[Porky Pig's Feat](/wiki/Porky_Pig%27s_Feat)* would be Bugs' only official appearance in a black-and-white *Looney Tunes* short.

Schlesinger sold the cartoon studio in 1944 to Warner Bros. and went into retirement; he died five years later.

### 1944–1964: The Golden era

More popular *Looney Tunes* characters were created (most of which first appeared in *Merrie Melodies* cartoons), such as [Pepé Le Pew](/wiki/Pep%C3%A9_Le_Pew) (debuted in 1945's *[Odor-able Kitty](/wiki/Odor-able_Kitty)*), [Sylvester](/wiki/Sylvester_the_Cat) (debuted in 1945's *[Life with Feathers](/wiki/Life_with_Feathers)*), [Yosemite Sam](/wiki/Yosemite_Sam) (debuted in 1945's *[Hare Trigger](/wiki/Hare_Trigger)*), [Foghorn Leghorn](/wiki/Foghorn_Leghorn) (debuted in 1946's *[Walky Talky Hawky](/wiki/Walky_Talky_Hawky)*), [Marvin the Martian](/wiki/Marvin_the_Martian) (debuted in 1948's *[Haredevil Hare](/wiki/Haredevil_Hare)*), [Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner](/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner) (debuted in 1949's *[Fast and Furry-ous](/wiki/Fast_and_Furry-ous)*), [Granny](/wiki/Granny_(Looney_Tunes)) (debuted in 1950's *[Canary Row](/wiki/Canary_Row)*), [Speedy Gonzales](/wiki/Speedy_Gonzales) (debuted in 1953's *[Cat Tails for Two](/wiki/Cat-Tails_for_Two)*), [the Tasmanian Devil](/wiki/Tasmanian_Devil_(Looney_Tunes)) (debuted in 1954's *[Devil May Hare](/wiki/Devil_May_Hare)*), and [Ralph Wolf and Sam Sheepdog](/wiki/Ralph_Wolf_and_Sam_Sheepdog) (debuted in 1953's *[Don't Give Up the Sheep](/wiki/Don%27t_Give_Up_the_Sheep)*).

It was during this era that the series won five [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Short_Film):

- *[Tweetie Pie](/wiki/Tweetie_Pie)* (1947)
- *[For Scent-imental Reasons](/wiki/For_Scent-imental_Reasons)* (1949)
- *[Speedy Gonzales](/wiki/Speedy_Gonzales_(film))* (1955)
- *[Birds Anonymous](/wiki/Birds_Anonymous)* (1957)
- *[Knighty Knight Bugs](/wiki/Knighty_Knight_Bugs)* (1958)

### 1964–1969: DePatie–Freleng and Seven Arts era

During the mid-late 1960s, the shorts were produced by [DePatie–Freleng Enterprises](/wiki/DePatie%E2%80%93Freleng_Enterprises) (and [Format Productions](/wiki/Format_Films)) (1964–1967) and [Warner Bros.-Seven Arts](/wiki/Warner_Bros.-Seven_Arts) (1967–1969) after Warner Bros. shut down their animation studio. The shorts from this era can be identified by their different title sequence, featuring stylized [limited animation](/wiki/Limited_animation) and graphics on a black background and a new arrangement, by [William Lava](/wiki/William_Lava), of "[The Merry-Go-Round Broke Down](/wiki/The_Merry-Go-Round_Broke_Down)" which had first been used in the 1963 experimental short "[Now Hear This](/wiki/Now_Hear_This_(film))" directed by Chuck Jones.

In 1967, Warner Bros.-Seven Arts commissioned an animation studio in [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea) to redraw 79 black-and-white Looney Tunes produced from 1935 to 1943 in color which were syndicated to TV stations from the late 1960s to the early 1990s.

The original *Looney Tunes* theatrical series ran from 1930's *Sinkin' in the Bathtub* to 1969's *[Injun Trouble](/wiki/Injun_Trouble_(1969_film))* by [Robert McKimson](/wiki/Robert_McKimson).

A [Cool Cat](/wiki/Cool_Cat_(Looney_Tunes)) cartoon called "Stage Cat" was planned, about Cool Cat being in a stage production, but it was cancelled when Warner Bros.-Seven Arts shut down.

### 1970–1999: Syndication and return to television and film

Looney Tunes franchise logo used from 1985 to 2024, based on the wordmark used in the original shorts from 1939 to 1964

The *Looney Tunes* series' popularity was further strengthened when it began airing on network and [syndicated television](/wiki/Syndicated_television) in the 1950s under various titles and formats. The *Looney Tunes* shorts were broadcast with edits to remove scenes of violence (particularly suicidal gags and scenes of characters performing dangerous stunts that impressionable viewers could easily imitate), stereotypes, and alcohol consumption.

Production of theatrical animated shorts was dormant from 1969 until 1979, when new shorts were made to introduce the *Looney Tunes* to a new generation of audiences. [New shorts](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_and_Merrie_Melodies_filmography_(1970%E2%80%93present)) have been produced and released sporadically for theaters since then, though usually as promotional tie-ins with various family movies produced by Warner Bros. While many have been released in limited releases theatrically for Academy Award consideration, only a few have gained theatrical releases with movies.

In the 1970s through the early 1990s, several feature-film compilations and [television specials](/wiki/Television_special) were produced, mostly centering on Bugs Bunny and/or Daffy Duck, with a mixture of new and old footage. These releases include *[The Bugs Bunny/Road Runner Movie](/wiki/The_Bugs_Bunny/Road_Runner_Movie)* (1979), *[The Looney Looney Looney Bugs Bunny Movie](/wiki/The_Looney_Looney_Looney_Bugs_Bunny_Movie)* (1981), *[Bugs Bunny's 3rd Movie: 1001 Rabbit Tales](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny%27s_3rd_Movie:_1001_Rabbit_Tales)* (1982), *[Daffy Duck's Fantastic Island](/wiki/Daffy_Duck%27s_Fantastic_Island)* (1983), and *[Daffy Duck's Quackbusters](/wiki/Daffy_Duck%27s_Quackbusters)* (1988).

In 1976, the *Looney Tunes* characters made their way into the amusement park business when they became the mascots for Marriott's two Great America theme parks in Gurnee, Illinois, and Santa Clara, California. After the [Gurnee park](/wiki/Six_Flags_Great_America) was sold to [Six Flags](/wiki/Six_Flags_(1961%E2%80%932024)) in 1984, they also claimed the rights to use the characters at the other Six Flags parks, which continues to the present. (Warner Bros. parent company Time Warner would own the Six Flags chain in whole or part for most of the 1990s.)

Between 1985 and 1992, [AMC Theatres](/wiki/AMC_Theatres) showed selected late 1950s/early 1960s-era Warner Bros. cartoons before several films, most of them comedies and family-oriented movies.

In 1988, several *Looney Tunes* characters appeared in cameo roles in the [Disney](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company) film *[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit)*. The more significant cameos featured Bugs, Daffy, Porky, Tweety, and Yosemite Sam. It is the only time in which *Looney Tunes* characters have shared screen time with their rivals at Disney (producers of the film)—particularly in the scenes where Bugs and [Mickey Mouse](/wiki/Mickey_Mouse) are [skydiving](/wiki/Skydiving), and when Daffy and [Donald Duck](/wiki/Donald_Duck) are performing their "Dueling Pianos" sequence.

On July 10, 1989, after a battle with heart problems, [Mel Blanc](/wiki/Mel_Blanc) died at the [Cedars-Sinai Medical Center](/wiki/Cedars-Sinai_Medical_Center) of [cardiovascular disease](/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease). A picture depicting the *Looney Tunes* characters entitled "Speechless" was released shortly after his death.

Viacom-owned [Nickelodeon](/wiki/Nickelodeon) aired *Looney Tunes* cartoons in a show called *Looney Tunes on Nickelodeon* between 1988 and 1999. Initially, the Nickelodeon package included cartoons that were typically omitted from the higher-profile Saturday morning network and syndicated weekday packages, including black-and-white Bosko cartoons that had not aired in many years and cartoons from the late DePatie–Freling and Seven Arts eras. In January 1999, it was reported that the cartoons shown on Nickelodeon would move to [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network) in the fall of that year. To date, *Looney Tunes on Nickelodeon* is the longest-airing [animated series](/wiki/Animated_series) on the network that was not a [Nicktoon](/wiki/Nicktoons).

In 1991, the Looney Tunes characters made their appearance at [Warner Bros. Movie World](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Movie_World) on the [Gold Coast](/wiki/Gold_Coast,_Queensland) in [Australia](/wiki/Australia). It marked the launch of "Looney Tunes Land," the park's inaugural themed area.

In 1996, *[Space Jam](/wiki/Space_Jam)*, a [live-action animated film](/wiki/Live-action_animated_film), was released to theaters starring Bugs Bunny and basketball player [Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan). Despite a mixed critical reception, the film was a major box-office success, grossing nearly $100 million in the U.S. alone, almost becoming the first non-Disney animated film to achieve that feat. For a two-year period, it was the highest grossing non-Disney animated film ever. The film also introduced the character [Lola Bunny](/wiki/Lola_Bunny), who subsequently became another recurring member of the *Looney Tunes* cast, usually as a love interest for Bugs.

In 1997, Bugs Bunny was featured on a U.S. 32 cent postage stamp, the first of five *Looney Tunes* themed stamps to be issued.

The *Looney Tunes* also achieved success in the area of television during this era, with appearances in several originally produced series, including *[Taz-Mania](/wiki/Taz-Mania)* (1991, starring Taz) and *[The Sylvester & Tweety Mysteries](/wiki/The_Sylvester_%26_Tweety_Mysteries)* (1995, starring Sylvester, Tweety, and Granny). The gang also made frequent cameos in the 1990 spinoff series *[Tiny Toon Adventures](/wiki/Tiny_Toon_Adventures)*, from executive producer [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg), where they played teachers and mentors to a younger generation of cartoon characters ([Plucky Duck](/wiki/Plucky_Duck), [Hamton J. Pig](/wiki/Hamton_J._Pig), [Babs and Buster Bunny](/wiki/Babs_and_Buster_Bunny), etc.), plus occasional cameos in the later Warner Bros. shows such as *[Animaniacs](/wiki/Animaniacs)* (also from Spielberg) and *[Histeria!](/wiki/Histeria!)*. [Traditional cel animation](/wiki/Traditional_cel_animation) was used to animate the characters for *Looney Tunes'* cartoons until 1999 when it was replaced with [digital ink and paint animation](/wiki/Digital_ink_and_paint_animation).

In 1979, *[Bugs Bunny's Christmas Carol](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny%27s_Christmas_Carol)* premiered. After *[The Chocolate Chase](/wiki/The_Chocolate_Chase)*, there would not be another short released for seven years. In 1990, it was made so there would be about one short per year until 1998. In 2003, there would be seven shorts produced to promote *[Looney Tunes: Back in Action](/wiki/Looney_Tunes:_Back_in_Action)*. The first of these to be released was *[Whizzard of Ow](/wiki/Whizzard_of_Ow)*, which appeared on a DVD release of *Back in Action* that was sold exclusively at [Wal-Mart](/wiki/Walmart) stores. Only about half of the shorts were shown in theaters; the rest would not be made available until 2004, when all seven shorts were included on the general home video release of the film. In 2010, five computer-animated shorts would be released and directed by [Matthew O'Callaghan](/wiki/Matthew_O%27Callaghan), who would also direct another short, *[Flash in the Pain](/wiki/Flash_in_the_Pain)*, in 2014.

### 2000–2014: Network exploration

In March 2000, it was revealed that the entire *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* library would be exclusive to Cartoon Network, starting with the fall of that year. *Looney Tunes* shorts were still airing on Disney's [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) as part of *[The Bugs Bunny and Tweety Show](/wiki/The_Bugs_Bunny_Show)* at the time and the decision led to the show's cancellation. This decision would remain in effect for over 20 years until [MeTV](/wiki/MeTV) began airing the classic Warner Bros. cartoons (along with MGM and Paramount's library) in January 2021. In 2003, another feature film was released, this time in an attempt to recapture the spirit of the original shorts: the live-action/animated *[Looney Tunes: Back in Action](/wiki/Looney_Tunes:_Back_in_Action)*. Although the film was not financially successful, it was met with mixed-to-positive reviews from film critics and has been argued by animation historians and fans as the finest original feature-length appearance of the cartoon characters. In 2006, [Warner Home Video](/wiki/Warner_Home_Video) released a new and Christmas-themed *Looney Tunes* direct-to-video film called *[Bah, Humduck! A Looney Tunes Christmas](/wiki/Bah,_Humduck!_A_Looney_Tunes_Christmas)*, a parody of [Charles Dickens](/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' *[A Christmas Carol](/wiki/A_Christmas_Carol)*. Other *Looney Tunes* TV series made during this time were *[Baby Looney Tunes](/wiki/Baby_Looney_Tunes)* (2001–2006), *[Duck Dodgers](/wiki/Duck_Dodgers_(TV_series))* (2003–2005) and *[Loonatics Unleashed](/wiki/Loonatics_Unleashed)* (2005–2007).

On October 22, 2007, *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* cartoons became available for the first time in [High-definition](/wiki/High-definition_television) via [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft)'s [Xbox Live](/wiki/Xbox_Live) service, including some in Spanish. From February 29 – May 18, 2008, many *Looney Tunes* artifacts, including original animation cels and concept drawings, were on display at the [Butler Institute of American Art](/wiki/Butler_Institute_of_American_Art) in [Youngstown, Ohio](/wiki/Youngstown,_Ohio), just off the campus of [Youngstown State University](/wiki/Youngstown_State_University), near where the Warners lived early in life.

At the 2009 Cartoon Network [upfront](/wiki/Upfront_(advertising)), *[The Looney Tunes Show](/wiki/The_Looney_Tunes_Show)* was announced. After several delays, the series premiered on May 3, 2011. Produced by [Warner Bros. Animation](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Animation), the series centers on Bugs and Daffy as they leave the woods and move to the suburbs with "colorful neighbors" including Sylvester, Tweety, Granny, Yosemite Sam, etc. The series introduced the character [Tina Russo](/wiki/List_of_Looney_Tunes_and_Merrie_Melodies_characters#Melissa_Duck), a duck who becomes Daffy's girlfriend. The show also features 2-minute music videos titled respectfully "Merrie Melodies" (as a tribute to the *Looney Tunes* sister shorts) which features the characters singing original songs, as well as CGI animated shorts starring Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner (which were removed after the first season). The series was cancelled after its second season.

Also, Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner returned to the big screen in a series of 3-D shorts that preceded select Warner Bros. films. There were six in the works that began with the first short, *[Coyote Falls](/wiki/Coyote_Falls)*, that preceded the film *[Cats & Dogs: The Revenge of Kitty Galore](/wiki/Cats_%26_Dogs:_The_Revenge_of_Kitty_Galore)*, which was released on July 30, 2010. On September 24, 2010, *[Fur of Flying](/wiki/Fur_of_Flying)* preceded the film, *[Legend of the Guardians: The Owls of Ga'Hoole](/wiki/Legend_of_the_Guardians:_The_Owls_of_Ga%27Hoole)*, and on December 17, 2010, *[Rabid Rider](/wiki/Rabid_Rider)* preceded the film, *[Yogi Bear](/wiki/Yogi_Bear_(film))*. On June 8, 2011, Warner Bros. Animation announced that there would be more *Looney Tunes* 3-D theatrical shorts; the first titled *[Daffy's Rhapsody](/wiki/Daffy%27s_Rhapsody)* with Daffy Duck and Elmer Fudd, the next being *[I Tawt I Taw a Puddy Tat](/wiki/I_Tawt_I_Taw_a_Puddy_Tat)* with Sylvester, Tweety, and Granny. *Daffy's Rhapsody* was to precede the film *[Happy Feet Two](/wiki/Happy_Feet_Two)*, until the studio decided to premiere *I Tawt I Taw a Puddy Tat* instead. *Daffy's Rhapsody* instead premiered in 2012, preceding *[Journey 2: The Mysterious Island](/wiki/Journey_2:_The_Mysterious_Island)*. All five shorts were directed by Matthew O'Callaghan.

In 2012, several announcements were made about a *Looney Tunes* reboot film titled *Acme*, in development. Former *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* cast member [Jenny Slate](/wiki/Jenny_Slate) was said to be on board as writer for the new film. Jeffrey Clifford, *[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter_(film_series))* producer [David Heyman](/wiki/David_Heyman), and *[Dark Shadows](/wiki/Dark_Shadows_(film))* writers David Katzenberg and [Seth Grahame-Smith](/wiki/Seth_Grahame-Smith) were slated to produce the film. On August 27, 2014, writers [Ashley Miller](/wiki/Ashley_Miller_(screenwriter)) and [Zack Stentz](/wiki/Zack_Stentz) were hired to script the film, directors [Glenn Ficarra](/wiki/Glenn_Ficarra) and John Requa were in talks to direct the film, while actor [Steve Carell](/wiki/Steve_Carell) was rumored to be starring in a lead role. By October 2014, the project was no longer in the works.

### 2015–2022: Revival

At the 2014 Cartoon Network [upfront](/wiki/Upfront_(advertising)), another series titled *[Wabbit: A Looney Tunes Production](/wiki/New_Looney_Tunes)* (later *New Looney Tunes*) was announced. Starring Bugs Bunny, the series premiered on both Cartoon Network and its sister channel [Boomerang](/wiki/Boomerang_(TV_network)) in late 2015. The series had an unusually slow rollout, with the series being moved to the Boomerang streaming service in 2017, and was eventually cancelled on January 30, 2020.

On June 11, 2018, another series, titled *[Looney Tunes Cartoons](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Cartoons)*, was announced by Warner Bros. Animation. It premiered on May 27, 2020, on the streaming service [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max). The series features "1,000 minutes of new one-to-six minute cartoons featuring the brand's marquee characters", voiced by their current voice actors in "simple, gag-driven and visually vibrant stories" that are rendered by multiple artists employing "a visual style that will resonate with fans", most noticeably having a style reminiscent of the styles of [Tex Avery](/wiki/Tex_Avery), [Bob Clampett](/wiki/Bob_Clampett), [Chuck Jones](/wiki/Chuck_Jones), [Friz Freleng](/wiki/Friz_Freleng) and [Robert McKimson](/wiki/Robert_McKimson). According to co-executive producer [Peter Browngardt](/wiki/Peter_Browngardt), "We're not doing guns, but we can do cartoony violence — TNT, the [Acme](/wiki/Acme_Corporation) stuff. All that was kind of grandfathered in." [Sam Register](/wiki/Sam_Register), president of Warner Bros. Animation also served as a co-executive producer for the series. The final season was released on July 27, 2023, and a bonus episode was released on June 13, 2024.

On February 11, 2021, it was announced two new series were in the works: *[Bugs Bunny Builders](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny_Builders)* and *Tweety Mysteries*. *Bugs Bunny Builders* began airing on Cartoon Network as part of [Cartoonito](/wiki/Cartoonito) and HBO Max on July 25, 2022; *Tweety Mysteries* would also air on [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network). *Bugs Bunny Builders* is aimed towards preschoolers; while *Tweety Mysteries* would have been a live-action/animated hybrid. However, the latter was scrapped for unknown reasons.

A sequel to *Space Jam* titled *[Space Jam: A New Legacy](/wiki/Space_Jam:_A_New_Legacy)*, starring basketball player [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James), was released in theaters and HBO Max on July 16, 2021, after a [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) special screening on July 12, 2021. It is a film with a story of LeBron James' second son, Dom (Cedric Joe), who gets kidnapped by an evil [AI](/wiki/AI) named Al. G Rhythm ([Don Cheadle](/wiki/Don_Cheadle)), into the Warner Bros. server-verse. LeBron then assembles the Tune Squad to play against the algorithm and get his son back. It received generally negative reviews and underperformed at the box office.

### 2022–present: Warner Bros. Discovery ownership

A reboot of *Tiny Toon Adventures* titled *[Tiny Toons Looniversity](/wiki/Tiny_Toons_Looniversity)* premiered on September 8, 2023, on [Max](/wiki/Max_(streaming_service)) and then aired the following day on Cartoon Network. The Looney Tunes characters reprise their roles as the professors at Acme Looniversity in this series.

In September 2021, it was reported that a film based on the *Looney Tunes Cartoons*, titled *[The Day the Earth Blew Up](/wiki/The_Day_the_Earth_Blew_Up)* and starring [Daffy Duck](/wiki/Daffy_Duck) and [Porky Pig](/wiki/Porky_Pig), was announced for release on HBO Max and Cartoon Network. However, it was reported in August 2022 that the film would instead be shopped around to other streaming services. In October 2023, it was announced the film would instead be released in theaters, becoming the first animated non-compilation feature film in the franchise to do so. In early August 2024, it was announced that [Ketchup Entertainment](/wiki/Ketchup_Entertainment) acquired the North American theatrical distribution rights to the film. The film was given a one-week limited release in Los Angeles on December 13, 2024, before a wide release in theaters in the United States on March 14, 2025.

On December 31, 2022, 256 classic *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* shorts were removed from HBO Max, including *[What's Opera, Doc?](/wiki/What%27s_Opera,_Doc%3F)* and *[Duck Amuck](/wiki/Duck_Amuck)*, though many were later re-added in March 2024 when the shorts on the service were rotated.

In August 2024, it was reported that Warner Bros. was planning to relaunch the *Looney Tunes* theatrical film series in 2028. In October 2024, it was reported that Warner Bros. was working on a "super secret *Looney Tunes* project" with directors Todd Wilderman and Hamish Grieve.

On March 16, 2025, two days after *The Day the Earth Blew Up* expanded to a wide release, all of the classic *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* shorts were removed from the Max streaming service. Three days later, it was reported that Warner Bros. was in talks with Ketchup, who had distributed *The Day the Earth Blew Up* in the United States, to sell the distribution rights to the shelved movie *[Coyote vs. Acme](/wiki/Coyote_vs._Acme)*. On March 31, the negotiations proved successful. The film is currently scheduled to be released in theaters in the United States on August 28, 2026.

At [CinemaCon](/wiki/CinemaCon) 2025, [Warner Bros. Pictures Animation](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures_Animation) confirmed development on a new *Looney Tunes* animated feature film.

On August 15, 2025, the classic *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* shorts began streaming on the ad-supported free streaming service [Tubi](/wiki/Tubi).

On January 26, 2026, it was announced that [Turner Classic Movies](/wiki/Turner_Classic_Movies) would air the series, with each short preceding feature films as a homage to its origins.

### Home media

In the 1980s, the shorts received [VHS](/wiki/VHS) releases, with the pre-August 1948 shorts released by [MGM/UA Home Video](/wiki/MGM_Home_Entertainment) and the post-July 1948 shorts released by [Warner Home Video](/wiki/Warner_Home_Video). In 2003, Warner Home Video began releasing select shorts on [DVD](/wiki/DVD-Video), aimed at collectors, in four-disc sets known as the *[Looney Tunes Golden Collection](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection)* starting with [Volume 1](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection:_Volume_1). This continued until 2008, when [the final volume of the Golden Collection](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection:_Volume_6) was released. Then, from 2010 until 2013, the company released the *[Looney Tunes Super Stars](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Super_Stars)* DVDs. There have been numerous complaints regarding the *Super Stars* releases, however (particularly the first two), having the post-1953 shorts in a 16:9 widescreen format. The last DVD in the *Super Stars* series was *Sylvester and Hippety Hopper: Marsupial Mayhem*, released on April 23, 2013. 2010 and 2011 saw the releases of *[The Essential Bugs Bunny](/wiki/The_Essential_Bugs_Bunny)* and *[The Essential Daffy Duck](/wiki/The_Essential_Daffy_Duck)* DVDs. In 2011, the shorts were released on [Blu-ray Disc](/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc) for the first time with the *Looney Tunes Platinum Collection* series. On September 19, 2017, Warner Home Video's [Warner Archive Collection](/wiki/Warner_Archive_Collection) released the five-disc *[Porky Pig 101](/wiki/Porky_Pig_101)* DVD set.

In 2023, it was announced that a new line of single-disc Blu-ray sets called the *[Looney Tunes Collector's Choice](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Collector%27s_Choice)* would release beginning on May 30. The following shorts known to be on this set are all newly remastered from the original negatives. It is a successor to the *Looney Tunes Platinum Collection* DVD/Blu-ray sets. Four Blu-ray sets were released until November 26, 2024. A two-disc successor to *Collector's Choice*, titled *[Looney Tunes Collector's Vault](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Collector%27s_Vault)*, began releasing on June 17, 2025.

## Licensing and ownership

In 1933, [Harman and Ising](/wiki/Harman_and_Ising) left, taking the rights to [Bosko](/wiki/Bosko) with them. However, Warner Bros. retained the rights to the cartoons and the *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* brand names, leaving their former producer Leon Schlesinger to start his own animation studio to continue the *Looney Tunes* series. With their retained Bosko rights, Harman and Ising began making [cartoons](/wiki/Happy_Harmonies) at [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) in 1934 until they were fired in 1937 due to a lack of success. MGM proceeded to form [their own studio](/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer_cartoon_studio) to create its own cartoons. Time Warner eventually acquired the Bosko characters from Harman and Ising's estates. Meanwhile, the Schlesinger studio continued to make popular cartoons until 1944 when Schlesinger sold his studio to Warner Bros. Since then, Warner Bros. has owned all rights to all post-1933 characters created by Leon Schlesinger Productions and Warner Bros. Cartoons, even after the rights to individual cartoons were placed in other hands.

In February 1955, Warner Bros. sold the television distribution rights to 191 of its cartoons (which included the black-and-white *Looney Tunes* and the black-and-white *Merrie Melodies* made after Harman and Ising left) to Guild Films. The deal was done through Sunset Productions, an entity owned by Warner Bros. that became "Warner Bros. TV Commercial and Industrial Films" in April 1957 and was merged into Warner Bros. Cartoons in January 1960. The cartoons were distributed by Guild Films until it went bankrupt on March 6, 1961, after which the TV rights would return to WB and then be acquired by [Seven Arts Associated](/wiki/Seven_Arts_Productions). Seven Arts bought WB in 1967, resulting in the TV distribution rights to the 191 black-and-white cartoons returning to WB again.

In 1956, [Associated Artists Productions](/wiki/Associated_Artists_Productions) (a.a.p.) acquired the copyrights to most of Warner Bros.' pre-1950 library, including all *Merrie Melodies* (except for those sold to Guild and *[Lady, Play Your Mandolin!](/wiki/Lady,_Play_Your_Mandolin!)*) and color *Looney Tunes* shorts that were released prior to August 1948, while Warner retained character merchandising rights. Unlike the previous TV package, this package had the Warner titles kept intact and an "Associated Artists Productions presents" title inserted at the head of each reel (as a result, each post-1936 *Merrie Melodies* cartoon and pre-1956 [Blue Ribbon](/wiki/List_of_Warner_Bros._cartoons_with_Blue_Ribbon_reissues) cartoon had the song "[Merrily We Roll Along](/wiki/Merrily_We_Roll_Along_(song))" playing twice). Two years later, [United Artists](/wiki/United_Artists) bought a.a.p. (which had also bought Paramount's *[Popeye](/wiki/Popeye_the_Sailor_(film_series))* films) who merged the company into its television division, [United Artists Television](/wiki/United_Artists_Television). In 1981, UA was sold to [MGM](/wiki/MGM). In 1982, [Warner Communications](/wiki/Warner_Communications) was in talks to buy back rights to the pre-1950 Warner Bros. Pictures library (which consists of the live-action and animation short titles made before 1948) from MGM/UA for $100 million in cash. The deal was called off on July 28 of that year; the negotiations fell apart because of dozens of unresolved points, probably relating to the oldest Warner Bros. films. When in 1986, [Ted Turner](/wiki/Ted_Turner) acquired all of MGM's legacy content up to that point, he also acquired the rights to the a.a.p. library. Turner's company, [Turner Broadcasting System](/wiki/Turner_Broadcasting_System) (whose [Turner Entertainment Co.](/wiki/Turner_Entertainment_Co.) division oversaw the film library), was purchased by Warner Bros.' corporate parent, Time Warner (now [Warner Bros. Discovery](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery)), in 1996. When MGM/UA terminated its distribution deal with Time Warner in 1999, it surrendered its home video rights to the a.a.p. library to Warner Home Video, restoring full rights to the pre-August 1948 cartoons to Warner Bros.

Starting in 1960, the cartoons were repackaged into several different TV programs that remained popular for several decades before being purchased by Turner Broadcasting System. Turner's [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network) reran the cartoons from its launch in 1992 until 2004, again from 2009 until 2017, and making a temporary return in April 2023 to celebrate WB's 100th anniversary. *The Looney Tunes Show* (not to be confused with the 2010s animated series of [the same name](/wiki/The_Looney_Tunes_Show)), an early 2000s anthology produced by Warner Bros. Animation for the network, was broadcast from 2001 to 2004. The show featured shorts from the original *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* theatrical series. As of 2025, classic cartoons continue to air on CN's sister channels [Boomerang](/wiki/Boomerang_(TV_network)) and [Discovery Family](/wiki/Discovery_Family) and are licensed to [Weigel Broadcasting](/wiki/Weigel_Broadcasting)-owned [MeTV](/wiki/MeTV). Starting in 2024, the classic cartoons began airing on MeTV's sister channel [MeTV Toons](/wiki/MeTV_Toons).

### Public domain status

Five dozen *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* shorts from before 1944 have fallen into the [American public domain](/wiki/Public_domain_in_the_United_States) and are thus freely distributed through various unofficial releases. All shorts from 1930 and 1931 and most shorts from 1932 are believed to be in the public domain due to non-renewal. The earliest short with a known copyright renewal is *[Ride Him, Bosko!](/wiki/Ride_Him,_Bosko!)* from 1932.

## Characters

Main article: [List of Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies characters](/wiki/List_of_Looney_Tunes_and_Merrie_Melodies_characters)

The major characters of the original *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* series are [Bugs Bunny](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny), a clever and insouciant rabbit who is portrayed as a [trickster](/wiki/Trickster); [Daffy Duck](/wiki/Daffy_Duck), a black duck who was originally portrayed as a [screwball](/wiki/Screwball_comedy), but later became greedy and egocentric; [Porky Pig](/wiki/Porky_Pig), a stuttering pig who often appears as the [straight man](/wiki/Straight_man) to Daffy, and is the oldest of the franchise's recurring characters; [Sylvester the Cat](/wiki/Sylvester_the_Cat), his prey [Tweety](/wiki/Tweety) (a small canary), and their elderly owner [Granny](/wiki/Granny_(Looney_Tunes)); [Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner](/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner), who routinely engage in high-speed chases in their home in the [Southwest American](/wiki/Southwestern_United_States) desert; [Elmer Fudd](/wiki/Elmer_Fudd), an unintelligent hunter who is Bugs' oldest nemesis; [Yosemite Sam](/wiki/Yosemite_Sam), a hot-tempered cowboy who is another of Bugs' archenemies; [Foghorn Leghorn](/wiki/Foghorn_Leghorn), a rooster who is known for his often excessive ranting; [Marvin the Martian](/wiki/Marvin_the_Martian), an alien commander from the planet [Mars](/wiki/Mars), who aims to conquer the Earth; the [Tasmanian Devil](/wiki/Tasmanian_Devil_(Looney_Tunes)) (often nicknamed "Taz" in later media), a vicious, brutal marsupial with an insatiable appetite; [Pepé Le Pew](/wiki/Pep%C3%A9_Le_Pew), a French skunk who is always looking for love and romance; and [Speedy Gonzales](/wiki/Speedy_Gonzales), the self-proclaimed "fastest mouse in all of Mexico". One additional major character was introduced in post-Golden Age *Looney Tunes* media (starting with *[Space Jam](/wiki/Space_Jam)*): [Lola Bunny](/wiki/Lola_Bunny), a female rabbit who is usually portrayed as Bugs' girlfriend.

## Racial stereotypes and censorship controversies

[![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Hittin%27_The_Trail_For_Hallelujah_Land.webm/250px--Hittin%27_The_Trail_For_Hallelujah_Land.webm.jpg)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/Hittin%27_The_Trail_For_Hallelujah_Land.webm)

*[Hittin' the Trail for Hallelujah Land](/wiki/Hittin%27_the_Trail_for_Hallelujah_Land)*

[![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Friz_Freleng_-_Merrie_Melodies_-_Jungle_Jitters_%281938%29.webm/250px-seek%3D299-Friz_Freleng_-_Merrie_Melodies_-_Jungle_Jitters_%281938%29.webm.jpg)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/cd/Friz_Freleng_-_Merrie_Melodies_-_Jungle_Jitters_%281938%29.webm/Friz_Freleng_-_Merrie_Melodies_-_Jungle_Jitters_%281938%29.webm.480p.vp9.webm)

*[Jungle Jitters](/wiki/Jungle_Jitters)*

Due to content considered offensive, [stereotyped](/wiki/Stereotype) or insensitive, in 1968 [United Artists](/wiki/United_Artists), then the owners of the pre-August 1948 color cartoon library, removed the "[Censored Eleven](/wiki/Censored_Eleven)" episodes of *Looney Tunes* and *Merrie Melodies* cartoons from broadcast or distribution. Depictions included those of [African Americans](/wiki/African_Americans) (as in *[Coal Black and de Sebben Dwarfs](/wiki/Coal_Black_and_de_Sebben_Dwarfs)* and *[Jungle Jitters](/wiki/Jungle_Jitters)*), [Native Americans](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), [Japanese people](/wiki/Japanese_people) (especially during WWII, as in *[Tokio Jokio](/wiki/Tokio_Jokio)* and *[Bugs Bunny Nips the Nips](/wiki/Bugs_Bunny_Nips_the_Nips)*), [Germans](/wiki/Germans), [Italians](/wiki/Italians), [White Southerners](/wiki/White_Southerners), and [Mexicans](/wiki/Mexicans).

In 1999, [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network) ceased broadcast of all of Speedy Gonzales' cartoons, due to concerns about stereotyping of Mexicans. Many [Latinos](/wiki/Latinos) protested that they were not offended, and expressed fondness for Speedy; the character's shorts were made available for broadcast on CN again in 2002.

Many Warner Bros. cartoons contain fleeting or sometimes extended gags that make reference to racial or ethnic stereotypes, or use [ethnic humor](/wiki/Ethnic_humor). The release of the *[Looney Tunes Golden Collection: Volume 3](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection:_Volume_3)* includes a disclaimer at the beginning of each DVD in the volume given by [Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg). She explains that the cartoons are products of their time and contain racial and ethnic stereotypes that "were wrong then and they are wrong today", but the cartoons are presented on the DVD uncut and uncensored because "editing them would be the same as denying that the stereotypes existed." A similarly phrased written disclaimer is shown at the beginning of each DVD in the *[Looney Tunes Golden Collection: Volume 4](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection:_Volume_4)*, *[Volume 5](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection:_Volume_5)*, and *[Volume 6](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Golden_Collection:_Volume_6)* sets, as well as the Daffy Duck, Foghorn Leghorn, and Porky Pig *[Looney Tunes Super Stars](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Super_Stars)* sets, the *[Looney Tunes Platinum Collection: Volume 2](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Platinum_Collection:_Volume_2)* and *[Volume 3](/wiki/Looney_Tunes_Platinum_Collection:_Volume_3)* sets, the *[Porky Pig 101](/wiki/Porky_Pig_101)* set, and the [Warner Bros. Home Entertainment Academy Awards Animation Collection](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Home_Entertainment_Academy_Awards_Animation_Collection).

### Comic books

*Looney Tunes* comic books were published beginning in 1941 by [Dell Comics](/wiki/Dell_Comics) under license. These comics, like many published by Dell, were produced in partnership with [Western Publishing](/wiki/Western_Publishing). After Dell and Western ended their partnership in 1962, Western continued the series under their [Gold Key Comics](/wiki/Gold_Key_Comics) and Whitman imprints through 1984. Beginning in 1990, [DC Comics](/wiki/DC_Comics), which is owned by Warner Bros., has published *Looney Tunes* comics.

#### Dell Comics (1941–1962)

- *Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies Comics* #1–165 (1941–1955)/*Looney Tunes* #166–246 (1955–1962)

- *Tweety and Sylvester* #1–37 (1952–1962)

### Video games

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of Looney Tunes video games](/wiki/List_of_Looney_Tunes_video_games).

Video games based on *Looney Tunes* characters began in 1979 with the *Road Runner* pinball machine. More titles would continue to be released as video game hardware evolved throughout the 1980s, 1990s, 2000s, and 2010s. Prominent characters who have received multiple video games include Bugs Bunny, Daffy Duck, the Tasmanian Devil, Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner, Speedy Gonzales, and Sylvester and Tweety.

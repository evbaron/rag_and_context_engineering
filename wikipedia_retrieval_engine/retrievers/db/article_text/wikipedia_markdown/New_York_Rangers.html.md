The **New York Rangers** are a professional [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) team based in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City). The Rangers compete in the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) (NHL) as a member of the [Metropolitan Division](/wiki/Metropolitan_Division) in the [Eastern Conference](/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(NHL)). The team plays its home games at [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden), an arena they share with the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks) of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA). They are one of three NHL franchises located in the [New York metropolitan area](/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area); the others being the [New Jersey Devils](/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) and [New York Islanders](/wiki/New_York_Islanders).

Founded in 1926 by [Tex Rickard](/wiki/Tex_Rickard), the Rangers are one of the [Original Six](/wiki/Original_Six) teams that competed in the NHL before its [1967 expansion](/wiki/1967_NHL_expansion), along with the [Boston Bruins](/wiki/Boston_Bruins), [Chicago Blackhawks](/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks), [Detroit Red Wings](/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings), [Montreal Canadiens](/wiki/Montreal_Canadiens) and [Toronto Maple Leafs](/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs). The team attained success early on under the guidance of [Lester Patrick](/wiki/Lester_Patrick), who coached a team containing [Frank Boucher](/wiki/Frank_Boucher), [Murray Murdoch](/wiki/Murray_Murdoch), and [Bun](/wiki/Bun_Cook) and [Bill Cook](/wiki/Bill_Cook) to win the [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup) in only their second season. In 1928, they became the first NHL franchise in the United States to win the trophy, and are still the fastest true expansion team in NHL history to do so. The team won two more Stanley Cups in 1933 and 1940.

Following this initial grace period, the franchise struggled between the 1940s and 1960s, where playoff appearances and successes were infrequent. The team enjoyed a mini-renaissance in the 1970s, where they made the [Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/Stanley_Cup_Final) twice, losing to the Bruins in [1972](/wiki/1972_Stanley_Cup_Final) and the Canadiens in [1979](/wiki/1979_Stanley_Cup_Final). The Rangers subsequently embraced a rebuild for much of the 1980s and early 1990s, which eventually paid dividends in [1994](/wiki/1994_Stanley_Cup_Final), where the team, led by [Mark Messier](/wiki/Mark_Messier), [Brian Leetch](/wiki/Brian_Leetch), [Adam Graves](/wiki/Adam_Graves), and [Mike Richter](/wiki/Mike_Richter), captured their fourth Stanley Cup.

The team was unable to duplicate that success in the years that followed, and entered into another period of mediocrity, enduring a franchise-record seven-year postseason drought from 1998 to 2005. After the arrival of goaltender [Henrik Lundqvist](/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist) in 2006, the Rangers thrived, missing the playoffs just once between then and 2017. The Rangers returned to the Stanley Cup Final in [2014](/wiki/2014_Stanley_Cup_Final), falling to the [Los Angeles Kings](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) in five games. Between 2012 and 2024, they reached the Eastern Conference finals five times.

## History

Main article: [History of the New York Rangers](/wiki/History_of_the_New_York_Rangers)

[Tex Rickard](/wiki/Tex_Rickard), president of [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden), was awarded the Rangers in 1926.

### Early years (1926–1942)

[George Lewis "Tex" Rickard](/wiki/Tex_Rickard), president of [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_(1925)), was awarded an NHL franchise for the [1926–27 season](/wiki/1926%E2%80%9327_NHL_season) to compete with the [New York Americans](/wiki/New_York_Americans), who had begun play at the Garden the previous season. The Americans' early success in their inaugural season exceeded expectations, leading Rickard to pursue a second team for the Garden despite promising the Americans that they were going to be the only ice hockey team to play there. The team was originally incorporated under the name "New York Giants Professional Hockey Club" during a league meeting with NHL president [Frank Calder](/wiki/Frank_Calder) on April 17, 1926, but during the meeting the name was then changed to "New York Rangers Hockey Club." The franchise's nickname is attributed to sports editor of the *[New York Herald Tribune](/wiki/New_York_Herald_Tribune)*, George Haley, who referred to the new team as "Tex's Rangers" because of Rickard's decision to bring a new NHL team to New York.

Rickard's franchise began play in the 1926–27 season. The first team crest played on the club's name with a rearing horse sketched in blue carrying a cowboy waving a hockey stick aloft, with the word "TEX'S" in a crescent at the top of the emblem with "RANGERS" below it. Rickard rejected that design and management settled on a crest similar to the Americans, a "shield" shape with "NEW YORK" horizontally across the top of the shield and "RANGERS" written diagonally from the top left to bottom right. The diagonal positioning of "RANGERS" carried over to the front of the solid blue jerseys. Future [Toronto Maple Leafs](/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) owner [Conn Smythe](/wiki/Conn_Smythe) was hired to assemble the team. However, he had a falling-out with Rickard's associate, [John S. Hammond](/wiki/John_S._Hammond), and was fired as manager-coach on the eve of the first season – he was paid a then-hefty $2,500 to leave. Smythe was replaced by [Pacific Coast Hockey Association](/wiki/Pacific_Coast_Hockey_Association) co-founder [Lester Patrick](/wiki/Lester_Patrick). The new team Smythe assembled turned out to be a winner. The Rangers won the [American Division](/wiki/American_Division_(NHL)) title their first year but lost to the [Boston Bruins](/wiki/Boston_Bruins) in the playoffs.

The team's early success led to players becoming minor celebrities and fixtures in New York City's [Roaring Twenties](/wiki/Roaring_Twenties) nightlife. It was during this time, playing at the Garden on 49th Street, blocks away from [Times Square](/wiki/Times_Square), that the Rangers obtained their nickname "The Broadway Blueshirts". On December 13, 1929, the Rangers became the first team in the NHL to travel by plane when they hired the [Curtiss-Wright Corporation](/wiki/Curtiss-Wright_Corporation) to fly them to [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto) for a game against the Maple Leafs, which they lost 7–6.

#### Stanley Cup success (1927–1942)

In only their [second season](/wiki/1927%E2%80%9328_New_York_Rangers_season), they won the [1928 Stanley Cup](/wiki/1928_Stanley_Cup_Final), defeating the [Montreal Maroons](/wiki/Montreal_Maroons) in five games. One of the most memorable stories that emerged from the Cup Final series involved Patrick playing in goal at the age of 44. At the time, teams were not required to dress a backup [goaltender](/wiki/Goaltender). When the Rangers' starting goaltender, [Lorne Chabot](/wiki/Lorne_Chabot), left a game with an eye injury, Maroons head coach [Eddie Gerard](/wiki/Eddie_Gerard) vetoed Patrick's original choice for an emergency replacement, [Alex Connell](/wiki/Alex_Connell) of the [Ottawa Senators](/wiki/Ottawa_Senators_(original)), who was in attendance. An angry Patrick lined up between the pipes for two periods in game two of the Stanley Cup Final, allowing one goal to Maroons center [Nels Stewart](/wiki/Nels_Stewart). [Frank Boucher](/wiki/Frank_Boucher) scored the game-winning goal in overtime for New York.

After a loss to the Bruins in the [1929 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/1929_Stanley_Cup_Final), the Rangers, led by brothers [Bill](/wiki/Bill_Cook) and [Bun Cook](/wiki/Bun_Cook) on the right and left wings, respectively, and Frank Boucher at center, defeated the Maple Leafs in the [1933 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/1933_Stanley_Cup_Final) to win their second Stanley Cup. Lester Patrick stepped down as head coach and was replaced by Frank Boucher.

The Bread Line was the Rangers' first notable [line](/wiki/Line_(ice_hockey)). Consisting of [Bill Cook](/wiki/Bill_Cook), [Bun Cook](/wiki/Bun_Cook) and [Frank Boucher](/wiki/Frank_Boucher), they played together from 1926 to 1937.

In the [1939–40 season](/wiki/1939%E2%80%9340_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers finished the regular season in second place behind Boston. The two teams then met in the first round of the playoffs. The Bruins gained a 2–1 series lead on New York, but the Rangers recovered to win three straight games, defeating the first-place Bruins four games to two. The Rangers' first-round victory advanced them to the Stanley Cup Final, where the Rangers defeated the [Toronto Maple Leafs](/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) in six games. Following the [Brooklyn Americans](/wiki/New_York_Americans#"Brooklyn"_Americans) fold in 1942, and the league reneging on [Red Dutton](/wiki/Red_Dutton)'s promise to have the Americans return, Dutton swore a [curse](/wiki/Curse_of_1940) on the Rangers.

### Original Six era (1942–1967)

The Rangers collapsed by the mid-1940s, losing games by scores as lopsided as 15–0. In [1943–44](/wiki/1943%E2%80%9344_NHL_season), goaltender [Ken McAuley](/wiki/Ken_McAuley) led the league with 39 losses and 310 goals allowed in 50 games played; his 6.24 goals-against average that year remains the worst in NHL history by a goaltender playing at least 25 games in a season. They missed the playoffs for five consecutive seasons before earning the fourth and final playoff spot in [1947–48](/wiki/1947%E2%80%9348_NHL_season), losing in the first round. In December, 1948, Boucher resigned as head coach remaining as general manager, giving control to [Lynn Patrick](/wiki/Lynn_Patrick), the son of Lester Patrick. In the [1950 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/1950_Stanley_Cup_Final), the Rangers were forced to play all of their games, including "home" games, in Toronto, while the [circus](/wiki/Ringling_Bros._and_Barnum_%26_Bailey_Circus) was held at the Garden. They lost to the Detroit Red Wings in overtime in the seventh game of the series.

During this time, Red Wings owner [James E. Norris](/wiki/James_E._Norris) became the largest stockholder in the Garden. However, he did not buy controlling interest in the arena, which would have violated the NHL's rule against one person owning more than one team. Nonetheless, he had enough support on the board to exercise de facto control. The Rangers missed the playoffs 12 of the next 16 years within the remainder of the [Original Six](/wiki/Original_Six) era. Boucher, who had been general manager of the squad, resigned citing depression over the team's inability to be a contender. His successor, [Muzz Patrick](/wiki/Muzz_Patrick), the younger brother of Lynn Patrick, did not fare well either, resigning in 1964 following criticism about management's unwillingness to improve the team by spending money. [Emile Francis](/wiki/Emile_Francis) took over as both coach and general manager. After a five-year absence, the team made the playoffs, aided by goaltender [Eddie Giacomin](/wiki/Eddie_Giacomin) and 37-year-old former [Montreal Canadiens](/wiki/Montreal_Canadiens) right wing [Bernie "Boom Boom" Geoffrion](/wiki/Bernie_Geoffrion), who signed out of retirement in 1966.

### Post-Original Six era (1967–1993)

#### GAG line and championship appearances (1967–1980)

[Jean Ratelle](/wiki/Jean_Ratelle) played with the Rangers from 1960 to 1975.

In 1968, the Rangers moved into the [fourth version of Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden#Current_Garden). During the 1969–70 season, the Rangers acquired veteran goaltender [Terry Sawchuk](/wiki/Terry_Sawchuk), playing his final season with New York.

In [1971–72](/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers reached the [1972 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/1972_Stanley_Cup_Final) despite losing high-scoring center [Jean Ratelle](/wiki/Jean_Ratelle) to injury during the last half of the regular season. The strength of players such as [Brad Park](/wiki/Brad_Park), Jean Ratelle, [Vic Hadfield](/wiki/Vic_Hadfield) and [Rod Gilbert](/wiki/Rod_Gilbert) (the last three constructing the famed "[GAG line](/wiki/GAG_line)", standing for "goal-a-game") carried them through the playoffs. They defeated the defending-champion Canadiens in the first round and the Chicago Black Hawks in the second, but lost to the Bruins in the Cup Final.

The following season in [1972–73](/wiki/1972%E2%80%9373_New_York_Rangers_season), the team made the semifinals against the Black Hawks again, but lost in five games. The Rangers' conference semifinals series against the [Philadelphia Flyers](/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) in the [1974 playoffs](/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_NHL_season#playoffs), which they lost in seven games became the first Original Six club to lose a playoff series to a 1967 expansion team. This series was noted for a game seven fight between [Dale Rolfe](/wiki/Dale_Rolfe) of the Rangers and [Dave Schultz](/wiki/Dave_Schultz_(ice_hockey)) of the Flyers. The Rangers' new [rivals](/wiki/Islanders%E2%80%93Rangers_rivalry), the [New York Islanders](/wiki/New_York_Islanders), who entered the League in [1972–73](/wiki/1972%E2%80%9373_NHL_season) after paying a territorial fee – $4 million – to the Rangers, were their first-round opponents in the [1975 playoffs](/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_NHL_season#playoffs). After splitting the first two games, the Islanders defeated the Rangers 11 seconds into overtime of the deciding game three.

In a blockbuster trade with the [Boston Bruins](/wiki/Boston_Bruins), the Rangers acquired Esposito and [Carol Vadnais](/wiki/Carol_Vadnais) from the Bruins for Park, Ratelle and [Joe Zanussi](/wiki/Joe_Zanussi) in [1975–76](/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_New_York_Rangers_season). On January 8, 1976, [Emile Francis](/wiki/Emile_Francis) was fired after the team got to a bad start. [Ron Stewart](/wiki/Ron_Stewart) who was coaching the team was also fired. [John Ferguson Sr.](/wiki/John_Ferguson_Sr.) took over as both coach and general manager for the remainder of the season. To assist in Ferguson's contention building, the team acquired [Ken Hodge](/wiki/Ken_Hodge) from Bruins in exchange for [Rick Middleton](/wiki/Rick_Middleton). However, the team failed to make a playoff push and Ferguson was fired in 1978. Madison Square Garden's new owner, [Sonny Werblin](/wiki/Sonny_Werblin), hired [Fred Shero](/wiki/Fred_Shero) to take over as head coach and general manager. In the [1979 Stanley Cup playoffs](/wiki/1979_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Rangers defeated the Islanders in the semifinals and advanced to the [1979 Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/1979_Stanley_Cup_Final), losing to the Canadiens.

#### Playoff struggles (1980–1993)

In four consecutive playoffs ([1981](/wiki/1981_Stanley_Cup_playoffs)–[1984](/wiki/1984_Stanley_Cup_playoffs)), the Rangers were eliminated by the rival Islanders, who went on to win the Stanley Cup in [1981](/wiki/1981_Stanley_Cup_Final), [1982](/wiki/1982_Stanley_Cup_Final), and [1983](/wiki/1983_Stanley_Cup_Final).

[Marcel Dionne](/wiki/Marcel_Dionne), acquired by the Rangers in 1987

The Rangers stayed competitive through the 1980s and early 1990s, making the playoffs each year. In the [1986 Stanley Cup playoffs](/wiki/1986_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Rangers, behind the play of rookie goaltender [John Vanbiesbrouck](/wiki/John_Vanbiesbrouck), upended the [Patrick Division](/wiki/Patrick_Division)-winning Flyers in five games followed by a six-game win over the [Washington Capitals](/wiki/Washington_Capitals) in the Patrick Division finals. Montreal disposed of the Rangers in the Wales Conference finals behind a rookie goaltender of their own, [Patrick Roy](/wiki/Patrick_Roy). During the [1986–87 season](/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_New_York_Rangers_season), the team acquired superstar center [Marcel Dionne](/wiki/Marcel_Dionne) after almost 12 years with the [Los Angeles Kings](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings).

Frustration was at its peak when the [1991–92 Rangers](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_New_York_Rangers_season) captured the [Presidents' Trophy](/wiki/Presidents%27_Trophy). They took a 2–1 series lead on the defending champion [Pittsburgh Penguins](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) and then faltered in three-straight (some observers note a [Ron Francis](/wiki/Ron_Francis) slapshot from outside the blue line that eluded goaltender [Mike Richter](/wiki/Mike_Richter) as the series' turning point). The following year, they missed the playoffs and head coach [Roger Neilson](/wiki/Roger_Neilson) was fired and replaced by [Ron Smith](/wiki/Ron_Smith_(ice_hockey,_born_1944)) mid-season. Smith was dismissed at the conclusion of the season.

During this period, the Rangers were owned by [Gulf+Western](/wiki/Gulf%2BWestern), which was renamed to Paramount Communications in 1989.

### Ending the curse (1993–94)

The [1993–94 season](/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_New_York_Rangers_season) was the Rangers' most successful in 54 years. With several transactions up to this point, the Rangers had acquired seven players who had been part of the [Edmonton Oilers](/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers)' Cup-winning teams: Oilers captain (and new Rangers captain) [Mark Messier](/wiki/Mark_Messier), [Adam Graves](/wiki/Adam_Graves), [Kevin Lowe](/wiki/Kevin_Lowe), [Jeff Beukeboom](/wiki/Jeff_Beukeboom), [Esa Tikkanen](/wiki/Esa_Tikkanen), [Craig MacTavish](/wiki/Craig_MacTavish), and [Glenn Anderson](/wiki/Glenn_Anderson). Graves set a team record with 52 goals, breaking the prior record of 50 held by [Vic Hadfield](/wiki/Vic_Hadfield). The Rangers clinched the Presidents' Trophy by finishing with the best record in the NHL at 52–24–8, setting a franchise record with 112 points earned. In the playoffs the Rangers made it past the first two rounds of the playoffs, sweeping the New York Islanders, and then defeating the Washington Capitals in five games. However, in the conference finals against the third-seeded [New Jersey Devils](/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils), with the Rangers down 3–2 in the series and heading back to the [Meadowlands](/wiki/Meadowlands_Arena) for the sixth game, Messier guaranteed a win to the press. During the game he scored a [hat trick](/wiki/Hat_trick#Hockey) in the third period to lead the Rangers to a 4–2 win, setting up a seventh game back at Madison Square Garden. The Rangers won game seven, 2–1, when [Stephane Matteau](/wiki/St%C3%A9phane_Matteau) scored a goal in double overtime, leading the team to the Cup Final for the first time since [1979](/wiki/1979_Stanley_Cup_Final). In the Final, the Rangers led the series 3–1, but the Canucks won the next two games to force a seventh game. In the seventh game, the Rangers took a 2–0 first period lead, with Messier scoring later to put the Rangers up 3–1, the eventual Cup-winning goal they won 3–2. Defenceman [Brian Leetch](/wiki/Brian_Leetch) became the first American-born player to win the [Conn Smythe Trophy](/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy) as playoff MVP, while [Alexander Karpovtsev](/wiki/Alexander_Karpovtsev), [Alexei Kovalev](/wiki/Alexei_Kovalev), [Sergei Nemchinov](/wiki/Sergei_Nemchinov) and [Sergei Zubov](/wiki/Sergei_Zubov) became the first Russians to have their names engraved on the Cup.

### Expensive acquisitions and postseason drought (1994–2005)

Despite coaching the Rangers to a regular season first-place finish and the Stanley Cup victory, head coach Mike Keenan left after a dispute with general manager [Neil Smith](/wiki/Neil_Smith_(ice_hockey)). Paramount Communications, the owners of the Rangers, were acquired by Viacom who then sold the team to [ITT Corporation](/wiki/ITT_Corporation) and [Cablevision](/wiki/Cablevision) in August 1994. During the [lockout](/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_NHL_lockout)-shortened [1994–95 season](/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_NHL_season), the Rangers won their first-round series with the [Quebec Nordiques](/wiki/Quebec_Nordiques), but lost in the second round of the playoffs to the Philadelphia Flyers in four games with succeeding head coach [Colin Campbell](/wiki/Colin_Campbell_(ice_hockey_executive)). Neil Smith orchestrated a deal that sent Sergei Zubov and center [Petr Nedved](/wiki/Petr_Nedv%C4%9Bd) to Pittsburgh in exchange for defenseman [Ulf Samuelsson](/wiki/Ulf_Samuelsson) and left-winger [Luc Robitaille](/wiki/Luc_Robitaille) in the summer of 1995. The Rangers defeated the Canadiens in six games in the [1996 playoffs](/wiki/1996_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), but lost their second-round series to the Penguins in five games.

The Rangers acquired [Wayne Gretzky](/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky) as a free agent in the 1996 offseason.

The Rangers then acquired [Wayne Gretzky](/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky) in 1996. With Gretzky, the team went to the [conference finals](/wiki/1997_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) in 1997, where they lost 4–1 to the Flyers. During the season, ITT sold their stake of the Rangers to Cablevision. Mark Messier, a former Oiler teammate of Gretzky's, left in the summer of 1997 and the team failed in a bid to replace him with [Colorado Avalanche](/wiki/Colorado_Avalanche) superstar [Joe Sakic](/wiki/Joe_Sakic). Robitaille was also traded during the summer for [Kevin Stevens](/wiki/Kevin_Stevens). The Rangers missed the playoffs for seven consecutive seasons, finishing no higher than fourth in their division. Gretzky retired at the end of the [1998–99 season](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_NHL_season).

In March 2000, Smith was fired along with head coach [John Muckler](/wiki/John_Muckler), and that summer, Cablevision CEO and owner [James Dolan](/wiki/James_L._Dolan) hired [Glen Sather](/wiki/Glen_Sather) as general manager. By the end of the [2000–01 season](/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers had acquired some veterans. Messier returned to New York, [Theoren Fleury](/wiki/Theoren_Fleury) joined the Rangers after spending most of his career with the [Calgary Flames](/wiki/Calgary_Flames) and Eric Lindros was traded to the Rangers by the Flyers. The Rangers also acquired [Pavel Bure](/wiki/Pavel_Bure) late in [2001–02](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_New_York_Rangers_season) from the [Florida Panthers](/wiki/Florida_Panthers). Approaching the [2004–05 NHL lockout](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout), they also acquired [Alexei Kovalev](/wiki/Alexei_Kovalev), [Jaromir Jagr](/wiki/Jarom%C3%ADr_J%C3%A1gr), [Martin Rucinsky](/wiki/Martin_Ru%C4%8Dinsk%C3%BD), and [Bobby Holik](/wiki/Bobby_Hol%C3%ADk). However, none of the acquisitions helped them obtain a playoff spot. Towards the end of the [2003–04 season](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_NHL_season), general manager Glen Sather traded away Brian Leetch, Kovalev, Rucinsky, and Nedved.

### Henrik Lundqvist era (2005–2020)

#### Return to the playoffs (2005–2011)

Stellar performances by rookie goaltender [Henrik Lundqvist](/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist) during the [2005–06 season](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_NHL_season) led to the Rangers' best record since [1993–94](/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_NHL_season).

With the emergence of Swedish rookie goaltender [Henrik Lundqvist](/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist) during the [2005–06 season](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers finished the season with a record of 44–26–12, their best record since 1993–94. Jagr broke the Rangers' single-season points record with a first-period assist in a 5–1 win against the New York Islanders on March 29, 2006. The assist gave him 110 points on the season, breaking [Jean Ratelle](/wiki/Jean_Ratelle)'s record. Less than two weeks later, on April 8, Jagr scored his 53rd goal of the season against the Boston Bruins, breaking the club record previously held by Adam Graves. Two games prior, on April 4, the Rangers defeated the Philadelphia Flyers 3–2, in a shootout, to clinch a playoff spot for the first time since [1996–97](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_NHL_season). In the [2006 conference quarterfinals](/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Rangers faced off against the Devils and were defeated in a four-game sweep. Jagr fell two points short of winning his sixth [Art Ross Trophy](/wiki/Art_Ross_Trophy) as scoring champion in 2005–06, but did win his third [Pearson Award](/wiki/Ted_Lindsay_Award) as the players' choice for the most outstanding player.

[Jaromir Jagr](/wiki/Jarom%C3%ADr_J%C3%A1gr) was named team captain at the start of the [2006–07 NHL season](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_NHL_season).

In the [2006–07 offseason](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers signed [Triple Gold Club](/wiki/Triple_Gold_Club) winner and 12-time 30-goal scorer [Brendan Shanahan](/wiki/Brendan_Shanahan) to a one-year contract. On October 5, 2006, Jagr was named the first team captain since Mark Messier's retirement. On February 5, 2007, the Rangers acquired forward [Sean Avery](/wiki/Sean_Avery) in a trade with the Los Angeles Kings. The Rangers swept the [Atlanta Thrashers](/wiki/Atlanta_Thrashers) in the first round of the [2007 playoffs](/wiki/2007_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). However, they were eliminated in the next round by the [Buffalo Sabres](/wiki/Buffalo_Sabres).

At the [2007 NHL entry draft](/wiki/2007_NHL_entry_draft), the Rangers chose [Alexei Cherepanov](/wiki/Alexei_Cherepanov) 17th overall, who had been ranked by the [NHL Central Scouting Bureau](/wiki/NHL_Central_Scouting_Bureau) as the number one European skater. In the 2007 offseason, they signed [Scott Gomez](/wiki/Scott_Gomez) to a seven-year contract, as well as [Chris Drury](/wiki/Chris_Drury) to a five-year deal. The Rangers made the playoffs for the third consecutive season and the second round for the second consecutive season. In the first round against the New Jersey Devils, in game three, Sean Avery faced himself towards Devils goaltender [Martin Brodeur](/wiki/Martin_Brodeur) and moved his arms and stick in an attempt to screen any shots from the Rangers. After the game, the league made it an unsportsmanlike conduct penalty to purposefully screen the goalie with your arms and stick. Following their first-round victory, they lost to the Pittsburgh Penguins in the second round. The following offseason saw the departures of captain Jaromir Jagr to the KHL and [Martin Straka](/wiki/Martin_Straka), who left to play in the Czech Republic.

[John Tortorella](/wiki/John_Tortorella) was named the team's head coach in 2009, maintaining the position until 2013.

Following Jagr's departure, Chris Drury was named captain on October 3, 2008. The Rangers were one of four NHL teams to open the [2008–09 season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_New_York_Rangers_season) in Europe, being featured in the [Victoria Cup](/wiki/Victoria_Cup_(ice_hockey)) final, defeating the European Champions Cup winner [Metallurg Magnitogorsk](/wiki/Metallurg_Magnitogorsk) in [Bern](/wiki/Bern), Switzerland. This was followed by two NHL regular season games against Tampa Bay in [Prague](/wiki/Prague) on October 4 and 5; the Rangers won both games. However, on October 13, 2007, 2007 first-round pick, Alexei Cherepanov collapsed and died during a KHL game in Russia. After the Rangers went 2–7–3 in 12 games, coach Tom Renney was fired, with [2004 Stanley Cup](/wiki/2004_Stanley_Cup_Final) and [Jack Adams Award](/wiki/Jack_Adams_Award) winner [John Tortorella](/wiki/John_Tortorella) named as his replacement. The Rangers made the [2009 playoffs](/wiki/2009_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), but gave up a 3–1 series lead to the Washington Capitals to lose in seven games.

On June 30, 2009, the Rangers traded Scott Gomez, [Tom Pyatt](/wiki/Tom_Pyatt), and [Michael Busto](/wiki/Michael_Busto) to the Montreal Canadiens for [Ryan McDonagh](/wiki/Ryan_McDonagh), [Chris Higgins](/wiki/Chris_Higgins_(ice_hockey)), [Pavel Valentenko](/wiki/Pavel_Valentenko), and [Doug Janik](/wiki/Doug_Janik). The Rangers also signed [Marian Gaborik](/wiki/Mari%C3%A1n_G%C3%A1bor%C3%ADk) on the first day of free agency. In the [2009–10 season](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers failed to make the playoffs for the first time in five years, losing the final game in a shootout against the Philadelphia Flyers.

On September 12, 2011, [Ryan Callahan](/wiki/Ryan_Callahan) was named the 26th captain in Rangers history.

For the [2010–11 season](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_New_York_Rangers_season), the team signed Norwegian forward [Mats Zuccarello](/wiki/Mats_Zuccarello). The Rangers' playoff chances came down to the final day of the regular season for the second consecutive year. The team defeated the New Jersey Devils and passed the [Carolina Hurricanes](/wiki/Carolina_Hurricanes) in the standings, putting the Rangers in the playoffs. The Rangers faced Washington in the first round and lost the series in five games.

On May 13, 2011, Rangers forward [Derek Boogaard](/wiki/Derek_Boogaard) was found dead in his [Minnesota](/wiki/Minnesota) apartment. On June 29, the Rangers bought out captain Chris Drury's contract. On July 3, the Rangers signed free agent [Brad Richards](/wiki/Brad_Richards) to a nine-year contract. On September 12, [Ryan Callahan](/wiki/Ryan_Callahan) was named the 26th captain in the Rangers' history.

#### Return to the Stanley Cup Final and third Presidents' Trophy (2011–2016)

In the [2011–12 season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_New_York_Rangers_season), the team finished as the top seed in the Eastern Conference, recording 51 wins and 109 points. Lunqvist was awarded the [Vezina Trophy](/wiki/Vezina_Trophy) for the League's best goaltender. In the playoffs, they defeated both the Senators and Capitals in seven games respectively. In the conference finals, they faced the New Jersey Devils. After leading the series 2–1, the Rangers lost the next three games, losing game six to New Jersey in overtime.

On July 23, 2012, the Rangers traded [Brandon Dubinsky](/wiki/Brandon_Dubinsky), [Artem Anisimov](/wiki/Artem_Anisimov), [Tim Erixon](/wiki/Tim_Erixon) and a [2013](/wiki/2013_NHL_entry_draft) first-round draft pick to the [Columbus Blue Jackets](/wiki/Columbus_Blue_Jackets) in exchange for [Rick Nash](/wiki/Rick_Nash), [Steven Delisle](/wiki/Steven_Delisle), and a 2013 conditional third-round pick. At the 2013 trade deadline on April 3, the Rangers traded Marian Gaborik and Steven Delisle to Columbus for [Derick Brassard](/wiki/Derick_Brassard), [Derek Dorsett](/wiki/Derek_Dorsett), [John Moore](/wiki/John_Moore_(ice_hockey)), and a 2014 sixth-round draft pick. After the Rangers were eliminated in the second round of the playoffs by Boston, management fired head coach John Tortorella. On June 21, 2013, general manager Glen Sather formally introduced former Canucks head coach [Alain Vigneault](/wiki/Alain_Vigneault) as Tortorella's replacement.

The Rangers acquired [Rick Nash](/wiki/Rick_Nash) in a multi-player trade with the [Columbus Blue Jackets](/wiki/Columbus_Blue_Jackets) in 2012.

On March 5, 2014, the Rangers traded their captain [Ryan Callahan](/wiki/Ryan_Callahan), along with a first-round draft pick in [2015](/wiki/2015_NHL_entry_draft), a conditional second-round pick in [2014](/wiki/2014_NHL_entry_draft), and a conditional seventh-round pick in 2015, for Tampa Bay captain [Martin St. Louis](/wiki/Martin_St._Louis). The trade occurred both due to the Rangers' and Callahan's inability to reach a contract extension, as well as St. Louis' growing tension with the Lightning organization and subsequent request to be traded to New York. The [2013–14 Rangers](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_New_York_Rangers_season) set a new franchise record of 25 road game wins. New York defeated Philadelphia in seven games in the first round of the [2014 playoffs](/wiki/2014_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), and in the next round rallied from a 3–1 series deficit to defeat Pittsburgh in seven games. They then defeated the Montreal Canadiens in six games to move onto the [Cup Final](/wiki/2014_Stanley_Cup_Final) to face the [Los Angeles Kings](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings). The Rangers lost the series in five games.

On June 20, 2014, a week after their season ended, the Rangers [bought out](/wiki/Compliance_buyout) the remaining six years of Brad Richards' contract. On October 6, defenseman [Ryan McDonagh](/wiki/Ryan_McDonagh) was named the Rangers' 27th captain in team history. In [2014–15](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers won the Presidents' Trophy for the third time in franchise history by finishing with the best record in the NHL at 53–22–7. The 53 wins and 113 points both set franchise records. In the [2015 Stanley Cup playoffs](/wiki/2015_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Rangers dispatched the Pittsburgh Penguins in five games in the first round. The Rangers then came back from a 3–1 series deficit to win their second-round series against the Capitals in seven games, becoming the first team in NHL history to battle back from a 3–1 deficit in back-to-back seasons and sending the Rangers to the conference finals for the third time in four years. However, the Lightning shutout the Rangers 2–0 in game seven, ending the Rangers' season and marking the first occasion the Rangers had ever lost a game seven at home in franchise history.

On June 27, 2015, the Rangers traded [Carl Hagelin](/wiki/Carl_Hagelin) to the Anaheim Ducks, [Cam Talbot](/wiki/Cam_Talbot) and a draft pick to the Edmonton Oilers, and prospect [Ryan Haggerty](/wiki/Ryan_Haggerty) to the Chicago Blackhawks for goaltender [Antti Raanta](/wiki/Antti_Raanta). On July 1, [Glen Sather](/wiki/Glen_Sather) resigned as the general manager, with [Jeff Gorton](/wiki/Jeff_Gorton) taking his place. On July 2, Martin St. Louis announced his retirement. The team then re-signed [Jesper Fast](/wiki/Jesper_Fast), [J. T. Miller](/wiki/J._T._Miller), and Derek Stepan.

The Rangers started the [2015–16 season](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_New_York_Rangers_season) with a 14–2–2 record after 18 games, including a nine-game winning streak. The Rangers finished the season with 101 points. In the [2016 playoffs](/wiki/2016_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Rangers were eliminated in the first round by the Pittsburgh Penguins.

#### Rebuilding (2016–2020)

On July 18, 2016, the Rangers traded Derick Brassard and a 2018 seventh-round draft pick in exchange for [Mika Zibanejad](/wiki/Mika_Zibanejad) and a 2018 second-round draft pick. The team also signed [Michael Grabner](/wiki/Michael_Grabner) to a two-year deal and [Jimmy Vesey](/wiki/Jimmy_Vesey) to a two-year entry-level contract. The Rangers finished [2016–17](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_New_York_Rangers_season) in fourth place in the Metropolitan Division with 102 points. In the first round of the [2017 Stanley Cup playoffs](/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), they won their series with the Montreal Canadiens in six games. They lost in the second round to the Ottawa Senators in six games.

On June 14, 2017, the Rangers announced a buyout of Dan Girardi's contract. Just over a week later, the Rangers traded Derek Stepan and Antti Raanta to the [Arizona Coyotes](/wiki/Arizona_Coyotes) in exchange for a first-round draft pick (seventh overall) and former first-round pick [Tony DeAngelo](/wiki/Tony_DeAngelo). Rangers' management also signed top free agent defenseman [Kevin Shattenkirk](/wiki/Kevin_Shattenkirk) to a four-year deal. By February 8, 2018, the team had a 25–24–5 record, leading the front office to issue a social media post to fans announcing the Rangers would be committing to a rebuild and may "lose some familiar faces" in the process. [Rick Nash](/wiki/Rick_Nash) was traded the day before the 2018 [NHL trade deadline](/wiki/NHL_trade_deadline) to the Bruins for a 2018 first-round pick, a 2019 seventh-round pick, [Matt Beleskey](/wiki/Matt_Beleskey), [Ryan Spooner](/wiki/Ryan_Spooner) and [Ryan Lindgren](/wiki/Ryan_Lindgren). The following day, the Rangers traded captain [Ryan McDonagh](/wiki/Ryan_McDonagh) and [J. T. Miller](/wiki/J._T._Miller) to the [Tampa Bay Lightning](/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning) for picks and prospects. With the team missing the playoffs for the first time since 2010 and placing last in the Metropolitan Division, head coach Vigneault was fired at the conclusion of the season.

On May 23, [David Quinn](/wiki/David_Quinn_(ice_hockey)) was hired as the team's new head coach. During the offseason, Hayes, Vesey, [Brady Skjei](/wiki/Brady_Skjei) and Spooner all filed for salary arbitration and all were re-signed. Gorton and his team remained committed to a rebuild in the [2018–19 season](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_New_York_Rangers_season); the Rangers traded away veteran players at the deadline, including [Kevin Hayes](/wiki/Kevin_Hayes) and [Mats Zuccarello](/wiki/Mats_Zuccarello), who was sent to the Dallas Stars in exchange for two draft picks.

The Rangers received the second overall pick in the [2019 NHL entry draft](/wiki/2019_NHL_entry_draft), and used it to select forward [Kaapo Kakko](/wiki/Kaapo_Kakko). On May 17, 2019, former Ranger goaltender and broadcaster [John Davidson](/wiki/John_Davidson_(ice_hockey)) resigned from his position as president of the Columbus Blue Jackets and returned to New York to become the organization's new president. The Rangers then acquired prospect [Adam Fox](/wiki/Adam_Fox) from the Carolina Hurricanes for a pair of picks, and also traded with the [Winnipeg Jets](/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) for defenseman [Jacob Trouba](/wiki/Jacob_Trouba). The team also signed free agent [Artemi Panarin](/wiki/Artemi_Panarin) to a seven-year deal on July 1.

[Artemi Panarin](/wiki/Artemi_Panarin) signed with the Rangers in July 2019

The [2019–20 season](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_New_York_Rangers_season) saw improvement as Panarin earned a [Hart Trophy](/wiki/Hart_Trophy) nomination, Chris Kreider signed a seven-year contract extension, and Mika Zibanejad recorded a five-goal game against Washington on March 5 and ended up with 41 goals in 57 games played. By early March 2020, the Rangers were within striking distance of the second wild card position when the [coronavirus pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_New_York_(state)) halted the regular season. In May 2020, the league announced a [24-team playoff tournament](/wiki/2020_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) to complete the season, where the Rangers were seeded 11th and faced the Carolina Hurricanes; the Hurricanes swept the Rangers. After being eliminated from the playoffs the Rangers were entered into the second phase of the NHL draft lottery where the team won the lottery and were awarded the first pick in the [2020 NHL entry draft](/wiki/2020_NHL_entry_draft), which Gorton used to select [Alexis Lafreniere](/wiki/Alexis_Lafreni%C3%A8re). Later in the offseason, the team traded veteran defenseman [Marc Staal](/wiki/Marc_Staal) to the Red Wings.

In September 2020, the Rangers bought out the final year of [Henrik Lundqvist](/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist)'s contract, ending his tenure in New York after 15 years.

### The Drury years (2020–present)

#### Struggles, playoff return, and fourth Presidents' Trophy (2020–2024)

On January 31, 2021, defenseman Tony DeAngelo was placed on waivers following reports that he had an altercation with teammate [Alexandar Georgiev](/wiki/Alexandar_Georgiev) following an overtime loss. According to *[The Athletic](/wiki/The_Athletic),* his continued "maturity" issues, combined with a marked decline in his play, led the Rangers to put him on the market; there were no takers. In a game against the [Washington Capitals](/wiki/Washington_Capitals) on May 3, 2021, Capitals forward [Tom Wilson](/wiki/Tom_Wilson_(ice_hockey)) cross-checked [Pavel Buchnevich](/wiki/Pavel_Buchnevich) in the head and slammed [Artemi Panarin](/wiki/Artemi_Panarin) into the ice, ending his season. Wilson, a repeat offender, was fined the league maximum of $5,000 for the incident. The Rangers organization released a statement expressing disappointment in this decision, calling head of player safety [George Parros](/wiki/George_Parros) "unfit to continue in his current role". The NHL subsequently fined the Rangers $250,000 for their comments. Two days later, Rangers owner [James Dolan](/wiki/James_L._Dolan) fired president [John Davidson](/wiki/John_Davidson_(ice_hockey)) and general manager [Jeff Gorton](/wiki/Jeff_Gorton). Despite the timing, Dolan stated the firings were not related to the Wilson incident and statement, citing "culture" issues within the organization. [Chris Drury](/wiki/Chris_Drury) was then announced as the Rangers' new president and general manager. On May 12, Drury fired head coach David Quinn and replaced him with [Gerard Gallant](/wiki/Gerard_Gallant). Despite a tumultuous season, a major bright spot was the play of [Adam Fox](/wiki/Adam_Fox); he led NHL defensemen with 42 assists, finished second in points with 47, and won the [James Norris Memorial Trophy](/wiki/James_Norris_Memorial_Trophy).

The Rangers finished the [2021–22 season](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_New_York_Rangers_season) with a record of 52–24–6. For the third time in franchise history, the Rangers overcame a 3–1 series deficit, this time against the [Pittsburgh Penguins](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) in a first round matchup that concluded with a game-winning overtime goal from [Artemi Panarin](/wiki/Artemi_Panarin). After defeating the [Carolina Hurricanes](/wiki/Carolina_Hurricanes) in seven games, they faced the back-to-back defending champions, the [Tampa Bay Lightning](/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning), and lost the series in six games. [Igor Shesterkin](/wiki/Igor_Shesterkin) was named the [Vezina Trophy](/wiki/Vezina_Trophy) winner following the season's conclusion. By the end of the [following season](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers had acquired [Vladimir Tarasenko](/wiki/Vladimir_Tarasenko) and [Niko Mikkola](/wiki/Niko_Mikkola) from the [St. Louis Blues](/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) and [Patrick Kane](/wiki/Patrick_Kane) from the Blackhawks, but lost to their [river rivals](/wiki/Devils%E2%80%93Rangers_rivalry), the [New Jersey Devils](/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils), in the first round of the playoffs. Gallant was then fired as head coach and replaced with [Peter Laviolette](/wiki/Peter_Laviolette).

In the [2023–24 season](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_New_York_Rangers_season), the Rangers clinched their fourth [Presidents' Trophy](/wiki/Presidents%27_Trophy), and in the first round of the [2024 playoffs](/wiki/2024_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) swept the Washington Capitals. After defeating the Carolina Hurricanes in the second round, they lost to the eventual Stanley Cup champion [Florida Panthers](/wiki/Florida_Panthers) in the conference finals.

#### Retooling (2024–present)

The [2024–25 season](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_New_York_Rangers_season) was disastrous, as the Rangers became just the fourth team to miss the playoffs the year following a Presidents' Trophy win. Veteran players, such as Chris Kreider and Mika Zibanejad, received criticism throughout the season, captain [Jacob Trouba](/wiki/Jacob_Trouba), [Kaapo Kakko](/wiki/Kaapo_Kakko), and [Filip Chytil](/wiki/Filip_Chytil) were traded, and Laviolette was fired at the end of the season. [J. T. Miller](/wiki/J._T._Miller) was also reacquired during the Chytil trade.

On May 2, 2025, [Mike Sullivan](/wiki/Mike_Sullivan_(ice_hockey)) was named head coach. Later that offseason, Chris Kreider was traded to the [Anaheim Ducks](/wiki/Anaheim_Ducks) after a 13-year career with the Rangers. The [following season](/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_New_York_Rangers_season) would also prove to be disastrous, as by January 16, 2026, the team had a 20–22–6 record with a 5–14–3 home record. That day, Drury declared that the team would retool, stating, "it will be built around our core players and prospects." Near the trade deadline, the Rangers traded Panarin to the Los Angeles Kings for prospect [Liam Greentree](/wiki/Liam_Greentree), a conditional third-round pick in [2026](/wiki/2026_NHL_entry_draft) and a fourth-round pick in [2028](/wiki/2028_NHL_entry_draft).

## Uniforms

The classic Rangers sweater has been in use since the franchise's foundation, with several alterations along the way. The current blue uniform has the serifed word "RANGERS" in red and white drop shadow arranged diagonally, with red and white stripes on the sleeves and tail. Originally, the uniform was light blue, before it switched to a darker classic Rangers "Broadway Blue" in 1929. In addition, the original versions neither had a drop shadow nor were serifed. During the 1946–47 season, the word "RANGERS" was arranged in an arch form above the sweater number. It adopted its current form the next season, along with dropshadowed numbers, except for a brief period where the city name was used, a tie-down collar was not used and the tail and sleeve stripes were separated by thin blue stripes. Red pants have been used with the uniform since the 1929–30 season.

The white jerseys were first unveiled in the 1951–52 season. The serifed word "RANGERS" is also arranged diagonally, but in blue with red drop shadow. The white sweaters, with minor changes such as a tie-down collar and arched player names, have remained virtually unchanged since.

During the tenure of general manager [John Ferguson Sr.](/wiki/John_Ferguson_Sr.), he sought to modernize the Rangers sweater by featuring rounded numbers, a darker shade of blue and the shield logo, which was unveiled in the 1976–77 season. A blue and red stripe (white and red stripe in the blue sweaters) extend from the yoke to the sleeves, while blue pants were used. However, it proved unpopular with the fans, and following the 1977–78 season it was replaced by an updated version of their classic uniforms. Ferguson used this similar design when he became general manager of the original [Winnipeg Jets](/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets_(1972%E2%80%931996)).

From 1996 to 2007 the Rangers' alternate jersey featured the head of the Statue of Liberty with the team abbreviation below it.

The modernized classic uniforms introduced in 1978 featured some subtle changes. Both jerseys featured a V-neck collar in a red-white-red pattern, and bolder stripes on the sleeves and waistline. On the blue jersey, the red and white stripes were separated by thin blue stripes, along with the waistline stripes being raised above the hemline so that the patterns on both jerseys matched. From 1978 to 1987, the blue jersey (then the road jersey) featured "NEW YORK" diagonally across the front instead of the traditional "RANGERS" wordmark, similar to their 2010s heritage alternate jerseys.> In 1997, the Rangers reverted the blue jersey's design, restoring the old striping pattern, and becoming the first team to re-introduce lace-up collars. and the design was carried over to the Reebok Edge template in 2007.

On October 7, 2001, the Rangers wore a modified version of their blue jerseys in a home game against the [Buffalo Sabres](/wiki/Buffalo_Sabres). This design combined the current/traditional striping with the "NEW YORK" wordmark of the 1978–1987 uniforms. The uniforms were worn in the wake of the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks).

The Rangers previously had a navy alternate jersey featuring the head of the [Statue of Liberty](/wiki/Statue_of_Liberty) with the team abbreviation (NYR) below in a futuristic script. Silver was used as an accent color, but the player names and numbers retain the same color schemes as the regular jerseys, except for a darker shade of blue. Other than a white version used in the 1998–99 season, this jersey was used from 1996 to 2007.

During the 2010–11 season, the Rangers debuted a heritage blue jersey as their new alternate uniform. The jersey featured a darker shade of blue, as well as a cream trim. Unlike the regular jerseys, the font of the alternate was [sans-serif](/wiki/Sans-serif) and did not feature a drop-shadow, much like the original Rangers jersey. The Rangers wore the jerseys at home on Saturdays and when they played against Original Six teams.

In the [2012 Winter Classic](/wiki/2012_NHL_Winter_Classic), the Rangers wore a cream jersey combining classic and current styles. A different version of the shield logo was used, while the player names were arranged in a straight line. The stripes were also lessened, giving it a minimalist, vintage look, as most Winter Classic jerseys are.

For the [2014 Stadium Series](/wiki/2014_NHL_Stadium_Series), the Rangers used white jerseys with the city name in navy, silver and red. In addition, they feature diagonal stripes and sleeve numbers, and enlarged numbers on the back to make them more readable to spectators. The chrome version of the shield logo is placed in the left shoulder. Like the Winter Classic sweaters, player names are in a straight position.

The [2018 Winter Classic](/wiki/2018_NHL_Winter_Classic) saw the Rangers wear a navy jersey with a combination of elements from prior uniform designs. The striping design was inspired from their current uniforms, while the white "RANGERS" wordmark was a nod from the team's late 1920s jerseys. A white silhouette of the Rangers' shield logo contained either the abbreviation "N.Y." or the alternate captain "A" and captain "C" designations. Player names are arranged in a straight position.

During the 2020–21 season, the Rangers released a "Reverse Retro" alternate uniform in collaboration with Adidas. The uniform featured the "Lady Liberty" design worn from 1996 to 2007, but with a few changes in the striping. This same design was again used for their 2022–23 "Reverse Retro" uniform, but the lighter Broadway Blue served as the base color while the lower sleeves were recolored red with white and navy stripes.

Introduced in the 2023–24 season, the Rangers began wearing a new third jersey, bringing back the shield logo as the main crest for the first time since the 1976–1978 redesign. The navy blue-based jersey features thin white, red and Broadway blue stripes on the sleeves, waist and socks, along with white letters.

For the [2024 Stadium Series](/wiki/2024_NHL_Stadium_Series), the Rangers wore white jerseys with an enlarged "NYR" diagonal lettering in red with blue drop shadows. Enlarged numbers also employ the same color scheme as the "NYR" wordmark, along with thick alternating blue and red sleeve stripes.

The Rangers unveiled their centennial edition jersey ahead of the 2025–26 season. Based on their inaugural 1926–27 uniform, it featured a light blue base with the traditional red and cream stripes, and cream letters featuring the sans-serif "RANGERS" written diagonally in white. The 100th anniversary patch was placed on the left shoulder, and a sponsor patch featuring sports and entertainment brand "Game 7" was placed on the right shoulder. A cream variation with a diagonal sans-serif "NEW YORK" wordmark serves as their uniform for the [2026 NHL Winter Classic](/wiki/2026_NHL_Winter_Classic).

### Hall of Famers

The New York Rangers acknowledge an affiliation with a number of inductees to the [Hockey Hall of Fame](/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame). Rangers inductees include 53 former players and ten builders of the sport. The nine individuals recognized as builders by the Hall of Fame includes former Rangers executives, general managers, head coaches, and owners. In addition to players and builders, several broadcasters were also awarded the [Foster Hewitt Memorial Award](/wiki/Foster_Hewitt_Memorial_Award) from the Hockey Hall of Fame. [Sal Messina](/wiki/Sal_Messina), a [color commentator](/wiki/Color_commentator), was the first Rangers broadcaster to be awarded the Foster Hewitt Memorial Award. Other Rangers broadcasters awarded the Foster Hewitt Memorial Award include [John Davidson](/wiki/John_Davidson_(ice_hockey)) (awarded in 2009), and [Sam Rosen](/wiki/Sam_Rosen_(sportscaster)) (awarded in 2016).

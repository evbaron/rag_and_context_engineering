# 1932 Summer Olympics

## Infobox

- **Location:** Los Angeles, United States
- **Nations:** 37
- **Athletes:** 1,332 (1,206 men, 126 women)
- **Events:** 117 in 14 sports (20 disciplines)
- **Opening:** July 30, 1932
- **Closing:** August 14, 1932
- **Opened by:** Vice President Charles Curtis [ 1 ]
- **Stadium:** Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum

The **1932 Summer Olympics** (officially the **Games of the X Olympiad** and also known as **Los Angeles 1932**) were an international multi-sport event held from July 30 to August 14, 1932, in [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles), California, United States. The Games were held during the worldwide [Great Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression), with some nations not traveling to Los Angeles as a result; [37](#Participating_nations) countries competed, compared to the [46](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics#Participating_nations) at the [1928 Games](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) in [Amsterdam](/wiki/Amsterdam), and even then-U.S. [President](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Herbert Hoover](/wiki/Herbert_Hoover) did not attend the Games. The organizing committee did not report the financial details of the Games, although contemporary newspapers stated that the Games had made a [profit](/wiki/Profit_(accounting)) of [US$](/wiki/United_States_dollar)1 million (equivalent to [US$](/wiki/United_States_dollar)18.67 million in 2024).

## Host city selection

Main article: [Los Angeles bid for the 1932 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Los_Angeles_bid_for_the_1932_Summer_Olympics)

The selection of the host city for the 1932 Summer Olympics was made at the 23rd IOC Session in [Rome](/wiki/Rome), Italy, on April 9, 1923. Remarkably, the selection process consisted of a single bid, from Los Angeles's Olympic Committee led by Billy May Garland, and as there were no bids from any other city, Los Angeles was selected by default to host the 1932 Games.

## Highlights

### Facilities and opening ceremony

The Australian Olympic Team at the Olympic Stadium, Los Angeles, 1932

[Lauri Lehtinen](/wiki/Lauri_Lehtinen) (left) and [Ralph Hill](/wiki/Ralph_Hill_(runner)) finishing the 5000 m race at the 1932 Olympics

[Charles Curtis](/wiki/Charles_Curtis) became the first and only [U.S. Vice President](/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States) to inaugurate the [Summer Olympic Games](/wiki/Summer_Olympic_Games). An [Olympic Village](/wiki/Olympic_Village) was built for the first time and became a model for future games, in [Baldwin Hills](/wiki/Baldwin_Hills,_Los_Angeles), occupied by male athletes. Female athletes were housed at the Chapman Park Hotel on [Wilshire Boulevard](/wiki/Wilshire_Boulevard). Tenth Street, a major thoroughfare in Los Angeles, was renamed [Olympic Boulevard](/wiki/Olympic_Boulevard_(Los_Angeles)) in honor of the Games of the Tenth Olympiad. The [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum) was known in 1932 as *Olympic Stadium*.

The victory podium was used for the first time at the Summer Games (a podium was also used earlier in the year at the [1932 Winter Olympics](/wiki/1932_Winter_Olympics) in Lake Placid). An [Olympic mascot](/wiki/Olympic_mascot), Scottish Terrier [Smoky](/wiki/Smoky_(Olympic_mascot)), was featured for the first time in history, albeit unofficially.

### Athletics and field events

[Babe Didrikson](/wiki/Babe_Didrikson) won two gold medals in the [javelin](/wiki/Javelin_throw) and the hurdles event. She also competed in a jump-off for a gold in the high jump. Her technique in the jump-off was ruled illegal, leaving Didrikson with second place.

Finland's [Paavo Nurmi](/wiki/Paavo_Nurmi#1932_Olympics_and_later_career) was suspended from competition by the [IAAF](/wiki/International_Association_of_Athletics_Federations) for alleged violation of amateur rules. Finns charged that the Swedish officials had used devious tricks in their campaign against Nurmi's amateur status, and ceased all athletic relations with Sweden. A year earlier, controversies on the track and in the press had led Finland to withdraw from the [Finland-Sweden athletics international](/wiki/Finland-Sweden_athletics_international). After Nurmi's suspension, Finland did not agree to return to the event until 1939.

[Eddie Tolan](/wiki/Eddie_Tolan) won both the 100 m and 200 m sprint events. Poland's [Stanisława Walasiewicz](/wiki/Stanis%C5%82awa_Walasiewicz) won the gold medal in the women's 100 m; she also won the silver medal in the event four years later. After her death in 1980, it was discovered that she was [intersex](/wiki/Intersex) and would have been ineligible to participate under modern rules.

Due to an official's error, the [3,000 m steeplechase](/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1932_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_3000_metres_steeplechase) went for 3,460 m, or one extra lap.

Several women's events debuted at these games, among them the [80 meters hurdles](/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1932_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_80_metres_hurdles) and [javelin throw](/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1932_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_javelin_throw). [Babe Didrikson](/wiki/Babe_Didrikson) won both events and also competed in the [high jump](/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1932_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_high_jump) where she was controversially denied gold, leaving her with silver. As women, unlike men, were only allowed to enter a maximum of three events, Didrikson could not compete in the discus throw, long jump, and relay where she would have likely medaled based on her prior results. Had the 200 meters and pentathlon been contested at these games (they debuted in 1964), Didrikson would have won them easily based on her performances prior to the Olympics.

### Team sports

In [field hockey](/wiki/Field_hockey), only three nations took part. The host nation lost both matches, 1–24 to India and 2–9 to Japan, but still won a bronze medal.

### Gymnastics

[Romeo Neri](/wiki/Romeo_Neri) won three gold medals in gymnastics. Although women's team gymnastics debuted in the [previous Olympics](/wiki/Gymnastics_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_artistic_team_all-around), the event was not held in these games; however, there were women gymnasts who traveled to Los Angeles and participated in exhibition events at the 1932 games.

### Swimming

[Helene Madison](/wiki/Helene_Madison) won three gold medals in swimming, while the Japanese upset the men's events and took all but one title. [Kusuo Kitamura](/wiki/Kusuo_Kitamura) won the gold medal in the men's 1500 meter freestyle swimming race. He was and continues to be the youngest ever male swimmer to win a gold medal at the Olympic Games.

### Equestrian

[Takeichi Nishi](/wiki/Takeichi_Nishi) (Baron Nishi) was the gold medalist with his horse Uranus in the equestrian [show jumping individual event](/wiki/Equestrian_at_the_1932_Summer_Olympics). Nishi's gold medal is Japan's only gold medal in the equestrian event to this day. Nishi died in 1945 as an officer stationed in the defense of the island of [Iwo Jima](/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima), and as such is an important character in [Clint Eastwood](/wiki/Clint_Eastwood)'s film, *[Letters from Iwo Jima](/wiki/Letters_from_Iwo_Jima)*.

### Cycling

[Dunc Gray](/wiki/Dunc_Gray) won Australia's first cycling gold medal; he set a world record of 1m 13s in the 1000 [time trial](/wiki/Track_time_trial). The [Dunc Gray Velodrome](/wiki/Dunc_Gray_Velodrome), built for the [2000 Sydney Olympic Games](/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics), was named after him.

## Venues

The Rose Bowl hosted the track cycling events for the 1932 Summer Olympics

Fifteen sports venues were used for the 1932 Summer Olympics. In order to control costs in the wake of the [Great Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States), existing venues were used. They included two [golf courses](/wiki/Golf_course), two [city parks](/wiki/City_park), three public highways, and a city road. The [Swimming Stadium](/wiki/LA84_Foundation/John_C._Argue_Swim_Stadium) was the only new venue constructed for these games. The [Rose Bowl](/wiki/Rose_Bowl_(stadium)), constructed in 1921, was made into a temporary [velodrome](/wiki/Velodrome) for [track cycling](/wiki/Track_cycling) events under the auspices of the [Union Cycliste Internationale](/wiki/Union_Cycliste_Internationale) (UCI). The [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum), constructed in 1923, was used as the Olympic Stadium. The Olympic Auditorium was constructed in 1924 in preparation for Los Angeles being awarded the Games; it was modified to meet the specifications of the boxing, weightlifting, and wrestling federations. Long Beach Marine Stadium was created in 1925 when [Alamitos Bay](/wiki/Alamitos_Bay) was dredged, then further dredged seven years later in time for the 1932 Games. [Elysian Park](/wiki/Elysian_Park,_Los_Angeles), the oldest city park in Los Angeles, was founded in 1886, and has been part of the [Los Angeles Police Department](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Police_Department) (LAPD) training academy since 1925. The Riviera Country Club opened in 1926 as the [Los Angeles Athletic Club](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Athletic_Club) Golf Course and was renamed Riviera by the time of the 1932 Games. The Swimming Stadium, constructed adjacent to the Coliseum in 1932, was intended to be a temporary structure. Riverside Drive, Los Angeles Avenue, Vineyard Avenue, and the Pacific Coast Highway were common driving routes in [California](/wiki/California) at the time of the 1932 Games.

The Coliseum was the first (& temporary) Los Angeles home for the National League (NL) [Dodgers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Dodgers) baseball team when it moved from [Brooklyn, New York](/wiki/Brooklyn) before the [1958 season](/wiki/1958_in_baseball). The following year, it hosted the 2nd 1959 [MLB All-Star Game](/wiki/1959_Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game_(second_game)) and games 3, 4 & 5 of the 1959 [World Series](/wiki/1959_World_Series). When [Dodger Stadium](/wiki/Dodger_Stadium) was completed in [1962](/wiki/1962_Los_Angeles_Dodgers_season), the Dodgers moved there where they have been since. The National Football League (NFL) [Los Angeles Rams](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) used the Coliseum as its host stadium from [1946](/wiki/1946_Los_Angeles_Rams_season) to [1979](/wiki/1979_Los_Angeles_Rams_season) when it moved to [Anaheim](/wiki/Anaheim,_California), located southeast of Los Angeles. It also hosted what became known as [Super Bowl I](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I) in 1967. Even the [American Football League](/wiki/American_Football_League)'s [Chargers](/wiki/San_Diego_Chargers) used the Coliseum as a venue in 1960 until their move to [San Diego](/wiki/San_Diego) the following year. The Coliseum continues to host [USC Trojans football](/wiki/USC_Trojans_football) games to this day, and also hosted [UCLA Bruins football](/wiki/UCLA_Bruins_football) for a number of years. The Rams returned to the Coliseum for a span of four years from 2016 to 2019 while [SoFi Stadium](/wiki/SoFi_Stadium) was being built.

The track constructed in the Rose Bowl was given to the Tournament of Roses Association upon completion of the 1932 Games. The Bowl was expanded between 1932 and the [1984 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) three times, increasing its capacity from 83,000 in 1931 to 104,594 in 1972. It hosted [Super Bowl XI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XI) in 1977, where the [Oakland Raiders](/wiki/1976_Oakland_Raiders_season) defeated the [Minnesota Vikings](/wiki/1976_Minnesota_Vikings_season) 32–14. It is the current home of [UCLA Bruins football](/wiki/UCLA_Bruins_football) and the [Rose Bowl Game](/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Game), and was the home of the Major League Soccer (MLS) [Los Angeles Galaxy](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Galaxy) from 1996 to 2003.

Elysian Park's [shooting range](/wiki/Shooting_range) was left intact for the LAPD to use. Sunset Fields Golf Club was renamed Brentwood Country Club in 1941 and is still in use as of 2010. All of the road courses were returned to public usage after the Olympics. The Olympic Auditorium continued to be of use for boxing and [roller derby](/wiki/Roller_derby) events until June 2005 when it was bought to be used as a [megachurch](/wiki/Megachurch). Los Angeles Harbor continues to be a major [sea port](/wiki/Sea_port) in the Western United States, employing 919,000 people and generating US$39.1 billion in annual wages and tax revenues as of 2007. The Riveria Country Club continues to host [golf](/wiki/Golf) events, hosting the [1948 U.S. Open](/wiki/1948_U.S._Open_(golf)) and the [PGA Championship](/wiki/PGA_Championship) in [1983](/wiki/1983_PGA_Championship) and [1995](/wiki/1995_PGA_Championship). The Swim Stadium was renovated in 2003 and continues to be in use as of 2010.

For the [1984 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics), the Coliseum and the Rose Bowl were used as [venues](/wiki/Venues_of_the_1984_Summer_Olympics). Both will become the first stadiums to have ever hosted three different Olympiads during the [2028 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2028_Summer_Olympics) in Los Angeles. Other venues that were used as Olympic venues in 1932 will also host Olympic events in 2028. Those venues include [Los Angeles Harbor](/wiki/Port_of_Los_Angeles), the [Long Beach Marine Stadium](/wiki/Long_Beach_Marine_Stadium) and the [Riviera Country Club](/wiki/Riviera_Country_Club).

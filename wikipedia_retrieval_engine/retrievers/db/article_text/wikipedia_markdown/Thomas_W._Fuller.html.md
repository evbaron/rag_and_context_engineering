# Thomas W. Fuller

## Infobox

- **Born:** May 3, 1865 Ottawa, Canada West
- **Died:** November 4, 1951 (1951-11-04) (aged 86) Ottawa, Ontario, Canada
- **Occupation:** Architect
- **Practice:** Chief Dominion Architect

**Thomas William Fuller** (May 3, 1865 – November 4, 1951), the son of [Thomas Fuller](/wiki/Thomas_Fuller_(architect)), was a [Canadian](/wiki/Canadians) architect. Before his selection as Dominion Architect, Fuller designed a number of federal buildings in [Dawson City, Yukon](/wiki/Dawson_City,_Yukon), some of which are now designated as [National Historic Sites of Canada](/wiki/National_Historic_Sites_of_Canada). These include the Post Office (1899); Court House (1900–01); Territorial Administration Building, 5th Avenue (1901); Public School (1901) which burned 1957; and Commissioner's Residence (1901).

He served as [Chief Dominion Architect](/wiki/Chief_Dominion_Architect) from 1927 to 1936, designing a number of prominent public buildings in Canada. Thomas W. Fuller designed a number of post offices:

- [Outremont, Quebec](/wiki/Outremont,_Quebec), Bernard Avenue, (1928–29); [Hespeler, Ontario](/wiki/Hespeler,_Ontario) Queen Street East, (1928); [Fort Frances, Ontario](/wiki/Fort_Frances,_Ontario) (1929); [Saskatoon, Saskatchewan](/wiki/Saskatoon,_Saskatchewan), 1st Avenue, 1929; [Moncton, New Brunswick](/wiki/Moncton,_New_Brunswick) (1931); [Penetanguishene, Ontario](/wiki/Penetanguishene,_Ontario) (1931); [Perth, Ontario](/wiki/Perth,_Ontario) (1933); [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec), St. James Street (1932); [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec) Notre-Dame-de-Grace Sherbrooke Street East (1934–35); [Fort William, Ontario](/wiki/Fort_William,_Ontario), Syndicate Avenue near Donald Street (1934); [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), [Quebec](/wiki/Quebec), Central Post Office, Peel Street, (1935); [Amherst, Nova Scotia](/wiki/Amherst,_Nova_Scotia) (1935).

His son, [Thomas G. Fuller](/wiki/Thomas_G._Fuller), founded Thomas Fuller Construction company in 1958 which built many public buildings in [Ottawa](/wiki/Ottawa) as well as the sheltered harbour for the [Britannia Yacht Club](/wiki/Britannia_Yacht_Club). In 2002, the company was awarded a contract to renovate the Canadian [Library of Parliament](/wiki/Library_of_Parliament).

**Judith Marcia Blume** ([née](/wiki/N%C3%A9e) **Sussman**; born February 12, 1938) is an American writer of [children's](/wiki/Chapter_books), [young adult](/wiki/Young_adult_fiction), and adult fiction. She began writing in 1959 and has published more than 26 novels. Among her best-known works are *[Superfudge](/wiki/Superfudge)* (1980), *[Are You There God? It's Me, Margaret.](/wiki/Are_You_There_God%3F_It%27s_Me,_Margaret.)* (1970), *[Tales of a Fourth Grade Nothing](/wiki/Tales_of_a_Fourth_Grade_Nothing)* (1972), *[Deenie](/wiki/Deenie)* (1973), *[Blubber](/wiki/Blubber_(novel))* (1974) and *[Double Fudge](/wiki/Double_Fudge)* (2002). Blume's books have significantly contributed to children's and young adult literature. She was named one of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100) by *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* magazine in 2023.

Blume was born and raised in [Elizabeth, New Jersey](/wiki/Elizabeth,_New_Jersey), and graduated from [New York University](/wiki/New_York_University) in 1961. Seeking to explore a creative outlet that would complement her role as a homemaker, Blume began writing stories. Blume was one of the first young adult authors to write novels focused on such controversial topics as [masturbation](/wiki/Masturbation), [menstruation](/wiki/Menstruation), [teen sex](/wiki/Teen_sex), [birth control](/wiki/Birth_control), and [death](/wiki/Death). Her novels have sold over 82 million copies and have been translated into 32 languages.

Blume's novels have been praised for teaching children and young adults about their bodies. However, in the United States, the topics of her works have generated criticism, controversy and she is one of the [most frequently banned authors in US schools and libraries](/wiki/List_of_most_commonly_challenged_books_in_the_United_States). There have been several film adaptations of Blume's novels, including *[Tiger Eyes](/wiki/Tiger_Eyes_(film))*, released in 2012 with [Willa Holland](/wiki/Willa_Holland) starring as Davey, and *[Are You There God? It's Me, Margaret.](/wiki/Are_You_There_God%3F_It%27s_Me,_Margaret._(film))*, released in 2023. A large collection of her papers is held at the [Beinecke Rare Book & Manuscript Library](/wiki/Beinecke_Rare_Book_%26_Manuscript_Library) at [Yale University](/wiki/Yale_University).

## Biography

### Early life

Blume was born Judith Marcia Sussman on February 12, 1938, and raised in [Elizabeth](/wiki/Elizabeth,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](/wiki/New_Jersey) in a Jewish family. She was the daughter of homemaker Esther Sussman (née Rosenfeld) and dentist Rudolph Sussman. She has an older brother, David. Blume described her upbringing as culturally rather than religiously Jewish.

Blume witnessed hardships and death throughout her childhood that influenced her writing. When she was in third grade, Blume's older brother had a kidney infection that led Blume, her brother, and her mother to temporarily move to Miami Beach to help him recover for two years.  Additionally, in 1951 and 1952, there were three airplane crashes in her hometown of Elizabeth ([1951 Miami Airlines C-46 crash](/wiki/1951_Miami_Airlines_C-46_crash), [American Airlines Flight 6780](/wiki/American_Airlines_Flight_6780), and [National Airlines Flight 101](/wiki/National_Airlines_Flight_101)). A total of 121 people died in these crashes, and Blume's father, who was a dentist, helped to identify the unrecognizable remains. Blume says that she "buried" these memories until she began writing her 2015 novel *[In the Unlikely Event](/wiki/In_the_Unlikely_Event_(novel))*, the plot of which revolves around the crashes.

Throughout her childhood, Blume was actively involved in many creative activities such as dance and piano. Blume has recalled spending much of her childhood creating stories in her head, and  despite the love of stories, she did not dream of being a writer.

She graduated from the all-girls' [Battin High School](/wiki/Battin_High_School) in 1956 and enrolled in [Boston University](/wiki/Boston_University). However she took a leave of absence after being diagnosed with [mononucleosis](/wiki/Mononucleosis). She later transferred to [New York University](/wiki/New_York_University), where she studied early education. In 1959, Blume's father died suddenly of a heart attack. Shortly after her father's death, on August 15, 1959, she married lawyer John M. Blume, whom she had met through college friends. Blume graduated from [New York University](/wiki/New_York_University) in 1961 with a [bachelor's degree](/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree) in early education.

### Adult life

After returning to [New Jersey](/wiki/New_Jersey), Blume gave birth to her daughter, Randy Lee Blume in 1961 and became a homemaker. She gave birth to her son Lawrence Andrew Blume in 1963. As her children grew older she began pursuing creative outlets, initially with felt art, some of which she sold to [Bloomingdale's](/wiki/Bloomingdale%27s) in New York City, before turning to writing.

Blume divorced her first husband, John M. Blume, in 1975. Shortly after in 1976 she married physicist Thomas A. Kitchens. The couple married in 1976, and moved to [London](/wiki/London) and then [Los Alamos, New Mexico](/wiki/Los_Alamos,_New_Mexico) for two years for Kitchens' work. They got divorced in 1978.

In 1987 Blume met George Cooper, a [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University) law professor turned non-fiction writer. They were married in 1987, and Blume became a stepmother to his daughter, Amanda.

In August 2012, Blume announced that she was diagnosed with [breast cancer](/wiki/Breast_cancer). Six weeks after her diagnosis, Blume underwent a [mastectomy](/wiki/Mastectomy) and breast reconstruction. Blume was cancer-free following this surgery and able to recover.

Her daughter, Randy Blume became a therapist with a sub-specialty in helping writers complete their works. She has one child, Elliot Kephart, who is credited with encouraging his grandmother, Judy Blume, to write the most recent "Fudge" books. Lawrence Blume, is now a movie director, producer, and writer. As of 2021, Cooper and Blume resided in [Key West](/wiki/Key_West), where they co-own a bookstore located in the arts center within [The Studios of Key West](/wiki/The_Studios_of_Key_West).

### Career

Blume first began writing through while taking courses at New York University  Following two years of publisher rejections, Blume published her first book, *[The One in the Middle Is the Green Kangaroo](/wiki/The_One_in_the_Middle_Is_the_Green_Kangaroo)*, in 1969. followed by her second book, *[Iggie's House](/wiki/Iggie%27s_House)* (1970), which was adapted from a story she wrote for *Trailblazer* magazine.  The decade that followed proved to be her most prolific, with 13 more books being published. Her third book, *[Are You There God? It's Me, Margaret.](/wiki/Are_You_There_God%3F_It%27s_Me,_Margaret.)* (1970), became a commercial success and established her as a prominent author in both children's and young adult literature. Some of Blume's other novels during the decade include *[Tales of a Fourth Grade Nothing](/wiki/Tales_of_a_Fourth_Grade_Nothing)* (1972), *[Otherwise Known as Sheila the Great](/wiki/Otherwise_Known_as_Sheila_the_Great)* (1972), and *[Blubber](/wiki/Blubber_(novel))* (1974).

In 1975, Blume published *[Forever](/wiki/Forever..._(novel))*, which displayed teen sex as normal. The book has been widely read and challenged in schools and libraries. Blume has expressed that she writes about subjects such as sexuality, bullying, family relationships, and body image, because it is what she believes children need to know about and were things she had wondered as a child. All of which has contributed to both her popularity as well as controversy.

In addition to writing for younger audiences, Blume published works for adults, including  *[Wifey](/wiki/Wifey_(novel))* (1978) and *[Smart Women](/wiki/Smart_Women)* (1983) which reached the top of [*The New York Times* Best Seller list](/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_list). Blume's third adult novel, *[Summer Sisters](/wiki/Summer_Sisters)* (1998), was widely praised and sold more than three million copies. Despite its popularity, *Summer Sisters* (1998) faced a lot of criticism for its sexual content and inclusion of homosexual themes. Several of Blume's books appear on the list of top all-time bestselling children's books. As of 2020, her books have sold over 82 million copies and they have been translated into 32 languages.

As well as writing, Blume has been an activist against the banning of books in the United States. In the 1980s, when her books started facing censorship and controversy, she began reaching out to other writers, as well as teachers and librarians, to join the fight against censorship. This led Blume to join the [National Coalition Against Censorship](/wiki/National_Coalition_Against_Censorship) which aims to protect the freedom to read. As of 2020, Blume is still a board member for the National Coalition Against Censorship. She is also the founder and trustee of The Kids Fund, a charitable and educational foundation. Blume serves on the board for other organizations such as, the [Authors Guild](/wiki/Authors_Guild); the [Society of Children's Book Writers and Illustrators](/wiki/Society_of_Children%27s_Book_Writers_and_Illustrators); the [Key West Literary Seminar](/wiki/Key_West_Literary_Seminar); and the National Coalition Against Censorship."

## Reception

Blume's novels have been read by millions and have flourished throughout generations. The element in her work readers are said to love most is Blume's openness and honesty regarding issues like divorce, sexuality, puberty, and bullying. Her first-person narrative writing has gained positive appraisal for its relatability and its ability to discuss difficult subjects without judgment or harshness. Following the publishing of *[Are You There God? It's Me, Margaret.](/wiki/Are_You_There_God%3F_It%27s_Me,_Margaret.)* (1970), Blume received many letters from young girls telling her how much they loved the book and identified with Margaret. Female novelists have praised Blume for her “taboo-trampling” literature that left readers feeling like they learned something about their bodies from reading her books. For example, *Deenie* (1973) explained masturbation and *Forever* (1975) taught young women about losing their virginity. Blume's children's books have also been praised for their delicate way of portraying the hardships that kids can face at a young age. *It’s Not the End of the World* (1972) helped many kids understand divorce and the Fudge book series explored the various aspects of loving siblings despite the rivalry.

Blume's novels have received much criticism and controversy. Parents, librarians, book critics, and political groups have wanted her books to be banned. When her first books were published in the 1970s, Blume has recalled facing little censorship. Since 1980, Blume's novels have been a central topic of controversy in young adult literature. Critics of Blume's novels say that she places too much emphasis on the physical and sexual sides of growing up, ignoring the development of morals and emotional maturity. Five of Blume's books were included in the [American Library Association](/wiki/American_Library_Association) (ALA) list of the top 100 most banned books of the 1990s, with *Forever* (1975) in seventh place. *Forever* is censored for its inclusion of teen sex and birth control. Blume recalls that the principal of her children's elementary school would not put *Are You There God? It’s Me, Margaret* in the library because the story involves menstruation. Conservative and religious groups continuously attempt to ban *Are You There God? It’s Me, Margaret* for the novel's portrayal of a young girl going through puberty claiming that it violates certain religious views. Blume's children's novels have also been criticized for these reasons, especially *Blubber* (1974), which many believed sent the message to readers that kids could do wrong and not face punishment.

## Awards and honors

Judy Blume has won more than 90 literary awards, including three lifetime achievement awards in the United States. In 1994, she received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement). The ALA [Margaret A. Edwards Award](/wiki/Margaret_A._Edwards_Award) recognizes one author who has made significant contributions to young adult literature. Blume won the annual award in 1996 and the ALA considered her book [*Forever,*](/wiki/Forever..._(novel)) published in 1975, was groundbreaking for its honest portrayal of high school seniors in love for the first time. In April 2000, the [Library of Congress](/wiki/Library_of_Congress) named her to its *[Living Legends](/wiki/Library_of_Congress_Living_Legend)* in the Writers and Artists category for her significant contributions to America's cultural heritage. Blume received an honorary doctor of arts degree from [Mount Holyoke College](/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College) and was the main speaker at their annual commencement ceremony in 2003. In 2004 she received the annual [Distinguished Contribution to American Letters](/wiki/Distinguished_Contribution_to_American_Letters) Medal of the [National Book Foundation](/wiki/National_Book_Foundation) for her enrichment of American literary heritage. In 2009, the [National Coalition Against Censorship (NCAC)](/wiki/National_Coalition_Against_Censorship) honored Blume for her lifelong commitment to free speech and her courage to battle censorship in literature. Blume also received the 2017 [E.B. White](/wiki/E.B._White) Award from the [American Academy of Arts and Letters](/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Letters) for lifetime achievement in children's literature. In 2020, Blume was named an Honoree for Distinguished Service to the Literary Community by the [Authors Guild Foundation](/wiki/Authors_Guild).

## Media adaptations

The first media adaptation of Blume's novels was the production of a [TV film](/wiki/Forever_(1978_film)) based on Blume's novel *Forever* that premiered on CBS in 1978. *Forever* is the story of two teenagers in high school, Katherine Danziger and Michael Wagner, who fall in love for the first time. The film starred [Stephanie Zimbalist](/wiki/Stephanie_Zimbalist) as Katherine Danziger and [Dean Butler](/wiki/Dean_Butler_(actor)) as Michael Wagner. A decade later, in 1988, Blume and her son wrote and executive produced a small film adaptation of *Otherwise Known as Sheila the Great*. The film was later shown on [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company). In 1995, a Fudge TV series was produced based on Blume's novel *Fudge-a-Mania.* The show ran from 1995 to 1997 with the first season aired on ABC and the second on [CBS](/wiki/CBS). The series starred [Jake Richardson](/wiki/Jake_Richardson) as [Peter Warren Hatcher](/wiki/Peter_Warren_Hatcher), the storyteller, and [Luke Tarsitano](/wiki/Luke_Tarsitano) as [Farley Drexel "Fudge" Hatcher](/wiki/Farley_Drexel_%22Fudge%22_Hatcher).

In 2012, Blume's 1981 novel *[Tiger Eyes](/wiki/Tiger_Eyes)* was adapted into [a film version](/wiki/Tiger_Eyes_(film)). This was the first of Blume's novels to be turned into a theatrical feature film. *Tiger Eyes* is the story of a teenage girl, Davey, who struggles to cope with the sudden death of her father, Adam Wexler. The screenplay was co-written by Blume and her son, Lawrence Blume, who was also the director. *Tiger Eyes* stars [Willa Holland](/wiki/Willa_Holland) as Davey and [Amy Jo Johnson](/wiki/Amy_Jo_Johnson) as Gwen Wexler.

Blume is the subject of the 2018 song "Judy Blume" by [Amanda Palmer](/wiki/Amanda_Palmer). Thematically, the song explains to the listener Blume's role in Palmer's adolescent life. The song explains Blume's books as influential in Palmer's understanding of intimate and female-centered subjects such as [puberty](/wiki/Puberty), [menstruation](/wiki/Menstruation), and the [male gaze](/wiki/Male_gaze), and universal subjects like [molestation](/wiki/Molestation), [eating disorders](/wiki/Eating_disorders), [poverty](/wiki/Poverty), [grief](/wiki/Grief), and parental [divorce](/wiki/Divorce).

Blume is the subject of the [Peabody Award](/wiki/Peabody_Award)-winning documentary *[Judy Blume Forever](/wiki/Judy_Blume_Forever)*, which premiered at the [2023 Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/2023_Sundance_Film_Festival).

*[Are You There God? It's Me, Margaret.](/wiki/Are_You_There_God%3F_It%27s_Me,_Margaret._(film))* was released as a feature film, directed by [Kelly Fremon Craig](/wiki/Kelly_Fremon_Craig), in 2023.

In 2025, Blume's novel [*Forever*](/wiki/Forever..._(novel)) was adapted into [a Netflix series show](/wiki/Forever_(2025_TV_series)).

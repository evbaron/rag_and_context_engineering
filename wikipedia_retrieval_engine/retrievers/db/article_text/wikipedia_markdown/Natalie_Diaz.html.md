**Natalie Diaz** (born September 4, 1978) is a [Pulitzer Prize](/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize)-winning [Mojave](/wiki/Mohave_people) [American](/wiki/United_States) [poet](/wiki/Poet), language activist, former professional basketball player, and educator. She is enrolled in the [Gila River Indian Community](/wiki/Gila_River_Indian_Community) and identifies as [Akimel O'odham](/wiki/Akimel_O%27odham). She is currently an associate professor at [Arizona State University](/wiki/Arizona_State_University).

## Early life

Natalie Diaz was born in [Needles](/wiki/Needles,_California), [California](/wiki/California), on September 4, 1978. She grew up in the [Fort Mojave](/wiki/Fort_Mojave_Indian_Reservation) Indian Village in Needles, California, on the border of California, [Arizona](/wiki/Arizona), and [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada). She attended [Old Dominion University](/wiki/Old_Dominion_University), where she played point guard on the [women's basketball team](/wiki/Old_Dominion_Monarchs_women%27s_basketball), reaching the [NCAA Final Four](/wiki/1997_NCAA_Division_I_women%27s_basketball_tournament) as a freshman and the bracket of sixteen her other three years. She earned a bachelor's degree. After playing professional basketball in Europe and Asia, she returned to Old Dominion University, and completed an MFA in poetry and fiction, in 2006.

## Career

Her work appeared in *Narrative*, *[Poetry](/wiki/Poetry_(magazine)) magazine*, *Drunken Boat*, *[Prairie Schooner](/wiki/Prairie_Schooner)*, *[Iowa Review](/wiki/Iowa_Review)*, and *Crab Orchard Review*.

Diaz's debut book of poetry, *When My Brother Was an Aztec*, "portrays experiences rooted in Native American life with personal and mythic power." It was a 2012 Lannan Literary Selection, was shortlisted for the 2013 [PEN/Open Book Award](/wiki/PEN/Open_Book_Award), and was a 2013 [American Book Award](/wiki/American_Book_Awards) winner. One important focus of the book is a sister struggling with her brother's addiction to crystal meth.

In 2012, she was interviewed about her poetry and language rehabilitation work on the *[PBS NewsHour](/wiki/PBS_NewsHour)*.

In 2018, she was named as the Maxine and Jonathan Marshall Chair in Modern and Contemporary Poetry at Arizona State University.

In 2019, she was faculty at the [CantoMundo](/wiki/CantoMundo) Retreat.

In 2021, her book *Postcolonial Love Poem* won the [Pulitzer Prize for Poetry](/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Poetry). It was called "a collection of tender, heart-wrenching and defiant poems that explore what it means to love and be loved in an America beset by conflict." The book was also a finalist for the 2020 National Book Award, a finalist for the 2020 Los Angeles Times Book Prize, a finalist for the 2020 Forward Prize for Best Collection, and shortlisted for the 2020 T. S. Eliot Prize.

## Personal life

Diaz currently lives in [Mohave Valley, Arizona](/wiki/Mohave_Valley,_Arizona), where she used to work on language revitalization at [Fort Mojave](/wiki/Fort_Mojave_Indian_Reservation), her home reservation. She worked with the last Elder speakers of the Mojave language. She is enrolled as member of the Gila Indian Community.

# Landlocked country

"Landlocked" redirects here. For other uses, see [Landlocked (disambiguation)](/wiki/Landlocked_(disambiguation)).

Two doubly landlocked countries

42 landlocked countries

A **landlocked country** is a country that has no coastlines on the world [ocean](/wiki/Ocean). As of 2026, there are 44 landlocked countries, two of them [doubly landlocked](#Doubly_landlocked) due to being surrounded by other landlocked nations ([Liechtenstein](/wiki/Liechtenstein) and [Uzbekistan](/wiki/Uzbekistan)), and three landlocked [de facto states](/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) in the world, [South Ossetia](/wiki/South_Ossetia), [Kosovo](/wiki/Kosovo) and [Transnistria](/wiki/Transnistria). [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan) is the world's largest landlocked country by area, [Kyrgyzstan](/wiki/Kyrgyzstan) is the farthest landlocked country from any ocean, and [Ethiopia](/wiki/Ethiopia) is the world's most populous landlocked country.

Generally, being landlocked creates political and economic disadvantages that having access to [international waters](/wiki/International_waters) would avoid. For this reason, nations large and small throughout history have fought to gain access to open waters, even at great expense in wealth, bloodshed, and [political capital](/wiki/Political_capital).

The economic disadvantages of being landlocked can be alleviated or aggravated depending on degree of development, surrounding [trade routes](/wiki/Trade_route) and [freedom of trade](/wiki/Free_trade), commonality of language, and other considerations. Some landlocked countries in [Europe](/wiki/Europe) are affluent, such as [Andorra](/wiki/Andorra), [Austria](/wiki/Austria), [Liechtenstein](/wiki/Liechtenstein), [Luxembourg](/wiki/Luxembourg), [San Marino](/wiki/San_Marino), [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland), and [Vatican City](/wiki/Vatican_City). With the exception of Luxembourg (a founding member of [NATO](/wiki/NATO)), these nations frequently practice [neutrality](/wiki/Neutral_country) in global political issues.

However, 32 out of the 44 landlocked countries, including those in [Africa](/wiki/Africa), [Asia](/wiki/Asia), and [South America](/wiki/South_America), have been classified as [Landlocked Developing Countries](/wiki/Landlocked_developing_countries) (LLDCs) by the [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations). Nine of the twelve countries with the lowest [Human Development Index](/wiki/Human_Development_Index) rankings are landlocked. International initiatives are aimed at reducing inequalities resulting from issues such as these, such as the United Nations [Sustainable Development Goal 10](/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_10), which aims to reduce inequality substantially by 2030.

## History

In 1990, there were only 30 landlocked countries in the world. However, the [dissolutions](/wiki/Dissolution_(politics)) of the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) and [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia); the [breakup of Yugoslavia](/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia); the [independence referendums](/wiki/Independence_referendum) of [South Ossetia](/wiki/1992_South_Ossetian_independence_referendum) (de facto state), [Eritrea](/wiki/1993_Eritrean_independence_referendum), [Montenegro](/wiki/2006_Montenegrin_independence_referendum), [South Sudan](/wiki/2011_South_Sudanese_independence_referendum), and the [Luhansk People's Republic](/wiki/2014_Donbas_status_referendums#Referendum_in_Luhansk_Oblast) (de facto state); and the [unilateral declaration of independence](/wiki/Unilateral_declaration_of_independence) of [Kosovo](/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence) (de facto state) created 15 new landlocked countries and five landlocked de facto states while the former landlocked country of [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia) ceased to exist on 1 January 1993.

On 19 September 2023, [Azerbaijan](/wiki/Azerbaijan) launched a new offensive against the [Republic of Artsakh](/wiki/Republic_of_Artsakh) (de facto state) and achieved a decisive victory. The [Government of Artsakh](/wiki/Government_of_Artsakh) was officially dissolved on 1 January 2024. As a result, Artsakh ceased to exist as a landlocked de facto state and the [Nagorno-Karabakh](/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh) region was reintegrated into Azerbaijan.

As of 1 April 2024, there were 44 landlocked countries and three landlocked [de facto states](/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) ([Kosovo](/wiki/Kosovo), [South Ossetia](/wiki/South_Ossetia), and [Transnistria](/wiki/Transnistria)) in the world.

## Significance

[Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia)'s loss of its coastline in the [War of the Pacific](/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) (1879–1884) remains a major political issue

Historically, being landlocked has been disadvantageous to a country's development. It cuts a nation off from important sea resources such as [fishing](/wiki/Fishing), and impedes or prevents direct access to [maritime trade](/wiki/Maritime_history), a crucial component of economic and social advance. As such, coastal regions, or inland regions that have access to the World Ocean, tended to be wealthier and more heavily populated than inland regions that have no access to the World Ocean. [Paul Collier](/wiki/Paul_Collier) in his book *[The Bottom Billion](/wiki/The_Bottom_Billion)* argues that being landlocked in a poor geographical neighbourhood is one of four major development "traps" by which a country can be held back. In general, he found that when a neighbouring country experiences better growth, it tends to spill over into favorable development for the country itself. For landlocked countries, the effect is particularly strong, as they are limited in their trading activity with the rest of the world. He states, "If you are coastal, you serve the world; if you are landlocked, you serve your neighbors." Others have argued that being landlocked has an advantage as it creates a "natural tariff barrier" that protects the country from cheap imports. In some instances, this has led to more robust local food systems.

Landlocked [developing countries](/wiki/Developing_country) have significantly higher costs of international cargo transportation compared to coastal developing countries (in Asia the ratio is 3:1).

Historically, traveling between a landlocked country and a country which did not border said country required the traveler to pass border controls twice or more. In recent times the advent of [air travel](/wiki/Air_travel) has largely negated this impediment.

### Actions to avoid being landlocked

Countries have acted to overcome being landlocked by acquiring land that reaches the sea:

- The [Republic of Ragusa](/wiki/Republic_of_Ragusa), in 1699, gave the town of [Neum](/wiki/Neum) to the [Ottoman Empire](/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) because it did not want to have a land border with the [Republic of Venice](/wiki/Republic_of_Venice). This small municipality was inherited by [Bosnia and Herzegovina](/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and now provides limited sea access, splitting the [Croatian](/wiki/Croatia) part of the Adriatic coast in two. Since Bosnia and Herzegovina is a new country, railways and ports have not been built for its need. There is no freight port along its short coastline at [Neum](/wiki/Neum), making it effectively landlocked, although there are plans to change this. Instead, the [Port of Ploče](/wiki/Port_of_Plo%C4%8De) in Croatia is used.
- The [International Congo Society](/wiki/International_Congo_Society), which owned the territory now constituting the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), was awarded a narrow piece of land cutting through [Angola](/wiki/Angola) to connect it to the sea by the [Conference of Berlin](/wiki/Conference_of_Berlin) in 1885.
- After [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I), in the [Treaty of Versailles](/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), a part of [Germany](/wiki/Germany) designated "the [Polish corridor](/wiki/Polish_corridor)" was given to the new [Second Polish Republic](/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic), for access to the [Baltic Sea](/wiki/Baltic_Sea). This gave [Poland](/wiki/Poland) a short coastline, but without a large harbour. This was also the pretext for making Danzig (now [Gdańsk](/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk)) with its harbour the [Free City of Danzig](/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig), to which Poland was given free access. However, the Germans placed obstacles to this free access, especially when it came to military material. In response, the small fishing harbour of [Gdynia](/wiki/Gdynia) was soon greatly enlarged.
- As a result of a 2005 territorial exchange with [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine), [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova) received a 600-metre (650-yard) long bank of the [Danube](/wiki/Danube) (which is an [international waterway](/wiki/International_waters)), subsequently building its [Port of Giurgiulești](/wiki/Port_of_Giurgiule%C8%99ti) there.

### Trade agreements

Countries can establish agreements to provide for free transport of goods through neighbouring countries:

- The [Treaty of Versailles](/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) required Germany to offer [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia) a lease for 99 years of parts of the ports in [Hamburg](/wiki/Hamburg) and [Stettin](/wiki/Szczecin), allowing Czechoslovakia sea trade via the [Elbe](/wiki/Elbe) and [Oder](/wiki/Oder) rivers. Stettin was annexed by [Poland](/wiki/Poland) after [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), but [Hamburg](/wiki/Hamburg) continued the [contract](/wiki/Contract) so that part of the port (now called [Moldauhafen](/wiki/Moldauhafen)) until 2028 could be used for sea trade by a successor of Czechoslovakia, the [Czech Republic](/wiki/Czech_Republic).
- The [Danube](/wiki/Danube) is [an international waterway](/wiki/Internationalization_of_the_Danube_River), and thus landlocked [Austria](/wiki/Austria), [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary), [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova), [Serbia](/wiki/Serbia) and [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovakia) have secure access to the [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea). However, oceangoing ships cannot use the Danube, so cargo must be transloaded anyway, and many overseas imports into Austria and Hungary use land transport from Atlantic and Mediterranean ports. A similar situation exists for the [Rhine](/wiki/Rhine) river where Switzerland has boat access, but not oceangoing ships. [Luxembourg](/wiki/Luxembourg) has such through the [Moselle](/wiki/Moselle_(river)), but [Liechtenstein](/wiki/Liechtenstein) has no boat access, even though it is located along the Rhine, as the Rhine is not navigable that far upstream.
- The [Mekong](/wiki/Mekong) is an international waterway so that landlocked [Laos](/wiki/Laos) has access to the [South China Sea](/wiki/South_China_Sea) (since Laos became independent from [French Indochina](/wiki/French_Indochina)). However, it is not navigable above the [Khone Phapheng Falls](/wiki/Khone_Phapheng_Falls).
- [Free ports](/wiki/Free_economic_zone) allow transshipment to short-distance ships or river vessels.
- The [TIR Convention](/wiki/TIR_Convention) allows sealed road transport without customs checks and charges, mostly in Europe.

### Political repercussions

Losing access to the sea is generally a great loss to a nation, politically, militarily, and economically. The following are examples of countries becoming landlocked.

- The independence of [Eritrea](/wiki/Eritrea), brought about by the 30-year [Eritrean War of Independence](/wiki/Eritrean_War_of_Independence), caused [Ethiopia](/wiki/Ethiopia) to become landlocked in 1991 (*de facto*)/1993 (*de jure*): the [Ethiopian Navy](/wiki/Ethiopian_Navy) operated from foreign ports in [Djibouti](/wiki/Djibouti) and [Yemen](/wiki/Yemen) before being dissolved in 1996.
- [Montenegro](/wiki/Montenegro)'s [decision](/wiki/2006_Montenegrin_independence_referendum) to abandon the [State Union of Serbia and Montenegro](/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro) caused the [federal unit of Serbia](/wiki/Republic_of_Serbia_(1992%E2%80%932006)) to become a landlocked [current independent state](/wiki/Serbia).
- [Bolivia](/wiki/Geography_of_Bolivia) lost its coastline to [Chile](/wiki/Chile) in the [War of the Pacific](/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) and accepted it in treaties signed in 1884 and 1904. The last treaty gives port storage facilities and special treatment for the transit of goods from and to Bolivia through Chilean ports and territory. Peru and Argentina have also given special treatment for the transit of goods. A fluvial [Bolivian Navy](/wiki/Bolivian_Navy), which did not exist at the time of the [War of the Pacific](/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific), was created later and both trains and operates in [Lake Titicaca](/wiki/Lake_Titicaca) and rivers. The Bolivian people annually celebrate a patriotic "[Dia del Mar](/wiki/Dia_del_Mar)" (Day of the Sea) to remember its territorial loss, which included both the coastal city of [Antofagasta](/wiki/Antofagasta) and what has proven to be one of the most significant and lucrative copper deposits in the world. Early in the 21st century, the selection of the route of gas pipes from Bolivia to the sea fueled [popular uprisings](/wiki/Bolivian_gas_conflict#Dispute_over_pipeline_route), as people were against the option of laying the pipes through Chilean territory.
- Austria and Hungary also lost their access to the sea as a consequence of the [Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye (1919)](/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_(1919)) and the [Treaty of Trianon](/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon) (1920) respectively. Previously, although [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia) had a limited constitutional [autonomy](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/autonomy) within the [Kingdom of Hungary](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), the City of [Fiume](/wiki/Fiume)/[Rijeka](/wiki/Rijeka) on the Croatian coast was governed directly from [Budapest](/wiki/Budapest) by an appointed governor as a *[corpus separatum](/wiki/Corpus_separatum_(Fiume))*, to provide Hungary with its only international [port](/wiki/Port) in the periods 1779–1813, 1822–1848 and 1868–1918. The most important ports in Austria were [Trieste](/wiki/Trieste) and [Pula](/wiki/Pula), now in [Italy](/wiki/Italy) and Croatia.
- In 1801, the [Nizam](/wiki/Nizam)'s dominion of [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State) assumed its current borders as a landlocked [princely state](/wiki/Princely_state) with territories in central Deccan, bounded on all sides by [British India](/wiki/British_India). For the preceding 150 years it had had a [considerable coastline](/wiki/Northern_Circars) on the [Bay of Bengal](/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal) that was annexed by the British.
- It is possible that one of the causes of the [Paraguayan War](/wiki/Paraguayan_War) was [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay)'s lack of direct ocean access (although this is disputed; see the linked article).
- When the [Entente Powers](/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) divided the former [Ottoman Empire](/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) under the [Treaty of Sèvres](/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) at the close of [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I), [Armenia](/wiki/Armenia) was promised part of the Trebizond vilayet (roughly corresponding to the modern [Trabzon](/wiki/Trabzon_Province) and [Rize](/wiki/Rize_Province) provinces in [Turkey](/wiki/Turkey)). This would have given Armenia access to the [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea). However, the Sèvres treaty collapsed with the [Turkish War of Independence](/wiki/Turkish_War_of_Independence) and was superseded by the [Treaty of Lausanne (1923)](/wiki/Treaty_of_Lausanne_(1923)), which firmly established Turkish rule over the area.
- In 2011, [South Sudan](/wiki/South_Sudan) broke off from the [Sudan](/wiki/Sudan), causing the former to become landlocked. There still remains conflict over the oil fields in South Sudan between the two countries.

The [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_on_the_Law_of_the_Sea) now gives a landlocked country a right of access to and from the sea without taxation of traffic through transit states. The [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations) has a programme of action to assist landlocked developing countries, and the current responsible Undersecretary-General is [Anwarul Karim Chowdhury](/wiki/Anwarul_Karim_Chowdhury).

Some countries have a long coastline, but much of it may not be readily usable for trade and commerce. For instance, in its early history, [Russia](/wiki/Russia)'s only ports were on the [Arctic Ocean](/wiki/Arctic_Ocean) and frozen shut for much of the year. The wish to gain control of a [warm-water port](/wiki/Port#Warm-water_port) was a major motivator of Russian expansion towards the [Baltic Sea](/wiki/Baltic_Sea), [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea), and [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean). On the other hand, some landlocked countries can have access to the ocean along wide navigable rivers. For instance, Paraguay (and Bolivia to a lesser extent) have access to the ocean through the [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_River) and [Paraná](/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_River) rivers.

Several countries have coastlines on landlocked bodies of water, such as the [Caspian Sea](/wiki/Caspian_Sea) and the [Dead Sea](/wiki/Dead_Sea). Since these seas are in effect [lakes](/wiki/Lake) without access to wider seaborne trade, countries such as [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan) are still considered landlocked. Although the Caspian Sea is connected to the Black Sea via the man-made [Volga–Don Canal](/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal), large oceangoing ships are unable to traverse it.

Some countries or important parts of countries have coastlines or river ports reachable by oceangoing ships, but only through a strait or river part of the territory of another country. The other country can put restriction on passage. Between 1429 and 1857 [Poland](/wiki/Poland), [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden), [Russia](/wiki/Russia) and other Baltic countries were subjected to the [Sound Dues](/wiki/Sound_Dues), a toll needed to be paid to reach Western European waters. Sweden bypassed it by conquering [Scania](/wiki/Scania) in 1658.

The landlocked nature of a country can also hinder its ability to enforce international treaties. For example, [Mongolia](/wiki/Mongolia) did not enforce an [International Criminal Court arrest warrant](/wiki/International_Criminal_Court_arrest_warrants_for_Russian_leaders) issued at [Vladimir Putin](/wiki/Vladimir_Putin) during his state visit, despite its obligation to do so as an [International Criminal Court](/wiki/International_Criminal_Court) state party; Mongolia is landlocked and only borders Russia and China (both of whom are not ICC parties and are important economic partners with the country), which would make the delivery of Putin to the ICC headquarters in [The Hague](/wiki/The_Hague) after his hypothetical arrest extremely difficult.

## By degree

Landlocked countries may be bordered by a single country having direct access to the [high seas](/wiki/International_waters), two or more such countries, or be surrounded by other landlocked countries, making a country doubly landlocked.

### Landlocked by a single country

Three countries are landlocked by a single country ([enclaved](/wiki/Enclave_and_exclave) countries):

- [Lesotho](/wiki/Lesotho), a state surrounded by [South Africa](/wiki/South_Africa).
- [San Marino](/wiki/San_Marino), a state surrounded by [Italy](/wiki/Italy).
- [Vatican City](/wiki/Vatican_City), a [city-state](/wiki/City-state) surrounded by [Italy](/wiki/Italy), specifically [Rome](/wiki/Rome).

### Landlocked by two countries

Seven landlocked countries are surrounded by only two mutually bordering neighbours (semi-enclaved countries):

- [Andorra](/wiki/Andorra) (between [France](/wiki/France) and [Spain](/wiki/Spain))
- [Bhutan](/wiki/Bhutan) (between [China](/wiki/China) and [India](/wiki/India))
- [Eswatini](/wiki/Eswatini) (between [Mozambique](/wiki/Mozambique) and [South Africa](/wiki/South_Africa))
- [Liechtenstein](/wiki/Liechtenstein) (between [Austria](/wiki/Austria) and [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland)) – one of the only two "doubly landlocked countries" in the world
- [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova) (between [Romania](/wiki/Romania) and [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine)) – ignoring [Transnistria](/wiki/Transnistria), a de facto state
- [Mongolia](/wiki/Mongolia) (between [China](/wiki/China) and [Russia](/wiki/Russia))
- [Nepal](/wiki/Nepal) (between [China](/wiki/China) and [India](/wiki/India))

To this group could be added three landlocked territories, two of them *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* [states with limited or no international recognition](/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition):

- [Transnistria](/wiki/Transnistria) (between [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova) and [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine)) – de facto state
- [South Ossetia](/wiki/South_Ossetia) (between [Georgia](/wiki/Georgia_(country)) and [Russia](/wiki/Russia)) – de facto state
- [West Bank](/wiki/West_Bank) (between [Israel](/wiki/Israel) and [Jordan](/wiki/Jordan)) – [Israeli-occupied territory](/wiki/Israeli-occupied_territories#West_Bank) partly administered by the [State of Palestine](/wiki/State_of_Palestine)

### Doubly landlocked

A country is "doubly landlocked" or "double-landlocked" when it is surrounded entirely by landlocked countries (i.e. requiring the crossing of at least two national borders to reach a coastline). There are two such countries:

- [Liechtenstein](/wiki/Liechtenstein) in [Western Europe](/wiki/Western_Europe), surrounded by [Austria](/wiki/Austria) and [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland).
- [Uzbekistan](/wiki/Uzbekistan) in [Central Asia](/wiki/Central_Asia), surrounded by [Afghanistan](/wiki/Afghanistan), [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan), [Kyrgyzstan](/wiki/Kyrgyzstan), [Tajikistan](/wiki/Tajikistan), and [Turkmenistan](/wiki/Turkmenistan).

After the dissolution of the [Holy Roman Empire](/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), the [Kingdom of Württemberg](/wiki/Kingdom_of_W%C3%BCrttemberg) became a doubly landlocked state, bordering [Bavaria](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria), [Baden](/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden), [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland), the [Grand Duchy of Hesse](/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Hesse) ([Wimpfen](/wiki/Bad_Wimpfen) exclave), [Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen](/wiki/Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen), and [Hohenzollern-Hechingen](/wiki/Hohenzollern-Hechingen). The latter two were themselves landlocked between each other, Württemberg, and Baden. In 1866 they became an exclave of [Prussia](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia), giving Württemberg a border with a coastal country but any path to a coast would still lead across at least two borders. The [Free City of Frankfurt](/wiki/Free_City_of_Frankfurt) which was independent between 1815 and 1866 was doubly landlocked as it bordered the [Electorate of Hesse](/wiki/Electorate_of_Hesse), the [Grand Duchy of Hesse](/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Hesse), [Hesse-Homburg](/wiki/Hesse-Homburg), and [Nassau](/wiki/Duchy_of_Nassau). In the [German Confederation](/wiki/German_Confederation) there were several other landlocked states that only bordered landlocked states and landlocked exclaves of coastal states: the [Grand Duchy of Hesse](/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Hesse), [Hesse-Homburg](/wiki/Hesse-Homburg), [Nassau](/wiki/Duchy_of_Nassau) (all until 1866), [Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld](/wiki/Saxe-Coburg-Saalfeld), [Saxe-Hildburghausen](/wiki/Saxe-Hildburghausen) (both until 1826), and [Reuss, elder line](/wiki/Principality_of_Reuss-Greiz) (until 1871). All of these bordered Prussia but not the main territory with sea access.

There were no doubly landlocked countries from the [unification of Germany](/wiki/Unification_of_Germany) in 1871 until the end of [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I). Liechtenstein bordered the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire), which had an [Adriatic](/wiki/Adriatic_Sea) coastline, and Uzbekistan was then part of the [Russian Empire](/wiki/Russian_Empire), which had both ocean and sea access.

With the [dissolution of Austria-Hungary](/wiki/Dissolution_of_Austria-Hungary) in 1918 and creation of an independent, landlocked [Austria](/wiki/Austria), Liechtenstein became the sole doubly landlocked country until 1938. In the [Anschluss](/wiki/Anschluss) that year, Austria was absorbed into [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany), which possessed a border on the [Baltic Sea](/wiki/Baltic_Sea) and the [North Sea](/wiki/North_Sea). After World War II, Austria regained its independence and Liechtenstein once again became doubly landlocked.

Uzbekistan, which had been part of the [Russian Empire](/wiki/Russian_Empire) and then the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union), gained its independence with the [dissolution of the latter](/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991 and became the second doubly landlocked country.

However, Uzbekistan's doubly landlocked status depends on whether the [Caspian Sea](/wiki/Caspian_Sea) is considered a lake or a sea. In the latter case, Uzbekistan is not doubly landlocked, since its neighbors Turkmenistan and [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan) have access to the Caspian Sea.

### Groupings

The landlocked countries and [de facto states](/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) can be grouped in *contiguous* groups as follows:

- Eastern, Middle, and Western African cluster (10): [Burkina Faso](/wiki/Burkina_Faso), [Burundi](/wiki/Burundi), the [Central African Republic](/wiki/Central_African_Republic), [Chad](/wiki/Chad), [Ethiopia](/wiki/Ethiopia), [Mali](/wiki/Mali), [Niger](/wiki/Niger), [Rwanda](/wiki/Rwanda), [South Sudan](/wiki/South_Sudan), and [Uganda](/wiki/Uganda)
- Eastern, Southern, and Western European cluster (9): [Austria](/wiki/Austria), [Czechia](/wiki/Czech_Republic), [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary), [Kosovo](/wiki/Kosovo), [Liechtenstein](/wiki/Liechtenstein), [North Macedonia](/wiki/North_Macedonia), [Serbia](/wiki/Serbia), [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovakia), and [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland)
- Central and Southern Asian cluster (6): [Afghanistan](/wiki/Afghanistan), [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan), [Kyrgyzstan](/wiki/Kyrgyzstan), [Tajikistan](/wiki/Tajikistan), [Turkmenistan](/wiki/Turkmenistan), and [Uzbekistan](/wiki/Uzbekistan)
- Eastern and Southern African cluster (4): [Botswana](/wiki/Botswana), [Malawi](/wiki/Malawi), [Zambia](/wiki/Zambia), and [Zimbabwe](/wiki/Zimbabwe)
- Eastern European group (2): [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova) and [Transnistria](/wiki/Transnistria) (de facto state)
- South American group (2): [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia) and [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay)
- Western Asian group (2): [Armenia](/wiki/Armenia) and [Azerbaijan](/wiki/Azerbaijan)

The United Nations created a dedicated work group to implement working programs for the Least Developed Countries, Landlocked Developing Countries and Small Island Developing States.

### "Single" landlocked countries

There are the following 12 "single" landlocked countries (each of them borders no other landlocked country):

- Asia (4): [Bhutan](/wiki/Bhutan), [Laos](/wiki/Laos), [Mongolia](/wiki/Mongolia), and [Nepal](/wiki/Nepal)
- Europe (6): [Andorra](/wiki/Andorra), [Belarus](/wiki/Belarus), [Luxembourg](/wiki/Luxembourg), [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova), [San Marino](/wiki/San_Marino), and [Vatican City](/wiki/Vatican_City) (the [Holy See](/wiki/Holy_See))
- Africa (2): [Eswatini](/wiki/Eswatini) and [Lesotho](/wiki/Lesotho)

## Landlocked countries by continent

According to the [United Nations geoscheme](/wiki/United_Nations_geoscheme) (excluding the [de facto states](/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition)), [Africa](/wiki/Africa) has the most landlocked countries, at 16, followed by [Europe](/wiki/Europe) (14), [Asia](/wiki/Asia) (12), and [South America](/wiki/South_America) (2). However, if [Armenia](/wiki/Armenia), [Azerbaijan](/wiki/Azerbaijan), [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan), and [South Ossetia](/wiki/South_Ossetia) (de facto state) are counted as parts of Europe, then Europe has the most landlocked countries, at 20 (including all three landlocked de facto states). If these [transcontinental](/wiki/List_of_transcontinental_countries) or [culturally](/wiki/Cultural_geography) European countries are included in Asia, then both Africa and Europe (including [Kosovo](/wiki/Kosovo) and [Transnistria](/wiki/Transnistria)) have the most, at 16. Depending on the status of [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan) and the [South Caucasian](/wiki/South_Caucasus) countries, Asia has between 9 and 13 (including [South Ossetia](/wiki/South_Ossetia)). South America only has two landlocked countries: [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia) and [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay).

[Australia](/wiki/Australia_(continent)) and [North America](/wiki/North_America) have no landlocked countries, while [Antarctica](/wiki/Antarctica) has no countries at all. [Oceania](/wiki/Oceania) (which is usually not considered a continent but a geographical region by the [English-speaking countries](/wiki/List_of_countries_by_English-speaking_population)) also has no landlocked countries.

All landlocked countries, except [Bolivia](/wiki/Bolivia) and [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay), are located on the [continental mainland](/wiki/Landmass) of [Afro-Eurasia](/wiki/Afro-Eurasia).

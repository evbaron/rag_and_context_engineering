**Sally Margaret Field** (born November 6, 1946) is an American actress. She has performed in movies, [Broadway theater](/wiki/Broadway_theater), and television, and made records of popular music. Known for [her extensive work on screen and stage](/wiki/Sally_Field_filmography), she has received [many accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Sally_Field) throughout her career spanning six decades, including two [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards), two [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and three [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), in addition to nominations for a [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) and two [British Academy Film Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards). She was presented with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 2014, the [National Medal of Arts](/wiki/National_Medal_of_Arts) in 2014, the [Kennedy Center Honor](/wiki/Kennedy_Center_Honor) in 2019, and the [Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Life_Achievement_Award) in 2023.

## Early life

Field was born on November 6, 1946, in [Pasadena, California](/wiki/Pasadena,_California), a suburb of [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles). Her mother, [Margaret Field](/wiki/Margaret_Field) (1922–2011), was an actress. Her father, Richard Dryden Field (1914–1993), was a pharmacist who served in the Army during World War II. Her ancestry includes English and Irish. Her brother is [Richard Dryden Field Jr.](/wiki/Richard_Dryden_Field_Jr.), a physicist and academic. Her parents were divorced in 1950; on January 21, 1952, in Tijuana, Mexico, her mother married [Jock Mahoney](/wiki/Jock_Mahoney), an actor and stuntman. Field said in her 2018 memoir that she was abused by Mahoney throughout her childhood, up to the age 14.

As a teen, Field attended [Portola Middle School](/wiki/Portola_Middle_School_(Tarzana)) and [Birmingham High School](/wiki/Birmingham_High_School) in [Van Nuys](/wiki/Van_Nuys), where she was a [cheerleader](/wiki/Cheerleader). Her class of 1964 classmates included financier [Michael Milken](/wiki/Michael_Milken) and talent agent [Michael Ovitz](/wiki/Michael_Ovitz), while actress [Cindy Williams](/wiki/Cindy_Williams) was a year behind Field.

Field has stated that when she was seventeen she got an illegal abortion in Mexico, and was molested by a technician during it.

## Career

### 1965–1976

Field and [John Davidson](/wiki/John_Davidson_(entertainer)) on [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s *[The Girl with Something Extra](/wiki/The_Girl_with_Something_Extra)* (1973)

Field got her start on television as the boy-crazy [surfer](/wiki/Surfer) girl in the sitcom *[Gidget](/wiki/Gidget_(TV_series))* ([ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company), 1965–1966). The show was not an initial success and was cancelled after a single season; however, summer reruns garnered respectable ratings, making the show a belated success. Wanting to find a new starring vehicle for Field, ABC next produced *[The Flying Nun](/wiki/The_Flying_Nun)* with Field cast as Sister Bertrille for three seasons, from 1967 to 1970. In an interview included on the Season One DVD release, Field said that she thoroughly enjoyed *Gidget* but hated *The Flying Nun* because she was not treated with respect by the show's directors. During her time on *The Flying Nun*, Field had a brief singing career, recording an album (*Star of The Flying Nun*, 1967) and a number of singles.

Field was then [typecast](/wiki/Typecast), finding respectable roles difficult to obtain. In 1971, Field starred in the ABC [television film](/wiki/Television_film) *[Maybe I'll Come Home in the Spring](/wiki/Maybe_I%27ll_Come_Home_in_the_Spring)*, playing a discouraged teen runaway who returns home with a bearded, drug-abusing hippie (played by [David Carradine](/wiki/David_Carradine)). She made several guest television appearances through the mid-1970s, including a role on the Western *[Alias Smith and Jones](/wiki/Alias_Smith_and_Jones)*, a popular series starring *Gidget* co-star [Pete Duel](/wiki/Pete_Duel). She also appeared in the episode "Whisper" on the thriller *[Night Gallery](/wiki/Night_Gallery)*.

In 1973, Field was cast in a starring role opposite [John Davidson](/wiki/John_Davidson_(entertainer)) in the series *[The Girl with Something Extra](/wiki/The_Girl_with_Something_Extra)* that aired from 1973 to 1974. Following the series' cancellation, Field studied at the [Actors Studio](/wiki/Actors_Studio) with acting teacher [Lee Strasberg](/wiki/Lee_Strasberg). Strasberg became a mentor to Field, helping her move past her television image of the girl next door. During this period, Field divorced her first husband in 1975.

Soon after studying with Strasberg, Field landed the title role in the 1976 television film *[Sybil](/wiki/Sybil_(1976_film))*, based on [the book](/wiki/Sybil_(Schreiber_book)) by [Flora Rheta Schreiber](/wiki/Flora_Rheta_Schreiber). Her dramatic portrayal of a young woman afflicted with [dissociative identity disorder](/wiki/Dissociative_identity_disorder) earned her an [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Award) for [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Special Program – Drama or Comedy](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie) in 1977 and enabled her to break through the typecasting of her sitcom work. She received further strong reviews for her work in the 1976 film *[Stay Hungry](/wiki/Stay_Hungry)*, opposite [Jeff Bridges](/wiki/Jeff_Bridges) and [Arnold Schwarzenegger](/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger).

### 1977–1989

Field with [Joanne Woodward](/wiki/Joanne_Woodward) in *Sybil* (1976)

In 1977, Field co-starred with [Burt Reynolds](/wiki/Burt_Reynolds), [Jackie Gleason](/wiki/Jackie_Gleason), and [Jerry Reed](/wiki/Jerry_Reed) in the [year's second-highest-grossing film](/wiki/1977_in_film), *[Smokey and the Bandit](/wiki/Smokey_and_the_Bandit)*. In 1979, she played the titular union organizer in *[Norma Rae](/wiki/Norma_Rae)*, a film that established her as a dramatic actress. [Vincent Canby](/wiki/Vincent_Canby), reviewing the film for *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, wrote: "*Norma Rae* is a seriously concerned contemporary drama, illuminated by some very good performances and one, Miss Field's, that is spectacular." For her role in *Norma Rae*, Field won the [Best Female Performance Prize](/wiki/Prix_d%27interpr%C3%A9tation_f%C3%A9minine) at the [Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival) and the [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress).

Field appeared with Reynolds in three more films: *[The End](/wiki/The_End_(1978_film))*, *[Hooper](/wiki/Hooper_(film))*, and *[Smokey and the Bandit II](/wiki/Smokey_and_the_Bandit_II)*. In 1981, she continued to change her image, playing a foul-mouthed prostitute opposite [Tommy Lee Jones](/wiki/Tommy_Lee_Jones) in the South-set film *[Back Roads](/wiki/Back_Roads_(1981_film))*. She was nominated for a Golden Globe for the 1981 drama *[Absence of Malice](/wiki/Absence_of_Malice)* and the 1982 comedy *[Kiss Me Goodbye](/wiki/Kiss_Me_Goodbye_(film))*.

In the 1984 drama *[Places in the Heart](/wiki/Places_in_the_Heart)*, she starred as Edna Spalding, a farm widow struggling to weather the [Great Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States). She won her second [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Motion_Picture_%E2%80%93_Drama) and second [Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress). Field's acceptance speech has since been both admired as earnest and parodied as excessive, mainly the line, "And I can't deny the fact that you like me...right now...you like me! (applause) Thank you!" Field later parodied herself when she delivered the line (often misquoted as "You like me, you *really* like me!") in a [Charles Schwab](/wiki/Charles_Schwab_Corporation) commercial.

In 1985, she co-starred with [James Garner](/wiki/James_Garner) in the romantic comedy *[Murphy's Romance](/wiki/Murphy%27s_Romance)*. The following year, Field appeared on the cover of the March 1986 issue of *[Playboy](/wiki/Playboy)* magazine, in which she was the interview subject. She did not appear as a pictorial subject in the magazine, although she did wear the classic [leotard](/wiki/Leotard) and bunny-ears outfit on the cover. That year, she received the [Women in Film](/wiki/Women_in_Film_Los_Angeles) [Crystal Award](/wiki/Women_in_Film_Crystal_%2B_Lucy_Awards#THE_CRYSTAL_AWARD). For her role as matriarch M'Lynn in the film version of *[Steel Magnolias](/wiki/Steel_Magnolias)* (1989), she was nominated for a 1990 Golden Globe Award for Best Actress.

### 1990–present

In the early 1990s, Field had supporting roles in a number of films. These included [Disney](/wiki/Disney)'s live-action film *[Homeward Bound: The Incredible Journey](/wiki/Homeward_Bound:_The_Incredible_Journey)* (1993), where she voiced the role of Sassy. In *[Mrs. Doubtfire](/wiki/Mrs._Doubtfire)* (1993), she played the wife of [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams)'s character and the love interest of [Pierce Brosnan](/wiki/Pierce_Brosnan)'s character. She then played [Tom Hanks](/wiki/Tom_Hanks)'s mother in *[Forrest Gump](/wiki/Forrest_Gump)* (1994), even though she was only 10 years older than Hanks, with whom she had co-starred six years earlier in *[Punchline](/wiki/Punchline_(film))*. For Forrest Gump, she received [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role) and [SAG](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role) nominations.

Field in [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) in 2002

Field's other 1990s films included *[Not Without My Daughter](/wiki/Not_Without_My_Daughter_(film))*, a controversial thriller based on the real-life experience of [Betty Mahmoody](/wiki/Betty_Mahmoody)'s escape from Iran with her daughter [Mahtob](/wiki/Mahtob); and *[Soapdish](/wiki/Soapdish)*, a comedy in which she played a pampered soap-opera star and was joined by a cast that included [Kevin Kline](/wiki/Kevin_Kline), [Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg), [Cathy Moriarty](/wiki/Cathy_Moriarty), [Elisabeth Shue](/wiki/Elisabeth_Shue), and [Robert Downey Jr.](/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr.) In 1996, Field reprised her role as Sassy in *[Homeward Bound 2: Lost in San Francisco](/wiki/Homeward_Bound_2:_Lost_in_San_Francisco)* and later that year, she received the [Berlinale Camera](/wiki/Berlinale_Camera) award at the [46th Berlin International Film Festival](/wiki/46th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) for her role as a grieving vigilante mother in director [John Schlesinger](/wiki/John_Schlesinger)'s film *[Eye for an Eye](/wiki/Eye_for_an_Eye_(1996_film))*. In 1997, Field guest starred on the *[King of the Hill](/wiki/King_of_the_Hill)* episode "Hilloween", in which she voiced religious woman Junie Harper, who contends with [Hank Hill](/wiki/Hank_Hill) ([Mike Judge](/wiki/Mike_Judge)) to ban Halloween. She co-starred with [Natalie Portman](/wiki/Natalie_Portman) in *[Where the Heart Is](/wiki/Where_the_Heart_Is_(2000_film))* (2000), and appeared opposite [Reese Witherspoon](/wiki/Reese_Witherspoon) in *[Legally Blonde 2: Red, White & Blonde](/wiki/Legally_Blonde_2:_Red,_White_%26_Blonde)*.

Field had a recurring role on *[ER](/wiki/ER_(TV_series))* in the 2000–2001 season as Dr. [Abby Lockhart](/wiki/Abby_Lockhart)'s mother, Maggie, who has [bipolar disorder](/wiki/Bipolar_disorder), a role for which she won an [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Award) in 2001. After her critically acclaimed stint on the show, she returned to the role in 2003 and 2006. She also starred in the 2002 series *[The Court](/wiki/The_Court_(TV_series))*.

Field's directorial career began with the television film [*The Christmas Tree*](/wiki/The_Christmas_Tree_(1996_film)) (1996). In 1998, she directed the episode "The Original Wives' Club" of the critically acclaimed TV miniseries *[From the Earth to the Moon](/wiki/From_the_Earth_to_the_Moon_(miniseries))*, also playing a minor role as Trudy, the wife of astronaut [Gordon Cooper](/wiki/Gordon_Cooper). In 2000, she directed the feature film *[Beautiful](/wiki/Beautiful_(2000_film))*.

Field was a late addition to the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) drama *[Brothers & Sisters](/wiki/Brothers_%26_Sisters_(2006_TV_series))*, which debuted in September 2006. In the show's pilot, the role of matriarch Nora Walker was played by [Betty Buckley](/wiki/Betty_Buckley). However, the show's producers decided to take the character in another direction, and offered the part to Field, who won the [2007 Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series](/wiki/59th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards#Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Drama_Series) for her performance. The drama also starred [Calista Flockhart](/wiki/Calista_Flockhart) and [Rachel Griffiths](/wiki/Rachel_Griffiths) as Nora's adult daughters. In November 2009, Field appeared on an episode of *[The Doctors](/wiki/The_Doctors_(2008_TV_series))* to talk about [osteoporosis](/wiki/Osteoporosis) and her Rally With Sally Foundation.

She portrayed [Aunt May](/wiki/Aunt_May) in the [Marvel Comics](/wiki/Marvel_Comics) films *[The Amazing Spider-Man](/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_(film))* (2012) as well as [the 2014 sequel](/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_2). Field's widely praised portrayal of [Mary Todd Lincoln](/wiki/Mary_Todd_Lincoln) in [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s film *[Lincoln](/wiki/Lincoln_(film))*, also in 2012, brought her Best Supporting Actress Award nominations at the [Oscars](/wiki/Oscars), [Golden Globes](/wiki/Golden_Globes), [BAFTA](/wiki/BAFTA), [Screen Actors Guild](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award), and [Critics' Choice](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Awards).

On May 5, 2014, Field received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) for her contributions to motion pictures. Her star is located in front of the [Hollywood Wax Museum](/wiki/Hollywood_Wax_Museum). In January 2015, it was announced that she would co-host [TCM](/wiki/Turner_Classic_Movies). The same year, Field portrayed the titular character in *[Hello, My Name Is Doris](/wiki/Hello,_My_Name_Is_Doris)*, for which she was nominated for the [Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actress in a Comedy](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Comedy).

Field in 2018

In 2017, Field reprised her role as Amanda Wingfield in *[The Glass Menagerie](/wiki/The_Glass_Menagerie)* on [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) at the [Belasco Theatre](/wiki/Belasco_Theatre). Performances began on February 7, 2017, in previews, and officially opened on March 9. The production closed on May 21, 2017, after 85 performance and 31 previews. Field had previously played the role in the [Kennedy Center](/wiki/Kennedy_Center) production in 2004. She was nominated for a [Tony Award for Best Actress in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Play) for her performance. Her memoir, *In Pieces*, was published by [Grand Central Publishing](/wiki/Grand_Central_Publishing) in September 2018.

Field returned to episodic television in 2018, starring in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) miniseries *[Maniac](/wiki/Maniac_(miniseries))*. Subsequently, in 2020, Field starred in the [AMC](/wiki/AMC_(TV_channel)) series *[Dispatches from Elsewhere](/wiki/Dispatches_from_Elsewhere)*.

In 2023, Field co-starred in the comedy movie *[80 for Brady](/wiki/80_for_Brady)*, which starred [NFL](/wiki/NFL) quarterback [Tom Brady](/wiki/Tom_Brady) along with fellow actresses [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda), [Lily Tomlin](/wiki/Lily_Tomlin) and [Rita Moreno](/wiki/Rita_Moreno). Also in 2023, Field was named the 58th recipient of the [Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Life_Achievement_Award), which she was presented at the [29th Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/29th_Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards).

## Personal life

Field was married to Steven Craig from 1968 to 1975, though they separated in 1973. The couple had two sons: [Peter Craig](/wiki/Peter_Craig), a novelist and [screenwriter](/wiki/Screenwriter) (b. 1969); and [Eli Craig](/wiki/Eli_Craig), an actor and director (b. 1972).

From 1976 to 1980, Field had a relationship with [Burt Reynolds](/wiki/Burt_Reynolds), during which time they co-starred in four films: *[Smokey and the Bandit](/wiki/Smokey_and_the_Bandit)*, *[Smokey and the Bandit II](/wiki/Smokey_and_the_Bandit_II)*, *[The End](/wiki/The_End_(1978_film))*, and *[Hooper](/wiki/Hooper_(film))*. Following their 1980 breakup, Field and Reynolds continued to date [on and off](/wiki/On-again,_off-again_relationship) before splitting permanently in 1982.

Field married her second husband, Alan Greisman, in 1984. Together, they had one son, Sam (b. 1987). Field and Greisman divorced in 1994.

On October 29, 1988, at [Aspen/Pitkin County Airport](/wiki/Aspen/Pitkin_County_Airport) in [Colorado](/wiki/Colorado), Field and three members of her family were in a private plane owned by media mogul [Merv Griffin](/wiki/Merv_Griffin) when it lost power and [rejected takeoff](/wiki/Rejected_takeoff), slamming into a parked aircraft. They all survived with minor injuries.

Field wrote in her memoir that she suffered from "severe depression" in her late teens. She stated that taking acting classes helped her overcome
"this urgency, this anxiety, this need to find something that was festering in me."

## Philanthropy and activism

In 2005, Field was diagnosed with [osteoporosis](/wiki/Osteoporosis). Her diagnosis led her to create the "Rally with Sally for Bone Health" campaign with support from [Roche](/wiki/Roche) and [GlaxoSmithKline](/wiki/GlaxoSmithKline) that controversially co-promoted [Boniva](/wiki/Ibandronic_acid), a [bisphosphonate](/wiki/Bisphosphonate) treatment for osteoporosis. Field's campaign encouraged the early diagnosis of such conditions through technology such as bone-density scans.

In 2005, Field received the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](/wiki/American_Academy_of_Achievement) presented in recognition of her lifetime of contributions to the arts as well as her dedication as a social activist.

During her acceptance speech at the 2007 [Emmy Awards](/wiki/Emmy_Award), when she won for Outstanding Lead Actress in a Drama Series, Field said: "If the mothers ruled the world, there would be no goddamn wars in the first place." [Fox Broadcasting Company](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company), which aired the show, cut the sound and picture after the word "god" and did not return camera/sound to the stage until after Field finished talking. An e-mail statement from the company the day after the incident explained that the [censorship](/wiki/Censorship) of Field's speech (among two other censorship incidents during the award ceremony) occurred because "some language during the live broadcast may have been considered inappropriate by some viewers. As a result, Fox's broadcast standards executives determined it appropriate to drop sound and picture during those portions of the show."

Field is an advocate for [women's rights](/wiki/Women%27s_rights). She has served on the board of directors of [Vital Voices Global Partnership](/wiki/Vital_Voices_Global_Partnership), an international women's NGO, and has co-hosted the [Global Leadership Awards](/wiki/Global_Leadership_Awards) six times. A Democrat, Field supported [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton)'s bid for the Democratic Party nomination in the [2008 presidential election](/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), and [Kamala Harris’s 2024 presidential campaign](/wiki/Kamala_Harris_2024_presidential_campaign).

Field is also an advocate for gay rights, and won the [Human Rights Campaign](/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign)'s Ally for Equality Award in 2012. Her youngest son, Samuel Greisman, is gay.

Field was arrested on December 13, 2019, while demonstrating with [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda)'s climate change activist group in Washington, D.C.

Having undergone a traumatic illegal abortion in Mexico at the age of seventeen, Field is a vocal advocate for [abortion rights in the United States](/wiki/Abortion_rights_in_the_United_States).

## Awards and nominations

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Sally Field](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Sally_Field)

Field has been recognized by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) for the following:

- [52nd Academy Awards](/wiki/52nd_Academy_Awards): [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), win, for *[Norma Rae](/wiki/Norma_Rae)* (1979)
- [57th Academy Awards](/wiki/57th_Academy_Awards): [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), win, for *[Places in the Heart](/wiki/Places_in_the_Heart)* (1984)
- [85th Academy Awards](/wiki/85th_Academy_Awards): [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress), nomination, for [*Lincoln*](/wiki/Lincoln_(film)) (2012)

In addition, she has been nominated for two [BAFTA Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_of_Film_and_Television_Arts), nine [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards) (winning three), eleven [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) (winning two), nine competitive [Actor Awards](/wiki/Actor_Awards) (winning one) along with an honorary [Life Achievement Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Life_Achievement_Award), one [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Awards), one [Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Actress) (winning one), and two [Critics' Choice Movie Awards](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Awards).

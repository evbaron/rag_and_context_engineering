**Lionel Andrés** "**Leo**" **Messi** (born 24 June 1987) is an Argentine professional [footballer](/wiki/Association_football) who plays as a [forward](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)) for and [captains](/wiki/Captain_(association_football)) both the [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) club [Inter Miami](/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF) and the [Argentina national team](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team). Widely regarded as one of the greatest players in history, Messi has set numerous records for [individual accolades won](/wiki/List_of_career_achievements_by_Lionel_Messi#Individual_honours) throughout [his professional footballing career](/wiki/Career_of_Lionel_Messi), including eight [Ballon d'Ors](/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or), six [European Golden Shoes](/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe), and eight times being named the [world's best player](/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Men%27s_Player#FIFA_Player_of_the_Year_(including_predecessors)) by [FIFA](/wiki/FIFA). In 2025, he was named the [All Time Men's World Best Player](/wiki/IFFHS#Top_10_players_of_all-time_(2025)) by the [IFFHS](/wiki/IFFHS).

Messi is the most decorated player in the history of professional football, having won [46 team trophies](/wiki/List_of_career_achievements_by_Lionel_Messi#Collective_awards). His [records](/wiki/List_of_career_achievements_by_Lionel_Messi#Records) include most goals in a calendar year (91), most goals for a single club (672 for [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona)), most goals in [La Liga](/wiki/La_Liga) (474), [most assists](/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_international_football_(all-time)) in international football (61), most [goal contributions](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_records_and_statistics#Goal_contributions) in the [FIFA World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup) (21), and most [goal contributions](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_records_and_statistics#Individual_records) in the [Copa América](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) (32). Messi has scored [over 900 senior career goals](/wiki/List_of_footballers_with_500_or_more_goals) and provided [over 400 assists](/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_all_competitions_(all-time)) for club and country, resulting in over 1,300 goal contributions—the highest total in the sport's history.

Messi made his competitive debut for [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) at age 17 in October 2004. He gradually established himself as an integral player for the club, and during his first uninterrupted season in [2008–09](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_FC_Barcelona_season) he helped Barcelona achieve the first [treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football)) in Spanish football. This resulted in Messi winning the [first](/wiki/2009_Ballon_d%27Or) of four consecutive Ballon d'Ors, and by the [2011–12 season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season) he had set the European record for most goals in a season and established himself as [Barcelona's all-time top scorer](/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_records_and_statistics#Top_goalscorers). During the [2014–15 season](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_FC_Barcelona_season), where he became the all-time top scorer in [La Liga](/wiki/La_Liga), he led Barcelona to a historic second treble.

Messi was named Barcelona's captain in 2018. While playing for the club, he won a [club-record](/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_records_and_statistics#Other_individual_records_for_the_club) 34 trophies, including ten La Liga titles and four [UEFA Champions Leagues](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League). Financial difficulties at Barcelona led Messi to depart in August 2021 and sign with [Paris Saint-Germain](/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain), where he won the [Ligue 1](/wiki/Ligue_1) title during both of his seasons there. He joined the Major League Soccer club Inter Miami in July 2023, and led them to their first [MLS Cup](/wiki/MLS_Cup) victory in [2025](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2025), while also winning back-to-back [league MVP awards](/wiki/Landon_Donovan_MVP_Award) in [2024](/wiki/2024_Major_League_Soccer_season) and [2025](/wiki/2025_Major_League_Soccer_season).

An Argentine international, Messi is the national team's [all-time leading goalscorer](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team#Top_goalscorers) and [most-capped player](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team#Most_appearances). Several years after his senior debut in 2005, he won the [gold medal](/wiki/Olympic_medal) at the [2008 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament). Assuming captaincy in 2011, he led Argentina to three consecutive finals in the [2014 World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup), the [2015 Copa América](/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and the [Copa América Centenario](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario), all of which they lost. After announcing his retirement from international football in 2016, he returned to help Argentina qualify for the [2018 World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup). Messi was central to ending Argentina's 28-year trophy drought by winning the [2021 Copa América](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). In 2022, he led Argentina to a [World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup) victory, the country's first in 36 years. Messi's third major international title arrived when Argentina won the [2024 Copa América](/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).

Messi has endorsed the sportswear company [Adidas](/wiki/Adidas) since 2006. He was ranked the [world's highest-paid athlete](/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_athletes) by *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* in 2019 and 2022, and was among *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))*'s [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100) in 2011, 2012, and 2023. In 2020 and 2023, he was named the [Laureus World Sportsman of the Year](/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award_for_Sportsman_of_the_Year), the first team-sport athlete to win the award. In 2020, Messi was named to the [Ballon d'Or Dream Team](/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or_Dream_Team) and became the second footballer and second team-sport athlete to surpass $1 billion in career earnings. Following his arrival and impact on football in the United States, Messi was named *Time*'s [Athlete of the Year](/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year#Athlete_of_the_Year) in 2023, and was awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) by US president [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) in 2025.

## Early life

Messi was born on 24 June 1987 in [Rosario](/wiki/Rosario), Santa Fe Province. He was the third of four children of Jorge Messi, head of department at the Acindar [pressed](/wiki/Stamping_(metalworking)) steel manufacturer in [Villa Constitución](/wiki/Villa_Constituci%C3%B3n), and his wife Celia Cuccittini, who worked in a magnet manufacturing workshop. His parents had met as youngsters in Rosario's south-eastern district of Las Heras, where Messi himself later grew up, and had married on Jorge Messi's completion of military service in the [Argentine Armed Forces](/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Argentine_Republic) in 1978. On his father's side, Messi is of Italian and Spanish descent, the great-grandson of immigrants from the north-central [Adriatic](/wiki/Adriatic_Sea) [Marche](/wiki/Marche) region of Italy (with his surname coming from [Porto Recanati](/wiki/Porto_Recanati)), and on his mother's side he has primarily Italian ancestry. Growing up in a tight-knit, football-loving family, "Leo" developed a passion for the sport from an early age, playing constantly with his older brothers, Rodrigo and Matías, and his cousins, [Maximiliano](/wiki/Maxi_Biancucchi) and [Emanuel Biancucchi](/wiki/Emanuel_Biancucchi), both of whom became professional footballers.

At the age of four, Messi joined the local [football club](/wiki/Football_club_(association_football)) Abanderado Grandoli FC, where he was coached by his father. His maternal grandmother, Celia, accompanied him to training and matches. He was greatly affected by her death, shortly before his eleventh birthday. Since then, as a devout [Catholic](/wiki/Catholic_Church), he has celebrated his goals by looking up and pointing to the sky in tribute to his grandmother.

## Youth career

### Newell's Old Boys

> "When you saw him you would think: this kid can't play ball. He's a dwarf, he's too fragile, too small. But immediately you'd realise that he was born different, that he was a phenomenon."

— [Newell's Old Boys](/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys) youth coach [Adrián Coria](/wiki/Adri%C3%A1n_Coria_(football_manager)), sharing his first impression of the 12-year-old Messi

Messi started playing for the [Rosario](/wiki/Rosario)-based football club [Newell's Old Boys](/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys) when he was seven years old; he would become a lifelong supporter of the club. During the six years he played for Newell's, he scored almost 500 goals, and was a member of "The Machine of '87", a nearly unbeatable youth team named for the year of their birth. Messi regularly entertained crowds by performing ball tricks during half-time, when the Newell's [first team](/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#First_team) played home games. His goalscoring idol growing up was Brazilian striker [Ronaldo](/wiki/Ronaldo_(Brazilian_footballer)), who Messi called "the best forward I've ever seen".

Messi's future as a professional player was threatened when, at the age of 10, he was diagnosed with a [growth hormone deficiency](/wiki/Growth_hormone_deficiency). He began [growth hormone therapy](/wiki/Growth_hormone_therapy) at age 11; however, his father's health insurance covered only two years of the therapy, which cost at least 1,000 pesos per month. Newell's agreed to contribute, but later reneged on their promise. Messi was scouted by the Buenos Aires club [River Plate](/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate), whose [playmaker](/wiki/Playmaker) [Pablo Aimar](/wiki/Pablo_Aimar) he idolised. It was speculated for a time that Messi failed to sign with River Plate due to his ill health; however, in a 2019 interview, he revealed that River Plate had wanted to sign him after he scored four goals at a trial, and even offered to pay for his medical treatment, but Newell's refused to release his player card, preventing the transfer.

### Barcelona

As the Messi family had relatives in Catalonia, they sought to arrange a trial with [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) in September 2000, when Messi was 13. First-team director [Carles Rexach](/wiki/Carles_Rexach) immediately wanted to sign him, but the board of directors hesitated; at the time it was highly unusual for European clubs to sign foreign players of such a young age. On 14 December, an ultimatum was issued for Barcelona to prove their commitment, and Rexach, with no other paper at hand, wrote a contract on a paper napkin. In February 2001, Messi's family relocated to Barcelona, where they moved into an apartment near the club's stadium, [Camp Nou](/wiki/Camp_Nou).

During his first year in Spain, Messi rarely played with the *Infantiles* due to a transfer conflict with Newell's; as a foreigner, he could only be fielded in friendlies and the Catalan league. Without football, he struggled to integrate into the team. He was reserved by nature, and was so quiet that some of his teammates initially believed he was mute. At home, he suffered from homesickness after his mother moved back to Rosario with his brothers and younger sister, María Sol, while he stayed in Barcelona with his father.

At age 13, Messi enrolled at Barcelona's youth academy, [La Masia](/wiki/La_Masia).

After a year at Barcelona's youth academy, [La Masia](/wiki/La_Masia), Messi was finally enrolled in the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) (RFEF) in February 2002. Now he could play in all competitions, and quickly befriended his teammates, including [Cesc Fàbregas](/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas) and [Gerard Piqué](/wiki/Gerard_Piqu%C3%A9). After completing his growth hormone therapy at 14, Messi became an integral part of the "Baby Dream Team", Barcelona's greatest-ever youth side. During his first full season (2002–03), he was the top scorer with 36 goals in 30 games for the *Cadetes* *A*, who won an unprecedented treble of the league, the Spanish cup, and the Copa Catalunya. In the Copa Catalunya final, their 4–1 victory over [Espanyol](/wiki/RCD_Espanyol) became referred to in club lore as the *partido de la máscara*, the final of the mask. A week after suffering a broken cheekbone during a league match, Messi was allowed to start the game on the condition that he wear a protective plastic mask. When the mask became a hindrance, Messi took it off and scored two goals in 10 minutes. At the close of the season, he received an offer to join [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.), his first from a foreign club. Although Fàbregas and Piqué soon left for [England](/wiki/Premier_League) with their own offers, Messi chose to remain in Barcelona.

Messi continued to progress through his club's ranks at a rapid pace, debuting for four youth teams during the 2003–04 season. After being named player of the tournament in four separate international pre-season competitions with the *Juvenil B*, he played only one official match with the team before being promoted to the *[Juvenil A](/wiki/FC_Barcelona_(youth)#Juvenil_A)*. In the *Juvenil A* he scored 18 goals in 11 league games. During the international break, Messi was one of several youth players called up to strengthen the depleted first team. [Ludovic Giuly](/wiki/Ludovic_Giuly), one of Barcelona's [wingers](/wiki/Winger_(soccer)), described Messi's performance during a training session: "He destroyed us all ... He would dribble past four players and score a goal. Even the team's starting centre-backs were nervous ... He was an alien."

> "It seemed as if he had been playing with us all his life."

— Barcelona's then assistant coach [Henk ten Cate](/wiki/Henk_ten_Cate) on Messi's first-team debut.

At 16 years old, Messi made his first-team debut when he came on in the 75th minute during a friendly against [FC Porto](/wiki/FC_Porto) on 16 November 2003. His performance impressed the technical staff, and he subsequently began training daily with the club's reserve side, [Barcelona B](/wiki/Barcelona_B), as well as weekly with the first team. After Messi's first training session with the senior squad, Barcelona's new star player [Ronaldinho](/wiki/Ronaldinho) told his teammates that he believed the 16-year-old would become an even better player than himself. Ronaldinho called Messi "little brother", and his friendship greatly eased Messi's transition onto the first team.

To gain further match experience, Messi joined his club's third team, [Barcelona C](/wiki/FC_Barcelona_C), in addition to the *Juvenil A*. He helped save the third team from the relegation zone of the [Tercera División](/wiki/Tercera_Divisi%C3%B3n), scoring five goals in ten games, including a [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick) in eight minutes during a [Copa del Rey Juvenil](/wiki/2004_Copa_del_Rey_Juvenil) match. His progress was reflected in his first professional contract, signed on 4 February 2004, which lasted until 2012 and contained an initial [buyout clause](/wiki/Buyout_clause) of €30 million. A month later, he made his debut for Barcelona B in the [Segunda División B](/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B), and his buyout clause automatically increased to €80 million. He played five games with the B team that season but did not score. He was physically weaker than his opponents, who were often much older and taller, and he worked on increasing his muscle mass and overall strength. Towards the end of the season, he returned to both youth teams, helping the *Juvenil B* win the league. He finished the campaign having scored goals for four of his five teams, with a total of 36 goals in all official competitions.

## Club career

Main article: [Career of Lionel Messi § Club career](/wiki/Career_of_Lionel_Messi#Club_career)

### Barcelona

#### 2004–2008: Rise to the first team

In 2004, Messi (pictured in 2005) began his 17-year professional career with [FC Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona)

Messi began the [2004–05 season](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FC_Barcelona_season) as a guaranteed starter for the [Barcelona B](/wiki/FC_Barcelona_B) team, but after some lobbying by the senior players, he was promoted to the first team by manager [Frank Rijkaard](/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard). He made his [La Liga](/wiki/La_Liga) debut for Barcelona on 16 October 2004, at 17 years old. He scored his first senior goal on 1 May 2005, becoming the youngest-ever scorer for the club at the time. He was at that time the youngest player to represent Barcelona in an official competition, and the club won the [league title](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_La_Liga) during that season.

On his 18th birthday, Messi signed his first contract as a senior team player, which kept him with Barcelona through 2010, and had a release clause of €150 million. Three months later, as his performance continued to make waves, his contract was updated to double his salary and keep him with the club until 2014. By the end of the [2005–06 season](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_FC_Barcelona_season), Barcelona had won [La Liga](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_La_Liga) again as well as the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Champions_League).

During the [2006–07 season](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FC_Barcelona_season), Messi scored his first [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick) during a game against [Real Madrid](/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF), becoming the first player to score a hat-trick in a *[Clásico](/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico)* in 12 years. Two goals scored by Messi against [Getafe](/wiki/Getafe_CF) and Espanyol during the season drew notice for their similarities to two famous goals scored by fellow Argentine [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona) in the [1986 World Cup match against England](/wiki/Argentina_v_England_(1986_FIFA_World_Cup)), leading to [comparisons between Messi and Maradona](#Comparisons_with_Diego_Maradona) that would endure throughout Messi's career. Barcelona finished the 2006–07 season with only one trophy – the [2006 Supercopa de España](/wiki/2006_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) – and the [2007–08 season](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_FC_Barcelona_season) without a single trophy, leading to Rijkaard's departure.

#### 2008–2012: Success under Pep Guardiola

At the beginning of the [2008–09 season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_FC_Barcelona_season) – his first under Barcelona's new manager, former captain [Pep Guardiola](/wiki/Pep_Guardiola) – Messi was given the [number 10](/wiki/Squad_number_(association_football)) shirt. Over time, he effectively became the tactical focal point of Guardiola's strategy, increasing his goalscoring rate as a result. During that season, Messi scored 38 goals, and alongside [Samuel Eto'o](/wiki/Samuel_Eto%27o) and [Thierry Henry](/wiki/Thierry_Henry), contributed to a total of 100 goals in all competitions, a record at the time for the club. Messi played in his first [final](/wiki/2009_Copa_del_Rey_Final) as Barcelona won the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Copa_del_Rey). Barcelona also won the [La Liga title](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_La_Liga) and the [Champions League](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Champions_League), thus achieving the first [treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football)) in the history of Spanish football.

During the first half of the [2009–10 season](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_FC_Barcelona_season), Barcelona won the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2009_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), the [UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/2009_UEFA_Super_Cup) and the [FIFA Club World Cup](/wiki/2009_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), becoming the first club to achieve the sextuple. Messi finished as the [Champions League top scorer](/wiki/List_of_European_Cup_and_UEFA_Champions_League_top_scorers#Top_scorers_by_season), the youngest in the tournament's history. For his efforts in 2009, Messi won the [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2009_Ballon_d%27Or), the [FIFA World Player of the Year](/wiki/2009_FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year) award and his first [European Golden Shoe](/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe). He scored a total of 47 goals in all competitions, equalling [Ronaldo](/wiki/Ronaldo_(Brazilian_footballer))'s club record from the [1996–97 campaign](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_FC_Barcelona_season). He signed a new 7-year contract with Barcelona through 2016.

During the [2010–11 season](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_FC_Barcelona_season), Barcelona won the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2010_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), the [Champions League](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Champions_League), and a third consecutive [La Liga title](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_La_Liga). Messi's club performances in 2010 earned him his second consecutive [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2010_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or#Winners_and_nominees). He was the top scorer in the Champions League for the third consecutive year, and the league's top scorer and assist provider. He became Barcelona's all-time single-season top scorer with 53 goals.

During the [2011–12 season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season) Barcelona won both the [Spanish](/wiki/2011_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) and [European Super Cup](/wiki/2011_UEFA_Super_Cup) trophies, and the [FIFA Club World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Club_World_Cup). Messi won the [Golden Ball](/wiki/List_of_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_awards#Golden_Ball) for the second time and the [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or#Winners_and_nominees) for the third time, as well as the inaugural [UEFA Best Player in Europe Award](/wiki/UEFA_Best_Player_in_Europe_Award). During the year 2012, Messi became the second player to be top scorer in four Champions League campaigns. He became the top goalscorer in Barcelona's history at 24 years old, overtaking [César Rodríguez](/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Rodr%C3%ADguez_(footballer,_born_1920))'s 57-year record of 232 goals. He finished [the season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season) as the league top scorer in Spain for the second time with 50 goals, a La Liga record. His [73 goals in all competitions](/wiki/European_association_football_club_records#Most_goals_in_a_season) made him the single-season top scorer in the history of European club football excluding regional and local competitions. Barcelona won the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2012_Copa_del_Rey_final) that season, their 14th trophy under Guardiola, who resigned after a four-year cycle of success.

#### 2012–2014: Record-breaking year and *Messidependencia*

For the start of the [2012–13 season](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_FC_Barcelona_season), Barcelona had virtually secured their La Liga title by the start of 2013. A double scored against [Real Betis](/wiki/Real_Betis) saw Messi becoming Barcelona's all-time top scorer in La Liga, and surpassed [Gerd Müller](/wiki/Gerd_M%C3%BCller)'s record of most goals scored in a calendar year; Messi scored a record 91 goals in all competitions for Barcelona and Argentina throughout 2012. Messi again won the [FIFA Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2012_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or#Winners_and_nominees), becoming the first player in history to win the Ballon d'Or four times. He signed a new contract committing himself to the club through 2018, and wore the captain's armband for the first time in a league match against [Rayo Vallecano](/wiki/Rayo_Vallecano). The team won [La Liga](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_La_Liga) again that year, Messi's sixth, equalling Real Madrid's 100-point record of the previous season. With 60 goals in all competitions, including 46 goals in La Liga, he finished the campaign as league top scorer in Spain and Europe for the second consecutive year, becoming the first player in history to win the [European Golden Shoe](/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe) three times.

Messi's overall input into the team's attack had increased significantly. Whereas he contributed to 24% of the team's goals in their treble-winning campaign in 2008–09, this number rose to more than 40% by the end of the 2012–13 season. These statistics, as well as lopsided losses in the Champions League where Messi was unfit, gave credence to the notion of *Messidependencia*, Barcelona's perceived tactical and psychological dependence on their star player.

To offset the load on Messi, Barcelona signed Brazilian forward [Neymar](/wiki/Neymar) from [Santos](/wiki/Santos_FC) before the [2013–14 season](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_FC_Barcelona_season). The team won the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2013_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) at the beginning of the season. Messi finished the campaign with his worst output in five seasons, though he still managed to score 41 goals in all competitions. For the first time in five years, Barcelona ended the season without a major trophy.

#### 2014–2017: Arrival of Luis Enrique and birth of MSN

Messi dribbling past [Patrice Evra](/wiki/Patrice_Evra) of [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus_FC) during the [2015 UEFA Champions League final](/wiki/2015_UEFA_Champions_League_final)

Barcelona hired coach [Luis Enrique](/wiki/Luis_Enrique) before the [2014–15 season](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_FC_Barcelona_season), and continued to aid Messi in the attack by signing Uruguayan forward [Luis Suárez](/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez), who had won the European Golden Shoe the year before at [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.). Luis Enrique's system featured quick transitions from defence to attack, led by the front three of Messi, Suárez and Neymar. The attacking trio, which colloquially became known as 'MSN', broke goalscoring records. A hat-trick scored against [Sevilla](/wiki/Sevilla_FC) earlier in the season also made him the [all-time top scorer in La Liga](/wiki/La_Liga#Most_goals), as he surpassed the 59-year record of 251 league goals held by [Telmo Zarra](/wiki/Telmo_Zarra). After securing the [La Liga title](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_La_Liga), the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2015_Copa_del_Rey_Final), and the [Champions League](/wiki/2015_UEFA_Champions_League_final) that year, Messi helped Barcelona become the first club to win the continental treble twice. He recorded 58 goals, but combined with Neymar and Suárez, the attacking trio scored a total of 122 goals in all competitions that season, a record in Spanish football.

Messi opened the [2015–16 season](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_FC_Barcelona_season) by helping Barcelona's win over Sevilla in the [UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/2015_UEFA_Super_Cup). Messi capped off the year by winning the [2015 FIFA Club World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) [final](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final) over [River Plate](/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate) in [Yokohama](/wiki/Yokohama), collecting his fifth club trophy of the calendar year. On 11 January 2016, Messi won the [FIFA Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or) for a record fifth time in his career. He ended the season by winning [La Liga](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_La_Liga) as well as the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2016_Copa_del_Rey_final) again. In total, Messi scored 41 goals, and Barcelona's attacking trio of him, Neymar and Suárez managed a Spanish record of 131 combined goals throughout the season, breaking the record they had set the previous season.

The [2016–17 season](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_FC_Barcelona_season) ended with Barcelona winning the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2016_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) and the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2017_Copa_del_Rey_final). Messi finished the season with 54 goals, while his 37 goals in La Liga saw him claim both the [Pichichi](/wiki/Pichichi_Trophy) and European Golden Shoe Awards for the fourth time in his career.

#### 2017–2021: Final years at Barcelona

Messi, wearing the captain's armband, against [Real Valladolid](/wiki/Real_Valladolid) in 2018

Messi signed a new deal with Barcelona on 25 November 2017, keeping him with the club through 2021. The [2017–18 season](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_FC_Barcelona_season) saw Barcelona winning [La Liga](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_La_Liga) and the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2018_Copa_del_Rey_final) once again. Messi again finished the season as the top scorer in La Liga, with 34 goals, and won his fifth European Golden Shoe award. With the departure of captain [Andrés Iniesta](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta) in May 2018, Messi was named the team's new captain for the [2018–19 season](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FC_Barcelona_season). He lifted his first trophy as Barcelona's captain, the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2018_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), following a 2–1 victory over Sevilla. He also helped Barcelona clinch the [La Liga title](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_La_Liga). With 36 goals in 34 appearances that season, Messi won his sixth La Liga Golden Shoe trophy. He also captured his sixth [European Golden Shoe](/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe) award, setting a record for winning the award three seasons in a row.

Messi won his sixth [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2019_Ballon_d%27Or), but the subsequent [2019–20 season](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_FC_Barcelona_season) saw Barcelona go trophyless for the first time since 2007–08. Following a disappointing season, Messi expressed to the club his desire to leave, but ultimately decided to fulfill the final year of his contract. In 2021, he led Barcelona to victory in the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2021_Copa_del_Rey_final). His last two seasons with Barcelona saw him lead La Liga in goal scoring, giving him a record-breaking total of eight Pichichi trophies.

Although Messi became a free agent after his contract expired, he was hoping to stay with Barcelona, even though the club was facing immense financial challenges due in part to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). Following initial progress in contract negotiations, Messi was expecting to sign a new contract with a 50% salary cut, but Barcelona surprised him by announcing that it could not re-sign him, citing financial constraints and structural obstacles posed by La Liga regulations. On 8 August, 2021, Messi held a tearful press conference at [Camp Nou](/wiki/Camp_Nou), confirming that he would leave the club.

### Paris Saint-Germain

Messi (middle) with Paris Saint-Germain teammates [Kylian Mbappé](/wiki/Kylian_Mbapp%C3%A9) (left) and [Neymar](/wiki/Neymar)

On 10 August 2021, two days after his farewell remarks to Barcelona, Messi joined the [Ligue 1](/wiki/Ligue_1) club [Paris Saint-Germain](/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC) (PSG) on a two-year contract with an option for an extra year. He chose 30 as his squad number, which is the number he wore when he made his senior debut for Barcelona. He scored his first goal for the club in a Champions League group stage win over former manager Pep Guardiola's [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.). After finishing the year with 40 goals at the club and international levels and leading Argentina to victory in the [2021 Copa América](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), Messi won a record [seventh Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2021_Ballon_d%27Or). He finished his debut season with PSG with 11 goals and 14 assists, and helped the club win their 10th [Ligue 1 title](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Ligue_1).

Near the start of the [2022–23 season](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), Messi won his second trophy with PSG in the [Trophée des Champions](/wiki/2022_Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions). A goal against [Nice](/wiki/OGC_Nice) saw him surpass [Cristiano Ronaldo](/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo) as the all-time highest goalscorer in European club football with 702 goals; during the match, he also achieved 1,000 career direct goal contributions at club level. By the end of the season, he had 21 goals across all competitors and the highest number of assists in the league with 16, which helped PSG clinch their 11th [Ligue 1 title](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ligue_1) and his second in a row. After two years with Paris Saint-Germain, Messi departed the club.

### Inter Miami

Messi with Inter Miami in 2025

On 15 July 2023, the [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) (MLS) club [Inter Miami CF](/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF) announced the signing of Messi on a two-and-a-half-year contract. Messi's pay set an MLS record, with his earnings from salary, signing bonus, and an equity stake in the club reportedly surpassing $50 million. According to [*Goal*](/wiki/Goal_(website)), Messi's arrival in the United States helped to raise the profile of MLS within the US and abroad. Inter Miami co-president Xavier Asensi said that for MLS "there is a before and after Messi. He has changed everything."

After Messi joined Inter Miami, the club's games began selling out. The frenzy over his arrival was dubbed "Messimania", and Inter Miami's No. 10 Messi jersey became the best-selling jersey in the league, and nearly the best-selling in the world. Messi was made the team captain of his new club, and began the [2023 season](/wiki/2023_Inter_Miami_CF_season) by scoring nine goals in his first six games. He led Inter Miami to their first-ever trophy as they defeated [Nashville SC](/wiki/Nashville_SC) in the [Leagues Cup final](/wiki/2023_Leagues_Cup_final). Miami missed the MLS playoffs, however, finishing second to last in the [Eastern Conference](/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(MLS)). On 30 October 2023, following his World Cup win with Argentina and Ligue 1 trophy with Paris Saint-Germain, Messi was awarded a record [eighth Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2023_Ballon_d%27Or). He was named [*Time* Athlete of the Year](/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year#Athlete_of_the_Year), the first footballer ever to win the award.

During the [2024 season](/wiki/2024_Inter_Miami_CF_season), Messi broke the record for the most assists in a single MLS game with five assists. On 2 October, he scored two goals in a 3–2 win over the [Columbus Crew](/wiki/Columbus_Crew), clinching the [Supporters' Shield](/wiki/Supporters%27_Shield) award for Miami. In the final game of the season, Messi scored his first hat-trick for the club in a 6–2 victory. Miami finished the regular season with 74 points, an MLS league record, while Messi finished with 20 goals and 16 assists in 19 matches, becoming the [all-time top goalscorer](/wiki/List_of_Inter_Miami_CF_records_and_statistics) for the young team. Miami made its first postseason appearance in the [2024 MLS Cup playoffs](/wiki/2024_MLS_Cup_playoffs) but was eliminated in the first round. Messi was named the [MLS Most Valuable Player](/wiki/Landon_Donovan_MVP_Award) for the season.

During the [2025 season](/wiki/2025_Inter_Miami_CF_season), Messi became the fastest player in MLS history to reach 40 league goals. He ended the season by winning the [MLS Golden Boot](/wiki/MLS_Golden_Boot) as the league's top scorer, with 29 goals and 19 assists in 28 games. On 23 October, he signed a contract extension with the club through 2028, by which time he would be 41 years old. During the Eastern Conference final against [New York City FC](/wiki/New_York_City_FC) in the [2025 MLS Cup playoffs](/wiki/2025_MLS_Cup_playoffs), an assist from Messi put his career assists across all competitions at 405, surpassing [Ferenc Puskás](/wiki/Ferenc_Pusk%C3%A1s) for the most [career assists of all time](/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_all_competitions_(all-time)). Messi led Miami to [MLS Cup 2025](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2025), where they won 3–1 over the [Vancouver Whitecaps](/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps) to win the team's first league championship. Providing two assists during the match, Messi was named [MLS Cup MVP](/wiki/MLS_Cup_MVP). At the end of the season, Messi was again named MLS MVP, becoming the first player in league history to win the award in back-to-back years.

In March 2026 it was reported that Messi was earning between $70 and $80 million per year from Inter Miami from his compensation and equity rights. On 18 March 2026, Messi scored his 900th career goal during Inter Miami's 1–1 draw with Nashville SC in the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](/wiki/2026_CONCACAF_Champions_Cup). He became the second player to reach the mark in top-level men's football after Cristiano Ronaldo.

## International career

Main article: [Career of Lionel Messi § International career](/wiki/Career_of_Lionel_Messi#International_career)

As a citizen of both Argentina and Spain, Messi was eligible to play for both national teams. He began his international career in 2004 with [Argentina's U20 team](/wiki/Argentina_national_under-20_football_team), and was subsequently included in the squad for the [2005 South American U-20 Championship](/wiki/2005_South_American_U-20_Championship), in which they finished third. Messi then [led the team to victory](/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship_final) in the [2005 FIFA World Youth Championship](/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship). He finished the tournament with six goals and two assists, and won the [Golden Ball](/wiki/FIFA_U-20_World_Cup#Awards).

Messi evades Brazil's [Marcelo](/wiki/Marcelo_(footballer,_born_1988)) in the semi-final of the [2008 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament).

Messi made his debut with the [senior national team](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) in 2005, at age 18, and scored his first international goal in a 2006 [friendly](/wiki/Friendly_(association_football)) against [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team). At the [2006 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), he became the youngest player to represent and score for Argentina in the World Cup. Messi was named the best young player of the [2007 Copa América](/wiki/2007_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), during which he scored two goals and provided one assist. At the [2008 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament), Messi led [Argentina's U23 team](/wiki/Argentina_national_under-23_football_team) to claim the [Olympic gold medal](/wiki/Olympic_medal) over [Nigeria](/wiki/Nigeria_national_under-23_football_team); he was singled out by FIFA as the stand-out player from the tournament's best team.

With the international retirement of [Juan Román Riquelme](/wiki/Juan_Rom%C3%A1n_Riquelme) in 2009, Messi was given Argentina's number 10 shirt. Argentina built their team around Messi ahead of the [2011 Copa América](/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), although Messi scored no goals during the tournament. At 24 years old, Messi became captain of the national team, but over the next several years was unable to lead Argentina to a trophy. During the [2014 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup), Argentina lost to Germany in the [final](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Final), though Messi was awarded the [Golden Ball](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_awards#Golden_Ball). The following year, Argentina lost to [Chile](/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) in the [2015 Copa América](/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) [final](/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final); Messi was reportedly selected to receive the [Golden Ball](/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica#Awards), but he rejected the honour. During a semi-final match against the [United States](/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_soccer_team) during the 2016 [Copa América Centenario](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario), Messi became [Argentina's all-time leading goalscorer](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team#Top_goalscorers) in international matches. Argentina won the semi-final but lost again to Chile in the [final](/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario_final).

Losing three finals in three consecutive years caused Messi to retire from international football, but a nationwide campaign in Argentina helped convince him to return to the national team to lead them to the [2018 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup). Argentina were in jeopardy of missing the tournament on the last game of the [qualifiers](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(CONMEBOL)) against [Ecuador](/wiki/Ecuador_national_football_team), but a [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick) from Messi secured their entry. They went on to lose to [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) in the round of 16 during the World Cup, with Messi having one goal and two assists in the tournament. The next year, Messi had only a single goal and one assist in the [2019 Copa América](/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). Argentina finished the tournament in third place after defeating Chile, a victory which marked the start of a 36-game winning streak for the team that lasted for more than three years.

Messi facing off against [Érick Gutiérrez](/wiki/%C3%89rick_Guti%C3%A9rrez) of [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team) in the [2022 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup) group stage

Messi led his team to the end of a 28-year trophy drought in the [2021 Copa América](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), during which he surpassed [Javier Mascherano](/wiki/Javier_Mascherano) for most international appearances for Argentina. Messi's team defeated Brazil in the [final](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_final), and he was named the player of the tournament, having either scored or assisted nine of Argentina's 12 goals. He led Argentina to another victory at the [2022 Finalissima](/wiki/2022_Finalissima), where he was named player of the match. At the [2022 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup), Messi led Argentina to its first World Cup victory in 36 years, scoring twice in the [final](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_final) to defeat France. Having scored seven goals and assisted three during the tournament, Messi again won the World Cup's Golden Ball, becoming the first player to win it twice.

A hat-trick in a 2023 [friendly](/wiki/Friendly_(association_football)) against [Curaçao](/wiki/Cura%C3%A7ao_national_football_team) saw Messi reach 100 international goals, the third player and the first South American in history to achieve the milestone. Later that year, he became the all-time top goalscorer in [CONMEBOL](/wiki/CONMEBOL) World Cup qualifiers. In 2024, Argentina won the [Copa América](/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) for a second consecutive time, with Messi setting a new record for most appearances at Copa América, with 39. After providing two assists in a friendly against [Puerto Rico](/wiki/Puerto_Rico_national_football_team) later that year, Messi became the [top assist-provider](/wiki/Assist_(association_football)#Players_with_most_assists_in_international_football_(all-time)) in men's international football history.

## Player profile

### Style of play

Messi prepares to shoot with his dominant left foot in the [2014 FIFA World Cup final](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

Due to his short stature, Messi has a lower centre of gravity than taller players, which gives him greater agility and allows him to change direction more quickly and evade opposing tackles; the Spanish media has dubbed him ***La Pulga Atómica*** ("The Atomic Flea"). Despite being physically unimposing, Messi possesses significant upper-body strength, which, when combined with his low centre of gravity and resulting balance, aids him in withstanding physical challenges from opponents; he has consequently been noted for his lack of [diving](/wiki/Diving_(association_football)) in a sport rife with playacting. Messi's short, strong legs allow him short bursts of acceleration, while his quick feet enable him to retain control of the ball when [dribbling](/wiki/Dribbling#Association_football) at speed. His former Barcelona manager [Pep Guardiola](/wiki/Pep_Guardiola) once stated, "Messi is the only player that runs faster with the ball than he does without it." Messi is predominantly a left-footed player, although he has improved his ability with his weaker right foot since his mid-20s. He usually begins dribbling runs with the outside of his left foot, while using the inside of that foot to score and provide passes and assists.

A prolific goalscorer, Messi is known for his positioning, quick reactions, and ability to make attacking runs to beat the defensive line. He also functions in a [playmaking](/wiki/Playmaker) role, courtesy of his vision and range of passing. He has often been described as a magician, conjuring goals and opportunities where none seemingly exist. He is known for the accuracy of his [free kicks](/wiki/Direct_free_kick) and [penalty kicks](/wiki/Penalty_kick_(association_football)), and as of March 2026 he ranks second in career goals scored from direct free kicks with 71. He also has a penchant for scoring from [chips](/wiki/Chip_(association_football)).

Messi's dribbling abilities allow him to weave past several defenders and orchestrate attacking plays.

Messi's pace and technical ability enable him to undertake individual dribbling runs towards the goal, in particular during counterattacks, usually starting from the halfway line or the right side of the pitch. He is widely considered to be one of the greatest dribblers of all time. Former Argentina manager [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona) said of him, "The ball stays glued to his foot ... I've never seen anyone with Messi's ball control." Beyond Messi's individual qualities, he is also a well-rounded, hard-working team player, known for his creative combinations, in particular with former Barcelona midfielders [Xavi](/wiki/Xavi_(footballer,_born_1980)) and [Andrés Iniesta](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta).

As his career advanced and his athleticism and tendency to dribble diminished slightly with age, Messi began to dictate play in [deeper areas of the pitch](/wiki/Midfielder#Deep-lying_playmaker_(Strolling_10)) and developed into one of the best passers and playmakers in football history. His work-rate off the ball and defensive responsibilities have decreased as his career has progressed; by covering less ground on the pitch, and instead conserving his energy for short bursts of speed, he has been able to improve his efficiency, movement, and positional play, and has been able to avoid muscular injuries. While he was injury-prone in his early career, he was able to decrease his injury count by running less off the ball, and by adopting a stricter diet, training regime, and sleep schedule.

### Tactical positioning

A versatile forward, Messi often plays as a classic [number 10](/wiki/Midfielder#Attacking_midfielder).

Tactically, Messi plays in a free attacking role; a versatile player, he is capable of attacking on either wing or through the centre of the pitch. His favoured position in childhood was the playmaker behind two strikers, known as the *enganche* in Argentine football, but he began his career in Spain as a [left-winger](/wiki/Association_football_positions#Winger) or [left-sided forward](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Second_striker). Upon his first-team debut, he was moved onto the right wing by manager [Frank Rijkaard](/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard); from this position, he could more easily cut through the defence into the middle of the pitch and [curl](/wiki/Curl_(football)) shots on goal with his left foot, rather than predominantly [cross](/wiki/Cross_(football)) balls for teammates. Under Guardiola and subsequent managers, he most often played in a [false nine](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#False_9) role; positioned as a [centre-forward](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Centre-forward) or lone [striker](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Striker), he roamed the centre, often moving deep into midfield and drawing defenders with him, in order to create and exploit spaces for passes, other teammates' attacking runs off the ball, his own dribbling runs, or combinations with Xavi and Iniesta.

Under the stewardship of Barcelona manager [Luis Enrique](/wiki/Luis_Enrique), Messi returned to playing in the right-sided position that characterised much of his early career. He played in the manager's 4–3–3 formation, and was increasingly deployed in a deeper, free playmaking role in later seasons. Under [Ernesto Valverde](/wiki/Ernesto_Valverde), Messi played in a variety of roles. While he was still occasionally deployed in a deeper role—from which he could make runs into the box, on the right wing or as a false nine— he was also used in a more offensive, central role in a 4–2–3–1. He was also used as a second striker in a 4–4–2 formation, where he was once again given the licence to drop deep, link up with midfielders, orchestrate his team's attacking plays, and create chances for his attacking partner [Luis Suárez](/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez). With the Argentina national team, Messi has similarly played anywhere along the frontline. Under various managers, he has been employed on the right wing, as a false nine, as an out-and-out striker, in a supporting role alongside another forward, or in a deeper, free creative role as a classic number 10 playmaker or [attacking midfielder](/wiki/Midfielder#Attacking_midfielder) behind the strikers.

## Reception

Messi is widely regarded as one of the two best players of his generation, alongside Portuguese forward [Cristiano Ronaldo](/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo). He is also considered one of the greatest footballers in the history of the sport. In 2025, he was named the [All Time Men's World Best Player](/wiki/IFFHS#Top_10_players_of_all-time_(2025)) by the [IFFHS](/wiki/IFFHS).

A prodigious talent as a teenager, Messi established himself among the world's best players before age 20. Shortly after teammate [Ronaldinho](/wiki/Ronaldinho) won the [2005 Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2005_Ballon_d%27Or), he commented, "I'm not even the best at Barça" in reference to his 18-year-old protégé. Four years later, after Messi won his first [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or) by a record margin, the public debate regarding his qualities as a player moved beyond his status in contemporary football to the possibility that he was one of the greatest players in history. An early proponent was his then-[Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) manager [Pep Guardiola](/wiki/Pep_Guardiola), who, as early as August 2009, described Messi as the best player he had ever seen and would ever see. In the following years, this opinion gained greater acceptance among pundits, managers, former and current players. By the end of Barcelona's second treble-winning season, the perception of Messi as one of the greatest footballers of all time had become the apparent view among many fans and pundits in continental Europe.

Messi received dismissals by critics throughout his career because he had not won an international tournament at the senior level with Argentina. However, his subsequent victories in the [2021 Copa América](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and the [2022 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup) resulted in him finally winning every top tier trophy at both the club and country levels, an achievement that many pundits felt cemented Messi's legacy.

### Comparisons with Cristiano Ronaldo

Main article: [Messi–Ronaldo rivalry](/wiki/Messi%E2%80%93Ronaldo_rivalry)

Messi has been compared with [Cristiano Ronaldo](/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo) (left) throughout much of their careers.

Among his contemporary peers, Messi is most often compared and contrasted with Cristiano Ronaldo, who many consider his [career rival](/wiki/Messi%E2%80%93Ronaldo_rivalry). Both achieved numerous individual accolades, won dozens of trophies for both club and country, and are the two [leading goal scorers in history](/wiki/List_of_footballers_with_500_or_more_goals). Although Messi has at times denied any rivalry, they are widely believed to push one another in their aim to be the best player in the world. Pundits have compared the ongoing rivalry to [past sports rivalries](/wiki/List_of_sports_rivalries) like the [Muhammad Ali](/wiki/Muhammad_Ali)–[Joe Frazier](/wiki/Joe_Frazier) rivalry in boxing, the [Prost–Senna rivalry](/wiki/Prost%E2%80%93Senna_rivalry) in motorsport, and the tennis rivalries between [Federer–Nadal](/wiki/Federer%E2%80%93Nadal_rivalry) and [Borg–McEnroe](/wiki/Borg%E2%80%93McEnroe_rivalry).

Fans and pundits alike regularly argue the individual merits of both players. Messi is lauded for his combination of dribbling, playmaking, passing and goalscoring, while Ronaldo has received praise for his exceptional speed and athleticism, goalscoring skills, and performance under pressure. Beyond their playing styles, the debate also revolves around their differing physiques – Ronaldo is 1.87 m (6 ft 1+1⁄2 in) with a muscular build, while Messi is much shorter at 1.70 m (5 ft 7 in) – and their contrasting public personalities, with Ronaldo's self-confidence and theatrics differing from Messi's humility. Regarding individual achievements, Messi has won eight Ballons d'Or to Ronaldo's five, eight [FIFA World's Best Player](/wiki/The_Best_FIFA_Men%27s_Player#FIFA_Player_of_the_Year_(including_predecessors)) awards to Ronaldo's five, and six [European Golden Shoes](/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe) to Ronaldo's four. Off the pitch, Ronaldo is his direct competitor in terms of salary, sponsorships, and social media fanbase.

Messi's [head-to-head record](/wiki/Messi-Ronaldo_rivalry#Head-to-head_summary) against teams that feature Ronaldo consists of 15 wins, 9 draws, and 10 losses in competitive club matches. The first competitive matchup between the two occurred in 2008, when Ronaldo's [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United) played Messi's Barcelona in the [2007–08 UEFA Champions League semi-finals](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_stage#Semi-finals); [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United) advanced and eventually won the [final](/wiki/2008_UEFA_Champions_League_final). The two players and their club teams met again in the next year's [Champions League final](/wiki/2009_UEFA_Champions_League_final), with Messi and Barcelona achieving a 2–0 victory. After this, Ronaldo transferred to [Real Madrid](/wiki/Real_Madrid), the main rivals of Barcelona, which resulted in Messi facing Ronaldo many times in *[El Clásico](/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico)*, which ranks among the world's most viewed annual sports events. A number of football critics, commentators, and players have said that Messi's triumph with Argentina in the 2022 World Cup has settled the contest between the two players.

### Comparisons with Diego Maradona

Main article: [New Maradona](/wiki/New_Maradona)

> "I have seen the player who will inherit my place in Argentinian football and his name is Messi."

— [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona) hailing the 18-year-old Messi as his successor in February 2006

Throughout his career, Messi has been compared with [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona), who was also considered the best player of his generation and one of the greatest in the history of the sport. The comparisons stem broadly from both players' short statures, their similar playing styles as left-footed [playmakers](/wiki/Playmaker), and their shared nationality as Argentines. Initially, Messi was merely one of many young Argentine players to receive the "New Maradona" moniker, but as his career progressed, he proved his similarity beyond all previous contenders, establishing himself as the best player Argentina had produced since Maradona. When Messi was 18 years old, Maradona called him the best player in the world and hailed him as his successor. From 2008 through the [2010 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup), Messi played for Argentina's [national team](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) while Maradona managed the team, and it was during this period that Messi was given the number 10 shirt that Maradona famously wore. Maradona gave Messi his blessing, telling him: "the No 10 is yours. There's nobody better than you to wear it."

Due to their similar size and style of play, Messi has frequently been compared to his compatriot [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona).

During the early and middle parts of Messi's career, he was generally held in lesser esteem than Maradona in Argentine society. Part of this had to do with Messi's lack of tournament success and perceived uneven output with the national team; Maradona had famously led Argentina to victory in the [1986 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup), which set an expectation for Messi to do the same. Argentines also identified more with the fiery, extroverted and controversial Maradona who came from the slums, as opposed to the reserved, introverted and unassuming Messi who had a comparably unremarkable upbringing in Rosario. Maradona, along with several pundits and footballing figures, questioned whether Messi's temperament made him a capable leader for the national team. Furthermore, Messi's lack of outward passion for the Argentina shirt, his early tendency not to sing the national anthem, and his relative lack of emotional displays have led to a perception that he feels more Catalan than Argentine. However, despite having lived in Barcelona since age 13, Messi rejected the option of representing Spain internationally, saying: "Argentina is my country, my family, my way of expressing myself. I would change all my records to make the people in my country happy."

Football journalist [Tim Vickery](/wiki/Tim_Vickery) said the perception of Messi among Argentines changed in 2019, when Messi made a conscious effort to become "more one of the group, more Argentine". Other pundits noted that Messi had grown more assertive as a leader during the [2019 Copa América](/wiki/2019_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) by becoming more vocal with his teammates both on and off the pitch, finally singing the national anthem with the team before matches, and speaking with journalists at length after matches. Following Argentina's 2022 World Cup win, Vickery felt that Messi would now be held in the same esteem by his compatriots as Maradona. Journalist and former footballer [Jorge Valdano](/wiki/Jorge_Valdano) said he saw a "Maradonian" edge to Messi's performances during the tournament, while his compatriot [Osvaldo Ardiles](/wiki/Osvaldo_Ardiles) said that Messi's provocative actions towards the Netherlands team during the [quarter-final](/wiki/Battle_of_Lusail) resembled Maradona's behavior, which further endeared Messi to his fellow Argentines. Messi himself later remarked that the World Cup victory "won over all the people of Argentina. Today 95% or 100% of Argentines love me and that's a beautiful feeling."

## Cultural impact

### Popularity

Messi's success on the pitch has earned him lucrative endorsement deals along with widespread recognition and popularity.

Messi was among the [*Time* 100](/wiki/Time_100), an annual list of the world's most influential people, in 2011, 2012 and 2023. According to a 2014 survey in 15 international markets, Messi was familiar to 87% of respondents around the world, of whom 78% perceived him favourably, making him the second-most recognised player globally, behind [Cristiano Ronaldo](/wiki/Cristiano_Ronaldo), and the most likeable of all contemporary players. [World Press Photo](/wiki/World_Press_Photo) selected "The Final Game", a photo of Messi facing the [World Cup trophy](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy) after Argentina's final defeat to Germany, as the best sports image of 2014.

Messi's fanbase on [Facebook](/wiki/Facebook) is among the largest of public figures: by July 2023, he had [more than 114 million followers](/wiki/List_of_most-followed_Facebook_pages), the second highest for an athlete after Cristiano Ronaldo. Messi has over 500 million [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram) followers, the [second highest for an individual](/wiki/List_of_most-followed_Instagram_accounts) and athlete after Ronaldo. Messi's World Cup celebration post from 18 December 2022 is the [most liked post on Instagram](/wiki/List_of_most-liked_Instagram_posts) with more than 75 million likes. In April 2023, Messi was featured in the [Thrissur Pooram](/wiki/Thrissur_Pooram) festival in [Kerala](/wiki/Kerala), India. In December 2023, a set of shirts Messi had worn during the 2022 World Cup was sold at auction for $7.8 million.

During an [exhibition match](/wiki/Hong_Kong_v_Inter_Miami) on 4 February 2024 between Inter Miami and [Hong Kong League](/wiki/Hong_Kong_League) players at [Hong Kong Stadium](/wiki/Hong_Kong_Stadium), Messi remained on the bench the entire game. His absence from the pitch caused massive backlash in Hong Kong and China, as thousands of spectators had hoped to see him play. One Messi commercial was taken off the air in those regions, while other commercials continued to air despite pressure from Chinese social-media users. The [Chinese Football Association](/wiki/Chinese_Football_Association) temporarily halted its partnership with the [Argentine Football Association](/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association) over the incident.

### Wealth and sponsorships

Messi was the world's highest-paid footballer for five out of six years between 2009 and 2014. His salary broke records in 2013 (€41 million) and 2014 (€65 million). *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* listed him as the world's second highest-paid athlete in 2016, and as the [world's highest-paid athlete](/wiki/Forbes%27_list_of_the_world%27s_highest-paid_athletes) in 2019. In 2018, he was the first player to exceed the €100 million benchmark for a calendar year, with earnings of €126 million in combined income from salaries, bonuses and endorsements. In 2020, Messi became the second footballer to surpass $1 billion in career earnings.

In addition to salary and bonuses, much of Messi's income comes from endorsements. His brand was at first based exclusively on his talents and achievements as a player, in contrast to arguably more glamorous players like Ronaldo and [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham). At the start of his career, Messi mainly held sponsorship contracts with companies that employed sports-oriented marketing, such as [Adidas](/wiki/Adidas) and Pepsi. Since 2006, Adidas has been his largest sponsor, and he eventually became the company's leading brand endorser. Starting in 2010, Messi's marketing appeal broadened as he reached higher achievements as a player. He signed long-term endorsement deals with luxury brands [Dolce & Gabbana](/wiki/Dolce_%26_Gabbana) and [Audemars Piguet](/wiki/Audemars_Piguet), and became a [brand ambassador](/wiki/Brand_ambassador) for [Gillette](/wiki/Gillette_(brand)), [Turkish Airlines](/wiki/Turkish_Airlines), [Ooredoo](/wiki/Ooredoo), and [Tata Motors](/wiki/Tata_Motors), among other companies. Messi has also been the face of two video game series: [Konami](/wiki/Konami)'s *[Pro Evolution Soccer](/wiki/Pro_Evolution_Soccer)* and [EA Sports](/wiki/EA_Sports)' *[FIFA](/wiki/FIFA_(video_game_series))*.

Messi's Argentina jersey, supplied by Adidas, was sold out worldwide during the 2022 World Cup.

*[SportsPro](/wiki/SportsPro)* cited Messi as one of the world's most marketable athletes every year between 2010 and 2015. He became the first footballer to receive his own sub-brand of Adidas boots, the Adidas Messi, in 2015. The same year, a Barcelona jersey with Messi's name and number was the best-selling replica jersey worldwide. During the 2022 World Cup, Adidas sold out of Messi's No. 10 Argentina jersey worldwide.

In 2013, a Turkish Airlines advertisement starring Messi and [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) became the most-watched advertisement on [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube), with 137 million views. It was voted by users as the best advertisement of the decade 2005–2015. In June 2021, Messi signed a five-year deal to become a brand ambassador for [Hard Rock Cafe](/wiki/Hard_Rock_Cafe), and the following year he became a tourism ambassador for [Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Saudi_Arabia). He was criticised for partnering with Saudi Arabia because of the country's poor record on human rights.

### Public art

One of many street art displays depicting Messi

[Street art](/wiki/Street_art) and public [murals](/wiki/Mural) depicting Messi have been painted around the world. One prominent work of art is the *[Sistine Chapel of Football](/wiki/Sistine_Chapel_of_Football)*, a parody of [Michelangelo](/wiki/Michelangelo)'s *[The Creation of Adam](/wiki/The_Creation_of_Adam)* that features Messi, [Diego Maradona](/wiki/Diego_Maradona) and several other Argentine footballers. The work is exhibited at the Sportivo Pereyra club in [Barracas](/wiki/Barracas,_Buenos_Aires), [Buenos Aires](/wiki/Buenos_Aires).

[Madame Tussauds](/wiki/Madame_Tussauds) unveiled a wax sculpture of Messi at [Wembley Stadium](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium) in 2012. Several days after Messi announced his retirement from [Argentina's national team](/wiki/Argentina_national_football_team) in June 2016, a bronze statue of him was erected in [Buenos Aires](/wiki/Buenos_Aires) in an attempt to convince him to return. Following Argentina's 2022 World Cup victory, a life-sized statue of Messi holding the World Cup trophy was unveiled outside the [CONMEBOL](/wiki/CONMEBOL) headquarters in [Luque, Paraguay](/wiki/Luque,_Paraguay) in March 2023. The statue of Messi stands alongside statues of [Pelé](/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) and Maradona. On 13 December 2025, during Messi's [tour of India](/wiki/Lionel_Messi%27s_2025_India_Tour), a 70-foot (21-metre) statue of Messi holding the World Cup trophy was unveiled at the Sree Bhumi Sporting Club in [Lake Town, Kolkata](/wiki/Lake_Town,_Kolkata).

### Media

*[Messi](/wiki/Messi_(2014_film))*, a documentary directed by [Álex de la Iglesia](/wiki/%C3%81lex_de_la_Iglesia), premiered at the [71st Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/71st_Venice_International_Film_Festival) in August 2014. *[Messi's World Cup: The Rise of a Legend](/wiki/Messi%27s_World_Cup:_The_Rise_of_a_Legend)*, a biographical docuseries focusing on Messi's career, his highs and lows with the Argentina national football team, and their eventual success at the 2022 FIFA World Cup, aired on [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B) on 21 February 2024.

### Business ventures

On 4 June 2024, Messi announced the release of [Más+](/wiki/M%C3%A1s%2B), an American brand of [sports drinks](/wiki/Sports_drink) and [energy drinks](/wiki/Energy_drink). Messi created the drinks because he could not find a flavourful and healthy hydration option that suited his needs. On 19 September, Messi launched the production company 525 Rosario, which will produce films, sporting events coverage and branded commercials for athletes worldwide. It is a joint venture with Smuggler Entertainment, which co-produced *Messi's World Cup: The Rise of a Legend* earlier in 2024. In 2025, Messi was invited by his [Inter Miami](/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF) teammate [Luis Suárez](/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez) to play a role in [Deportivo LSM](/wiki/Deportivo_LSM), the Uruguayan professional football club that Suárez founded. On 16 April 2026, Messi acquired full ownership of the Spanish football club [UE Cornellà](/wiki/UE_Cornell%C3%A0).

## Philanthropy

Throughout his career, Messi has been involved in charitable efforts aimed at children, a commitment that stems in part from the medical difficulties he faced in his own childhood. Since 2004, he has contributed his time and finances to the [United Nations Children's Fund](/wiki/UNICEF) (UNICEF), an organisation that has also collaborated with [FC Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona). Messi has served as a [UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador](/wiki/UNICEF_Goodwill_Ambassador) since 2010; he completed his first field mission for the organisation that year as he travelled to Haiti to bring awareness to the plight of the country's children following the [2010 Haiti earthquake](/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake). He has since participated in UNICEF campaigns related to [HIV prevention](/wiki/Prevention_of_HIV/AIDS), education, and the social inclusion of disabled children. In November 2013, Messi and his one-year-old son Thiago were part of a publicity campaign to raise awareness of mortality rates among disadvantaged children.

In 2007, Messi launched the Leo Messi Foundation, which supports access to health care, education, and sport for children. It was established following Messi's visit to a hospital in Boston for terminally ill children, an experience that resonated with him to the point that he decided to reinvest part of his earnings into society. Through his foundation, Messi has awarded research grants, financed medical training, and invested in the development of medical centres and projects in Argentina, Spain, and elsewhere in the world. In addition to receiving funds from Messi's own fundraising activities, the foundation receives financial support from various companies which have endorsement agreements with Messi, with Adidas as the main sponsor. A gold replica of Messi's left foot, weighing 25 kg (55 lb) and valued at $5.3 million, went on sale in Japan in 2013 to raise funds for victims of the [2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami).

Messi has invested in youth football in Argentina. He supports Sarmiento, a football club based in the Rosario neighbourhood where he was born, and in 2013 he committed to the refurbishment of the club's facilities and the installation of all-weather pitches. Messi also financially supports the management of several youth players at [Newell's Old Boys](/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys) (his boyhood club) and rival club [Rosario Central](/wiki/Rosario_Central), as well as at [River Plate](/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate) and [Boca Juniors](/wiki/Boca_Juniors) in Buenos Aires. In 2012, Messi funded the construction of a new gymnasium and a dormitory inside Newell's [stadium](/wiki/Estadio_Marcelo_Bielsa) for their youth academy. His youth coach at Newell's, Ernesto Vecchio, is employed by the Leo Messi Foundation as a talent scout for young players. On 7 June 2016, Messi won a libel case against [*La Razón* newspaper](/wiki/La_Raz%C3%B3n_(Madrid)) and was awarded €65,000 in damages, which he donated to the charity [Médecins Sans Frontières](/wiki/M%C3%A9decins_Sans_Fronti%C3%A8res). During the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), Messi and his FC Barcelona teammates announced they would take a 70% salary cut to offset the club's financial losses, and said they would make further contributions to ensure club employees received full salaries while unable to work. Messi also donated €1 million to fight the spread of COVID-19.

In November 2016, while the [Argentine Football Association](/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association) was being run by a FIFA committee due to an economic crisis, three of the national team's security staff told Messi that they had not received their salaries for six months. He paid the salaries of the three employees. In February 2021, Messi made a donation to the [Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya](/wiki/Museu_Nacional_d%27Art_de_Catalunya): the Adidas shoes he wore when he scored his 644th goal for Barcelona and broke [Pelé](/wiki/Pel%C3%A9)'s record for most goals scored for a single club. The museum later auctioned off the shoes for £125,000 to support a charity focused on children with cancer.

Ahead of the [2021 Copa América](/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), Messi donated three signed sweatshirts to the Chinese pharmaceutical firm [Sinovac Biotech](/wiki/Sinovac_Biotech) – whose directors spoke of their admiration for Messi – in order to secure 50,000 doses of Sinovac's [COVID-19 vaccine](/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine), [CoronaVac](/wiki/CoronaVac), in the hope of vaccinating all South American football players. The plan, which was brokered by Uruguay's president [Luis Lacalle Pou](/wiki/Luis_Lacalle_Pou), caused some controversy for its focus on football players, given widespread vaccine scarcity on the continent. In January 2025, Messi was awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom), the highest civilian award of the United States, for his contributions to healthcare and education programs for children around the world.

## Personal life

### Family and relationships

Since 2008, Messi has been in a relationship with [Antonela Roccuzzo](/wiki/Antonela_Roccuzzo), who he eventually married on 30 June 2017 in their hometown of [Rosario](/wiki/Rosario,_Santa_Fe). Roccuzzo is the cousin of Messi's childhood best friend, [Lucas Scaglia](/wiki/Lucas_Scaglia), and Messi has known her since he was five years old. After keeping their relationship private for a year, Messi first confirmed their romance in an interview in January 2009, before going public a month later during a carnival in [Sitges](/wiki/Sitges) after the [Barcelona–Espanyol derby](/wiki/Derbi_Barcelon%C3%AD).

Messi and Roccuzzo have three sons. To celebrate Roccuzzo's first pregnancy, Messi placed the ball under his shirt after scoring in Argentina's 4–0 win against Ecuador on 2 June 2012, before confirming the pregnancy in an interview two weeks later. The child, a son named Thiago, was born in Barcelona on 2 November 2012. In April 2015, Messi confirmed that they were expecting another child. In October 2017, his wife announced they were expecting their third child. Messi and his family are [Catholic](/wiki/Catholic).

Messi enjoys a close relationship with his immediate family members, particularly his mother, Celia, whose face he has tattooed on his left shoulder. His professional affairs are largely run as a family business: his father, Jorge, has been his agent since he was 14, and his oldest brother, Rodrigo, handles his daily schedule and publicity. His mother and other brother, Matías, manage his charitable organisation, the Leo Messi Foundation, and take care of personal and professional matters in Rosario.

Since leaving for Spain at age 13, Messi has maintained close ties to Rosario, even preserving his distinct [Rosarino accent](/wiki/Rioplatense_Spanish). He has retained ownership of his family's old house, although it has long stood empty, and he maintains a penthouse apartment in an exclusive residential building for his mother, as well as a family compound just outside the city. Messi keeps in frequent contact with a small group of confidants in Rosario, most of whom were fellow members of "The Machine of '87" at [Newell's Old Boys](/wiki/Newell%27s_Old_Boys). He was on bad terms with the club after his transfer to Barcelona, but by 2012 their public feud had ended, with Newell's embracing its ties with Messi and issuing a club membership card to his newborn son. Messi has long planned to return to Rosario to end his playing career at Newell's. Messi has citizenship in Argentina, Italy, and Spain.

### Tax fraud

Messi came under investigation in 2013 for suspected [tax evasion](/wiki/Tax_evasion). Offshore companies in [tax havens](/wiki/Tax_haven) such as [Uruguay](/wiki/Uruguay), [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland) and [Belize](/wiki/Belize) were used to evade €4.1 million in taxes related to his sponsorship earnings between 2007 and 2009. Messi, who pleaded ignorance to the scheme, voluntarily paid arrears of €5.1 million in August 2013. On 6 July 2016, Messi and his father were both found guilty of tax fraud and were given [suspended](/wiki/Suspended_sentence) 21-month prison sentences and ordered to pay €1.7 million and €1.4 million in fines, respectively. In court, Messi told the judge, "I just played football. I signed the contracts because I trusted my dad and the lawyers and we had decided that they would take charge of those things." An unrelated [shell company](/wiki/Shell_company) in Panama set up in 2012 was subsequently identified as belonging to the Messis in the [Panama Papers](/wiki/Panama_Papers) data leak.

# List of prime ministers of New Zealand

Four New Zealand prime ministers pictured in 1992 (from left) – [David Lange](/wiki/David_Lange), [Jim Bolger](/wiki/Jim_Bolger), [Robert Muldoon](/wiki/Robert_Muldoon) and [Mike Moore](/wiki/Mike_Moore_(New_Zealand_politician))

The [prime minister of New Zealand](/wiki/Prime_minister_of_New_Zealand) is the country's [head of government](/wiki/Head_of_government) and the leader of the [Cabinet](/wiki/Cabinet_of_New_Zealand), whose powers and responsibilities are defined by [convention](/wiki/Constitutional_convention_(political_custom)). Officially, the prime minister is appointed by the [governor-general](/wiki/Governor-General_of_New_Zealand), but by convention, the prime minister must have the confidence of the [House of Representatives](/wiki/New_Zealand_House_of_Representatives). The prime minister is always a member of parliament.

Originally, prime ministers headed loose [coalitions](/wiki/Political_coalition) of [independents](/wiki/Independent_politician), which were often unstable; since the advent of [political parties](/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_New_Zealand), the prime minister is usually the leader of the largest party represented in the house. Since 1935, every prime minister has been a member of either the [National](/wiki/New_Zealand_National_Party) party or the [Labour](/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party) party, reflecting their domination of [New Zealand politics](/wiki/Politics_of_New_Zealand). After the introduction of [mixed-member proportional](/wiki/Mixed-member_proportional) voting in 1996, prime ministers have usually needed to negotiate agreements with smaller parties to maintain a majority in Parliament.

The title of the office was originally "[colonial secretary](/wiki/Colonial_Secretary_of_New_Zealand)", which was formally changed to "premier" in 1869. That title remained in use almost exclusively for more than 30 years, until [Richard Seddon](/wiki/Richard_Seddon) changed it to "prime minister" during his tenure in the office; he used the title officially at the [1902 Colonial Conference](/wiki/1902_Colonial_Conference).

Some historians regard [James FitzGerald](/wiki/James_FitzGerald_(New_Zealand_politician)) as New Zealand's first prime minister, although a more conventional view is that neither he nor his successor ([Thomas Forsaith](/wiki/Thomas_Forsaith)) should properly be given that title, as New Zealand did not yet have [responsible government](/wiki/Responsible_government) when they served. Most commonly, [Henry Sewell](/wiki/Henry_Sewell), who [served during 1856](/wiki/1856_Sewell_Ministry), is regarded as New Zealand's first premier. Beginning with Sewell, 42 individuals have so far held the premiership, not including [Hugh Watt](/wiki/Hugh_Watt), who was [acting prime minister](/wiki/Acting_prime_minister) following the death of [Norman Kirk](/wiki/Norman_Kirk). Eight prime ministers have held the position for more than one period in office. Richard Seddon, prime minister for thirteen years between 1893 and 1906, held the office for the longest term. The youngest prime minister was [Edward Stafford](/wiki/Edward_Stafford_(politician)), who assumed office at age 37, and the oldest was [Walter Nash](/wiki/Walter_Nash), who left office at age 78. Three prime ministers have been women, a count equalled by [Finland](/wiki/Finland), [Lithuania](/wiki/Lithuania), [Poland](/wiki/Poland) and the United Kingdom, and only surpassed by [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland).

The current prime minister is [Christopher Luxon](/wiki/Christopher_Luxon), who assumed office on 27 November 2023.

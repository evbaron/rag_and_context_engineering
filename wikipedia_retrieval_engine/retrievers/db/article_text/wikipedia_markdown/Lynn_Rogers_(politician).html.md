**Lynn Wayne Rogers** (born September 11, 1958) is an American politician and businessman who served as the 41st [Kansas State Treasurer](/wiki/Kansas_State_Treasurer) from 2021 to 2023. He previously served as the 51st [lieutenant governor of Kansas](/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_of_Kansas) from 2019 to 2021. A [Democrat](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)), he had previously served on the board of [Wichita Public Schools](/wiki/Wichita_Public_Schools_USD_259) from 2001 to 2017, and in the [Kansas Senate](/wiki/Kansas_Senate) representing the [25th district](/wiki/Kansas%27s_25th_Senate_district) in west [Wichita](/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas) from 2017 to 2019.

## Early life and education

Rogers was born on September 11, 1958, in [Fremont, Nebraska](/wiki/Fremont,_Nebraska). Raised by [Republican](/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) parents, he grew up on a large [hog](/wiki/Domestic_pig) farm in rural Nebraska. His grandfather was a [county commissioner](/wiki/County_commissioner), and his father served on a local school board.

Rogers earned a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in [business administration](/wiki/Business_Administration) from the [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](/wiki/University_of_Nebraska%E2%80%93Lincoln) in 1980.

## Career

After graduating from college, Rogers began working as a marketing specialist for [Citibank](/wiki/Citibank) Savings. After working for Citibank in [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago), he moved with his wife to [Wichita, Kansas](/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas) in 1985, where he accepted a marketing position with the [Farm Credit Bank](/wiki/Farm_Credit_System) of Wichita. In 1993, Rogers became a vice president with [CoBank](/wiki/CoBank) Farm Credit Leasing, where he would remain until his election to the [Kansas Senate](/wiki/Kansas_Senate) in 2016.

### School district politics

In the 1990s, as a parent of a public school student, Rogers was active in a Wichita-area [Parent-teacher organization](/wiki/Parent-teacher_organization) (PTO), and aided fundraising for the [Wichita Public Schools](/wiki/Wichita_Public_Schools), including the district's bond drive in 2000. Rogers' first elected office was as a member of the [Wichita School Board](/wiki/Wichita_Public_Schools_USD_259), where he served from 2001 to 2018.

During his tenure on the school board, Rogers switched his affiliation from the Republican Party to the [Democratic Party](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)), citing frustration with Republicans in attempts to gut support for the public schools.

### Kansas Senate

In 2016, he was elected to the [Kansas Senate](/wiki/Kansas_Senate)'s 25th district, replacing outgoing Republican Senator [Michael O'Donnell](/wiki/Michael_O%27Donnell_(Kansas_politician)), who vacated the seat to run for [Sedgwick County Commissioner](/wiki/Sedgwick_County,_Kansas). In his time as a senator, Rogers worked to "reverse the [Brownback](/wiki/Sam_Brownback)-[Colyer](/wiki/Jeff_Colyer) tax experiment and invest more in our schools." Rogers focused primarily on education, agricultural and banking issues.

In the Kansas Senate, senator [Laura Kelly](/wiki/Laura_Kelly) sat at the desk next to his, and became his mentor.

### Lieutenant governor

On May 24, 2018, Senator Kelly announced that she had selected Rogers as her running mate in the [2018 Kansas gubernatorial election](/wiki/2018_Kansas_gubernatorial_election). On November 6, 2018, Kelly and Rogers defeated the Republican gubernatorial ticket of [Kris Kobach](/wiki/Kris_Kobach) and [Wink Hartman](/wiki/Wink_Hartman), as well as the independent ticket of [Greg Orman](/wiki/Greg_Orman) and [John Doll](/wiki/John_Doll_(Kansas_politician)). Rogers became the first lieutenant governor from Wichita since fellow Democrat [Thomas Docking](/wiki/Thomas_Docking) served in the 1980s.

In addition to representing Kansas in official visits to other states, Rogers has spent much of his tenure traveling extensively throughout the state, engaging in political outreach, and visiting over half of the state's 105 counties.

### Kansas Treasurer

In December 2020, Governor [Laura Kelly](/wiki/Laura_Kelly) announced that she had selected Rogers to replace [Jake LaTurner](/wiki/Jake_LaTurner) as [Kansas State Treasurer](/wiki/Kansas_State_Treasurer) after LaTurner was elected to the [United States House of Representatives](/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives). Rogers assumed office on January 2, 2021, with [David Toland](/wiki/David_Toland) succeeding him as Lieutenant Governor. He ran for a full term in [2022](/wiki/2022_Kansas_State_Treasurer_election), losing to Republican State Representative [Steven C. Johnson](/wiki/Steven_C._Johnson_(Kansas_politician)).

## Personal life

Rogers is married to Kris Rogers. They have grown children, including a son who serves as a staff attorney for the [Oklahoma Supreme Court](/wiki/Oklahoma_Supreme_Court). They have a daughter who teaches in the [Kansas City](/wiki/Kansas_City) area, and another son "involved in [Wichita's](/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas) arts community."

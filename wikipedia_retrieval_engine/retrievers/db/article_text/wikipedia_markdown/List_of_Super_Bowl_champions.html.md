# List of Super Bowl champions

The [Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) defeated the [Chiefs](/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) in the first [AFL–NFL World Championship Game](/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_World_Championship_Game) ([Super Bowl I](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I)).

The [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl) is the annual [American football](/wiki/American_football) game that determines the champion of the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League) (NFL). The game culminates a [season](/wiki/National_Football_League_regular_season) that begins in the previous calendar year, and is the conclusion of the [NFL playoffs](/wiki/National_Football_League_playoffs). The winner receives the [Vince Lombardi Trophy](/wiki/Vince_Lombardi_Trophy). The contest is held in an American city, chosen three to four years beforehand, usually at warm-weather sites or [domed stadiums](/wiki/Stadium#Types). Since January 1971, the winner of the [American Football Conference (AFC) Championship Game](/wiki/AFC_Championship_Game) has faced the winner of the [National Football Conference (NFC) Championship Game](/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) in the culmination of the NFL playoffs.

Before the [1970 merger](/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger) between the [American Football League](/wiki/American_Football_League) (AFL) and the NFL, the two leagues met in four such contests. The first two were marketed as the "AFL–NFL World Championship Game", but were also casually referred to as "the Super Bowl game" during the television broadcast. [Super Bowl III](/wiki/Super_Bowl_III) in January 1969 was the first such game that carried the "Super Bowl" moniker in official marketing; the names "[Super Bowl I](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I)" and "[Super Bowl II](/wiki/Super_Bowl_II)" were retroactively applied to the first two games.

A total of 20 franchises, including teams that have relocated to another city or changed their name, have won the Super Bowl. There are four [NFL teams](/wiki/NFL_team) that have never appeared in a Super Bowl: the [Cleveland Browns](/wiki/Cleveland_Browns), [Detroit Lions](/wiki/Detroit_Lions), [Jacksonville Jaguars](/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars), and [Houston Texans](/wiki/Houston_Texans), though both the Browns ([1950](/wiki/1950_NFL_Championship_Game), [1954](/wiki/1954_NFL_Championship_Game), [1955](/wiki/1955_NFL_Championship_Game), [1964](/wiki/1964_NFL_Championship_Game)) and Lions ([1935](/wiki/1935_NFL_Championship_Game), [1952](/wiki/1952_NFL_Championship_Game), [1953](/wiki/1953_NFL_Championship_Game), [1957](/wiki/1957_NFL_Championship_Game)) had won [NFL Championship Games](/wiki/NFL_Championship_Game) prior to the creation of the Super Bowl in the [1966 season](/wiki/1966_NFL_season).

The [1972 Miami Dolphins](/wiki/1972_Miami_Dolphins_season) capped off the only [perfect season](/wiki/Perfect_season) in [NFL history](/wiki/History_of_the_National_Football_League) with their victory in [Super Bowl VII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VII). Only two franchises have ever won the Super Bowl while hosting at their home stadiums: the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers) in [Super Bowl LV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LV) and the [Los Angeles Rams](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) in [Super Bowl LVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI). The reigning champions are the [Seattle Seahawks](/wiki/Seattle_Seahawks) following their victory over the [New England Patriots](/wiki/New_England_Patriots) in [Super Bowl LX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LX).

## Streaks and rematches

### Consecutive wins

The [Steelers](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) defeated the [Rams](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) in [Super Bowl XIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIV) to win an unprecedented four championships in six years.

Eight franchises have won consecutive Super Bowls, one of which (Pittsburgh) has accomplished it twice:

- [Green Bay Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) (Super Bowls [I](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I) and [II](/wiki/Super_Bowl_II))
- [Miami Dolphins](/wiki/Miami_Dolphins) ([VII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VII) and [VIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VIII))
- [Pittsburgh Steelers](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) (twice: [IX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_IX) and [X](/wiki/Super_Bowl_X); [XIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIII) and [XIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIV))
- [San Francisco 49ers](/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) ([XXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIII) and [XXIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIV))
- [Dallas Cowboys](/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys) ([XXVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII) and [XXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII))
- [Denver Broncos](/wiki/Denver_Broncos) ([XXXII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXII) and [XXXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIII))
- [New England Patriots](/wiki/New_England_Patriots) ([XXXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII) and [XXXIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIX))
- [Kansas City Chiefs](/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) ([LVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVII) and [LVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII))

Although no franchise to date has won three Super Bowls in a row, several have had eras of sustained success, nearly accomplishing a [three-peat](/wiki/Three-peat):

- The [Green Bay Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) won the first two Super Bowls, and also won the [NFL Championship Game the preceding year](/wiki/1965_NFL_Championship_Game). If the Super Bowl had been instituted that year, the Packers would have qualified and faced the Buffalo Bills of the AFL.
- The [Miami Dolphins](/wiki/Miami_Dolphins) appeared in three consecutive Super Bowls ([VI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VI), [VII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VII), and [VIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VIII)) – winning the last two.
- The [Pittsburgh Steelers](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) won two consecutive Super Bowls ([IX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_IX) and [X](/wiki/Super_Bowl_X)); they also won two more consecutive Super Bowls ([XIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIII) and [XIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIV)) for four titles in six seasons.
- The [Dallas Cowboys](/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys) won two consecutive Super Bowls ([XXVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII) and [XXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII)); the [Cowboys](/wiki/1995_Dallas_Cowboys_season) won [Super Bowl XXX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXX) the following year for three titles in four seasons.
- The [New England Patriots](/wiki/New_England_Patriots) won Super Bowls [XLIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIX), [LI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LI), and [LIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIII) for three titles in five seasons. They also appeared in and lost [Super Bowl LII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LII) to the [Philadelphia Eagles](/wiki/2017_Philadelphia_Eagles_season) following the 2017 season, giving them four Super Bowl appearances in five years.
- The [Kansas City Chiefs](/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) came the closest to a three-peat, winning back-to-back Super Bowls in [LVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVII) and [LVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII) but losing their third consecutive appearance in [LIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIX). They additionally won [LIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIV) several years beforehand, and lost [LV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LV), for a total of five Super Bowl appearances in six seasons.

### Consecutive losses

Three franchises have lost consecutive Super Bowls:

- [Buffalo Bills](/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) (4) (Super Bowls [XXV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXV), [XXVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVI), [XXVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII), and [XXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII)) – The only team to appear in four straight Super Bowls; they lost in all four appearances.
- [Minnesota Vikings](/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings) (2) ([VIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VIII) and [IX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_IX)) – They also lost [Super Bowl XI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XI), and were [knocked out of the 1975–76 playoffs](/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_Dallas_Cowboys_17,_Minnesota_Vikings_14) by the eventual [Super Bowl X](/wiki/Super_Bowl_X) losers, [the Dallas Cowboys](/wiki/1975_Dallas_Cowboys_season), for three losses in four seasons.
- [Denver Broncos](/wiki/Denver_Broncos) (2) ([XXI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXI) and [XXII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXII)) – They also lost [Super Bowl XXIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIV), but did not qualify for the [1988–89 playoffs](/wiki/1988%E2%80%9389_NFL_playoffs) for [Super Bowl XXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIII) for three losses in four seasons.

The [Dallas Cowboys](/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys) are the only team with three separate streaks (1970–1971, 1977–1978, and 1992–1993). The New England Patriots played their first championship game in [Super Bowl XX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XX) (*pictured*) where they lost to the [Bears](/wiki/Chicago_Bears). This is the most recent [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl) where both teams had their first Super Bowl appearance. The Patriots hold the record for most Super Bowl appearances (12) and most losses (6) while being tied with the [Steelers](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) for most wins (6).

### Teams with Super Bowl appearances but no victories

Eight teams have appeared in the Super Bowl without ever winning. In descending order of number of appearances and then years since their last appearance, they are:

- [Minnesota Vikings](/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings) (4) – appeared in Super Bowls [IV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_IV), [VIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_VIII), [IX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_IX), and [XI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XI); they won the [NFL Championship](/wiki/1969_NFL_Championship_Game) in [1969](/wiki/1969_NFL_season), the last year before the [AFL–NFL merger](/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger), but failed to win [the subsequent Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl_IV).
- [Buffalo Bills](/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) (4) – [XXV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXV), [XXVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVI), [XXVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII), and [XXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII); in [1964](/wiki/1964_AFL_season) and [1965](/wiki/1965_AFL_season), they won the [last](/wiki/1964_American_Football_League_Championship_Game) [two](/wiki/1965_American_Football_League_Championship_Game) AFL Championships before the [first Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I) in [1966](/wiki/1966_AFL_season).
- [Cincinnati Bengals](/wiki/Cincinnati_Bengals) (3) – [XVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XVI), [XXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIII), and [LVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI); an AFL expansion team in [1968](/wiki/1968_Cincinnati_Bengals_season), they have no pre-Super Bowl league championships.
- [Carolina Panthers](/wiki/Carolina_Panthers) (2) – [XXXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII) and [50](/wiki/Super_Bowl_50); a post-merger expansion team, their first season was in [1995](/wiki/1995_Carolina_Panthers_season).
- [Atlanta Falcons](/wiki/Atlanta_Falcons) (2) – [XXXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIII) and [LI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LI); an NFL expansion team in [1966](/wiki/1966_Atlanta_Falcons_season), they have no pre-Super Bowl league championships.
- [Los Angeles Chargers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers) (1) – [XXIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIX) as the [San Diego Chargers](/wiki/History_of_the_San_Diego_Chargers); their only [AFL Championship](/wiki/1963_American_Football_League_Championship_Game) was in [1963](/wiki/1963_AFL_season), also as the San Diego Chargers.
- [Tennessee Titans](/wiki/Tennessee_Titans) (1) – [XXXIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIV); they won the [first](/wiki/1960_American_Football_League_Championship_Game) [two](/wiki/1961_American_Football_League_Championship_Game) AFL Championships in [1960](/wiki/1960_AFL_season) and [1961](/wiki/1961_AFL_season) as the [Houston Oilers](/wiki/History_of_the_Houston_Oilers).
- [Arizona Cardinals](/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals) (1) – [XLIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIII); they won two [NFL Championships](/wiki/1947_NFL_Championship_Game), one in [1925](/wiki/1925_NFL_season) and then again in [1947](/wiki/1947_NFL_season), both as the [Chicago Cardinals](/wiki/History_of_the_Chicago_Cardinals).

### Teams with no Super Bowl appearances or long active droughts

The [Jets](/wiki/New_York_Jets)' most recent championship appearance was their victory over the [Colts](/wiki/Baltimore_Colts) in [Super Bowl III](/wiki/Super_Bowl_III).

Four current teams have never reached the Super Bowl (shown in **bold** below). Two of them (Jacksonville and Houston) joined the NFL relatively recently, and there are an additional eight teams whose Super Bowl appearance droughts began prior to [2002](/wiki/2002_NFL_season) (the year Houston joined the NFL). The other two teams that have never appeared in a Super Bowl (Cleveland and Detroit) both held [NFL league championships](/wiki/List_of_NFL_champions_(1920%E2%80%9369)) prior to [Super Bowl I](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I) in the [1966 NFL season](/wiki/1966_NFL_season). Teams are listed below according to the length of their current Super Bowl droughts (as of the end of the [2025 season](/wiki/2025_NFL_season)):

- **[Cleveland Browns](/wiki/Cleveland_Browns)**, 60 years – NFL champions four times in [1950](/wiki/1950_NFL_Championship_Game), [1954](/wiki/1954_NFL_Championship_Game), [1955](/wiki/1955_NFL_Championship_Game), and [1964](/wiki/1964_NFL_Championship_Game); appeared in seven other [NFL Championship Games](/wiki/List_of_NFL_champions_(1920%E2%80%931969)) in [1951](/wiki/1951_NFL_Championship_Game), [1952](/wiki/1952_NFL_Championship_Game), [1953](/wiki/1953_NFL_Championship_Game), [1957](/wiki/1957_NFL_Championship_Game), [1965](/wiki/1965_NFL_Championship_Game), [1968](/wiki/1968_NFL_Championship_Game), and [1969](/wiki/1969_NFL_Championship_Game); and appeared in three [AFC Championship Games](/wiki/AFC_Championship_Game) in the [1986](/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_Denver_Broncos_23,_Cleveland_Browns_20_(OT)), [1987](/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_Denver_Broncos_38,_Cleveland_Browns_33), and [1989](/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_Denver_Broncos_37,_Cleveland_Browns_21) seasons.
- **[Detroit Lions](/wiki/Detroit_Lions)**, 60 years – NFL champions four times in [1935](/wiki/1935_NFL_Championship_Game), [1952](/wiki/1952_NFL_Championship_Game), [1953](/wiki/1953_NFL_Championship_Game), and [1957](/wiki/1957_NFL_Championship_Game); appeared in one other [NFL Championship Game](/wiki/List_of_NFL_champions_(1920%E2%80%931969)) in [1954](/wiki/1954_NFL_Championship_Game); and appeared in two [NFC Championship Games](/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) in the [1991](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_Washington_Redskins_41,_Detroit_Lions_10) and [2023 seasons](/wiki/2023_NFL_season).
- [New York Jets](/wiki/New_York_Jets), 57 years – Won [Super Bowl III](/wiki/Super_Bowl_III), [1968 season](/wiki/1968_American_Football_League_season)
- [Minnesota Vikings](/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings), 49 years – Lost [Super Bowl XI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XI), [1976 season](/wiki/1976_NFL_season)
- [Miami Dolphins](/wiki/Miami_Dolphins), 41 years – Lost [Super Bowl XIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIX), [1984 season](/wiki/1984_NFL_season)
- [Washington Commanders](/wiki/Washington_Commanders), 34 years – Won [Super Bowl XXVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVI), [1991 season](/wiki/1991_NFL_season) (played as [Washington Redskins](/wiki/History_of_the_Washington_Football_Team))
- [Buffalo Bills](/wiki/Buffalo_Bills), 32 years – Lost [Super Bowl XXVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII), [1993 season](/wiki/1993_NFL_season)
- [Los Angeles Chargers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers), 31 years – Lost [Super Bowl XXIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIX), [1994 season](/wiki/1994_NFL_season) (played as [San Diego Chargers](/wiki/History_of_the_San_Diego_Chargers))
- **[Jacksonville Jaguars](/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars)**, 31 years – [1995](/wiki/1995_Jacksonville_Jaguars_season) [expansion team](/wiki/Expansion_team#National_Football_League); three [AFC Championship Game](/wiki/AFC_Championship_Game) appearances in the [1996](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_New_England_Patriots_20,_Jacksonville_Jaguars_6), [1999](/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_Tennessee_Titans_33,_Jacksonville_Jaguars_14), and [2017 seasons](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NFL_playoffs#AFC_Championship_Game:_New_England_Patriots_24,_Jacksonville_Jaguars_20).
- [Dallas Cowboys](/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys), 30 years – Won [Super Bowl XXX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXX), [1995 season](/wiki/1995_NFL_season)
- [Tennessee Titans](/wiki/Tennessee_Titans), 26 years – Lost [Super Bowl XXXIV](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIV), [1999 season](/wiki/1999_NFL_season)
- **[Houston Texans](/wiki/Houston_Texans)**, 24 years – [2002](/wiki/2002_Houston_Texans_season) [expansion team](/wiki/Expansion_team#National_Football_League); [Divisional Round](/wiki/NFL_playoffs#Current_playoff_system) appearances in the [2011](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_Baltimore_Ravens_20,_Houston_Texans_13), [2012](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_New_England_Patriots_41,_Houston_Texans_28), [2016](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_New_England_Patriots_34,_Houston_Texans_16), [2019](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_NFL_playoffs#AFC:_Kansas_City_Chiefs_51,_Houston_Texans_31), [2023](/wiki/2023_NFL_season), [2024](/wiki/2024_NFL_season), and [2025](/wiki/2025_NFL_season) seasons. They are the only NFL team to never reach the Conference Championship round.

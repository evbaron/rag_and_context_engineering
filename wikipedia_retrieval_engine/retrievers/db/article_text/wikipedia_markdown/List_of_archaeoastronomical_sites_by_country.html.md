# List of archaeoastronomical sites by country

This is a list of sites where claims for the use of [archaeoastronomy](/wiki/Archaeoastronomy) have been made, sorted by country.

The [International Council on Monuments and Sites](/wiki/International_Council_on_Monuments_and_Sites) (ICOMOS) and the [International Astronomical Union](/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) (IAU) jointly published a thematic study on heritage sites of astronomy and archaeoastronomy to be used as a guide to [UNESCO](/wiki/UNESCO) in its evaluation of the cultural importance of archaeoastronomical sites around the world, which discussed sample sites and provided categories for the classification of archaeoastronomical sites. The editors, Clive Ruggles and Michel Cotte, proposed that archaeoastronomical sites be considered in four categories: 1) Generally accepted; 2) Debated among specialists; 3) Unproven; and 4) Completely refuted.

## Brazil

- [Parque Arqueológico do Solstício](/wiki/Parque_Arqueol%C3%B3gico_do_Solst%C3%ADcio) (called "Amazon Stonehenge"): An archaeological park located in [Amapá](/wiki/Amap%C3%A1) state, [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil), near the city of [Calçoene](/wiki/Cal%C3%A7oene). Archaeologists believe that this site was built by indigenous peoples for astronomical, ceremonial, or burial purposes, and likely a combination.

## Bulgaria

- [Magura Cave](/wiki/Magura_Cave), [Bronze Age](/wiki/Bronze_Age) "paintings of staggered black and white squares could have been used to count the days in a calendar month", possibly indicating the number of days in the solar tropical year.

- [Phnom Bakheng](/wiki/Phnom_Bakheng), According to [Jean Filliozat](/wiki/Jean_Filliozat) of the École Française, the center tower represents the axis of the world and the 108 smaller ones represent the 4 lunar phases each with 27 days.

## Canada

- [Petroforms of North America](/wiki/Petroforms) are shapes and patterns made by lining up large rocks on the open ground made by various [Native American](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Canada), [First Nation](/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada), and [Inuit](/wiki/Inuit) peoples in North America. They are believed to have been made as [astronomical calendars](/wiki/Solar_calendar).
- [Medicine Wheel](/wiki/Medicine_Wheel)

## Colombia

- *[El Infiernito](/wiki/El_Infiernito)*, (Spanish for "Little hell"), is a pre-Columbian [Muisca](/wiki/Muisca_people) site located in the outskirts of [Villa de Leyva](/wiki/Villa_de_Leyva), [Boyacá Department](/wiki/Boyac%C3%A1_Department), [Colombia](/wiki/Colombia). It is composed of several earthworks surrounding a setting of [menhirs](/wiki/Menhir) (upright standing stones); several burial mounds are also present. The site was a center of religious ceremonies and spiritual purification rites, and also served as a rudimentary astronomical observatory.

## Egypt

- [Abu Simbel](/wiki/Abu_Simbel), The axis of the temple was positioned by the [ancient Egyptian architects](/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_architecture) in such a way that twice a year, on October 20 and February 20, the rays of the sun would penetrate the sanctuary and illuminate the sculpture on the back wall, except for the statue of [Ptah](/wiki/Ptah), the god connected with the [Underworld](/wiki/Underworld), who always remained in the dark.
- [Buto](/wiki/Buto) temple at the El-Faraeen archaeological site, sixth century BC, discovered October 2024.
- [Nabta Playa](/wiki/Nabta_Playa) is an archaeological site in southern [Egypt](/wiki/Egypt), containing what may be among the world's earliest known archeoastronomical devices from the [5th millennium BC](/wiki/5th_millennium_BC). These include alignments of stones that may have indicated the rising of certain stars and a "calendar circle" that indicates the approximate direction of summer solstice sunrise.
- [Precinct of Amun-Re](/wiki/Precinct_of_Amun-Re)

## Finland

- The so-called [Giants' Churches](/wiki/Giant%27s_Church) (Finn. *jätinkirkko*), which are large, from c. 20 metres (66 ft) to over 70 metres (230 ft) long rectangular or oval stone enclosures built in the Neolithic (c. 3000–1800 BC), have axis and doorway orientations towards the sunrises and sunsets of the solstices and other calendrically significant days. For example, the [Kastelli of Raahe](/wiki/Giant%27s_Church), which is one of the largest Giants' Churches, had its five "gates", i.e. wall openings, oriented towards the midsummer sunset, the [winter solstice](/wiki/Winter_solstice) sunrise, winter solstice sunset, the sunrises of the mid-quarter days of early May (Walpurgis, Beltaine) and August (Lammas), as well as the sunrise 11 days before the vernal equinox in 2500 BC.

## Ireland

- [Newgrange](/wiki/Newgrange), once a year, at the winter solstice, the rising sun shines directly along the long passage into the chamber for about 17 minutes and illuminates the chamber floor. (Generally accepted). It was built during the [Neolithic period](/wiki/Neolithic_period), around 3200 BC, making it older than [Stonehenge](/wiki/Stonehenge) and the [Egyptian pyramids](/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids).
- [Knowth](/wiki/Knowth) is a Neolithic passage grave and ancient monument estimated to date from c. 3200 BC.
- [Dowth](/wiki/Dowth) in [Boyne Valley](/wiki/Boyne_Valley), [County Meath](/wiki/County_Meath) is a Neolithic passage tomb date with Astronomical alignments from between approximately 3200 and 2900 BC.
- [Loughcrew](/wiki/Loughcrew) near [Oldcastle, County Meath](/wiki/Oldcastle,_County_Meath) is a group of [megalithic](/wiki/Megalith) tombs dating back to the 4th millennium BC, designed to receive the beams of the rising sun on the spring and autumnal [equinox](/wiki/Equinox) - the light shining down the passage and illuminating the art on the backstone.
- [Carrowkeel](/wiki/Carrowkeel_Megalithic_Cemetery)
- [Mound of the Hostages](/wiki/Mound_of_the_Hostages)
- [Drombeg stone circle](/wiki/Drombeg_stone_circle), at the winter solstice, the sun sets into a v formed by two distant overlapping hills and makes an alignment with the altar stone and the two main uprights. Due to the nature of the site and the western hills, local mid-winter sunset is c. 15:50.
- [Beltany stone circle](/wiki/Beltany_stone_circle)
- [Beaghmore Stone Circles](/wiki/Beaghmore), a complex of early [Bronze Age](/wiki/Bronze_Age) megalithic features, stone circles and cairns. Some archaeologists believe that the circles have been constructed in relation to the rising of the sun at the solstice, or to record the movements of the sun and moon acting as observatories for particular lunar, solar or stellar events. Three of the stone rows point to the sunrise at the time of the solstice and another is aligned towards moonrise at the same period.
- [Boheh Stone](/wiki/Boheh_Stone), believed to have been aligned to the sun's path along the mountain of [Croagh Patrick](/wiki/Croagh_Patrick)

## Syria

- [Rujm el-Hiri](/wiki/Rujm_el-Hiri) is an ancient megalithic monument consisting of concentric circles of stone with a tumulus at center, in the [Golan Heights](/wiki/Golan_Heights), [territory occupied](/wiki/Israeli-occupied_territories) by Israel. It is believed that the site was used as an ancient calendar. At the times of the two equinoxes, the sun's rays would pass between two rocks, at the eastern edge of the compound. The entrance to the center opens on sunrise of the summer solstice. Other notches in the walls indicate the spring and fall equinoxes. It is also believed the site was used for astronomical observations of the constellations, probably for religious calculations. Researchers found the site was built with dimensions and scales common for other period structures, and partly based on the stars' positions.

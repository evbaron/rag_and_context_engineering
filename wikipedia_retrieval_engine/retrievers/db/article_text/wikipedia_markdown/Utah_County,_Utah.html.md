**Utah County** is the second-most populous [county](/wiki/County_(United_States)) in the [U.S. state](/wiki/U.S._state) of [Utah](/wiki/Utah). The [county seat](/wiki/County_seat) and largest city is [Provo](/wiki/Provo,_Utah), which is the state's fourth-largest city, and the largest outside of [Salt Lake County](/wiki/Salt_Lake_County). As of the [2020 United States census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 659,399.

Utah County is one of [two](/wiki/Juab_County,_Utah) counties forming the [Provo-Orem metropolitan area](/wiki/Provo-Orem_metropolitan_area), and is part of the larger [Salt Lake City metropolitan area](/wiki/Salt_Lake_City_metropolitan_area). In 2020, the center of population of Utah was in Utah County, in the city of [Saratoga Springs](/wiki/Saratoga_Springs,_Utah).

Utah County is one of the fastest-growing counties in the United States, ranking among the top ten counties in numerical growth. Correspondingly, Provo–Orem is among the top eight metropolitan areas by percentage growth in the country.

Utah County is one of seven counties in the United States to have the same name as its state. The other six counties are [Arkansas County](/wiki/Arkansas_County,_Arkansas), [Hawaii County](/wiki/Hawaii_County,_Hawaii), [Idaho County](/wiki/Idaho_County,_Idaho), [Iowa County](/wiki/Iowa_County,_Iowa), [Oklahoma County](/wiki/Oklahoma_County,_Oklahoma) and [New York County](/wiki/Manhattan) (commonly known as Manhattan).

## History

The legislature of the [State of Deseret](/wiki/State_of_Deseret) created a county on January 31, 1850, to govern the civic affairs of [Utah Valley](/wiki/Utah_Valley), which by the 1850s was bustling with newly arrived settlers. The county name is derived from the valley name, which is derived from the Spanish name (*Yuta*) for the [Ute](/wiki/Ute_people) [Indians](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States). The State of Deseret dissolved soon after (April 5, 1851), but the counties it had set in place continued. There is little record of any official activity conducted by the fledgling county until April 18, 1852, when a full slate of county officials was published, and recordkeeping began. The first courthouse was built in central Provo in 1866–67. It was soon outgrown and was replaced by a second courthouse (1872–73). By the 1920s, this building was also cramped, and the decision was made to erect a combined city-county building, which was completed in 1926.

The county's boundaries were adjusted in 1852, 1854, 1856, 1862, 1880, and 1884. It has retained its present boundary since 1884.

## Geography

Mount Timpanogos in the Wasatch Range is visible from much of Utah County.

A partial view of [Utah Valley](/wiki/Utah_Valley) seen here from outside of [Salem](/wiki/Salem,_Utah)

Utah County terrain ranges from steep mountain ranges in the east (the [Wasatch Range](/wiki/Wasatch_Range)), dropping steeply to a large lake-filled valley. Most of the comparatively level ground is dedicated to agriculture or developed uses, while most of the steep terrain is covered with arid-climate forestation. The county generally slopes to the west and north, with its highest point (the northern peak of the twin-peaked [Mt. Nebo](/wiki/Mount_Nebo_(Utah)) in the southern part of the county), at 11,928 ft (3,636 m) ASL. The county has an area of 2,144 square miles (5,550 km2), of which 2,003 square miles (5,190 km2) is land and 141 square miles (370 km2) (6.6%) is water.

[Utah Valley](/wiki/Utah_Valley) lies at the center of the county, lined by the mountains of the [Wasatch Range](/wiki/Wasatch_Range) on the east. [Utah Lake](/wiki/Utah_Lake) occupies a large part of the valley. The elevation ranges from 4,487 feet (1,368 m) above sea level at the lake to 11,928 feet (3,636 m) at the peak of [Mount Nebo](/wiki/Mount_Nebo_(Utah)).

### 2020 census

According to the [2020 United States census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census) and [2020 American Community Survey](/wiki/American_Community_Survey), there were 659,399 people in Utah County with a [population density](/wiki/Population_density) of 329.0 people per square mile (127.0/km2). Among non-[Hispanic or Latino](/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) people, the racial makeup was 518,460 (78.6%) [White](/wiki/White_Americans), 4,110 (0.6%) [African American](/wiki/African_Americans), 2,533 (0.4%) [Native American](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 10,111 (1.5%) [Asian](/wiki/Asian_Americans), 6,541 (1.0%) [Pacific Islander](/wiki/Pacific_Islander_Americans), 2,421 (0.4%) from [other races](/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States_census), and 26,692 (4.0%) from [two or more races](/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). 88,531 (13.4%) people were Hispanic or Latino.

There were 330,884 (50.18%) males and 328,515 (49.82%) females, and the population distribution by age was 216,133 (32.8%) under the age of 18, 389,673 (59.1%) from 18 to 64, and 53,593 (8.1%) who were at least 65 years old. The median age was 25.9 years.

There were 184,558 households in Utah County with an average size of 3.57 of which 148,476 (80.4%) were families and 36,082 (19.6%) were non-families. Among all families, 124,424 (67.4%) were [married couples](/wiki/Marriage), 8,394 (4.5%) were male householders with no spouse, and 15,658 (8.5%) were female householders with no spouse. Among all non-families, 23,748 (12.9%) were a single person living alone and 12,334 (6.7%) were two or more people living together. 88,011 (47.7%) of all households had children under the age of 18. 124,353 (67.4%) of households were [owner-occupied](/wiki/Owner-occupancy) while 60,205 (32.6%) were [renter-occupied](/wiki/Renting).

The median income for a Utah County household was $77,057 and the median family income was $83,938, with a [per-capita income](/wiki/Per-capita_income) of $27,365. The median income for males that were [full-time employees](/wiki/Full-time_job) was $60,356 and for females $37,391. 10.0% of the population and 6.9% of families were below the [poverty line](/wiki/Poverty_threshold).

In terms of education attainment, out of the 310,161 people in Utah County 25 years or older, 14,999 (4.8%) had [not completed high school](/wiki/High_school_dropouts_in_the_United_States), 51,916 (16.7%) had a [high school diploma](/wiki/High_school_diploma) or equivalency, 115,689 (37.3%) had some college or [associate degree](/wiki/Associate_degree), 88,312 (28.5%) had a [bachelor's degree](/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree), and 39,245 (12.7%) had a [graduate](/wiki/Postgraduate_education) or [professional degree](/wiki/Professional_degree).

## Government

View of the cities of [Lehi](/wiki/Lehi,_Utah), [American Fork](/wiki/American_Fork,_Utah), [Pleasant Grove](/wiki/Pleasant_Grove,_Utah), [Lindon](/wiki/Lindon,_Utah) and [Orem](/wiki/Orem,_Utah) along [Interstate 15](/wiki/Interstate_15_in_Utah) and the northeast shore of [Utah Lake](/wiki/Utah_Lake)

The government is a three-member elected [county commission](/wiki/County_commission) elected at-large. Other elected officials include the county [sheriff](/wiki/Sheriff), the [county clerk](/wiki/County_clerk), county recorder, county assessor, county surveyor, county treasurer, and the [county attorney](/wiki/County_attorney). The current county attorney is Jeff Gray.

In 2020, Utah County voters rejected Proposition 9, which would have changed the county's government to a five-member elected county council with an elected county mayor.

The first sheriff of the county was John T. Willis, who was succeeded by [William Madison Wall](/wiki/William_Madison_Wall). Alexander Williams served during [John Cradlebaugh](/wiki/John_Cradlebaugh)'s court in 1859. He was succeeded by Eli Whipple, who resigned in 1861 and was replaced by Russell Kelly. In 2020, Sheriff Mike Smith publicly stated he would not enforce [COVID-19 face mask mandates](/wiki/Face_masks_during_the_COVID-19_pandemic).

The Utah County Fire Department provides emergency response to all unincorporated areas within Utah County and works with all the incorporated cities within the county plus all Utah state and federal lands. The department is primarily a [wildland fires](/wiki/Wildland_fire) response and [urban interspace](/wiki/Urban_interspace) service with some structure fire and [HAZMAT](/wiki/HAZMAT) abatement capability.

Utah County has been referred to as "the most Republican county in the most Republican state in the United States". It has voted for a Democratic presidential candidate nine times since statehood, but has not done so since 1964.

In the [1992 presidential election](/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election), [George H. W. Bush](/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) received the most votes and [Bill Clinton](/wiki/Bill_Clinton) was third in votes received. In the [2004 presidential election](/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election), 85.99% voted for [George W. Bush](/wiki/George_W._Bush). In the [2008 U.S. presidential election](/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), the county voted for [John McCain](/wiki/John_McCain) by a 58.9% margin over [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama), compared to McCain winning by 28.1% statewide. Eight other Utah counties voted more strongly in favor of McCain. In the [2012 election](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election), [Mitt Romney](/wiki/Mitt_Romney) received 88.32% of the vote. In [2016](/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), it gave a slim majority of the vote to [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump), and nearly 30% of the vote to independent candidate [Evan McMullin](/wiki/Evan_McMullin), who outperformed [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in the county. This was McMullin's largest share of the vote in any county in Utah and his second best nationwide after [Madison County, Idaho](/wiki/Madison_County,_Idaho). In [2020](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election), Democratic candidate [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) received over 75,000 votes, easily a record for the party – no Democratic candidate had previously received more than 30,000 votes in the county – but it amounted to barely a quarter of the county vote as Trump received two-thirds of the total.

Until [2013](/wiki/2010_United_States_redistricting_cycle), Utah County was represented entirely by one congressional district. Currently, the county is split between two congressional districts. Most of the county's population is in the [3rd District](/wiki/Utah%27s_3rd_congressional_district), represented by Republican [Mike Kennedy](/wiki/Mike_Kennedy), former Provo Mayor. Much of the county's area however, including Utah Lake, resides in the [4th District](/wiki/Utah%27s_4th_congressional_district) currently represented by Republican [Burgess Owens](/wiki/Burgess_Owens).

The county's Republican bent runs right through state and local politics. All five state senators representing the county, as well as all 14 state representatives, are Republicans.

## Social issues

Utah County saw high rates of opioid and other prescription drug addiction from the mid-2000s onwards, foreshadowing the national [opioid crisis](/wiki/Opioid_crisis). The 2008 documentary *Happy Valley* examined the problem.

*Giving USA*, which reports on charitable giving in the US, named Utah County as one of the three most generous counties in philanthropic donations, alongside [San Juan County, Utah](/wiki/San_Juan_County,_Utah) and [Madison County, Idaho](/wiki/Madison_County,_Idaho).

In 2019, one in eight people and one in six children in the county [did not have sufficient food](/wiki/Food_security).

## Infrastructure

Much of Utah's transportation infrastructure was built to support automobiles. Prior to the 1950s, Utah County relied on the [U.S. Highway System](/wiki/United_States_Numbered_Highway_System) for local transportation. When [I-15](/wiki/Interstate_15_in_Utah) was built in 1956 (parallel to [Highway 89](/wiki/U.S._Route_89_in_Utah)), it became the dominant transportation vein in the state. The I-15 CORE project added multiple lanes on I-15 through most of Utah County. This expanded 24 miles (39 km) of freeway and was completed in 2012. Other construction projects by UDOT have been done on I-15 since then, including the Technology Corridor project and the Point of the Mountain project. However, the highway system retains its significance in Utah County due to the mountainous terrain. [Highway 6](/wiki/U.S._Route_6_in_Utah) is the closest major road connecting Colorado to the Wasatch Front, running through Spanish Fork Canyon before converging with I-15 in the city of Spanish Fork. Portions of Highway 89 have become prominent local roads known collectively as 'State Street'. [Highway 189](/wiki/U.S._Route_189_in_Utah) is known as 'University Avenue' in the city of Provo, and runs through [Provo Canyon](/wiki/Provo_Canyon) into [Heber](/wiki/Heber_City,_Utah) in neighboring [Wasatch County](/wiki/Wasatch_County).

Utah County has seen significant growth in public transportation over the past 15 years, owing in part to the county's large student population of more than 70,000 commuting to-and-from [Brigham Young University](/wiki/Brigham_Young_University) (BYU) in Provo and [Utah Valley University](/wiki/Utah_Valley_University) (UVU) in Orem. The two cities jointly operate [UVX](/wiki/Utah_Valley_Express), a [bus rapid transit](/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) system, as part of their city bus routes. Provo also serves as the southernmost terminus of the [FrontRunner](/wiki/FrontRunner), Utah's intrastate commuter rail service. The Provo FrontRunner station is located on South University Avenue, directly southwest of Amtrak's Provo Station—which is Utah's third westbound stop, after Green River and Helper, for the California Zephyr Amtrak route. In addition to Provo, The FrontRunner currently has three stops in the county. The Orem FrontRunner station is located on the west side of I-15, served by a pedestrian bridge over the freeway that connects the UVU campus directly to the station. An additional stop in [Vineyard, Utah](/wiki/Vineyard) was completed in August 2022. Utah County also operates the American Fork FrontRunner Station and the Lehi FrontRunner Station located near [Thanksgiving Point](/wiki/Thanksgiving_Point). From Lehi, the FrontRunner leaves Utah County and enters [Salt Lake County](/wiki/Salt_Lake_County).

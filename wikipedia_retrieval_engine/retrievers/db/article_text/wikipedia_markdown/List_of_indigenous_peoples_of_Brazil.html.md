# List of Indigenous peoples of Brazil

[Indigenous Territories](/wiki/Indigenous_territory_(Brazil)) in [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil)

Main article: [Indigenous peoples in Brazil](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Brazil)

This is a list of **Brazil's Indigenous or Native peoples**, associated languages, Indigenous locations, and population estimates with dates. A particular group listing may include more than one area because the group is distributed in more than one area.

## Background

The [Indigenous peoples in Brazil](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Brazil) ([Portuguese](/wiki/Portuguese_language): *povos indígenas no Brasil*) comprise a large number of distinct [ethnic groups](/wiki/Ethnic_groups), who have inhabited the country prior to the [European](/wiki/Europe). The word *índios* ("Indians"), was by then established to designate the people of the Americas and is still used today in the [Portuguese language](/wiki/Portuguese_language) to designate these peoples, while the people of Asiatic India are called *indianos*.

At the time of first European contact, some of the [Indigenous peoples](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) were traditionally semi-[nomadic](/wiki/Nomadic) tribes who subsisted on [hunting](/wiki/Hunting), [fishing](/wiki/Fishing), [gathering](/wiki/Hunter-gatherer), [agriculture](/wiki/Agriculture), and [arboriculture](/wiki/Arboriculture). Many of the estimated 2,000 nations and tribes which existed in the 16th century died out as a consequence of the European settlement. Most of the Indigenous population died due to European diseases and warfare, declining from an estimated pre-Columbian high of millions to some 300,000 in 1997, grouped into some 200 tribes. A few tribes were assimilated into the Brazilian population.

In 2007, [FUNAI](/wiki/Funda%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Nacional_do_%C3%8Dndio) reported that it had confirmed the presence of 67 different uncontacted tribes in [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil), an increase from 40 in 2005. With this addition Brazil has now surpassed [New Guinea](/wiki/New_Guinea) as the country having the largest number of [uncontacted peoples](/wiki/Uncontacted_peoples).

Seven *[Terras Indígenas](/wiki/Indigenous_territory_(Brazil))* (TI) (Reservations) are exclusively reserved for isolated people:

- TI Alto Tarauacá in [Acre](/wiki/Acre_(state)) – Various tribes. (Isolados do Alto Tarauacá)
- TI [Hi-Merimã](/wiki/Hi-Merima_people) in [Amazonas](/wiki/Amazonas_(Brazilian_state)) – Himerimã. (Isolados do médio Purus)
- TI Massaco in [Rondônia](/wiki/Rond%C3%B4nia) – Sirionó (Isolados do rio São Simão)
- TI [Igarapé Omerê](/wiki/Igarape_Omere) in Rondônia – [Kanoe do Omerê](/wiki/Kanoe_do_Omere) & [Akuntsu](/wiki/Akuntsu)
- TI Rio Muqui in [Rondônia](/wiki/Rond%C3%B4nia) – Isolados das cabeceiras do rio Muqui (Given as Miqueleno-Kujubim in the table).
- TI Rio Pardo in Mato Grosso and Amazonas – [Isolados do Rio Pardo](/wiki/Rio_Pardo_(tribe)) (Tupi–Guarani–Kawahibi).
- [TI Xinane isolados](/wiki/Terra_Indigena_Xinane_Isolados) in Acre – Unidentified.

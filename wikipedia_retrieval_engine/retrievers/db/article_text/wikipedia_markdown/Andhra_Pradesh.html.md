**Andhra Pradesh** is a [state](/wiki/States_and_union_territories_of_India) on the [east coast](/wiki/East_Coast_of_India) of [southern India](/wiki/South_India). It is the [seventh-largest state](/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area) and the [tenth-most populous](/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population) in the country. [Telugu](/wiki/Telugu_language), one of the [classical languages of India](/wiki/Classical_languages_of_India), is the most widely spoken language in the state, as well as its official language. [Amaravati](/wiki/Amaravati) is the state capital, while the largest city is [Visakhapatnam](/wiki/Visakhapatnam). [Amaravati](/wiki/Amaravati) is a greenfield capital city developed near Vijayawada. Geographically both Amaravati and Vijayawada can be seen as Twin cities. Andhra Pradesh shares borders with [Odisha](/wiki/Odisha) to the northeast, [Chhattisgarh](/wiki/Chhattisgarh) to the north, [Karnataka](/wiki/Karnataka) to the west, [Tamil Nadu](/wiki/Tamil_Nadu) to the south, [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana) to northwest and the [Bay of Bengal](/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal) to the east.

[Archaeological](/wiki/Archaeology) evidence indicates that Andhra Pradesh has been continuously inhabited from early archaic [hominins](/wiki/Hominini) to [Neolithic](/wiki/Neolithic) settlements. The earliest reference to the [Andhras](/wiki/Andhras) appears in the [Aitareya Brahmana](/wiki/Aitareya_Brahmana) (c. 800 [BCE](/wiki/BCE)) of the [Rigveda](/wiki/Rigveda). Around 300 BCE, the Andhras living in the Godavari and Krishna river deltas were renowned for their formidable military strength—second only to the [Maurya Empire](/wiki/Maurya_Empire) in the subcontinent. The first major Andhra polity was the [Satavahana dynasty](/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) (2nd century BCE–2nd century CE) which ruled over the entire [Deccan Plateau](/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) and even distant areas of western and central India. They established trade relations with the [Roman Empire](/wiki/Roman_Empire), and their capital, [Dhanyakataka](/wiki/Dharanikota), was the most prosperous city in India during the 2nd century CE. Subsequent major dynasties included the [Vishnukundinas](/wiki/Vishnukundina_dynasty), [Eastern Chalukyas](/wiki/Eastern_Chalukyas), [Kakatiyas](/wiki/Kakatiya_dynasty), [Vijayanagara Empire](/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire), and [Qutb Shahis](/wiki/Sultanate_of_Golconda), followed by [British rule](/wiki/British_Raj). After gaining independence, [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) was carved out of [Madras State](/wiki/Madras_State) in 1953. In 1956, it merged with Telangana, comprising the Telugu-speaking regions of the former [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State_(1948%E2%80%931956)), to form [Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/United_Andhra_Pradesh). It reverted to its earlier form in 2014, when the new state of [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana) was bifurcated from it.

The [Eastern Ghats](/wiki/Eastern_Ghats) separate the coastal plains from the [peneplains](/wiki/Peneplain). Major rivers include the [Krishna](/wiki/Krishna_River), [Godavari](/wiki/Godavari_River), [Tungabhadra](/wiki/Tungabhadra_River) and [Penna](/wiki/Penna_River). Andhra Pradesh holds about one-third of India's [limestone](/wiki/Limestone) reserves and significant deposits of [baryte](/wiki/Baryte) and [granite](/wiki/Granite). Agriculture and related activities employ 62.17% of the population, with rice being the [staple crop](/wiki/Staple_food). The state contributes 30% to India's fish production and accounts for 35% of the country's seafood exports. The [Sriharikota Range](/wiki/Satish_Dhawan_Space_Centre), located on [Sriharikota](/wiki/Sriharikota) island in [Tirupati district](/wiki/Tirupati_district), serves as India's primary satellite launch centre.

Andhra is the birthplace of the [Amaravati school of art](/wiki/Amaravati_art), an ancient Indian art style that influenced [South Indian](/wiki/South_India), [Sri Lankan](/wiki/Sri_Lanka), and [Southeast Asian](/wiki/Southeast_Asia) art. It is also home to [Kuchipudi](/wiki/Kuchipudi), one of [India's classical dance forms](/wiki/Indian_classical_dance), and has produced several renowned [Carnatic music](/wiki/Carnatic_music) composers. The state features prominent [pilgrimage centres](/wiki/Tourism_in_Andhra_Pradesh#Religious_and_pilgrimage_sites) and [natural attractions](/wiki/Tourism_in_Andhra_Pradesh#Nature_tourism), including the [Venkateswara temple](/wiki/Venkateswara_Temple,_Tirumala) in [Tirumala](/wiki/Tirumala) and the [Araku Valley](/wiki/Araku_Valley). Notable products with [geographical indication](/wiki/Geographical_indication) (GI) registration include [Tirupati Laddu](/wiki/Tirupati_Laddu), [Banganapalle mangoes](/wiki/Banganapalle_(mango)), [Kondapalli toys](/wiki/Kondapalli_toys), [Dharmavaram sarees](/wiki/Dharmavaram_handloom_pattu_sarees_and_paavadas), and [Pootharekulu](/wiki/Pootharekulu).

## Etymology

Main article: [Andhras](/wiki/Andhras)

[Andhras](/wiki/Andhras) were mentioned in [Aitareya Brahmana](/wiki/Aitareya_Brahmana) of the [Rigveda](/wiki/Rigveda) (c. 800–500 BCE) as descendants of the sage [Vishvamitra](/wiki/Vishvamitra). Andhras were referred to as non-Aryans living on the fringes of Aryan settlements. The [Satavahanas](/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty), the earliest kings who ruled this region were mentioned by the names *Andhra*, *Andhrara-jateeya*, and *[Andhrabhrtya](/wiki/Andhrabhrtya)* in the [Puranic literature](/wiki/Puranas). "Andhra" is both a tribal and a territorial name. The region inhabited by Andhras was called [Andhradesa](/wiki/Andhradesa).

According to [Iravatham Mahadevan](/wiki/Iravatham_Mahadevan), the term *'āndhra'* is derived from the [Old Telugu](/wiki/Old_Telugu) masculine nominative marker *'aṉṟu'*, possibly used by [Indo-Aryan](/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples) people or [Buddhists](/wiki/Buddhists) to refer to [Telugu people](/wiki/Telugu_people).

[Pradesh](/wiki/Pradesh) means "state" or "province".

## History

Main article: [History of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/History_of_Andhra_Pradesh)

### Pre-history

Excavated stone tools from [Hanumanthunipadu](/wiki/Hanumanthunipadu) in [Markapuram](/wiki/Markapuram_district) district were dated to be 2.47 [lakh](/wiki/Lakh) (247,000) years old ([Middle Paleolithic](/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic)). This finding means that these tools were developed by archaic hominins (pre-humans) as such tools were thought to be used by modern man migrating out of Africa about 2.10 lakh (210,000) years ago. In the absence of fossil evidence, it became a riddle to understand what happened to them. The discovery of petroglyphs, pictographs, and dolmens in Chakrala Bodu, near Boyalapalli village in the Yerragondapalem mandal area of Markapuram district indicates the presence of Neolithic-age human settlements in the region.

### Early and medieval history

Ruins of the Buddhist Maha Stupa at [Bhattiprolu](/wiki/Bhattiprolu), built during the 3rd century BCE–2nd century BCE

[Megasthenes](/wiki/Megasthenes) reported in his [*Indica*](/wiki/Indica_(Megasthenes)) (c. 310 BCE) that Andhras were living in the [Godavari](/wiki/Godavari_River) and [Krishna](/wiki/Krishna) [river deltas](/wiki/River_delta) and were famous for their military strength which was second only to Mauryans in all of [India](/wiki/Indian_subcontinent). Archaeological evidence from places such as [Bhattiprolu](/wiki/Bhattiprolu), [Amaravathi](/wiki/Amaravathi,_Guntur_district), and [Dharanikota](/wiki/Dharanikota) suggests that the Andhra region was part of the [Mauryan empire](/wiki/Maurya_Empire). After the death of Emperor [Ashoka](/wiki/Ashoka), Mauryan rule weakened around 200 BCE and was replaced by several smaller kingdoms in the Andhra region. One of the earliest examples of the [Brahmi](/wiki/Br%C4%81hm%C4%AB_script) script, from Bhattiprolu was used on an urn containing the relics of Buddha. This is considered as the rosetta stone for decipherment of Tamil Brahmi. Kadamba script, derived from Bhattiprolu Brahmi later led to the evolution of Telugu and Kannada scripts.

The [Satavahana dynasty](/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) dominated the [Deccan Plateau](/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) from the 1st century BCE to the 3rd century CE. It had trade relations with the Roman Empire. The Satavahanas made [Dhanyakataka-Amaravathi](/wiki/Amaravathi,_Palnadu_district) their capital. According to historian [Stanley Wolpert](/wiki/Stanley_Wolpert), it might have been the most prosperous city in India in 2nd century CE. [Nagarjuna](/wiki/Nagarjuna), the philosopher of [Mahayana](/wiki/Mahayana), lived in this region. Mahayana spread to China, Japan, and Korea. It became the largest Buddhist denomination in the world. [Amaravati School of Art](/wiki/Amaravati_art) is regarded as one of the three major styles of ancient Indian art and had a great influence on art in South India, Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia. The [Andhra Ikshvakus](/wiki/Andhra_Ikshvaku), with their capital at [Vijayapuri](/wiki/Vijayapuri_South), succeeded the Satavahanas in the [Krishna River](/wiki/Krishna_River) valley in the latter half of the 2nd century CE. The [Salankayanas](/wiki/Salankayana_dynasty) were an ancient dynasty that ruled the Andhra region between Godavari and Krishna rivers with their capital at [Vengi](/wiki/Vengi) (modern [Pedavegi](/wiki/Pedavegi)) around 300 CE. [Telugu Cholas](/wiki/Telugu_Chodas) ruled present-day Kadapa region from the six to the thirteenth centuries intermittently. Kallamalla *sasanam* (law), engraved in 575 CE during the rule of Dhanamjaya, is the earliest completely Telugu inscription.

[Undavali caves](/wiki/Undavalli_Caves), Guntur district

The [Vishnukundinas](/wiki/Vishnukundina_dynasty) was the first dynasty in the fifth and sixth centuries to hold sway over South India. [Undavalli Caves](/wiki/Undavalli_Caves) is an example of [Indian rock-cut architecture](/wiki/Indian_rock-cut_architecture) of that time. The [Eastern Chalukyas](/wiki/Eastern_Chalukyas) of Vengi, whose dynasty lasted for around five hundred years from the 7th century until 1130 CE, eventually merged with the [Chola dynasty](/wiki/Chola_dynasty). They continued to rule under the protection of the Chola dynasty until 1189 CE. At the request of King [Rajaraja Narendra](/wiki/Rajaraja_Narendra), [Nannaya](/wiki/Nannaya), considered the first Telugu poet, took up the translation of the Mahabharata into Telugu in 1025 CE.

[Kakatiyas](/wiki/Kakatiya_dynasty) ruled this region and Telangana for nearly two hundred years between the 12th and 14th centuries. They were defeated by the [Delhi Sultanate](/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate). [Bahamani sultanate](/wiki/Bahmani_Sultanate) took over when Delhi sultanate became weak. Around the same time, [Musunuris](/wiki/Musunuri_Nayakas) and [Reddi Kingdom](/wiki/Reddi_Kingdom) ruled parts of this region in the early 14th century. [Reddy](/wiki/Reddy) kings constructed [Kondaveedu Fort](/wiki/Kondaveedu_Fort) and [Kondapalli Fort](/wiki/Kondapalli_Fort). [Gajpathis](/wiki/Gajapati_Kingdom) ruled parts of this region, before the entire region became part of the [Vijayanagara Empire](/wiki/Vijayanagara_Empire) during the reign of [Krishnadevaraya](/wiki/Krishnadevaraya). [Pemmasani Nayaks](/wiki/Pemmasani_Nayaks) controlled parts of Andhra Pradesh and had large mercenary armies that were the vanguard of the empire in the 16th century. Several tanks and anicuts were built. Some of these include the Cumbum tank, Mopad tank, and Koregal anicut, Vallabhapur anicut across the [Tungabhadra](/wiki/Tungabhadra_River) River. The empire's patronage enabled fine arts and literature to reach new heights in Kannada, Telugu, Tamil, and Sanskrit, while [Carnatic music](/wiki/Carnatic_music) evolved into its current form. The [Lepakshi](/wiki/Lepakshi) group of monuments built during this period have mural paintings of the Vijayanagara kings, Dravidian art, and inscriptions. These are put on the [tentative list of the UNESCO World Heritage committee](/wiki/World_Heritage_Site#Nomination_process).

### Modern history

See also: [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) and [Andhra Pradesh (1956–2014)](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_(1956%E2%80%932014))

Parts of Andhra Pradesh in 1765 (left) ruled by [Nizam](/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad), [Carnatic Sultanate](/wiki/Carnatic_Sultanate), British [East India Company](/wiki/East_India_Company) and [Kingdom of Mysore](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mysore) and transformation to British East India Company rule by 1801 (map dated 1805) (right)

Following the defeat of the Vijayanagara Empire, the [Qutb Shahi dynasty](/wiki/Qutb_Shahi_dynasty) held sway over most of present-day general Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Later, this region came under the rule of the Mughal Empire. [Chin Qilich Khan](/wiki/Nizam-ul-Mulk,_Asaf_Jah_I) who was initially appointed as viceroy of Deccan by the Mughal in 1713, established himself as a semi-independent ruler as [Nizam of Hyderabad](/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad). In 1765, British Lord [Robert Clive](/wiki/Robert_Clive) obtained from the Mughal emperor [Shah Alam II](/wiki/Shah_Alam_II) a grant of four circars to the British East India Company, that was formalised in 1778 treaty with Nizam Ali, the 5th Nizam of Hyderabad state with addition of another circar. Later, four territories were ceded to the British by Nizam Ali in 1800, which eventually became the Rayalaseema region. Meanwhile, in the present day North Andhra, Raja Viziaram Raz (Vijaya Ramaraju) established a [sovereign kingdom](/wiki/Vizianagaram_Estate) and acquired neighbouring estates with the support of British. Later it fell out with the British and as a result, was attacked and defeated in the [battle of Padmanabham](/wiki/Battle_of_Padmanabham) in 1794. It was annexed as a tributary estate like other principalities and remained so until it acceded to the [Indian Union](/wiki/Dominion_of_India) in 1949. Following the annexation of Carnatic sultanate in 1801, the last major piece of the present day Andhra Pradesh came under British East India Company rule as part of [Madras Presidency](/wiki/Madras_Presidency). After the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), the region became part of [British crown](/wiki/British_Raj) till [India became independent](/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) in 1947.

[Dowleswaram Barrage](/wiki/Dowleswaram_Barrage) built in 1850 by [Arthur Cotton](/wiki/Arthur_Cotton)

Anicut at [Dowleswaram](/wiki/Dowleswaram_Barrage) built in 1850 by [Arthur Cotton](/wiki/Arthur_Cotton) and several others at Vijayawada, Nellore, Sangam, Sunkesula, and Polampalli are examples of irrigation facilities built during the British raj, that irrigated lacs of acres across coastal districts. Buckingham Canal built during 1806–1878, running parallel to the Coramandal coast from Kakinada to Marakkanam in Tamil Nadu served as a major water transportation route for goods till the 1960s. Telegraph service initiated in 1850 served for over 160 years till it was stopped on 15 July 2013 citing poor patronage due to advances in mobile communications and short message service. [Charles Philip Brown](/wiki/Charles_Philip_Brown) did pioneering work in transforming Telugu to the print era and introduced [Vemana](/wiki/Vemana) poems to English readers. [Kandukuri Veeresalingam](/wiki/Kandukuri_Veeresalingam) is considered the father of the Telugu renaissance movement, as he encouraged the education of women and lower caste people. He fought against Brahmin marriage customs such as child marriage, the bride price system, and the prohibition of widow remarriage.

[Potti Sreeramulu](/wiki/Potti_Sreeramulu), whose fast unto death in 1952 led to the formation of [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State).

To gain an independent state based on linguistic identity and to protect the interests of the Telugu-speaking people of [Madras State](/wiki/Madras_State), [Potti Sreeramulu](/wiki/Potti_Sreeramulu) fasted to death in 1952. The Telugu-speaking area of [Andhra State](/wiki/Andhra_State) was carved out of Madras state on 1 October 1953, with [Kurnool](/wiki/Kurnool) as its capital city. On the basis of the [Gentlemen's Agreement of 1956](/wiki/Gentlemen%27s_Agreement_of_1956), the [States reorganisation act](/wiki/States_Reorganisation_Act,_1956) created [Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_(1956%E2%80%932014)) by merging the neighbouring Telugu-speaking areas of the [Hyderabad State](/wiki/Hyderabad_State) with [Hyderabad](/wiki/Hyderabad) as the capital on 1 November 1956.

In the unified state, [Indian National Congress](/wiki/Indian_National_Congress) (INC) enjoyed a monopoly in ruling the state till 1983. After that [Telugu Desam Party](/wiki/Telugu_Desam_Party) (TDP) led by [N. T. Rama Rao](/wiki/N._T._Rama_Rao) (NTR) came to power and became another major party to rule the state. [Nagarjuna Sagar Dam](/wiki/Nagarjuna_Sagar_Dam) commissioned in 1967, [Srisailam hydro electric project](/wiki/Srisailam_Dam) commissioned in 1982 are few examples of irrigation and electricity projects. The increased presence of women, Dalits, and tribals in the social and political spheres of the state, driven by social movements, led to a rise in violence against these groups. Securing access to resources like land remains an unachieved objective in the effort to expand their economic opportunities in the state. When the union cabinet decided to consider the formation of Telangana state in 2009 heeding to the demand of [relaunched Telangana movement](/wiki/Telangana_movement), [Samaikyandhra Movement](/wiki/Samaikyandhra_Movement) opposing it took shape and the state went through a turmoil. Finally, the [Andhra Pradesh reorganisation act](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Reorganisation_Act,_2014) bill was passed by the [parliament of India](/wiki/Parliament_of_India) for the formation of the Telangana state, despite opposition by the state legislature. The new state of Telangana came into existence on 2 June 2014 after approval from the [president of India](/wiki/President_of_India), with the residual state continuing as Andhra Pradesh.

TDP formed the first government of the residual state with Chandrababu Naidu as chief minister. In 2017, [the government of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Government_of_Andhra_Pradesh) began operating from its new greenfield capital, [Amaravati](/wiki/Amaravati), for which 33,000 acres were acquired from farmers through an innovative land pooling scheme. In the [2019 elections](/wiki/2019_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election), [Y. S. Jagan Mohan Reddy](/wiki/Y._S._Jagan_Mohan_Reddy), leader of the [YSR Congress Party](/wiki/YSR_Congress_Party), became the chief minister by winning 151 out of 175 seats. He introduced the 'village and ward volunteers' system, and reorganised the state with 26 districts. Introduction of English as the medium of instruction was done in almost all the state schools. The move to three capitals with Amaravati getting reduced to being the legislative capital, Vizag as the executive capital and Kurnool as the judicial capital was stuck down by the High Court. His government appealed to the Supreme Court.

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Geography_of_Andhra_Pradesh) and [List of rivers in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_rivers_in_Andhra_Pradesh)

Andhra Pradesh relief map

Andhra Pradesh is the [seventh-largest state](/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area) with an area of 163,275 km2 (63,041 sq mi). Politically, the state shares borders with [Orissa](/wiki/Odisha) to the northeast, [Chhattisgarh](/wiki/Chhattisgarh) to the north, [Karnataka](/wiki/Karnataka) to the southwest, [Tamil Nadu](/wiki/Tamil_Nadu) to the south, [Telangana](/wiki/Telangana) to northwest and the [Bay of Bengal](/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal) to the east. [Yanam district](/wiki/Yanam_district), an enclave of [Puducherry](/wiki/Puducherry_(union_territory)), is in the state bordering [Kakinada district](/wiki/Kakinada_district). It has a coastline of around 974 kilometres (605 mi), which makes it the second-longest coastline in the nation.

The [Eastern Ghats](/wiki/Eastern_Ghats) are a major dividing line separating coastal plains and [peneplains](/wiki/Peneplain) in the state's geography. These are discontinuous, and individual sections have local names. The ghats become more pronounced towards the south and extreme north of the coast. Some of these consist of the [Horsley Hills](/wiki/Horsley_Hills), the Seshachala Hills, the [Nallamala Hills](/wiki/Nallamala_Hills), and the Papi Hills. [Arma Konda](/wiki/Arma_Konda), located in Visakhapatnam district, is the highest peak in the state. Peneplains, part of Rayalaseema, slope towards the east. The [Eastern Coastal Plains](/wiki/Eastern_Coastal_Plains) comprise the area of coastal districts up to the Eastern ghats as their border along the Bay of Bengal, with variable width. These are, for the most part, delta regions formed by the [Krishna](/wiki/Krishna_River), [Godavari](/wiki/Godavari_River), and [Penna](/wiki/Penna_River) rivers. The state has five different soil types with the majority soil types being red lateritic and black soil types. Most of the coastal plains are put to intensive agricultural use. The Kadapa basin, formed by two arching branches of the Eastern ghats, is a mineral-rich area.

[Gandikota gorge](/wiki/Gandikota) in YSR district

The valleys include [Araku Valley](/wiki/Araku_Valley), which is rich in biodiversity and [Gandikota gorge](/wiki/Gandikota). Gandikota gorge is a canyon formed between the Erramala range of hills, through which the Penna (Pennar) river flows. [Borra Caves](/wiki/Borra_Caves), created millions of years ago by water activity and the country's second-longest cave system, the [Belum Caves](/wiki/Belum_Caves) are in the state. The state has several beaches in its coastal districts, such as [Rushikonda](/wiki/Rushikonda), [Mypadu](/wiki/Mypadu), [Suryalanka](/wiki/Suryalanka_Beach).

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Natural vegetation and wildlife of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Natural_vegetation_and_wildlife_of_Andhra_Pradesh)

[Rose-ringed parakeet](/wiki/Rose-ringed_parakeet) (parrot) (state bird) feeding on [Neem](/wiki/Azadirachta_indica) fruits (state tree).

The total forest cover of the state is 29,784.3 square kilometres (11,499.8 sq mi), amounting to 18.28% of the total area. The Eastern ghats region is home to dense tropical forests, while the vegetation becomes sparse as the ghats give way to the peneplains, where shrub vegetation is more common. The vegetation found in the state is largely of dry deciduous types, with a mixture of [teak](/wiki/Teak), and genera of *[Terminalia](/wiki/Terminalia_(plant))*, *[Dalbergia](/wiki/Dalbergia)*, *[Pterocarpus](/wiki/Pterocarpus)*, etc. The state possesses some rare and endemic plants like *[Cycas beddomei](/wiki/Cycas_beddomei)*, *[Pterocarpus santalinus](/wiki/Pterocarpus_santalinus)*, *Terminalia pallida*, *[Syzygium alternifolium](/wiki/Syzygium_alternifolium)*, *Shorea tumburgia* etc.

The state has 3 [national parks](/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_India) and 13 [wildlife sanctuaries](/wiki/List_of_wildlife_sanctuaries_of_India) as of 2019. The diversity of fauna includes [tigers](/wiki/Tiger), [leopards](/wiki/Leopards), [cheetals](/wiki/Cheetal), [sambars](/wiki/Sambar_deer), [sea turtles](/wiki/Sea_turtle), and several birds and reptiles. The estuaries of the Godavari and Krishna rivers support rich mangrove forests with [fishing cats](/wiki/Fishing_cat) and [otters](/wiki/Otter) as [keystone species](/wiki/Keystone_species). [Coringa Wildlife Sanctuary](/wiki/Coringa_Wildlife_Sanctuary) is an example of [mangrove forests](/wiki/Mangrove) and salt-tolerant forest ecosystems near the sea. The area of these forests is 582 km2 (225 sq mi), accounting for about 9% of the local forest area of the state. Other sanctuaries include [Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam Tiger Reserve](/wiki/Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam_Tiger_Reserve), [Kolleru Bird Sanctuary](/wiki/Kolleru_Bird_Sanctuary), and [Nelapattu Bird Sanctuary](/wiki/Nelapattu_Bird_Sanctuary). [Neem](/wiki/Azadirachta_indica) (Azadirachta indica) is the state tree, jasmine is the state flower, [rose ringed parakeet](/wiki/Rose-ringed_parakeet) is the state bird, and [blackbuck](/wiki/Blackbuck) is the state mammal.

### Mineral resources

Mangampet [Barytes](/wiki/Baryte)

The state, with its varied geological formations, contains a variety of industrial minerals and building stones. Major minerals found in significant quantities in the state include [beach sand](/wiki/Beach_sand), [bauxite](/wiki/Bauxite), [limestone](/wiki/Limestone), [granite](/wiki/Granite), and [diamonds](/wiki/Diamond). Minor minerals include [barytes](/wiki/Barytes), [calcite](/wiki/Calcite), and [mica](/wiki/Mica). The largest reserves of [uranium](/wiki/Uranium) are in Tummalapalli village of YSR district. The state also has reserves of [oil](/wiki/Oil_reserves) and [natural gas](/wiki/Natural_gas).

### Climate

The climate varies considerably, depending on the geographical region. Summers last from March to June. In the coastal plain, the summer temperatures are generally higher than in the rest of the state, with temperatures exceeding 35 °C (95 °F). The minimum temperature during summer is about 20 °C (68 °F) in the far southwest. July to September is the season for tropical rains from the [southwest monsoon](/wiki/Monsoon#Southwest_monsoon). The winter season is from October to February. Low-pressure systems and [tropical cyclones](/wiki/Tropical_cyclone) form in the Bay of Bengal along with the [northeast monsoon](/wiki/Monsoon#Northeast_monsoon) during October to December, bringing rains to the southern and coastal regions of the state. The range of winter temperatures is generally 30 to 35 °C (86 to 95 °F) except in the northeast where it could fall below 15 °C (59 °F). [Lambasingi](/wiki/Lambasingi) in [Alluri Sitharama Raju district](/wiki/Alluri_Sitharama_Raju_district) is nicknamed the "Kashmir of Andhra Pradesh" as its temperature ranges from 0 to 10 °C (32 to 50 °F). The normal rainfall for the state is 966 mm (38.0 in).

Based on the 2011 [Census of India](/wiki/Census_of_India), the population of Andhra Pradesh is 49,577,103, with a density of 304/km2 (790/sq mi). The state has a [sex ratio](/wiki/Human_sex_ratio) of 997 females per 1000 males, higher than the national average of 926 per 1000. The [literacy rate](/wiki/Literacy) in the state stands at 67.35%. Erstwhile West Godavari district has the highest literacy rate of 74.32%, and erstwhile Vizianagaram district has the lowest with 58.89%.

Human Development Index (HDI) of the state for the year 2022 is 0.673. As of 1 January 2023, there are 39,984,868 voters including 3,924 third-gender voters. Kurnool district has the maximum number of voters at 1,942,233, while Alluri Sitharama Raju district has the minimum at 729,085.

[Lambadi](/wiki/Lambadi) and several other languages are spoken by the scheduled tribes of the state. 19% of the population aged 12+ years can read and understand English, as per the Indian readership survey for Q4 2019.

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Culture_of_Andhra_Pradesh)

Museums in Andhra Pradesh

Andhra Pradesh has 32 museums featuring a varied collection of ancient sculptures, paintings, idols, weapons, cutlery, inscriptions, and religious artefacts. The [Amaravathi archaeological museum](/wiki/Archaeological_Museum,_Amaravati) has a display of art traditions of Amaravathi and images of Buddha. [Bapu museum](/wiki/Bapu_Museum,_Vijayawada) in Vijayawada has historical galleries, stone cut writings, coins, swords, body armour, shields, arms, and ornamentation. *[Telugu Samskruthika Niketanam](/wiki/Telugu_Samskruthika_Niketanam)* in Visakhapatnam displays historical artefacts of the pre-independence era. The [Archaeological Survey of India](/wiki/Archaeological_Survey_of_India) identified 135 centrally protected monuments in the state of Andhra Pradesh. These include the reconstructed monuments at Anupu and [Nagarjunakonda](/wiki/Nagarjunakonda). The state has 17 [geographical indication](/wiki/Geographical_indication) (GI) registrations in the categories of agriculture, [handicrafts](/wiki/Handicraft), [foodstuffs](/wiki/Foodstuff), and textiles as per the *Geographical indications of goods (Registration and protection) act, 1999*.

### Clothing

[Kalamkari](/wiki/Kalamkari) artwork on a cloth

The men's traditional wear consists of *Panche*, a 4.5 metres (15 ft) long, white rectangular piece of non-stitched cloth often bordered in brightly coloured stripes. Women traditionally wear a *[sari](/wiki/Sari)*, a garment that consists of a drape varying from 5 to 9 yards (4.6 to 8.2 m) in length and 2 to 4 feet (0.61 to 1.22 m) in breadth that is typically wrapped around the waist, with one end draped over the shoulder, baring the midriff, as according to Indian philosophy, the navel is considered as the source of life and creativity. Women wear colourful [silk](/wiki/Silk) saris on special occasions such as marriages. The traditional wear of young girls is a half-saree with blouse. The shift to wearing western clothing of pant and shirt has become common for boys and men, while women also wear *salwar kameez* in addition to saris. [Dharmavaram textiles](/wiki/Dharmavaram_handloom_pattu_sarees_and_paavadas), [Machilipatnam](/wiki/Pedana_Kalamkari), and [Srikalahasti](/wiki/Srikalahasti_Kalamkari) [Kalamkari](/wiki/Kalamkari) handicrafts are few examples in clothes category with GI status.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Telugu cuisine](/wiki/Telugu_cuisine)

Vegetarian Andhra meal, served on important occasions

Andhra meals are combinations of spicy, tangy, and sweet flavours. The use of chillies, tamarind, and *[gongura](/wiki/Gongura)* (leaves of roselle) is common in Andhra food. Curry leaves are used copiously in most preparations of curries and chutneys. Various types of *Pappu* are made using lentils in combination with [tomatoes](/wiki/Tomato), [spinach](/wiki/Spinach), gongura, [ridge gourd](/wiki/Ridge_gourd), etc. Apart from curries, *pulusu*, a stew made using [tamarind](/wiki/Tamarind) juice in combination with vegetables, seafood, chicken, mutton, etc., is popular. *Pachchadi*, a paste usually made with a combination of [groundnuts](/wiki/Peanut), fried vegetables, and chillies, is a must in a meal. Pickles made using mangoes, gooseberries, lemons, etc. are enjoyed in combination with *Pappu*. Buttermilk and yoghurt mixed with rice eaten towards the end of the meal soothe the body, especially after eating spicy food items earlier.

A tamarind-rich dish popularly known as *Pulihora* is made with a thick sauce of tamarind, chillies, salt and asafoetida. It is tempered with mustard, curry leaves, peanuts and mixed with rice. It is offered even to the gods and then served as a *prasadam* (divine offering) in the temples to the devotees. Popular vegetarian curries include *Dondakaya koora* (ivy gourd cooked with coconut and green chilli) and *Guttivankaya koora*(stuffed brinjal curry). *Thalakaya koora* (lamb head gravy), *Royyala koora* (prawns cooked in tamarind), and *Natukodi koora* (freehold chicken curry with chillies) are popular non-vegetarian dishes across Guntur and surrounding regions. *Yeta mamsam kobbari* biryani (mutton cooked with coconut), *Seema kodi* (Rayalaseema-style chicken), *Chennuru dum biryani*, and *Gongura mamsam* (mutton cooked in sorrel gravy) are popular non-vegetarian dishes in [Rayalaseema](/wiki/Rayalaseema). Among fish preparations, *Pulasa pulusu*, thick aromatic and tangy gravy made from a single fish of the Godavari Pulasa variety is most desired in [Konaseema](/wiki/Konaseema_district). *[Ariselu](/wiki/Ariselu)*, *Burelu*, *[Laddu](/wiki/Laddu)*, and *[Pootharekulu](/wiki/Pootharekulu)* are some of the sweets made for special festivals and occasions. *Pootharekulu*, a preparation of sugar and rice flour and *Taandri*, a mango-flavoured sun-dried fruit jelly are popular sweets originating from Athreyapuram in Konaseema. [Kakinada](/wiki/Kakinada_district) *Khaaja* layered flour deep fried and dipped in sugar is another popular sweetmeat. *[Bandar laddu](/wiki/Bandar_laddu)*, *[Tirupati Laddu](/wiki/Tirupati_Laddu)* are some of the food products with GI status.

### Literature

Main article: [Telugu literature](/wiki/Telugu_literature)

[Nannayya](/wiki/Nannayya), [Tikkana](/wiki/Tikkana), and [Yerrapragada](/wiki/Yerrapragada) form the trinity who translated the Sanskrit epic *[Mahabharata](/wiki/Mahabharata)* into [Telugu](/wiki/Telugu_language) during 11–14 centuries. Nannayya wrote the first treatise on Telugu grammar, called *Andhra Shabda Chintamani* in Sanskrit. [Pothana](/wiki/Pothana) translated *[Bhagavatam](/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana)* into Telugu. [Vemana](/wiki/Vemana) was an Indian philosopher who wrote Telugu poems using simple language and native idioms on a variety of subjects including [yoga](/wiki/Yoga), wisdom, and morality. [Potuluri Veerabrahmendhra swami](/wiki/Potuluri_Veerabrahmam), a clairvoyant and social reformer of 17th century, wrote [Kalagnanam](/wiki/Kalagnanam), a book of predictions.

Telugu literature after [Kandukuri Veeresalingam](/wiki/Kandukuri_Veeresalingam) is termed *Adhunika Telugu Sahityam* (modern Telugu literature). He was the author of the first Telugu [social novel](/wiki/Social_novel) *Rajasekhara Charitram*, published in 1880. The use of colloquial idiom rather than *grandhik* (classical) in literature, championed by [Gurajada Apparao](/wiki/Gurajada_Apparao) and [Gidugu Ramamurthy Panthulu](/wiki/Gidugu_Venkata_Ramamurthy) led to increased literacy. Various forms of literature such as poetry, novel, short story were vibrant as indicated by the responses to national and international developments in various spheres of human life. The modern Telugu poetry, which began around 1900 developed into three forms – *Bhava kavitvam* (lyrical poetry), *Abhyudaya kavitvam* (progressive poetry), and new experimental poetry, including *Viplava kavitvam* (revolutionary poetry). Gurajada Apparao, [Rayaprolu Subbarao](/wiki/Rayaprolu_Subba_Rao), [Gurram Jashuva](/wiki/Gurram_Jashuva), [Viswanatha Satyanarayana](/wiki/Viswanatha_Satyanarayana), [Devulapalli Krishnasastri](/wiki/Devulapalli_Krishnasastri), and [Sri Sri](/wiki/Sri_Sri_(writer)) were some of the modern Telugu poets. Palagummi Padmaraju's short story *Galivaana* won second prize in World Short Story competition in 1952. Rachakonda Viswanadha Sastry initiated a new trend by focussing on the downtrodden in his novel Raju-Mahishi. Women writers such as Malati Chandur and Ranganayakamma dominated novels in the 1950s and 1960s. However, in the late 1970s, [Yandamuri Veerendranath](/wiki/Yandamuri_Veerendranath) started writing popular novels with a focus on sex, suspense and violence, which were serialised in magazines. Viswanatha Satyanarayana was conferred the first [Jnanpith Award](/wiki/Jnanpith_Award) for Telugu literature in 1970. Telugu film song as literature took shape in the 1930s. Some of the famous lyric writers include [Samudrala Senior](/wiki/Samudrala_Sr.), [Arudra](/wiki/Aarudra), [Athreya](/wiki/Acharya_Aatreya), [Daasarathi](/wiki/Daasarathi), and [C. Narayana Reddy](/wiki/C._Narayana_Reddy).

### Architecture

[Venkateswara Temple, Tirumala](/wiki/Venkateswara_Temple,_Tirumala), showing *Gopuram* (big tower) in the front and *Vimanam* (small tower) in gold color at the back

Traditional temple architecture is influenced by Dravidian and Vijayanagara styles. In Dravidian architecture for which [Tirumala temple](/wiki/Venkateswara_Temple,_Tirumala) is an example, the temples consisted of porches or *mantapas* preceding the door leading to the sanctum, gate-pyramids or *[gopurams](/wiki/Gopuram)* in quadrangular enclosures that surround the temple, and *pillared halls* used for many purposes. Besides these, the temple usually has a tank called the *Kalyani* or *pushkarni*. The gopuram is a monumental tower, usually ornate at the entrance of the temple forms a prominent feature. They are topped by the *kalasam*, a bulbous stone [finial](/wiki/Finial). *[Vimanam](/wiki/Vimana_(architectural_feature))* are similar structures built over the *[garbhagriha](/wiki/Garbhagriha)* or inner sanctum of the temple but are usually smaller than the gopurams. In the Vijayanagar style for which [Lepakshi Veerabhadra temple](/wiki/Veerabhadra_Temple,_Lepakshi) is an example, the main temple is laid out in three parts, these are: The assembly hall known as the *mukha* [mantapa](/wiki/Mantapa); *arda* mantapa or *antarala* (ante chamber); and the *[garbhagriha](/wiki/Garbhagriha)* or the sanctum sanctorum. Sri [Venkateswara](/wiki/Venkateswara) institute of traditional sculpture and architecture, run by [Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams](/wiki/Tirumala_Tirupati_Devasthanams) offers diploma courses. Civic architecture which mainly featured courtyard structure to support joint family system and simple round houses is giving way to modern apartments.

### Arts

Main articles: [Music of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Music_of_Andhra_Pradesh), [Telugu theatre](/wiki/Telugu_theatre), and [Carnatic music](/wiki/Carnatic_music)

See also: [Tholu bommalata](/wiki/Tholu_bommalata)

[Kondapalli Toys](/wiki/Kondapalli_Toys) at a house in Vijayawada

[Kuchipudi](/wiki/Kuchipudi) dance

[Kondapalli Toys](/wiki/Kondapalli_Toys), the soft limestone idol carvings of [Durgi](/wiki/Durgi,_Palnadu_district) and [Etikoppaka](/wiki/Etikoppaka) lacquered wooden toys are few handicrafts with GI status. [Kuchipudi](/wiki/Kuchipudi), the cultural dance recognised as the official dance form of the state of Andhra Pradesh, originated in the village of [Kuchipudi](/wiki/Kuchipudi,_Krishna_district) in Krishna district. Several renowned composers of [Carnatic music](/wiki/Carnatic_music) like [Annamacharya](/wiki/Annamacharya), [Kshetrayya](/wiki/Kshetrayya), [Tyagaraja](/wiki/Tyagaraja), and [Bhadrachala Ramadas](/wiki/Bhadrachala_Ramadasu) hailed from the state. *Sannai* and *Dolu* are common musical instruments of marriages, household, and temple functions in the state. *Harikathaa Kalakshepam (or [Harikatha](/wiki/Harikatha))* involves the narration of a story, intermingled with various songs relating to the story. *Burra katha* is an [oral storytelling](/wiki/Oral_storytelling) technique in which the topic is either a Hindu mythological story or a contemporary social issue. *Drama* is an [Indian theatre](/wiki/Theatre_of_India) art form that is still popular. Gurajada Apparao wrote the play [Kanyasulkam](/wiki/Kanyasulkam) in spoken dialect for the first time. It was first presented in 1892. It is considered the greatest play in the Telugu language.

The Telugu film industry (known as "[Tollywood](/wiki/Telugu_cinema)") is primarily based in Hyderabad, though several films are shot in Vizag, Tirupati, Rajamahendravaram. About 300 films are produced annually, [C. Pullaiah](/wiki/C._Pullaiah) is cited as the father of the Telugu cinema. Film producer [D. Ramanaidu](/wiki/D._Ramanaidu) holds a [Guinness record](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) for the most films produced by a person. Music composers and playback singers of the state include [Ghantasala](/wiki/Ghantasala_(musician)), [S. P. Balasubrahmanyam](/wiki/S._P._Balasubrahmanyam), [P. Susheela](/wiki/P._Susheela), [S. Janaki](/wiki/S._Janaki), and [P. B. Sreenivas](/wiki/P._B._Sreenivas). "Naatu Naatu" from the film [*RRR*](/wiki/RRR_(film)) became the first song from an Asian film to win the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) in 2023. Efforts are on to make Vizag as the hub of film industry by offering incentives.

### Festivals

*[Sankranti](/wiki/Makar_Sankranti)* is the major harvest festival celebrated across the state. It is celebrated for four days in the second week of January. The first day of the Telugu new year *[Ugadi](/wiki/Ugadi)* which occurs during March/April is also a special festival with the preparation and sharing of pickles (*pachhadi*) made from raw mangoes, neem flowers, pepper powder, jaggery and tamarind. Tasting this pickle which is a mix of different tastes teaches the importance of taking positive/negative life experiences in one's stride. Celebrations end with the recitation of the coming year's astrological predictions called *Panchanga sravanam*. [Vijaya Dasami](/wiki/Vijayadashami) known commonly as Dussera and [Deepavali](/wiki/Diwali), the festival of lights are other major Hindu festivals. Shivaratri is celebrated at [Kotappakonda](/wiki/Kotappakonda), with people from nearby villages preparing 80–100 ft height frames called *prabhalu* and taking it in a procession to the shine. [Eid](/wiki/Eid_al-Fitr) is celebrated with special prayers. *[Rottela Panduga](/wiki/Rottela_Panduga)* is celebrated at [Bara Shaheed Dargah](/wiki/Bara_Shaheed_Dargah) in [Nellore](/wiki/Nellore) with participation across religious lines. Christians celebrate their religious festivals Good Friday, Easter and Christmas with processions and prayers.

## Government and administration

Main articles: [Government of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Government_of_Andhra_Pradesh), [Politics of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Politics_of_Andhra_Pradesh), [Andhra Pradesh Legislature](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Legislature), and [List of districts in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_districts_in_Andhra_Pradesh)

Secretariat buildings hosting legislative assembly and council, Amaravati

Andhra Pradesh districts

There are a total of 175 [assembly constituencies](/wiki/List_of_constituencies_of_the_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly) in the state legislative assembly. The [legislative council](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Council) is the upper house with 58 members. In the [Indian parliament](/wiki/Parliament_of_India), the state has 25 seats in the *[Lok Sabha](/wiki/Lok_Sabha)* and 11 seats in the *[Rajya Sabha](/wiki/Rajya_Sabha)*. In accordance with the [Constitution of India](/wiki/Constitution_of_India), the [governor](/wiki/Governor_of_Andhra_Pradesh) is a state's *[de jure](/wiki/De_jure)* head and appoints the [chief minister](/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Andhra_Pradesh) who has the *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* executive authority.

Reddy, Kamma and Kapu communities cornered a share of 66% of seats in the 2019 election results. In the [2024 assembly elections](/wiki/2024_Andhra_Pradesh_Legislative_Assembly_election), Reddy, Kamma and Kapu communities among the forward classes got more allocation than OBC in the unreserved category by major political parties or alliances. TDP-led [National Democratic Alliance](/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance) with [Janasena Party](/wiki/Janasena_Party) and [Bharatiya Janata Party](/wiki/Bharatiya_Janata_Party) emerged victorious defeating the incumbent [YSRCP](/wiki/YSR_Congress_Party) led by [Jagan Mohan Reddy](/wiki/Y._S._Jagan_Mohan_Reddy). It won 164 seats, while YSRCP got 11, a big drop from 151 it held. N. Chandrababu Naidu became the chief minister of the state for the fourth time. According to an opinion by Ayesha Minhaz in The Hindu daily, several factors including the prevailing [anti-incumbency](/wiki/Anti-incumbency) against the previous government and the alliance's promise of "Welfare with wealth generation" contributed to the success of TDP-led alliance.

Andhra Pradesh police are structured in district, subdivision, circle, and police station hierarchy. In 2022, crimes against women and children saw a rise of 43.66% with a case count of 25,503 cases in Andhra Pradesh as per the National crime records bureau (NCRB) data. The state stood fifth in the country with 2,341 cybercrime cases. [Andhra Pradesh High Court](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_High_Court) at [Amaravati](/wiki/Amaravati) is headed by Chief justice with 37 judges. District judiciary is organised in a three-tier system with district courts at the top, civil (senior) and assistant sessions courts in the middle and civil (junior) and judicial first-class magistrate courts at the lower level. Apart from these, there are special courts such as family, CBI, ACB, Land reforms appellate tribunals, and industrial tribunals.

Andhra Pradesh comprises two regions, namely [Coastal Andhra](/wiki/Coastal_Andhra) and [Rayalaseema](/wiki/Rayalaseema) from a historical perspective. The northern part of Coastal Andhra is sometimes mentioned separately as [North Andhra](/wiki/North_Andhra), to raise voice against underdevelopment.

### Administrative divisions

Main articles: [List of districts of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Andhra_Pradesh), [List of revenue divisions in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_revenue_divisions_in_Andhra_Pradesh), and [List of mandals of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_mandals_of_Andhra_Pradesh)

The state is divided into 28 districts. These are further divided into 81 [revenue divisions](/wiki/Revenue_division), and 688 [mandals](/wiki/Tehsil) and 13,324 village panchayats as part of the administrative organisation.
Each district is administered by a [District Collector](/wiki/District_magistrate), who oversees land revenue administration, law and order, elections, and developmental activities. Revenue divisions are headed by [Revenue Divisional Officers](/wiki/Sub-divisional_magistrate) (RDOs), who may be Sub-Collectors (IAS officers) or Deputy Collectors. [Mandals](/wiki/Tehsil), the key sub-district units, consist of multiple villages and are managed by [Mandal Revenue Officers](/wiki/Tehsildar) (MROs), serving as the primary link between the district administration and village-level governance.

### Local government

See also: [List of urban local bodies in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_urban_local_bodies_in_Andhra_Pradesh)

Local government in Andhra Pradesh functions through a three-tier [Panchayati Raj system](/wiki/Panchayati_raj) in rural areas and [urban local bodies](/wiki/Municipal_governance_in_India) in towns and cities. Rural governance includes [Gram Panchayats](/wiki/Gram_panchayat), [Mandal Parishads](/wiki/Panchayat_samiti), and [Zilla Parishads](/wiki/District_council_(India)), while urban areas are managed by Nagar Panchayats, [Municipalities](/wiki/Municipal_council_(India)) and [Municipal Corporations](/wiki/Municipal_corporation_(India)). These bodies are responsible for local administration, development works, and delivery of basic services.

There are 17 municipal corporations, 77 municipalities, and 29 Nagar Panchayats for urban governance in Andhra Pradesh. Rural governance is managed by 13,234 Gram Panchayats at the village level, 676 Mandal Parishads at the mandal level, and 26 Zilla Parishads at the district level. Each of these bodies has elected councils and members.

### Government revenue and expenditure

For 2021–22, total receipts of the Andhra Pradesh government were ₹2.05 lakh crore (US$22 billion), inclusive of ₹53,284 crore (US$5.6 billion) of loans. States' tax revenue was ₹70,979 crore (US$7.5 billion). The top three sources of tax revenue are statedcrores goods and services tax (GST) (₹23,809 crore), sales tax/value added tax (VAT) (₹20,808 crores), and state excise (₹14,703 crores). The government earned a revenue of ₹7,345 crore from 2.574 million transactions for registration services. Visakhapatnam, Vijayawada, Guntur, and Tirupati are the top contributors to the revenue.

The government's total expenditure was ₹1,91,594 crore, which includes debt repayment of ₹13,920 crore. The fiscal deficit was 2.1% of the GSDP. Outstanding debt was ₹3.89 lakh crore, an increase of almost ₹40,000 crore compared to the previous year. This accounts for 32.4 per cent of the GSDP, which is more than the usual limit of 25% as per the Fiscal Responsibility Budget Management Actthe and Budget Management ActActstated crores. The outstanding guarantee estimate was ₹1,38,875 crore, of which power sector accounts for ₹38,473 crore equal to 12% of GDP. Out of the audited total expenditure of ₹1,77,674 crore of the state in 2021–22, social services accounted for 42.65% and capital expenditure 9.21%. When compared to 2020–21, the capital expenditure decreased by 14 per cent. Of the total expenditure, major components of the social services breakdown are as follows. The social welfare category accounted for 16%, education, sport, arts and culture had a share of 15% and health and family welfare had a share of 6.3%. Comptroller and Auditor general in its 2023 report warned that the trend of increasing levels of revenue expenditure coupled with a low level of capital spend will adversely impact infrastructure development, development, aggregate demand, employment generation, and revenue generation in the long run.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Economy_of_Andhra_Pradesh)

[Gross state domestic product](/wiki/Gross_state_product) (GSDP/GDP) at current prices for the year 2022–23 is estimated at ₹1,317,728 crore (US$140 billion) (advanced estimates). The share of agriculture's contribution to the GSDP is at 36.19%, while industry is at 23.36%, and services are at 40.45%. The state posted a record growth of 7.02% at constant prices (2011–12) against the country's growth of 7%. GDP per capita is estimated at ₹219,518 (US$2,300). The sectoral growth rates at constant 2011–12 prices were for agriculture at 4.54%; industry at 5.66 and services at 10.05%. Poverty rate is reduced to 4.2% in 2023 from 11.77% in 2015–16 as per *Niti Ayog* report. The methodology, based on the global multidimensional poverty index uses 10 indicators, covering three areas health, education, and standard of living and additionally maternal health and bank accounts. Unemployment rate of people with graduation in Andhra Pradesh is estimated at 24% as per periodic labour force survey of July 2022 to June 2023, the third highest in the country. The number for the whole of India is at 13.4%.

According to the *Economic Survey of India 2024–25*, Andhra Pradesh was identified as the "leading performer" in the agriculture and allied sectors (excluding forestry and logging), recording a Compounded Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 8.80%. It was followed by Madhya Pradesh with 6.30% and Tamil Nadu with 4.80%. Andhra Pradesh also ranked 10th in Gross State Value Added (GSVA) in the services sector.

### Agriculture

Lush green farms in [Konaseema](/wiki/Konaseema_district)

The agricultural economy comprises agriculture, livestock, poultry farming, and fisheries. Four important rivers in India, the Godavari, Krishna, Penna, and [Tungabhadra](/wiki/Tungabhadra_River), flow through the state and provide irrigation. The population engaged in agriculture and related activities as per 2020–21 fiscal data is 62.17%. [Rice](/wiki/Rice) is the state's major food crop and staple food. Besides rice, farmers grow [jowar](/wiki/Sorghum_bicolor), [bajra](/wiki/Pearl_millet), [maize](/wiki/Maize), many varieties of [pulses](/wiki/Legume), [sugarcane](/wiki/Sugarcane), [cotton](/wiki/Cotton), [tobacco](/wiki/Tobacco), fruits, and vegetables. The state contributed to 30% of fish production of India and it had a share of 35% in total sea food exports of India in 2022–23. The state has three agricultural export zones: the undivided Chittoor district for mango pulp and vegetables, the undivided Krishna district for mangoes, and the undivided Guntur district for [chillies](/wiki/Chili_pepper). [Banaganapalle mangoes](/wiki/Banganapalle_(mango)) produced in the state were accorded GI status in 2017.

*Rythu Seva Kendras* (RSK) or farmer facilitation centres are initiated by the government in 2020 to serve as a hassle-free, one-stop solution for the requirements of farmers from seed-to-sale. Banking services through banking correspondent are also integrated. As of 2023, 10,778 RSKs are functioning. AP land titling act 2023 was brought in to change from presumptive land ownership system based on possession, registration or inheritance documents to conclusive land ownership system, with government standing as surety for the ownership. Lands in 6000 villages were surveyed with drones and land title certificates were issued to owners, with [georeferencing](/wiki/Georeferencing) of their land parcels. The benefits of the act include a reduction in land disputes and easier acquisition of lands for public requirements. The newly formed TDP led NDA alliance government decided to repeal the act citing the scope for misuse of authority and deviation from the centre's draft bill.

The Commission on Inclusive and Sustainable Agricultural Development of Andhra Pradesh, chaired by Prof. R. Radhakrishna, estimated the number of tenant farmers at 24.25 lakh in 2014. This consists of 6.29 lakh landless tenants and the rest owning some land. Out of 60.73 lakh hectares under cultivation, 27.15 lakh hectares or 44% was cultivated by tenants. Departmental estimates for 2021 put the number of tenant farmers at about 16 lakh. To identify and serve the needs of tenants, the Crop Cultivator Rights Act (CCRA) of 2019 was brought out. Only 26% of tenants obtained the CCRA or loan eligibility cards as per 2022 data.

Chandrayaan-3 – Launch vehicle lifting off from the second launch pad of [SDSC](/wiki/Satish_Dhawan_Space_Centre)-SHAR, Sriharikota

As per the annual survey of industries 2019–20, the number of factories was 12,582 with 681,224 employees. The top four employment providers are food products (25.48%), non-metallic minerals (11.26%), textiles (9.35%), and pharmaceuticals (8.68%). Gross value added (GVA) contributed by the industrial sector is ₹55,035 crore (US$5.8 billion), of which food products (18.95%), pharmaceuticals (17.01%), and non-metallic minerals (16.25%) are the top three contributors. From a district perspective, the top three districts were Visakhapatnam, Tirupati and undivided Krishna.

Andhra Pradesh hosts over 50 notified SEZs, spanning sectors like IT/ITES, pharmaceuticals, multi-product, biotechnology, and textiles, with a focus on export-oriented growth. Key operational examples include: [Visakhapatnam Special Economic Zone](/wiki/Visakhapatnam_Special_Economic_Zone), [Sri City](/wiki/Sri_City), [Andhra Pradesh Special Economic Zone](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_Special_Economic_Zone) and several others.

The defence administered Hindustan Shipyard Limited built the first ship in [India](/wiki/India) in 1948. The state has 36 big auto players, such as [Ashok Leyland](/wiki/Ashok_Leyland), [Hero Motors](/wiki/Hero_Motors), [Isuzu Motors India](/wiki/Isuzu_Motors_India), and [Kia Motors](/wiki/Kia_Motors_India), The mining sector contributed ₹3,390 crore (US$360 million) in revenue to the state during 2021–22. Nearly 225 million barrels of crude oil was produced from [Ravva block](/wiki/Ravva_oil_field), in the shallow offshore area of the Krishna Godavari basin during 1994–2011. The state accounted for 0.6% of had and codensate production, and 2.9% of natural gas production of India in 2020–21.

As of 20 June 2023, there are 190 science and technology organisations in Andhra Pradesh, including 12 central labs and research institutions. [Satish Dhawan Space Centre](/wiki/Satish_Dhawan_Space_Centre) (SDSC), known as Sriharikota range (SHAR), on the barrier island of [Sriharikota](/wiki/Sriharikota) in [Tirupati district](/wiki/Tirupati_district), is the primary satellite launching station operated by the [Indian Space Research Organisation](/wiki/Indian_Space_Research_Organisation).

### Services

Trade, hotels & restaurants registered the highest growth rate of 16.64%, while public administration, the lowest growth rate of 4.24% for the year 2022–23 at constant 2011–12 prices among the services category. The state is ranked third in domestic tourist footfalls for the year 2021, with 93.2 million domestic tourists, which amounts to 13.8% of all domestic tourists in India. A major share of the tourists visit temples in Tirupati, Vijayawada, and Srisailam. The value of [information technology](/wiki/Information_technology_in_India) exports from the state in 2021–22 was ₹926 crore (US$98 million), which is 0.14% of the IT exports from India. Exports have remained below 0.2% in the past five years.

#### Railways

[Renigunta](/wiki/Renigunta_Junction_railway_station) was the first among major stations to appear on the railway map of Andhra Pradesh when [Madras Railway](/wiki/Madras_Railway) connected it to [Madras](/wiki/Madras) in 1862. [Rajahmundry](/wiki/Rajahmundry)-[Vizag](/wiki/Vizag) and [Vijayawada](/wiki/Vijayawada)-[Kovvur](/wiki/Kovvur) sections were opened in 1893. Vijayawada-Madras section was started in 1899. It was extended to [Howrah](/wiki/Howrah) in 1900. As of 2022, the state has a total [broad-gauge](/wiki/Broad-gauge_railway) route of 3,969 km (2,466 mi) with rail density of 24.36 km (15.14 mi) per 1,000 km2 (390 sq mi). The railway network in Andhra Pradesh is under the [South Central railway](/wiki/South_Central_Railway_zone), [East Coast railway](/wiki/East_Coast_Railway_zone), and [South Western railway](/wiki/South_Western_Railway_zone) zones. During 2014–2022, 350 km (220 mi) of new lines were constructed at a rate of 44 km (27 mi) per year in Andhra Pradesh under the South Central railway division. The rate of construction was only 2 km (1.2 mi) per year in the preceding five years. The [Nadikudi–Srikalahasti line](/wiki/Nadikudi%E2%80%93Srikalahasti_section) with a length of 308.70 km (191.82 mi), which was sanctioned at a budget of ₹22.89 billion (US$240 million) in 2011–12 as a joint project of the centre and state, is progressing slowly, with only phase one of 46 km (29 mi) between [New Piduguralla](/wiki/Piduguralla) and [Savalyapuram](/wiki/Savalyapuram) completed by 2021–22. There are three A1 and 23 A-category railway stations in the state, as per the assessment in 2017. [Visakhapatnam](/wiki/Visakhapatnam_railway_station) was declared [the cleanest railway station](/wiki/List_of_cleanest_railway_stations_in_India) in the country, as per the assessment in 2018. The railway station in [Shimiliguda](/wiki/Shimiliguda) is the second broad gauge railway station at high altitude in the country next to [Qazigund](/wiki/Qazigund_railway_station) in Jammu and Kashmir. A new railway zone [South Coast railway](/wiki/South_Coast_Railway_zone) (SCoR), with headquarters in Visakhapatnam, was announced in 2019, but is yet to be implemented.

### Communication

As per socio-economic survey released in January 2023, Andhra Pradesh has 10,605 post offices. Bharat sanchar nigam limited (BSNL) operates 1,310 telephone exchanges with 20.6 million landline connections and also has 5.4 million wireless subscribers. Airtel, Jio, Vodaphone Idea, and BSNL provide mobile services in the state. As of 2023, the number of mobile phone users reached 82 million, while the number of internet subscribers reached 67.1 million in Andhra Pradesh. Mobile network services are available in 15,322 out of 17,328 villages. 5G network connectivity is provided by 16,714 base transfer stations. Several providers including state government owned [Andhra Pradesh state fibre net limited](/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh_State_FiberNet_Limited) operate wireline services supporting internet connectivity, telephony, and [Internet Protocol television](/wiki/Internet_Protocol_television). BSNL and the National Knowledge Network multi-purposeCricket link district headquarters with state headquarters with a bandwidth of 34 Mbit/s. Mandal headquarters are connected with a bandwidth of 8 Mbit/s.

### Water

The state has 40 major and medium rivers and 40,000 minor irrigation sources. Godavari, Krishna, and Penna are major rivers. The total cultivable area is 19.904 million acres. Major, medium, and minor irrigation projects irrigate 10.172 million acres or 51.1% as of 2015. Several water projects in the state are facing issues. The [Polavaram Project](/wiki/Polavaram_Project) is a multi purpose terminal reservoir project located 42 km upstream of Davaleswaram barrage. It is a national project as per the AP Reorganisation Act of 2014. It has a reservoir capacity of 194.6 TMC and is expected to utilise 322.73 TMC of water in a year. The under-construction project suffered setbacks with damage to its diaphragm wall during the 2022 floods. The [Veligonda Project](/wiki/Veligonda_Project), taken up to serve needs of Prakasam, Nellore, and YSR Kadapa districts is progressing slowly. The Annamayya project, washed away in the 2021 floods, is set to be redesigned for 787 crores. Following the bifurcation, [disputes with Telangana regarding the allocation of Krishna and Godavari](/wiki/Disputes_between_Andhra_Pradesh_and_Telangana) waters continue to dog the state.

### Healthcare

The health infrastructure consists of 13 district hospitals, 28 sub-district hospitals, and 6,049 urban and rural health centres as of 2019–20. Doorstep healthcare support services are provided through 42,060 Accredited social health activist(ASHA) workers as of July 2019. The 108 service provides fast emergency management services by shifting patients to a nearby healthcare facility. The 104 service provides health care services at the doorstep of villages through mobile medical units that visit at least once a month. All the poor families are covered by the free state health insurance scheme up to a limit of ₹2,500,000 (US$26,000). The scheme serves 42.5 million people. The services are provided in government and private hospitals under the network. During 2014–2018, though the nominal mean claim amount of beneficiaries went up significantly, it decreased after accounting for inflation. Mortality rates have significantly decreased, which indicates better outcomes are being achieved at a lower cost. Out of 2,700 private hospitals registered under the state health insurance scheme, 540 (20%) are speciality hospitals. The number of patients treated under the state health insurance scheme increased from 1.2 million in 2022–23 to 1.39 million in 2023–24. whereas the budget remained constant at ₹3,350 crore. The National family health survey-5 conducted in 2019–21 data provided an insight into the economic and health status of households. Housing, electricity, clean fuel, access to toilets, mobile phone usage, and bank account access were available for more than 85% of households. Piped water facilities were available for only 22% of households. The state health insurance scheme, the employee health scheme, the *Rashtriya swasthya bima yojana*, the Employees' state insurance scheme, and the Central government health scheme covered 70% of households with at least one member covered.

As of 31 March 2023, there are 33 private sector banks, 12 public sector banks, three rural banks, three small finance banks, three payment banks, one cooperative bank, and one state financial corporation operating in the state. As of September 2022, Banks have deposits amounting to ₹435,183.49 crore (US$46 billion) and extended credit amounting to ₹628,134.9 crore (US$66 billion), with credit deposit ratio of 144% against Reserve Bank of India norm of 60%. ## Education

Main articles: [Education in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Education_in_Andhra_Pradesh) and [List of institutions of higher education in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/List_of_institutions_of_higher_education_in_Andhra_Pradesh)

[Andhra University, Visakhapatnam](/wiki/Andhra_University)

Primary and secondary school education is imparted by government and private schools, regulated by the school education department of the state. The government decided to stop financial aid to aided schools with history dating to 1870s, forcing them to handover the schools with the aided staff to government or become private in 2021. As per Unified district information system for education plus (UDISE+) report of 2021–22, there were a total of 8,244,647 students enrolled in 61,948 schools. Performance of rural students on reading, arithmetic skills in grades 1–8 declined during 2018–2022 partly attributed to Covid outbreak. As an example, for the reading assessment in Class VIII students, it nosedived 67% in 2022 from over 78% in 2018. However, the student enrolment at government schools improved from 63.2% to 71%. In the March 2024 secondary school certificate (SSC) exam 616,615 students appeared in the regular stream. Parvathipuram-Manyam district achieved the highest pass percentage of 96.37 among districts. The overall pass percentage was 86.69%, an increase of 14.43% over the previous year, with 100% in 2,803 schools.
In intermediate (higher secondary) examinations held in March 2024, 393,757 students appeared. The pass percentage was 78%, which is an increase of 7% over the previous year.

The state initiated education reforms in 2020 by creating six types of schools: satellite foundation schools (pre-primary), foundational schools (pre-primary – class II), foundational school plus (pre-primary – class V), pre-high school (class III – class VII/VIII), high school (class III – class X), and high school plus (class III – class XII). The transition to English-medium education in all government schools was started in the academic year 2020–2021. It is expected to reach completion by 2024–25. Affiliation of 1,000 government schools to the [Central Board of Secondary Education](/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) in 2022–23 was done as an initial step. The state government went ahead with the English medium based on the parents survey despite protests and court cases. The state initiative is being funded in part by a loan from the World Bank to the tune of $250 million over 2021–2026 through the "Supporting Andhra's learning transformation" project to improve the learning outcomes of children up to class II level.

As of 2023, there are 169 government-aided degree colleges and 55 private-aided degree colleges in the state. National assessment and accreditation council grades were awarded to 66 government colleges and 48 private-aided colleges. There are 85 government-aided and 175 private polytechnic colleges with a sanctioned strength of 75,906 students. In the category of technical education, there are 685 institutions offering diploma, undergraduate, and postgraduate Visakhapatnamthe courses, with an intake of 299,608. The AP state council of higher education organises various entrance tests for different streams and conducts counselling for admissions. The AP state skill development corporation is set up to support skill development and placement for the educated. As of 2021, there are a total of 36 universities: three central universities, 23 state public universities, six state private universities, and four deemed universities. [Andhra University](/wiki/Andhra_University) is the oldest of the universities in the state, established in 1926. The government established [Rajiv Gandhi university of knowledge technologies](/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_University_of_Knowledge_Technologies,_Nuzvid) in 2008 to cater to the education needs of the rural youth of the state. NTR University of health sciences oversees medical education in 348 affiliated colleges spanning the entire range from traditional medicine to modern medicine. The public universities, including the legacy universities such as Andhra, Sri Venkateswara, and Nagarjuna, are suffering from a severe fund crunch and staff shortage, managing with only 20% of sanctioned full-time staff. [KL University](/wiki/Koneru_Lakshmaiah_College_of_Engineering) bagged the 50th rank, while Andhra university bagged the 76th rank in the overall category of India rankings for 2023 as per the national institute ranking framework of the union ministry of education in which 2,478 institutions, including 242 institutions from the state, participated. The gross enrolment ratio (GER) in higher education for the age group 18–23 for the state was at 35.2% for the year 2019–20, which comparing favourably with the GER for all of India at 27.1%. With a female GER of 35.3 and a male GER of 38.2, the [Gender Parity Index](/wiki/Gender_Parity_Index) was 0.84. The corresponding ratio for India was 1.01.

There were 510 industrial training institutes (ITI) in 2020–21 in Andhra Pradesh, with 82 under government management and 417 under private management. The total available seats in 2021 were 93,280, out of which 48.90% were filled. In 2020, 10,053 students completed ITI. The state has 2,510 public libraries, including four regional libraries and 13 district central libraries under government management. [Saraswata Niketanam](/wiki/Saraswata_Niketanam) at [Vetapalem](/wiki/Vetapalem) in [Bapatla district](/wiki/Bapatla_district), one of the oldest libraries established under private management in 1918, is losing its attraction as the Internet spreads. The government is planning to develop digital libraries at the village panchayat level.

## Media

The total number of registered newspapers and periodicals in the state for the years 2020–21 was 5,798. There were 1,645 dailies, 817 weeklies, 2,431 monthlies, and 623 fortnightlies. Telugu dailies number 787 with a circulation of 9,911,005, while English dailies account for 103 with a circulation of 1,646,453. *[Eenadu](/wiki/Eenadu)*, *[Sakshi](/wiki/Sakshi_(newspaper))*, and *[Andhra Jyothi](/wiki/Andhra_Jyothi)* are the top three Telugu daily newspapers in terms of circulation in India and are also the top three Telugu news sites. BBC Telugu news was launched on 2 October 2017. Several privately owned news media outlets are considered biased towards specific political parties in the state.

There were 23 news channels, 10 general entertainment channels, two health channels, six religious channels, two other channels, and two cable distribution channels, for a total of 45 channels empanelled by the Andhra Pradesh information and public relations department. As of 2019, [Akashvani](/wiki/All_India_Radio) previously known as All India Radio, operates 3 MW, 17 FM transmitters from 14 locations in the state. It reaches 99% of the area and 99.5% of the population. Akashvani's FM coverage alone reaches 36% of the area and 45% of the population. Five private operators run 13 FM stations, with [Red FM](/wiki/Red_FM_93.5) operating from five locations.

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Andhra Pradesh](/wiki/Sports_in_Andhra_Pradesh)

[ACA–VDCA Cricket Stadium](/wiki/ACA%E2%80%93VDCA_Cricket_Stadium) in Visakhapatnam

[Traditional games](/wiki/Traditional_games_of_Andhra_Pradesh) played during childhood include *Dagudu Mootalu* (Hide and seek), *[Tokkudu Billa](/wiki/Tokkudu_Billa)*, *[Yedu Penkulata](/wiki/Seven_stones)*, [*Vamanaguntalu*](/wiki/Pallanguzhi), *Chadarangam* ([Chess](/wiki/Chess)), *[Puli Joodam](/wiki/Puli_Joodam)*, [*Ashta Chamma*](/wiki/Ashta_Chamma_%E2%80%93_Board_Game), *Vaikuntapali* ([Snakes and ladders](/wiki/Snakes_and_ladders)), [*Nalugu Stambalata*](/wiki/Nalugu_Stambhalata_(game)), and [*Nalugu ralla aata*](/wiki/Nalugu_Rallu_Aata_%E2%80%93_Game_of_4_stones). *[Karra samu](/wiki/Karra_Samu)* (stick fight) is a traditional martial art form of the state. It is a form of self-defence to prevent thefts and robbery in villages. It is a discipline in the national sports, though it is not recognised for the sports quota category of reservations in Andhra Pradesh.

Sports Authority of Andhra Pradesh is the governing body that looks after the infrastructure development, coaching, and administration of sports promotion schemes. Sports authority of India (SAI) operates three SAI Training centres in Andhra Pradesh as of 2022. Dr. YSR Sports School, Putlampalli, Kadapa district was selected for upgradation as *Khelo* India centre of excellence in 2021. [Andhra Cricket Visakhapatnam District Association cricket Association (ACA-VDCA) stadium](/wiki/Dr._Y._S._Rajasekhara_Reddy_ACA%E2%80%93VDCA_Cricket_Stadium) in Visakhapatnam is known for hosting international cricket matches.

[Pullela Gopichand](/wiki/Pullela_Gopichand) is a former Indian [badminton](/wiki/Badminton) player. He won the [All England Open Badminton Championships](/wiki/All_England_Open_Badminton_Championships) in 2001, becoming the second Indian to win after [Prakash Padukone](/wiki/Prakash_Padukone). [P. V. Sindhu](/wiki/P._V._Sindhu) is one of India's most successful athletes, having won medals in major tournaments like the [Olympics](/wiki/Olympic_Games) and the [BWF circuit](/wiki/BWF_World_Tour), including a gold at the [2019 World Championships](/wiki/2019_BWF_World_Championships). She is the first Indian to become a badminton world champion and only the second to win two consecutive Olympic medals. [Karnam Malleswari](/wiki/Karnam_Malleswari) is the first female Indian to win an [Olympic medal](/wiki/Olympic_medal). She won bronze medal in 69 kg weightlifting event at 2000 Olympics games. Srikanth Kidambi, a badminton player, is the first Indian to reach the world championships final in 2021 in the men's singles and win a silver medal. The state secured 16 medals at the 36th national games held in 2022. It was ranked 21st in the competition. The state sportpersons won 11 medals in Tennis, Archery, Badminton, Athletics, Chess and Cricket disciplines in the 19th Asian games held in China in 2022. The state was ranked at 13th spot in the sixth edition of Khelo India youth games-2023, by winning 27 medals.

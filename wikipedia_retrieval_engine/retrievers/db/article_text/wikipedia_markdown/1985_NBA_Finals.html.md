The **1985 NBA World Championship Series** was the championship round of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA)'s [1984–85 season](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_NBA_season), and the culmination of the [season's playoffs](/wiki/1985_NBA_playoffs). It featured the defending NBA champion and [Eastern Conference](/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Boston Celtics](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Boston_Celtics_season) against the [Western Conference](/wiki/Western_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season).

The Celtics were looking to repeat as NBA champions for the first time since the [1968–69](/wiki/1968%E2%80%9369_NBA_season) season. The Celtics had home court advantage for the second year in a row as they finished the regular season with a 63–19 record while the Lakers compiled a 62–20 record. The Lakers looked to bounce back from the previous year's painful loss to the Celtics in the championship series, and were still seeking to beat Boston for the first time ever in NBA Finals history. Also for the first time since [1955](/wiki/1955_NBA_Finals), the Finals implemented a 2–3–2 format with Games 1 and 2 in Boston while the next three games were in Los Angeles. The final two games of the series would be played in Boston, if required. This change of format came after [David Stern](/wiki/David_Stern) had a conversation with Celtics legend [Red Auerbach](/wiki/Red_Auerbach) in 1984, who disliked the frequent traveling between games. The 2–3–2 format would be used until the [2013 NBA Finals](/wiki/2013_NBA_Finals), after which the 2–2–1–1–1 format returned [in 2014](/wiki/2014_NBA_Finals). The 2–3–2 format has long been used by [Major League Baseball](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball) in the [World Series](/wiki/World_Series) and was also used in the [League Championship Series](/wiki/League_Championship_Series) upon changing to a best-of-seven format later that year. The [Stanley Cup Final](/wiki/Stanley_Cup_Final) in the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) also went to a 2–3–2 format [that same year](/wiki/1985_Stanley_Cup_Final) as well as [the previous year](/wiki/1984_Stanley_Cup_Final) but returned to a 2–2–1–1–1 format from [1986](/wiki/1986_Stanley_Cup_Final) onward.

With the help of Kareem Abdul-Jabbar and Magic Johnson, the Los Angeles Lakers achieved their first NBA Finals victory over the Boston Celtics in nine meetings, four to two games. The Lakers recovered after losing in a rout in game 1, dubbed as the "Memorial Day Massacre". The series was the last time the NBA World Championship Series branding would be in use as the NBA Finals branding would replace it for 1986. The video documentary *Return to Glory* recaps the [1985 NBA playoffs](/wiki/1985_NBA_playoffs) action.

## Background

### Los Angeles Lakers

Main article: [1984–85 Los Angeles Lakers season](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season)

After losing to the Celtics in the [previous year's finals](/wiki/1984_NBA_Finals), the Lakers entered the [1984–85 NBA season](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_NBA_season) with a mission. Once again using the effective [Showtime offense](/wiki/Showtime_offense), they ran away with the Western Conference-leading 62 wins. The team as a whole underwent a slight evolution, as [James Worthy](/wiki/James_Worthy) supplanted [Jamaal Wilkes](/wiki/Jamaal_Wilkes) as the starting small forward, while [Byron Scott](/wiki/Byron_Scott) began to earn more minutes as the backup to both [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) and [Michael Cooper](/wiki/Michael_Cooper).

In the playoffs, the Lakers eliminated the [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Phoenix_Suns_season), [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Portland_Trail_Blazers_season) and [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Denver_Nuggets_season), going 11–2 in the three playoff rounds.

### Boston Celtics

Main article: [1984–85 Boston Celtics season](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Boston_Celtics_season)

The Celtics repeated with the NBA's best record by winning 63 games. For the second straight season, [Larry Bird](/wiki/Larry_Bird) won the [MVP award](/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award), while [Kevin McHale](/wiki/Kevin_McHale_(basketball)) won [Sixth Man Award](/wiki/NBA_Sixth_Man_of_the_Year_Award) for the second year running, despite making the transition from bench cog to starter late in the season with [Cedric Maxwell](/wiki/Cedric_Maxwell) nursing a knee injury. [Danny Ainge](/wiki/Danny_Ainge) also emerged as the team's starting shooting guard, after the Celtics traded [Gerald Henderson](/wiki/Gerald_Henderson) to the [Seattle SuperSonics](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Seattle_SuperSonics_season) in the offseason.

The Celtics defeated the [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Cleveland_Cavaliers_season), [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Detroit_Pistons_season) and [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Philadelphia_76ers_season), finishing with an 11–4 record heading into the finals. By that point, Boston's classic starting five under head coach [K. C. Jones](/wiki/K._C._Jones) was solidified, featuring Bird, McHale and [Robert Parish](/wiki/Robert_Parish) in the frontcourt, and Ainge and [Dennis Johnson](/wiki/Dennis_Johnson) in the backcourt.

The Celtics defeated the Lakers 148–114. It was dubbed the "[Memorial Day](/wiki/Memorial_Day_(United_States)) Massacre" and a profound embarrassment for the Lakers team. [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) had only 12 points and 3 rebounds, while [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) pulled down only one rebound. [Danny Ainge](/wiki/Danny_Ainge) of the Celtics started hot, scoring 15 points in the first quarter. [Scott Wedman](/wiki/Scott_Wedman) made all 11 shots he took from the field. Afterwards, Abdul-Jabbar apologized to his teammates for his terrible performance.

The 34-point differential set a new record for a Finals game between the Celtics and Lakers in their [rivalry](/wiki/Celtics%E2%80%93Lakers_rivalry). This record would stand until the [2008 NBA Finals](/wiki/2008_NBA_Finals), when the Celtics defeated the Lakers, 131–92.

In the series clincher, the game was tied at halftime. The Lakers would hold the Celtics to just 18 third quarter points to pull away. Abdul-Jabbar scored 29 points and James Worthy scored 28 of his own, as Los Angeles became the first road team to clinch a title at the [Boston Garden](/wiki/Boston_Garden). Magic Johnson dished out a game-high 14 assists. Celtics' forward Kevin McHale scored 32 points and grabbed 16 rebounds—both game-highs—before fouling out in the fourth quarter. Larry Bird's dismal shooting continued as he hit just 12 of 29 shots.

The 38-year-old Abdul-Jabbar was named [MVP of the series](/wiki/NBA_Finals_MVP_Award), his second Finals MVP award and first since [1971](/wiki/1971_NBA_Finals) (back when he was known as Lew Alcindor), averaging 25.7 points, 9.0 rebounds, 5.2 assists and 1.5 blocks in six games. Worthy averaged 23.7 points for the Lakers, while Magic Johnson scored 18.3 points per game to go along with 14.0 assists and 6.8 rebounds. Los Angeles shot 51.2% as a team for the series.

McHale led Boston in scoring (26.0) and rebounding (10.7) while shooting 59.8% from the floor. Bird averaged 23.8 points and 8.8 rebounds on just 44.9% shooting. Celtics' guard Dennis Johnson led both teams in minutes played (247). Boston shot 47.6% from the floor while out-rebounding Los Angeles 259–256.

It was the first time (and only time until 2022) in NBA Finals history where the other team clinched the championship against the Celtics in Boston. It was also only the Celtics' second Finals series defeat, having previously lost to the [St. Louis Hawks](/wiki/Atlanta_Hawks) in [1958](/wiki/1958_NBA_Finals).

## Television coverage

The Finals were telecast by [CBS](/wiki/NBA_on_CBS) in the United States, with its coverage anchored by [Brent Musburger](/wiki/Brent_Musburger). [Dick Stockton](/wiki/Dick_Stockton) did play-by-play with [Tom Heinsohn](/wiki/Tom_Heinsohn) as color analyst, working their second Finals together. [Pat O'Brien](/wiki/Pat_O%27Brien_(television)) worked sideline duties for both teams.

## Celebration

The Lakers were invited to a reception at the [White House](/wiki/White_House) with [President](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Ronald Reagan](/wiki/Ronald_Reagan), where Kareem Abdul-Jabbar presented the President with a jersey. The following Tuesday would be declared "Laker Day" by [Los Angeles Mayor](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Mayor) [Tom Bradley](/wiki/Tom_Bradley_(American_politician)) with a parade beginning at 9th in Broadway.

## Aftermath

Further information: [1987 NBA Finals](/wiki/1987_NBA_Finals)

After appearing in and winning both the [1981](/wiki/1981_NBA_Finals) and [1984 NBA Finals](/wiki/1984_NBA_Finals), this finals championship marked the first loss for the Larry Bird-led Celtics, the first Finals loss to the Lakers in the history of the rivalry, and the Celtics' first finals loss since [1958](/wiki/1958_NBA_Finals). Boston would appear in two more NBA Finals before the decade was over, winning it all [the next year](/wiki/1986_NBA_Finals) against the [Hakeem Olajuwon](/wiki/Hakeem_Olajuwon)-led [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) in six games, only to lose in a rematch to the Lakers in [1987](/wiki/1987_NBA_Finals).

Footage of the Lakers' locker room celebration after the Finals was later used in the sports blooper-laden music video for "[Walk of Life](/wiki/Walk_of_Life)" by [Dire Straits](/wiki/Dire_Straits), released later that year.

# David Arnold

For other people named David Arnold, see [David Arnold (disambiguation)](/wiki/David_Arnold_(disambiguation)).

**David Arnold** (born 23 January 1962) is an English film composer whose credits include scoring five [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond) films (1997–2008), as well as *[Stargate](/wiki/Stargate_(film))* (1994), *[Independence Day](/wiki/Independence_Day_(1996_film))* (1996), *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1998_film))* (1998), *[Shaft](/wiki/Shaft_(2000_film))* (2000), *[2 Fast 2 Furious](/wiki/2_Fast_2_Furious)* (2003), *[Four Brothers](/wiki/Four_Brothers_(film))* (2005), *[Hot Fuzz](/wiki/Hot_Fuzz)* (2007), and the television series *[Little Britain](/wiki/Little_Britain_(sketch_show))* and *[Sherlock](/wiki/Sherlock_(TV_series))*. For *Independence Day*, he received a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for Best Instrumental Composition Written for a Motion Picture or for Television, and for *Sherlock*, he and co-composer [Michael Price](/wiki/Michael_Price_(composer)) won a [Creative Arts Emmy](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Music_Composition_for_a_Miniseries,_Movie,_or_a_Special) for the score of "[His Last Vow](/wiki/His_Last_Vow)", the final episode in the third series. Arnold scored the BBC / [Amazon Prime](/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video) series [*Good Omens*](/wiki/Good_Omens_(TV_series)) (2019) adapted by [Neil Gaiman](/wiki/Neil_Gaiman) from his book *[Good Omens](/wiki/Good_Omens)*, written with [Terry Pratchett](/wiki/Terry_Pratchett). Arnold is a fellow of the [British Academy of Songwriters, Composers and Authors](/wiki/British_Academy_of_Songwriters,_Composers_and_Authors).

## Career

While attending a [Sixth Form College in Luton](/wiki/Luton_Sixth_Form_College), Arnold became friends with director [Danny Cannon](/wiki/Danny_Cannon). Cannon initially created short films for which Arnold was asked to write the music. The two made their respective major film debuts with *[The Young Americans](/wiki/The_Young_Americans_(film))*. "[Play Dead](/wiki/Play_Dead_(song))", a song from the film with singer [Björk](/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk), charted No. 12 in the UK. The following year he scored *[Stargate](/wiki/Stargate_(film))* and *[Last of the Dogmen](/wiki/Last_of_the_Dogmen)*, with excerpts from the former ranking third in the most commonly used soundtrack cues for film trailers.

Arnold then composed music for *Stargate* director [Roland Emmerich](/wiki/Roland_Emmerich)'s next two movies, *[Independence Day](/wiki/Independence_Day_(1996_film))* and *[Godzilla](/wiki/Godzilla_(1998_film))*, as well as four movies for director [John Singleton](/wiki/John_Singleton). In addition, he has scored various comedies, dramas, and nineteenth-century period pieces, as well as providing music for several British television shows including the 2000 remake of *[Randall and Hopkirk (Deceased)](/wiki/Randall_and_Hopkirk_(Deceased)_(2000_TV_series))* and *[Little Britain](/wiki/Little_Britain_(sketch_show))*. During film production, his compositions are conducted by Nicholas Dodd. In 2010, he composed the music for *[Come Fly With Me](/wiki/Come_Fly_with_Me_(2010_TV_series))*, a British television series from the producers of *Little Britain*.

He is a member of the [British Academy of Songwriters, Composers and Authors](/wiki/British_Academy_of_Songwriters,_Composers_and_Authors) (BASCA). On Thursday 29 November 2012, Arnold received an honorary degree from [University of West London](/wiki/University_of_West_London). Now a university honorary, he will work closely with the University in particular [London College of Music](/wiki/London_College_of_Music), a faculty within the institute. In 2014, he appeared as himself in *[The Life of Rock with Brian Pern](/wiki/The_Life_of_Rock_with_Brian_Pern)*.

### Film music concerts

Arnold performed his debut orchestral concert, showcasing his film and television music, on Sunday 6 July 2014 at London's [Royal Festival Hall](/wiki/Royal_Festival_Hall). The line-up featured Nicholas Dodd conducting, [David McAlmont](/wiki/David_McAlmont) as surprise guest vocalist ("My secret weapon!" said Arnold) and the Urban Voices Collective choir, plus [Mark Gatiss](/wiki/Mark_Gatiss) and [Amanda Abbington](/wiki/Amanda_Abbington) introducing the suite of *[Sherlock](/wiki/Sherlock_(TV_series))* music, for which Arnold's collaborator on the project, [Michael Price](/wiki/Michael_Price_(composer)), replaced Dodd.

He performed his music in a series of orchestral concerts in 2015: Dublin) in January (with the [RTÉ Concert Orchestra](/wiki/RT%C3%89_Concert_Orchestra)); Manchester (with the [Manchester Camerata](/wiki/Manchester_Camerata)) in April; and London (with the [Royal Philharmonic Orchestra](/wiki/Royal_Philharmonic_Orchestra)), Birmingham and Nottingham (with the [City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/City_of_Birmingham_Symphony_Orchestra) (CBSO)) in June 2015. He was also the special guest at 'The music of David Arnold', a concert in Lucerne in October 2015, with Ludwig Wicki conducting the 21st Century Symphony Orchestra and Chorus. On 5 June 2016 Film Music Prague performed a concert of his work, with Arnold in attendance (and performing) as special guest. In February 2016 the Royal Albert Hall announced the premiere of *[Independence Day](/wiki/Independence_Day_(1996_film))* Live on 22 September 2016. This celebrated the 20th anniversary of the film's release with a live orchestral performance. David Arnold gave a pre-show talk about his work and the [Royal Philharmonic Concert Orchestra](/wiki/Royal_Philharmonic_Concert_Orchestra) and Maida Vale Singers (conducted by Gavin Greenaway) performed the original music while the film screened. The Upcoming magazine gave the event a five star review noting that "with unrivalled acoustics and a ceiling filled with floating UFO-shaped objects, the Hall set the ideal scene for the audience and the musicians alike" and that the production "kept the audience on the edge of their seats as if the film had just been released for the first time."

Arnold hosted another two concerts of his music in Dublin, at the [Bord Gáis Energy Theatre](/wiki/Bord_G%C3%A1is_Energy_Theatre) on 19 and 20 May 2017, with the RTÉ Concert Orchestra. The first concert showcased his career in writing music for film and television, the second was *Independence Day* Live with the film screened as the orchestra played the score alongside. The first James Bond film ever to be screened with a live orchestra was *[Casino Royale in Concert](/wiki/Casino_Royale_(2006_film))* which took place at the Royal Albert Hall on Saturday 30 September 2017; David Arnold held a pre-concert question and answer session.

### James Bond

Arnold at the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) in London in October 2022 with the [Royal Philharmonic Orchestra](/wiki/Royal_Philharmonic_Orchestra). He curated *The Sound of 007 in Concert*, marking 60 years of the Bond film series.

Arnold was a Bond fan from an early age and also a fan of Bond composer [John Barry](/wiki/John_Barry_(composer)). In 1997, Arnold produced *[Shaken and Stirred: The David Arnold James Bond Project](/wiki/Shaken_and_Stirred:_The_David_Arnold_James_Bond_Project)*, an album featuring new versions of the themes from various James Bond films. The album featured a variety of contemporary artists including [Jarvis Cocker](/wiki/Jarvis_Cocker), [Chrissie Hynde](/wiki/Chrissie_Hynde), [David McAlmont](/wiki/David_McAlmont), [Propellerheads](/wiki/Propellerheads) and [Iggy Pop](/wiki/Iggy_Pop); a version of *You Only Live Twice* by [Björk](/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk) was recorded but not included on the album. John Barry, the composer of many of the themes on the album, was complimentary about Arnold's interpretation of his work: "He was very faithful to the melodic and harmonic content, but he's added a whole other rhythmic freshness and some interesting casting in terms of the artists chosen to do the songs. I think it's a terrific album. I'm very flattered." Barry contacted [Barbara Broccoli](/wiki/Barbara_Broccoli), producer of the then-upcoming *[Tomorrow Never Dies](/wiki/Tomorrow_Never_Dies)*, to recommend Arnold as the film's composer. Arnold was hired to score the instalment and, returning the compliment to the man he refers to as "The guvnor", included musical references to Barry's score for *[From Russia with Love](/wiki/From_Russia_with_Love_(soundtrack)),* as well as, of course, the *[James Bond Theme](/wiki/James_Bond_Theme)* composed by [Monty Norman](/wiki/Monty_Norman) with Barry's arrangement.

Arnold scored the four subsequent Bond films: *[The World Is Not Enough](/wiki/The_World_Is_Not_Enough)*, *[Die Another Day](/wiki/Die_Another_Day)* (in which he included references to John Barry's score for *[On Her Majesty's Secret Service](/wiki/On_Her_Majesty%27s_Secret_Service_(soundtrack))*), *[Casino Royale](/wiki/Casino_Royale_(2006_film))* and *[Quantum of Solace](/wiki/Quantum_of_Solace)*. Arnold did not score the 23rd James Bond film, *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)*, with [Thomas Newman](/wiki/Thomas_Newman) taking his place. Arnold commented that Newman had been selected by the film's director, [Sam Mendes](/wiki/Sam_Mendes), because of their history of working together, rather than because of Arnold's commitment to working with director [Danny Boyle](/wiki/Danny_Boyle) as composer for the Opening Ceremony of the [2012 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics). However, a part of Arnold's composition work on [*Casino Royale*](/wiki/Casino_Royale_(2006_film)) was reused, with a credit, in *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)* and again in *[Spectre](/wiki/Spectre_(2015_film))*.

Arnold also co-wrote the main theme songs for *The World Is Not Enough* ("[The World Is Not Enough](/wiki/The_World_Is_Not_Enough_(song))" by [Garbage](/wiki/Garbage_(band))) and *Casino Royale* ("[You Know My Name](/wiki/You_Know_My_Name)" by [Chris Cornell](/wiki/Chris_Cornell)), as well as "Surrender" by [k.d. lang](/wiki/K.d._lang) which appears during the end credits of *Tomorrow Never Dies* having been originally proposed as the opening theme. Arnold also contributed the main themes to Kevin Kiner's score for Activision's *[GoldenEye 007](/wiki/GoldenEye_007_(2010_video_game))*, the remake of the 1997 game of the same name.

In 2017, a part of a track entitled "Vesper" from Arnold's composition work on the [*Casino Royale*](/wiki/Casino_Royale_(2006_film)) [soundtrack](/wiki/Casino_Royale_(2006_soundtrack)) was reused in a *[Sherlock](/wiki/Sherlock_(TV_series))* episode entitled "[The Final Problem](/wiki/The_Final_Problem_(Sherlock))", the third episode of the fourth series, in a track entitled "Pick Up" composed by Arnold himself and [Michael Price](/wiki/Michael_Price_(composer)).

Arnold co-wrote and composed the main theme for the 2026 video game *[007 First Light](/wiki/007_First_Light)* with [Lana Del Rey](/wiki/Lana_Del_Rey).

## Other work

Arnold has collaborated with such musical acts as [Cast](/wiki/Cast_(band)), [Kaiser Chiefs](/wiki/Kaiser_Chiefs), [Massive Attack](/wiki/Massive_Attack), and [Pulp](/wiki/Pulp_(band)), and solo artists [Natasha Bedingfield](/wiki/Natasha_Bedingfield), [Melanie C](/wiki/Melanie_C), [Björk](/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk), [Chris Cornell](/wiki/Chris_Cornell), [Shirley Manson](/wiki/Shirley_Manson), [Mark Morriss](/wiki/Mark_Morriss), [Nina Persson](/wiki/Nina_Persson) and in 2009 produced [Shirley Bassey](/wiki/Shirley_Bassey)'s album *[The Performance](/wiki/The_Performance)*.

In 2001, he provided a new arrangement of [Ron Grainer](/wiki/Ron_Grainer)'s *[Doctor Who](/wiki/Doctor_Who)* [theme music](/wiki/Doctor_Who_theme_music) for the [Eighth Doctor](/wiki/Eighth_Doctor) audio dramas from [Big Finish Productions](/wiki/Big_Finish_Productions). His version was used as the Eighth Doctor theme starting with 2001's *[Storm Warning](/wiki/Storm_Warning_(audio_drama))* until 2008, when it was replaced with a new version arranged by [Nicholas Briggs](/wiki/Nicholas_Briggs) starting with *[Dead London](/wiki/Dead_London)*. Arnold's theme returned to the Eighth Doctor releases with the 2012 box set, *[Dark Eyes](/wiki/Dark_Eyes_(audio_drama))*.

Arnold is the second cousin of Irish singer-songwriter [Damien Rice](/wiki/Damien_Rice), and is an ambassador for aid agency [CARE International](/wiki/CARE_International) in the UK. He has made minor appearances in two different episodes of *[Little Britain](/wiki/Little_Britain_(sketch_show))* as separate characters.

In February 2011, it was announced that he had been appointed Musical Director for the [2012 Olympic Games](/wiki/2012_Olympic_Games) and the [2012 Paralympic Games](/wiki/2012_Paralympic_Games) in London.

In May 2011, he was part of the United Kingdom's jury for the [Eurovision Song Contest 2011](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2011).

Arnold took part in a tribute to [John Barry](/wiki/John_Barry_(composer)) on 20 June 2011 at the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) in London, singing a song that was composed by Barry and playing the guitar part of the James Bond theme.

In 2014, Arnold teamed up with [Richard Thomas](/wiki/Richard_Thomas_(musician)), to write the music and lyrics for the new [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) musical *[Made in Dagenham](/wiki/Made_in_Dagenham_(musical))*.

In October 2015 he collaborated with [Lethal Bizzle](/wiki/Lethal_Bizzle) and [Sinead Harnett](/wiki/Sinead_Harnett) to create a song combining orchestral, [grime](/wiki/Grime_(music_genre)) and soul elements. The song, 'Come This Far', was performed live at a special event at One Mayfair, as part of [Bulmers](/wiki/Bulmers) Cider's LiveColourful LIVE promotion, and made available as a free download from Bulmers' website. He and *[Sherlock](/wiki/Sherlock_(TV_series))* co-composer [Michael Price](/wiki/Michael_Price_(composer)) also composed the music for ITV's Jekyll and Hyde television series which premiered in October 2015.

In September 2016 the Royal Albert Hall hosted an orchestral performance of *Independence Day* with the score performed live to picture, David Arnold gave a pre-show talk.

In 2019, David Arnold provided additional production for [Sophie Ellis-Bextor](/wiki/Sophie_Ellis-Bextor)'s orchestral album, *[The Song Diaries](/wiki/The_Song_Diaries)*. Later in 2020, he co-produced an orchestral cover of '[My Favourite Things](/wiki/My_Favorite_Things_(song))' (from *[The Sound of Music](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music)*) along with [Richard Jones](/wiki/Richard_Jones_(The_Feeling)) (of [The Feeling](/wiki/The_Feeling)) for Sophie's 2020 compilation album *[Songs From The Kitchen Disco](/wiki/Songs_From_The_Kitchen_Disco)*.

In July 2023, it was announced that Arnold and Ellis-Bextor would collaborate with lyricist [Don Black](/wiki/Don_Black_(lyricist)) for the track to [Channel 4](/wiki/Channel_4) and [Universal Pictures](/wiki/Universal_Pictures) film, "Mog's Christmas", based upon [the children's book series](/wiki/Mog_(Judith_Kerr)) by [Judith Kerr](/wiki/Judith_Kerr). The track, titled "As Long As I Belong", is about "the importance of belonging". The film is slated for a Q4 2023 release.

- Won: [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) – [Best Instrumental Composition Written for a Motion Picture or for Television](/wiki/Best_Instrumental_Composition_Written_for_a_Motion_Picture_or_for_Television) – *[Independence Day](/wiki/Independence_Day_(1996_film))*

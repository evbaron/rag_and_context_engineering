# Saffron Burrows

## Infobox

- **Citizenship:** British U.S.

- **Years active:** 1991–present

**Saffron Burrows** (born 22 October 1972) is a British actress who has appeared in films such as *[Circle of Friends](/wiki/Circle_of_Friends_(1995_film)),* *[Wing Commander](/wiki/Wing_Commander_(film)),* *[Deep Blue Sea](/wiki/Deep_Blue_Sea_(1999_film)),* *[Gangster No. 1](/wiki/Gangster_No._1),* *[Enigma](/wiki/Enigma_(2001_film)),* *[Troy](/wiki/Troy_(film)),* *[Reign Over Me](/wiki/Reign_Over_Me)*, and *[The Bank Job](/wiki/The_Bank_Job)*. On the small screen she starred as [Lorraine Weller](/wiki/Lorraine_Weller) on *[Boston Legal](/wiki/Boston_Legal),* Dr. Norah Skinner on *[My Own Worst Enemy](/wiki/My_Own_Worst_Enemy_(TV_series)),* [Detective Serena Stevens](/wiki/Detective_Serena_Stevens) on *[Law & Order: Criminal Intent](/wiki/Law_%26_Order:_Criminal_Intent),* and [Victoria Hand](/wiki/Victoria_Hand_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe)) on *[Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.](/wiki/Agents_of_S.H.I.E.L.D.)*. She appeared as Cynthia Taylor on the [Prime Video](/wiki/Prime_Video) series *[Mozart in the Jungle](/wiki/Mozart_in_the_Jungle)* and as [Dottie Quinn](/wiki/Dottie_Quinn) in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) series *[You](/wiki/You_(TV_series))*.

## Early life

Saffron Burrows was born in [St Pancras](/wiki/St_Pancras,_London) in central [London](/wiki/London) and grew up in [Stoke Newington](/wiki/Stoke_Newington). Her mother is a trade unionist and primary school teacher in [Hackney](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Hackney) and her father is an architect and teacher. Her parents and stepfather were [Socialist Workers Party](/wiki/Socialist_Workers_Party_(UK)) members, and Burrows was politically active from a young age.

Burrows attended William Tyndale Primary School in [Islington](/wiki/Islington) and then [Stoke Newington School](/wiki/Stoke_Newington_School) in [Stoke Newington](/wiki/Stoke_Newington). When asked for her preference of a school in Hackney or [Hampstead](/wiki/Hampstead), she said she wanted to be in a multicultural and inclusive environment. She enrolled in acting classes at the [Anna Scher Theatre](/wiki/Anna_Scher_Theatre) in Islington when she was 11.

Burrows had a successful modelling career after she was discovered at age 15 in [Covent Garden](/wiki/Covent_Garden) by fashion photographer Beth Boldt. For five years she divided her time between London and [Paris](/wiki/Paris), where she learned [French](/wiki/French_language). She found the modelling world's "obsession with the ideal of the body" troubling.

## Career

### Acting

Burrows made her film debut in 1993 with a small role in [Jim Sheridan](/wiki/Jim_Sheridan)'s *[In the Name of the Father](/wiki/In_the_Name_of_the_Father)*. Her first significant acting roles came in 1995, as an ambitious young Irishwoman in *[Circle of Friends](/wiki/Circle_of_Friends_(1995_film)),* and in [Ngozi Onwurah](/wiki/Ngozi_Onwurah)'s *Welcome II The Terrordome*. In 1996 she starred in the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) production of [Dennis Potter](/wiki/Dennis_Potter)'s *[Karaoke](/wiki/Karaoke_(TV_series))* and in *[Hotel de Love](/wiki/Hotel_de_Love)*; the next year she was seen in *[Lovelife](/wiki/Lovelife)*, *[Nevada](/wiki/Nevada_(1997_film))*, *[The Matchmaker](/wiki/The_Matchmaker_(1997_film))* and [Mike Figgis](/wiki/Mike_Figgis)'s *[One Night Stand](/wiki/One_Night_Stand_(1997_film))*. In 1999 she appeared in Figgis's experimental film *[The Loss of Sexual Innocence](/wiki/The_Loss_of_Sexual_Innocence)*, in which she played twins – one raised in England, the other in Italy. In 1999, she appeared in the thriller *[Deep Blue Sea](/wiki/Deep_Blue_Sea_(1999_film)),* and had the title role in Figgis's film adaptation *[Miss Julie](/wiki/Miss_Julie_(1999_film))*, which premiered at the [Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival). She starred for Figgis again alongside [Stellan Skarsgård](/wiki/Stellan_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) in *[Timecode](/wiki/Timecode_(film))* (2000), a split-screen digital experimental film shot in a single take with no edits. She followed it with *[Gangster No. 1](/wiki/Gangster_No._1)*, starring [Malcolm McDowell](/wiki/Malcolm_McDowell), [Paul Bettany](/wiki/Paul_Bettany) and [David Thewlis](/wiki/David_Thewlis). She next co-starred with [Kate Winslet](/wiki/Kate_Winslet) and [Dougray Scott](/wiki/Dougray_Scott) in [Michael Apted](/wiki/Michael_Apted)'s 1940s drama *[Enigma](/wiki/Enigma_(2001_film))* and starred in *[Tempted](/wiki/Tempted_(film))*, an improvised thriller set in New Orleans, with [Burt Reynolds](/wiki/Burt_Reynolds) and [Peter Facinelli](/wiki/Peter_Facinelli).

Figgis's ensemble feature *[Hotel](/wiki/Hotel_(2001_film))* followed, reuniting Burrows with colleagues from *Timecode* including [Salma Hayek](/wiki/Salma_Hayek) and [Danny Huston](/wiki/Danny_Huston) on location in Venice, where she played the Duchess of Malfi. She had a cameo in *[Frida](/wiki/Frida_(2002_film))*, Hayek's 2002 biopic of artist [Frida Kahlo](/wiki/Frida_Kahlo).
Burrows performed in Spanish in *The Galindez File*, a film written by Spanish novelist [Vazquez Montalban](/wiki/Vazquez_Montalban), about a woman seeking the truth about the disappearance of a critic of the [Dominican](/wiki/Dominican_Republic) dictator Trujillo. In 2004, she played the role of [Andromache](/wiki/Andromache), the wife of Trojan Prince [Hector](/wiki/Hector), played by [Eric Bana](/wiki/Eric_Bana), in *[Troy](/wiki/Troy_(film))*.

Burrows dedicated herself to stage work in the early 2000s. She appeared at the [Royal National Theatre](/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre) in [Jeanette Winterson](/wiki/Jeanette_Winterson)'s *The Powerbook*, directed by [Deborah Warner](/wiki/Deborah_Warner); the play also went on tour, visiting the Theatre National Du Chaillot in Paris and the Teatro Argentina in Rome.
In January 2005, she created the role of Janey Morris in the world premiere of *Earthly Paradise* at the [Almeida Theatre](/wiki/Almeida_Theatre). The play centered on the love triangle of Janey, her husband [William Morris](/wiki/William_Morris) (the writer and proponent of the [Arts and Crafts](/wiki/Arts_and_Crafts) movement), and the [Pre-Raphaelite](/wiki/Pre-Raphaelite) painter [Dante Gabriel Rossetti](/wiki/Dante_Gabriel_Rossetti). London theatre critic Nicholas De Jongh said of her performance: "Burrows takes to the stage like a swan to water ... She deserves no end of watching." On 30 October 2005, she appeared on stage at the [Old Vic](/wiki/Old_Vic) theatre in London in a rehearsed reading of the 24-hour play *Night Sky*, alongside [Christopher Eccleston](/wiki/Christopher_Eccleston). Burrow has since participated in the "24 Hour Plays" in New York and Los Angeles.

In 2006, Burrows was the female lead in the New Zealand thriller *[Perfect Creature](/wiki/Perfect_Creature)*, appeared in [Hal Hartley](/wiki/Hal_Hartley)'s film *[Fay Grim](/wiki/Fay_Grim)*, and co-starred in *[Klimt](/wiki/Klimt_(film))*, Chilean director [Raoul Ruiz](/wiki/Raoul_Ruiz)' cinematic portrait of [Gustav Klimt](/wiki/Gustav_Klimt). Burrows played opposite [John Malkovich](/wiki/John_Malkovich) as the artist's lover, a woman of many personalities and nationalities. Onstage in 2006, she starred opposite [David Schwimmer](/wiki/David_Schwimmer) in the world premiere of [Neil LaBute](/wiki/Neil_LaBute)'s *[Some Girl(s)](/wiki/Some_Girl(s))* at the Gielgud Theatre, London. She then appeared in [Mike Binder](/wiki/Mike_Binder)'s film *[Reign Over Me](/wiki/Reign_Over_Me)* with [Don Cheadle](/wiki/Don_Cheadle) and [Adam Sandler](/wiki/Adam_Sandler). Burrows also had lead roles in the Indian thriller *Broken Thread* and in director [Peter Howitt](/wiki/Peter_Howitt_(actor))'s drama *Dangerous Parking*. On television, she played attorney [Lorraine Weller](/wiki/Lorraine_Weller) on [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)'s *[Boston Legal](/wiki/Boston_Legal)* (season 4) from 2007 to 2008. She starred in the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) series *[My Own Worst Enemy](/wiki/My_Own_Worst_Enemy_(TV_series))* in 2008.

Also in 2008, Burrows starred in the independent film *[The Guitar](/wiki/The_Guitar_(film))* as a woman who suffers several life setbacks all at once, including a cancer diagnosis and bankruptcy, and survives them all. [Amy Redford](/wiki/Amy_Redford)'s directorial debut, the film premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival). She also had a starring role in Roger Donaldson's heist film *[The Bank Job](/wiki/The_Bank_Job)*, and appeared opposite [Kevin Spacey](/wiki/Kevin_Spacey) in Jonas Pate's *[Shrink](/wiki/Shrink_(film))*. She contributed to an *Actors Come Clean for Congo* video for the [Enough Project](/wiki/Enough_Project)'s campaign in support of [conflict mineral](/wiki/Resource_war) issues. In 2010, she starred as [Detective Serena Stevens](/wiki/Detective_Serena_Stevens) on *[Law & Order: Criminal Intent](/wiki/Law_%26_Order:_Criminal_Intent)*, departing at the end of the ninth season. In September 2010, she took part in the documentary feature *The People Speak*, televised on the [History Channel](/wiki/History_Channel). The film was directed and produced by [Colin Firth](/wiki/Colin_Firth) and Anthony Arnove. Burrows modelled for [Marks & Spencer](/wiki/Marks_%26_Spencer)'s autumn 2010 campaign for their [Portfolio](/wiki/List_of_Marks_%26_Spencer_brands#Portfolio) range. In 2012, she performed opposite [Rob Lowe](/wiki/Rob_Lowe) in the political comedy *Knife Fight*.

In 2013 and 2014, Burrows joined the [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) television show *[Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.](/wiki/Agents_of_S.H.I.E.L.D.)*, part of the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe). She played agent [Victoria Hand](/wiki/Victoria_Hand_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe)) in a recurring role. She also starred in the [Amazon Video](/wiki/Amazon_Video) series *[Mozart in the Jungle](/wiki/Mozart_in_the_Jungle)* as Cynthia Taylor, a cellist with the New York Symphony. The series ran for four seasons from 2014 to 2018. In 2019, Burrows starred in the recurring role of [Dottie Quinn](/wiki/Dottie_Quinn) on the [second season](/wiki/You_(season_2)) of the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) thriller *[You](/wiki/You_(TV_series))*. She reprised her role in the [third season](/wiki/You_(season_3)), which was released in October 2021.

### Writing

Burrows has written diaries, book reviews and newspaper and magazine articles for *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)*, *[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)*, and *[The Times](/wiki/The_Times)* and the *[New Statesman](/wiki/New_Statesman)*.

## Personal life

Burrows is a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Arts](/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Arts). She is [bisexual](/wiki/Bisexual), and said that she "prefers the company of women". She was engaged to actor [Alan Cumming](/wiki/Alan_Cumming) in the 1990s and dated director [Mike Figgis](/wiki/Mike_Figgis) for five years until 2002. She was previously in a relationship with actress [Fiona Shaw](/wiki/Fiona_Shaw).
Burrows married writer Alison Balian, her girlfriend of six years, in August 2013. Burrows gave birth to their first child in 2012 and their daughter in 2017. Burrows and Balian separated in 2020.

Burrows has expressed sympathy for European-style social democracies and for French [Socialist politician](/wiki/Socialist_Party_(France)) [Ségolène Royal](/wiki/S%C3%A9gol%C3%A8ne_Royal). She joined an [anti-racism](/wiki/Anti-racism) group when she was 11 years old and went on to become the Vice President of the National Civil Rights Movement. Burrows is a campaigner for disabled rights and equality. In 2009, she became an American citizen.

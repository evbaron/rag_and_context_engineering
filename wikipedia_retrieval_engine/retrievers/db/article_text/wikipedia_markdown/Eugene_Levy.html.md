**Eugene Levy** (born December 17, 1946) is a Canadian actor and comedian. He often plays flustered and unconventional figures. He is best known for appearing in the sketch comedy series *[SCTV](/wiki/Second_City_Television)*, which aired from 1976 until 1984, and the *[American Pie](/wiki/American_Pie_(film_series))* series of films. He is a regular collaborator of actor-director [Christopher Guest](/wiki/Christopher_Guest), appearing in and co-writing four of his films, commencing with *[Waiting for Guffman](/wiki/Waiting_for_Guffman)* (1996). From 2015 to 2020, he starred as [Johnny Rose](/wiki/Johnny_Rose) in *[Schitt's Creek](/wiki/Schitt%27s_Creek)*, a comedy series that he co-created with his son and co-star [Dan Levy](/wiki/Dan_Levy_(Canadian_actor)).

Levy won a [Grammy Award for Best Song Written for Visual Media](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Song_Written_for_Visual_Media) in 2004 for "A Mighty Wind" from the [film of the same name](/wiki/A_Mighty_Wind) that he co-wrote. He received the [Governor General's Performing Arts Award](/wiki/Governor_General%27s_Performing_Arts_Award), Canada's highest honour in the performing arts, in 2008. He was appointed to the [Order of Canada](/wiki/Order_of_Canada) in 2011. In 2019 and 2020, he was nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series), which he won in 2020.

## Early life and education

Levy was born to a Jewish family in downtown [Hamilton](/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), [Ontario](/wiki/Ontario). His mother, Rebecca (née Kudlatz; 1910–1988), was a homemaker, and his father, Joseph (1909–1985), was a foreman at a car factory. He has a brother, Fred, and sister, Barbara. His mother was born in the [Gorbals](/wiki/Gorbals) area of [Glasgow](/wiki/Glasgow), Scotland, to [Polish Jewish](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) parents, and later moved to Canada. His father was [Sephardi Jewish](/wiki/Sephardic_Jews), with ancestors from Spain and Bulgaria.

Levy attended [Westdale Secondary School](/wiki/Westdale_Secondary_School), where he was student council president. He faced [antisemitic](/wiki/Antisemitism) bullying, with his campaign posters defaced with the word "Jew", which he refused to take down. He subsequently attended [McMaster University](/wiki/McMaster_University), graduating in 1969 with a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in [sociology](/wiki/Sociology). He was vice-president of the McMaster Film Board, a student film group, where he met filmmaker [Ivan Reitman](/wiki/Ivan_Reitman).

## Career

### 1972–1989: *Godspell* and *SCTV*

The 1972–1973 Toronto production of the hit musical *[Godspell](/wiki/Godspell)* opened at the [Royal Alexandra Theatre](/wiki/Royal_Alexandra_Theatre) and was intended to be a run of a few dozen performances for a subscription audience. The cast was drawn entirely from local performers, instead of a touring cast. The Toronto production launched the careers of many actors, including Levy, [Victor Garber](/wiki/Victor_Garber), [Andrea Martin](/wiki/Andrea_Martin), [Gilda Radner](/wiki/Gilda_Radner), [Dave Thomas](/wiki/Dave_Thomas_(actor)), and [Martin Short](/wiki/Martin_Short), as well as the show's musical director, [Paul Shaffer](/wiki/Paul_Shaffer). [Howard Shore](/wiki/Howard_Shore) played saxophone for this production. After an enthusiastic response from the audience, the scheduled run at the Royal Alexandra ended, and the show moved uptown to the Bayview Playhouse in [Leaside](/wiki/Leaside). The Bayview Playhouse production ran until August 1973, with a then-record run of 488 performances.

An alumnus of both [the Second City](/wiki/The_Second_City), Toronto and the sketch comedy series *[Second City Television](/wiki/Second_City_Television)*, Levy often plays unusual supporting characters with nerdy streaks. Perhaps his best-known role on *SCTV* is the dimwitted [Earl Camembert](/wiki/Earl_Camembert), a news anchor for the "SCTV News" and a parody of real-life Canadian newsman [Earl Cameron](/wiki/Earl_Cameron_(broadcaster)). Celebrities impersonated by Levy on SCTV include [Perry Como](/wiki/Perry_Como), [Ricardo Montalbán](/wiki/Ricardo_Montalb%C3%A1n), [Alex Trebek](/wiki/Alex_Trebek), [Gino Vannelli](/wiki/Gino_Vannelli), [Sean Connery](/wiki/Sean_Connery), [Howard Cosell](/wiki/Howard_Cosell), [Henry Kissinger](/wiki/Henry_Kissinger), [Menachem Begin](/wiki/Menachem_Begin), [Bud Abbott](/wiki/Bud_Abbott), [Milton Berle](/wiki/Milton_Berle), [John Charles Daly](/wiki/John_Charles_Daly), [Gene Shalit](/wiki/Gene_Shalit), [Judd Hirsch](/wiki/Judd_Hirsch), [Jack Carter](/wiki/Jack_Carter_(comedian)), [Muammar al-Gaddafi](/wiki/Muammar_al-Gaddafi), [Tony Dow](/wiki/Tony_Dow), [James Caan](/wiki/James_Caan_(actor)), [Lorne Greene](/wiki/Lorne_Greene), [Rex Reed](/wiki/Rex_Reed), Ralph Young (of [Sandler and Young](/wiki/Sandler_and_Young)), [F. Lee Bailey](/wiki/F._Lee_Bailey), [Ernest Borgnine](/wiki/Ernest_Borgnine), former Ontario chief coroner and talk show host Dr. [Morton Shulman](/wiki/Morton_Shulman), [Norman Mailer](/wiki/Norman_Mailer), [Neil Sedaka](/wiki/Neil_Sedaka) and [Howard McNear](/wiki/Howard_McNear) as Floyd the Barber.

Original Levy characterizations on *SCTV* are news reporter Earl Camembert, comic Bobby Bittman, scandal sheet entrepreneur Dr. Raoul Withers, "report on business" naïf Brian Johns, 3-D horror auteur Woody Tobias Jr., cheerful Leutonian accordionist Stan Schmenge, lecherous dream interpreter Raoul Wilson, hammer-voiced sports broadcaster Lou Jaffe, diminutive union patriarch Sid Dithers ("San Francisckie! Did you drove or did you flew?"), fey current-events commentator Joel Weiss, buttoned-down panel show moderator Dougal Currie, smarmy *Just for Fun* emcee Stan Kanter, energetic used car salesman Al Peck, guileless security guard Gus Gustofferson, Phil the Garment King (also of Phil's Nails), and the inept teen dance show host Rockin' Mel Slirrup.

### 1990–2014: *American Pie* and other comedy films

Levy in 2011

Levy at the *[American Reunion](/wiki/American_Reunion)* film premiere in 2012

Though he has been the "above the title" star in only two films, [*Armed and Dangerous*](/wiki/Armed_and_Dangerous_(1986_film)) (1986) and *[The Man](/wiki/The_Man_(2005_film))* (2005), he has featured prominently in many films. He is the co-writer and frequent cast member of [Christopher Guest](/wiki/Christopher_Guest)'s mockumentary features, particularly *[A Mighty Wind](/wiki/A_Mighty_Wind)* (2003), where his sympathetic performance as emotionally unstable folksinger Mitch Cohen won kudos; his accolades included a [Satellite Award for Best Supporting Actor – Musical or Comedy](/wiki/Satellite_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_-_Motion_Picture) and the prestigious [New York Film Critics Circle Award for Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/New_York_Film_Critics_Circle_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor). Other collaborations with Guest include *[Waiting for Guffman](/wiki/Waiting_for_Guffman)* (1996), *[Best in Show](/wiki/Best_in_Show_(film))* (2000), and *[For Your Consideration](/wiki/For_Your_Consideration_(film))* (2006).

From the 1980s through the 2010s, Levy appeared in *[National Lampoon's Vacation](/wiki/National_Lampoon%27s_Vacation)* (1983), *[Splash](/wiki/Splash_(film))* (1984), *[Club Paradise](/wiki/Club_Paradise)* (1986), *[Father of the Bride](/wiki/Father_of_the_Bride_(1991_film))* (1991), *[Father of the Bride Part II](/wiki/Father_of_the_Bride_Part_II)* (1995), *[Stay Tuned](/wiki/Stay_Tuned_(film))* (1996), *[Multiplicity](/wiki/Multiplicity_(film))* (1996), the *[American Pie](/wiki/American_Pie_(film_series))* film series (1999–2012), *[Serendipity](/wiki/Serendipity_(film))* (2001), *[Bringing Down the House](/wiki/Bringing_Down_the_House_(film))* (2003), *[Cheaper by the Dozen 2](/wiki/Cheaper_by_the_Dozen_2)* (2005), *[Madea's Witness Protection](/wiki/Madea%27s_Witness_Protection)* (2012), and other comedies. He also voiced characters in films such as *[Over the Hedge](/wiki/Over_the_Hedge)* (2006), *[Night at the Museum: Battle of the Smithsonian](/wiki/Night_at_the_Museum:_Battle_of_the_Smithsonian)* (2009), and *[Finding Dory](/wiki/Finding_Dory)* (2016). Levy was the creator of *[Maniac Mansion](/wiki/Maniac_Mansion_(TV_series))*, a television sitcom based on the [LucasArts](/wiki/LucasArts) [video game of the same name](/wiki/Maniac_Mansion_(video_game)). He was also seriously considered for the role of [Toby Ziegler](/wiki/Toby_Ziegler) on *[The West Wing](/wiki/The_West_Wing)*, a role that went to actor [Richard Schiff](/wiki/Richard_Schiff).

### 2015–present: *Schitt's Creek* and acclaim

Levy, along with his son [Dan Levy](/wiki/Dan_Levy_(Canadian_actor)), was co-creator of the [CBC](/wiki/CBC_Television)/[Pop TV](/wiki/Pop_(American_TV_channel)) sitcom *[Schitt's Creek](/wiki/Schitt%27s_Creek)* which ran from 2015-2020. He also starred in the show alongside his son as head of the Rose family, Johnny Rose. His daughter, [Sarah Levy](/wiki/Sarah_Levy_(actress)), portrayed Twyla Sands, the waitress at the Schitt's Creek diner. Eugene Levy's brother Fred was also a producer on the show.

Levy hosted and executive produced an [Apple Original](/wiki/List_of_Apple_TV_original_programming) [travel series](/wiki/Travel_documentary) titled *[The Reluctant Traveler](/wiki/The_Reluctant_Traveler)* (2023) on [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B), which is now in its third season.

In 2024, Levy attended a ceremony honouring him with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame).

## Personal life

Levy married Deborah Divine in 1977. Divine's career has been in TV production. The couple have two children whom they raised in Toronto: actors [Dan](/wiki/Dan_Levy_(Canadian_actor)) and [Sarah](/wiki/Sarah_Levy_(actress)), both of whom starred alongside their father on *Schitt's Creek*.

Levy is an advocate for [autism](/wiki/Autism) awareness and treatment, and supports [ABA methods](/wiki/Autism#Applied_behavior_analysis). He was a close friend of actor and fellow *SCTV* cast member [John Candy](/wiki/John_Candy). Levy is a member of the Canadian charity Artists Against Racism.

In 2021, he was named [honorary mayor](/wiki/Honorary_Mayor_of_Pacific_Palisades) of [Pacific Palisades](/wiki/Pacific_Palisades,_Los_Angeles). In October 2022, Levy was one of the honorees of [Creative Community For Peace](/wiki/Creative_Community_for_Peace)'s 10th annual gala, and also spoke at the event.

In 2025, his home in the neighbourhood was destroyed in the [Palisades wildfire](/wiki/Palisades_Fire).

## Awards and nominations

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Eugene Levy](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Eugene_Levy)

Levy, along with [Christopher Guest](/wiki/Christopher_Guest) and [Michael McKean](/wiki/Michael_McKean), was awarded the 2003 [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for [Best Song Written for a Motion Picture, Television or Other Visual Media](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Song_Written_for_a_Motion_Picture,_Television_or_Other_Visual_Media) for the title song from *A Mighty Wind*. Levy received the [Governor General's Performing Arts Award](/wiki/Governor_General%27s_Performing_Arts_Award), Canada's highest honour in the performing arts, in 2008.

In March 2006, it was announced that he would receive a star on [Canada's Walk of Fame](/wiki/Canada%27s_Walk_of_Fame). In 2002, the entire cast of *SCTV* was given a group star, and although Levy is not mentioned on the actual star, he was still inducted as a part of the group. This makes him one of only four two-time honourees, alongside fellow *SCTV* alumni [John Candy](/wiki/John_Candy), [Martin Short](/wiki/Martin_Short), and [Catherine O'Hara](/wiki/Catherine_O%27Hara). Levy is one of only a handful of people who have won at least five [Canadian Comedy Awards](/wiki/Canadian_Comedy_Awards), including two for Best Writing (*[Best in Show](/wiki/Best_in_Show_(film))* in 2001 and *[A Mighty Wind](/wiki/A_Mighty_Wind)* in 2004) and three for Best Male Performer (*Best in Show*, *[American Pie 2](/wiki/American_Pie_2)* in 2002, and *A Mighty Wind*).

In 2008, the [governor general of Canada](/wiki/Governor_General_of_Canada) presented Levy with the Governor General's Performing Arts Awards (GGPAA), a lifetime achievement award considered "for their outstanding body of work and enduring contribution to the performing arts in Canada.". In 2010, Levy was awarded the [ACTRA Award](/wiki/ACTRA_Award) by the [union representing Canada's actors](/wiki/ACTRA). In 2011, Levy was made a Member of the [Order of Canada](/wiki/Order_of_Canada) "for his contributions as a comic actor and writer, and for his dedication to charitable causes" and promoted to the rank of Companion in 2022.

On May 22, 2012, Levy delivered a commencement address at [Dalhousie University](/wiki/Dalhousie_University), in Halifax, Nova Scotia, and was awarded the degree Doctor of Laws (honoris causa). On June 11, 2012, Levy was presented with the [Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II_Diamond_Jubilee_Medal) by the lieutenant governor of Ontario.

On March 13, 2016, Levy took home the award for "Best Performance by an Actor in a Continuing Leading Comedic Role" at the 4th Annual [Canadian Screen Awards](/wiki/Canadian_Screen_Awards), for his performance as Johnny Rose in the [CBC](/wiki/CBC_Television)/[Pop TV](/wiki/Pop_(American_TV_channel)) sitcom *[Schitt's Creek](/wiki/Schitt%27s_Creek)*.

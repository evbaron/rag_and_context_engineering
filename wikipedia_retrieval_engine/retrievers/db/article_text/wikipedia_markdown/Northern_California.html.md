This article is about the northern part of the U.S. state. For the historic region, see [Alta California](/wiki/Alta_California). For the megaregion, see [Northern California megaregion](/wiki/Northern_California_megaregion).

**Northern California** (commonly shortened to **NorCal**) is a geocultural region that comprises the northern portion of the U.S. state of [California](/wiki/California), spanning the northernmost 48 of the state's [58 counties](/wiki/List_of_counties_in_California). Northern California in its largest definition is determined by dividing the state into two regions, the other being [Southern California](/wiki/Southern_California). The main northern population centers include the [San Francisco Bay Area](/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) (anchored by the cities of [San Jose](/wiki/San_Jose,_California), [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco), and [Oakland](/wiki/Oakland,_California)), the [Greater Sacramento](/wiki/Greater_Sacramento) area (anchored by the state capital [Sacramento](/wiki/Sacramento,_California)), the [Redding, California](/wiki/Redding,_California), area south of the [Cascade Range](/wiki/Cascade_Range), and the [Metropolitan Fresno](/wiki/Metropolitan_Fresno) area (anchored by the city of [Fresno](/wiki/Fresno,_California)). Northern California is coterminous with the natural range of the [coast redwood](/wiki/Sequoia_sempervirens) and the [giant sequoia](/wiki/Sequoiadendron_giganteum), with many well-known old-growth forests and smaller groves. It contains most of the [Sierra Nevada](/wiki/Sierra_Nevada), including [Yosemite Valley](/wiki/Yosemite_Valley) and part of [Lake Tahoe](/wiki/Lake_Tahoe), [Mount Shasta](/wiki/Mount_Shasta) (the second-highest peak in the Cascade Range after [Mount Rainier](/wiki/Mount_Rainier) in [Washington](/wiki/Washington_(state))), and most of the [Central Valley](/wiki/Central_Valley_(California)), one of the world's most productive agricultural regions. Northern California is also home to [Silicon Valley](/wiki/Silicon_Valley), the global headquarters for several of the largest and most powerful companies in the world, including [Alphabet Inc. (Google)](/wiki/Alphabet_Inc.), [Apple](/wiki/Apple_Inc.), [Meta](/wiki/Meta_Platforms), and [Nvidia](/wiki/Nvidia).

The [Northern California Megaregion](/wiki/Northern_California_Megaregion), one of the 11 [megaregions of the United States](/wiki/Megaregions_of_the_United_States) is centered in Northern California, and extends from Metropolitan Fresno north to Greater Sacramento, and from the Bay Area east across the [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada) state line to encompass the entire [Lake Tahoe](/wiki/Lake_Tahoe)–[Reno](/wiki/Reno,_Nevada) area.

Evidence of [Native American](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) habitation in the area dates from at least 19,000 years ago and successive waves of arrivals led to one of the most densely populated areas of pre-Columbian North America. The arrival of European [explorers](/wiki/List_of_explorers) from the early 16th to the mid-18th centuries did not establish European settlements in northern California. In 1770, the [Spanish mission](/wiki/Spanish_missions_in_California) at [Monterey](/wiki/Monterey,_California) was the first European settlement in the area, followed by other missions along the coast—eventually extending as far north as [Sonoma County](/wiki/Sonoma_County,_California).

## Description

### North–south divisions of California

Map of northern California counties

California experienced a population boom during [its gold rush](/wiki/California_gold_rush) (1848–1855), bringing more than 300,000 new residents, with very few of these settling in the southern part of the state. The northern two-thirds of the state was seen as the main part, and was often referred to as simply "California", while the southern third was called "Southern California". At that time, the state was profoundly divided by the [Tehachapi Mountains](/wiki/Tehachapi_Mountains) which were a barrier to travelers before highways were built, and remain a [bioregion](/wiki/Bioregion) barrier. This geographical barrier curves from [Point Conception](/wiki/Point_Conception) at the Pacific Ocean eastward through the [Transverse Ranges](/wiki/Transverse_Ranges) including [Mount Pinos](/wiki/Mount_Pinos) and [Tejon Pass](/wiki/Tejon_Pass), continuing through the Tehachapi Mountains including [Tehachapi Pass](/wiki/Tehachapi_Pass), then cutting northward through the southern [Sierra Nevada](/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) mountain range to [Mount Patterson](/wiki/Mount_Patterson_(California)) and the [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada) border. The [Mojave Desert](/wiki/Mojave_Desert) and the [Great Basin Desert](/wiki/Great_Basin_Desert) are separated from Northern California by mountain ranges in this definition of bioregions. Southern California in the 1850s was a backwater of mainly Spanish-speaking [Californios](/wiki/Californios) who resented paying state taxes without receiving state projects.

The Pico Act of 1859 proposed to divide California to create the [Territory of Colorado](/wiki/Territory_of_Colorado_(California)) in the south, with the northern area retaining the name of California.

In 1859, as proposed by the Californio politician [Andrés Pico](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Pico), the [California State Legislature](/wiki/California_State_Legislature) passed the Pico Act aiming to divide the state and create a new territory in the south. The border started in the west at a latitude of six standard parallels south of [Mount Diablo](/wiki/Mount_Diablo)—meaning 144 miles south of Mount Diablo at the latitude of 35°45'N, currently the border between [Monterey](/wiki/Monterey_County,_California) and [San Luis Obispo](/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo_County,_California) counties. The proposed border bent southward to capture more of the Central Valley, then it angled northeast from [Old Tejon Ranch](/wiki/Old_Tejon_Ranch) to the California border. The new southern portion was to be called the [Territory of Colorado](/wiki/Territory_of_Colorado_(California)) because much of its eastern border was the [Colorado River](/wiki/Colorado_River). This legislation was signed by Governor [John B. Weller](/wiki/John_B._Weller) and sent to the [United States Congress](/wiki/United_States_Congress) for ratification, but it never came to a vote. Congress was too divided with tensions which would soon break out into the [American Civil War](/wiki/American_Civil_War), and despite the efforts of Senator [Milton Latham](/wiki/Milton_Latham), the bill died. However, the proposed east-west line continued to define one of the views of north–south division of the state.

"Northern California" may refer to the state's northernmost 48 counties, using the line established by the Pico Act, or it may refer to the portion north of the geographic barrier formed by the Transverse Ranges, the Tehachapi Mountains, and the Southern Sierras. Because of California's large size and diverse geography, the state can be subdivided in other ways as well. For example, the [Central Valley](/wiki/Central_Valley_(California)) is a region that is distinct both culturally and topographically from coastal California, though in northern versus southern California divisions, the [Sacramento Valley](/wiki/Sacramento_Valley) and most of the [San Joaquin Valley](/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley) are usually placed in northern California. Some observers describe three partitions of California, with north and south sections separated by [Central California](/wiki/Central_California).

Technically, California's exact north–south midway division is around [37°N](/wiki/37th_parallel_north), near the latitude of [Morgan Hill](/wiki/Morgan_Hill,_California) and [Chowchilla](/wiki/Chowchilla,_California). The [geographic center](/wiki/Geographical_centre) of California is at [37°09′58″N 119°26′58″W / 37.16611°N 119.44944°W / 37.16611; -119.44944](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Northern_California&params=37_09_58_N_119_26_58_W_) near [North Fork](/wiki/North_Fork,_California), northeast of Fresno.

The state is often considered as having an additional division north of the urban areas of the [San Francisco Bay Area](/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) and [Sacramento](/wiki/Sacramento,_California) metropolitan areas. Extreme northern residents have felt under-represented in state government and, in 1941, attempted to form a new state with southwestern Oregon to be called [Jefferson](/wiki/Jefferson_(Pacific_state)), or more recently to introduce legislation to [split California into two or three states](/wiki/Partition_and_secession_in_California).

### Popular usage

The coastal area north of the Bay Area is often referred to as the [North Coast](/wiki/North_Coast_(California)), touching the counties of [Mendocino](/wiki/Mendocino_County,_California), [Lake](/wiki/Lake_County,_California), [Humboldt](/wiki/Humboldt_County,_California), and [Del Norte](/wiki/Del_Norte_County,_California). The interior region north of Sacramento metropolitan area is referred to by locals as the **Northstate**, consisting of about 20 counties.

"Northern California" was used by [Tim Draper](/wiki/Tim_Draper) as the name of the northernmost state to be created by splitting California into three new states. The bill, [Cal 3](/wiki/Cal_3), was prevented from appearing on the 2018 California ballot because of a constitutional review by the [Supreme Court of California](/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_California).

## Significance

Since the events of the [California Gold Rush](/wiki/California_Gold_Rush), Northern California has been a leader on the world's economic, scientific, and cultural stages. From the development of gold mining techniques and logging practices in the 19th century that were later adopted around the world, to the development of world-famous and online business models (such as [Apple](/wiki/Apple_Inc.), [Hewlett-Packard](/wiki/Hewlett-Packard), [Google](/wiki/Google), [Yahoo!](/wiki/Yahoo!), and [eBay](/wiki/EBay)), northern California has been at the forefront of new ways of doing business. In science, advances range from being [the first](/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_National_Laboratory) to isolate and name fourteen [transuranic](/wiki/Transuranic) [chemical elements](/wiki/Chemical_element), to breakthroughs in microchip technology. Cultural contributions include the works of [Ansel Adams](/wiki/Ansel_Adams), [George Lucas](/wiki/George_Lucas), and [Clint Eastwood](/wiki/Clint_Eastwood), as well as [beatniks](/wiki/Beatniks), the [Summer of Love](/wiki/Summer_of_Love), [winemaking](/wiki/California_wine), the cradle of the international environmental movement, and the open, casual workplace first popularized in the [Silicon Valley](/wiki/Silicon_Valley) [dot-com boom](/wiki/Dot-com_boom) and now widely in use around the world. Other examples of innovation across diverse fields range from [Genentech](/wiki/Genentech) (development and commercialization of genetic engineering) to [CrossFit](/wiki/CrossFit) as a pioneer in extreme human fitness and training.
It is also home to one of the largest Air Force Bases on the West Coast, and the largest in California by square meters, [Travis Air Force Base](/wiki/Travis_Air_Force_Base).

## Cities

Northern California's largest [metropolitan area](/wiki/Metropolitan_area) is the [San Francisco Bay Area](/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area) which consists of 9 counties: Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Napa, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Solano, and Sonoma counties. The Bay Area consists of the major cities of [San Jose](/wiki/San_Jose,_California), [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco,_California), [Oakland](/wiki/Oakland,_California), and their many [suburbs](/wiki/Suburbs). Although not a part of the Bay Area, in recent years the Bay Area has drawn more commuters from as far as [Central Valley](/wiki/Central_Valley_(California)) cities such as [Sacramento](/wiki/Sacramento,_California), [Stockton](/wiki/Stockton,_California), [Fresno](/wiki/Fresno,_California), [Turlock](/wiki/Turlock,_California) and [Modesto](/wiki/Modesto,_California). These cities in the central part of the Central Valley and Sierra Nevada foothills may be viewed as part of a single [megalopolis](/wiki/Megalopolis_(city_type)). The 2010 U.S. Census showed that the Bay Area grew at a faster rate than the [Greater Los Angeles Area](/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Area) while Greater Sacramento had the largest growth rate of any metropolitan area in California.

The state's larger inland cities are considered part of Northern California in cases when the state is divided into two parts. Key cities in the region which are not in major metropolitan areas include [Eureka](/wiki/Eureka,_California) on the far [North Coast](/wiki/North_Coast_(California)), [Redding](/wiki/Redding,_California), at the northern end of the Central Valley, [Chico](/wiki/Chico,_California), and [Yuba City](/wiki/Yuba_City) in the mid-north of the Central Valley, as well as [Fresno](/wiki/Fresno,_California) and [Visalia](/wiki/Visalia,_California) on the southern end. Though smaller in each case, with the notable exception of [Fresno](/wiki/Fresno,_California), than the larger cities of the general region, these smaller regional centers are often of historical and economic importance for their respective size, due to their locations, which are primarily rural or otherwise isolated.

- [San Jose](/wiki/San_Jose,_California), the most populous city in Northern California and the [San Francisco Bay Area](/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area), and the 13th most populous city in the United States.
- [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco), the second most populated city in Northern California and a major economic, cultural, and financial center for the region.
- [Fresno](/wiki/Fresno), the fourth most populated city in northern California, as seen from [Chukchansi Park](/wiki/Chukchansi_Park). Fresno is the largest city by population in the [San Joaquin Valley](/wiki/San_Joaquin_Valley).
- [Sacramento](/wiki/Sacramento,_California), the third most populous city in Northern California, the capital city of the State of [California](/wiki/California), and the principal city of the [Sacramento metropolitan area](/wiki/Sacramento_metropolitan_area).
- [Oakland](/wiki/Oakland,_California), the fifth-largest city by population in Northern California. Oakland is the major [port city](/wiki/Port_of_Oakland) of the region and the center of Northern California's [African American](/wiki/African_Americans) community.

## History

Map of negotiation of the border between Mexico and the United States (1845-1848) as part of the American intervention in México.

### Prehistory to 1847

Inhabited for millennia by [Native Americans](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), from the [Shasta](/wiki/Shasta_(tribe)) tribe in the north, to the [Miwoks](/wiki/Miwok) in the central coast and Sierra Nevada, to the [Yokuts](/wiki/Yokuts) of the southern Central Valley, northern California was among the most densely populated areas of pre-Columbian North America.

#### European explorers

The first European to explore the coast was [Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo](/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo), sailing for the Spanish Crown; in 1542, Cabrillo's expedition sailed perhaps as far north as the [Rogue River](/wiki/Rogue_River_(Oregon)) in today's [Oregon](/wiki/Oregon). Beginning in 1565, the Spanish [Manila galleons](/wiki/Manila_galleon) crossed the Pacific Ocean from [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico) to the Spanish [Philippines](/wiki/Philippines), with silver and gemstones from Mexico. The Manila galleons returned across the northern Pacific, and reached North America usually off the coast of northern California, and then continued south with their Asian trade goods to Mexico.

In 1579, northern California was visited by the English explorer [Sir Francis Drake](/wiki/Francis_Drake) who landed north of today's [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco) and claimed the area for England. In 1602, the Spaniard [Sebastián Vizcaíno](/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Vizca%C3%ADno) explored California's coast as far north as [Monterey Bay](/wiki/Monterey_Bay), where he went ashore. Other Spanish explorers sailed along the coast of northern California for the next 150 years, but no settlements were established.

#### Spanish era

The first European inhabitants were Spanish missionaries, who built [missions](/wiki/Spanish_missions_in_California) along the California coast. The mission at [Monterey](/wiki/Mission_San_Carlos_Borromeo_de_Carmelo) was first established in 1770, and at [San Francisco](/wiki/Mission_San_Francisco_de_As%C3%ADs) in 1776. In all, ten missions stretched along the coast from [Sonoma](/wiki/Sonoma,_California) to Monterey (and still more missions to the southern tip of [Baja California](/wiki/Baja_California)). In 1786, the [French](/wiki/France) signaled their interest in the northern California area by sending a voyage of exploration to Monterey.

The first twenty years of the 19th century continued the colonization of the northern California coast by Spain. By 1820, Spanish influence extended inland approximately 25 to 50 miles (80 km) from the missions. Outside of this zone, perhaps 200,000 to 250,000 Native Americans continued to lead traditional lives. The [Adams-Onís Treaty](/wiki/Adams-On%C3%ADs_Treaty), signed in 1819 between Spain and the young United States, set the northern boundary of the Spanish claims at the 42nd parallel, effectively creating today's northern boundary of northern California.

#### Russian presence

In 1812, the Russian state-sponsored [Russian-American Company](/wiki/Russian-American_Company) established [Fort Ross](/wiki/Fort_Ross), a fur trading outpost on the coast of today's [Sonoma County](/wiki/Sonoma_County). Fort Ross was the southernmost Russian settlement, located some 60 miles (97 km) north of Spanish colonies in San Francisco. In 1839, the settlement was abandoned due to its inability to meet resource demands, and the increasing Mexican and American presence in the region.

#### Mexican era

After [Mexico gained independence](/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence) from Spain in 1821, Mexico continued Spain's missions and settlements in northern California as well as Spain's territorial claims. The Mexican *[Californios](/wiki/Californio)* (Spanish-speaking Californians) in these settlements primarily traded cattle hides and [tallow](/wiki/Tallow) with American and European merchant vessels.

[Coast Redwoods](/wiki/Sequoia_sempervirens) in [Muir Woods National Monument](/wiki/Muir_Woods_National_Monument), in [Marin County](/wiki/Marin_County)

In 1825, the [Hudson's Bay Company](/wiki/Hudson%27s_Bay_Company) established a major trading post [just north](/wiki/Fort_Vancouver) of today's [Portland, Oregon](/wiki/Portland,_Oregon). British fur trappers and hunters then used the [Siskiyou Trail](/wiki/Siskiyou_Trail) to travel throughout northern California. The leader of a further French scientific expedition to northern California, [Eugene Duflot de Mofras](/wiki/Eugene_Duflot_de_Mofras), wrote in 1840 "*...it is evident that California will belong to whatever nation chooses to send there a man-of-war and two hundred men*." By the 1830s, a significant number of non-*Californios* had immigrated to northern California. Chief among these was [John Sutter](/wiki/John_Sutter), a European immigrant from [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland), who was granted 48,827 acres (197.60 km2) [centered](/wiki/Sutter%27s_Fort) on the area of today's [Sacramento](/wiki/Sacramento,_California).

#### American interest

American trappers began entering northern California in the 1830s. In 1834, American visionary [Ewing Young](/wiki/Ewing_Young) led a herd of horses and mules over the [Siskiyou Trail](/wiki/Siskiyou_Trail) from missions in northern California to British and American settlements in [Oregon](/wiki/Oregon). Although a small number of American traders and trappers had lived in northern California since the early 1830s, the first organized overland party of American immigrants to arrive in northern California was the [Bartleson-Bidwell Party](/wiki/Bartleson-Bidwell_Party) of 1841 via the new [California Trail](/wiki/California_Trail). Also in 1841, an overland exploratory party of the [United States Exploring Expedition](/wiki/United_States_Exploring_Expedition) came down the [Siskiyou Trail](/wiki/Siskiyou_Trail) from the Pacific Northwest. In 1846, the [Donner Party](/wiki/Donner_Party) earned notoriety as they struggled to enter northern California.

#### Californian independence and beginning of the United States era

When the [Mexican–American War](/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) was declared on May 13, 1846, it took almost two months (mid-July 1846) for word to get to California. On June 14, 1846, some 30 non-Mexican settlers, mostly Americans, staged a revolt and seized the small Mexican garrison in Sonoma. They raised the "[Bear Flag](/wiki/Flag_of_California)" of the [California Republic](/wiki/California_Republic) over Sonoma. The "Bear Flag Republic" lasted only 26 days, until the U.S. Army, led by [John Frémont](/wiki/John_Fr%C3%A9mont), took over on July 9. The California state flag today is based on this original Bear Flag, and continues to contain the words "California Republic."

Commodore [John Drake Sloat](/wiki/John_Drake_Sloat) ordered his naval forces to occupy [Yerba Buena](/wiki/Yerba_Buena,_California) (present [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco)) on July 7 and within days American forces controlled San Francisco, Sonoma, and [Sutter's Fort](/wiki/Sutter%27s_Fort) in Sacramento. The [treaty](/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo) ending the Mexican–American War was signed on February 2, 1848, and Mexico formally ceded [Alta California](/wiki/Alta_California) (including all of present-day northern California) to the United States.

### Gold Rush and California statehood

The [California Gold Rush](/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) took place almost exclusively in northern California from 1848 to 1855. It began on January 24, 1848, when [gold](/wiki/Gold) was discovered at [Sutter's Mill](/wiki/Sutter%27s_Mill) in [Coloma](/wiki/Coloma,_California). News of the discovery soon spread, resulting in some 300,000 people coming to California from the rest of the United States and abroad. [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco,_California) grew from a tiny hamlet, home to about 1,000 [Californios](/wiki/Californios) into a [boomtown](/wiki/Boomtown) of over 50,000 people in the 12 years between 1848 and 1860. New roads, churches, and schools were built, and new towns sprung up, aided in part by the development of new methods of transportation such as [steamships](/wiki/Steamship) which came into regular service and [railroads](/wiki/California_and_the_railroads) which now connected the coasts. The Gold Rush also had negative effects: [American colonists chose to use genocide as a tool](/wiki/California_Genocide) to remove the [Indigenous people](/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_California) so that they could look for gold on their land.

The Gold Rush also increased pressure to make California a [U.S. state](/wiki/U.S._state). Pro-slavery politicians initially attempted to permanently divide northern and southern California at 36 degrees, 30 minutes, the line of the [Missouri Compromise](/wiki/Missouri_Compromise). But instead, the passing of the [Compromise of 1850](/wiki/Compromise_of_1850) enabled California to be admitted to the Union as a [free state](/wiki/Slave_and_free_states).

### Population and agricultural expansion (1855–1899)

Farm near [Mount Shasta](/wiki/Mount_Shasta)

The decades following the Gold Rush brought dramatic expansion to northern California, both in population and economically – particularly in agriculture. The completion of the [First transcontinental railroad](/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad) in 1869, with its terminus in Sacramento (and then later, Oakland), meant that northern California's agricultural produce (and some manufactured goods) could now be shipped economically to the rest of the United States. In return, immigrants from the rest of the United States (and Europe) could comfortably come to northern California. A network of railroads spread throughout northern California, and in 1887, a [rail link](/wiki/Siskiyou_Trail) was completed to the [Pacific Northwest](/wiki/Pacific_Northwest). Almost all of these railways came under the control of the [Southern Pacific Railroad](/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Railroad), headquartered in San Francisco, and San Francisco continued as a financial and cultural center.

Substantial tensions during this era included nativist sentiments (primarily against Chinese immigrants), tensions between the increasing power of the Southern Pacific Railroad and small farmers, and the beginnings of the labor union movement.

## Economy

Satellite image of Northern California at night

Northern California's economy is noted for being the de facto world leader in high-tech industry ([software](/wiki/Software), [semiconductor](/wiki/Semiconductor)/micro-electronics, [biotechnology](/wiki/Biotechnology) and [medical devices](/wiki/Medical_devices)/instruments), as well as being known for [clean power](/wiki/Environmental_engineering), biomedical, government, and finance. Other significant industries include tourism, shipping, manufacturing, and agriculture. Its economy is diverse, though more concentrated in high technology. It is home to the state capital, as well as several Western United States regional offices in San Francisco, such as the [Federal Reserve](/wiki/Federal_Reserve) and [9th Circuit Court](/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Ninth_Circuit).

## Climate

[Köppen climate types](/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) in northern California

Northern California has a warm or mild to cool climate, in which the [Sierra mountains](/wiki/Sierra_Nevada) gets snow in the late fall through winter and occasionally into spring. Summers are mild along the coast and generally warm and dry, while winters are cool and usually wet. The high temperatures range from 50s °F (10–15 °C) to 30s °F (−1 – +4 °C) in the winters while summers temperature range is 90s °F (32–37 °C) to 60s °F (15–20 °C) or 50s °F (10–15 °C), with highs well into the 100s °F (37–42 °C) for the Sacramento region. Snow covers the mountains (generally above 3,000 feet (910 m)) in mid January through February. Fog occurs infrequently or occurs normally in the west and coast, especially in the summer, creating some of the coolest summer conditions in North America. Since the first decade of the 21st century, [droughts](/wiki/Droughts_in_California) and [wildfires](/wiki/List_of_California_wildfires) have increased in frequency as a consequence of [climate change](/wiki/Climate_change).

The population of the forty-eight counties of northern California has shown a steady increase over the years.

The largest percentage increase outside the Gold Rush era (52%) came during the 1940s, as the region was the destination of many post-War veterans and their families, attracted by the greatly expanding industrial base and (often) by their time stationed in northern California during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). The largest absolute increase occurred during the 1980s (over 2.1 million person increase), attracted by job opportunities in part by the expansion taking place in [Silicon Valley](/wiki/Silicon_Valley) and the [Cold War](/wiki/Cold_War)–era expansion of the defense industry. Since the 2000 U.S. Census, Northern California has grown at a faster rate than Southern California due to the strong economic performances of the Bay Area and Sacramento.

## Parks and other protected areas

### National Park System

Main articles: [List of areas in the United States National Park System](/wiki/List_of_areas_in_the_United_States_National_Park_System) and [List of national parks of the United States](/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_the_United_States)

The U.S. [National Park System](/wiki/National_Park_System) controls a large and diverse group of parks in northern California. The best known is [Yosemite National Park](/wiki/Yosemite_National_Park), which is displayed on the reverse side of the [California state quarter](/wiki/File:California_quarter,_reverse_side,_2005.jpg). Other prominent parks are the [Kings Canyon](/wiki/Kings_Canyon_National_Park)-[Sequoia National Park](/wiki/Sequoia_National_Park) complex, [Redwood National Park](/wiki/Redwood_National_Park), [Pinnacles National Park](/wiki/Pinnacles_National_Park), [Lassen Volcanic National Park](/wiki/Lassen_Volcanic_National_Park) and the largest in the contiguous forty-eight states, [Death Valley National Park](/wiki/Death_Valley_National_Park).

### National Monuments and other federally protected areas

Main articles: [List of national monuments of the United States](/wiki/List_of_national_monuments_of_the_United_States), [United States National Marine Sanctuary](/wiki/United_States_National_Marine_Sanctuary), [List of National Wildlife Refuges of the United States](/wiki/List_of_National_Wildlife_Refuges_of_the_United_States), and [List of national forests of the United States](/wiki/List_of_national_forests_of_the_United_States)

Other areas under federal protection include [Muir Woods National Monument](/wiki/Muir_Woods_National_Monument), [Giant Sequoia National Monument](/wiki/Giant_Sequoia_National_Monument), [Devils Postpile National Monument](/wiki/Devils_Postpile_National_Monument), [Lava Beds National Monument](/wiki/Lava_Beds_National_Monument), [Point Reyes National Seashore](/wiki/Point_Reyes_National_Seashore), the [Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary](/wiki/Monterey_Bay_National_Marine_Sanctuary), and the [Cordell Bank](/wiki/Cordell_Bank_National_Marine_Sanctuary) and [Gulf of the Farallones](/wiki/Greater_Farallones_National_Marine_Sanctuary) National Marine Sanctuaries (both off the coast of San Francisco). Included within the latter National Marine Sanctuary is the [Farallon National Wildlife Refuge](/wiki/Farallon_National_Wildlife_Refuge); this [National Wildlife Refuge](/wiki/National_Wildlife_Refuge) is one of approximately twenty-five such refuges in northern California. [National forests](/wiki/National_forest_(United_States)) occupy large sections of northern California, including the [Shasta–Trinity](/wiki/Shasta%E2%80%93Trinity_National_Forest), [Klamath](/wiki/Klamath_National_Forest), [Modoc](/wiki/Modoc_National_Forest), [Lassen](/wiki/Lassen_National_Forest), [Mendocino](/wiki/Mendocino_National_Forest), [Eldorado](/wiki/Eldorado_National_Forest), [Tahoe](/wiki/Tahoe_National_Forest), and [Sequoia](/wiki/Sequoia_National_Forest) national forests, among others. Included within (or adjacent to) national forests are federally protected wilderness areas, including the [Trinity Alps](/wiki/Trinity_Alps_Wilderness), [Castle Crags](/wiki/Castle_Crags), [Granite Chief](/wiki/Granite_Chief_Wilderness), and [Desolation](/wiki/Desolation_Wilderness) wilderness areas.

In addition, the [California Coastal National Monument](/wiki/California_Coastal_National_Monument) protects all islets, reefs, and rock outcroppings from the shore of northern California out to a distance of 12 nmi (22 km), along the entire northern California coastline. In addition, the National Park Service administers protected areas on [Alcatraz Island](/wiki/Alcatraz_Island), the [Golden Gate National Recreation Area](/wiki/Golden_Gate_National_Recreation_Area), [Whiskeytown–Shasta–Trinity National Recreation Area](/wiki/Whiskeytown%E2%80%93Shasta%E2%80%93Trinity_National_Recreation_Area), and the [Smith River National Recreation Area](/wiki/Smith_River_National_Recreation_Area). The NPS also administers the [Manzanar](/wiki/Manzanar) [National Historic Site](/wiki/National_Historic_Site_(United_States)) in [Inyo County](/wiki/Inyo_County,_California), the [Rosie the Riveter World War II Home Front National Historical Park](/wiki/Rosie_the_Riveter_World_War_II_Home_Front_National_Historical_Park) in [Richmond](/wiki/Richmond,_California), and the [Tule Lake National Monument](/wiki/Tule_Lake_National_Monument) outside of [Tulelake](/wiki/Tulelake,_California).

## Educational institutions

Cities include [Eureka](/wiki/Eureka,_California), [Ukiah](/wiki/Ukiah,_California), [Santa Rosa](/wiki/Santa_Rosa,_California), [Napa](/wiki/Napa,_California), [Vallejo](/wiki/Vallejo,_California) and [Fairfield](/wiki/Fairfield,_California).
- [831](/wiki/Area_code_831) — [Monterey](/wiki/Monterey_County,_California), [San Benito](/wiki/San_Benito_County,_California) and [Santa Cruz Counties](/wiki/Santa_Cruz_County,_California). Originally part of area code 408.

Originally part of area codes 415 and 510.

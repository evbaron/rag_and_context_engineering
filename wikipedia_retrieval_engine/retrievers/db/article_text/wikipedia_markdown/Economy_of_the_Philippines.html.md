The **economy of the Philippines** is an [emerging market](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_market), and considered as a [newly industrialized country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_country) in the [Asia-Pacific](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific) region. In 2026, the Philippine economy is estimated to be at ₱30.22 trillion ($512.22 billion), making it the world's [35th largest by nominal GDP](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_(nominal)) and [14th largest](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Asia) in [Asia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia) according to the [International Monetary Fund](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund).

The Philippine economy is a service-oriented economy, with relatively more modest contributions from the manufacturing and agriculture sectors. It has experienced significant economic growth and transformation in the past, posting one of the highest GDP growth rates in Asia. With an average annual growth rate of around 6 percent since 2010, the country has emerged as one of the fastest-growing economies in the world. The Philippines is a founding member of the [United Nations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations), [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Southeast_Asian_Nations), [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation), [East Asia Summit](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit) and the [World Trade Organization](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization). The [Asian Development Bank](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Development_Bank) (ADB) is headquartered in the [Ortigas Center](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Center) located in the city of [Mandaluyong](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaluyong), [Metro Manila](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila).

The country's primary exports include [semiconductors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor) and [electronic products](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), [transport equipment](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport), [garments](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garment), [chemical products](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical), [copper](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [nickel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel), [abaca](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abaca), [coconut oil](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_oil), and [fruits](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruits). Its major trading partners include [Japan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan), [China](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China), the [United States](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), [Singapore](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore), [South Korea](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea), the [Netherlands](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), [Hong Kong](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong), [Germany](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), [Taiwan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan), and [Thailand](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand). In 2017, the Philippine economy was projected to become the 9th largest in Asia and 19th largest in the world by 2050. By 2035, the Filipino economy is predicted to be the 22nd largest in the world.

The Philippines has been named as one of the [Tiger Cub Economies](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Cub_Economies), alongside [Indonesia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia), [Malaysia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia), [Vietnam](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam), and Thailand. However, major problems remain, mainly related to alleviating the wide income and growth disparities between the country's different regions and socioeconomic classes, reducing [corruption](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_the_Philippines), and investing in the infrastructure necessary to ensure future growth. In 2024, the [World Economic Forum](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum) chief [Børge Brende](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rge_Brende) said that "there is a real opportunity for this country to become a $2-trillion economy."

The Philippines exhibits one of the highest economic densities in Southeast Asia, characterized by a high concentration of economic output relative to its land area. As of 2024–2025, the Philippines ranks third among ASEAN nations in GDP Density (Nominal GDP per square kilometer), trailing only the city-state of Singapore and the resource-rich sultanate of Brunei. This is so, because there is such large economic activity that is happening on such low amount of land area that's scattered amongst a fragmented archipelagic geography. This is expressed in the concept called [GDP density](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_density).

In 2025, the Philippines enacted Republic Act No. 12252, amending the Investors’ Lease Act to allow foreign investors to lease private land for up to 99 years, up from the previous limit of 75 years (50 + 25 extension). This reform aims to enhance the country's competitiveness in attracting long-term foreign direct investment by offering greater leasehold security for commercial and industrial projects. A project called the "Luzon Economic Corridor" is on the works, which will further develop the Philippine economy. It is the first country in the world to put its entire national budget under [blockchain](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain) technology.

The Philippine economy has been growing steadily over decades and the International Monetary Fund in 2014 reported it as the 39th largest economy in the world. The Philippines posted a high GDP growth rate of 7.6 percent in 2022. ## Composition by sector

Filipino men at work in Brunei

As a [newly industrialized country](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_country), the Philippines is still an economy with a large agricultural sector; however, the country's service industry has expanded recently. Much of the industrial sector is based on processing and assembly operations in the manufacturing of electronics and other high-tech components, usually from foreign multinational corporations.

Filipinos who go abroad to work–-known as [Overseas Filipino Workers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipino_Worker) or OFWs—are a significant contributor to the economy but are not reflected in the below sectoral discussion of the domestic economy. OFW remittances is also credited for the Philippines' recent economic growth resulting in investment status upgrades from credit ratings agencies such as the [Fitch Group](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitch_Group) and [Standard & Poor's](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_%26_Poor%27s). From more than [US$](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Dollar)2 billion worth of remittance from Overseas Filipinos sent to the Philippines in 1994, this significantly increased to a record US$38.34 billion in 2024 and represented 8.3 percent of the country's GDP.

### Agriculture, aquaculture and forestry

Main articles: [Agriculture in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_the_Philippines), [Aquaculture in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture_in_the_Philippines), [Fisheries in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_in_the_Philippines), and [Deforestation in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_the_Philippines)

As of 2022, agriculture employs 24 percent of the Filipino workforce accounting for 8.9 percent of the total GDP. The type of activity ranges from small subsistence farming and fishing to large commercial ventures with significant export focus.

The Philippines is the [world's third largest producer](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_coconut_production) of [coconuts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut), and the world's largest exporter of coconut products. [Coconut production](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_production_in_the_Philippines) is generally concentrated in medium-sized farms. The Philippines is also the [world's third largest producer](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_pineapple_production) of [pineapples](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), producing 2,862,000 metric tons (2,817,000 long tons; 3,155,000 short tons) in 2021.

[Rice production in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_production_in_the_Philippines) is important to the food supply in the country and economy. The Philippines is the 8th largest rice producer in the world as of 2019, accounting for 2.5 percent of global rice production. Rice is the most important food crop, a staple food in most of the country; it is produced extensively in [Central Luzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon)), [Western Visayas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Visayas), [Cagayan Valley](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley), [Soccsksargen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccsksargen), and [Ilocos Region](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region).

The Philippines is one of the [largest producers of sugar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_industry_of_the_Philippines) in the world. At least 17 provinces located in eight regions of the nation have grown sugarcane crops, of which the [Negros Island Region](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region) accounts for half of the country's total production. As of Crop Year 2012–2013, 29 mills are operational divided as follows: 13 mills in [Negros](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros), 6 mills in [Luzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon), 4 mills in [Panay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panay), 3 mills in [Eastern Visayas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Visayas) and 3 mills in [Mindanao](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao). A range from 360,000 to 390,000 hectares (890,000 to 960,000 acres) are devoted to sugarcane production. The largest sugarcane areas are found in the [Negros Island Region](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region), which accounts for 51 percent of sugarcane areas planted. This is followed by [Mindanao](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao) which accounts for 20 percent; [Luzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon) with 17 percent; [Panay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panay) with 7 percent and [Eastern Visayas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Visayas) with 4 percent.

### Automotive and aerospace

Main article: [Automotive industry in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_the_Philippines)

The [ABS](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-lock_braking_system) used in [Mercedes-Benz](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz), [BMW](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW), and [Volvo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo) cars are made in the Philippines. Automotive sales in the Philippines increased to 467,252 in 2024 from 429,807 units a year prior. [Toyota](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) sells the most vehicles in the country followed by [Mitsubishi](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Motors), [Ford](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company), [Nissan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nissan), and [Suzuki](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki). [Honda](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda) and Suzuki produce motorcycles in the country. Since around the 2010s, several Chinese car brands have entered the Philippine market; these include [Chery](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chery) and [Foton Motor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foton_Motor).

Aerospace products in the Philippines are mainly for the export market and include manufacturing parts for aircraft built by both [Boeing](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing) and [Airbus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus). [Moog](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moog_Inc.) is the biggest aerospace manufacturer with base in [Baguio](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio); the company produces aircraft actuators in their manufacturing facility. Total export output of aerospace products in the Philippines reached US$780 million in 2019.

### Electronics

A Texas Instruments integrated circuit

A [Texas Instruments](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments) plant in [Baguio](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio) has been operating for 20 years and is the largest producer of [DSP chips](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processor) in the world. Texas Instruments' Baguio plant produces all the chips used in [Nokia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia) [cell phones](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone) and 80% of chips used in [Ericsson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ericsson) cell phones in the world. [Toshiba](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshiba) [hard disk drives](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_disk_drives) are manufactured in [Santa Rosa, Laguna](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Rosa,_Laguna). Printer manufacturer [Lexmark](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexmark) has a factory in [Cebu City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_City). Electronics and other light industries are concentrated in Laguna, [Cavite](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite), [Batangas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batangas) and other [Calabarzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon) provinces with sizable numbers found in Southern Philippines that account for most of the country's export.

The Philippine Electronics Industry is classified into (73%) Semiconductor Manufacturing Services (SMS) and (27%) Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) according to SEIPI, the largest organization of foreign and Filipino electronics companies in the Philippines. Electronic products continued to be the country's top export with total earnings of US$45.66 billion and accounted for 57.8 percent of the total export of goods in 2022.

#### Semiconductor

The semiconductor industry plays a critical role in the Philippine economy by integrating the Philippines into the global electronics value chain. The country is primarily involved in the *assembly, testing, and packaging stages of semiconductor production* rather than chip design or wafer fabrication, serving as a key link in the global supply chain for electronic components.

According to the Semiconductor and Electronics Industries in the Philippines Foundation (SEIPI), semiconductor manufacturing services account for the majority of the country’s electronics industry output. Semiconductor products are widely used in consumer electronics, telecommunications equipment, automotive systems, and industrial applications.

Semiconductor manufacturing facilities in the Philippines are largely operated by multinational firms and are concentrated in major industrial zones, particularly in the CALABARZON region as well as parts of Central Luzon and the Visayas. The industry benefits from a skilled technical workforce, competitive production costs, and government investment incentives.

As part of the broader electronics sector, semiconductors contribute significantly to export earnings, employment generation, and foreign direct investment. The industry also supports technology transfer and skills development, reinforcing its role as one of the Philippines’ most important high-technology manufacturing sectors.

Main article: [Mining in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_the_Philippines)

See also: [List of mines in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mines_in_the_Philippines) and [Coal mining in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining_in_the_Philippines)

[Geothermal power](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power) station in [Negros Oriental](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Oriental)

The Philippines is rich in mineral and [geothermal energy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power_in_the_Philippines) resources. In 2019, it produced 1,928 megawatts (2,585,000 hp) of electricity from geothermal sources (7.55% of total [electricity production](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_the_Philippines)). A 1989 discovery of natural gas reserves in the [Malampaya oil fields](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Oil_Company) off the island of [Palawan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_(island)) is being used to generate electricity in three gas-powered plants. Philippine [gold](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold), [nickel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel), [copper](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [palladium](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium) and [chromite](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite) deposits are among the largest in the world. Other important minerals include [silver](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver), [coal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [gypsum](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), and [sulphur](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur). Significant deposits of [clay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay), [limestone](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [marble](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [silica](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silica), and [phosphate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) exist.

About 60 percent of total mining production are accounted for by non-metallic minerals, which contributed substantially to the industry's steady output growth between 1993 and 1998, with the value of production growing 58 percent. Philippine mineral exports amounted to US$4.22 billion in 2020. Low metal prices, high production costs, lack of investment in infrastructure, and a challenge to the new mining law have contributed to the mining industry's overall decline.

The industry rebounded starting in late 2004 when the [Supreme Court](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines) upheld the constitutionality of an important law permitting foreign ownership of Philippines mining companies. In 2019, the country was the 2nd largest world producer of nickel and the 4th largest world producer of [cobalt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt). According to [Philippine Statistics Authority](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority), the total monetary value of four key metallic minerals which were appraised to Class A namely: copper, chromite, gold and nickel were valued at US$9.01 billion in 2022. Class A mineral resources are commercially recoverable that could contribute to economy annually.

### Offshoring and outsourcing

Main articles: [Business process outsourcing in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing_in_the_Philippines) and [Call center industry in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_center_industry_in_the_Philippines)

A business process outsourcing office in [Cainta, Rizal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta,_Rizal)

The [Cebu IT Park](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_IT_Park) in [Cebu City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_City)

[Business process outsourcing](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing) (BPO) and the call center industry contribute to the Philippines' economic growth resulting in investment status upgrades from credit ratings agencies such as Fitch and S&P. In 2008, the Philippines has surpassed India as the world leader in [business process outsourcing](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing) (BPO). The industry generated 100,000 jobs, and total revenues were placed at US$960 million for 2005. In 2011, BPO sector employment ballooned to over 700,000 people and is contributing to a growing [middle class](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_class); this increased to around 1.3 million employees by 2022. BPO facilities are concentrated in [IT parks](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IT_park) and centers in [economic zones](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_special_economic_zones_in_the_Philippines) across the Philippines: [Metro Manila](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila), [Metro Cebu](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Cebu), [Metro Clark](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Clark), [Bacolod](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod), [Davao City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_City), and [Iloilo City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo_City); other areas with significant presence of the BPO industry include [Baguio](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio), [Cagayan de Oro](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro), [Dasmariñas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasmari%C3%B1as), [Dumaguete](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete), [Lipa](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipa,_Batangas), [Naga](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga,_Camarines_Sur), and [Santa Rosa, Laguna](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Rosa,_Laguna). The majority of the top ten BPO firms of the United States operate in the Philippines.

[Call centers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_centers) began in the Philippines as plain providers of email response and managing services and is a major source of employment. Call center services include customer relations, ranging from travel services, technical support, education, customer care, financial services, online business to customer support, and online business-to-business support. The Philippines is considered as a location of choice due to its many outsourcing benefits such as less expensive operational and labor costs, the high proficiency in spoken [English](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) of a significant number of its people, and a highly educated labor pool.

The growth in the BPO industry is promoted by the Philippine government. The industry is highlighted by the Philippines Development Plan as among the 10 high potential and priority development areas. The government provides incentive programs such as tax holidays, tax exemptions, and simplified export and import procedures. Additionally, training is also available for BPO applicants.

### Energy

Main articles: [Energy in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_the_Philippines), [Renewable energy in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_the_Philippines), and [Electricity sector in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_the_Philippines)

Solar module installation in [Bulacan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulacan)

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Energy in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_the_Philippines).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Energy_in_the_Philippines&action=edit)]

The Philippines relies on a mix of fossil fuels and renewable resources for its energy needs. Electricity demand is driven by population growth, urbanisation and industry. Energy policy and planning are overseen by the [Department of Energy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy_(Philippines)). Usage patterns vary across [Luzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon), the [Visayas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayas) and [Mindanao](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao), reflecting differences in generation assets, grid interconnections and local demand.

The population of the Philippines is over 101 million people, and as a rapidly developing nation, has seen a rapid increase in GDP, averaging 6.1% from 2011 to 2015. Energy-intensive manufacturing and retail industries are the driving factors of the Philippines' economic growth. Given its large population and rapidly growing economy, the country's energy needs are growing rapidly. According to the Philippines [Department of Energy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy_(Philippines)), the Philippines consumed 75,266 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of electricity in 2013. Of this, 27.39% went to powering residential areas, 24.31% to commercial establishments and 27.46% to the industrial sector. With 72.84% of electrical energy being consumed by [Luzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon), 14.75% by Visayas, and 12.41% by Mindanao in 2013.

The Philippines has significant [potential in solar energy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_in_the_Philippines); however, as of 2021, most of the domestically produced electricity is based on fossil fuel resources, particularly coal. The country produced 7,399 megawatts (9,922,000 hp) of renewable energy in 2019.

On November 15, 2022, the [renewable energy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy) sector was granted the ability to operate with 100 percent foreign ownership, an increase from the previous 40 percent limit. This change allows for the infusion of foreign capital into the renewable energy (RE) industries. The [Department of Energy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy_(Philippines)) is targeting an increase in the share of renewable energy in the country's power generation mix, aiming for 35 percent by 2030 and 50 percent by 2040, up from the current 22 percent. Danish firm [Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners (CIP)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Infrastructure_Partners) is investing US$5 billion to develop three offshore wind energy projects with a potential capacity of 2,000 megawatts (2,700,000 hp); it will be located in [Camarines Norte](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarines_Norte) and [Camarines Sur](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarines_Sur) (1000 MW), [Northern Samar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Samar) (650 MW), [Pangasinan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan) and [La Union](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union) (350 MW). In 2022, the share of RE in the energy mix was at 22.8 percent.

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Electricity sector in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_the_Philippines).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Electricity_sector_in_the_Philippines&action=edit)]

The [electricity sector in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_the_Philippines) provides electricity through power generation, transmission, and distribution to many parts of the country. The Philippines is divided into three [electrical grids](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_grid), one each for [Luzon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon), the [Visayas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayas) and [Mindanao](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindanao).

As of June 2016, the total installed capacity in the Philippines was 20,055 [megawatts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts) (MW), of which 71.5% was on the Luzon grid. About 12% of Filipinos have no access to electricity. The Philippines is also one of the countries in the world that has a fully functioning [electricity market](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_market) since 2006 called the Philippine Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) and is operated by an independent market operator. By 2017, coal-powered electric energy began to comprise more than 50% of the nationwide power generation in the country.

As of 2025, the Philippines has the second-highest electricity rate in [Southeast Asia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia) after [Singapore](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore).

### Shipbuilding and repair

[Hanjin Subic Shipyard](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agila_Subic_Shipyard) in [Subic, Zambales](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic,_Zambales)

The Philippines is a significant player in the global [shipbuilding](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) industry with 118 registered [shipyards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipyard) in 2021 distributed in [Subic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic_Bay), [Cebu](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu), [Bataan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan), [Navotas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navotas) and [Batangas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batangas). As of 2022, it is the seventh largest shipbuilding nation by gross tonnage. Subic-made [cargo vessels](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_vessel) are exported to countries where shipping operators are based. South Korea's [Hanjin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanjin_Heavy_Industries_and_Construction_Philippines) started production in Subic in 2007 of the 20 ships ordered by German and Greek shipping operators. [Bulk carriers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_carrier), container ships and big passenger ferries are built in the country's shipyards. [General Santos](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos)' shipyard is mainly for ship repair and maintenance.

Surrounded by waters, the country has abundant natural deep-sea ports ideal for development as production, construction and repair sites. In the ship repair sector, the Navotas complex in [Metro Manila](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Manila) is expected to accommodate 96 vessels for repair. Shipbuilding is part of Philippines' maritime heritage; employing over 600,000 people and contributing almost 15 percent of revenues to the ocean-based industries.

### Education

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Education in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Philippines).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Education_in_the_Philippines&action=edit)]

[Education in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Philippines) is [compulsory](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education) at the basic education level, composed of [kindergarten](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten), [elementary school](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_school) (grades 1–6), [junior high school](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school) (grades 7–10), and [senior high school](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school) (grades 11–12). The educational system is managed by three [government](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Philippines) agencies by level of education: the [Department of Education](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_(Philippines)) (DepEd) for basic education; the [Commission on Higher Education](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Higher_Education) (CHED) for [higher education](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_the_Philippines); and the [Technical Education and Skills Development Authority](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_Education_and_Skills_Development_Authority) (TESDA) for technical and [vocational education](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_education). Public education is funded by the national government.

Private schools are generally free to determine their curriculum in accordance with existing laws and regulations. Institutions of [higher education](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) are classified as public or private; public institutions are subdivided into [state universities and colleges](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_the_Philippines) (SUCs) and [local colleges and universities](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_colleges_and_universities_(Philippines)) (LCUs).

Enrollment in basic education has increased steadily since the effectivity of the K-12 curriculum, first implemented on May 20, 2008 through its 9 years of implementation until June 5, 2017, on April 24, 2012, with over 28 million students enrolled in the 2022-2023 school year. In 2020, there were approximately 32 million learners aged 5 to 24 enrolled nationwide. An additional 640,000 out-of-school youth participated in the [Alternative Learning System](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Learning_System_(Philippines)), while 1.6 million children aged 5 to 17 remained out of school as of 2023. Completion rates for primary and lower secondary education are relatively high, but drop-out rates and barriers to upper secondary and tertiary education remain, particularly among lower-income students.

### Healthcare

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Health care in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_the_Philippines).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Health_care_in_the_Philippines&action=edit)]

[Health care in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_the_Philippines) varies with private, public and barangay health centers (many in rural municipalities). Most of the national burden of health care is provided by private health providers, with the cost shouldered by the state or by patients. The 2019 Universal Health Care Act (UHC Act) represents a significant effort to bridge the quality and accessibility gap, aiming to enroll all Filipinos in the National Health Insurance Program (PhilHealth). However, disparities persist, particularly between urban and rural areas, and funding constraints continue to impact service delivery. The Philippine healthcare system categorizes hospitals into three distinct levels, reflecting their capabilities and resources, with Level 1 representing basic care and Level 3 the most advanced. Beyond these levels, Philippine hospitals are further differentiated by their ownership structure (government/public vs private) and the breadth of medical services they offer (generic vs specialised vs emergency, etc.).

The Philippine healthcare system, a blend of public and private sectors, faces challenges in providing equitable and comprehensive care. Historically rooted in traditional medicine and shaped by colonial influences, the system now navigates a landscape where private providers shoulder much of the burden, with costs borne by the state or patients. Despite the UHC Act's intent to improve care for all, the system remains fragmented, with significant disparities in service quality and quantity between the wealthy and the poor. Factors contributing to this include low budgets, personnel shortages exacerbated by nurse migration, and historical neglect of underserved populations. Compared to developed nations, the Philippines allocates a comparatively small percentage of its GDP to healthcare. Addressing these challenges remains a priority for the nation.

### Transport

The [MRT Line 3](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRT_Line_3_(Metro_Manila)), and [EDSA Carousel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Carousel) alongside [Epifanio de los Santos Avenue](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA) in 2024.

[Transportation in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_the_Philippines) covers the transportation methods within the [archipelagic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic) nation of over 7,600 islands. From a previously underdeveloped state of transportation, the [government of the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Philippines) has been improving transportation through various direct infrastructure projects, and these include an increase in air, sea, road, and rail transportation and transport hubs. However, relative to the size of population and level of income, public transportation remains underdeveloped in the Philippines.

[Kalesa](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalesa) historicaly, was one of the main land transportation in the Philippines during the [Spanish](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1565%E2%80%931898)) and [American](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_(1898%E2%80%931946)) era.

[Jeepneys](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepney) are a popular and iconic [public utility vehicle](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility_vehicle); they have become a symbol of the [Philippine culture](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_culture). Another popular mode of [public transportation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transportation) in the country is the [motorized tricycles](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorized_tricycle_(Philippines)), especially common in smaller urban and rural areas. The Philippines has [four railway lines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transportation_in_the_Philippines): [Manila Light Rail Transit System Line 1](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Light_Rail_Transit_System_Line_1) (LRT Line 1), [LRT Line 2](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Light_Rail_Transit_System_Line_2), [MRT Line 3](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Metro_Rail_Transit_System_Line_3), and the [PNR Metro Commuter Line](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNR_Metro_Commuter_Line) operated by the [Philippine National Railways](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways). There are also steam engines found in the Visayas, mostly Negros island, which operate sugar mills such as Central Azucarera. [Taxis](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_of_the_Philippines) and [buses](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bus_companies_of_the_Philippines) are also important modes of public transport in urban areas.

The Philippines has [12 international airports](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Philippines) and more than 20 major and minor domestic airports serving the country. [Ninoy Aquino International Airport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport) is the country's chief airport.

### Tourism

[Boracay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boracay) white beach

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Tourism in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Philippines).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tourism_in_the_Philippines&action=edit)]

[Tourism](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Philippines) is an important sector for the [Philippine economy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Philippines). The travel and tourism industry contributed 8.9% to the country's [GDP](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) in 2024; this was lower than the 12.7% recorded in 2019 prior to the COVID-19 lockdowns. Coastal tourism, encompassing beach and diving activities, constitutes 25% of the Philippines' tourism revenue, serving as its primary income source in the sector. Popular destinations among tourists include [Boracay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boracay), [Palawan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan), [Cebu](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu) and [Siargao](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siargao). While the Philippines has encountered political and social challenges that have affected its tourism industry, the country has also taken steps to address these issues. Over the past years, there have been efforts to improve political stability, enhance security measures, and promote social inclusivity, all of which contribute to creating a more favorable environment for tourism, such as the Boracay rehabilitation.

As of 2024, 6.75 million Filipinos were employed in the tourism industry, it generated ₱760.5 billion (US$13.1 billion) in revenue from foreign tourists, coming mostly from South Korea, the United States and Japan. The country attracted a total of 5,360,682 foreign visitors in 2015 through its tourism campaign of *[It's More Fun in the Philippines!](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_More_Fun_in_the_Philippines!)* In 2019, foreign arrivals peaked at 8,260,913.

[Vigan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigan), a [World Heritage Site](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site)

The country is also home to one of the [New 7 Wonders of Nature](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_7_Wonders_of_Nature), the [Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Princesa_Subterranean_River_National_Park), and one of the [New 7 Wonders Cities](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_7_Wonders_Cities), the Heritage City of [Vigan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigan). It is also home to six [UNESCO](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO) [World Heritage Sites](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites) scattered in nine different locations, three UNESCO [biosphere reserves](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_reserves), three UNESCO [intangible cultural heritage](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage), four UNESCO memory of the world documentary heritage, three UNESCO [creative cities](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_city), two UNESCO World Heritage cities, seven [Ramsar](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_site) wetland sites, and eight [ASEAN Heritage Parks](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASEAN_Heritage_Parks).

## Labor, income and welfare

See also: [Labor policy in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_policy_in_the_Philippines), [Philippine labor migration policy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_labor_migration_policy), [Social Security System (Philippines)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_System_(Philippines)), [Unemployment benefits in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_benefits_in_the_Philippines), [Income inequality in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality_in_the_Philippines), and [Poverty in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_Philippines)

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Labor policy in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_policy_in_the_Philippines).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Labor_policy_in_the_Philippines&action=edit)]

The [labor policy in the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_policy_in_the_Philippines) is primarily defined by the [Labor Code of the Philippines](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Code_of_the_Philippines) and other related labor laws. As of 2025, these laws apply to approximately 38 million Filipinos who are part of the labor force, including, to some extent, [overseas workers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipino_Worker). They are intended to establish the legal rights of workers and set limitations concerning the hiring process, working conditions, employee benefits, labor policymaking within companies, and employer-employee relations.

The Labor Code and other labor legislation are implemented mainly by government agencies, including the [Department of Labor and Employment](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Labor_and_Employment_(Philippines)) and the [Department of Migrant Workers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Migrant_Workers) (formerly the Philippine Overseas Employment Administration). Non-governmental entities, such as trade unions and employer organizations, also contribute to the development and enforcement of labor standards in the country.

## Regional accounts

Several terms redirect here. For other uses, see [WWII (disambiguation)](/wiki/WWII_(disambiguation)), [The Second World War (disambiguation)](/wiki/The_Second_World_War_(disambiguation)), and [World War II (disambiguation)](/wiki/World_War_II_(disambiguation)).

**World War II**, or the **Second World War** (1 September 1939 – 2 September 1945), was a [global conflict](/wiki/World_war) between two [coalitions](/wiki/Coalition): the [Allies](/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) and the [Axis powers](/wiki/Axis_powers). [Nearly all of the world's countries](/wiki/World_War_II_by_country) participated. [Tanks](/wiki/Tanks_in_World_War_II) and [aircraft played major roles](/wiki/Air_warfare_of_World_War_II), the latter enabling the [strategic bombing](/wiki/Strategic_bombing) of cities and delivery of the only [nuclear weapons](/wiki/Nuclear_weapon) used in war. World War II was the [deadliest conflict](/wiki/List_of_wars_by_death_toll) in history, causing the death of 60 to 75 million people. Millions died as a result of massacres, starvation, disease, and [genocides](/wiki/Genocide), including [the Holocaust](/wiki/The_Holocaust). After the Allied victory, [Germany](/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany), [Austria](/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria), [Japan](/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan), and [Korea](/wiki/Division_of_Korea#Post–World_War_II) were occupied, and [German](/wiki/Nuremberg_trials) and [Japanese](/wiki/International_Military_Tribunal_for_the_Far_East) leaders were tried for [war crimes](/wiki/War_crime).

The [causes of World War II](/wiki/Causes_of_World_War_II) included unresolved tensions in the [aftermath of World War I](/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_I) and the rise of [fascism in Europe](/wiki/Fascism_in_Europe) and [militarism in Japan](/wiki/Japanese_militarism). Key events preceding the war included [Japan's invasion of Manchuria](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria) in 1931, the [Spanish Civil War](/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), the outbreak of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) in 1937, and Germany's [annexations of Austria](/wiki/Anschluss) and [the Sudetenland](/wiki/Munich_Agreement). World War II is generally considered to have begun on 1 September 1939, when [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany), under [Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler), [invaded Poland](/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), after which the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939)) and [France declared war](/wiki/French_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939)) on Germany. Poland was also invaded by the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union) in mid-September and was partitioned between Germany and the Soviet Union under the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact). In 1940, the Soviet Union [annexed the Baltic states](/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Baltic_states) and [parts of Finland](/wiki/Winter_War) and [Romania](/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina), while Germany [conquered Norway](/wiki/Norwegian_campaign), [Denmark](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_(1940)), [Belgium](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_(1940)), [Luxembourg](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Luxembourg), and the [Netherlands](/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands). After the [fall of France](/wiki/Battle_of_France) in June 1940, the war continued mainly between Germany, now assisted by [Fascist Italy](/wiki/Fascist_Italy), and the [British Empire](/wiki/British_Empire) and [British Commonwealth](/wiki/British_Commonwealth), with [fighting in the Balkans](/wiki/Balkans_campaign_(World_War_II)), [Mediterranean and Middle East](/wiki/Mediterranean_and_Middle_East_theatre_of_World_War_II), [East Africa](/wiki/East_African_campaign_(World_War_II)), the aerial [Battle of Britain](/wiki/Battle_of_Britain) and [the Blitz](/wiki/The_Blitz), and the naval [Battle of the Atlantic](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic). By mid-1941, [Yugoslavia](/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia) and [Greece](/wiki/Axis_occupation_of_Greece) had also been defeated by [Axis](/wiki/Axis_powers) countries. In June 1941, Germany [invaded the Soviet Union](/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), opening the [Eastern Front](/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II)).

In December 1941, Japan attacked American and British territories [in Asia and the Pacific](/wiki/Pacific_War#Japanese_offensives,_1941–1942), including [Pearl Harbor in Hawaii](/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), leading the [United States](/wiki/United_States) to enter the war against the Axis. Japan conquered much of coastal China and [Southeast Asia](/wiki/Southeast_Asia), but its advances in the Pacific were halted in June 1942 at the [Battle of Midway](/wiki/Battle_of_Midway). In early 1943, Axis forces were defeated in [North Africa](/wiki/North_African_campaign) and at [Stalingrad](/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad) in the Soviet Union. An Allied invasion of Italy in July resulted in the [fall of its fascist regime](/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy), and Allied offensives in the Pacific and the Soviet Union forced the Axis to retreat on all fronts. In 1944, the Western Allies [invaded France at Normandy](/wiki/Normandy_landings), and the Soviet Union advanced into Central Europe. Japan also suffered major setbacks including the crippling of its navy by the United States, the loss of key Western Pacific islands, and defeats in [Burma](/wiki/Burma_campaign).

The war in Europe concluded with the liberation of [German-occupied territories](/wiki/German-occupied_Europe) and the invasion of Germany by the Allies, which culminated in the [fall of Berlin](/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin) to Soviet troops and [Germany's unconditional surrender](/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender) on [8 May 1945](/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day). On 6 and 9 August, the US [dropped atomic bombs](/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) on [Hiroshima and Nagasaki](/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) followed by a [Soviet invasion of Japanese-occupied Manchuria](/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Manchuria). Japan announced its [unconditional surrender](/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) on 15 August and signed [a surrender document](/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender) on [2 September 1945](/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day). World War II transformed the political, economic, and social structures of the world, and established the foundation of international relations for the rest of the 20th century and into the 21st century. The [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations) was created to foster international cooperation and prevent future conflicts, with the victorious great powers—China, France, the Soviet Union, the UK, and the US—becoming the [permanent members](/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) of its [Security Council](/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council). The Soviet Union and the US emerged as rival [superpowers](/wiki/Superpower), setting the stage for the [Cold War](/wiki/Cold_War). In the wake of Europe's devastation, the influence of its great powers waned, triggering the [decolonisation of Africa](/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa) and [of Asia](/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia). Many countries whose industries had been damaged moved towards [economic recovery and expansion](/wiki/Post%E2%80%93World_War_II_economic_expansion).

## Start and end dates

See also: [List of timelines of World War II](/wiki/List_of_timelines_of_World_War_II)

Most historians agree that World War II began with the [German invasion of Poland](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Poland) on 1 September 1939 and the [British](/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939)) and [French](/wiki/French_declaration_of_war_on_Germany_(1939)) declarations of war on Germany two days later. Dates for the beginning of the [Pacific War](/wiki/Pacific_War) include the start of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) on 7 July 1937, or the earlier [Japanese invasion of Manchuria](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria) on 18 September 1931. Other proposed starting dates include the [Italian invasion of Abyssinia](/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War) on 3 October 1935. The British historian [Antony Beevor](/wiki/Antony_Beevor) views the beginning of World War II as the [Battles of Khalkhin Gol](/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol) fought between [Japan](/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) and the forces of [Mongolia](/wiki/Mongolian_People%27s_Republic) and the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union) from May to September 1939. Others view the [Spanish Civil War](/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) as the start or prelude to World War II.

The date of the war's end is also not universally agreed upon. It was generally accepted at the time that the war ended with the [armistice](/wiki/Armistice) of 15 August 1945 ([V-J Day](/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day)), rather than with the formal [surrender of Japan](/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) on 2 September 1945, which officially [ended the war in Asia](/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Asia). A [peace treaty between Japan and the Allies](/wiki/Treaty_of_San_Francisco) was signed in 1951. A 1990 [treaty regarding Germany's future](/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Final_Settlement_with_Respect_to_Germany) allowed the [reunification of East and West Germany](/wiki/German_reunification) to take place. No formal peace treaty between Japan and the Soviet Union was ever signed, although the state of war between the two countries was terminated by the [Soviet–Japanese Joint Declaration of 1956](/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_Joint_Declaration_of_1956), which also restored full diplomatic relations between them.

## Background

Main article: [Causes of World War II](/wiki/Causes_of_World_War_II)

### Aftermath of World War I

The [League of Nations](/wiki/League_of_Nations) assembly, held in [Geneva](/wiki/Geneva), Switzerland (1930)

[World War I](/wiki/World_War_I) radically altered the European political map. The most prominent nations of the [Central Powers](/wiki/Central_Powers) each lost territory in their respective peace treaties at the conclusion of the conflict. New [nation-states](/wiki/Nation_state) were created out of the dissolution of the [Austro-Hungarian](/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [Ottoman](/wiki/Ottoman_Empire), and [Russian Empires](/wiki/Russian_Empire).

To prevent a future world war, the [League of Nations](/wiki/League_of_Nations) was established in 1920 by the [Paris Peace Conference](/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference_(1919%E2%80%931920)). The organisation's primary function was to prevent armed conflict through collective security, military, and [naval disarmament](/wiki/Washington_Naval_Treaty), as well as settling international disputes through peaceful negotiations and arbitration.

Despite strong pacifist sentiment [after World War I](/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_I), [irredentist](/wiki/Irredentism) and [revanchist](/wiki/Revanchism) [nationalism](/wiki/Nationalism) had emerged in several European states. These sentiments were especially pronounced in Germany due to the significant territorial, colonial, and financial losses imposed by the [Treaty of Versailles](/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles). Under the treaty, Germany lost around 13 percent of its home territory and all [its overseas possessions](/wiki/German_colonial_empire), while German annexation of other states was prohibited, [reparations](/wiki/World_War_I_reparations) were imposed, and limits were placed on the size and capability of the country's [armed forces](/wiki/Reichswehr).

### Germany and Italy

The [German Empire](/wiki/German_Empire) was dissolved in the [German revolution of 1918–1919](/wiki/German_revolution_of_1918%E2%80%931919), and a democratic government, later known as the [Weimar Republic](/wiki/Weimar_Republic), was created. The [interwar period](/wiki/Interwar_period) saw strife between supporters of the new republic and hardline opponents on both the political right and left. Italy, as an Entente ally, had made some post-war territorial gains; however, Italian nationalists were angered that the [promises made](/wiki/Treaty_of_London_(1915)) by the United Kingdom and France to secure Italian entrance into the war were not fulfilled in the peace settlement. From 1922 to 1925, the [fascist](/wiki/Italian_fascism) movement led by [Benito Mussolini](/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) seized power in Italy with a nationalist, [totalitarian](/wiki/Totalitarianism), and [class collaborationist](/wiki/Class_collaboration) agenda that abolished [representative democracy](/wiki/Representative_democracy), repressed socialist, left-wing, and liberal forces, and pursued an aggressive expansionist foreign policy aimed at making Italy a world power, promising the creation of a "New Roman Empire".

[Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) at a German [Nazi](/wiki/Nazism) political rally in [Nuremberg](/wiki/Nuremberg), August 1933

[Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler), after an [unsuccessful attempt to overthrow the German government](/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch) in 1923, eventually [became the chancellor of Germany](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler%27s_rise_to_power) in 1933 when President [Paul von Hindenburg](/wiki/Paul_von_Hindenburg) and the [Reichstag](/wiki/Reichstag_(Weimar_Republic)) appointed him. The Nazis soon abolished parliamentary democracy, espousing a [radical, racially motivated revision of the world order](/wiki/New_Order_(Nazism)), and began a massive [rearmament campaign](/wiki/German_rearmament). Following Hindenburg's death in 1934, Hitler proclaimed himself *Führer* of Germany. France, seeking to secure its alliance with Italy, [allowed Italy a free hand in Ethiopia](/wiki/Franco-Italian_Agreement_of_1935), which Italy desired as a colonial possession. The situation was aggravated in early 1935 when the [Territory of the Saar Basin](/wiki/Territory_of_the_Saar_Basin) was legally reunited with Germany, and Hitler repudiated the [Treaty of Versailles](/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), accelerated his rearmament programme, and introduced [conscription](/wiki/Conscription).

### European treaties

The United Kingdom, France and Italy formed the [Stresa Front](/wiki/Stresa_Front) in April 1935 in order to contain Germany, a key step towards [military globalisation](/wiki/Military_globalization); however, that June, the United Kingdom made an [independent naval agreement](/wiki/Anglo-German_Naval_Agreement) with Germany, easing prior restrictions. The Soviet Union, concerned by Germany's [goals of capturing vast areas of Eastern Europe](/wiki/Drang_nach_Osten), drafted a treaty of mutual assistance with France. Before taking effect, though, the [Franco-Soviet pact](/wiki/Franco-Soviet_Treaty_of_Mutual_Assistance) was required to go through the bureaucracy of the League of Nations, which rendered it essentially toothless. The United States, concerned with events in Europe and Asia, passed the [Neutrality Act](/wiki/Neutrality_Acts_of_the_1930s) in August of the same year.

Hitler defied the Versailles and [Locarno Treaties](/wiki/Locarno_Treaties) by [remilitarising the Rhineland](/wiki/Remilitarisation_of_the_Rhineland) in March 1936, encountering little opposition due to the policy of [appeasement](/wiki/Appeasement). In October 1936, Germany and Italy formed the [Rome–Berlin Axis](/wiki/Axis_powers). A month later, Germany and Japan signed the [Anti-Comintern Pact](/wiki/Anti-Comintern_Pact), which Italy joined the following year.

### Asia

The [Kuomintang](/wiki/Kuomintang) party in China launched a [unification campaign](/wiki/Northern_Expedition) against [regional warlords](/wiki/Warlord_Era) and nominally unified China in the mid-1920s, but was soon embroiled in [a civil war](/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) against its former [Chinese Communist Party](/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party) (CCP) allies and [new regional warlords](/wiki/Central_Plains_War). In 1931, an [increasingly militaristic](/wiki/Statism_in_Sh%C5%8Dwa_Japan) [Empire of Japan](/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), which had long sought influence in China as the first step of what its government saw as the country's [right to rule Asia](/wiki/Hakk%C5%8D_ichiu), staged the [Mukden incident](/wiki/Mukden_incident) as a pretext to [invade Manchuria](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria) and establish the [puppet state](/wiki/Puppet_state) of [Manchukuo](/wiki/Manchukuo).

China appealed to the [League of Nations](/wiki/League_of_Nations) to stop the Japanese invasion of Manchuria. Japan withdrew from the League of Nations after being [condemned](/wiki/Lytton_Report) for its incursion into Manchuria. The two nations then fought several battles, in [Shanghai](/wiki/January_28_incident), [Rehe](/wiki/Battle_of_Rehe), and [Hebei](/wiki/Defense_of_the_Great_Wall), until the [Tanggu Truce](/wiki/Tanggu_Truce) was signed in 1933. Thereafter, Chinese volunteer forces continued the resistance to Japanese aggression in [Manchuria](/wiki/Pacification_of_Manchukuo), and [Chahar and Suiyuan](/wiki/Actions_in_Inner_Mongolia_(1933%E2%80%931936)). After the 1936 [Xi'an Incident](/wiki/Xi%27an_Incident), the Kuomintang and [CCP](/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party) forces agreed on a ceasefire to present [a united front](/wiki/Second_United_Front) to oppose Japan.

## Pre-war events

### Italian invasion of Ethiopia (1935)

Main article: [Second Italo-Ethiopian War](/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War)

[Benito Mussolini](/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) inspecting troops during the [Italo-Ethiopian War](/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War), 1935

The [Second Italo-Ethiopian War](/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War) was a [colonial war](/wiki/Colonial_war) that began in October 1935 and ended in May 1936. The war began with the invasion of the [Ethiopian Empire](/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire) (also known as [Abyssinia](/wiki/Etymology_of_Ethiopia)) by the armed forces of the [Kingdom of Italy](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) (*Regno d'Italia*), which was launched from [Italian Somaliland](/wiki/Italian_Somaliland) and [Eritrea](/wiki/Italian_Eritrea). The war resulted in the [military occupation](/wiki/Military_occupation) of Ethiopia and its [annexation](/wiki/Annexation) into the newly created colony of [Italian East Africa](/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) (*Africa Orientale Italiana*); in addition it exposed the weakness of the [League of Nations](/wiki/League_of_Nations) as a force to preserve peace. Both Italy and Ethiopia were member nations, [but the League did little](/wiki/Appeasement) when the former clearly violated Article X of the League's [Covenant](/wiki/Covenant_of_the_League_of_Nations). The United Kingdom and France supported imposing sanctions on Italy for the invasion, although the sanctions were not fully enforced and failed to end the Italian invasion. Italy subsequently dropped its objections to Germany's goal of absorbing [Austria](/wiki/Federal_State_of_Austria).

### Spanish Civil War (1936–1939)

Main article: [Spanish Civil War](/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War)

When civil war broke out in Spain, Hitler and Mussolini lent military support to the [Nationalist rebels](/wiki/Nationalist_faction_(Spanish_Civil_War)), led by General [Francisco Franco](/wiki/Francisco_Franco). Italy supported the Nationalists to a greater extent than the Nazis: Mussolini sent more than 70,000 ground troops, 6,000 aviation personnel, and 720 aircraft to Spain. The Soviet Union supported the existing government of the [Spanish Republic](/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic). More than 30,000 foreign volunteers, known as the [International Brigades](/wiki/International_Brigades), also fought against the Nationalists. Both Germany and the Soviet Union used this [proxy war](/wiki/Proxy_war) as an opportunity to test in combat their most advanced weapons and tactics. The Nationalists won the civil war in April 1939; Franco, now dictator, remained officially neutral during World War II but [generally favoured the Axis](/wiki/Spain_during_World_War_II). His largest collaboration with Germany was the sending of [volunteers](/wiki/Blue_Division) to fight on the [Eastern Front](/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II)).

### Japanese invasion of China (1937)

Main article: [Second Sino-Japanese War](/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War)

[Chinese soldiers](/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Army) battle the [Imperial Japanese Army](/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) in [Taierzhuang](/wiki/Taierzhuang), 1938

In July 1937, Japan captured the former Chinese imperial capital of [Peking](/wiki/Beijing) after instigating the [Marco Polo Bridge incident](/wiki/Marco_Polo_Bridge_incident), which culminated in the Japanese campaign to invade all of China following years of [tension](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Manchuria) and [low-level](/wiki/January_28_incident) [conflicts](/wiki/Suiyuan_campaign). The Soviets quickly signed a [non-aggression pact with China](/wiki/Sino-Soviet_Non-Aggression_Pact) to lend [materiel](/wiki/Materiel) support, effectively ending China's [prior cooperation with Germany](/wiki/China%E2%80%93Germany_relations_(1912%E2%80%931949)).

From September to November, the Japanese attacked [Taiyuan](/wiki/Battle_of_Taiyuan), engaged the [Kuomintang Army](/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Army) [around Xinkou](/wiki/Battle_of_Xinkou), fought [Communist forces](/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party) [in Pingxingguan](/wiki/Battle_of_Pingxingguan),Yang Kuisong, "On the reconstruction of the facts of the Battle of Pingxingguan" and wrestled control over China's northern railway network. Nationalist [Generalissimo](/wiki/Generalissimo) [Chiang Kai-shek](/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) deployed his [best army](/wiki/List_of_German-trained_divisions_of_the_National_Revolutionary_Army) to [defend Shanghai](/wiki/Battle_of_Shanghai), but after three months of heavy fighting, Shanghai fell. The Japanese continued to push Chinese forces back, [capturing the capital Nanking](/wiki/Battle_of_Nanking) in December 1937.

In March 1938, Nationalist Chinese forces won their [first major victory at Taierzhuang](/wiki/Battle_of_Taierzhuang), but [ultimately lost control of the city](/wiki/Battle_of_Xuzhou) of [Xuzhou](/wiki/Xuzhou) in May. In June 1938, Chinese forces stalled the Japanese advance by [flooding the Yellow River](/wiki/1938_Yellow_River_flood); buying time for the Chinese to prepare their defences at [Wuhan](/wiki/Wuhan) at heavy cost to the local civilian population, but the [city was taken](/wiki/Battle_of_Wuhan) by October after heavy fighting along the Yangtze River.

Japanese military victories did not destroy Chinese resistance; instead, the Chinese government relocated inland to [Chongqing](/wiki/Chongqing) and continued the war. Aiming to break Chinese morale, Japanese aircraft began [striking cities in the Sichuan basin](/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing) in a bombing campaign, killing tens of thousands of civilians.

### Soviet–Japanese border conflicts

Main article: [Soviet–Japanese border conflicts](/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_border_conflicts)

In the mid-to-late 1930s, Japanese forces in [Manchukuo](/wiki/Manchukuo) had sporadic border clashes with the Soviet Union and [Mongolia](/wiki/Mongolian_People%27s_Republic). The Japanese doctrine of [Hokushin-ron](/wiki/Hokushin-ron), which emphasised Japan's expansion northward, was favoured by the Imperial Army during this time. This policy would prove difficult to maintain in light of the Japanese defeat at [Khalkin Gol](/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol) in 1939, the ongoing Second [Sino-Japanese War](/wiki/Sino-Japanese_War_(1931-1945)) and ally Nazi Germany pursuing neutrality with the Soviets. Japan and the Soviet Union eventually signed a [Neutrality Pact](/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_Neutrality_Pact) in April 1941, and Japan adopted the doctrine of [Nanshin-ron](/wiki/Nanshin-ron), promoted by the Navy, which took its focus southward and eventually led to war with the United States and the Western Allies.

### European occupations and agreements

From left to right: [Chamberlain](/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain), [Daladier](/wiki/%C3%89douard_Daladier), [Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler), [Mussolini](/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), and [Ciano](/wiki/Galeazzo_Ciano) pictured just before signing the [Munich Agreement](/wiki/Munich_Agreement), 29 September 1938

In Europe, Germany and Italy were becoming more aggressive. In March 1938, Germany [annexed Austria](/wiki/Anschluss), again provoking [little response](/wiki/Appeasement) from other European powers. Encouraged, Hitler began pressing German claims on the [Sudetenland](/wiki/Sudetenland), an area of [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia) with a predominantly [ethnic German](/wiki/Germans) population. Soon the United Kingdom and France followed the appeasement policy of British Prime Minister [Neville Chamberlain](/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain) and conceded this territory to Germany in the [Munich Agreement](/wiki/Munich_Agreement), which was made against the wishes of the Czechoslovak government, in exchange for a promise of no further territorial demands. Soon afterwards, Germany and Italy forced Czechoslovakia to [cede additional territory](/wiki/First_Vienna_Award) to Hungary, and Poland annexed the [Trans-Olza](/wiki/Trans-Olza) region of Czechoslovakia.

Although all of Germany's stated demands had been satisfied by the agreement, privately Hitler was furious that British interference had prevented him from seizing all of Czechoslovakia in one operation. In subsequent speeches Hitler attacked British and Jewish "war-mongers" and in January 1939 [secretly ordered a major build-up of the German navy](/wiki/Plan_Z) to challenge British naval supremacy. In March 1939, [Germany invaded the remainder of Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Occupation_of_Czechoslovakia_(1938%E2%80%931945)) and subsequently split it into the German [Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia](/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia) and a pro-German [client state](/wiki/Client_state), the [Slovak Republic](/wiki/Slovak_Republic_(1939%E2%80%931945)). Hitler also delivered an [ultimatum to Lithuania](/wiki/1939_German_ultimatum_to_Lithuania) on 20 March 1939, forcing the concession of the [Klaipėda Region](/wiki/Klaip%C4%97da_Region), formerly the German *Memelland*.

Following further demands from Hitler regarding the [Free City of Danzig](/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig) and the [Polish corridor](/wiki/Polish_corridor), the United Kingdom and France [guaranteed their support for Polish independence](/wiki/Anglo-Polish_alliance#British_assurance_to_Poland). When [Italy conquered Albania](/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_Albania) in April 1939, the same guarantee was extended to the [Kingdoms of Romania](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) and [Greece](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece). Shortly after the [Franco](/wiki/Franco-Polish_alliance)-[British](/wiki/Anglo-Polish_alliance) pledge to Poland, Germany and Italy formalised their own alliance with the [Pact of Steel](/wiki/Pact_of_Steel). Hitler accused the United Kingdom and Poland of trying to encircle Germany and renounced the [Anglo-German Naval Agreement](/wiki/Anglo-German_Naval_Agreement) and the [German–Polish declaration of non-aggression](/wiki/German%E2%80%93Polish_declaration_of_non-aggression).

German Foreign Minister [Joachim von Ribbentrop](/wiki/Joachim_von_Ribbentrop) (right) and the Soviet leader [Joseph Stalin](/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) after signing the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact), 23 August 1939

The situation became a crisis in late August as Germany concentrated its forces near the Polish border. On 23 August, the Soviet Union signed [a non-aggression pact](/wiki/Molotov%E2%80%93Ribbentrop_Pact) with Germany, after tripartite negotiations for a military alliance between France, the United Kingdom, and Soviet Union had stalled. This pact had a secret protocol that defined German and Soviet spheres of influence: Lithuania for Germany; Finland, [Estonia](/wiki/Estonia), [Latvia](/wiki/Latvia) and [Bessarabia](/wiki/Bessarabia) for the Soviet Union; and Poland to be partitioned between the two powers. The pact ensured that Germany would not face a war with the Soviet Union when it invaded Poland. Immediately afterwards, Hitler ordered the attack to proceed on 26 August, but upon hearing that the United Kingdom had concluded a formal mutual assistance pact with Poland and that Italy would maintain neutrality, he decided to delay it.

Germany offered Britain an alliance if Britain helped Germany gain Danzig, the Polish corridor and its former colonies, but Britain replied that it would honour its guarantee to Poland. On 29 August, Hitler demanded that a Polish [plenipotentiary](/wiki/Plenipotentiary) travel to Berlin by the following day to negotiate a solution to the crisis, but Britain rejected the timeline as unreasonable. On the night of 30–31 August, the British ambassador [Nevile Henderson](/wiki/Nevile_Henderson) met German Foreign Minister [Joachim von Ribbentrop](/wiki/Joachim_von_Ribbentrop). Ribbentrop handed him Hitler's demands regarding Poland then announced that the deadline for Poland's acceptance had already passed.

## Course of the war

For a chronological guide, see [List of timelines of World War II](/wiki/List_of_timelines_of_World_War_II).

See also: [Diplomatic history of World War II](/wiki/Diplomatic_history_of_World_War_II) and [World War II by country](/wiki/World_War_II_by_country)

### War breaks out in Europe (1939–1940)

Main article: [European theatre of World War II](/wiki/European_theatre_of_World_War_II)

A [German propaganda](/wiki/Propaganda_in_Nazi_Germany) photograph reenacting the removal of the Polish border crossing in [Sopot](/wiki/Sopot)

On 1 September 1939, Germany [invaded Poland](/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) after [having staged](/wiki/Operation_Himmler) several [false flag border incidents](/wiki/Gleiwitz_incident) as a pretext to initiate the invasion. The first German attack of the war came [against the Polish defences at Westerplatte](/wiki/Battle_of_Westerplatte). The United Kingdom responded with an [ultimatum](/wiki/Ultimatum) for Germany to cease military operations, and on 3 September, after the ultimatum was ignored, Britain and France declared war on Germany. During the [Phoney War](/wiki/Phoney_War) period, the alliance provided no direct military support to Poland, outside of a [cautious French probe into the Saarland](/wiki/Saar_Offensive). The Western Allies also began a [naval blockade of Germany](/wiki/Blockade_of_Germany_(1939%E2%80%931945)), which aimed to damage the country's economy and war effort. Germany responded by ordering [U-boat warfare](/wiki/Submarine_warfare#World_War_II) against Allied merchant and warships, which would later escalate into the [Battle of the Atlantic](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic).
On 8 September, German troops reached the suburbs of [Warsaw](/wiki/Warsaw). The [Polish counter-offensive](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Bzura) to the west halted the German advance for several days, but it was outflanked and encircled by the *[Wehrmacht](/wiki/Wehrmacht)*. Remnants of the Polish army broke through to [besieged Warsaw](/wiki/Siege_of_Warsaw_(1939)). On 17 September 1939, two days after signing a [cease-fire with Japan](/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol), the [Soviet Union invaded Poland](/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland) under the supposed pretext that the Polish state had ceased to exist. On 27 September, the Warsaw garrison surrendered to the Germans, and [the last large operational unit of the Polish Army](/wiki/Independent_Operational_Group_Polesie) [surrendered on 6 October](/wiki/Battle_of_Kock_(1939)). Despite the military defeat, Poland never surrendered; instead, it formed the [Polish government-in-exile](/wiki/Polish_government-in-exile) and a [clandestine state apparatus remained](/wiki/Polish_Underground_State) in occupied Poland. A significant part of Polish military personnel [evacuated to Romania](/wiki/Romanian_Bridgehead) and Latvia; many of them later [fought against the Axis](/wiki/Military_history_of_Poland_during_World_War_II) in other theatres of the war.

Germany [annexed western Poland](/wiki/Polish_areas_annexed_by_Nazi_Germany) and [occupied central Poland](/wiki/General_Government); the Soviet Union [annexed eastern Poland](/wiki/Territories_of_Poland_annexed_by_the_Soviet_Union). Small shares of Polish territory were transferred to [Lithuania](/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_Poland#World_War_II) and [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovak_invasion_of_Poland). On 6 October, Hitler made a public peace overture to the United Kingdom and France but said that the future of Poland was to be determined exclusively by Germany and the Soviet Union. The proposal was rejected and Hitler ordered an immediate offensive against France, which was postponed until the spring of 1940 due to bad weather.

[Mannerheim Line](/wiki/Mannerheim_Line) and [Karelian Isthmus](/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus) on the last day of the [Winter War](/wiki/Winter_War), 13 March 1940

After the outbreak of war in Poland, Stalin threatened [Estonia](/wiki/Estonia), [Latvia](/wiki/Latvia), and [Lithuania](/wiki/Lithuania) with military invasion, forcing the three [Baltic countries](/wiki/Baltic_states) to sign [pacts](/wiki/Background_of_the_occupation_of_the_Baltic_states#Soviet_ultimatums_and_occupation) allowing the creation of Soviet military bases in these countries; in October 1939, significant Soviet military contingents were moved there. [Finland](/wiki/Finland) refused to sign a similar pact and rejected ceding part of its territory to the Soviet Union. [The Soviet Union invaded Finland](/wiki/Winter_War#Soviet_invasion) in November 1939, and was subsequently expelled from the [League of Nations](/wiki/League_of_Nations) for this crime of aggression. Despite overwhelming numerical superiority, Soviet military success during the [Winter War](/wiki/Winter_War) was modest, and the Finno–Soviet war ended in March 1940 with [some Finnish concessions of territory](/wiki/Moscow_Peace_Treaty).

In June 1940, the Soviet Union [occupied](/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_the_Baltic_states_(1940)) the entire territories of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, as well as the Romanian regions of [Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina, and the Hertsa region](/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina). In August 1940, Hitler imposed the [Second Vienna Award](/wiki/Second_Vienna_Award) on Romania which led to the transfer of [Northern Transylvania](/wiki/Northern_Transylvania) to Hungary. In September 1940, Bulgaria demanded [Southern Dobruja](/wiki/Southern_Dobruja) from Romania with German and Italian support, leading to the [Treaty of Craiova](/wiki/Treaty_of_Craiova). The loss of one-third of Romania's 1939 territory caused a coup against [King Carol II](/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania), turning Romania into a fascist dictatorship under Marshal [Ion Antonescu](/wiki/Ion_Antonescu), with a course set towards the Axis in the hopes of a German guarantee. Meanwhile, German–Soviet political relations and economic co-operation gradually stalled, and both states began preparations for war.

### Western Europe (1940–1941)

Main article: [Western Front (World War II)](/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_II))

German advance into Belgium and Northern France, 10 May – 4 June 1940, sweeping past the [Maginot Line](/wiki/Maginot_Line) (shown in dark red)

In April 1940, [Germany invaded Denmark and Norway](/wiki/Operation_Weser%C3%BCbung) to protect shipments of [iron ore from Sweden](/wiki/Swedish_iron-ore_industry_during_World_War_II), which the Allies were [attempting to cut off](/wiki/Operation_Wilfred). [Denmark capitulated after six hours](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Denmark_(1940)), and [despite Allied support](/wiki/Norwegian_campaign), Norway was conquered within two months. [British discontent over the Norwegian campaign](/wiki/Norway_Debate) led to the resignation of Prime Minister [Neville Chamberlain](/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain), who was replaced by [Winston Churchill](/wiki/Winston_Churchill) on 10 May 1940.

On the same day, Germany [launched an offensive against France](/wiki/Battle_of_France). To circumvent the strong [Maginot Line](/wiki/Maginot_Line) fortifications on the Franco-German border, Germany directed its attack at the neutral nations of [Belgium](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Belgium_(1940)), [the Netherlands](/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands), and [Luxembourg](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Luxembourg). The Germans carried out a flanking manoeuvre through the [Ardennes](/wiki/Ardennes) region, which was mistakenly perceived by the Allies as an impenetrable natural barrier against armoured vehicles. By successfully implementing new *[Blitzkrieg](/wiki/Blitzkrieg)* tactics, the *Wehrmacht* rapidly advanced to the Channel and cut off the Allied forces in Belgium, trapping the bulk of the Allied armies in a cauldron on the Franco-Belgian border near Lille. The United Kingdom was able [to evacuate a significant number of Allied troops](/wiki/Dunkirk_evacuation) from the continent by early June, although they had to abandon almost all their equipment.

On 10 June, [Italy invaded France](/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_France), declaring war on both France and the United Kingdom. The Germans turned south against the weakened French army, and [Paris](/wiki/Paris_in_World_War_II) fell to them on 14 June. Eight days later [France signed an armistice with Germany](/wiki/Armistice_of_22_June_1940); it was divided into [German](/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II) and [Italian occupation zones](/wiki/Italian_occupation_of_France), and an unoccupied [rump state](/wiki/Rump_state) under the [Vichy Regime](/wiki/Vichy_France), which, though officially neutral, was generally aligned with Germany. France kept its fleet, which [the United Kingdom attacked](/wiki/Attack_on_Mers-el-K%C3%A9bir) on 3 July in an attempt to prevent its seizure by Germany.

London's [St Paul's Cathedral](/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cathedral) after the German [Blitz](/wiki/The_Blitz) on 29 December 1940

The air [Battle of Britain](/wiki/Battle_of_Britain) began in early July with [Luftwaffe attacks on shipping and harbours](/wiki/Kanalkampf). The [German campaign for air superiority](/wiki/Adlertag) started in August but its failure to defeat [RAF Fighter Command](/wiki/RAF_Fighter_Command) forced the indefinite postponement of the [proposed German invasion of Britain](/wiki/Operation_Sea_Lion). The German [strategic bombing](/wiki/Strategic_bombing) offensive intensified with night attacks on London and other cities in [the Blitz](/wiki/The_Blitz), but largely ended in May 1941 after failing to significantly disrupt the British war effort.

Using newly captured French ports, the German Navy [enjoyed success](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic#'The_Happy_Time'_(June_1940_–_February_1941)) against an over-extended [Royal Navy](/wiki/Royal_Navy), using [U-boats](/wiki/U-boat) against British shipping [in the Atlantic](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Atlantic). The British [Home Fleet](/wiki/Home_Fleet) scored a significant victory on 27 May 1941 by [sinking the German battleship *Bismarck*](/wiki/Last_battle_of_Bismarck).

In November 1939, the United States was assisting China and the Western Allies, and had amended the [Neutrality Act](/wiki/Neutrality_Acts_of_the_1930s) to allow "[cash and carry](/wiki/Cash_and_carry_(World_War_II))" purchases by the Allies. In 1940, following the German capture of Paris, the size of the [United States Navy](/wiki/United_States_Navy) was [significantly increased](/wiki/Two-Ocean_Navy_Act). In September the United States further agreed to a [trade of American destroyers for British bases](/wiki/Destroyers-for-bases_deal). Still, a large majority of the American public continued to oppose any direct military intervention in the conflict well into 1941. In December 1940, President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) accused Hitler of planning world conquest and ruled out any negotiations as useless, calling for the United States to become an "[arsenal of democracy](/wiki/Arsenal_of_Democracy)" and promoting [Lend-Lease](/wiki/Lend-Lease) programmes of military and humanitarian aid to support the British war effort; Lend-Lease was later extended to the other Allies, including the Soviet Union after it was [invaded](/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) by Germany. The United States started strategic planning to prepare for a full-scale offensive against Germany.

At the end of September 1940, the [Tripartite Pact](/wiki/Tripartite_Pact) formally united Japan, Italy, and Germany as the [Axis powers](/wiki/Axis_powers). The Tripartite Pact stipulated that any country—with the exception of the Soviet Union—that attacked any Axis Power would be forced to go to war against all three. The Axis expanded in November 1940 when [Hungary](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_(1920%E2%80%931946)), [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovak_Republic_(1939%E2%80%931945)), and [Romania](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) joined. [Romania](/wiki/Romania_in_World_War_II) and [Hungary](/wiki/Hungary_in_World_War_II) later made major contributions to the Axis war against the Soviet Union, in Romania's case partially to recapture [territory ceded to the Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina).

### Mediterranean (1940–1941)

Main article: [Mediterranean and Middle East theatre of World War II](/wiki/Mediterranean_and_Middle_East_theatre_of_World_War_II)

In early June 1940, the Italian *[Regia Aeronautica](/wiki/Regia_Aeronautica)* [attacked and besieged Malta](/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_(World_War_II)), a British possession. From late summer to early autumn, Italy [conquered British Somaliland](/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_British_Somaliland) and made an [incursion into British-held Egypt](/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_Egypt). In October, [Italy attacked Greece](/wiki/Greco-Italian_War), but the attack was repulsed with heavy Italian casualties; the campaign ended within months with minor territorial changes. To assist Italy and prevent Britain from gaining a foothold, Germany prepared to invade the Balkans, which would threaten Romanian oil fields and strike against British dominance of the Mediterranean.

German [Panzer III](/wiki/Panzer_III) of the [Afrika Korps](/wiki/Afrika_Korps) advancing across the North African desert, April 1941

In December 1940, British Empire forces began [counter-offensives](/wiki/Operation_Compass) against Italian forces in Egypt and [Italian East Africa](/wiki/East_African_campaign_(World_War_II)). The offensives were successful; by early February 1941, Italy had lost control of eastern Libya, and large numbers of Italian troops had been taken prisoner. The [Italian Navy](/wiki/Regia_Marina) also suffered significant defeats, with the Royal Navy putting three Italian battleships out of commission after a [carrier attack at Taranto](/wiki/Battle_of_Taranto), and neutralising several more warships at the [Battle of Cape Matapan](/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Matapan).

Italian defeats prompted Germany to [deploy an expeditionary force](/wiki/Operation_Sonnenblume) to North Africa; at the end of March 1941, [Erwin Rommel](/wiki/Erwin_Rommel)'s [Afrika Korps](/wiki/Afrika_Korps) [launched an offensive](/wiki/Western_Desert_campaign) which drove back Commonwealth forces. In less than a month, Axis forces advanced to western Egypt and [besieged the port of Tobruk](/wiki/Siege_of_Tobruk).

By late March 1941, [Bulgaria](/wiki/Tsardom_of_Bulgaria_(1908%E2%80%931946)) and [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Yugoslavia) signed the [Tripartite Pact](/wiki/Tripartite_Pact); however, the Yugoslav government was [overthrown two days later](/wiki/Yugoslav_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat) by pro-British nationalists. Germany and Italy responded with simultaneous invasions of both [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Invasion_of_Yugoslavia) and [Greece](/wiki/German_invasion_of_Greece), commencing on 6 April 1941 with a [massive bombing of Belgrade](/wiki/German_bombing_of_Belgrade); both nations were forced to surrender within the month. The airborne [invasion of the Greek island of Crete](/wiki/Battle_of_Crete) at the end of May completed the German conquest of the Balkans. Partisan warfare subsequently broke out against the [Axis occupation of Yugoslavia](/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia), which continued until the end of the war.

In the Middle East in May, Commonwealth forces [quashed an uprising in Iraq](/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War) which had been supported by German aircraft from bases within Vichy-controlled [Syria](/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon). Between June and July, British-led forces [invaded and occupied the French possessions of Syria and Lebanon](/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign), assisted by the [Free French](/wiki/Free_France).

### Axis attack on the Soviet Union (1941)

Main article: [Eastern Front (World War II)](/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II))

[European theatre of World War II](/wiki/European_theatre_of_World_War_II) animation map, 1939–1945 – Red: [Western Allies](/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) and the Soviet Union after 1941; Green: [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union) before 1941; Blue: [Axis powers](/wiki/Axis_powers)

With the situation in Europe and Asia relatively stable, Germany, Japan, and the Soviet Union made preparations for war. With the Soviets wary of mounting tensions with Germany, and the Japanese planning to take advantage of the European War by seizing resource-rich European possessions in [Southeast Asia](/wiki/Southeast_Asia), the two powers signed the [Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact](/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_Neutrality_Pact) in April 1941. By contrast, the Germans were steadily making preparations for an attack on the Soviet Union, massing forces on the Soviet border.

Hitler believed that the United Kingdom's refusal to end the war was based on the hope that the United States and the Soviet Union would enter the war against Germany. On 31 July 1940, Hitler decided that the Soviet Union should be eliminated and aimed for the conquest of [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), the [Baltic states](/wiki/Baltic_states) and [Byelorussia](/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). However, other senior German officials like Ribbentrop saw an opportunity to create a Euro-Asian bloc against the British Empire by inviting the Soviet Union into the Tripartite Pact. In November 1940, [negotiations took place](/wiki/German%E2%80%93Soviet_Axis_talks) to determine if the Soviet Union would join the pact. The Soviets showed some interest but asked for concessions from Finland, Bulgaria, Turkey, and Japan that Germany considered unacceptable. On 18 December 1940, Hitler issued the directive to prepare for an invasion of the Soviet Union.
On 22 June 1941, Germany, supported by Italy and Romania, invaded the Soviet Union in [Operation Barbarossa](/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), with Germany accusing the Soviets of [plotting against them](/wiki/Soviet_offensive_plans_controversy); they were joined shortly by Finland and Hungary. The primary targets of this surprise offensive were the [Baltic region](/wiki/Baltic_region), Moscow and Ukraine, with the [ultimate goal](/wiki/Strategic_goal_(military)) of ending the 1941 campaign near the [Arkhangelsk–Astrakhan line](/wiki/A%E2%80%93A_line)—from the [Caspian](/wiki/Caspian_Sea) to the [White Seas](/wiki/White_Sea). Hitler's objectives were to eliminate the Soviet Union as a military power, exterminate [communism](/wiki/Communism), generate *[Lebensraum](/wiki/Lebensraum)* ("living space") by [dispossessing the native population](/wiki/Generalplan_Ost), and guarantee access to the strategic resources needed to defeat Germany's remaining rivals.

Members of the [Einsatzgruppen](/wiki/Einsatzgruppen) prepares to shoot a Jewish mother holding her child, [Ivangorod](/wiki/Ivanhorod), Ukraine

Although the [Red Army](/wiki/Red_Army) was preparing for strategic [counter-offensives](/wiki/Counter-offensive) before the war, Operation Barbarossa forced the [Soviet supreme command](/wiki/Stavka) to adopt [strategic defence](/wiki/Strategic_defence). During the summer, the Axis made significant gains into Soviet territory, inflicting immense losses in both personnel and materiel, mainly in massive [encirclements](/wiki/Encirclement) around [Minsk](/wiki/Battle_of_Bia%C5%82ystok%E2%80%93Minsk), [Smolensk](/wiki/Battle_of_Smolensk_(1941)), and [Uman](/wiki/Battle_of_Uman).

[Nazi policy](/wiki/Commissar_Order) entailed that Wehrmacht subject Soviet POWs to murderous treatment, executing all Jewish and Communist POWs immediately per the [Commissar Order](/wiki/Commissar_Order), and subjecting the remainder to forced marches to open-air concentration camps, where they were to be [deliberately starved to death](/wiki/German_atrocities_committed_against_Soviet_prisoners_of_war). By the end of the winter of 1941, 2.8 million Soviet POWs had died in German captivity. Some 3.3 million Soviet POWs would die in German captivity by the war's end in total, a nearly 60% mortality rate.

By mid-August, however, the German [Army High Command](/wiki/Oberkommando_des_Heeres) decided to [suspend the offensive](/wiki/Battle_of_Smolensk_(1941)) of a considerably depleted [Army Group Centre](/wiki/Army_Group_Centre), and to divert the [2nd Panzer Group](/wiki/2nd_Panzer_Army) to reinforce troops advancing towards central Ukraine and Leningrad. The [Kiev offensive](/wiki/Battle_of_Kiev_(1941)) was overwhelmingly successful, resulting in encirclement and elimination of four Soviet armies, and made possible further [advance into Crimea](/wiki/Crimean_campaign) and industrially-developed eastern Ukraine (the [First Battle of Kharkov](/wiki/First_Battle_of_Kharkov)).

The diversion of three-quarters of the Axis troops and the majority of their air forces from France and the central Mediterranean to the [Eastern Front](/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II)) prompted the United Kingdom to reconsider its [grand strategy](/wiki/Grand_strategy). In July, the UK and the Soviet Union formed a [military alliance against Germany](/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_Agreement) and in August, the United Kingdom and the United States jointly issued the [Atlantic Charter](/wiki/Atlantic_Charter), which outlined British and American goals for the post-war world. In late August the British and Soviets [invaded neutral Iran](/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran) to secure the [Persian Corridor](/wiki/Persian_Corridor), Iran's [oil fields](/wiki/Oil_fields), and preempt any Axis advances through Iran toward the Baku oil fields or India.

Russian civilians leaving destroyed houses after a German bombardment during the [siege of Leningrad](/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad) ([Saint Petersburg](/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)), 10 December 1942

By October, Axis powers had achieved [operational objectives](/wiki/Operational_objective) in Ukraine and the Baltic region, with only the sieges of [Leningrad](/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad) and [Sevastopol](/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_(1941%E2%80%931942)) continuing. A major [offensive against Moscow](/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow) was renewed; after two months of fierce battles in increasingly harsh weather, the German army almost reached the outer suburbs of Moscow, where the exhausted troops were forced to suspend the offensive. Large territorial gains were made by Axis forces, but their campaign had failed to achieve its main objectives: two key cities remained in Soviet hands, the Soviet [capability to resist](/wiki/Military_capability) was not broken, and the Soviet Union retained a considerable part of its military potential. The *blitzkrieg* [phase](/wiki/Phase_(combat)) of the war in Europe had ended.

By early December, freshly mobilised [reserves](/wiki/Military_reserve_force) allowed the Soviets to achieve numerical parity with Axis troops. This, as well as [intelligence data](/wiki/Richard_Sorge#Wartime_intelligence) which established that a minimal number of Soviet troops in the East would be sufficient to deter any attack by the Japanese [Kwantung Army](/wiki/Kwantung_Army), allowed the Soviets to begin a [massive counter-offensive](/wiki/Winter_campaign_of_1941%E2%80%931942) that started on 5 December all along the front and pushed German troops 100–250 kilometres (62–155 mi) west.

### War breaks out in the Pacific (1941)

Main article: [Pacific War](/wiki/Pacific_War)

Japanese soldiers [entering Hong Kong](/wiki/Battle_of_Hong_Kong), 8 December 1941

Following the Japanese [false flag](/wiki/False_flag) [Mukden incident](/wiki/Mukden_incident) in 1931, the Japanese shelling of the American [gunboat USS *Panay*](/wiki/USS_Panay_incident) in 1937, and the 1937–1938 [Nanjing Massacre](/wiki/Nanjing_Massacre), [Japanese-American relations deteriorated](/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93United_States_relations#1937–1941). In 1939, the United States notified Japan that it would not be extending its trade treaty and American public opinion opposing Japanese expansionism led to a series of economic sanctions—the [Export Control Acts](/wiki/Export_Control_Act)—which banned US exports of chemicals, minerals and military parts to Japan, and increased economic pressure on the Japanese regime. During 1939 Japan launched its [first attack against Changsha](/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1939)), but was repulsed by late September. Despite [several offensives](/wiki/1939%E2%80%931940_Winter_Offensive) by both sides, by 1940 the war between China and Japan was at a stalemate. To increase pressure on China by blocking supply routes, and to better position Japanese forces in the event of a war with the Western powers, Japan invaded and [occupied northern Indochina](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_French_Indochina) in September 1940.

Chinese nationalist forces launched a large-scale [counter-offensive](/wiki/1939%E2%80%931940_Winter_Offensive) in early 1940. In August, [Chinese communists](/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party) launched an [offensive in Central China](/wiki/Hundred_Regiments_Offensive); in retaliation, Japanese armies in North China implemented the [Three Alls Policy](/wiki/Three_Alls_policy), a massive scorched earth initiative to depopulate regions deemed hostile to Japanese occupation. Continued antipathy between Chinese communist and nationalist forces [culminated in armed clashes in January 1941](/wiki/New_Fourth_Army_incident), effectively ending their co-operation. In March, the Japanese 11th army attacked the headquarters of the nationalist Chinese 19th army but was repulsed during the [Battle of Shanggao](/wiki/Battle_of_Shanggao). In September, Japan attempted to [take the city of Changsha](/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1941)) again and clashed with Chinese nationalist forces.

German successes in Europe prompted Japan to increase pressure on European governments in [Southeast Asia](/wiki/Southeast_Asia). The Dutch government agreed to provide Japan with oil supplies from the [Dutch East Indies](/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies), but negotiations for additional access to their resources ended in failure in June 1941. In July 1941 Japan sent troops to southern Indochina, threatening British and Dutch possessions in the Far East. The United States, the United Kingdom, and other Western governments reacted to this move with a freeze on Japanese assets and a total oil [embargo](/wiki/Economic_sanctions). At the same time, Japan was [planning an invasion of the Soviet Far East](/wiki/Kantokuen), intending to take advantage of the German invasion in the west, but abandoned the operation after the sanctions.

Since early 1941, the United States and Japan had been engaged in negotiations in an attempt to improve their strained relations and end the war in China. Japan advanced a number of proposals which were dismissed by the Americans as inadequate. At the same time the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Netherlands engaged in secret discussions for the joint defence of their territories, in the event of a Japanese attack against any of them. Roosevelt reinforced [the Philippines](/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) (an American protectorate scheduled for independence in 1946) and warned Japan that the United States would react to Japanese attacks against any "neighboring countries".

Frustrated at the lack of progress and pressured by American–British–Dutch sanctions, especially in oil, Japan prepared for war. Emperor [Hirohito](/wiki/Hirohito), after initial hesitation about Japan's chances of victory, began to favour Japan's entry into the war. As a result, Prime Minister [Fumimaro Konoe](/wiki/Fumimaro_Konoe) resigned. Hirohito refused the recommendation to appoint [Prince Naruhiko Higashikuni](/wiki/Prince_Naruhiko_Higashikuni) in his place, choosing War Minister [Hideki Tojo](/wiki/Hideki_Tojo) instead. On 3 November, Nagano explained in detail the plan of the [attack on Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) to the Emperor. On 5 November, Hirohito approved in imperial conference the operations plan for the war. On 20 November, the new government presented an interim proposal as its final offer. It called for the end of American aid to China and for lifting the embargo on the supply of oil and other resources to Japan. In exchange, Japan promised not to launch any attacks in Southeast Asia and to withdraw its forces from southern Indochina. The American counter-proposal of 26 November required that Japan evacuate all of China without conditions and conclude non-aggression pacts with all Pacific powers. That meant Japan was essentially forced to choose between abandoning its ambitions in China, or seizing the natural resources it needed in the Dutch East Indies by force; the Japanese military did not consider the former an option, and many officers considered the oil embargo an unspoken declaration of war.

The [USS *Arizona*](/wiki/USS_Arizona) was a total loss in the [Japanese surprise air attack](/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) on the [United States Pacific Fleet](/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Fleet) at [Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Pearl_Harbor), Sunday 7 December 1941

Japan planned to seize European colonies in Asia to create a large defensive perimeter stretching into the Central Pacific. The Japanese would then be free to exploit the resources of Southeast Asia while exhausting the over-stretched Allies by fighting a defensive war. To prevent American intervention while securing the perimeter, it was further planned to neutralise the [United States Pacific Fleet](/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Fleet) and the American military presence in the Philippines from the outset. On 7 December 1941 (8 December in Asian time zones), Japan attacked British and American holdings with near-simultaneous [offensives against Southeast Asia and the Central Pacific](/wiki/Japanese_expansion_(1941%E2%80%9342)). These included an [attack on the American fleets at Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) and [the Philippines](/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1941%E2%80%931942)), as well as invasions of [Guam](/wiki/Battle_of_Guam_(1941)), [Wake Island](/wiki/Battle_of_Wake_Island), [Malaya](/wiki/Malayan_campaign), [Thailand](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Thailand), and [Hong Kong](/wiki/Battle_of_Hong_Kong).

These attacks led the [United States](/wiki/United_States_declaration_of_war_on_Japan), [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_on_Japan), China, Australia, and several other states to formally declare war on Japan, whereas the Soviet Union, being heavily involved in large-scale hostilities with European Axis countries, maintained its neutrality agreement with Japan. Germany, followed by the other Axis states, declared war on the United States in solidarity with Japan, citing as justification the American attacks on German war vessels that had been ordered by Roosevelt.

### Axis advance stalls (1942–1943)

On 1 January 1942, the [Allied Big Four](/wiki/Four_Policemen)—the Soviet Union, China, the United Kingdom, and the United States—and 22 smaller or exiled governments issued the [Declaration by United Nations](/wiki/Declaration_by_United_Nations), thereby affirming the [Atlantic Charter](/wiki/Atlantic_Charter) and agreeing not to sign a [separate peace](/wiki/Separate_peace) with the Axis powers.

During 1942, Allied officials debated on the appropriate [grand strategy](/wiki/Grand_strategy) to pursue. All agreed that [defeating Germany](/wiki/Europe_first) was the primary objective. The Americans favoured a straightforward, [large-scale attack](/wiki/Operation_Sledgehammer) on Germany through France. The Soviets demanded a second front. The British argued that military operations should target peripheral areas to wear out German strength, leading to increasing demoralisation, and bolstering [resistance forces](/wiki/Resistance_during_World_War_II); Germany itself would be subject to a heavy bombing campaign. An offensive against Germany would then be launched primarily by Allied armour, without using large-scale armies. Eventually, the British persuaded the Americans that a landing in France was infeasible in 1942 and they should instead focus on driving the Axis out of North Africa.

At the [Casablanca Conference](/wiki/Casablanca_Conference) in early 1943, the Allies reiterated the statements issued in the 1942 Declaration and demanded the [unconditional surrender](/wiki/Unconditional_surrender) of their enemies. The British and Americans agreed to continue to press the initiative in the Mediterranean by invading Sicily to fully secure the Mediterranean supply routes. Although the British argued for further operations in the Balkans to bring Turkey into the war, in May 1943, the Americans extracted a British commitment to limit Allied operations in the Mediterranean to an invasion of the Italian mainland, and to invade France in 1944.

### Pacific (1942–1943)

Map of Japanese military advances through mid-1942

Japanese forces achieved naval victories in the [South China Sea](/wiki/Sinking_of_Prince_of_Wales_and_Repulse), [Java Sea](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Java_Sea), and [Indian Ocean](/wiki/Indian_Ocean_raid), and [bombed the Allied naval base](/wiki/Bombing_of_Darwin) at [Darwin](/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), Australia. On 16 April, 7,000 British soldiers were encircled by the Japanese 33rd Division during the [Battle of Yenangyaung](/wiki/Battle_of_Yenangyaung) in Burma and rescued by the Chinese 38th Division. Despite stubborn [resistance by Filipino and US forces](/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1941%E2%80%931942)), the [Philippine Commonwealth](/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) was eventually captured in May, forcing its government into exile. Following the capture of Bataan, Japanese armies forced some 75,000 Filipino and American prisoners on a [42km death march](/wiki/Bataan_Death_March), resulting in thousands of deaths.

By the end of April, Japan and its ally [Thailand](/wiki/Thailand_in_World_War_II) had conquered [Malaya](/wiki/Malayan_campaign), [the Dutch East Indies](/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_campaign), [Singapore](/wiki/Fall_of_Singapore), [Rabaul](/wiki/Battle_of_Rabaul_(1942)) and most of [Burma](/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Burma), inflicting severe losses on Allied troops and taking a large number of prisoners. Japanese advances were accompanied by numerous atrocities, including the [Sook Ching Massacre](/wiki/Sook_Ching) in Singapore. The only Allied success against Japan was a Chinese [victory at Changsha](/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1941%E2%80%931942)). The Japanese victories left it overconfident and overextended.

Japanese torpedo bombers fly past the [USS Yorktown](/wiki/USS_Yorktown_(CV-5)) during the [Battle of Midway](/wiki/Battle_of_Midway), 4 June 1942

In early May 1942, Japan initiated operations to [capture Port Moresby](/wiki/Operation_Mo) by [amphibious assault](/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) and thus sever communications and supply lines between the United States and Australia. The planned invasion was thwarted when an Allied task force, centred on two American fleet carriers, fought Japanese naval forces to a draw in the [Battle of the Coral Sea](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Coral_Sea). Japan's next plan, motivated by the earlier [Doolittle Raid](/wiki/Doolittle_Raid), was to seize [Midway Atoll](/wiki/Midway_Atoll) and lure American carriers into battle to be eliminated; as a diversion, Japan would also send forces to [occupy the Aleutian Islands](/wiki/Aleutian_Islands_campaign) in Alaska. In mid-May, Japan started the [Zhejiang-Jiangxi campaign](/wiki/Zhejiang-Jiangxi_campaign) in China, with the goal of inflicting retribution on the Chinese who aided the surviving American airmen in the Doolittle Raid by destroying Chinese air bases and fighting against the Chinese 23rd and 32nd Army Groups. In early June, Japan put its operations into action, but the Americans had broken [Japanese naval codes](/wiki/Japanese_naval_codes) in late May and were fully aware of the plans and order of battle, and used this knowledge to achieve a decisive [victory at Midway](/wiki/Battle_of_Midway) over the [Imperial Japanese Navy](/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy).

With its capacity for aggressive action greatly diminished as a result of the Midway battle, Japan attempted to capture [Port Moresby](/wiki/Port_Moresby) by an [overland campaign](/wiki/Kokoda_Track_campaign) in the [Territory of Papua](/wiki/Territory_of_Papua). The Americans planned a counterattack against Japanese positions in the southern [Solomon Islands](/wiki/Solomon_Islands), primarily [Guadalcanal](/wiki/Guadalcanal), as a first step towards capturing [Rabaul](/wiki/Rabaul), the main Japanese base in Southeast Asia.

Both plans started in July, but by mid-September, the [Battle for Guadalcanal](/wiki/Guadalcanal_campaign) took priority for the Japanese, and troops in New Guinea were ordered to withdraw from the Port Moresby area to the [northern part of the island](/wiki/Oro_Province), where they faced Australian and United States troops in the [Battle of Buna–Gona](/wiki/Battle_of_Buna%E2%80%93Gona). Guadalcanal soon became a focal point for both sides with heavy commitments of troops and ships in the battle for Guadalcanal, with Japanese forces suffering massive losses in the attrition, especially amongst their elite pilots. By the start of 1943, the Japanese were defeated on the island and [withdrew their troops](/wiki/Operation_Ke). In Burma, Commonwealth forces mounted two operations. The first was a disastrous [offensive into the Arakan region](/wiki/Arakan_campaign_(1942%E2%80%931943)) in late 1942 that forced a retreat back to India by May 1943. The second was the [insertion of irregular forces](/wiki/Operation_Longcloth) behind Japanese frontlines in February which, by the end of April, had achieved mixed results.

### Eastern Front (1942–1943)

Despite considerable losses, in early 1942 Germany and its allies stopped a major Soviet offensive in [central](/wiki/Central_Russia) and [southern Russia](/wiki/Southern_Russia), keeping most territorial gains they had achieved during the previous year. In May, the Germans defeated Soviet offensives in the [Kerch Peninsula](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Kerch_Peninsula) and at [Kharkov](/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Kharkov). The fortress city of Sevastopol, which the Red Army had held out against Axis siege for nearly 250 days, was finally seized with the use of massive artillery bombardments and poison gas.

In June 1942 Germany launched its main [summer offensive](/wiki/Case_Blue) against southern Russia, to seize the [oil fields of the Caucasus](/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Azerbaijan) and occupy the [Kuban](/wiki/Kuban) [steppe](/wiki/Steppe), while maintaining positions on the northern and central areas of the front. The Germans split [Army Group South](/wiki/Army_Group_South) into two groups: [Army Group A](/wiki/Army_Group_A) advanced to the lower [Don River](/wiki/Don_(river)) and struck south-east to the Caucasus, while [Army Group B](/wiki/Army_Group_B) headed towards the [Volga River](/wiki/Volga). The Soviet Union decided to make its stand at Stalingrad on the Volga.

[Red Army](/wiki/Red_Army) soldiers on the counterattack during the [Battle of Stalingrad](/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad), February 1943

By mid-November, the Germans had [nearly taken Stalingrad](/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad) in bitter [street fighting](/wiki/Urban_warfare). The Soviet Union began its second winter counter-offensive, starting with an [encirclement of the German Sixth Army at Stalingrad](/wiki/Operation_Uranus), and an assault on the [Rzhev salient near Moscow](/wiki/Operation_Mars), though the latter failed. By early February 1943, the German army had taken tremendous losses; German troops at Stalingrad had been defeated, and the front-line had been pushed back beyond its position before the summer offensive. In mid-February, after the Soviet push had tapered off, the Germans launched another [attack on Kharkov](/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Kharkov), creating a [salient](/wiki/Salient_(military)) in their front line around the Soviet city of [Kursk](/wiki/Kursk).

### Western Europe/Atlantic and Mediterranean (1942–1943)

American [Eighth Air Force](/wiki/Eighth_Air_Force) [Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress](/wiki/Boeing_B-17_Flying_Fortress) bombing raid on the Focke-Wulf factory in Germany, 9 October 1943

Exploiting poor American naval command decisions, [the German navy ravaged Allied shipping off the American Atlantic coast](/wiki/Second_Happy_Time). By November 1941, Commonwealth forces had launched a counter-offensive in North Africa, [Operation Crusader](/wiki/Operation_Crusader), and reclaimed all the gains the Germans and Italians had made. The Germans also launched a North African offensive in January, pushing the British back to positions at the [Gazala line](/wiki/Battle_of_Gazala#Gazala_line) by early February, followed by a temporary lull in combat which Germany used to prepare for their upcoming offensives. Concerns that the Japanese might use bases in [Vichy-held Madagascar](/wiki/French_Madagascar) caused the British to [invade the island](/wiki/Battle_of_Madagascar) in early May 1942. An Axis [offensive in Libya](/wiki/Battle_of_Gazala) forced an Allied retreat deep inside Egypt until Axis forces were [stopped at El Alamein](/wiki/First_Battle_of_El_Alamein). On the Continent, raids of Allied [commandos](/wiki/Commando) on strategic targets, culminating in the failed [Dieppe Raid](/wiki/Dieppe_Raid), demonstrated the Western Allies' inability to launch an invasion of continental Europe without much better preparation, equipment, and operational security.

In August 1942, the Allies succeeded in repelling a [second attack against El Alamein](/wiki/Battle_of_Alam_el_Halfa) and, at a high cost, managed to [deliver desperately needed supplies to the besieged Malta](/wiki/Operation_Pedestal). A few months later, the Allies [commenced an attack of their own](/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein) in Egypt, dislodging the Axis forces and beginning a drive west across Libya. This attack was followed up shortly after by [Anglo-American landings in French North Africa](/wiki/Operation_Torch), which resulted in the region joining the Allies. Hitler responded to the French colony's defection by ordering the [occupation of Vichy France](/wiki/Case_Anton); although Vichy forces did not resist this violation of the armistice, they managed to [scuttle their fleet](/wiki/Scuttling_of_the_French_fleet_at_Toulon) to prevent its capture by German forces. Axis forces in Africa withdrew into [Tunisia](/wiki/Tunisia), which was [conquered by the Allies](/wiki/Tunisian_campaign) in May 1943.

In June 1943, the British and Americans began [a strategic bombing campaign](/wiki/Combined_Bomber_Offensive) against Germany with a goal to disrupt the war economy, reduce morale, and "[de-house](/wiki/Dehousing)" the civilian population. The [firebombing of Hamburg](/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II) was among the first attacks in this campaign, inflicting significant casualties and considerable losses on infrastructure of this important industrial centre.

### Allies gain momentum (1943–1944)

[US Navy](/wiki/United_States_Navy) [SBD-5](/wiki/Douglas_SBD_Dauntless) [scout](/wiki/Scout_plane)/[dive bomber](/wiki/Dive_bomber) flying patrol over [USS *Washington*](/wiki/USS_Washington_(BB-56)) and [USS *Lexington*](/wiki/USS_Lexington_(CV-16)) during the [Gilbert and Marshall Islands campaign](/wiki/Gilbert_and_Marshall_Islands_campaign), 1943

After the Guadalcanal campaign, the Allies initiated several operations against Japan in the Pacific. In May 1943, Canadian and US forces were sent to [eliminate Japanese forces from the Aleutians](/wiki/Aleutian_Islands_campaign#Allied_response). Soon after, the United States, with support from Australia, New Zealand and Pacific Islander forces, began major ground, sea and air operations to [isolate Rabaul by capturing surrounding islands](/wiki/Operation_Cartwheel), and [breach the Japanese Central Pacific perimeter at the Gilbert and Marshall Islands](/wiki/Gilbert_and_Marshall_Islands_campaign). By the end of March 1944, the Allies had completed both of these objectives and had also [neutralised the major Japanese base at Truk](/wiki/Operation_Hailstone) in the [Caroline Islands](/wiki/Caroline_Islands). In April, the Allies launched an operation to [retake Western New Guinea](/wiki/Western_New_Guinea_campaign).

In the Soviet Union, both the Germans and the Soviets spent the spring and early summer of 1943 preparing for large offensives in [central Russia](/wiki/Central_Russia). On 5 July 1943, Germany [attacked Soviet forces around the Kursk Bulge](/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk). Within a week, German forces had exhausted themselves against the Soviets' well-constructed defences, and for the first time in the war, Hitler cancelled an operation before it had achieved tactical or operational success. This decision was partially affected by the Western Allies' [invasion of Sicily](/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Sicily) launched on 9 July, which, combined with previous Italian failures, resulted in the [ousting and arrest of Mussolini](/wiki/Fall_of_the_Fascist_regime_in_Italy) later that month.

[Red Army](/wiki/Red_Army) troops behind [T34/76](/wiki/T-34_variants)(1942 variant) in a counter-offensive on German positions at the [Battle of Kursk](/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk), July 1943

On 12 July 1943, the Soviets launched their own [counter-offensives](/wiki/Operation_Kutuzov), thereby nearly completely dispelling any chance of German victory or even stalemate in the east. The Soviet victory at Kursk marked the end of German superiority, giving the Soviet Union the initiative on the Eastern Front. The Germans tried to stabilise their eastern front along the hastily fortified [Panther–Wotan line](/wiki/Panther%E2%80%93Wotan_line), but the Soviets broke through it at [Smolensk](/wiki/Smolensk_operation) and the [Lower Dnieper Offensive](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Dnieper).

On 3 September 1943, the Western Allies [invaded the Italian mainland](/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Italy), following [Italy's armistice with the Allies](/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile) and the ensuing German occupation of Italy. Germany, with the help of local fascists, responded to the armistice by [disarming Italian forces](/wiki/Operation_Achse) that were in many places without superior orders, seizing military control of Italian areas, and creating a series of defensive lines. German special forces then [rescued Mussolini](/wiki/Gran_Sasso_raid), who then soon established a new client state in German-occupied Italy named the [Italian Social Republic](/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic), causing an [Italian civil war](/wiki/Italian_Civil_War). The Western Allies fought through several lines until reaching the [main German defensive line](/wiki/Winter_Line) in mid-November.

German operations in the Atlantic also suffered. By [May 1943, as Allied counter-measures became increasingly effective](/wiki/Black_May_(1943)), the resulting sizeable German submarine losses forced a temporary halt of the German Atlantic naval campaign. In November 1943, [Franklin D. Roosevelt](/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) and Winston Churchill met with [Chiang Kai-shek](/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) [in Cairo](/wiki/Cairo_Conference) and then with Joseph Stalin [in Tehran](/wiki/Tehran_Conference). The former conference determined the post-war return of Japanese territory and the military planning for the [Burma campaign](/wiki/Burma_campaign), while the latter included agreement that the Western Allies would invade Europe in 1944 and that the Soviet Union would declare war on Japan within three months of Germany's defeat.

From November 1943, during the seven-week [Battle of Changde](/wiki/Battle_of_Changde), the Chinese awaited Allied relief as they forced Japan to fight a costly war of attrition. In January 1944, the Allies launched a [series of attacks in Italy against the line at Monte Cassino](/wiki/Battle_of_Monte_Cassino) and tried to outflank it with [landings at Anzio](/wiki/Battle_of_Anzio).

On 27 January 1944, [Soviet](/wiki/Leningrad_Front) troops launched [a major offensive](/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad#Soviet_relief_of_the_siege) that expelled German forces from the [Leningrad region](/wiki/Leningrad_Oblast), thereby ending the [most lethal siege in history](/wiki/List_of_battles_by_casualties#Sieges_and_urban_combat). The [following Soviet offensive](/wiki/Leningrad%E2%80%93Novgorod_offensive) was [halted on the pre-war Estonian border](/wiki/Battle_of_Narva_(1944)) by the German [Army Group North](/wiki/Army_Group_North) aided by [Estonians](/wiki/German_occupation_of_Estonia_during_World_War_II#Estonians_in_Nazi_German_military_units) hoping to [re-establish national independence](/wiki/Estonian_government-in-exile#Failure_to_reestablish_independence). This delay slowed subsequent Soviet operations in the [Baltic Sea](/wiki/Baltic_Sea) region. By late May 1944, the Soviets had [liberated Crimea](/wiki/Crimean_offensive), [largely expelled Axis forces from Ukraine](/wiki/Dnieper%E2%80%93Carpathian_offensive), and made [incursions into Romania](/wiki/First_Jassy%E2%80%93Kishinev_offensive), which were repulsed by the Axis troops. The Allied offensives in Italy had succeeded and, at the cost of allowing several German divisions to retreat, Rome was captured on 4 June.

The Allies had mixed success in mainland Asia. In March 1944, the Japanese launched the first of two invasions, [an operation against Allied positions in Assam, India](/wiki/Operation_U-Go), and soon besieged Commonwealth positions at [Imphal](/wiki/Battle_of_Imphal) and [Kohima](/wiki/Battle_of_Kohima). In May 1944, British and Indian forces mounted a counter-offensive that drove Japanese troops back to Burma by July, and Chinese forces that had [invaded northern Burma](/wiki/Battle_of_Northern_Burma_and_Western_Yunnan) in late 1943 [besieged Japanese troops](/wiki/Siege_of_Myitkyina) in [Myitkyina](/wiki/Myitkyina). The [second Japanese invasion](/wiki/Operation_Ichi-Go) of China aimed to destroy China's main fighting forces, secure railways between Japanese-held territory and capture Allied airfields. By June, the Japanese had conquered the province of [Henan](/wiki/Henan) and begun a [new attack on Changsha](/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1944)).

### Allied offensives (1944)

American troops approaching [Omaha Beach](/wiki/Omaha_Beach) during the [invasion of Normandy](/wiki/Operation_Overlord) on [D-Day](/wiki/Normandy_landings), 6 June 1944

On 6 June 1944 (commonly known as [D-Day](/wiki/Normandy_landings)), after three years of Soviet pressure, the Western Allies [invaded northern France](/wiki/Operation_Overlord). After reassigning several Allied divisions from Italy, they also [attacked southern France](/wiki/Operation_Dragoon). These landings were successful and led to the defeat of the [German Army units in France](/wiki/Falaise_pocket). [Paris](/wiki/Paris) was [liberated](/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris) on 25 August by the [local resistance](/wiki/French_Resistance) assisted by the [Free French Forces](/wiki/Free_French_Forces), both led by General [Charles de Gaulle](/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle), and the Western Allies continued to [push back German forces](/wiki/Siegfried_Line_campaign) in western Europe during the latter part of the year. An attempt to advance into northern Germany spearheaded by [a major airborne operation](/wiki/Operation_Market_Garden) in the Netherlands failed. After that, the Western Allies slowly pushed into Germany, but [failed to cross the Roer river](/wiki/Operation_Queen). In Italy, the Allied advance slowed due to the [last major German defensive line](/wiki/Gothic_Line).

On 22 June, the Soviets launched [a strategic offensive in Belarus](/wiki/Operation_Bagration) that nearly destroyed the German [Army Group Centre](/wiki/Army_Group_Centre). Soon after that, [another Soviet strategic offensive](/wiki/Lvov%E2%80%93Sandomierz_offensive) forced German troops from Western Ukraine and Eastern Poland. The Soviet Red Army however halted in the [Praga](/wiki/Praga) district on the other side of the [Vistula](/wiki/Vistula) as the Germans quelled the [Warsaw Uprising](/wiki/Warsaw_Uprising) initiated by the [Home Army](/wiki/Home_Army) (the main faction of the [Polish resistance](/wiki/Polish_resistance_movement_in_World_War_II), loyal to the non-communist government-in exile), killing over 150,000 Poles. The [national uprising](/wiki/Slovak_National_Uprising) in [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovak_Republic_(1939%E2%80%931945)) was also quelled by the Germans. The Soviet [Red Army](/wiki/Red_Army)'s [strategic offensive in eastern Romania](/wiki/Second_Jassy%E2%80%93Kishinev_offensive) cut off and destroyed the [considerable German troops there](/wiki/Army_Group_South_Ukraine) and triggered [a successful coup d'état in Romania](/wiki/1944_Romanian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat) and [in Bulgaria](/wiki/1944_Bulgarian_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat), followed by those countries' shift to the Allied side.

[General](/wiki/General_(United_States)) [Douglas MacArthur](/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) returns to the [Philippines](/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) during the [Battle of Leyte](/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte), 20 October 1944

In September 1944, Soviet troops advanced into [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Democratic_Federal_Yugoslavia) and forced the rapid withdrawal of German Army Groups [E](/wiki/Army_Group_E) and [F](/wiki/Army_Group_F) in [Greece](/wiki/Axis_occupation_of_Greece), [Albania](/wiki/German_occupation_of_Albania), and Yugoslavia to rescue them from being cut off. By this point, the communist-led [Partisans](/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans) under Marshal [Josip Broz Tito](/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito), who had led an [increasingly successful guerrilla campaign](/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia) against the occupation since 1941, controlled much of the territory of Yugoslavia and engaged in delaying efforts against German forces further south. In northern [Serbia](/wiki/Territory_of_the_Military_Commander_in_Serbia), the Soviet [Red Army](/wiki/Red_Army), with limited support from Bulgarian forces, assisted the Partisans in a joint [liberation of the capital city of Belgrade](/wiki/Belgrade_offensive) on 20 October. A few days later, the Soviets launched a [massive assault](/wiki/Budapest_offensive) against [German-occupied](/wiki/Operation_Panzerfaust) Hungary that lasted until [the fall of Budapest](/wiki/Siege_of_Budapest) in February 1945. Unlike rapid Soviet victories in the Balkans, [bitter Finnish resistance](/wiki/Continuation_War) to the [Soviet offensive](/wiki/Vyborg%E2%80%93Petrozavodsk_offensive) in the [Karelian Isthmus](/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus) denied the Soviets occupation of Finland and led to a [Soviet-Finnish armistice](/wiki/Moscow_Armistice) on relatively mild conditions, although Finland was obligated to [fight their German former allies](/wiki/Lapland_War).

By the start of July 1944, Commonwealth forces in Southeast Asia had repelled the Japanese sieges in [Assam](/wiki/Assam), pushing the Japanese back to the [Chindwin River](/wiki/Chindwin_River) while the Chinese captured Myitkyina. In September 1944, Chinese forces [captured Mount Song](/wiki/Battle_of_Mount_Song) and reopened the [Burma Road](/wiki/Burma_Road). In China, the Japanese had more successes, having finally [captured Changsha](/wiki/Battle_of_Changsha_(1944)) in mid-June and the city of [Hengyang](/wiki/Battle_of_Hengyang) by early August. Soon after, they invaded the province of [Guangxi](/wiki/Guangxi), winning major engagements against Chinese forces at [Guilin and Liuzhou](/wiki/Battle_of_Guilin%E2%80%93Liuzhou) by the end of November and successfully linking up their forces in China and Indochina by mid-December.

In the Pacific, US forces continued to push back the Japanese perimeter. In mid-June 1944, they began their [offensive against the Mariana and Palau islands](/wiki/Mariana_and_Palau_Islands_campaign) and decisively defeated Japanese forces in the [Battle of the Philippine Sea](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Philippine_Sea). These defeats led to the resignation of the Japanese Prime Minister, [Hideki Tojo](/wiki/Hideki_Tojo), and provided the United States with air bases to launch intensive heavy bomber attacks on the Japanese home islands. In late October, American forces [invaded the Filipino island of Leyte](/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte); soon after, Allied naval forces scored another large victory in the [Battle of Leyte Gulf](/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte_Gulf), one of the largest naval battles in history.

### Axis collapse and Allied victory (1944–1945)

[Yalta Conference](/wiki/Yalta_Conference) held in February 1945, with [Winston Churchill](/wiki/Winston_Churchill), [Franklin D. Roosevelt](/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), and [Joseph Stalin](/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)

On 16 December 1944, Germany made a last attempt to split the Allies on the Western Front by using most of its remaining reserves to launch [a massive counter-offensive in the Ardennes](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Bulge) and [along the French-German border](/wiki/Operation_Northwind_(1944)), hoping to encircle large portions of Western Allied troops and prompt a political settlement after capturing their primary supply port at [Antwerp](/wiki/Antwerp). By 16 January 1945, this offensive had been repulsed with no strategic objectives fulfilled. In Italy, the Western Allies remained stalemated at the German defensive line. In mid-January 1945, the Red Army attacked in Poland, [pushing from the Vistula to the Oder](/wiki/Vistula%E2%80%93Oder_offensive) river in Germany, and [overran East Prussia](/wiki/East_Prussian_offensive). On 4 February Soviet, British, and US leaders met for the [Yalta Conference](/wiki/Yalta_Conference). They agreed on the occupation of post-war Germany, and on when the Soviet Union would join the war against Japan.

In February, the Soviets [entered Silesia](/wiki/Silesian_offensives) and [Pomerania](/wiki/East_Pomeranian_offensive), while the [Western Allies entered western Germany](/wiki/Western_Allied_invasion_of_Germany) and closed to the [Rhine](/wiki/Rhine) river. By March, the Western Allies crossed the Rhine [north](/wiki/Operation_Plunder) and [south](/wiki/Remagen) of the [Ruhr](/wiki/Ruhr_(river)), [encircling the German Army Group B](/wiki/Ruhr_pocket). In early March, in an attempt to protect its last oil reserves in Hungary and retake Budapest, Germany launched [its last major offensive](/wiki/Operation_Spring_Awakening) against Soviet troops near [Lake Balaton](/wiki/Lake_Balaton). Within two weeks, the offensive had been repulsed, the Soviets advanced to [Vienna](/wiki/Vienna_offensive), and captured the city. In early April, Soviet troops [captured Königsberg](/wiki/Battle_of_K%C3%B6nigsberg), while the Western Allies finally [pushed forward in Italy](/wiki/Spring_1945_offensive_in_Italy) and swept across western Germany capturing [Hamburg](/wiki/Capture_of_Hamburg) and [Nuremberg](/wiki/Battle_of_Nuremberg_(1945)). [American and Soviet forces met at the Elbe river](/wiki/Elbe_Day) on 25 April, leaving unoccupied pockets in southern Germany and around Berlin.

Soviet troops [stormed and captured Berlin](/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin) in late April. In Italy, [German forces surrendered](/wiki/Surrender_of_Caserta) on 29 April, while the [Italian Social Republic](/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic) capitulated two days later. On 30 April, the [Reichstag](/wiki/Reichstag_building) was captured, signalling the military defeat of Nazi Germany.

Major changes in leadership occurred on both sides during this period. On 12 April, President Roosevelt died and was succeeded by his vice president, [Harry S. Truman](/wiki/Harry_S._Truman). Benito Mussolini [was killed](/wiki/Death_of_Benito_Mussolini) by [Italian partisans](/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement) on 28 April. On 30 April, [Hitler committed suicide](/wiki/Death_of_Adolf_Hitler) in his [headquarters](/wiki/F%C3%BChrerbunker), and was succeeded by [Grand Admiral](/wiki/Grand_Admiral) [Karl Dönitz](/wiki/Karl_D%C3%B6nitz) (as [President of the Reich](/wiki/President_of_Germany_(1919%E2%80%931945))) and [Joseph Goebbels](/wiki/Joseph_Goebbels) (as [Chancellor of the Reich](/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Reich)). Goebbels also committed suicide on the following day and was replaced by [Lutz Graf Schwerin von Krosigk](/wiki/Lutz_Graf_Schwerin_von_Krosigk), in what would later be known as the [Flensburg Government](/wiki/Flensburg_Government). [Total and unconditional surrender](/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender) in Europe was signed [on 7 and 8 May](/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day), to be effective by the end of [8 May](/wiki/Victory_Day_(9_May)). German Army Group Centre [resisted in Prague](/wiki/Prague_offensive) until 11 May. On 23 May, all remaining members of the German government were arrested by Allied forces in [Flensburg](/wiki/Flensburg). On 5 June, all German political and military institutions were placed under Allied control through the [Berlin Declaration](/wiki/Berlin_Declaration_(1945)).

A Filipino woman and child killed by Japanese troops in [Manila](/wiki/Manila), 1945

In the Pacific theatre, American forces accompanied by the forces of the [Philippine Commonwealth](/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) advanced [in the Philippines](/wiki/Philippines_campaign_(1944%E2%80%931945)), [clearing Leyte](/wiki/Battle_of_Leyte) by the end of April 1945. They [landed on Luzon](/wiki/Battle_of_Luzon) in January 1945 and [recaptured Manila](/wiki/Battle_of_Manila_(1945)) in March, during which Japanese forces [killed 100,000 Filipino civilians](/wiki/Manila_massacre) in the city. Fighting continued on Luzon, [Mindanao](/wiki/Battle_of_Mindanao), and other islands of the Philippines until the [end of the war](/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Asia).

Meanwhile, the [United States Army Air Forces](/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) launched [a massive firebombing campaign](/wiki/Air_raids_on_Japan) of strategic cities in Japan in an effort to destroy Japanese war industry and civilian morale. A devastating [bombing raid on Tokyo of 9–10 March](/wiki/Bombing_of_Tokyo) was the deadliest conventional bombing raid in history.

In May 1945, Australian troops [landed in Borneo](/wiki/Borneo_campaign), overrunning the oilfields there. British, American, and Chinese forces defeated the Japanese in northern [Burma](/wiki/Burma_campaign) in March, and the British pushed on to reach [Rangoon](/wiki/Yangon) by 3 May. Chinese forces started a counterattack in the [Battle of West Hunan](/wiki/Battle_of_West_Hunan) that occurred between 6 April and 7 June 1945. American naval and amphibious forces also moved towards Japan, taking [Iwo Jima](/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima) by March, and [Okinawa](/wiki/Battle_of_Okinawa) by the end of June. At the same time, a naval blockade by [submarines](/wiki/Allied_submarines_in_the_Pacific_War) was strangling Japan's economy and drastically reducing its ability to supply overseas forces.

On 11 July, Allied leaders [met in Potsdam, Germany](/wiki/Potsdam_Conference). They [confirmed earlier agreements](/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement) about Germany, and the American, British and Chinese governments reiterated the demand for unconditional surrender of Japan, specifically stating that "[the alternative for Japan is prompt and utter destruction](/wiki/Potsdam_Declaration)". During this conference, the United Kingdom [held its general election](/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election), and [Clement Attlee](/wiki/Clement_Attlee) replaced Churchill as Prime Minister.

Japanese foreign affairs minister [Mamoru Shigemitsu](/wiki/Mamoru_Shigemitsu) signs the [Japanese Instrument of Surrender](/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender) on board [USS *Missouri*](/wiki/USS_Missouri_(BB-63)), 2 September 1945

The call for unconditional surrender was rejected by the Japanese government, which believed it would be capable of negotiating for more favourable surrender terms. In early August, the United States [dropped atomic bombs](/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) on the Japanese cities of [Hiroshima](/wiki/Hiroshima) and [Nagasaki](/wiki/Nagasaki). Between the two bombings, the Soviets, pursuant to the Yalta agreement, [declared war on Japan](/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Japanese_War), [invaded Japanese-held Manchuria](/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Manchuria) and quickly defeated the [Kwantung Army](/wiki/Kwantung_Army), which was the largest Japanese fighting force. These two events persuaded previously adamant Imperial Army leaders to accept surrender terms. The Red Army also captured the [southern part of Sakhalin Island](/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_South_Sakhalin) and the [Kuril Islands](/wiki/Invasion_of_the_Kuril_Islands). On the night of 9–10 August 1945, Emperor Hirohito ordered the Japanese cabinet to accept the terms demanded by the Allies in the [Potsdam Declaration](/wiki/Potsdam_Declaration). On 15 August, the Emperor communicated this decision to the Japanese people through a speech broadcast on the radio ([*Gyokuon-hōsō*](/wiki/Hirohito_surrender_broadcast), literally "broadcast in the Emperor's voice"). On 15 August 1945, [Japan surrendered](/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan), with the [surrender documents](/wiki/Japanese_Instrument_of_Surrender) finally signed at [Tokyo Bay](/wiki/Tokyo_Bay) on the deck of the American battleship [USS *Missouri*](/wiki/USS_Missouri_(BB-63)) on 2 September 1945, ending the war.

## Aftermath

Main articles: [Aftermath of World War II](/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II) and [Consequences of Nazism](/wiki/Consequences_of_Nazism)

Defendants at the [Nuremberg trials](/wiki/Nuremberg_trials), where the Allied forces prosecuted prominent members of the political, military, judicial, and economic leadership of [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) for [crimes against humanity](/wiki/Crimes_against_humanity)

The Allies established occupation administrations in [Austria](/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria) and [Germany](/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany), both of which were initially divided between western and eastern occupation zones controlled by the Western Allies and the Soviet Union, respectively. However, their paths soon diverged. In Germany, the [western](/wiki/Trizone) and [eastern occupation zones](/wiki/Soviet_occupation_zone_in_Germany) officially ended in 1949, with the respective zones becoming separate countries, [West Germany](/wiki/West_Germany) and [East Germany](/wiki/East_Germany). In Austria, however, occupation continued until 1955, when a joint settlement between the Western Allies and the Soviet Union permitted the reunification of Austria as a democratic state officially non-aligned with any political bloc (although in practice having better relations with the Western Allies). A [denazification](/wiki/Denazification) program in Germany led to the prosecution of Nazi war criminals in the [Nuremberg trials](/wiki/Nuremberg_trials) and the removal of ex-Nazis from power, although this policy moved towards amnesty and re-integration of ex-Nazis into West German society.

Germany lost a quarter of its pre-war (1937) territory. Among the eastern territories, [Silesia](/wiki/Silesia), [Neumark](/wiki/Neumark) and most of [Pomerania](/wiki/Pomerania) were taken over by Poland, and [East Prussia](/wiki/East_Prussia) was divided between Poland and the Soviet Union, followed by the [expulsion to Germany](/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_(1944%E2%80%931950)) of the nine million Germans from these provinces, as well as three million Germans from the [Sudetenland](/wiki/Sudetenland) in Czechoslovakia. By the 1950s, one-fifth of West Germans were refugees from the east. The Soviet Union also took over the Polish provinces east of the [Curzon Line](/wiki/Curzon_Line), from which [two million Poles were expelled](/wiki/Polish_population_transfers_(1944%E2%80%931946)). Northeastern Romania, parts of eastern Finland, and the [Baltic states](/wiki/Baltic_states) were [annexed into the Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_re-occupation_of_the_Baltic_states_(1944)). Italy [lost its monarchy](/wiki/1946_Italian_institutional_referendum), [colonial empire](/wiki/Italian_Empire), and some [European territories](/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_between_Italy_and_the_Allied_Powers).

In an effort to maintain [world peace](/wiki/World_peace), the Allies formed the [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations), which officially came into existence on 24 October 1945, and adopted the [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) in 1948 as a common standard for all [member nations](/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations). The [great powers](/wiki/Great_powers) that were the victors of the war—France, China, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, and the United States—became the [permanent members](/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) of the UN's [Security Council](/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council). The five permanent members remain so to the present, although there have been two seat changes, [between](/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_2758) the [Republic of China](/wiki/Taiwan) and the [People's Republic of China](/wiki/China) in 1971, and between the Soviet Union and its [successor state](/wiki/Succession_of_states), the [Russian Federation](/wiki/Russia), following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991. The alliance between the Western Allies and the Soviet Union had begun to deteriorate even before the war was over.

Post-war border changes in [Central Europe](/wiki/Central_Europe) and creation of the [Communist](/wiki/Communism) [Eastern Bloc](/wiki/Eastern_Bloc)

Besides Germany, the rest of Europe was also divided into Western and Soviet [spheres of influence](/wiki/Spheres_of_influence). Most eastern and central European countries fell into the [Soviet sphere](/wiki/Eastern_Bloc), which led to the establishment of Communist-led regimes, with full or partial support of the Soviet occupation authorities. As a result, [East Germany](/wiki/East_Germany), [Poland](/wiki/Polish_People%27s_Republic), [Hungary](/wiki/Hungarian_People%27s_Republic), [Romania](/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania), [Bulgaria](/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_Bulgaria), [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovak_Socialist_Republic), and [Albania](/wiki/People%27s_Socialist_Republic_of_Albania) became Soviet [satellite states](/wiki/Satellite_state#Soviet_satellite_states). Communist [Yugoslavia](/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) conducted a fully [independent policy](/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), causing [tension with the Soviet Union](/wiki/Tito%E2%80%93Stalin_split). A [communist uprising in Greece](/wiki/Greek_Civil_War) was put down with Anglo-American support and the country remained aligned with the West.

Post-war division of the world was formalised by two international military alliances, the United States-led [NATO](/wiki/NATO) and the Soviet-led [Warsaw Pact](/wiki/Warsaw_Pact). The long period of political tensions and military competition between them—the [Cold War](/wiki/Cold_War)—would be accompanied by an unprecedented [arms race](/wiki/Arms_race) and a number of [proxy wars](/wiki/Proxy_war) throughout the world.

In Asia, the United States led the [occupation of Japan](/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan) and [administered Japan's former islands](/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands) in the Western Pacific, while the Soviets annexed [South Sakhalin](/wiki/South_Sakhalin) and the [Kuril Islands](/wiki/Kuril_Islands). [Korea](/wiki/Korea), formerly [under Japanese colonial rule](/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule), was [divided and occupied](/wiki/Division_of_Korea) by the Soviet Union in the [North](/wiki/North_Korea) and the United States in the [South](/wiki/South_Korea) between 1945 and 1948. Separate republics emerged on both sides of the 38th parallel in 1948, each claiming to be the legitimate government for all of Korea, which led ultimately to the [Korean War](/wiki/Korean_War).

In China, nationalist and communist forces resumed [the civil war](/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) in June 1946. Communist forces prevailed and established the People's Republic of China on the mainland, while nationalist forces retreated to [Taiwan](/wiki/Taiwan) in 1949. In the Middle East, the Arab rejection of the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) and the [creation of Israel](/wiki/Creation_of_Israel) marked the escalation of the [Arab–Israeli conflict](/wiki/Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_conflict). While European powers attempted to retain some or all of their [colonial empires](/wiki/Colonial_empire), their losses of prestige and resources during the war rendered this unsuccessful, leading to [decolonisation](/wiki/Decolonization).

The global economy suffered heavily from the war, although participating nations were affected differently. The United States emerged much richer than any other nation, leading to a [baby boom](/wiki/Mid-20th_century_baby_boom), and by 1950 its [gross domestic product](/wiki/Gross_domestic_product) per person was much greater than that of any of the other powers, and it dominated the world economy. The Allied occupational authorities pursued a policy of [industrial disarmament in Western Germany](/wiki/Allied_plans_for_German_industry_after_World_War_II) from 1945 to 1948. Due to international trade interdependencies, this policy led to an economic stagnation in Europe and delayed European recovery from the war for several years.

At the [Bretton Woods Conference](/wiki/Bretton_Woods_Conference) in July 1944, the Allied nations drew up an economic framework for the post-war world. The agreement created the [International Monetary Fund](/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund) (IMF) and the [International Bank for Reconstruction and Development](/wiki/International_Bank_for_Reconstruction_and_Development) (IBRD), which later became part of the [World Bank Group](/wiki/World_Bank_Group). The [Bretton Woods system](/wiki/Bretton_Woods_system) lasted until 1973. Recovery began with the mid-1948 [currency reform in West Germany](/wiki/Deutsche_Mark), and was sped up by the liberalisation of European economic policy that the US [Marshall Plan](/wiki/Marshall_Plan) economic aid (1948–1951) both directly and indirectly caused. The post-1948 West German recovery has been called the [German economic miracle](/wiki/Wirtschaftswunder#West_Germany). Italy also experienced an [economic boom](/wiki/Italian_economic_miracle) and the [French economy rebounded](/wiki/Trente_Glorieuses). By contrast, the United Kingdom was in a state of economic ruin, and although receiving a quarter of the total Marshall Plan assistance, more than any other European country, it continued in relative economic decline for decades. The Soviet Union, despite enormous human and material losses, also experienced rapid increases in production in the immediate post-war era, having seized and transferred most of Germany's industrial plants and exacted [war reparations](/wiki/World_War_II_reparations) from its satellite states. Japan recovered much later. China returned to its pre-war industrial production by 1952.

## Impact

Main article: [Historiography of World War II](/wiki/Historiography_of_World_War_II)

### Casualties

Main article: [World War II casualties](/wiki/World_War_II_casualties)

Bodies of Chinese civilians killed by the [Imperial Japanese Army](/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) during the [Nanjing Massacre](/wiki/Nanjing_Massacre) in December 1937

An estimated 60 million to more than 75 million people died in the war including at least 20 million who died from deprivation, famine and disease. Civilian deaths have been estimated to comprise 67% to 80% of all direct and indirect deaths from the war. The Soviet Union had the highest overall death toll (estimated at 20 million to 28 million), followed by China (at least 15 million), Germany (6 million to 8.7 million) and Poland (5 million to 6.5 million).

The countries which sustained the most military deaths were the Soviet Union (about 8.7 million), Germany (around 5.3 million), China (2 million to 3 million) and Japan (1.7 million to 2.5 million). Of the 20 million to 25 million military deaths in the war, the majority were of German and Soviet soldiers, including prisoners of war (POWs), on the Eastern Front.

The high civilian death toll relative to military deaths was unusual for major wars up to that time. Some 10 million to 15 million people died of starvation and disease in China and the Soviet Union, and 8 million to 10 million in India and under Japanese occupation elsewhere in Asia and the Pacific. Around 15 million civilians died in genocides and other deliberate killings, while millions in total died in concentration camps, aerial bombings and in combat zones.

### Genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity

[Prisoner identity photograph of a Polish girl](/wiki/Czes%C5%82awa_Kwoka) taken by the German [SS](/wiki/SS) in [Auschwitz](/wiki/Auschwitz). Approximately 230,000 children were held prisoner and used in forced labour and [Nazi medical experiments](/wiki/Nazi_human_experimentation).

In [trials following the war](/wiki/List_of_Axis_war_crime_trials), representatives of the Axis powers and their accomplices were convicted of numerous [war crimes](/wiki/War_crime), [crimes against humanity](/wiki/Crimes_against_humanity) and complicity in [genocides](/wiki/Genocide). The Nazis killed about 6 million Jews in a racially motivated genocide known as the [Holocaust](/wiki/The_Holocaust). They also killed millions of [Slavs](/wiki/Slavs), over 130 thousand [Romani](/wiki/Romani_people), and members of other groups that they considered racially inferior. Almost 300,000 people with mental and physical disabilities were also [systematically killed](/wiki/Aktion_T4) in Germany and its occupied territories.

The killing of civilians and POWs through massacres and deliberate starvation was especially common in the Eastern European and Asia-Pacific theatres. German forces on the Eastern Front destroyed or confiscated available food, massacred civilians in reprisals and routinely shot prisoners. The Japanese killed millions of civilians in occupied areas through massacres and [scorched earth](/wiki/Scorched_earth) strategies; for example, in their "[kill all, burn all, loot all](/wiki/Three_Alls_policy)" policy in China. In the [Nanking Massacre](/wiki/Nanjing_Massacre), between 40,000 and 200,000 Chinese civilians and POWs were killed. Japan also [used biological weapons in China](/wiki/Unit_731) and in [early conflicts against the Soviets](/wiki/Battles_of_Khalkhin_Gol). The Soviet Union was responsible for imprisoning, deporting and often [executing](/wiki/NKVD_prisoner_massacres) hundreds of thousands of civilians and POWs from occupied or annexed territories. This included the [Katyn massacre](/wiki/Katyn_massacre) of 22,000 Polish officers and intellectuals.

War crimes were also committed in civil wars and conflicts between resistance groups in occupied territories. In Yugoslavia, numerous war crimes, including the massacre of civilians and prisoners, were committed by Axis forces and the Axis-aligned Croatian [Ustaše](/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e). The main resistance groups, the Serbian-Nationalist [Chetniks](/wiki/Chetniks) and the Communist-led [partisans](/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans), also committed massacres and persecutions of their enemies. In Poland, about 100,000 Poles were killed by the [Ukrainian Insurgent Army](/wiki/Ukrainian_Insurgent_Army) in the [Volhynia massacres](/wiki/Massacres_of_Poles_in_Volhynia_and_Eastern_Galicia) between 1943 and 1945. About 10,000 Ukrainians were killed by Poles in reprisal attacks. In Greece, Axis forces were mainly responsible for civilian deaths through deliberate starvation and reprisal massacres, although the major resistance forces, [ELAS](/wiki/ELAS) and [EDES](/wiki/EDES), also committed war crimes.

The Soviet Union, Japan and Germany inflicted high death rates on POWs through executions, starvation, forced labour and other mistreatment. The Soviet Union and Japan had not ratified the [Geneva Convention](/wiki/Geneva_Convention_on_Prisoners_of_War) on treatment of POWs, and Germany regarded itself as exempt from the convention on the Eastern Front. About a third of those taken prisoner by the Japanese died, as did almost 60 per cent of Soviet prisoners of the Germans. About a third of Germans taken prisoner by the Soviet Union died in captivity. The mortality rate of German and Japanese prisoners of the Western allies was 1 to 2 per cent.

[Schutzstaffel](/wiki/Schutzstaffel) (SS) women camp guards removing prisoners' bodies from lorries and carrying them to a mass grave, inside the German [Bergen-Belsen concentration camp](/wiki/Bergen-Belsen_concentration_camp), 1945

Germany, Japan and the Soviet Union made extensive use of forced labour of foreign civilians and POWs. In greater Germany, there were 7.6 million foreign workers (including POWs undertaking forced labour) and about 500,000 slave labourers in concentration camps by late 1944. Those civilians and POWs from Soviet-occupied territories who were deported to the Soviet Union were usually imprisoned in the Soviet forced labour camps, known as the [gulag](/wiki/Gulag)**.** Between 200,000 and one million Soviet POWs and civilians repatriated from German camps were also sent to the gulag as alleged Axis collaborators where many died from malnutrition, the harsh climate and overwork. The Japanese conscripted millions of foreign civilians and POWs to undertake forced labour in Japan and its occupied territories where they suffered harsh treatment and high death rates. Up to 200,000 Korean and Chinese women were forced into sex slavery.

While troops of all the major belligerents committed rapes, the rape of civilians was particularly widespread among the German, Japanese and Soviet military. The post-war international military tribunals found the evidence of rape by German forces "overwhelming". Twenty-nine Japanese defendants, mostly generals, were convicted of complicity in mass rape. Soviet soldiers also committed mass rapes in occupied territories, especially Germany.

War crimes were committed by the Western Allied powers, but not on the same scale as those of the Axis powers and the Soviet Union. The Western Allies prosecuted a number of war crimes committed by their own forces, but Allied war crimes were not prosecuted by the post-war international military tribunals. There has been continued debate over whether the [area bombing](/wiki/Area_bombardment) of cities in Germany and Japan, and the [atomic bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki](/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki), were war crimes.

### Occupation

Main articles: [German-occupied Europe](/wiki/German-occupied_Europe), [Resistance during World War II](/wiki/Resistance_during_World_War_II), [Collaboration with Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy](/wiki/Collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany_and_Fascist_Italy), [Collaboration with the Empire of Japan](/wiki/Collaboration_with_the_Empire_of_Japan), and [Nazi plunder](/wiki/Nazi_plunder)

Polish civilians wearing blindfolds photographed just before being massacred by German soldiers in [Palmiry forest](/wiki/Palmiry_massacre), 1940

In Europe, occupation came under two forms. In Western, Northern, and Central Europe (France, Norway, Denmark, the Low Countries, and the [annexed portions of Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia)) Germany established economic policies through which it collected roughly 69.5 billion reichsmarks (27.8 billion US dollars) by the end of the war; this figure does not include the [plunder](/wiki/Nazi_plunder) of industrial products, military equipment, raw materials and other goods. Thus, the income from occupied nations was over 40 percent of the income Germany collected from taxation, a figure which increased to nearly 40 percent of total German income as the war went on.

In the East, the intended gains of *[Lebensraum](/wiki/Lebensraum)* were never attained as fluctuating front-lines and Soviet [scorched earth](/wiki/Scorched_earth) policies denied resources to the German invaders. Unlike in the West, the [Nazi racial policy](/wiki/Racial_policy_of_Nazi_Germany) encouraged extreme brutality against what it considered to be the "[inferior people](/wiki/Untermensch)" of [Slavic](/wiki/Slavic_peoples) descent; most German advances were thus followed by [mass atrocities and war crimes](/wiki/Generalplan_Ost). The Nazis [killed an estimated 2.8 million ethnic Poles](/wiki/Nazi_crimes_against_the_Polish_nation) in addition to Polish-Jewish [victims of the Holocaust](/wiki/Victims_of_the_Holocaust). Although by 1942 [resistance groups](/wiki/Resistance_during_World_War_II) formed in most occupied territories, the assessments of the effectiveness of [Soviet partisans](/wiki/Soviet_partisans) and [French Resistance](/wiki/French_Resistance) suggests that they did not significantly hamper German operations until late 1943.

[Soviet partisans](/wiki/Soviet_partisans) hanged by the German army. The [Russian Academy of Sciences](/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) reported in 1995 that [civilian victims in the Soviet Union](/wiki/World_War_II_casualties_of_the_Soviet_Union) at German hands totalled 13.7 million dead, twenty percent of the 68 million people in the occupied Soviet Union.

In Asia, Japan termed nations under its occupation as being part of the [Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere](/wiki/Greater_East_Asia_Co-Prosperity_Sphere), essentially a Japanese [hegemony](/wiki/Hegemony) which it claimed was for purposes of liberating colonised peoples. Although Japanese forces were sometimes welcomed as liberators from European domination, [Japanese war crimes](/wiki/Japanese_war_crimes) frequently turned local public opinion against them. During Japan's initial conquest, it captured 4,000,000 barrels (640,000 m3) of oil (~550,000 tonnes) left behind by retreating Allied forces; and by 1943, was able to get production in the Dutch East Indies up to 50 million barrels (7,900,000 m3) of oil (~6.8 million tonnes), 76 percent of its 1940 output rate.

### Home fronts and production

Main articles: [Military production during World War II](/wiki/Military_production_during_World_War_II) and [Home front during World War II](/wiki/Home_front_during_World_War_II)

Allies to Axis GDP ratio throughout the war

In the 1930s, Britain and the United States together controlled almost 75% of world mineral output—essential for projecting military power.

In Europe, before the outbreak of the war, the Allies had significant advantages in both population and economics. In 1938, the Western Allies (United Kingdom, France, Poland and the British Dominions) had a 30 percent larger population and a 30 percent higher gross domestic product than the European Axis powers (Germany and Italy); including colonies, the Allies had more than a 5:1 advantage in population and a nearly 2:1 advantage in GDP. In Asia at the same time, China had roughly six times the population of Japan but only an 89 percent higher GDP; this reduces to three times the population and only a 38 percent higher GDP if Japanese colonies are included.

The United States produced about two-thirds of all munitions used by the Allies in World War II, including warships, transports, warplanes, artillery, tanks, trucks, and ammunition. Although the Allies' economic and population advantages were largely mitigated during the initial rapid blitzkrieg attacks of Germany and Japan, they became the decisive factor by 1942, after the United States and Soviet Union joined the Allies and the war evolved into one of [attrition](/wiki/Attrition_warfare). While the Allies' ability to out-produce the Axis was partly due to more access to natural resources, other factors, such as Germany and Japan's reluctance to employ women in the [labour force](/wiki/Workforce), Allied [strategic bombing](/wiki/Strategic_bombing_during_World_War_II), and Germany's late shift to a [war economy](/wiki/War_economy) contributed significantly. Additionally, neither Germany nor Japan planned to fight a protracted war, and had not equipped themselves to do so. Germany, Japan and the Soviet Union used millions of [slave labourers](/wiki/Slavery) in war-related industries.

### Advances in technology and its application

Main article: [Technology during World War II](/wiki/Technology_during_World_War_II)

A [V-2 rocket](/wiki/V-2_rocket) launched from a fixed site in [Peenemünde](/wiki/Peenem%C3%BCnde), 21 June 1943

Aircraft were used for [reconnaissance](/wiki/Reconnaissance_aircraft), as [fighters](/wiki/Fighter_aircraft), [bombers](/wiki/Bomber), and [ground-support](/wiki/Close_air_support), and each role developed considerably. Innovations included [airlift](/wiki/Airlift) (the capability to quickly move limited high-priority supplies, equipment, and personnel); and [strategic bombing](/wiki/Strategic_bombing) (the bombing of enemy industrial and population centres to destroy the enemy's ability to wage war). [Anti-aircraft weaponry](/wiki/Anti-aircraft_warfare) also advanced, including defences such as [radar](/wiki/Radar) and surface-to-air artillery, in particular the introduction of the [proximity fuze](/wiki/Proximity_fuze). The use of the [jet aircraft](/wiki/Jet_aircraft) was pioneered and led to jets becoming standard in air forces worldwide.

Advances were made in nearly every aspect of [naval warfare](/wiki/Naval_warfare), most notably with [aircraft carriers](/wiki/Aircraft_carrier) and [submarines](/wiki/Submarine). Although [aeronautical](/wiki/Aeronautics) warfare had relatively little success at the start of the war, [actions at Taranto](/wiki/Battle_of_Taranto), [Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), and the [Coral Sea](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Coral_Sea) established the carrier as the dominant capital ship (in place of the battleship). In the Atlantic, [escort carriers](/wiki/Escort_carrier) became a vital part of Allied convoys, increasing the effective protection radius and helping to close the [Mid-Atlantic gap](/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_gap). Carriers were also more economical than [battleships](/wiki/Battleship) due to the relatively low cost of aircraft and because they are not required to be as heavily armoured. Submarines, which had proved to be an effective weapon during the [First World War](/wiki/World_War_I), were expected by all combatants to be important in the second. The British focused development on [anti-submarine](/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) [weaponry](/wiki/Anti-submarine_weapon) and tactics, such as [sonar](/wiki/Sonar) and convoys, while Germany focused on improving its offensive capability, with designs such as the [Type VII submarine](/wiki/Type_VII_submarine) and [wolfpack](/wiki/Wolfpack_(naval_tactic)) tactics. Gradually, improving Allied technologies such as the [Leigh Light](/wiki/Leigh_Light), [Hedgehog](/wiki/Hedgehog_(weapon)), [Squid](/wiki/Squid_(weapon)), and [homing torpedoes](/wiki/Mark_24_mine) proved effective against German submarines.

Nuclear *Gadget* being raised to the top of the detonation "shot tower", at [Alamogordo Bombing Range](/wiki/Alamogordo_Bombing_and_Gunnery_Range); [Trinity nuclear test](/wiki/Trinity_(nuclear_test)), [New Mexico](/wiki/New_Mexico), July 1945

[Land warfare](/wiki/Land_warfare) changed from the static frontlines of [trench warfare](/wiki/Trench_warfare) of World War I, which had relied on improved [artillery](/wiki/Artillery) that outmatched the speed of both [infantry](/wiki/Infantry) and [cavalry](/wiki/Cavalry), to increased mobility and [combined arms](/wiki/Combined_arms). The [tank](/wiki/Tank), which had been used predominantly for infantry support in the First World War, had evolved into the primary weapon. In the late 1930s, tank design was considerably more advanced than it had been during World War I, and [advances continued throughout the war](/wiki/Tanks_in_World_War_II) with increases in speed, armour and firepower. At the start of the war, most commanders thought enemy tanks should be met by tanks with superior specifications. This idea was challenged by the poor performance of the relatively light early tank guns against armour, and German doctrine of avoiding tank-versus-tank combat. This, along with Germany's use of combined arms, were among the key elements of their highly successful blitzkrieg tactics across Poland and France. Many means of [destroying tanks](/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare), including [indirect artillery](/wiki/Indirect_fire), [anti-tank guns](/wiki/Anti-tank_gun) (both towed and [self-propelled](/wiki/Self-propelled_artillery)), [mines](/wiki/Anti-tank_mine), short-ranged infantry antitank weapons, and other tanks were used. Even with large-scale mechanisation, infantry remained the backbone of all forces, and throughout the war, most infantry were equipped similarly to World War I. The portable machine gun spread, a notable example being the German [MG 34](/wiki/MG_34), and various [submachine guns](/wiki/Submachine_gun) which were suited to [close combat](/wiki/Close_combat) in urban and jungle settings. The [assault rifle](/wiki/Assault_rifle), a late war development incorporating many features of the rifle and submachine gun, became the standard post-war infantry weapon for most armed forces.

Most major belligerents attempted to solve the problems of complexity and security involved in using large [codebooks](/wiki/Codebook) for [cryptography](/wiki/Cryptography) by designing [ciphering](/wiki/Cipher) machines, the most well-known being the German [Enigma machine](/wiki/Enigma_machine). Development of [SIGINT](/wiki/SIGINT) (*sig*nals *int*elligence) and [cryptanalysis](/wiki/Cryptanalysis) enabled the countering process of decryption. Notable examples were the Allied decryption of [Japanese naval codes](/wiki/Japanese_naval_codes) and British [Ultra](/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography)), a [pioneering method](/wiki/Bombe#The_British_Bombe) for decoding Enigma that benefited from information given to the United Kingdom by the [Polish Cipher Bureau](/wiki/Cipher_Bureau_(Poland)#Gift_to_allies), which had been decoding early versions of Enigma before the war. Another component of [military intelligence](/wiki/Military_intelligence) was [deception](/wiki/Deception), which the Allies used to great effect in operations such as [Mincemeat](/wiki/Operation_Mincemeat) and [Bodyguard](/wiki/Operation_Bodyguard).

Other technological and engineering feats achieved during, or as a result of, the war include the world's first programmable computers ([Z3](/wiki/Z3_(computer)), [Colossus](/wiki/Colossus_computer), and [ENIAC](/wiki/ENIAC)), [guided missiles](/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb) and [modern rockets](/wiki/V-2_rocket), the [Manhattan Project](/wiki/Manhattan_Project)'s development of [nuclear weapons](/wiki/Nuclear_weapon), [operations research](/wiki/Operations_research), the development of [artificial harbours](/wiki/Mulberry_harbour), and [oil pipelines under the English Channel](/wiki/Operation_Pluto). Although [penicillin](/wiki/Penicillin) was discovered before the war, the [development](/wiki/History_of_penicillin) of industrial production technology as well as the mass production and use began during the war.

# 1972 Winter Olympics

The **1972 Winter Olympics**, officially the **XI Olympic Winter Games** and commonly known as **Sapporo 1972**, were a winter [multi-sport event](/wiki/Multi-sport_event) held from February 3 to 13, 1972, in [Sapporo](/wiki/Sapporo), Hokkaido Prefecture, Japan. It was the first [Winter Olympic Games](/wiki/Winter_Olympic_Games) to take place outside Europe and North America.

## Host city selection

Main article: [Bids for the 1972 Winter Olympics](/wiki/Bids_for_the_1972_Winter_Olympics)

Sapporo first won the rights to host the [1940 Winter Olympics](/wiki/1940_Winter_Olympics), but Japan resigned as the Games' host after its [1937 invasion of China](/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War). The 1940 Games were later cancelled. All the cities awarded Games that were cancelled due to war have since hosted the Games ([London](/wiki/London), [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo), [Helsinki](/wiki/Helsinki), [Sapporo](/wiki/Sapporo) and [Cortina d'Ampezzo](/wiki/Cortina_d%27Ampezzo)).

Sapporo competed with Banff, Lahti, and Salt Lake City. The Games were awarded at the 64th [IOC Session](/wiki/IOC_Session) in Rome, Italy, on April 26, 1966.

In preparation, the Japanese constructed new largescale facilities at Sapporo and conducted a trial run a full year in advance of the Games. An international sport week was held in February, 1971, to assess the city's preparations as well as "to test its civic mettle and hospitality", and this effort was acclaimed by Olympic observers as "a complete success". The development of new infrastructure proved to be a huge boon for the Sapporo economy: by the time of the Games, the national government had invested some US$500 million in upgrades, including a new subway. The Games' organizers themselves turned a healthy profit in part because they arranged a record $8.47 million for broadcasting rights with American companies.

## Highlights

Official poster for the 1972 Winter Olympics

- [Emperor Hirohito](/wiki/Hirohito) became the third dignitary to open the Olympic Games twice (first time in [summer 1964](/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics)), after [Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) had done in [winter](/wiki/1936_Winter_Olympics) and [summer](/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) 1936, and [Giovanni Gronchi](/wiki/Giovanni_Gronchi) in [winter 1956](/wiki/1956_Winter_Olympics) and [summer 1960](/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics).
- Prior to these games, Japan had never won a gold medal, and had won only one medal (silver by [Chiharu Igaya](/wiki/Chiharu_Igaya) in 1956) overall, in the Winter Olympics. The host country's fans in Sapporo were boosted when three Japanese athletes, led by [Yukio Kasaya](/wiki/Yukio_Kasaya), swept the [ski jumping](/wiki/Ski_jumping) 70 m (current K-90 normal hill) event for gold (Kasaya), silver ([Akitsugu Konno](/wiki/Akitsugu_Konno)), and bronze ([Seiji Aochi](/wiki/Seiji_Aochi)); those would also be the only medals Japan would earn in these Olympics.
- [Galina Kulakova](/wiki/Galina_Kulakova) of the USSR won all three cross-country skiing events for women.
- Dutch skater [Ard Schenk](/wiki/Ard_Schenk) won three gold medals in speed skating.
- In Women's Alpine skiing, American [Barbara Cochran](/wiki/Barbara_Cochran), one of three siblings on the U.S. Ski Team, became the first U.S. woman since [Andrea Mead Lawrence](/wiki/Andrea_Mead_Lawrence) to win a gold medal in skiing when she took first place in the [slalom](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1972_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_slalom).
- In Alpine skiing, virtual unknown Swiss [Marie-Thérès Nadig](/wiki/Marie-Theres_Nadig) won both the [downhill](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1972_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_downhill) and the [giant slalom](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1972_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_giant_slalom) events.
- [Magnar Solberg](/wiki/Magnar_Solberg) from Norway was the first repeat winner in the individual 20 km biathlon event, having first won in [Grenoble](/wiki/Biathlon_at_the_1968_Winter_Olympics).
- Spain scored its first Winter gold medal courtesy of [slalom](/wiki/Slalom_skiing) skier [Francisco Fernández Ochoa](/wiki/Francisco_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Ochoa). Poland did the same with [Wojciech Fortuna](/wiki/Wojciech_Fortuna) winning the large hill [ski jumping](/wiki/Ski_jumping) competition.
- American female speedskaters [Anne Henning](/wiki/Anne_Henning) (16-year-old) and [Dianne Holum](/wiki/Dianne_Holum) made the United States' best showing in the Winter Games, winning two gold, a silver, and a bronze.
- Three days before the Games, controversy over amateur status arose when IOC president [Avery Brundage](/wiki/Avery_Brundage) threatened to disqualify 40 alpine skiers who received endorsement and other deals. Austrian skier [Karl Schranz](/wiki/Karl_Schranz), who received over $50,000 per year from ski manufacturers, was banned as an example. Meanwhile, Canada refused to send an ice hockey team, maintaining that professional ice hockey players from Communist nations were allowed to compete with no restrictions.
- On an historical note, these Games were the last where a skier won a gold medal using all-wooden skis. Since this time, top-level cross-country skiers have used skis made mostly of fibreglass synthetics.
- In female [Figure skating](/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_1972_Winter_Olympics) event, American skater [Janet Lynn](/wiki/Janet_Lynn) won not only a bronze medal, but also tremendous popularity among Japanese audiences because of her artistic free program, as to make appearance on the cover of "Olympic Winter Games, Sapporo 1972" photo books published in Japan, and even on Japanese TV commercials later.
- Luge had its only tie in the history of the Winter Olympics in the men's doubles event.

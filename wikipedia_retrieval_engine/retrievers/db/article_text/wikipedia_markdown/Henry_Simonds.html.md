**Henry John Simonds** [JP](/wiki/Justice_of_the_Peace#United_Kingdom) (23 March 1828 – 25 January 1896) was an [English](/wiki/English_people) first-class [cricketer](/wiki/Cricket) and barrister.

The son of William May Simonds, he was born at [Caversham](/wiki/Caversham,_Reading) in March 1828. He was educated at [Eton College](/wiki/Eton_College), before going up to [King's College, Cambridge](/wiki/King%27s_College,_Cambridge). While studying at Cambridge, he played two [first-class cricket](/wiki/First-class_cricket) matches for [Cambridge University Cricket Club](/wiki/Cambridge_University_Cricket_Club), against [Cambridge Town Club](/wiki/Cambridge_Town_Club) at [Fenner's](/wiki/Fenner%27s) in 1849 and in [The University Match](/wiki/The_University_Match_(cricket)) against [Oxford University](/wiki/Oxford_University_Cricket_Club) at [Oxford](/wiki/Magdalen_Ground) in 1850. He had little success in these two matches, scoring just 5 runs. He was a [fellow](/wiki/Fellow) of King's College from 1849 to 1858.

A student of the [Inner Temple](/wiki/Inner_Temple), he was [called to the bar](/wiki/Call_to_the_bar#England_and_Wales) in January 1853 to practice on the [Western Circuit](/wiki/Circuit_court#England_and_Wales). He was appointed a [justice of the peace](/wiki/Justice_of_the_peace#England_and_Wales) for [Oxfordshire](/wiki/Oxfordshire) in January 1868. He was a partner in the family brewing business, [H & G Simonds Ltd](/wiki/H_%26_G_Simonds_Ltd), which was named by his father after him and his brother, George. He was active in local politics and was a Chairman of his local [Conservative Association](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)). Simonds died at his residence in Caversham in January 1896.

**Kelly Michael Buchberger** (born December 2, 1966) is a Canadian professional [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) coach and former player. He played for several [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) teams from 1986 to 2004. As a coach, he has been an assistant for the [New York Islanders](/wiki/New_York_Islanders) of the [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) (NHL) and a head coach of the [Tri City Americans](/wiki/Tri_City_Americans) of the [Western Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Hockey_League) (WHL).

## Playing career

Buchberger has played for the [Edmonton Oilers](/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers), [Atlanta Thrashers](/wiki/Atlanta_Thrashers), [Los Angeles Kings](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings), [Phoenix Coyotes](/wiki/Phoenix_Coyotes), and the [Pittsburgh Penguins](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins). He also played minor hockey with the [Western Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Hockey_League) [Moose Jaw Warriors](/wiki/Moose_Jaw_Warriors) and pro hockey with the [American Hockey League](/wiki/American_Hockey_League) [Nova Scotia Oilers](/wiki/Nova_Scotia_Oilers).

He was drafted in the ninth round by the Edmonton Oilers in the [1985 NHL entry draft](/wiki/1985_NHL_entry_draft), making him the 188th overall pick. He made his NHL debut in 1987 Stanley Cup finals. During his playing career, he was known best for his gritty play and leadership, having captained the Oilers for four years as the team's 9th leader in franchise history. He won the [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup) twice with Edmonton, in [1987](/wiki/1987_Stanley_Cup_Final) and [1990](/wiki/1990_Stanley_Cup_Final).

Buchberger was the last remaining active member of the Oilers' roster to have been on one of their five Stanley Cup-winning teams, along with [Marty McSorley](/wiki/Marty_McSorley). He remained with the Oilers until 1999, when he was selected by the Atlanta Thrashers in the [1999 NHL Expansion Draft](/wiki/1999_NHL_Expansion_Draft).

## Coaching career

After retiring, Buchberger was an assistant coach with the AHL [Edmonton Road Runners](/wiki/Edmonton_Road_Runners) team in 2004–05. He then joined the Oilers management as a development coach. On August 3, 2007, he was named head coach of the Oilers' American Hockey League affiliate, the [Springfield Falcons](/wiki/Springfield_Falcons), and guided the team to a 35–35–10 record, the team's first .500 season since 1998–99. Buchberger then was promoted to the Edmonton Oilers during the 2008 offseason, becoming an assistant coach with them. On June 10, 2014, he was reassigned to the role of player personnel and replaced as assistant coach by [Craig Ramsay](/wiki/Craig_Ramsay).

On July 11, 2017, Buchberger was hired as the assistant coach by the [New York Islanders](/wiki/New_York_Islanders). In 2018, he was named the head coach of the [Tri-City Americans](/wiki/Tri-City_Americans) in the [Western Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Hockey_League). In 2021, his contract with the Americans was not renewed.

Buchberger was hired as the first general manager and head coach of the [Devon Xtreme](/wiki/Devon_Xtreme), a [junior ice hockey](/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey) expansion franchise to debut in the [Alberta Junior Hockey League](/wiki/Alberta_Junior_Hockey_League) in the 2024–25 season.

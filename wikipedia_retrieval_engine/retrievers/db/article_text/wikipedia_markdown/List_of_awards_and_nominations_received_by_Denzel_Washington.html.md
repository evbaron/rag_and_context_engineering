American actor, producer, and director [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington) has received numerous accolades including two [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards), an [Actor Award](/wiki/Actor_Awards), two [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and a [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) as well as nominations for a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award), and two [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards). Over his career, he has received numerous honorary awards including the [BAFTA Los Angeles Britannia Award](/wiki/Britannia_Awards) in 2007, the [Golden Globe Cecil B. DeMille Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Cecil_B._DeMille_Award) in 2016, and the [AFI Life Achievement Award](/wiki/AFI_Life_Achievement_Award) in 2019. He was supposed to be awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom) by [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) in 2022, but he didn't receive it until 2025.

Washington has received ten [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) nominations winning once for [Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) for his portrayal of a [soldier](/wiki/American_Civil_War) in the [Edward Zwick](/wiki/Edward_Zwick) [Civil War](/wiki/American_Civil_War) film *[Glory](/wiki/Glory_(1989_film))* (1989), and [Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) for playing a corrupt cop in [Antoine Fuqua](/wiki/Antoine_Fuqua)'s crime thriller *[Training Day](/wiki/Training_Day)* (2001). He was Oscar-nominated for playing [Steve Biko](/wiki/Steve_Biko) in the historical epic *[Cry Freedom](/wiki/Cry_Freedom)* (1987), the [title role](/wiki/Malcolm_X) in the biographical drama *[Malcolm X](/wiki/Malcolm_X_(1992_film))* (1992), [Rubin Carter](/wiki/Rubin_Carter) in the [sports drama](/wiki/Sports_drama) *[The Hurricane](/wiki/The_Hurricane_(1999_film))* (1999), an [Airline pilot](/wiki/Airline_pilot) fighting [substance abuse](/wiki/Substance_abuse) in *[Flight](/wiki/Flight_(2012_film))* (2012), a working class family man in the [period drama](/wiki/Period_drama) *[Fences](/wiki/Fences_(film))* (2015), a [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) lawyer in the [legal drama](/wiki/Legal_drama) *[Roman J. Israel, Esq.](/wiki/Roman_J._Israel,_Esq.)* (2017), and [Macbeth](/wiki/Macbeth) in the thriller *[The Tragedy of Macbeth](/wiki/The_Tragedy_of_Macbeth_(2021_film))* (2021). Washington was also nominated as a producer for *[Fences](/wiki/Fences_(film))*.

Washington is one of nine actors who has been nominated for an acting Academy Award in five different decades (1980s, '90s. 2000s, '10s and '20s), joining [Laurence Olivier](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier), [Katharine Hepburn](/wiki/Katharine_Hepburn), [Paul Newman](/wiki/Paul_Newman), [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson), [Michael Caine](/wiki/Michael_Caine), [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep), [Frances McDormand](/wiki/Frances_McDormand) and [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro). He has received nine [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nominations winning twice for his performances in *Glory* (1989) and *[The Hurricane](/wiki/The_Hurricane_(1999_film))* (1999). He has also received seven [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) nominations winning for [Outstanding Actor in a Leading Role](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) for his performance in the film adaptation of *[Fences](/wiki/Fences_(film))* (2015).

For his work on stage he received the [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Play) for his role as Troy Maxon in the [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) revival of the [August Wilson](/wiki/August_Wilson) play *[Fences](/wiki/Fences_(play))* (2010). He was Tony-nominated for playing Hickey, a hardware salesman, in the revival of the [Eugene O'Neill](/wiki/Eugene_O%27Neill) play *[The Iceman Cometh](/wiki/The_Iceman_Cometh)* (2018). He received two [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nominations for his work with documentary series on the baseball player [Hank Aaron](/wiki/Hank_Aaron) and photographer [Gordon Parks](/wiki/Gordon_Parks). He was nominated for the [Grammy Award for Best Spoken Word Album for Children](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Spoken_Word_Album_for_Children) for the folk tale, *[John Henry](/wiki/John_Henry_(folklore))* in 1996.

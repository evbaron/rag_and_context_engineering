**Jackson Hole Mountain Resort** (**JHMR**) is a [ski resort](/wiki/Ski_resort) in the [western](/wiki/Western_United_States) [United States](/wiki/United_States) in [Teton Village, Wyoming](/wiki/Teton_Village,_Wyoming). It is in the [Teton Range](/wiki/Teton_Range) of the [Rocky Mountains](/wiki/Rocky_Mountains), twelve miles (20 km) northwest of [Jackson](/wiki/Jackson,_Wyoming) and due south of [Grand Teton National Park](/wiki/Grand_Teton_National_Park).

It is named after the historically significant [Jackson Hole](/wiki/Jackson_Hole) valley and is known for its steep terrain and large continuous vertical drop of 4,139 ft (1,262 m). JHMR appears frequently in the media as one of [North America](/wiki/North_America)'s most expensive ski resorts.

## Ski area information

The ski area partially covers [Rendezvous](/wiki/Rendezvous_Mountain) and Apres Vous Mountains and is known for its challenging terrain, including the infamous [Corbet's Couloir](/wiki/Corbet%27s_Couloir). Half of the terrain is rated expert, 40% intermediate, and only 10% beginner. The intermediate terrain is primarily on south-facing Apres Vous Mountain, while Rendezvous Mountain has Jackson Hole's more advanced terrain, including bowls, glades, and chutes. At over 4,000 vertical feet of skiing, Jackson Hole boasts one of the greatest continuous inbounds rises in the U.S, after nearby [Big Sky](/wiki/Big_Sky_Resort), [Montana](/wiki/Montana), which has an overall vertical of 4,350 feet (1,326 m), but its continuous vertical is 700 feet (213 m) less; and [Snowmass](/wiki/Snowmass_Ski_Area) in [Colorado](/wiki/Colorado), which has a vertical drop of 4,406 feet (1,343 m)). [Timberline Lodge ski area](/wiki/Timberline_Lodge_ski_area) on Mount Hood, East of Portland Oregon has the greatest vertical drop of any ski area in the United States at 4,540'. The Upper elevation is 8,540 and the lowest elevation is 4,000.

In addition to the skiable terrain in-bounds, there is an even larger area to be explored [off-piste](/wiki/Off-piste) (out of bounds). These areas are accessed through marked gates by expert skiers/boarders who are equipped with avalanche safety gear.

Jackson Hole's original [aerial tram](/wiki/Aerial_tramway) was closed to the public in the spring of 2006 and replaced with a new tram that opened in 2008. The tram's vertical rise is 4,139 feet (1,262 m) to an elevation of 10,450 feet (3,185 m) above [sea level](/wiki/Sea_level). Construction on the new, 100-passenger [Doppelmayr CTEC](/wiki/Doppelmayr_CTEC) tram began the day after the Resort closed for the 2006-2007 ski season. Service began on December 20, 2008. During the two seasons without a tram, a temporary double chairlift named East Ridge was built to service the runs at the top of Rendezvous Mountain. This lift was subsequently moved and renamed the Marmot Chair, which provides access from the base of the Thunder Lift to the Bridger Gondola summit.

In the summer, the resort offers numerous activities such as mountain biking, hiking, paragliding, bungee trampoline, ropes course, rock climbing, and the Via ferrata in Casper Bowl at the top of the Bridger gondola. The resort and region is served by the [Jackson Hole Airport](/wiki/Jackson_Hole_Airport) KJAC, located 11 miles north east of Teton Village and offers seasonal service to 20 cities across the United States.

Before 1961, the area of the future resort was the Crystal Springs [Girl Scout](/wiki/Girl_Scouts_of_the_USA) Ranch. Paul McCollister purchased the ranch and formed the Jackson Hole Ski Corporation in 1963 with partners Alex Morley and Gordon Graham. Construction began a year later, and Apres Vous mountain opened to the public the 1965-66 winter with 3 double chairlifts. Eagles rest, Teewinot, and Apres Vous. The original tram on Rendezvous opened on July 31, 1966; with capacity for 62 people and 1 conductor. It took between 8–12 minutes, depending on speed set, to reach the summit of Rendezvous Mountain at 10,450 ft. The Aerial Tram officially opened to the skiing public Winter Season 1966/67. The Resort opened winter of 1965/66 and reigning [Olympic](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1964_Winter_Olympics) [gold medalist](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_1964_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_slalom) [Josef "Pepi" Stiegler](/wiki/Josef_Stiegler) of Austria was hired that same year as ski school director. In 1992, McCollister sold his interests in the resort to [John Kemmerer III](/wiki/Jay_Kemmerer). From there, the Kemmerer Family made multiple new changes to the resort, including new lifts, hotels, and new ski runs.

Jackson Hole hosted [World Cup](/wiki/FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) ski races in the inaugural [1967](/wiki/1967_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) season, and again in [1970](/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) and [1975](/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup). The most recent races in 1975 were [downhills](/wiki/Downhill_(ski_competition)), won by [Franz Klammer](/wiki/Franz_Klammer) and [Marie-Theres Nadig](/wiki/Marie-Theres_Nadig). The first national Powder 8 Championship was held at Jackson Hole in 1970.

## Avalanches

Jackson Hole was the site of two in-bounds [avalanches](/wiki/Avalanche) in late 2008, first on December 27 and another two days later on December 29. The first avalanche resulted in the death of skier David Nodine, one of three in-bound deaths in the American West in the 2008-09 ski season, the most since three skiers were killed at [Alpine Meadows](/wiki/Alpine_Meadows,_California) in 1976. The second avalanche occurred in the Headwall area and buried part of the Bridger Restaurant but resulted in no injuries.

**Archibald Robertson** (29 June 1853 – 29 January 1931) was the seventh [Principal of King's College London](/wiki/List_of_Principals_of_King%27s_College_London) who later served as [Bishop of Exeter](/wiki/Bishop_of_Exeter).

## Early life and education

He was born at [Sywell](/wiki/Sywell) rectory, [Northamptonshire](/wiki/Northamptonshire), the eldest son of George Samuel Robertson, curate of Sywell, (1825–1874) and his wife, Helen née Kerr, and grandson of [Archibald Robertson](/wiki/Archibald_Robertson_(physician)) and William Charles Kerr *junior*, both physicians of Northampton. He was educated at [Bradfield College](/wiki/Bradfield_College) and [Trinity College, Oxford](/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford), where he graduated in 1876 with a [first class degree](/wiki/First_class_degree) in Classics ([Lit. Hum.](/wiki/Literae_Humaniores)). He became a Fellow of Trinity in 1876 (until 1886), Dean of the same (1879–1883), and a [Doctor of Divinity](/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity) (DD). He was ordained (both times by [John Mackarness](/wiki/John_Mackarness), [Bishop of Oxford](/wiki/Bishop_of_Oxford)): a deacon on [Trinity Sunday](/wiki/Trinity_Sunday) (16 June) 1878 in [Cuddesdon Parish Church](/wiki/Cuddesdon_Parish_Church); and a priest on [St Thomas's](/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle) day (21 December) 1882 in [Christ Church](/wiki/Christ_Church_Cathedral,_Oxford).

## Career

Portrait of Robertson as Bishop of Exeter

From 1883 to 1897 he was Principal of [Bishop Hatfield's Hall](/wiki/Hatfield_College,_Durham) in Durham. He went on to serve as [Principal of King's College London](/wiki/List_of_Principals_of_King%27s_College_London) from 1897 to 1903, during which he was elected to serve as [Vice-Chancellor of the University of London](/wiki/List_of_Vice-Chancellors_of_the_University_of_London) for the year 1902–1903. He also served as an [examining chaplain](/wiki/Examining_chaplain) for [Forrest Browne](/wiki/Forrest_Browne), [Bishop of Bristol](/wiki/Bishop_of_Bristol), in 1897, and became a [Fellow of King's College](/wiki/Fellow_of_King%27s_College) in 1899. He received an honorary doctorate (Doctor of Divinity, DD) from Durham University in 1893 and a further one ([Legum Doctor](/wiki/Legum_Doctor), LLD) from the [University of Glasgow](/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) in June 1901. He was [Boyle Lecturer](/wiki/Boyle_Lecturer) in 1900 and [Bampton Lecturer](/wiki/Bampton_Lecturer) in 1901, became an honorary fellow of Trinity in 1903 and was a Vice-President of the [Clan-Donnachaid Society](/wiki/Clan_Robertson). He was also briefly Governor of [Rugby School](/wiki/Rugby_School) in 1903.

In his academic career, Robertson was a specialist in [patristics](/wiki/Patristics) and [church history](/wiki/Church_history), in which field he was widely published and respected. He was elected [Bishop of Exeter](/wiki/Bishop_of_Exeter) on Easter Monday (13 April) 1903, consecrated (ordained) as a bishop on 1 May (by [Randall Davidson](/wiki/Randall_Davidson), [Archbishop of Canterbury](/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury), at [St Paul's Cathedral](/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cathedral)) and enthroned and installed at [Exeter Cathedral](/wiki/Exeter_Cathedral) on 5 May; he legally took up the See upon the [confirmation of his election](/wiki/Confirmation_of_bishops), which took place between his election and consecration (i.e. during April 1903). In 1912 Lollards Tower on the Exeter City Wall was rebuilt and several sculpted stone tablets displaying the arms of Bishop Robertson were set into the walls, including one over the arched entrance known as Bishop Carey's Postern. He served as diocesan bishop until 1916, when he resigned due to ill health, retiring to [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford), where he died at home in 1931.

Robertson was strongly in favour of Great Britain's declaration of War against Germany in August, 1914. He believed that Germany's ultimate aim was to destroy Great Britain. "Whether it pleases God to give us victory or not, we believe we are fighting for the fraternity and equal right of nations, against the claim that [might is right](/wiki/Might_is_right)! We are, furthermore, fighting in self-defence." Although frequently absent from his post because of ill-health, Robertson prepared a Briefing Note for clergy to teach parishioners about the reasons justifying the War, encouraged prayers for animals such as horses used by cavalry and artillery and praised clergy whose families were active participants. An especial mention was made of the Rector of [Dartington](/wiki/Dartington), 5 of whose 7 sons were in the army, the sixth had been in the Royal Navy and the youngest was still at school but in the Officers Training Corps.

## Family

Robertson married in 1885, shortly after his arrival in Durham. His wife was Julia Mann, daughter of Charles, Rector of [Mawgan-in-Meneage](/wiki/Mawgan-in-Meneage) and [St Issey](/wiki/St_Issey), [Cornwall](/wiki/Cornwall). They had three sons. The eldest of these, of the same name, was the communist and atheist [Archibald Robertson (atheist)](/wiki/Archibald_Robertson_(atheist)) (1886–1961).

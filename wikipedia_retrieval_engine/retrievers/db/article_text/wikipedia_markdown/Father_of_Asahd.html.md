***Father of Asahd*** is the eleventh studio album by American [disc jockey](/wiki/Disc_jockey) and record producer [DJ Khaled](/wiki/DJ_Khaled). It was released on May 17, 2019, by [We the Best Music Group](/wiki/We_the_Best_Music_Group) and [Epic Records](/wiki/Epic_Records). It features the singles "[Top Off](/wiki/Top_Off)" and "[No Brainer](/wiki/No_Brainer_(song))." On May 16, DJ Khaled revealed the track listing and features, as well as that the music videos for "[Higher](/wiki/Higher_(DJ_Khaled_song))", "[Just Us](/wiki/Just_Us_(DJ_Khaled_song))", "Celebrate", "Jealous" and "Holy Mountain" would be released throughout the day of May 17, followed by the video for "[You Stay](/wiki/You_Stay)" on May 18, and "[Wish Wish](/wiki/Wish_Wish)" and "Weather the Storm" on May 20. DJ Khaled scored his third career [Grammy](/wiki/Grammy_Award) nomination with "Higher" which won the [Best Rap/Sung Performance](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap/Sung_Performance) at the [62nd Grammy Awards](/wiki/62nd_Annual_Grammy_Awards). The album also features guest appearances from [Buju Banton](/wiki/Buju_Banton), [Sizzla](/wiki/Sizzla), [Mavado](/wiki/Mavado_(singer)), [070 Shake](/wiki/070_Shake), [Cardi B](/wiki/Cardi_B), [21 Savage](/wiki/21_Savage), [Chris Brown](/wiki/Chris_Brown), [Lil Wayne](/wiki/Lil_Wayne), [Big Sean](/wiki/Big_Sean), [SZA](/wiki/SZA), [Meek Mill](/wiki/Meek_Mill), [J Balvin](/wiki/J_Balvin), [Lil Baby](/wiki/Lil_Baby), [Jeremih](/wiki/Jeremih), [Travis Scott](/wiki/Travis_Scott), [Post Malone](/wiki/Post_Malone), [Nipsey Hussle](/wiki/Nipsey_Hussle), [John Legend](/wiki/John_Legend), [Nas](/wiki/Nas), [CeeLo Green](/wiki/CeeLo_Green), [Jeezy](/wiki/Jeezy), [Rick Ross](/wiki/Rick_Ross), [Gunna](/wiki/Gunna_(rapper)), [Jay-Z](/wiki/Jay-Z), [Future](/wiki/Future_(rapper)), [Beyoncé](/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9), [Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber), [Chance the Rapper](/wiki/Chance_the_Rapper), and [Quavo](/wiki/Quavo).

## Background

In March 2018, DJ Khaled announced the title, upon releasing the lead single, called "[Top Off](/wiki/Top_Off)". He named the album [after his eldest child](/wiki/Kunya_(Arabic)), Asahd Tuck Khaled. In March 2019, he announced the release date, while in the meantime, promoting the album extensively on his social media accounts. He also posted a trailer for the album. DJ Khaled has also teased numerous collaborations on his social media, which, along with [Future](/wiki/Future_(rapper)), [Jay-Z](/wiki/Jay-Z) and [Beyoncé](/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9) on "Top Off", and [Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber), [Chance the Rapper](/wiki/Chance_the_Rapper) and [Quavo](/wiki/Quavo) on "[No Brainer](/wiki/No_Brainer_(song))", include [J Balvin](/wiki/J_Balvin), [Cardi B](/wiki/Cardi_B), [Meek Mill](/wiki/Meek_Mill), [Post Malone](/wiki/Post_Malone), [Travis Scott](/wiki/Travis_Scott), [Chief Keef](/wiki/Chief_Keef), [Bryson Tiller](/wiki/Bryson_Tiller), Sizzla, and [2 Chainz](/wiki/2_Chainz). In April 2019, [TMZ](/wiki/TMZ) reported that "Higher" featuring [Nipsey Hussle](/wiki/Nipsey_Hussle) and [John Legend](/wiki/John_Legend) would appear on the album. DJ Khaled, Nipsey Hussle, and John Legend shot the music video before Nipsey's death on March 31, 2019.

*Father of Asahd* received mixed reviews among critics. On [Metacritic](/wiki/Metacritic) which assigns a rating of up to 100, the album received an average of 59, indicating "Mixed or average reviews" based on 7 critics.

Andy Kellman of [AllMusic](/wiki/AllMusic) gave the album 3.5 stars out of 5 and also noted in his review on the album by saying "Although instant, maximum commercial impact is no doubt the primary objective, the album does come across as more considered than the average Khaled LP."

## Commercial performance

*Father of Asahd* debuted at number two on the US [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) with 136,000 [album-equivalent units](/wiki/Album-equivalent_unit), with 34,000 copies as pure album sales in its first week. In the second week, the album remained at number two on the chart, earning an additional 58,000 album-equivalent units. In its third week, the album dropped to number three on the chart, earning 39,000 more units. In its fourth week, the album dropped to number eight on the chart, earning 31,000 more units, bringing its four-week total to 264,000 album-equivalent units. On May 17, 2020, the album was certified [platinum](/wiki/RIAA_Certification) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA) for combined sales and album-equivalent units of over 1,000,000 units in the United States.

## Controversy

On June 5, 2019, sources reported DJ Khaled was fuming at his label after *Father of Asahd* failed to debut at the top of the *Billboard* 200. *Father of Asahd* debuted behind American rapper and record producer [Tyler, the Creator](/wiki/Tyler,_the_Creator)'s sixth studio album, *[Igor](/wiki/Igor_(album))*.

On June 6, DJ Khaled posted a now-deleted video on his [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram) page that many believe was aimed at Tyler, the Creator after his album beat out *Father of Asahd* for the number one spot on the *Billboard* charts. In the video, Khaled stated, "Here's the thing, I make albums so people can play it. And you actually hear it. You know, driving your car you hear another car playing it. Go to the barber shop, you hear them playing it. You know, turn the radio on, and you hear them playing it. It's playing everywhere - it's called great music. It's called albums that you actually hear the songs. Not no mysterious shit, and you never hear it." Many people took the "mysterious shit" line to be about Tyler, who responded on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter) when a follower told him, "Wow doin pretty good for some mysterious shit." Tyler told the follower, "yeah i am, IGOR OUT NOW."

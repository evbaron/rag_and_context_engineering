The **2016 [Cleveland Indians](/wiki/Cleveland_Indians) season** was the 116th season for the franchise and the 23rd season at [Progressive Field](/wiki/Progressive_Field). The Indians won the [American League Central](/wiki/American_League_Central) for the first time since [2007](/wiki/2007_Cleveland_Indians_season) and also beat the [Boston Red Sox](/wiki/2016_Boston_Red_Sox_season) in the [ALDS](/wiki/2016_American_League_Division_Series) for their first playoff win in nine years. They defeated the [Toronto Blue Jays](/wiki/2016_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) in five games in the [ALCS](/wiki/2016_American_League_Championship_Series) before losing to the [Chicago Cubs](/wiki/2016_Chicago_Cubs_season) in seven games in the [World Series](/wiki/2016_World_Series), despite holding a 3–1 series lead. This was their first appearance in the World Series since [1997](/wiki/1997_World_Series).

### April

The Cleveland Indians opened up their 2016 season with a three-game series against the [Boston Red Sox](/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) at [Progressive Field](/wiki/Progressive_Field). [Opening Day](/wiki/Opening_Day) was scheduled for April 4, but was postponed due to a mixture of rain and snow until April 5. The Indians lost the opener 6–2. They would get their first win a day later, as newly acquired 1B [Mike Napoli](/wiki/Mike_Napoli) hit the go-ahead home run in a 7–6 victory over his former team.

The Indians struggled during the month of April, going just 10-11 during the month. On April 24, SP [Carlos Carrasco](/wiki/Carlos_Carrasco_(baseball)) left a game with a hamstring injury. He would miss the next six weeks. [Trevor Bauer](/wiki/Trevor_Bauer), who started the year in the bullpen, would take Carrasco's spot in the rotation. SP Josh Tomlin was a bright spot for April, as he went 3-0 throughout the month.

### May

Outfielder [Michael Brantley](/wiki/Michael_Brantley) came back from a shoulder injury he suffered late in [2015](/wiki/2015_Cleveland_Indians_season). He would only play 11 games before re-aggravating his shoulder. He would not return the rest of the season. The Indians won 12 of 17 games between May 2–20, to close to within two games of the division leading [Chicago White Sox](/wiki/2016_Chicago_White_Sox_season). The Indians would take three of four in Chicago later in the month to take the division lead. The team finished the month 16–3.

### June

The Indians continued their success into June, as they would win their first six games of the month. Later in the month, the Indians would win a franchise record 14 straight games from June 17 through July 1. The Indians were a perfect 11–0 at home in June. They spent the month battling with the [Detroit Tigers](/wiki/2016_Detroit_Tigers_season), [Kansas City Royals](/wiki/2016_Kansas_City_Royals_season), and fading [Chicago White Sox](/wiki/2016_Chicago_White_Sox_season) atop the AL Central. The Indians finished the month two games ahead of Kansas City.

SP [Danny Salazar](/wiki/Danny_Salazar), who went 5–0 with a 1.91 ERA and a .177 opponent's batting average, was named the American League Pitcher of the Month for June. OF [Tyler Naquin](/wiki/Tyler_Naquin) was named the AL Rookie of the Month after [batting](/wiki/Batting_average_(baseball)) .338 with an AL-best 1.219 [OPS](/wiki/On-base_plus_slugging).

### July

The Indians won their game on July 1, 2–1 over the [Toronto Blue Jays](/wiki/2016_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season), in 19 innings - the longest game of the 2016 season - to extend their winning streak to 14 games, but it would end the next day with a 9–6 loss to the Blue Jays. The Indians would maintain a 5–7 game lead over the [Detroit Tigers](/wiki/2016_Detroit_Tigers_season) throughout much of July. However the team struggled near the end of the month and the lead slipped down to 4 games. Adding to the struggle was an injury to C [Yan Gomes](/wiki/Yan_Gomes), who would not return until the final game of the season.

On July 5, SP [Danny Salazar](/wiki/Danny_Salazar) and SS [Francisco Lindor](/wiki/Francisco_Lindor) were named to the All Star team. On July 8, SP [Corey Kluber](/wiki/Corey_Kluber) was also named to the team as an injury replacement. OF [Tyler Naquin](/wiki/Tyler_Naquin) was named the AL Rookie of the Month for the second straight month.

The Indians were active at the trade deadline. On July 31, they acquired RP [Andrew Miller](/wiki/Andrew_Miller_(baseball)) from the [New York Yankees](/wiki/2016_New_York_Yankees_season) for four prospects including OF [Clint Frazier](/wiki/Clint_Frazier) and P [Justus Sheffield](/wiki/Justus_Sheffield), widely considered two of the top four prospects in the Indians' farm system. The Indians also had a deal on the table for [Milwaukee Brewers](/wiki/2016_Milwaukee_Brewers_season) C [Jonathan Lucroy](/wiki/Jonathan_Lucroy), but Lucroy exercised his no-trade clause and vetoed the trade to Cleveland. He would later be traded to the [Texas Rangers](/wiki/2016_Texas_Rangers_season).

### August

The Indians struggled in early August, going just 3–6 in their first nine games. Their lead over the [Tigers](/wiki/2016_Detroit_Tigers_season) decreased to just 2 games. However, On August 11, the team opened an 11-game home stand - its longest of the season - that would put them on the winning track. The Indians swept a four-game series from the [Anaheim Angels](/wiki/2016_Los_Angeles_Angels_of_Anaheim_season) to open up the home stand and took two out of three games from the [Toronto Blue Jays](/wiki/2016_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) to end the home stand. The Blue Jays series, which was believed to be a potential playoff preview, featured three one-run games including a walk-off inside-the-park home run by OF [Tyler Naquin](/wiki/Tyler_Naquin). The Indians' lead over Detroit was back to 71⁄2 games by August 22.

The Indians would then go on a west-coast trip that saw them go 2–5, and score one run or less in six of the seven games. However, the team returned home to sweep the [Minnesota Twins](/wiki/2016_Minnesota_Twins_season) to end the month. On August 31, the team acquired OF [Coco Crisp](/wiki/Coco_Crisp) from the [Oakland Athletics](/wiki/Oakland_Athletics) for cash considerations. The acquisition of Crisp added outfield depth for the pennant race and postseason run.

### September/October

The Indians won six of their first eight games in September to maintain a sizable lead over the [Detroit Tigers](/wiki/2016_Detroit_Tigers_season). However, the team's rotation, widely believed to be their biggest strength, suffered two major injuries during the month. All-Star SP [Danny Salazar](/wiki/Danny_Salazar) left the game on September 9 with a strained elbow, resulting in a 3-4 week recovery time. On September 17, SP [Carlos Carrasco](/wiki/Carlos_Carrasco_(baseball)) left the game with a broken hand as the result of a line-drive. This injury ended Carrasco's season. The Indians would put the Tigers away in September by winning two of three September 16–18. On September 26, the Indians clinched the division title - the team's first since 2007 - with a win over the Tigers.

The Indians would end the season with a record of 94–67, earning the No. 2 seed in the American League.

## Postseason

### American League Division Series

Main article: [2016 American League Division Series](/wiki/2016_American_League_Division_Series)

By virtue of winning the [AL Central](/wiki/American_League_Central), the Indians were placed in the American League Division Series against the [AL East](/wiki/American_League_East) champion [Boston Red Sox](/wiki/2016_Boston_Red_Sox_season). Because the Indians had the better regular season record, they had home field advantage in the best-of-five series.

The Indians won Game 1, 5–4, thanks in part to a three-run 3rd inning that featured home runs by [Roberto Perez](/wiki/Roberto_Perez), [Jason Kipnis](/wiki/Jason_Kipnis), and [Francisco Lindor](/wiki/Francisco_Lindor), the first postseason home run in each of their careers. The Indians' pitching was led by starter [Trevor Bauer](/wiki/Trevor_Bauer) and relievers [Andrew Miller](/wiki/Andrew_Miller_(baseball)) and [Cody Allen](/wiki/Cody_Allen), who each threw over 40 pitches, to clinch the victory. Indians' ace [Corey Kluber](/wiki/Corey_Kluber) went seven shutout innings in Game 2, a 6-0 Indians' win. The big blow for the Indians was a [Lonnie Chisenhall](/wiki/Lonnie_Chisenhall) 3-run home run.

The Indians and Red Sox then had two days off - a regularly scheduled travel day and rainout as the remnants of [Hurricane Matthew](/wiki/Hurricane_Matthew) affected Boston. When the teams got back to baseball on October 10, [Josh Tomlin](/wiki/Josh_Tomlin) and the Indians' bullpen would lead the team to a 4–3 victory and a sweep of the division series. The Red Sox mounted a rally in the bottom of the 9th, but Cody Allen was able to close out the game. The Indians would advance to the American League Championship Series for the first time since 2007.

### American League Championship Series

Main article: [2016 American League Championship Series](/wiki/2016_American_League_Championship_Series)

The Indians would face the [Toronto Blue Jays](/wiki/2016_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) in the American League Championship Series, again having home-field advantage. The Indians would win a pair of pitchers' duels in Games 1 and 2, 2-0 and 2–1. In Game 1, the Indians scored their only runs on a [Francisco Lindor](/wiki/Francisco_Lindor) home run in the sixth inning. Indians' starter [Corey Kluber](/wiki/Corey_Kluber) went six shutout innings and relievers [Andrew Miller](/wiki/Andrew_Miller_(baseball)) and [Cody Allen](/wiki/Cody_Allen) completed the shutout. In Game 2, [Carlos Santana](/wiki/Carlos_Santana)'s leadoff home run in the second off of Toronto pitcher [J. A. Happ](/wiki/J._A._Happ) gave the Indians a 1−0 lead, but the Blue Jays tied it in the third with a [Josh Donaldson](/wiki/Josh_Donaldson) RBI double. Cleveland responded with a Lindor RBI single in the third. Starter [Josh Tomlin](/wiki/Josh_Tomlin), along with [Bryan Shaw](/wiki/Bryan_Shaw_(baseball)), Miller, and Allen, would make that 2–1 lead stand up the rest of the game.

The series shifted to Toronto for Game 3. Indians' starter [Trevor Bauer](/wiki/Trevor_Bauer) left the game in the bottom of the inning due to a bloody finger as a result of being cut from a drone before the series started. The bullpen answered the call and gave up two runs in more than 8 innings of work. Led by [Mike Napoli](/wiki/Mike_Napoli) and [Jason Kipnis](/wiki/Jason_Kipnis) home runs, the Indians won this game 4–2, to take a 3–0 series lead.

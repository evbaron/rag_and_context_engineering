***Family Guy*** is an American [animated sitcom](/wiki/Animated_sitcom) created by [Seth MacFarlane](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane) for the [Fox Broadcasting Company](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company). The series premiered on January 31, 1999, following [Super Bowl XXXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIII), with the rest of the first season airing from April 11, 1999. The show centers around the [Griffins](/wiki/Griffin_family), a [dysfunctional family](/wiki/Dysfunctional_family) consisting of parents [Peter](/wiki/Peter_Griffin) and [Lois](/wiki/Lois_Griffin), their children, [Meg](/wiki/Meg_Griffin), [Chris](/wiki/Chris_Griffin), and [Stewie](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin), and their [anthropomorphic](/wiki/Anthropomorphism) [pet dog](/wiki/Pet_dog), [Brian](/wiki/Brian_Griffin). Set in the fictional city of [Quahog](/wiki/Quahog,_Rhode_Island), [Rhode Island](/wiki/Rhode_Island), the show exhibits much of its humor in the form of [cutaway gags](/wiki/Cutaway_(filmmaking)) that often lampoon [American culture](/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States).

The family was conceived by MacFarlane after he developed two animated films, [*The Life of Larry* and *Larry & Steve*](/wiki/The_Life_of_Larry_and_Larry_%26_Steve). MacFarlane redesigned the films' protagonist, Larry, and his dog, Steve, and renamed them Peter and Brian, respectively. MacFarlane pitched a fifteen-minute pilot to Fox in May 1998, and the show was greenlit and began production. *Family Guy*'s cancellation was announced shortly after the [third season](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_3) had aired in 2002, with [one unaired episode](/wiki/When_You_Wish_Upon_a_Weinstein) eventually premiering on [Adult Swim](/wiki/Adult_Swim) in 2003, finishing the series' original run. Favorable DVD sales and high [ratings](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings) from syndicated reruns since then convinced Fox to revive the show in 2004; a [fourth season](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_4) began airing the following year, on May 1, 2005.

*Family Guy* received generally positive reviews during its first seven seasons. Since then, the series has received criticism for a perceived decline in quality. In 2009, it was nominated for a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Comedy_Series), the first time an animated series had been nominated for the award since *[The Flintstones](/wiki/The_Flintstones)* in 1961. In 2013, *[TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide)* ranked *Family Guy* as the ninth-greatest TV cartoon. The series has drawn significant [criticism](/wiki/Family_Guy_controversies) for its storylines, humor, and character portrayals, including allegations of racism, sexism, homophobia, and reliance on offensive stereotypes and graphic violence.

Many [tie-in](/wiki/Tie-in) media based on the show have been released, including *[Stewie Griffin: The Untold Story](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin:_The_Untold_Story)*, a [straight-to-DVD](/wiki/Direct-to-video) special released in 2005; *[Family Guy: Live in Vegas](/wiki/Family_Guy:_Live_in_Vegas)*, a soundtrack-DVD combo released in 2005, featuring music from the show as well as music created by MacFarlane and [Walter Murphy](/wiki/Walter_Murphy); a [video game](/wiki/Family_Guy_Video_Game!) and [pinball machine](/wiki/Pinball), released in 2006 and 2007, respectively; since 2005, six books published by [Harper Adult](/wiki/HarperCollins); and *[Laugh It Up, Fuzzball: The Family Guy Trilogy](/wiki/Laugh_It_Up,_Fuzzball:_The_Family_Guy_Trilogy)* (2010), a collection of three episodes parodying the [original *Star Wars* trilogy](/wiki/Star_Wars_original_trilogy). A [spin-off](/wiki/Spin-off_(media)) series, *[The Cleveland Show](/wiki/The_Cleveland_Show)*, featuring [Cleveland Brown](/wiki/Cleveland_Brown), aired from September 27, 2009 to May 19, 2013.

On April 2, 2025, it was announced that *Family Guy* would be renewed for four more seasons in what is considered a "mega deal" with parent company [Disney](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company). This renewal will take the show through the 2028–29 television season. The twenty-fourth season premiered in October 2025, and regular episodes premiered on February 15, 2026.

In March 2026, it was announced that a second spin-off series, *Stewie*, was given a two-season order for Fox. *Stewie* is set to premiere during the 2027–28 season.

## Premise

### Characters

Main article: [List of characters in the *Family Guy* franchise](/wiki/List_of_characters_in_the_Family_Guy_franchise)

The [Griffin family](/wiki/Griffin_family). From left: [Chris](/wiki/Chris_Griffin), [Peter](/wiki/Peter_Griffin), [Stewie](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin) (in baby carrier), [Lois](/wiki/Lois_Griffin), [Brian](/wiki/Brian_Griffin) (dog in front), and [Meg](/wiki/Meg_Griffin).

The show centers around the adventures and activities of the dysfunctional [Griffin family](/wiki/Griffin_family), consisting of father [Peter Griffin](/wiki/Peter_Griffin), a bumbling and clumsy [blue-collar worker](/wiki/Blue-collar_worker); his wife [Lois](/wiki/Lois_Griffin), a stay-at-home mother and piano teacher (in early episodes) who is a member of the affluent [Pewterschmidt family](/wiki/Pewterschmidt_family); [Meg](/wiki/Meg_Griffin), their often bullied teenage daughter who is constantly ridiculed or ignored by the family; [Chris](/wiki/Chris_Griffin), their awkward teenage son, who is overweight, unintelligent, unathletic, and in many respects a younger version of his father; and [Stewie](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin), their diabolical, adult-mannered infant son of ambiguous sexual orientation who is an [evil genius](/wiki/Mad_scientist) and uses stereotypical [archvillain](/wiki/Archenemy) phrases. Living with the family is their witty, smoking, [martini-swilling](/wiki/Martini_(cocktail)), sarcastic, English-speaking anthropomorphic pet dog [Brian](/wiki/Brian_Griffin), although he is still considered a pet in many ways.

Recurring characters appear alongside the Griffin family. These include the family's neighbors: sex-crazed airline pilot bachelor [Glenn Quagmire](/wiki/Glenn_Quagmire); deli owner/mail carrier [Cleveland Brown](/wiki/Cleveland_Brown) and his wife Loretta (later [Donna](/wiki/Donna_Tubbs)); [paraplegic](/wiki/Paraplegia) police officer [Joe Swanson](/wiki/Joe_Swanson), his wife [Bonnie](/wiki/Bonnie_Swanson), their son [Kevin](/wiki/Kevin_Swanson_(Family_Guy)) and their baby daughter [Susie](/wiki/Susie_Swanson_(Family_Guy)); neurotic Jewish pharmacist [Mort Goldman](/wiki/Mort_Goldman), his wife [Muriel](/wiki/Muriel_Goldman), and their geeky and annoying son [Neil](/wiki/List_of_Family_Guy_characters#Spooner_Street_neighbors), and elderly child molester [Herbert](/wiki/Herbert_(Family_Guy)). TV [news anchors](/wiki/News_presenter) [Tom Tucker](/wiki/List_of_Family_Guy_characters#Tom_Tucker) and [Diane Simmons](/wiki/List_of_Family_Guy_characters#Diane_Simmons), *Asian reporter* [Tricia Takanawa](/wiki/Tricia_Takanawa), and *Blaccu-Weather* [meteorologist](/wiki/Meteorologist) [Ollie Williams](/wiki/Ollie_Williams) also make frequent appearances. Actor [James Woods](/wiki/James_Woods) guest stars as himself in multiple episodes, as did [Adam West](/wiki/Adam_West), prior to his death.

The primary setting of *Family Guy* is Quahog ([/ˈk(w)oʊhɒɡ/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*K(W)OH-hog*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), a [fictional city](/wiki/List_of_fictional_towns_in_animation) in [Rhode Island](/wiki/Rhode_Island) that was founded by Peter's ancestor, Griffin Peterson. MacFarlane resided in Providence during his time as a student at the [Rhode Island School of Design](/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design) (RISD), and the show contains distinct Rhode Island landmarks similar to real-world locations.

## Production

### Development

MacFarlane conceived *Family Guy* in 1995 while studying animation at the [Rhode Island School of Design](/wiki/Rhode_Island_School_of_Design). During college, he created his thesis film titled *[The Life of Larry](/wiki/The_Life_of_Larry_and_Larry_%26_Steve#The_Life_of_Larry)*, which was submitted by his professor at RISD to [Hanna-Barbera](/wiki/Hanna-Barbera). MacFarlane was hired by the company. In 1996, MacFarlane created a sequel to *The Life of Larry* titled *[Larry and Steve](/wiki/Larry_shorts)*, which featured a middle-aged character named Larry and an intellectual dog, Steve; the short was broadcast in 1997 as one of [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network)'s *[World Premiere Toons](/wiki/World_Premiere_Toons)*.

Executives at [Fox](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) saw the *Larry* shorts and contracted MacFarlane to create a series, titled *Family Guy*, based on the characters. Fox proposed that MacFarlane complete a 15-minute short and gave him a budget of $50,000. MacFarlane pitched the completed demo short to Fox on May 15, 1998, which featured the first appearance of the Griffin family. The first seven minutes of the pitch pilot, which had a storyline parallel to the pilot that aired on television in January 1999, would be included in *Family Guy* DVD sets. The full fifteen-minute pitch demo remained lost until it was uploaded to the internet by Robert Paulson on his personal website in 2022. Despite this, the pitch demo was not publicly seen until March 2025.

Several aspects of *Family Guy* were inspired by the *Larry* shorts. While MacFarlane worked on the series, the characters of Larry and his dog Steve slowly evolved into Peter and Brian Griffin. MacFarlane stated that the difference between *The Life of Larry* and *Family Guy* was that "*Life of Larry* was shown primarily in my dorm room and *Family Guy* was shown after the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIII)."

*Family Guy* was originally planned to start out as short movies for the sketch show *[Mad TV](/wiki/Mad_TV)*, but the plan changed, because *MADtv*'s budget was not large enough to support animation production. MacFarlane noted that he then wanted to pitch it to Fox, as he thought that it was the place to create a prime-time animation show. *Family Guy* was originally pitched to Fox in the same year as *[King of the Hill](/wiki/King_of_the_Hill)*, but the show was not bought until years later, when *King of the Hill* became successful.

After the pilot episode of *Family Guy* aired, the series was given the [greenlight](/wiki/Greenlight). MacFarlane drew inspiration from several sitcoms such as *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)* and *[All in the Family](/wiki/All_in_the_Family)*. Premises were drawn from several 1980s [Saturday-morning cartoons](/wiki/Saturday-morning_cartoon) he watched as a child, such as *[The Fonz and the Happy Days Gang](/wiki/The_Fonz_and_the_Happy_Days_Gang)* and *[Rubik, the Amazing Cube](/wiki/Rubik,_the_Amazing_Cube)*.

### Renewals

On January 26, 2023, Fox announced that the series had been renewed for seasons 22 and 23, taking the show through the 2024–25 television season. Season 22 premiered on October 1, 2023. Season 23 premiered on February 16, 2025.

On April 2, 2025, it was announced that *Family Guy* would be renewed for four more seasons in what is considered a "mega deal" with parent company [Disney](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company). This renewal will take the show through the 2028–2029 television season, which coincides with the 30th anniversary of the shows' launch in January 1999. Additionally, each season will consist of 15 episodes.

### Executive producers

MacFarlane has served as an [executive producer](/wiki/Executive_producer#Motion_pictures) throughout the show's entire history. The first executive producers were [David Zuckerman](/wiki/David_Zuckerman_(TV_producer)), [Lolee Aries](/wiki/Lolee_Aries), David Pritchard, and Mike Wolf. *Family Guy* has had many executive producers in its history, including [Daniel Palladino](/wiki/Daniel_Palladino), [Kara Vallow](/wiki/Kara_Vallow), and [Danny Smith](/wiki/Danny_Smith_(writer)). [David A. Goodman](/wiki/David_A._Goodman) joined the show as a co-executive producer in season three and eventually became an executive producer.

### Writing

Main article: [List of *Family Guy* writers](/wiki/List_of_Family_Guy_writers)

The first team of writers assembled for the show consisted of [Chris Sheridan](/wiki/Chris_Sheridan_(writer)), Danny Smith, [Gary Janetti](/wiki/Gary_Janetti), [Ricky Blitt](/wiki/Ricky_Blitt), Neil Goldman, Garrett Donovan, [Matt Weitzman](/wiki/Matt_Weitzman), and [Mike Barker](/wiki/Mike_Barker_(producer)).

[Matt Weitzman](/wiki/Matt_Weitzman) (left) is a former staff writer, and [Mike Barker](/wiki/Mike_Barker_(producer)) is a former producer and writer of the show. Both left the series to create the ongoing adult animated sitcom *[American Dad!](/wiki/American_Dad!)* with Seth MacFarlane. Barker later left *American Dad!* as well following production of the show's 10th season.

These scripts generally include [cutaway gags](/wiki/Cutaway_gag). Various gags are pitched to MacFarlane and the rest of the staff, and those deemed the funniest are included in the episode. MacFarlane has explained that it normally takes 10 months to produce an episode because the show uses hand-drawn animation. The show rarely comments on current events for this reason. The show's initial writers had never written for an animated show, and most came from live-action sitcoms.

MacFarlane explained that he is a fan of 1930s and 1940s radio programs, particularly the radio thriller anthology *[Suspense](/wiki/Suspense_(radio_drama))*, which led him to give early episodes ominous titles like "[Death Has a Shadow](/wiki/Death_Has_a_Shadow)" and "[Mind Over Murder](/wiki/Mind_Over_Murder)". MacFarlane said that the team dropped the naming convention after individual episodes became hard to identify, and the novelty wore off. For the first few months of production, the writers shared one office, lent to them by the *King of the Hill* production crew.

Credited with 19 episodes, [Steve Callaghan](/wiki/Steve_Callaghan) is the most prolific writer on the *Family Guy* staff. Many of the writers that have left the show have gone on to create or produce other successful series. Neil Goldman and Garrett Donovan co-wrote 13 episodes for the NBC sitcom *[Scrubs](/wiki/Scrubs_(TV_series))* during their eight-year run on the show, while also serving as co-producers and working their way up to executive producers. [Mike Barker](/wiki/Mike_Barker_(producer)) and [Matt Weitzman](/wiki/Matt_Weitzman) left the show and went on to create the long-running and still ongoing adult animated series *[American Dad!](/wiki/American_Dad!)*. (MacFarlane is also a co-creator of *American Dad!*) On November 4, 2013, it was announced that Barker had departed *American Dad!* during its run as well, after 10 seasons of serving as producer and co-showrunner over the series.

During the [2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike), official production of the show halted for most of December 2007 and for various periods afterward. Fox continued producing episodes without MacFarlane's final approval, which he termed "a colossal dick move" in an interview with *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))*. Though MacFarlane refused to work on the show, his contract under Fox required him to contribute to any episodes it would subsequently produce. Production officially resumed after the end of the strike, with regularly airing episodes recommencing on February 17, 2008. According to MacFarlane, in 2009, it cost about $2 million to make an episode of *Family Guy*.

During his September 2017 [AMA](/wiki/Reddit#IAmA_and_AMA) on [Reddit](/wiki/Reddit), MacFarlane revealed that he had not written for the show since 2010, choosing instead to focus on production and voice acting.

On May 12, 2023, it was announced that the showrunners of *Family Guy*, including Seth MacFarlane, would temporarily leave the show as a result of the [2023 Writers Guild of America Strike](/wiki/2023_Writers_Guild_of_America_strike).

Seth MacFarlane voices three of the show's main characters: [Peter Griffin](/wiki/Peter_Griffin), [Brian Griffin](/wiki/Brian_Griffin), and [Stewie Griffin](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin). Since MacFarlane had a strong vision for these characters, he chose to voice them himself, believing it would be easier than for someone else to attempt it. MacFarlane drew inspiration for the voice of Peter from a security guard he overheard talking while attending the Rhode Island School of Design. Stewie's voice was based on the voice of English actor [Rex Harrison](/wiki/Rex_Harrison), especially his performance in the 1964 musical drama film *[My Fair Lady](/wiki/My_Fair_Lady_(film))*. MacFarlane uses his regular speaking voice when playing Brian. MacFarlane also provides the voices for various other recurring and one-time-only characters, most prominently those of the Griffins' neighbor [Glenn Quagmire](/wiki/Glenn_Quagmire), news anchor [Tom Tucker](/wiki/List_of_Family_Guy_characters#Tom_Tucker), and Lois' father, [Carter Pewterschmidt](/wiki/Carter_Pewterschmidt).

[Alex Borstein](/wiki/Alex_Borstein) voices Peter's wife [Lois Griffin](/wiki/Lois_Griffin), [Asian correspondent Tricia Takanawa](/wiki/Tricia_Takanawa), [Loretta Brown](/wiki/Loretta_Brown), and Lois' mother, [Babs Pewterschmidt](/wiki/Babs_Pewterschmidt). Borstein was asked to provide a voice for the pilot while she was working on *MADtv*. She had not met MacFarlane or seen any of his artwork and said it was "really sight unseen". At the time, Borstein was performing in a stage show in Los Angeles. She played a redheaded mother whose voice she had based on one of her cousins.

[Seth Green](/wiki/Seth_Green) primarily voices [Chris Griffin](/wiki/Chris_Griffin) and [Neil Goldman](/wiki/Neil_Goldman_(Family_Guy)). Green stated that he did an impression of the character [Buffalo Bill](/wiki/Buffalo_Bill_(character)) from the thriller film *[The Silence of the Lambs](/wiki/The_Silence_of_the_Lambs_(film))* during his audition.

[Mila Kunis](/wiki/Mila_Kunis) and [Lacey Chabert](/wiki/Lacey_Chabert) have both voiced [Meg Griffin](/wiki/Meg_Griffin). Chabert left the series after the first season because of time conflicts with schoolwork (at the time) and her role on *[Party of Five](/wiki/Party_of_Five)*. When Kunis auditioned for the role, she was called back by MacFarlane, who instructed her to speak slower. He then told her to come back another time and enunciate more. Once she claimed that she had it under control, MacFarlane hired her.

[Mike Henry](/wiki/Mike_Henry_(voice_actor)) voices [Herbert](/wiki/Herbert_(Family_Guy)), [Bruce the Performance Artist](/wiki/Bruce_the_Performance_Artist), [Consuela](/wiki/Consuela_(Family_Guy)), the [Greased-up Deaf Guy](/wiki/Greased-up_Deaf_Guy), and until 2021, [Cleveland Brown](/wiki/Cleveland_Brown). Henry met MacFarlane at the Rhode Island School of Design and kept in touch with him after they graduated. A few years later, MacFarlane contacted him about being part of the show; he agreed and came on as a writer and voice actor. During the show's first four seasons, he was credited as a guest star, but beginning with [season five](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_5)'s "[Prick Up Your Ears](/wiki/Prick_Up_Your_Ears_(Family_Guy))", he has been credited as a main cast member. On June 26, 2020, after twenty years of voicing the character, Mike Henry announced on Twitter that he was stepping down from voicing Cleveland, stating "persons of color should play characters of color." ### Early history and cancellation

*Family Guy* officially premiered after Fox's broadcast of [Super Bowl XXXIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIII) on January 31, 1999, with "[Death Has a Shadow](/wiki/Death_Has_a_Shadow)". The show debuted to 22 million viewers and immediately generated controversy regarding its adult content. The show returned on April 11, 1999, with "[I Never Met the Dead Man](/wiki/I_Never_Met_the_Dead_Man)". *Family Guy* garnered decent ratings in Fox's 8:30 pm slot on Sunday, scheduled between *The Simpsons* and *[The X-Files](/wiki/The_X-Files)*. At the end of its first season, the show ranked No. 33 in the [Nielsen ratings](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings), with 12.8 million households tuning in. The show launched its second season in a new time slot, Thursday at 9 pm, on September 23, 1999. *Family Guy* was pitted against NBC's *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)*, and the series' ratings declined sharply. Subsequently, Fox removed *Family Guy* from its schedule and began airing episodes irregularly. The show returned on March 7, 2000, at 8:30 pm on Tuesdays, where it was constantly beaten in the ratings by [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)'s then-new breakout hit *[Who Wants to Be a Millionaire](/wiki/Who_Wants_to_Be_a_Millionaire_(American_game_show))*, coming in at No. 114 in the Nielsen ratings with 6.3 million households tuning in. Fox announced that the show had been canceled in May 2000, at the end of the second season. However, following a last-minute reprieve, on July 24, 2000, Fox ordered 13 additional episodes to form a third season.

The show returned on November 8, 2001, once again in a tough time slot: Thursday nights at 8 pm. This slot brought it into competition with *[Survivor](/wiki/Survivor_(American_TV_series))* and *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)* (a situation later referenced in *[Stewie Griffin: The Untold Story](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin:_The_Untold_Story)*). During its second and third seasons, Fox frequently moved the show around to different days and time slots with little or no notice and, consequently, the show's ratings suffered. Upon Fox's annual unveiling of its 2002 fall line-up on May 15, 2002, *Family Guy* was absent. Fox announced that the show had been officially canceled shortly thereafter.

### Cult success and revival

Fox attempted to sell the rights for reruns of the show, but finding networks that were interested was difficult; [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network) eventually bought the rights "basically for free", according to the president of [20th Century Fox Television](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox_Television). *Family Guy* premiered in reruns on Adult Swim on April 20, 2003, and immediately became the block's top-rated program, dominating late-night viewing in its time period versus cable and broadcast competition and boosting viewership by 239%. The complete first and second seasons were released on DVD the same week the show premiered on Adult Swim, and the show became a cult phenomenon, selling 400,000 copies within one month. Sales of the DVD set reached 2.2 million copies, becoming the best-selling television DVD of 2003 and the second-highest-selling television DVD ever, behind the first season of [Comedy Central](/wiki/Comedy_Central)'s *[Chappelle's Show](/wiki/Chappelle%27s_Show)*. The third-season DVD release also sold more than a million copies. The show's popularity in DVD sales and reruns rekindled Fox's interest, and, on May 20, 2004, Fox ordered 35 new episodes of *Family Guy*, marking the first revival of a television show based on DVD sales.

"[North by North Quahog](/wiki/North_by_North_Quahog)", which premiered May 1, 2005, was the first episode to be broadcast after the show's hiatus. It was written by MacFarlane and directed by [Peter Shin](/wiki/Peter_Shin). MacFarlane believed the show's three-year hiatus was beneficial, because animated shows do not normally have hiatuses, and towards the end of their seasons, "... you see a lot more sex jokes and *bodily function* jokes and signs of a fatigued staff that their brains are just fried". With "North by North Quahog", the writing staff tried to keep the show "[...] exactly as it was" before its cancellation, and "None of us had any desire to make it look any slicker". The episode was watched by 11.85 million viewers, the show's highest ratings since the airing of the [first season](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_1) episode "[Brian: Portrait of a Dog](/wiki/Brian:_Portrait_of_a_Dog)".

### Lawsuits

In March 2007, comedian [Carol Burnett](/wiki/Carol_Burnett) filed a $6 million lawsuit against 20th Century Fox, claiming that her [charwoman](/wiki/Charwoman) cartoon character had been portrayed on the show without her permission. She stated it was a trademark infringement and that Fox violated her publicity rights. On June 4, 2007, United States District Judge [Dean D. Pregerson](/wiki/Dean_D._Pregerson) rejected the lawsuit, stating that the parody was protected under the [First Amendment](/wiki/First_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution), citing *[Hustler Magazine v. Falwell](/wiki/Hustler_Magazine_v._Falwell)* as a precedent.

On October 3, 2007, [Bourne Co. Music Publishers](/wiki/Bourne_Co._Music_Publishers) filed a lawsuit accusing the show of [infringing its copyright](/wiki/Copyright_infringement) on the song "[When You Wish Upon a Star](/wiki/When_You_Wish_Upon_a_Star)", through a parody song titled "I Need a Jew" appearing in the episode "[When You Wish Upon a Weinstein](/wiki/When_You_Wish_Upon_a_Weinstein)". Bourne Co., the sole United States copyright owner of the song, alleged the parody pairs a "thinly veiled" copy of its music with [antisemitic](/wiki/Antisemitism) lyrics. Named in the suit were [20th Century Fox Film Corp.](/wiki/20th_Century_Fox_Film_Corporation), [Fox Broadcasting Co.](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company), [Cartoon Network](/wiki/Cartoon_Network), [MacFarlane](/wiki/Seth_MacFarlane), and [Murphy](/wiki/Walter_Murphy); the suit sought to stop the program's distribution and asked for unspecified damages. Bourne argued that "I Need a Jew" uses the copyrighted melody of "When You Wish Upon a Star" without commenting on that song, and that it was therefore not a First Amendment-protected parody per the ruling in *[Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.](/wiki/Campbell_v._Acuff-Rose_Music,_Inc.)* On March 16, 2009, United States District Judge [Deborah Batts](/wiki/Deborah_Batts) held that *Family Guy* did not infringe on Bourne's copyright when it transformed the song for comical use in an episode.

In December 2007, *Family Guy* was again accused of copyright infringement when actor [Art Metrano](/wiki/Art_Metrano) filed a lawsuit regarding a scene in *[Stewie Griffin: The Untold Story](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin:_The_Untold_Story)*, in which Jesus performs Metrano's signature "magic" act involving absurd "faux" magical hand gestures while humming the distinctive tune "[Fine and Dandy](/wiki/Fine_and_Dandy_(song))". 20th Century Fox, MacFarlane, Callaghan and Borstein were all named in the suit. In July 2009, a federal district court judge rejected Fox's motion to dismiss, saying that the first three fair use factors involved – "purpose and character of the use", "nature of the infringed work" and "amount and substantiality of the taking" – counted in Metrano's favor, while the fourth – "economic impact" – had to await more fact-finding. In denying the dismissal, the court held that the reference in the scene made light of Jesus and his followers – not Metrano or his act.
The case was settled out of court in 2010 with undisclosed terms.

## Hallmarks

### "Road to" episodes

Further information: [Road to ... (*Family Guy*)](/wiki/Road_to_..._(Family_Guy))

The "Road to" episodes are a series of hallmark travel episodes. They are a parody of the seven *[Road to ...](/wiki/Road_to_...)* comedy films starring [Bing Crosby](/wiki/Bing_Crosby) and [Bob Hope](/wiki/Bob_Hope). These episodes have always involved [Stewie](/wiki/Stewie_Griffin) and [Brian](/wiki/Brian_Griffin) in some foreign, supernatural or science-fiction location, unrelated to the show's normal location in Quahog. The first, titled "[Road to Rhode Island](/wiki/Road_to_Rhode_Island)", aired on May 30, 2000, during the [second season](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_2). The episodes are known for featuring elaborate [musical numbers](/wiki/Number_(music)), similar to the *Road* films. The episodes contain several trademarks, including a special version of the opening sequence, custom [musical cues](/wiki/Musical_cue) and musical numbers, and parodies of science fiction and fantasy films.

The original idea for the "Road to" episodes came from MacFarlane, as he is a fan of the films of Crosby, Hope, and [Dorothy Lamour](/wiki/Dorothy_Lamour). The first episode was directed by [Dan Povenmire](/wiki/Dan_Povenmire), who would direct the rest of the "Road to" episodes until the episode "[Road to Rupert](/wiki/Road_to_Rupert)", at which point he had left the show to create *[Phineas and Ferb](/wiki/Phineas_and_Ferb)* with [Jeff "Swampy" Marsh](/wiki/Jeff_%22Swampy%22_Marsh). Series regular Greg Colton then took over Povenmire's role as director of the "Road to" episodes.

The "Road to" episodes are generally considered by critics and fans to be some of the greatest in the series, thanks to the developing relationship between Stewie and Brian and the strong plotlines of the episodes themselves.

### Humor

*Family Guy* uses the film-making technique of [cutaways](/wiki/Cutaway_(filmmaking)), which occur in the majority of *Family Guy* episodes.

Early episodes based much of their comedy on Stewie's "super villain" antics, such as his constant plans for total world domination, his evil experiments, plans and inventions to get rid of things he dislikes, and his constant attempts at [matricide](/wiki/Matricide). As the series progressed, the writers and MacFarlane agreed that his personality and the jokes were starting to feel dated, so they began writing him with a different personality. *Family Guy* often includes [self-referential](/wiki/Meta-reference) humor. The most common form is jokes about the Fox network, and occasions where the characters [break the fourth wall](/wiki/Fourth_wall) by addressing the audience. For example, in "[North by North Quahog](/wiki/North_by_North_Quahog)", the first episode that aired after the show's revival, included Peter telling the family that they had been canceled because Fox had to make room in their schedule for shows like *[Dark Angel](/wiki/Dark_Angel_(American_TV_series))*, *[Titus](/wiki/Titus_(TV_series))*, *[Undeclared](/wiki/Undeclared)*, *[Action](/wiki/Action_(TV_series))*, *[That '80s Show](/wiki/That_%2780s_Show)*, *[Wonderfalls](/wiki/Wonderfalls)*, *[Fastlane](/wiki/Fastlane_(TV_series))*, *[Andy Richter Controls the Universe](/wiki/Andy_Richter_Controls_the_Universe)*, *[Skin](/wiki/Skin_(American_TV_series))*, *[Girls Club](/wiki/Girls_Club_(TV_series))*, *[Cracking Up](/wiki/Cracking_Up_(TV_series))*, *[The Pitts](/wiki/The_Pitts)*, *[Firefly](/wiki/Firefly_(TV_series))*, *[Get Real](/wiki/Get_Real_(American_TV_series))*, *[FreakyLinks](/wiki/FreakyLinks)*, *[Wanda at Large](/wiki/Wanda_at_Large)*, *[Costello](/wiki/Costello_(TV_series))*, *[The Lone Gunmen](/wiki/The_Lone_Gunmen_(TV_series))*, *[A Minute with Stan Hooper](/wiki/A_Minute_with_Stan_Hooper)*, *[Normal, Ohio](/wiki/Normal,_Ohio)*, *[Pasadena](/wiki/Pasadena_(TV_series))*, *[Harsh Realm](/wiki/Harsh_Realm)*, *[Keen Eddie](/wiki/Keen_Eddie)*, *[The Street](/wiki/The_Street_(2000_TV_series))*, *[The American Embassy](/wiki/The_American_Embassy)*, *[Cedric the Entertainer Presents](/wiki/Cedric_the_Entertainer_Presents)*, *[The Tick](/wiki/The_Tick_(2001_TV_series))*, *[Luis](/wiki/Luis_(TV_series))*, and *[Greg the Bunny](/wiki/Greg_the_Bunny)*. Lois asks whether there is any hope, to which Peter replies that if all these shows are canceled they might have a chance; the shows were indeed canceled during *Family Guy*'s hiatus.

The show uses [catchphrases](/wiki/Catchphrase), and most of the primary and secondary characters have them. Notable expressions include Quagmire's "Giggity giggity goo", Peter's "Freakin' sweet", Cleveland's "Oh, that's nasty", and Joe's "Bring it on!"

## Reception and legacy

In 2016, a *[New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* study of the 50 TV shows with the most [Facebook Likes](/wiki/Facebook_Like) found that, like other satirical comedies, *Family Guy* is most popular in cities. The show's popularity was more correlated with [support for Hillary Clinton](/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election) than any other show". As of 2008, the franchise has generated $1 billion in total revenue, including $400 million from [TV syndication](/wiki/TV_syndication), $400 million from [DVD](/wiki/DVD) sales, and $200 million from merchandise sales.

### Critical reception

*Family Guy* has received generally mixed reviews. Catherine Seipp of [National Review Online](/wiki/National_Review) described it as a "nasty but extremely funny" cartoon. Caryn James of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* called it a show with an "outrageously satirical family" that "includes plenty of comic possibilities and parodies". *[The Sydney Morning Herald](/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* named *Family Guy* the "Show of the Week" on April 21, 2009, hailing it a "pop culture-heavy masterpiece". Frazier Moore from *[The Seattle Times](/wiki/The_Seattle_Times)* called it an "endless craving for humor about bodily emissions". He thought it was "breathtakingly smart" and said a "blend of the ingenious with the raw helps account for its much broader appeal". He summarized it as "rude, crude and deliciously wrong". *[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker)*'s Nancy Franklin said that *Family Guy* is becoming one of the best animated shows; she commented on its ribaldry and popularity. The show has become a hit on [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu); it is the second-highest viewed show after *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*. [IGN](/wiki/IGN) called *Family Guy* a great show and commented that it has gotten better since its revival. They stated that they cannot imagine another half-hour sitcom that provides as many laughs as *Family Guy*. *[Empire](/wiki/Empire_(magazine))* praised the show and its writers for creating really hilarious moments with unlikely material. They commented that one of the reasons they love the show is because nothing is sacred—it makes jokes and gags of almost everything. Robin Pierson of *The TV Critic* praised the series as "a different kind of animated comedy which clearly sets out to do jokes which other cartoons can't do". *Family Guy* has proven popular in the United Kingdom, regularly obtaining between 700,000 and 1 million viewers for re-runs on [BBC Three](/wiki/BBC_Three).

The series has attracted many celebrities. [Robert Downey Jr.](/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr.) telephoned the show production staff and asked if he could produce or assist in an episode's creation, as his son is a fan of the show; the producers subsequently created a character for Downey. [Lauren Conrad](/wiki/Lauren_Conrad) met MacFarlane while recording a *[Laguna Beach](/wiki/Laguna_Beach:_The_Real_Orange_County)* clip for the episode "[Prick Up Your Ears](/wiki/Prick_Up_Your_Ears_(Family_Guy))" (season 5, 2006). She has watched *Family Guy* for years and considers Stewie her favorite character. Commenting on his appearance in the episode "Big Man on Hippocampus" (season 8, 2010), actor [Dwayne Johnson](/wiki/Dwayne_Johnson) stated that he was a "big fan" of *Family Guy*. Johnson befriended MacFarlane after he had a minor role in Johnson's 2010 film *[Tooth Fairy](/wiki/Tooth_Fairy_(2010_film))*. R&B singer [Rihanna](/wiki/Rihanna) has admitted to being a fan of *Family Guy*, as has pop singer [Britney Spears](/wiki/Britney_Spears); she tries to imitate Stewie's English accent. Spears, who was mocked for her personal problems in the *[South Park](/wiki/South_Park)* episode "[Britney's New Look](/wiki/Britney%27s_New_Look)" in 2008, offered to appear in a cameo to hit back at the similar animated show, but MacFarlane declined, stating that he did not want to start a feud with the series.

### Awards

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by *Family Guy*](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Family_Guy)

*Family Guy* and its cast have been nominated for 27 [Emmy Awards](/wiki/Emmy_Award), with 8 wins. MacFarlane won the [Outstanding Voice-Over Performance](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Voice-Over_Performance) award for his performance as Stewie; Murphy and MacFarlane won the Outstanding Music and Lyrics award for the song "You Got a Lot to See" from the episode "[Brian Wallows and Peter's Swallows](/wiki/Brian_Wallows_and_Peter%27s_Swallows)"; [Steven Fonti](/wiki/Steven_Fonti) won the Outstanding Individual Achievement in Animation award for his storyboard work in the episode "[No Chris Left Behind](/wiki/No_Chris_Left_Behind)"; and Greg Colton won the Outstanding Individual Achievement in Animation award for his storyboard work in the episode "[Road to the Multiverse](/wiki/Road_to_the_Multiverse)". The show was nominated for eleven [Annie Awards](/wiki/Annie_Award), and won three times, twice in 2006 and once in 2008. In 2009, it was nominated for an Emmy for [Outstanding Comedy Series](/wiki/61st_Primetime_Emmy_Awards), becoming the first animated program to be nominated in this category since *[The Flintstones](/wiki/The_Flintstones)* in 1961. *The Simpsons* was almost nominated in 1993, but voters were hesitant to pit cartoons against live action programs. The show was nominated for a [Grammy](/wiki/Grammy) in 2011. *Family Guy* has been nominated and has won various other awards, including the [Teen Choice Awards](/wiki/Teen_Choice_Awards) and the [People's Choice Awards](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards). In the 1,000th issue of *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)*, Brian Griffin was selected as the dog for "The Perfect TV Family". *[Wizard Magazine](/wiki/Wizard_(magazine))* rated Stewie the 95th-greatest villain of all time. British newspaper *[The Times](/wiki/The_Times)* rated *Family Guy* the 45th-best American show in 2009. IGN ranked *Family Guy* number seven in the "Top 100 Animated Series" and number six in the "Top 25 Primetime Animated Series of All Time". *Empire* named it the twelfth-greatest TV show of all time in 2008. In 2005, viewers of the UK television channel [Channel 4](/wiki/Channel_4) voted *Family Guy* number 5 on their list of the 100 Greatest Cartoons. Brian was awarded the 2009 Stoner of the Year award by *[High Times](/wiki/High_Times)* for the episode "[420](/wiki/420_(Family_Guy))", marking the first time an animated character received the honor. In 2004 and 2007, TV Guide ranked *Family Guy* number 12 and number 15 in their list of top cult shows ever. *Family Guy* has garnered six [Golden Reel Awards](/wiki/Golden_Reel_Award_(Motion_Picture_Sound_Editors)) nominations, winning three. In 2013, [TV Guide](/wiki/TV_Guide) ranked *Family Guy* the ninth Greatest TV Cartoon of All Time.

### Criticism and controversy

See also: [*Family Guy* controversies](/wiki/Family_Guy_controversies)

One of the initial critics to give the show negative reviews was Ken Tucker from *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)*; he called it "*The Simpsons* as conceived by a singularly sophomoric mind that lacks any reference point beyond other TV shows". The [Parents Television Council](/wiki/Parents_Television_Council) (PTC), a conservative non-profit [watchdog](/wiki/Watchdog_journalism), has attacked the series since its premiere and has branded various episodes as "Worst TV Show of the Week". In May 2000, the PTC launched a letter-writing campaign to the Fox network in an effort to persuade the network to cancel the show. The PTC has placed the show on their annual lists of "Worst Prime-Time Shows for Family Viewing" in 2000, 2005, and 2006. The [Federal Communications Commission](/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) (FCC) has received multiple petitions requesting that the show be blocked from broadcasting on [indecency](/wiki/Communications_Decency_Act) grounds. Tucker and the PTC have both accused the show of portraying religion negatively, and of being racist. Because of the PTC, some advertisers have canceled their contracts after reviewing the content of the episodes, claiming it to be unsuitable. Critics have compared the show's humor and characters with those of *The Simpsons*.

Various episodes of the show have generated controversy. In "[420](/wiki/420_(Family_Guy))" ([season seven](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_7), 2009), Brian decides to start a campaign to [legalize cannabis](/wiki/Legality_of_cannabis_by_U.S._jurisdiction) in Quahog; the [Venezuelan government](/wiki/Venezuelan_government) reacted negatively to the episode and banned *Family Guy* from airing on their local networks, which generally syndicate American programming. Venezuelan [justice minister](/wiki/Justice_minister) [Tareck El Aissami](/wiki/Tareck_El_Aissami), citing the promotion of the use of cannabis, stated that any cable stations that did not stop airing the series would be fined; the government showed a clip which featured Brian and Stewie singing the praises of marijuana as a demonstration of how the United States supports cannabis use. In "[Extra Large Medium](/wiki/Extra_Large_Medium)" ([season eight](/wiki/Family_Guy_season_8), 2010) a character named Ellen (who has [Down syndrome](/wiki/Down_syndrome)) states that her mother is the former [governor of Alaska](/wiki/Governor_of_Alaska), which strongly implies that her mother is [Sarah Palin](/wiki/Sarah_Palin), the only woman to have served in the office of governor in the state. Palin, the mother of [a child with Down syndrome](/wiki/Trig_Palin), criticized the episode in an appearance on *[The O'Reilly Factor](/wiki/The_O%27Reilly_Factor)*, calling those who made the show "cruel, cold-hearted people".

## Broadcast and streaming

In the United States, the show currently airs on [Adult Swim](/wiki/Adult_Swim), [Comedy Central](/wiki/Comedy_Central), and [FXX](/wiki/FXX). The show was first syndicated to Adult Swim and [TBS](/wiki/TBS_(American_TV_channel)) from 2003 to 2021, sharing the rights to the first fifteen seasons. It was syndicated to various local stations from 2007 to 2024. In April 2019, [FX Networks](/wiki/FX_Networks) began airing reruns of season 16 on FXX, with season 17 debuting that October, and shared off-network rights to both seasons with sister channel [Freeform](/wiki/Freeform_(TV_channel)). After Adult Swim and TBS' rights expired on September 18, 2021, FXX and Freeform began airing the first fifteen seasons. The show also joined FX's lineup that same month.

The show's departure from Adult Swim on September 18, 2021, was commemorated with a remembrance bumper created by the network, which played after the final airing (the episode "[Stewie is Enceinte](/wiki/Stewie_is_Enceinte)"). The bumper showed animations of several Adult Swim characters bidding farewell to *Family Guy*, including shots of Peter crying at the beginning and the Griffin family waving goodbye towards the end.

Comedy Central began airing the show on September 2, 2024, as part of a non-exclusive licensing deal between [Paramount](/wiki/Paramount_Global) and [Disney](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company). Comedy Central debuted the show with an all-day marathon on Labor Day. A similar licensing deal was then struck with [Warner Bros. Discovery Networks](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Discovery_Networks) for the show to return to Adult Swim starting January 1, 2025, with the network planning a 3-day marathon to celebrate.

In the United States, the series is available for streaming exclusively on [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu). Additionally, Hulu launched two holiday specials of *Family Guy* in late 2024, marking the first time a new episode of the show airs on a streaming platform; Internationally, *Family Guy* is available to stream on [Star on Disney+](/wiki/Star_(Disney%2B)). The first such special premiered on October 14, 2024. The second special would then air on November 25, 2024. The show's second Halloween special for Hulu would air on October 6, 2025. The United States is the only country where the show is not available on either [Disney+](/wiki/Disney%2B) or [Star+](/wiki/Star%2B).

Initially, *Family Guy* was not available to stream on Disney+ in Latin America, as it had been available on the standalone service [Star+](/wiki/Star%2B), however, Star+ content merged with Disney+ content on June 26, 2024, and Star+ was discontinued altogether on July 24, 2024.

*Family Guy* premiered in Australia on April 9, 1999, on the [Seven Network](/wiki/Seven_Network), in 2000 on [Fox8](/wiki/Fox8), and on [7mate](/wiki/7mate) on September 27, 2010. Initially, only 2 seasons were available to stream on Disney+ Star due to pre-existing contracts. The other 17 seasons were added on December 1, 2021, after the contract expired.

In Canada, the series premiered January 31, 1999, on [Global](/wiki/Global_Television_Network) and September 1, 2003, on [Teletoon at Night](/wiki/Teletoon_at_Night). Beginning in the 2015–2016 season, the show moved to [Citytv](/wiki/Citytv); the show would once again return to the channel in 2023. Starting in November 2021, the series moved to [Disney+](/wiki/Disney%2B). In addition to Teletoon at Night, the show has been syndicated to TVtropolis (now [DTour](/wiki/DTour)). As of January 2025, [Adult Swim Canada](/wiki/Adult_Swim_(Canadian_TV_channel)) and [FXX Canada](/wiki/FXX_(Canadian_TV_channel)) syndicate the series. Since 2024, [CHCH](/wiki/CHCH-DT) and Citytv share the broadcast rights to the series due to scheduling issues with new episodes streaming the next day on Disney+.

The show airs in India on [Star World Premiere](/wiki/Star_World_Premiere), in Ireland on [3e](/wiki/3e), and in New Zealand on [TVNZ Duke](/wiki/TVNZ_Duke); previously it screened on defunct channel [Four](/wiki/Four_(New_Zealand)).

In the United Kingdom, *Family Guy* premiered in September 1999, originally on [Channel 4](/wiki/Channel_4) and [Sky One](/wiki/Sky_1). In January 2005, [Fox UK](/wiki/Fox_(UK_and_Ireland)) (then known as FX) began broadcasting the show. From October 2005, [BBC Two](/wiki/BBC_Two) started screening *Family Guy* before the show moved to [BBC Three](/wiki/BBC_Three) in September 2006. Beginning with season 14, the show moved to [ITV2](/wiki/ITV2), premiering on February 29, 2016, while the BBC would continue to hold the rights for past seasons until 2017.

## Franchise

Main article: [*Family Guy* (franchise)](/wiki/Family_Guy_(franchise))

### Books

"Family Guy: It Takes a Village Idiot, and I Married One" redirects here. For the episode, see [It Takes a Village Idiot, and I Married One](/wiki/It_Takes_a_Village_Idiot,_and_I_Married_One).

***Family Guy: It Takes a Village Idiot, and I Married One*** was written by executive story editor [Cherry Chevapravatdumrong](/wiki/Cherry_Chevapravatdumrong) and actress [Alex Borstein](/wiki/Alex_Borstein). The book was first published on May 8, 2007. The book is a biographical monologue by Lois Griffin covering a portion of her life, spanning from her memories of growing up to her attempted run for mayor in the town of Quahog. Though the book primarily consists of a loose narrative monologue by Lois, it is also interspersed with sections from other characters such as Peter Griffin. The book covers events featured in the *Family Guy* episode "[It Takes a Village Idiot, and I Married One](/wiki/It_Takes_a_Village_Idiot,_and_I_Married_One)", with which it shares a title. It was published in the United Kingdom in 2007 by [Orion Books](/wiki/Orion_Books).

A comic book based on the *Family Guy* universe was produced. Published by [Titan Comics](/wiki/Titan_Magazines), edited by [Steve White](/wiki/Steve_White_(comics)), and illustrated by [Anthony Williams](/wiki/Anthony_Williams_(comics)) and S. L. Gallant, the writing and the illustrations were supervised by the show's producers. The first comic book was released on July 27, 2011.

### Live performances

As promotion for the show and as Newman described "[to] expand interest in the show beyond its diehard fans", Fox organized four *Family Guy Live!* performances, which featured cast members reading old episodes aloud. The cast also performed musical numbers from the *[Family Guy: Live in Vegas](/wiki/Family_Guy:_Live_in_Vegas)* comedy album. The stage shows were an extension of a performance by the cast during the 2004 [Montreal Comedy Festival](/wiki/Montreal_Comedy_Festival). The *Family Guy Live!* performances, which took place in Los Angeles and New York, sold out and were attended by around 1,200 people each.

In 2009, a special televised performance show aired, titled *Family Guy Presents Seth & Alex's Almost Live Comedy Show*, in which voice actors [Alex Borstein](/wiki/Alex_Borstein) and MacFarlane performed songs from the show, as well as a parody of [Lady Gaga](/wiki/Lady_Gaga)'s song "[Poker Face](/wiki/Poker_Face_(Lady_Gaga_song))" in the voice of [Marlee Matlin](/wiki/Marlee_Matlin), who appeared on stage as a guest during the performance. Some new animated gags also appeared in the show.

### Film

In a July 22, 2007, interview with *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*, MacFarlane announced that he may start working on a feature film, although "nothing's official". In *[TV Week](/wiki/TV_Week)* on July 18, 2008, MacFarlane confirmed plans to produce a theatrically released *Family Guy* feature film sometime "within the next year". He came up with an idea for the story, "something that you could not do on the show, which [to him] is the only reason to do a movie." He later went on to say he imagines the film to be "an old-style musical with dialogue" similar to *[The Sound of Music](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music_(film))*, saying that he would "really be trying to capture, musically, that feel". On October 13, 2011, MacFarlane confirmed that a deal for a *Family Guy* film had been made, and that it would be written by him and series co-producer Ricky Blitt.

On November 30, 2012, MacFarlane confirmed plans to produce a *Family Guy* film. The project was put on hold while MacFarlane worked on *[Ted 2](/wiki/Ted_2)*.

On August 10, 2018, Fox announced that a live-action/animated film based on the series was in development.

In July 2019, MacFarlane confirmed that there would be a *Family Guy* film.

During [PaleyFest](/wiki/PaleyFest) in April 2024, MacFarlane revealed that he has known what the plot of the film would be for the past 15 years, but had not had the time to write it.

### Spin-offs

Main article: [*The Cleveland Show*](/wiki/The_Cleveland_Show)

MacFarlane co-created—alongside [Mike Henry](/wiki/Mike_Henry_(voice_actor)) and [Richard Appel](/wiki/Richard_Appel)—the *Family Guy* spin-off *[The Cleveland Show](/wiki/The_Cleveland_Show)*, which premiered September 27, 2009. They began discussing the project in 2007.

On March 12, 2026, it was announced that a second spin-off series, *Stewie*, was given a two-season order for [Fox](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company). The new animated comedy is reported to follow Stewie Griffin in preschool and will explore time and space travel. Unlike Cleveland Brown, who was not seen on *Family Guy* while appearing on *The Cleveland Show*, Stewie Griffin will remain on *Family Guy* and will be seen on both shows. The spin-off is set to premiere during the 2027–28 season.

### Merchandise

Main article: [List of *Family Guy* home video releases](/wiki/List_of_Family_Guy_home_video_releases)

As of 2009, six books have been released about the *Family Guy* universe, all published by [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins) since 2005. The first, *[Family Guy: Stewie's Guide to World Domination](/wiki/Family_Guy:_Stewie%27s_Guide_to_World_Domination)* ([ISBN](/wiki/ISBN_(identifier)) [978-0-06-077321-2](/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-0-06-077321-2)) by Steve Callahan, was released on April 26, 2005. Written in the style of a [graphic novel](/wiki/Graphic_novel), the plot follows Stewie's plans to rule the world. Other books include *[Family Guy: It Takes a Village Idiot, and I Married One](/wiki/Family_Guy:_It_Takes_a_Village_Idiot,_and_I_Married_One)* ([ISBN](/wiki/ISBN_(identifier)) [978-0-7528-7593-4](/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-0-7528-7593-4)), which covers the events of the [episode of the same name](/wiki/It_Takes_a_Village_Idiot,_and_I_Married_One); and *Family Guy and Philosophy: A Cure for the Petarded* ([ISBN](/wiki/ISBN_(identifier)) [978-1-4051-6316-3](/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-1-4051-6316-3)), a collection of 17 essays exploring the connections between the series and historical philosophers. A book written from Brian's point of view (written by [Andrew Goldberg](/wiki/Andrew_Goldberg_(writer))) was published in 2006, called *Brian Griffin's Guide to Booze, Broads and the Lost Art of Being a Man*.

*Family Guy* has been commercially successful in the home market. The show was the first to be resurrected because of high DVD sales. The first volume, covering the show's first two seasons, sold 1.67 million units, topping TV DVD sales in 2003, while the second volume sold another million units. Volumes six and seven debuted at fifth place in United States DVD sales; volume seven was the highest-selling television DVD, selling 171,000 units by June 21, 2009. *Family Guy Presents Blue Harvest*, the DVD featuring the *Star Wars* special "[Blue Harvest](/wiki/Blue_Harvest_(Family_Guy))", was released on January 15, 2008, and premiered at the top of United States DVD sales. The DVD was the first *Family Guy* DVD to include a digital copy for download to the iPod. In 2004, the first series of *Family Guy* toy figurines was released by [Mezco Toyz](/wiki/Mezco_Toyz); each member of the Griffin family had their own toy, with the exception of Stewie, of whom two different figures were made. Over the course of two years, four more series of toy figures were released, with various forms of Peter. In 2008, the character Peter appeared in advertisements for [Subway Restaurants](/wiki/Subway_(restaurant)), promoting the restaurant's massive feast sandwich.

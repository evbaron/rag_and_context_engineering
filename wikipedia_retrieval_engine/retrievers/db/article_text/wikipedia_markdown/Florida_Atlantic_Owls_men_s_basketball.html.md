The **Florida Atlantic Owls** men's basketball team represents [Florida Atlantic University](/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_University) and competes in the [American Conference](/wiki/American_Conference_(NCAA)) of [NCAA Division I](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I) [college basketball](/wiki/College_basketball). Their home games are played on the Abessinio Court in the [Eleanor R. Baldwin Arena](/wiki/Eleanor_R._Baldwin_Arena) on the school's [Boca Raton, Florida](/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida) campus. The Owls have appeared in the [NCAA tournament](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament) three times: in [2002](/wiki/2002_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament), [2023](/wiki/2023_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament), and [2024](/wiki/2024_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament). The Owls joined Conference USA (C-USA) in 2013 as part of the [early-2010s NCAA conference realignment](/wiki/2010%E2%80%932014_NCAA_conference_realignment). After the 2022–23 season, FAU left [C-USA](/wiki/Conference_USA) for the American Conference. After not winning a single NCAA Tournament game coming into 2023, the Owls qualified for their first Final Four in program history by defeating the [Kansas State Wildcats](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Kansas_State_Wildcats_men%27s_basketball_team) 79–76.

## Overview and history

Main article: [List of Florida Atlantic Owls men's basketball seasons](/wiki/List_of_Florida_Atlantic_Owls_men%27s_basketball_seasons)

Florida Atlantic University basketball began play in the 1988–89 season. The Owls competed as an [NCAA Division II independent](/wiki/NCAA_Division_II) until the 1993–94 season, when they moved up to [Division I](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I) and the Atlantic Sun Conference, now known as the ASUN Conference. After spending 11 seasons in the Atlantic Sun, the Owls moved to the [Sun Belt Conference](/wiki/Sun_Belt_Conference), with the rest of Florida Atlantic University's intercollegiate sports teams. The Owls moved to C-USA in July 2013, and then moved to The American in July 2023.

### Beginning: FAU gets basketball (1988–1993)

Florida Atlantic University played its first-ever intercollegiate basketball contest on November 18, 1988, against fellow local [NCAA Division II](/wiki/NCAA_Division_II) school, [Palm Beach Atlantic University](/wiki/Palm_Beach_Atlantic_Sailfish). The Owls won the game 111–62. Even though the newly formed college squad hung 111 points in their first game, they would finish the inaugural season 9–19, which included a 12-game losing streak in the middle of the year. Head coach, Lonnie Williams, was known to be a "program-builder", but after one year, left FAU after its first season to become head coach at the [University of California, Davis](/wiki/UC_Davis_Aggies_men%27s_basketball). Williams would only last one year at UC Davis, posting an 11–16 record.

Replacing Williams was [Penn State](/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_basketball) assistant coach, Tim Loomis. Loomis started his coaching career with a 21–7 record, and followed that up with back-to-back 15–13 seasons. The three consecutive winning seasons is a feat that no other Owls coach has ever matched, until Dusty May did it in his first three seasons. Along with the winning seasons, Loomis was able to schedule games against neighbor and powerhouse, [University of Miami Hurricanes](/wiki/Miami_Hurricanes_men%27s_basketball). Though Miami would beat the Owls soundly in the appearances, the ability to schedule games against them was seen as the Owls basketball program's growth. Loomis would lose the core of that inaugural team after the Owls' fourth season, and the Owls would suffer devastating 3–24 (1992–93) and 3–25 (1993–94) seasons the latter being their first official year as Division One. The members of these teams were true pioneers and super fundraisers for the athletic department as they hit the road to play teams like [Florida State](/wiki/Florida_State_Seminoles_men%27s_basketball), [Georgetown](/wiki/Georgetown_Hoyas_men%27s_basketball), and [Iowa State](/wiki/Iowa_State_Cyclones_men%27s_basketball) while taking large paydays and suffering losses to higher ranked (#10 Georgetown) opponents. In fact, Loomis' team beat [NC State](/wiki/NC_State_Wolfpack_men%27s_basketball) on the road in 1993–94 to become the first [Atlantic Sun](/wiki/ASUN_Conference) team to ever beat an [ACC](/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Conference) team at the time.

### Move to Division I (1993–2005)

The 1993–94 season would mark the beginning of the Owls competition in the [NCAA Division I](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I). The Owls joined the [Atlantic Sun Conference](/wiki/ASUN_Conference) later, mostly due to the fact that the [Owls baseball](/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_baseball) team was exceeding expectations so early in its development. In 1994–95, the team improved to 9–19 while in Loomis' last year. The team repeated that win total in 1995–96 under new coach Kevin Billerman but showed improvement by taking [South Florida](/wiki/South_Florida_Bulls_men%27s_basketball), [Saint Louis](/wiki/Saint_Louis_Billikens_men%27s_basketball), and [Southern Illinois](/wiki/Southern_Illinois_Salukis_men%27s_basketball) to close road losses determined by the last possession, while winning two conference games to end the season. Former [Duke Blue Devil](/wiki/Duke_Blue_Devils_men%27s_basketball) captain and leader, Kevin Billerman, was hired to further lead the Owls into Division I; his second season produced the most wins of any Division One FAU men's basketball coach up to that point. Due to NCAA's rule, the Owls were not eligible for the NCAA tournament for four years, with their first season of tournament eligibility coming in 1993–94. In Billerman's four seasons, the Owls had two 20+ loss seasons (his last two) and only one winning season (his second).

For the 1999–2000 season, FAU hired decorated [UNLV](/wiki/UNLV_Runnin%27_Rebels_basketball) [All-American](/wiki/All-America) [forward/center](/wiki/Forward-center) and [NBA](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) first-round draft pick [Sidney Green](/wiki/Sidney_Green_(basketball)). In his first season, Green's Owls went 2–28, with a 25-game losing streak in the middle of the season. In each of his first and second seasons, the Owls would win their opening round game in the Atlantic Sun Conference Tournament, but would lose in the second round.

#### 2001–02 season: A-Sun champions

During the 2001–02 season, Green would lead the Owls to his only winning season, as FAU finished 19–12 and 13–7 in the Atlantic Sun. In the first-round of the [2002 Atlantic Sun Conference Tournament](/wiki/2002_Atlantic_Sun_men%27s_basketball_tournament), FAU would again win their opening game, this time defeating [Jacksonville University](/wiki/Jacksonville_Dolphins_men%27s_basketball). The Owls would break their second-round losing tradition, beating [Jacksonville State University](/wiki/Jacksonville_State_Gamecocks_men%27s_basketball). In the Atlantic Sun Conference Championship, the Owls faced [Georgia State University](/wiki/Georgia_State_Panthers_men%27s_basketball) and won 76–75, clinching their first conference championship and a bid to the [NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament).

In the [2002 NCAA tournament](/wiki/2002_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament), the Owls were seeded as a 15-seed, and placed in the [South Region](/wiki/2002_NCAA_Division_I_men%27s_basketball_tournament#South_region), and were paired with 2-seed [Alabama](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Alabama_Crimson_Tide_men%27s_basketball_team). Though the Owls hung tight throughout the game, heavily favored Alabama held on to win 86–78.

### Coaching carousel (2005–2018)

Green was fired after the 2004–05 season and the Owls landed former NCAA Coach of the Year and [Tar Heel](/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_men%27s_basketball) [Matt Doherty](/wiki/Matt_Doherty_(basketball)). Doherty led the Owls to a 15–13 record and then left abruptly to become the head coach at [Southern Methodist University](/wiki/SMU_Mustangs_men%27s_basketball).

With the rapid success of the [football program](/wiki/Florida_Atlantic_Owls_football), created from scratch just in 2001, the Florida Atlantic sports programs again moved up in level of competition. This time, the Owls would move to the [Sun Belt Conference](/wiki/Sun_Belt_Conference), and basketball played its first season of competition in the Sun Belt in the 2006–07 season. To replace Doherty, assistant coach and former [Kansas Jayhawk](/wiki/Kansas_Jayhawks_men%27s_basketball) star, [Rex Walters](/wiki/Rex_Walters), was elevated. In his first season, Walters' Owls were 16–15 and 10–8 in the Sun Belt. The 16–15 marked the first time FAU posted back-to-back winning seasons since the 1989–90 and 1990–91 seasons. The 10–8 conference record was only the fourth time the Owls had a winning record in conference play. Walters' sophomore season regressed a bit, as FAU finished 15–18. After the 2007–08 season completed, Rex Walters accepted the head coaching job at the [University of San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco_Dons_men%27s_basketball), and for the third time in four years, the Owls were searching for a new coach.

FAU hired [Mike Jarvis](/wiki/Mike_Jarvis) as their new coach. Jarvis had been the head coach at [Boston University](/wiki/Boston_University_Terriers_men%27s_basketball), [George Washington University](/wiki/George_Washington_Colonials_men%27s_basketball) and, most recently, [St. John's University](/wiki/St._John%27s_Red_Storm_men%27s_basketball). Between the three schools, Jarvis led his teams to nine total NCAA tournament appearances, including an [Elite Eight](/wiki/Elite_Eight) appearance with St. John's in [1999](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_St._John%27s_Red_Storm_men%27s_basketball_team). Jarvis was fired from St. John's on December 19, 2003—the first [Big East Conference](/wiki/Big_East_Conference_(1979%E2%80%932013)) coach to be fired during the season. It later emerged that school officials had fired Jarvis in part due to a series of embarrassing off-court incidents. Among these, a junior college transfer had been charged with assaulting a female student, and a senior guard had been kicked off the team after being caught smoking [marijuana](/wiki/Cannabis_(drug)) near St. John's campus in [Queens](/wiki/Queens). During the 2003–04 season, St. John's center Abe Keita claimed that a member of Jarvis's basketball staff had paid him nearly $300 a month for the past four seasons. As a result, St. John's placed itself on two years' probation, withdrew from postseason consideration for the 2004–05 season, and forfeited 43 wins including the 2002 NCAA Tournament and the 2003 NIT. Jarvis was faulted for not properly monitoring Keita's situation. After his ouster, Jarvis was criticized for ignoring New York City's rich pool of high school players, which particularly rankled fans used to seeing national powerhouses built primarily on New York City talent. At FAU, he looked to become the first coach in NCAA basketball history to win 100 games at four different schools. Following the 2014 season, Jarvis resigned after reports indicated that he was going to be fired. He finished with a 76–112 after six years in Boca Raton.

On April 7, 2014, the school hired [Michael Curry](/wiki/Michael_Curry_(basketball)), a former NBA player and coach of the [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons). Curry's teams never finished with more than 12 wins overall or more than six wins in conference. They never finished higher than 11th in C-USA. Following the hiring of a new athletic director, Brian White, Curry was fired after four seasons at the school and a 39–84 record.

### Dusty May era and 2023 Final Four run (since 2018)

May in 2023

White, brother to then-[Florida](/wiki/Florida_Gators_men%27s_basketball) head coach [Mike White](/wiki/Mike_White_(basketball)), thanked Curry for his years at FAU, but saw no reason the Owls could not compete in C-USA. On March 22, 2018, the school hired Florida assistant coach [Dusty May](/wiki/Dusty_May) as head coach.

The FAU Owls began to experience much improved results and consistent success under May, as they finished that first season in 2018-19 with an above .500 record at 17-15 and an invite to the 2019 CIT, where they lost to the [Charleston Southern Buccaneers](/wiki/Charleston_Southern_Buccaneers) in the first round, 68-66. The Owls followed that success with winning records in each of the first 5 seasons with Dusty May at the helm.

The 2022-23 FAU Owls, led by May, had their most successful season in program history, going 31-3 en-route to winning that season’s Conference USA Championship for both the regular season and the conference tournament. During that season, FAU was ranked in the Associated Press Top 25 for the first time in school history, as they at one point won 20 consecutive games. In March of 2023, the Owls made the NCAA Tournament for only the second time in program history and remarkably, made it to the Final Four in [Houston](/wiki/Houston) after defeating the [Kansas State Wildcats](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Kansas_State_Wildcats_men%27s_basketball_team) 79-76 in the Elite Eight at [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden). The Owls then narrowly lost to the [San Diego State Aztecs](/wiki/San_Diego_State_Aztecs_men%27s_basketball) in that Final Four, 72-71, as San Diego State’s [Lamont Butler](/wiki/Lamont_Butler) hit a buzzer-beating jumpshot to help the Aztecs advance to the 2023 NCAA Tournament National Championship Game. The 2022-23 FAU Owls finished the season 35-4, the most wins of any program in Men’s Division I College Basketball that season.

Despite making the Final Four and rumors of players transferring the following season, FAU ran it back with their squad for the 2023-24 season. The team finished 25-9, making their second-straight March Madness appearance. However, FAU lost in the first round of the 2024 NCAA Tournament to the [Northwestern Wildcats](/wiki/Northwestern_Wildcats_men%27s_basketball), 77-65, in overtime at the [Barclays Center](/wiki/Barclays_Center).

### John Jakus enters the scene

After making it to two straight NCAA Tournament appearances, head coach Dusty May left FAU to take a job with the [Michigan Wolverines](/wiki/Michigan_Wolverines_men%27s_basketball). It was a five year deal worth $18.75 million in base compensation.

As a result of May's departure, FAU lost their star players to the transfer portal. FAU's leading scorer [Johnell Davis](/wiki/Johnell_Davis) transferred to [Arkansas](/wiki/Arkansas_Razorbacks_men%27s_basketball). [Alijah Martin](/wiki/Alijah_Martin) transferred to Florida. Nick Boyd transferred to San Diego State. Giancarlo Rosado transferred to Charlotte. [Vlad Goldin](/wiki/Vladislav_Goldin) joined May and transferred to Michigan.

In replace of May, FAU hired [John Jakus](/wiki/John_Jakus) as their new head coach on March 27, 2024.

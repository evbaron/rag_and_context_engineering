**Paul Colm Michael Mescal** ([/ˈmɛskəl/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*MESS-kəl*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born 2 February 1996) is an Irish actor. [His accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Paul_Mescal) include two [BAFTA Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards) and a [Laurence Olivier Award](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Awards), in addition to nominations for an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award), a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), and a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards).

Born in [Dublin](/wiki/Dublin) and raised in [Maynooth](/wiki/Maynooth), he studied acting at [The Lir Academy](/wiki/The_Lir_Academy) and then performed in plays in [Dublin](/wiki/Dublin) theatres. Mescal's breakthrough came with a leading role in the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) / [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) [romantic drama](/wiki/Romantic_drama) miniseries *[Normal People](/wiki/Normal_People_(TV_series))* (2020), earning him the [British Academy Television Award for Best Actor](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Actor) and a nomination for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Limited or Anthology Series or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie).

Mescal transitioned to film acting with his debut in the psychological drama *[The Lost Daughter](/wiki/The_Lost_Daughter_(film))* (2021). For his performance as a troubled father in the [coming-of-age](/wiki/Coming-of-age) drama *[Aftersun](/wiki/Aftersun)* (2022), he received a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor). He gained further recognition for his performances in the psychological drama *[All of Us Strangers](/wiki/All_of_Us_Strangers)* (2023), the historical action film *[Gladiator II](/wiki/Gladiator_II)* (2024), and for his portrayal of [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare) in *[Hamnet](/wiki/Hamnet_(film))* (2025).

On stage, he portrayed [Stanley Kowalski](/wiki/Stanley_Kowalski) in a [West End](/wiki/West_End_(theatre)) revival of the [Tennessee Williams](/wiki/Tennessee_Williams) play *[A Streetcar Named Desire](/wiki/A_Streetcar_Named_Desire)* (2022), which won him the [Laurence Olivier Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award_for_Best_Actor).

## Early life and education

Paul Colm Michael Mescal was born on 2 February 1996 in [Dublin](/wiki/Dublin) and raised in [Maynooth](/wiki/Maynooth), [County Kildare](/wiki/County_Kildare), by Dearbhla Mescal Ni Molloy, a [Garda](/wiki/Garda_S%C3%ADoch%C3%A1na), and Paul Mescal, a [teacher](/wiki/Teacher) who also acted semi-professionally. Mescal has a younger sister, the singer-songwriter [Nell Mescal](/wiki/Nell_Mescal), and a younger brother, Donnacha.

Mescal attended [Maynooth Post Primary School](/wiki/Maynooth_Post_Primary_School). He was a minor and under-21 [Gaelic football](/wiki/Gaelic_football) player for [Kildare](/wiki/Kildare_GAA) and a member of the [Maynooth GAA](/wiki/Maynooth_GAA) club. Gaelic footballer [Brian Lacey](/wiki/Brian_Lacey_(Gaelic_footballer)) praised Mescal's skills as a defender, while physical trainer [Cian O'Neill](/wiki/Cian_O%27Neill) described him as "mature beyond his years... very developed and very strong". Mescal gave up the sport after a jaw injury.

He performed on stage for the first time at age 16 in a school production, portraying the eponymous [Phantom](/wiki/Erik_(The_Phantom_of_the_Opera)) in the musical *[The Phantom of the Opera](/wiki/The_Phantom_of_the_Opera_(1986_musical))*, after which he auditioned and gained admission to [The Lir Academy](/wiki/The_Lir_Academy) at [Trinity College Dublin](/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin). He graduated with a Bachelor of Arts in acting in 2017. He secured agents for his acting career prior to his graduation.

## Career

### 2017–2020: Career beginnings

After earning his Bachelor of Arts degree, Mescal was offered roles in two theatre productions, *[Angela's Ashes](/wiki/Angela%27s_Ashes#Musical)* and *[The Great Gatsby](/wiki/The_Great_Gatsby#Stage)*; he took on the latter and starred as the titular [Jay Gatsby](/wiki/Jay_Gatsby) at the [Gate Theatre](/wiki/Gate_Theatre) in Dublin. In *[The Irish Times](/wiki/The_Irish_Times)*, Peter Crawley highlighted his work as a "butterfly of self-creation among an ensemble in constant motion and fluttering improvisation". He also portrayed the Prince in a contemporary retelling of [Hans Christian Andersen](/wiki/Hans_Christian_Andersen)'s *[The Red Shoes](/wiki/The_Red_Shoes_(fairy_tale))* at the same theatre that year. Mescal appeared in the world premiere of the 2018 play *[Asking for It](/wiki/Asking_for_It_(novel))* by [Louise O'Neill](/wiki/Louise_O%27Neill), co-produced by [Landmark Productions](/wiki/Landmark_Productions) and [The Everyman](/wiki/Everyman_Palace_Theatre) in [Cork](/wiki/Cork_(city)). The production later transferred to the [Abbey Theatre](/wiki/Abbey_Theatre) in Dublin; Steve Cummins of *[The Times](/wiki/The_Times)* commended his distinctive performance. The same year, he made his London stage debut in *[The Plough and the Stars](/wiki/The_Plough_and_the_Stars)* at the [Lyric Hammersmith](/wiki/Lyric_Hammersmith) and starred in the [Rough Magic](/wiki/Rough_Magic_(theatre_company)) productions of *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)* for the [Kilkenny Arts Festival](/wiki/Kilkenny_Arts_Festival) and *[A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man](/wiki/A_Portrait_of_the_Artist_as_a_Young_Man#Adaptations)* for the [Dublin Theatre Festival](/wiki/Dublin_Theatre_Festival). In 2020, he performed in the play *[The Lieutenant of Inishmore](/wiki/The_Lieutenant_of_Inishmore)* at Dublin's [Gaiety Theatre](/wiki/Gaiety_Theatre,_Dublin).

Mescal starred in his first television role in the drama miniseries *[Normal People](/wiki/Normal_People_(miniseries))*, an adaptation of the 2018 [novel of the same name](/wiki/Normal_People) by [Sally Rooney](/wiki/Sally_Rooney). It premiered in the UK on [BBC Three](/wiki/BBC_Three) and in the US on [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) in 2020. He played student Connell Waldron; he viewed the role as different from himself in the way Waldron's traits include hesitance and emotional unavailability. Like the actor did in real life, the character plays [Gaelic football](/wiki/Gaelic_football) and attends Trinity College. The role propelled Mescal to fame. He earned acclaim as well as the [BAFTA TV Award for Best Actor](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Actor) for his performance. In their respective reviews for *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* and *[Slate](/wiki/Slate_(magazine))*, [Caroline Framke](/wiki/Caroline_Framke) called Mescal's navigation through the character's emotional collapse "breathtaking", while Willa Paskin noted his concurrent embodiment of "intelligence, insecurity and quiet confidence". He also received nominations for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Limited Series or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie) and the [Critics' Choice Television Award for Best Actor in a Movie/Miniseries](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Movie/Miniseries).

Mescal starred in *Drifting*, a short film, which was screened at the 2020 [Galway Film Fleadh](/wiki/Galway_Film_Fleadh). He played a firefighter in the [Channel 5](/wiki/Channel_5_(British_TV_channel)) miniseries *The Deceived* and appeared in the music video for the song "[Scarlet](/wiki/Scarlet_(Rolling_Stones_song))" by [The Rolling Stones](/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones) in August. Reviewing *The Deceived*, *[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)* critic Ed Power highlighted Mescal's effortless "sleepy-eyed charm" and "flawless" [Donegal](/wiki/County_Donegal) accent.

### 2021–present: Rise to prominence

Mescal, [Maggie Gyllenhaal](/wiki/Maggie_Gyllenhaal), and [Dakota Johnson](/wiki/Dakota_Johnson) promoting *[The Lost Daughter](/wiki/The_Lost_Daughter_(film))*

Mescal made his feature film debut with a supporting role in *[The Lost Daughter](/wiki/The_Lost_Daughter_(film))*, directed by [Maggie Gyllenhaal](/wiki/Maggie_Gyllenhaal) in her directorial debut. Released in 2021, the [psychological drama](/wiki/Psychological_drama) garnered favourable reviews. A year later, Mescal starred as a man accused of [sexual assault](/wiki/Sexual_assault) in the psychological drama *[God's Creatures](/wiki/God%27s_Creatures_(film))* and as a troubled young father in the drama *[Aftersun](/wiki/Aftersun)*, both of which premiered at the [2022 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2022_Cannes_Film_Festival). The two films received positive reviews with Mescal's performances gaining praise. [IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire) critic Kate Erbland found Mescal's work in *God's Creatures* "powerful" and "quietly chilling". Reviewing *Aftersun*, [Peter Travers](/wiki/Peter_Travers) of [ABC News](/wiki/ABC_News_(United_States)) highlighted his "disarming charm and elemental power" in his portrayal of a "complex role". He received nominations for the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) and the [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) for the latter.

Mescal starred in *[Carmen](/wiki/Carmen_(2022_film))*, which premiered at the [2022 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2022_Toronto_International_Film_Festival) and was theatrically released in 2023. IndieWire's [David Ehrlich](/wiki/David_Ehrlich) said that Mescal asserts his "gravitas" and "poise" playing a veteran of the US military. Mescal portrayed [Stanley Kowalski](/wiki/Stanley_Kowalski) in a revival of the play *[A Streetcar Named Desire](/wiki/A_Streetcar_Named_Desire)* by [Tennessee Williams](/wiki/Tennessee_Williams) at the [Almeida Theatre](/wiki/Almeida_Theatre) beginning in December 2022. The production met with acclaim and transferred to the [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) in March 2023. *The Times*'s Dominic Maxwell found him "tremendous" and opined that he "makes the latent violence of Stanley Kowalski into something easy, tangible, vibrant yet unactorly". Mescal won the [Laurence Olivier Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Award_for_Best_Actor) for the role.

Mescal at the 2022 [Directors' Fortnight](/wiki/Directors%27_Fortnight) in [Cannes](/wiki/Cannes)

In 2023, Mescal starred alongside [Andrew Scott](/wiki/Andrew_Scott_(actor)) in *[All of Us Strangers](/wiki/All_of_Us_Strangers)*, a loose adaptation of the novel *[Strangers](/wiki/Strangers_(Yamada_novel))* by [Taichi Yamada](/wiki/Taichi_Yamada). He pursued the project as he was keen to play a "vivacious" and "front-footed" character for the first time in his career. It premiered at the [50th Telluride Film Festival](/wiki/50th_Telluride_Film_Festival). *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*'s David Rooney commended the "warm, sexy chemistry" between Mescal and Scott. For the role, Mescal received a nomination for the [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role). He also starred opposite [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan) in *[Foe](/wiki/Foe_(film))*, an adaptation of [Iain Reid](/wiki/Iain_Reid)'s science fiction [novel of the same name](/wiki/Foe_(Reid_novel)); the film was not well received by critics.

In 2024, Mescal was honoured by the [Academy Museum of Motion Pictures](/wiki/Academy_Museum_of_Motion_Pictures) with the Vantage Award, for emerging artists. He expanded to big-budget films with his starring role in *[Gladiator II](/wiki/Gladiator_II)*, [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott)'s sequel to the [2000 historical drama](/wiki/Gladiator_(2000_film)), taking over the role played in childhood by [Spencer Treat Clark](/wiki/Spencer_Treat_Clark). Scott cast him after watching his performance in *Normal People*. In preparation, Mescal practiced strength training, fight choreography, horse training, and sword fighting for six months. *[Slant Magazine](/wiki/Slant_Magazine)*'s Jake Cole wrote, "Mescal gives a performance that recalls [Russell Crowe](/wiki/Russell_Crowe)'s no-nonsense Maximus from *Gladiator*, right down to speaking in some of the same cadences and inflections". The film earned $460 million worldwide.

Mescal starred in *[The History of Sound](/wiki/The_History_of_Sound)*, a 2025 romantic drama opposite [Josh O'Connor](/wiki/Josh_O%27Connor), and portrayed [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare) in [Chloé Zhao](/wiki/Chlo%C3%A9_Zhao)'s 2025 period drama *[Hamnet](/wiki/Hamnet_(film))*, based on the [novel of the same name](/wiki/Hamnet_(novel)). He replaced [Blake Jenner](/wiki/Blake_Jenner) in the lead role of composer Franklin Shepard in [Richard Linklater](/wiki/Richard_Linklater)'s *[Merrily We Roll Along](/wiki/Merrily_We_Roll_Along_(film))*, a film adaptation of the [1981 musical of the same name](/wiki/Merrily_We_Roll_Along_(musical)), which is set to be filmed over 20 years. Mescal has also been cast as [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney) in [Sam Mendes](/wiki/Sam_Mendes)' four-part biopic on [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles), with the first scheduled to be released in April 2028. Mescal will return to the stage in 2027 in revivals of *[A Whistle in the Dark](/wiki/A_Whistle_in_the_Dark)* at the [Abbey Theatre](/wiki/Abbey_Theatre) and [National Theatre](/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre), and *[Death of a Salesman](/wiki/Death_of_a_Salesman)* at the National Theatre.

## Personal life

Mescal speaks [English](/wiki/English_language) and [Irish](/wiki/Irish_language). He moved from his native Ireland to London in 2020. In 2022, he said that he had bought a property in Ireland, with the intention of spending time there when he is not working. He was in a relationship with American singer [Phoebe Bridgers](/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers) for approximately two years, until December 2022. He appeared in Bridgers' music video for the song "Savior Complex", and provided backing vocals for Bridgers' cover of "So Much Wine", originally by [The Handsome Family](/wiki/The_Handsome_Family). Proceeds from the song were donated to the [Los Angeles LGBT Center](/wiki/Los_Angeles_LGBT_Center). Since 2024, Mescal has been in a relationship with American singer and songwriter [Gracie Abrams](/wiki/Gracie_Abrams).

He is open about seeing a therapist, which he says is "to keep sane". He has said that fans should not feel entitled to know about his personal life and that he is not comfortable inviting any access into that part of his life.

Mescal plays the piano and has performed covers of songs with his sister, singer [Nell Mescal](/wiki/Nell_Mescal). In July 2020, he performed spoken word and sang with singer [Dermot Kennedy](/wiki/Dermot_Kennedy) at the [Natural History Museum](/wiki/Natural_History_Museum,_London) in London. He participated in a virtual stage reading of the play *[This Is Our Youth](/wiki/This_Is_Our_Youth)* by [Kenneth Lonergan](/wiki/Kenneth_Lonergan) as part of a series benefiting the [Actors Fund of America](/wiki/Actors_Fund_of_America) charity in October 2020.

This article is about the metropolitan borough in the West Midlands. For the town it is named for, see [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull).

The **Metropolitan Borough of Solihull**, often colloquially just called **Solihull**, is a metropolitan borough in the east of the [West Midlands county](/wiki/West_Midlands_(county)), England. It is named after its largest town, [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull), from which [Solihull Metropolitan Borough Council](/wiki/Solihull_Metropolitan_Borough_Council) is based. Much of the borough is considered to be part of the [West Midlands conurbation](/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation), and the borough's area was historically in Northwestern [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire). For [Eurostat](/wiki/Eurostat) purposes it is a NUTS 3 region (code UKG32) and is one of seven boroughs or unitary districts that comprise the "West Midlands" [NUTS 2 region](/wiki/NUTS_statistical_regions_of_the_United_Kingdom).

Much of the large residential population in the north of the borough centres on the communities of [Chelmsley Wood](/wiki/Chelmsley_Wood), [Castle Bromwich](/wiki/Castle_Bromwich), [Marston Green](/wiki/Marston_Green) and [Smith's Wood](/wiki/Smith%27s_Wood) as well as the towns of [Kingshurst](/wiki/Kingshurst) and [Fordbridge](/wiki/Fordbridge). In the south are the towns of [Shirley](/wiki/Shirley,_West_Midlands) and Solihull. The borough also contains the large villages of [Knowle](/wiki/Knowle,_West_Midlands), [Dorridge](/wiki/Dorridge), [Meriden](/wiki/Meriden,_West_Midlands) and [Balsall Common](/wiki/Balsall_Common), all of which are outside of the West Midlands conurbation, to its east.

Since 2011, Solihull has formed part of the [Greater Birmingham & Solihull Local Enterprise Partnership](/wiki/Greater_Birmingham_%26_Solihull_Local_Enterprise_Partnership) along with neighbouring authorities [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham_City_Council), [Bromsgrove](/wiki/Bromsgrove_District), [Cannock Chase](/wiki/Cannock_Chase_District), [East Staffordshire](/wiki/East_Staffordshire), [Lichfield](/wiki/Lichfield_District_Council), [Redditch](/wiki/Redditch), [Tamworth](/wiki/Tamworth_(borough)) and [Wyre Forest](/wiki/Wyre_Forest_District).

## History

The [coat of arms](/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of Solihull Metropolitan Borough Council

Solihull probably derived its name from a 'miry or muddy' or soily hill. The parish church was built on a hill of stiff red [marl](/wiki/Marl), which turned to sticky mud in wet weather.

Solihull was an [ancient parish](/wiki/Ancient_parish), covering the town itself and adjoining rural areas, including [Shirley](/wiki/Shirley,_West_Midlands). Solihull was made the centre of a [poor law union](/wiki/Poor_law_union) in 1836, covering eleven parishes: [Baddesley Clinton](/wiki/Baddesley_Clinton), [Balsall](/wiki/Balsall), [Barston](/wiki/Barston), [Elmdon](/wiki/Elmdon,_West_Midlands), [Knowle](/wiki/Knowle,_West_Midlands), [Lapworth](/wiki/Lapworth), [Nuthurst](/wiki/Hockley_Heath#Nuthurst), [Packwood](/wiki/Packwood,_England), Solihull, [Tanworth](/wiki/Tanworth-in-Arden) and [Yardley](/wiki/Yardley,_Birmingham). Yardley was in [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire) and the rest of the parishes were in [Warwickshire](/wiki/Warwickshire).

Such poor law unions formed the basis for later local government areas. In 1872 poor law unions also became [rural sanitary districts](/wiki/Rural_sanitary_district) for the parts of their areas without urban authorities; there were no urban authorities in the Solihull union. In 1894 rural sanitary districts were converted into [rural districts](/wiki/Rural_district) with their own elected councils under the [Local Government Act 1894](/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1894). The 1894 Act split districts which straddled county boundaries, and so Yardley became a separate rural district (which was later absorbed into [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) in 1911), whilst the Warwickshire parishes from the Solihull poor law union became the Solihull Rural District. The 1894 Act also created parish councils for [rural parishes](/wiki/Rural_parish), including Solihull.

The Solihull Rural District and the Solihull Parish Council were abolished in 1932. A new [urban district](/wiki/Urban_district_(England_and_Wales)) of Solihull was created; the parishes of Solihull, Elmdon, Knowle, Nuthurst, Packwood and Sheldon were abolished and most of their combined area became the new urban district, subject to various adjustments to the boundaries with neighbouring parishes. The parts of the old Solihull Rural District not included were added to neighbouring rural districts.

Expansion continued and [Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II_of_the_United_Kingdom) granted a charter in 1954 making Solihull into a [Municipal Borough](/wiki/Municipal_Borough); ten years later it was given the status of [County Borough](/wiki/County_Borough). Reorganisation of boundaries and council responsibilities in 1974 created the Metropolitan Borough of Solihull by the merger of the [Solihull](/wiki/Solihull) [County Borough](/wiki/County_Borough) and most of the [Meriden Rural District](/wiki/Meriden_Rural_District), which forms the main rural part of the borough and county. It included Balsall Common, Barston, Berkswell, Bickenhill, Castle Bromwich, Chelmsley Wood, Elmdon, Fordbridge, Hampton in Arden, Hockley Heath, Kingshurst, Knowle, Marston Green, Meriden, Olton, Smiths Wood, Solihull, Shirley and Temple Balsall.

In 1986 the Solihull borough effectively became a [unitary authority](/wiki/Unitary_authority) when the [West Midlands County Council](/wiki/West_Midlands_County_Council) was abolished. It remains part of the West Midlands for [ceremonial purposes](/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England), and for functions such as police, fire and public transport. There is some support to return the borough to Warwickshire for ceremonial purposes, as was the case when the County of Avon was abolished and Bath was returned to Somerset. There were also complaints that Solihull was not involved in the design of the [Warwickshire flag](/wiki/Flag_of_Warwickshire) in 2016.

## Geography

The borough is bordered by the [M6](/wiki/M6_motorway) and the [M40](/wiki/M40_motorway) and split by the [M42](/wiki/M42_motorway) which divides the urban centre of the borough from the rural south and east. The borough's transport links have led to a number of established large businesses being based in the borough, such as [Land Rover](/wiki/Land_Rover), the [National Exhibition Centre](/wiki/National_Exhibition_Centre) and [Birmingham Airport](/wiki/Birmingham_Airport). A short automatic light transport system links the airport to the nearby [Birmingham International railway station](/wiki/Birmingham_International_railway_station). Around three-quarters of the borough is [greenbelt](/wiki/Green_belt) and a large proportion of that is worked farmland. The borough shares its boundaries with [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham) to the west and north, [Coventry](/wiki/Coventry) to the east, Warwickshire to both the north and south and [Worcestershire](/wiki/Worcestershire) to the south west. The borough contains a sizeable rural area known as the *[Meriden Gap](/wiki/Meriden_Gap)* (after the village of [Meriden](/wiki/Meriden,_West_Midlands)) which serves as a [green belt](/wiki/Green_belt) separating the Birmingham conurbation from the city of Coventry. Parts of Solihull neighbour the suburbs of [Minworth](/wiki/Minworth) and [Hall Green](/wiki/Hall_Green). Parts also lie close to (but not contiguous with) the town of [Coleshill](/wiki/Coleshill,_Warwickshire) and city of [Coventry](/wiki/Coventry).

## Coat of arms

The constituent parts of the borough's coat of arms are:

- Battlements, sickles and an oak tree with golden acorns, which represent the rural and agricultural nature of the Forest of Arden.
- The Black Griffin is taken from the arms of the [Earls of Aylesford](/wiki/Earl_of_Aylesford), who are associated with Meriden.
- The Silver Fleur-de-lys comes from the Digby family, who were associated with Fordbridge.
- The Black Greyhound is taken from the arms of the Greswolds, the family who built the 15th century house called the Manor House in the High Street, Solihull.

A stylised version of the coat of arms can be seen on the top left of Solihull Metropolitan Borough Council's website pages, and the official, heraldic version appears on a dedicated page on the same site – external links below.

## Economy

Solihull is regarded as having one of the strongest subregional economies in the West Midlands, with a significantly higher nominal GVA per capita and Silhillians enjoying considerably higher disposable income rates than both regional and UK averages.

Analysis also shows Solihull as having the lowest (and fastest falling) claimant count for [Jobseeker's Allowance](/wiki/Jobseeker%27s_Allowance) in the region.

**Margot Elise Robbie** ([/ˈmɑːrɡoʊ ˈrɒbi/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*MAR-goh ROB-ee*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born 2 July 1990) is an Australian actress and producer. The [world's highest-paid actress](/wiki/List_of_highest-paid_film_actors#Highest_annual_earnings) in 2023, she is known for [her performances](/wiki/Margot_Robbie_filmography) in both [blockbuster](/wiki/Blockbuster_(entertainment)) and [independent films](/wiki/Independent_film). [Robbie has been nominated](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Margot_Robbie) for three [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards), four [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and six [British Academy Film Awards](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards).

Born and raised in [Queensland](/wiki/Queensland), Robbie began her acting career in 2008 on the television soap opera *[Neighbours](/wiki/Neighbours)*, where she appeared as a regular until 2011. After relocating to the US, she starred in the television drama *[Pan Am](/wiki/Pan_Am_(TV_series))* (2011–2012) and gained international recognition with her breakthrough role in [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese)'s black comedy *[The Wolf of Wall Street](/wiki/The_Wolf_of_Wall_Street_(2013_film))* (2013). She garnered wider acclaim for her performances as [Jane Porter](/wiki/Jane_Porter_(Tarzan)) in *[The Legend of Tarzan](/wiki/The_Legend_of_Tarzan_(film))* (2016) and [Harley Quinn](/wiki/Harley_Quinn_(DC_Extended_Universe)) in three [superhero films](/wiki/Superhero_films) of the [DC Extended Universe](/wiki/DC_Extended_Universe): *[Suicide Squad](/wiki/Suicide_Squad_(2016_film))* (2016), *[Birds of Prey](/wiki/Birds_of_Prey_(2020_film))* (2020) and *[The Suicide Squad](/wiki/The_Suicide_Squad_(film))* (2021).

Robbie earned a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress) for portraying figure skater [Tonya Harding](/wiki/Tonya_Harding) in the biopic *[I, Tonya](/wiki/I,_Tonya)* (2017). She received critical acclaim for her performances as [Elizabeth I](/wiki/Elizabeth_I) in *[Mary Queen of Scots](/wiki/Mary_Queen_of_Scots_(2018_film))* (2018), [Sharon Tate](/wiki/Sharon_Tate) in *[Once Upon a Time in Hollywood](/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Hollywood)* (2019), and a [Fox News](/wiki/Fox_News) employee in *[Bombshell](/wiki/Bombshell_(2019_film))* (2019), the latter earning her a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress). She starred as an aspiring actress in the period drama *[Babylon](/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film))* (2022) and played the [titular fashion doll](/wiki/Barbie) in the fantasy comedy [*Barbie*](/wiki/Barbie_(film)) (2023), her highest-grossing release, which earned her a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) as a producer. She has since produced and starred in *[Wuthering Heights](/wiki/Wuthering_Heights_(2026_film))* (2026).

In 2017, [*Time*](/wiki/Time_(magazine)) included Robbie among its list of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100). She is married to filmmaker [Tom Ackerley](/wiki/Tom_Ackerley), with whom she has one child. Together, they co-founded the production company [LuckyChap Entertainment](/wiki/LuckyChap_Entertainment) in 2014. Under the label, they have produced several projects, including the films *I, Tonya*, *[Promising Young Woman](/wiki/Promising_Young_Woman)* (2020), *Barbie*, and *[Saltburn](/wiki/Saltburn_(film))* (2023), as well as the [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) series *[Dollface](/wiki/Dollface)* (2019–2022) and the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) miniseries *[Maid](/wiki/Maid_(miniseries))* (2021). Outside the entertainment industry, Robbie is an advocate for [human rights](/wiki/Human_rights), [women's rights](/wiki/Women%27s_rights), [gender equality](/wiki/Gender_equality) and [LGBT rights](/wiki/LGBTQ_movements).

## Early life and education

Margot Elise Robbie was born in [Dalby, Queensland](/wiki/Dalby,_Queensland), on 2 July 1990. Her father, Doug Robbie, was a former farm-owner and [sugarcane](/wiki/Sugarcane) [tycoon](/wiki/Tycoon), while her mother, Sarie Kessler, was a [physiotherapist](/wiki/Physiotherapist). She has paternal Scottish heritage. Margot is the third of four children: she has an older sister and brother, and a younger brother. When she was five years old, her father, whom she has described as "awful", separated from the family and she moved to her mother's hometown of the [Gold Coast](/wiki/Gold_Coast,_Queensland). Robbie recalled that she and her siblings "didn't make life easy" for their single mother, remembering that they often fought and that her mother had to be a "very strong woman to hold things together". The family spent most of her childhood on her grandparents' [Currumbin Valley](/wiki/Currumbin_Valley) farm in the [Gold Coast hinterland](/wiki/Gold_Coast_hinterland).

As a child, Robbie frequently went [boar hunting](/wiki/Boar_hunting) and [surfing](/wiki/Surfing) with her siblings, and developed an early interest in [agriculture](/wiki/Agriculture) and [animal husbandry](/wiki/Animal_husbandry)—an upbringing she described as one "you'd never expect to lead anyone into acting". Around this time, her classmates began nicknaming her "Maggot", which originated from a teacher's mispronunciation during roll call and stayed with her into adulthood. Robbie often staged performances at home, and her mother later enrolled her in a [circus school](/wiki/Circus_school), where she excelled in [trapeze](/wiki/Trapeze) and earned a certificate at the age of eight. During her teenage years, she became obsessed with the *[Harry Potter](/wiki/Harry_Potter)* novels, admitting that she once lied to an [optometrist](/wiki/Optometrist) in order to get glasses like the [titular wizard](/wiki/Harry_Potter_(character)). For her secondary education, Robbie attended [Somerset College](/wiki/Somerset_College), where she studied [drama](/wiki/Drama). During this period, she held three jobs: tending bar, cleaning houses, and working at [Subway](/wiki/Subway_(restaurant)). After graduating, with experience in commercials and [independent](/wiki/Independent_film) [thriller films](/wiki/Thriller_film), she relocated to [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne) to pursue acting professionally.

## Career

Main articles: [Margot Robbie filmography](/wiki/Margot_Robbie_filmography) and [List of awards and nominations received by Margot Robbie](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Margot_Robbie)

Robbie at the [2011 Logie Awards](/wiki/Logie_Awards_of_2011)

### 2008–2012: Early work and *Neighbours*

Robbie's first acting roles came when she was in high school. She starred in two low-budget independent thriller films, called *Vigilante* and *[I.C.U.](/wiki/I.C.U._(film))*, both released years later. She described the experience of being on a [film set](/wiki/Film_set) as "a dream come true". She made her television debut in a 2008 guest role as Caitlin Brentford in the drama series *[City Homicide](/wiki/City_Homicide)* and followed this with a two-episode arc in the children's television series *[The Elephant Princess](/wiki/The_Elephant_Princess)*, in which she starred alongside [Liam Hemsworth](/wiki/Liam_Hemsworth).

With agent encouragement at the time and as Robbie recalled on *[The Graham Norton Show](/wiki/The_Graham_Norton_Show)*, she called [FremantleMedia](/wiki/FremantleMedia) on a daily basis. "One day, I got put through by accident to the casting director for Neighbours," and she said, "I'm in town working on something." The casting director asked how old she was, and she responded "seventeen". She was told, "We're looking for exactly that, come in and audition" for the television soap opera *[Neighbours](/wiki/Neighbours)*. In June 2008, she began playing [Donna Freedman](/wiki/Donna_Freedman), a role that was meant to be a guest character, but Robbie was promoted to the regular cast after she made her debut. In her three-year stint on the soap, she received two [Logie Award](/wiki/Logie_Award) nominations.

Shortly after arriving in America, Robbie landed the role of Laura Cameron, a newly trained flight attendant in the period drama series *[Pan Am](/wiki/Pan_Am_(TV_series))* (2011). The series premiered to high ratings and positive reviews but was cancelled after one season due to falling ratings.

### 2013–2015: Breakthrough

Robbie next appeared in [Richard Curtis](/wiki/Richard_Curtis)' romantic comedy *[About Time](/wiki/About_Time_(2013_film))* (2013), co-starring [Domhnall Gleeson](/wiki/Domhnall_Gleeson) and [Rachel McAdams](/wiki/Rachel_McAdams). It tells the story of a young man with the ability to time travel who tries to change his past in hopes of improving his future. To play Gleeson's unattainable teenage love interest, she adopted a British accent. The film was a modest commercial success. Robbie's breakthrough came the same year with the role of [Naomi Lapaglia](/wiki/Naomi_Lapaglia), the wife of protagonist [Jordan Belfort](/wiki/Jordan_Belfort), in [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese)'s biographical black comedy *[The Wolf of Wall Street](/wiki/The_Wolf_of_Wall_Street_(2013_film))*. In her audition for the role, Robbie improvised a slap on co-star [Leonardo DiCaprio](/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio) during a fight scene which ultimately won her the part. The film and her performance received positive reviews; she was particularly praised for her on-screen [Brooklyn accent](/wiki/New_York_City_English). Critic [Sasha Stone](/wiki/Sasha_Stone_(blogger)) wrote of Robbie's performance, "She's Scorsese's best blonde bombshell discovery since [Cathy Moriarty](/wiki/Cathy_Moriarty) in *[Raging Bull](/wiki/Raging_Bull)*. Robbie is funny, hard and kills every scene she's in." *The Wolf of Wall Street* was a box office success, grossing $392 million worldwide, making it Scorsese's highest-grossing film to date. Robbie was nominated for the [MTV Movie Award for Best Breakthrough Performance](/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award_for_Best_Breakthrough_Performance) and won the [Empire Award for Best Newcomer](/wiki/Empire_Award_for_Best_Newcomer).

Robbie at the 2015 [San Diego Comic-Con](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con)

She later said that the fame and attention the movie brought her led her to consider quitting acting, but her mother was philosophical about her profession and explained to her that it was probably too late to quit. She fully understood and stuck with it. With the aim to produce more female-driven projects, Robbie and her future husband, Tom Ackerley, and their respective longtime friends Sophia Kerr and Josey McNamara, started their own production company [LuckyChap Entertainment](/wiki/LuckyChap_Entertainment). The company was founded in 2014, and its name was inspired by [Charlie Chaplin](/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin).

Robbie appeared in four films released in 2015. The first of these was opposite [Will Smith](/wiki/Will_Smith) in [Glenn Ficarra](/wiki/Glenn_Ficarra) and [John Requa](/wiki/John_Requa)'s $158.8 million-grossing romantic comedy-drama film *[Focus](/wiki/Focus_(2015_film))*. In the film, she played an inexperienced [grifter](/wiki/Confidence_trick) learning the craft from Smith's character; she learned how to [pickpocket](/wiki/Pickpocket) from [Apollo Robbins](/wiki/Apollo_Robbins) for the role. Reviews of the film were generally mixed, but Robbie's performance was praised; [Peter Travers](/wiki/Peter_Travers) of *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* wrote, "Robbie is wow and then some. Even when *Focus* fumbles, [she] deals a winning hand."
She was nominated for the [Rising Star Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Rising_Star_Award) at the [68th British Academy Film Awards](/wiki/68th_British_Academy_Film_Awards). Her next appearance was alongside [Michelle Williams](/wiki/Michelle_Williams_(actress)) and [Kristin Scott Thomas](/wiki/Kristin_Scott_Thomas) in [Saul Dibb](/wiki/Saul_Dibb)'s war romantic drama *[Suite Française](/wiki/Suite_Fran%C3%A7aise_(film))*, a film based on the second part of [Irène Némirovsky](/wiki/Ir%C3%A8ne_N%C3%A9mirovsky)'s 2004 [novel of the same name](/wiki/Suite_fran%C3%A7aise_(N%C3%A9mirovsky)). In the film, she played a woman falling for a German soldier during the [German occupation of France during World War II](/wiki/German_occupation_of_France_during_World_War_II), a role which Leslie Felperin of *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* found "underwritten".

She followed this with [Craig Zobel](/wiki/Craig_Zobel)'s post-apocalyptic drama *[Z for Zachariah](/wiki/Z_for_Zachariah_(film))* opposite [Chris Pine](/wiki/Chris_Pine) and [Chiwetel Ejiofor](/wiki/Chiwetel_Ejiofor), in her first leading role. Partially based on [Robert C. O'Brien](/wiki/Robert_C._O%27Brien_(author))'s [book of the same name](/wiki/Z_for_Zachariah), the film follows Ann Burden (Robbie) as she finds herself in an emotionally charged love triangle with the last known survivors of a nuclear disaster that wipes out most of civilisation. In preparation for the film, Robbie dyed her hair brown and learned to speak in an [Appalachian](/wiki/Appalachian_Americans) accent. The film received positive reviews, and Robbie's performance was widely praised, with Drew McWeeny of [HitFix](/wiki/HitFix) asserting that "Robbie's work here establishes her as one of the very best actresses in her age range today." Her fourth release of 2015 was a [cameo appearance](/wiki/Cameo_appearance) in [Adam McKay](/wiki/Adam_McKay)'s comedy-drama *[The Big Short](/wiki/The_Big_Short_(film))*, in which she breaks the [fourth wall](/wiki/Fourth_wall) to explain [subprime mortgages](/wiki/Subprime_mortgages) while in a bathtub. *The Big Short* was a commercial and critical success and Robbie's cameo became a [trending topic](/wiki/Twitter_trends) six years later, in the wake of the [GameStop short squeeze](/wiki/GameStop_short_squeeze), as her explanation provided reference points for what was happening with the GameStop and related stocks.

### 2016–2018: Worldwide recognition

In 2016, Robbie reunited with Ficarra and Requa, playing a British [war correspondent](/wiki/War_correspondent) in the film adaptation of *[The Taliban Shuffle](/wiki/The_Taliban_Shuffle)*, called *[Whiskey Tango Foxtrot](/wiki/Whiskey_Tango_Foxtrot_(film))*, co-starring [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey) and [Martin Freeman](/wiki/Martin_Freeman). Later that year, Robbie took on the part of [Jane Porter](/wiki/Jane_Porter_(Tarzan)) in [David Yates](/wiki/David_Yates)'s adventure film *[The Legend of Tarzan](/wiki/The_Legend_of_Tarzan_(film))*. She was adamant about not losing weight and ensuring the role was not a [damsel in distress](/wiki/Damsel_in_distress) like in previous [Tarzan](/wiki/Tarzan) adaptations. Reviews of the film were generally unfavourable, but [Manohla Dargis](/wiki/Manohla_Dargis) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* credited Robbie for "holding her own" in her supporting role alongside the all-male cast with [Alexander Skarsgård](/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) and [Samuel L. Jackson](/wiki/Samuel_L._Jackson).

Robbie in 2016

Robbie became the first person to portray [DC Comics](/wiki/DC_Comics) villain [Harley Quinn](/wiki/Harley_Quinn) in [live-action](/wiki/Live-action) when she signed on to [David Ayer](/wiki/David_Ayer)'s 2016 superhero film *[Suicide Squad](/wiki/Suicide_Squad_(2016_film))* alongside an [ensemble cast](/wiki/Ensemble_cast) that included Will Smith, [Jared Leto](/wiki/Jared_Leto) and [Viola Davis](/wiki/Viola_Davis). She admitted to having never read the comics, but felt a huge responsibility to do the character justice and satisfy the fans. Robbie began preparing for the role of the [supervillainess](/wiki/Supervillainess) six months prior to the film shoot; her schedule consisted of [gymnastics](/wiki/Gymnastics), [boxing](/wiki/Boxing), [aerial silk](/wiki/Aerial_silk) training and learning how to hold her breath underwater for five minutes. She performed the majority of her own stunts in the film. *Suicide Squad* was a commercial success and was [tenth-highest-grossing film of 2016](/wiki/2016_in_film#Highest-grossing_films) with global revenues of $746.8 million, and Robbie's performance was considered its prime asset. Writing for *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))*, [Stephanie Zacharek](/wiki/Stephanie_Zacharek) found Robbie to be "a criminally appealing actress, likable in just about every way" despite finding flaws in the character and [Christopher Orr](/wiki/Christopher_Orr_(film_critic)) of *[The Atlantic](/wiki/The_Atlantic)* called her performance "genuinely terrific". At the annual [People's Choice Awards](/wiki/People%27s_Choice_Awards) ceremony, she won the [Favourite Action Movie Actress](/wiki/43rd_People%27s_Choice_Awards) award and was also awarded the [Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actress in an Action Movie](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_an_Action_Movie).
In October 2016, Robbie hosted the [season 42](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_(season_42)) premiere of [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s late-night sketch comedy *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*; her appearances included a parody of [Ivanka Trump](/wiki/Ivanka_Trump). The series logged its strongest season premiere ratings in eight years. Robbie collaborated with Domhnall Gleeson in [Simon Curtis](/wiki/Simon_Curtis_(filmmaker))' *[Goodbye Christopher Robin](/wiki/Goodbye_Christopher_Robin)* (2017), a biographical drama about the lives of *[Winnie-the-Pooh](/wiki/Winnie-the-Pooh)* creator [A. A. Milne](/wiki/A._A._Milne) and his family. The film, and her performance, received modest reviews and was a commercial failure.

Her final release of 2017 and LuckyChap Entertainment's first release was [Craig Gillespie](/wiki/Craig_Gillespie)'s sports black comedy *[I, Tonya](/wiki/I,_Tonya)*, based on the life of American figure skater [Tonya Harding](/wiki/Tonya_Harding) (Robbie) and her connection to the [1994 assault](/wiki/Assault_of_Nancy_Kerrigan) on rival [Nancy Kerrigan](/wiki/Nancy_Kerrigan). In preparation, Robbie met with Harding, watched old footage and interviews of her, worked with a voice coach to speak in Harding's [Pacific Northwest](/wiki/Pacific_Northwest_English) accent and vocal timbre at different ages, and underwent several months of rigorous skating instruction with choreographer [Sarah Kawahara](/wiki/Sarah_Kawahara). *I, Tonya* premiered at the [2017 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2017_Toronto_International_Film_Festival) to critical acclaim. James Luxford of *[Metro](/wiki/Metro_(British_newspaper))* deemed it Robbie's best performance to date, and [Mark Kermode](/wiki/Mark_Kermode) of *[The Observer](/wiki/The_Observer)* wrote, "Margot Robbie's performance in this satirical, postmodern tale of the disgraced star is a tour-de-force tornado that balances finely nuanced character development with impressively punchy physicality". She received numerous accolades for her performance, including nominations for an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress), a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Comedy_or_Musical), a [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) and a [Critics' Choice Movie Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Actress), all for Best Actress.

Robbie began 2018 with the voice role of [Flopsy Rabbit](/wiki/Flopsy_Rabbit) in *[Peter Rabbit](/wiki/Peter_Rabbit_(film))*, an animated comedy from director [Will Gluck](/wiki/Will_Gluck), which is based on the [Beatrix Potter](/wiki/Beatrix_Potter) [book series](/wiki/Peter_Rabbit). The animated feature was a box office success, grossing $351.3 million worldwide against a production budget of $50 million. Her next two 2018 films—the neo-noir thriller *[Terminal](/wiki/Terminal_(film))* and comedy-horror *[Slaughterhouse Rulez](/wiki/Slaughterhouse_Rulez)*—were critical and commercial failures. The historical drama *[Mary Queen of Scots](/wiki/Mary_Queen_of_Scots_(2018_film))*, directed by [Josie Rourke](/wiki/Josie_Rourke), was her final release of 2018. The film featured [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan) as the [titular character](/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots) and Robbie as her cousin [Queen Elizabeth I](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_I), and it chronicles the [1569 conflict between their two countries](/wiki/Rising_of_the_North). Robbie had initially turned down the role for being "terrified" of not living up to the history of portrayals of the Queen. Before each day of shooting, she spent three hours in the make-up chair while a prosthetic nose, painted on boils and blisters were applied. Critics dismissed the film for its screenplay and several historical inaccuracies, but praised the performances of Robbie and Ronan. Yolanda Machado of *[TheWrap](/wiki/TheWrap)* wrote, "[B]ow down to Ronan and Robbie for taking two legendarily complex characters, [...] and completely owning both roles. Ronan's fiery Mary and Robbie's emotionally complex Elizabeth truly reign divine on screen." For her portrayal, Robbie received nominations for a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role) and for a [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role).

### 2019–present: Established actress and producer

Robbie at the premiere of *[Once Upon a Time in Hollywood](/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Hollywood)* in 2019

Robbie's first release of 2019 was the LuckyChap Entertainment production *[Dreamland](/wiki/Dreamland_(2019_American_film))*, a poorly received period crime thriller set during the 1930s [Dust Bowl](/wiki/Dust_Bowl). She began executive producing the comedy series *[Dollface](/wiki/Dollface)*, which streamed on [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) from 2019 to 2022. Robbie was filmmaker [Quentin Tarantino](/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino)'s only choice to portray the late actress [Sharon Tate](/wiki/Sharon_Tate) in his period film *[Once Upon a Time in Hollywood](/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Hollywood)*, starring Leonardo DiCaprio and [Brad Pitt](/wiki/Brad_Pitt). With the [Tate–LaBianca murders](/wiki/Tate%E2%80%93LaBianca_murders) serving as a backdrop, the film tells the story of a fading [character actor](/wiki/Rick_Dalton) (DiCaprio) and his [stunt double](/wiki/Stunt_double) (Pitt) as they navigate [New Hollywood](/wiki/New_Hollywood) in 1969 Los Angeles. Feeling "an enormous sense of responsibility", Robbie prepared for the role by meeting Tate's family members and friends, watching all of her films and reading the autobiography by Tate's then-husband, [Roman Polanski](/wiki/Roman_Polanski). *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* premiered at the [2019 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2019_Cannes_Film_Festival) to critical acclaim, and was a commercial success with a worldwide gross of $374.3 million. Despite many bemoaning Robbie's lack of lines in the film, Robbie Collin of *[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)* highlighted a scene with Robbie in the cinema, which he found to be the film's "most delightful" scene.

Also in 2019, she starred as Kayla Pospisil, a [composite character](/wiki/Composite_character) based on several [Fox News](/wiki/Fox_News) employees, in [Jay Roach](/wiki/Jay_Roach)'s drama *[Bombshell](/wiki/Bombshell_(2019_film))*. Co-starring [Charlize Theron](/wiki/Charlize_Theron) and [Nicole Kidman](/wiki/Nicole_Kidman), the film recounts stories of various female personnel at the news network and their sexual harassment by the network's chairman [Roger Ailes](/wiki/Roger_Ailes). Robbie based her character's accent on [Katherine Harris](/wiki/Katherine_Harris). The film received positive reviews; [Kenneth Turan](/wiki/Kenneth_Turan) of the *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* wrote, "Robbie [is] at her best, the arc of her story is so crushing that it stays with you the longest." For her performances in *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* and *Bombshell*, she received two nominations for the [BAFTA Award for Best Actress in a Supporting Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actress_in_a_Supporting_Role), and for the latter she received nominations for an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award), a [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe) and a Screen Actors Guild Award; all in the Best Supporting Actress category.

Robbie began the new decade by reprising the role of Harley Quinn in [Cathy Yan](/wiki/Cathy_Yan)'s *[Birds of Prey](/wiki/Birds_of_Prey_(2020_film))* (2020). Determined to make a female ensemble action film, she pitched the idea for the film to [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) in 2015. Robbie spent the subsequent three years developing the project under her production company, making a concerted effort to hire a female director and screenwriter. *Birds of Prey*, along with Robbie's performance, gained generally positive reviews; [Ian Freer](/wiki/Ian_Freer) of *[Empire](/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine))* wrote that "the [MVP](/wiki/Most_valuable_player) is Robbie, who lends Harley charming quirk and believable menace, hinting at Harley's inner life without reams of dialogue." She received two nominations at the [46th People's Choice Awards](/wiki/46th_People%27s_Choice_Awards).

Robbie served as a producer on *[Promising Young Woman](/wiki/Promising_Young_Woman)* (2020), a comedy thriller by writer-director [Emerald Fennell](/wiki/Emerald_Fennell), starring [Carey Mulligan](/wiki/Carey_Mulligan) as a woman who seeks to avenge the rape and death of her best friend. The film received acclaim, earning a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture). In 2021, Robbie reprised her voice role as Flopsy Rabbit in *[Peter Rabbit 2: The Runaway](/wiki/Peter_Rabbit_2:_The_Runaway)*, which received mixed reviews and grossed $153.8 million worldwide. She also made her third outing as Quinn in the [standalone sequel](/wiki/Standalone_sequel) *[The Suicide Squad](/wiki/The_Suicide_Squad_(film))*, written and directed by [James Gunn](/wiki/James_Gunn). Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), the film was simultaneously released theatrically and on the streaming service [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max). [Owen Gleiberman](/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman) praised Robbie's "delectable performance" in it. She also served as an executive producer for the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) miniseries *[Maid](/wiki/Maid_(miniseries))*.

Robbie at the Sydney premiere of *[Barbie](/wiki/Barbie_(film))* in 2023

In 2022, Robbie reprised her role as Donna Freedman for the [final episode](/wiki/Neighbours:_The_Finale) of *Neighbours*. She starred alongside an ensemble cast in [David O. Russell](/wiki/David_O._Russell)'s period comedy *[Amsterdam](/wiki/Amsterdam_(2022_film))*, based on the 1933 [Business Plot](/wiki/Business_Plot). The film emerged as a critical and commercial failure. In her second film release of the year, she played Nellie LaRoy, an actress inspired by silent movie star [Clara Bow](/wiki/Clara_Bow), in [Damien Chazelle](/wiki/Damien_Chazelle)'s comedy-drama *[Babylon](/wiki/Babylon_(2022_film))*. In preparation, she studied the works of Bow and researched her traumatic childhood. She described LaRoy as "the most physically and emotionally draining character I've ever played". The film polarised critics and had poor box office returns, though her performance received praise; [Caryn James](/wiki/Caryn_James) of [BBC Culture](/wiki/BBC_Culture) opined, "Robbie's bold, charismatic performance makes Nellie a daring, endlessly spiraling, sympathetic figure". She received another Golden Globe nomination for Best Actress, although the underperformance of her two major releases that year led some commentators to brand her as "[box office poison](/wiki/Box_Office_Poison_(magazine_article))" at the time.

In the following year, Robbie had a single scene in [Wes Anderson](/wiki/Wes_Anderson)'s ensemble comedy film *[Asteroid City](/wiki/Asteroid_City)*. Chris Hewitt of the *[Star Tribune](/wiki/Star_Tribune)* described her "impassioned acting in her lone scene" as "perfectly judged". The fantasy comedy *[Barbie](/wiki/Barbie_(film))*, co-starring [Ryan Gosling](/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) as [Ken](/wiki/Ken_(doll)), was her next film release. As producer, Robbie secured the rights from [Mattel](/wiki/Mattel) for a film about the [eponymous fashion doll](/wiki/Barbie) in 2018. She hired [Greta Gerwig](/wiki/Greta_Gerwig) to write and direct the film, and took on the title role herself after [Gal Gadot](/wiki/Gal_Gadot) declined her offer. In preparation, Gerwig and Robbie watched old [Technicolor](/wiki/Technicolor) musicals such as *[The Red Shoes](/wiki/The_Red_Shoes_(1948_film))* (1948) and *[The Umbrellas of Cherbourg](/wiki/The_Umbrellas_of_Cherbourg)* (1964). *Variety* reported that Robbie earned $12.5 million for the role, the highest for an actress in Hollywood that year. *[Vulture](/wiki/Vulture_(website))*'s Alison Willmore took note of how much Robbie fit the part, and commended her for combining both "heartbreaking earnestness" and "humor" in her performance. With a worldwide gross of over $1.4 billion, *Barbie* emerged as Robbie's highest-grossing release. She received further BAFTA and Golden Globe nominations for her performance, in addition to a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture).

Also in 2023, Robbie produced Fennell's second directorial, *[Saltburn](/wiki/Saltburn_(film))*. The [2024 Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/2024_Sundance_Film_Festival) marked the release of her next production, [Megan Park](/wiki/Megan_Park)'s comedy film *[My Old Ass](/wiki/My_Old_Ass)*. Robbie starred alongside [Colin Farrell](/wiki/Colin_Farrell) in [Kogonada](/wiki/Kogonada)'s 2025 film *[A Big Bold Beautiful Journey](/wiki/A_Big_Bold_Beautiful_Journey)* Robbie starred as [Catherine Earnshaw](/wiki/Catherine_Earnshaw) alongside [Jacob Elordi](/wiki/Jacob_Elordi) as [Heathcliff](/wiki/Heathcliff_(Wuthering_Heights)) in *[Wuthering Heights](/wiki/Wuthering_Heights_(2026_film))*, the romantic drama film written and directed by [Emerald Fennell](/wiki/Emerald_Fennell) and loosely based on the [Emily Brontë novel](/wiki/Wuthering_Heights). The film was released theatrically on February 13, 2026.

### Upcoming projects

Robbie is set to produce and star in [Tim Burton](/wiki/Tim_Burton)'s upcoming remake of *[Attack of the 50 Foot Woman](/wiki/Attack_of_the_50_Foot_Woman)* for [Warner Bros Pictures](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures). The film will be a contemporary reimagining of the original story about a wealthy heiress who grows to gigantic size after an alien encounter, and will be co-produced through Robbie's production company, [LuckyChap Entertainment](/wiki/LuckyChap_Entertainment).

## Personal life

Despite significant media attention, Robbie rarely discusses her personal life. Robbie moved from Melbourne to [Williamsburg, Brooklyn](/wiki/Williamsburg,_Brooklyn), in the early 2010s, having previously shared a flat with *Neighbours* co-star [Ashleigh Brewer](/wiki/Ashleigh_Brewer). During that period, she became an avid ice hockey fan; she supports the [New York Rangers](/wiki/New_York_Rangers) and previously played [right wing](/wiki/Winger_(ice_hockey)) in an amateur ice hockey league.

Robbie met British assistant director [Tom Ackerley](/wiki/Tom_Ackerley) on the set of *[Suite Française](/wiki/Suite_Fran%C3%A7aise_(film))* in 2013. As a child, Ackerley served as an extra in the first three films of the [*Harry Potter* film series](/wiki/Harry_Potter_(film_series)), which Robbie has been a fan of since childhood. In 2014, she moved to London with Ackerley and the other [LuckyChap Entertainment](/wiki/LuckyChap_Entertainment) co-founders Sophia Kerr and Josey McNamara. Later that year, Robbie and Ackerley began a romantic relationship. In December 2016, they married in a private wedding ceremony in [Byron Bay, Australia](/wiki/Byron_Bay,_Australia). The couple reside in [Venice Beach, California](/wiki/Venice_Beach,_California). Robbie gave birth to their son in 2024.

## Other work

Robbie has been a vocal supporter of [human rights](/wiki/Human_rights), [women's rights](/wiki/Women%27s_rights), [gender equality](/wiki/Gender_equality) and [LGBTQ](/wiki/LGBTQ) rights. Through LuckyChap Entertainment, she and her co-founders focus on promoting female stories from female storytellers, whether it would be writers, directors, producers or all the above. In 2014, she was part of a fundraising event in support of the [Motion Picture & Television Fund](/wiki/Motion_Picture_%26_Television_Fund), which helps people in the film and television industries with limited or no resources; she joined the same event on two other occasions, in 2018 and 2020. In 2015, she helped raise $12 million through the [BGC Global Charity Day](/wiki/BGC_Partners) fundraising event, which donates money to different charities around the world. In 2016, Robbie joined other celebrities and [UN Refugee Agency](/wiki/UN_Refugee_Agency) staff in a petition aiming to gather public support for the growing number of families forced to flee conflict and persecution worldwide. Later in the year, she joined [Oxfam](/wiki/Oxfam)'s "I Hear You" project, which was designed to amplify the personal stories of the world's most vulnerable refugees and donated more than $50.000 to [UNICEF](/wiki/UNICEF)'s "Children First" campaign, in support of refugee children.

In October 2016, while hosting *Saturday Night Live*, Robbie made a stand for [same-sex marriage](/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Australia) in her native Australia wearing a T-shirt that read "Say 'I Do' Down Under", with a map of the country in rainbow colours. A year later, she joined fellow actor [Chris Hemsworth](/wiki/Chris_Hemsworth) in advocating for the same purpose. In 2018, she pledged to support the [Time's Up](/wiki/Time%27s_Up_(organization)) initiative to protect women from harassment and discrimination. In April 2021, Robbie was announced as the recipient of the inaugural RAD Impact Award, for inspiring purpose with her philanthropy. She chose to share the prize with [Youngcare](/wiki/Youngcare), a charity she had previously worked with, and therefore an impact donation was made to fund a project benefiting young people with extensive care needs.

## Public image

Robbie in 2013

Robbie is known for starring in both high-profile, mainstream productions and low-budget independent films, in which she has been able to display both her dramatic and comedic range.

For her role in *The Wolf of Wall Street*, *[Vanity Fair](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine))* named her one of its breakthrough actors of 2013. In 2017, she appeared on the annual [*Forbes* 30 Under 30](/wiki/Forbes_30_Under_30) list, a compilation of the brightest young entrepreneurs, innovators and game changers in the world and was included on a similar list compiled by *The Hollywood Reporter*. That same year, *Time* named her one of the [100 most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100); her *The Wolf of Wall Street* director Martin Scorsese penned the article in the magazine, referring to Robbie as having "a unique audacity that surprises and challenges and just burns like a brand into every character she plays. [...] Margot is stunning in all she is and all she does, and she will astonish us forever." In 2019, *Forbes* ranked her among the world's highest-paid actresses, with annual earnings of $23.5 million, and *The Hollywood Reporter* listed her among the 100 most powerful people in entertainment. In 2021, she was named one of the 100 most influential women in entertainment by *The Hollywood Reporter*. In December 2023, Robbie has been listed in *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)'*s 2023 Women in Entertainment Power 100. In 2024, she was named by *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* as the [world's highest-paid actress](/wiki/List_of_highest-paid_film_actors) in 2023, with earnings of $78 million.

*Vogue* has named her "one of the most glamorous starlets", and she was ranked as one of the best-dressed women in 2018 and 2019 by luxury fashion retailer [Net-a-Porter](/wiki/Net-a-Porter). In 2016, Robbie was placed at number one on *[FHM](/wiki/FHM)*'s "[100 Sexiest Women in the World](/wiki/FHM%27s_100_Sexiest_Women_(UK))" list. Since 2016, she has been chosen as the ambassador for brands such as [Calvin Klein](/wiki/Calvin_Klein_(fashion_house)), [Nissan](/wiki/Nissan) and [Chanel](/wiki/Chanel). She was the last brand ambassador picked by [Karl Lagerfeld](/wiki/Karl_Lagerfeld) before his death in February 2019. She is the latest ambassador of [Chanel No. 5](/wiki/Chanel_No._5).

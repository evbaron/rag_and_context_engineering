# List of Nobel Memorial Prize laureates in Economic Sciences

The announcement of the 2008 [Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences](/wiki/Nobel_Memorial_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences) in Nobel Prize Press Conference. The laureate of the Prize was [Paul Krugman](/wiki/Paul_Krugman).

The **Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences**, officially the **Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of [Alfred Nobel](/wiki/Alfred_Nobel)** ([Swedish](/wiki/Swedish_language): *Sveriges riksbanks pris i ekonomisk vetenskap till Alfred Nobels minne*), commonly referred to as the **Nobel Prize in Economics**, is an award in the field of [Economic Sciences](/wiki/Economic_Sciences) administered by the [Nobel Foundation](/wiki/Nobel_Foundation).

The first Prize in Economic Sciences was awarded in 1969 to [Ragnar Frisch](/wiki/Ragnar_Frisch) and [Jan Tinbergen](/wiki/Jan_Tinbergen). Each recipient receives a [medal](/wiki/Medal), a [diploma](/wiki/Diploma) and a monetary award that has varied throughout the years. In 1969, Frisch and Tinbergen were given a combined 375,000 [SEK](/wiki/Swedish_krona), which is equivalent to 2,871,041 SEK in December 2007. The award is presented in [Stockholm](/wiki/Stockholm) at an annual ceremony on December 10, the anniversary of Nobel's death.

As of the awarding of the 2023 prize, 55 Prizes in Economic Sciences have been given to 93 individuals. As of October 2023, the department of economics with the most affiliated [laureates](/wiki/Laureate) in economic sciences is the [University of Chicago](/wiki/University_of_Chicago), with 16 affiliated laureates. As of 2023, the institutions with the most [PhD](/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (or equivalent) graduates who went on to receive the prize are Harvard University and MIT (13 each), followed by the University of Chicago (10).
As of 2024, the institutions with the most affiliated faculty at the time of the award are the University of Chicago (15), followed by MIT (10), then by Princeton University and Harvard University (8 each).

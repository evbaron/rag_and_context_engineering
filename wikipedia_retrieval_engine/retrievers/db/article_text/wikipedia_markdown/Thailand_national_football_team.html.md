The **Thailand national football team** ([Thai](/wiki/Thai_language): ฟุตบอลทีมชาติไทย, [RTGS](/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): **futbon thim chat thai**, pronounced [[fút.bɔ̄n tʰīːm t͡ɕʰâːt tʰāj]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Thai)) represents [Thailand](/wiki/Thailand) in senior international men's [football](/wiki/Association_football) and is controlled by the [Football Association of Thailand](/wiki/Football_Association_of_Thailand).

In the regional competition, Thailand is the most successful football team in Southeast Asia with seven [ASEAN Championship](/wiki/ASEAN_Championship#Results) trophies and nine senior-level gold medals from the [Southeast Asian Games](/wiki/Football_at_the_Southeast_Asian_Games#Men's_tournament), the most of any Southeast Asian country. In higher levels, Thailand achieved the third place in the [1972 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/1972_AFC_Asian_Cup) where it was the host, and has a total of seven appearances in the [AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) so far. Furthermore, the team reached the fourth-place in the [1990](/wiki/Football_at_the_1990_Asian_Games) and [1998](/wiki/Football_at_the_1998_Asian_Games) [Asian Games](/wiki/Football_at_the_Asian_Games) and participated in the [Summer Olympics](/wiki/Summer_Olympics) twice. However, Thailand has failed to obtain higher achievements in [the continental](/wiki/Football_in_Asia) and global records. The team obtained their first win in the AFC Asian Cup in [2007](/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup) and had to wait 47 years to finally sneak out of the group stage in [2019](/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup). Thailand also advanced to the final round of [World Cup qualification](/wiki/World_Cup_qualification) twice, in [2002](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(AFC)) and [2018](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(AFC)), but failed to qualify for the [FIFA World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup).

## History

### 1915–1995: dynastic establishment

King [Vajiravudh](/wiki/Vajiravudh), the founder of the Football Association of Thailand

Siam (Thailand) association football squad's pioneers, 1915

The team's predecessor, which operated under the name of *Siam*, was founded in 1915 and played its first unofficial match against a team of Europeans at the [Royal Bangkok Sports Club](/wiki/Royal_Bangkok_Sports_Club) Stadium on 20 December that year. The team played its first international match in 1930 against the [Indochina](/wiki/French_Indochina) national team, which included both South Vietnamese and [French](/wiki/French_people) players.

Thailand football members at the [1956 Melbourne Olympics](/wiki/Association_football_at_the_1956_Summer_Olympics) before their biggest defeat by the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom_national_football_team)

Thailand appeared in the [1956 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1956_Summer_Olympics) in [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne), where their loss to [Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain_national_football_team) 0–9, was the largest to that point, thus failing to advance to the quarter-finals. In 1959, Thailand as the host won silver medals in the [Southeast Asian Peninsular Games](/wiki/Football_at_the_1959_Southeast_Asian_Peninsular_Games) after losing 1–3 to [South Vietnam](/wiki/South_Vietnam_national_football_team) in the final. In 1965, Thailand harbored its first distinct title: the very first place in the [Southeast Asian Games](/wiki/Southeast_Asian_Games). They made their second and latest appearance at the [Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) in [1968](/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics), losing all three matches by at least 3 goals margin to [Bulgaria](/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team), [Guatemala](/wiki/Guatemala_national_football_team), and [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) hence en route to a first-round exit.

During the [1992 AFC Asian Cup qualification](/wiki/1992_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification), Thailand gained a significant success defeating [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea_national_football_team) 2–1 and [Bangladesh](/wiki/Bangladesh_national_football_team) 1–0 to top the group and qualify to the [1992 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/1992_AFC_Asian_Cup). The team's performance at the final tournament was drawing first two matches with [Qatar](/wiki/Qatar_national_football_team) and eventual 3rd place [China](/wiki/China_national_football_team) then losing 0–4 to [Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team). In 1994, manager [Thawatchai Sartjakul](/wiki/Thawatchai_Sartjakul) assembled a team that has been denounced as the "dream team" with players like [Kiatisuk Senamuang](/wiki/Kiatisuk_Senamuang), [Tawan Sripan](/wiki/Tawan_Sripan) and [Dusit Chalermsan](/wiki/Dusit_Chalermsan).

### 1996–2016: flag bearer of Southeast Asia

In 1996, Thailand defeated Malaysia 1–0 and win the [1996 AFF Championship](/wiki/1996_AFF_Championship) for the first time. Thailand were favorites to regain the crown in 2007, 2008 and 2012 only to lose tight finals to [Singapore](/wiki/Singapore_national_football_team) and [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) respectively.

The regional [1998 AFF Championship](/wiki/1998_AFF_Championship) saw Thailand met Indonesia in a match that ill-hearted players from both team deliberately making actions aimed to avoid facing hosts [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) in the semi-finals and undergoing technical burden of moving training bases from [Ho Chi Minh City](/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh_City) to [Hanoi](/wiki/Hanoi). FIFA fined both teams $40,000 for "violating the spirit of the game". Thailand eventually lost the match, inevitably encountered and failed to Vietnam in the semi-finals.

Thailand consecutively qualified to and participated in two AFC Asian Cup final tournaments both held within [Western Asia](/wiki/Western_Asia) in [1996](/wiki/1996_AFC_Asian_Cup) and [2000](/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup) when their "dream team" was beginning its golden period. Coincidentally in both editions, the team's opponents all came from Western Asia and they are [Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), [Lebanon](/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team), [Iran](/wiki/Iran_national_football_team) and [Iraq](/wiki/Iraq_national_football_team), with the latter two share the same group with Thailand twice. In both editions, Thailand made two draws and lost the rest, bottomed the group stage of the first and is the worst third-placed team of the second edition.

The final [2000 AFF Championship](/wiki/2000_AFF_Championship) match between Thailand and Indonesia, at a sold-out Rajamangala, was almost a carbon copy of their group stage encounter. The War Elephants triumphed 4–1 again with Worrawoot setting up camp at the opponents' goal. The 28-year-old scored twice in their first match and in the final struck a hat-trick in the first 32 minutes. In the final [2002 AFF Cup](/wiki/2002_AFF_Championship) final, Thailand again met [Indonesia](/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team) (who was now the host) and won the game in penalty shootout despite taking a 2–0 lead.

Thailand again qualified to the [2004 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup) and was put into a group with [Japan](/wiki/Japan_national_football_team), Iran and debutant [Oman](/wiki/Oman_national_football_team). Despite vast experiences in the Asian Cup, the team has yet to show a sign of improvement as they lost all matches and became the worst-performed team in the whole tournament.

Thailand team against [Oman](/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) in [2007 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup) Group A match at [Rajamangala Stadium](/wiki/Rajamangala_Stadium)

The sign of improvement only came in the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup) when Thailand participated as a well-prepared co-host and was placed with the debutant [Australia](/wiki/Australia_men%27s_national_soccer_team), Oman, and Iraq. The team managed a draw to Iraq and a historic win over Oman. With 4 points ahead, Thailand's chance to qualify for the next round for the first time since 1972 was all but shattered by the likes of Australia in a 0–4 demolition. The tournament witnessed the end of Thailand's recognizable generation with later retirements of Kiatisuk, Tawan, and [Pipat](/wiki/Pipat_Thonkanya).

In September 2008, Thailand signed a four-year contract with the [English](/wiki/English_people) coach [Peter Reid](/wiki/Peter_Reid) but Reid left his position by mutual consent after only a year in charge as his team fail to clinch the championship of [2008 AFF Championship](/wiki/2008_AFF_Championship) after 2–3 on aggregate lost to [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) in the finals.

In September 2009, [Bryan Robson](/wiki/Bryan_Robson) agreed to coach Thailand in his first foray into international football management and was contracted to manage the team through to the [2014 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup). In November, Robson celebrated his first competitive match in charge of the team with an away victory against [Singapore](/wiki/Singapore_national_football_team) in a [2011 AFC Asian Cup qualifying](/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification) group match but then lose to the same opponent back home. Then, two goalless draws with [Jordan](/wiki/Jordan_national_football_team) and [Iran](/wiki/Iran_national_football_team) in January 2010 and an 0–1 away lost to Iran in March all effectively ended the chance of qualifying for the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup). In preparations for the [2010 AFF Championship](/wiki/2010_AFF_Championship), Robson led Thailand to victorious run against Singapore and [Bob Houghton](/wiki/Bob_Houghton)'s [India](/wiki/India_national_football_team) in a series of friendlies. However, when entering the tournament in December, he failed to bring Thailand past group A after managing only draws against [Laos](/wiki/Laos_national_football_team) and [Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team) and losing to [Indonesia](/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team). Robson resigned as Thailand's manager on 8 June 2011, citing health problems as the reason and was replaced by [Winfried Schäfer](/wiki/Winfried_Sch%C3%A4fer), who would be the ninth [German](/wiki/Germans) person to coach the Thailand team.

Thailand's head coach [Winfried Schäfer](/wiki/Winfried_Sch%C3%A4fer) talking to players during the [World Cup 2014 third round qualifying match](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) against [Oman](/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) at [Rajamangala Stadium](/wiki/Rajamangala_Stadium) in 2011

The new coach called up starlets for the [2014 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_(AFC)) and have the starting set of matches losing minimal to Australia, defeating Oman 3–0 and drawing [Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) but did not make it after losing to these teams altogether in the second set. In the [2012 AFF Championship](/wiki/2012_AFF_Championship), Thailand topped their group and surpassed Malaysia in the semi-finals but handed the crown to Singapore in the finals. In the [2015 Asian Cup qualification](/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification), Thailand showed a setback with its defensive frailties exposed by Middle Eastern rivals (Iran, [Kuwait](/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team), [Lebanon](/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team)) when losing all 6 games in the qualifiers, conceding 21 goals in the process.

In June 2013, Schäfer canceled his contract. The FA of Thailand appointed the former player [Kiatisuk Senamuang](/wiki/Kiatisuk_Senamuang) as the new caretaker coach for the national team. His first ride was a [friendly](/wiki/Exhibition_game) against [China PR](/wiki/China_PR_national_football_team) on 15 June, which Thailand surprisingly won 5–1.

Thailand team celebrated after winning the [2014 AFF Championship](/wiki/2014_AFF_Championship) at the [Bukit Jalil National Stadium](/wiki/Bukit_Jalil_National_Stadium) in [Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia)

In 2014, Thailand ended a 12-year drought of the [AFF Championship](/wiki/AFF_Championship) title from the late goals by [Charyl Chappuis](/wiki/Charyl_Chappuis) and [Chanathip Songkrasin](/wiki/Chanathip_Songkrasin) which gave them a dramatic 4–3 aggregate victory over Malaysia in the second leg of the finals at Bukit Jalil. The team did not lose any match up until the second leg of the finals and often featured a [tiki-taka](/wiki/Tiki-taka) playing style, for instance including 27 consecutive passes during the first leg of the finals against Malaysia. Kiatisuk consequently became the first person to win the ASEAN Football Championship as both a player and a coach. Thailand succeeded in protecting AFF Championship reign two years later in [2016](/wiki/2016_AFF_Championship), defeating Indonesia 3–2 aggregately despite losing the first leg.

In 2015, evasion fuelled hope for both the players and Thailand fans of finally reaching the World Cup tournament and tension is mounting as the national team commenced AFC's [second round for 2018 FIFA World Cup qualification](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round). [Teerasil Dangda](/wiki/Teerasil_Dangda), Thailand's renowned striker, rejoined the rank of the national team after his loan with [UD Almería](/wiki/UD_Almer%C3%ADa) ended earlier. Drawn in Group F along with [Chinese Taipei](/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_football_team), [Iraq](/wiki/Iraq_national_football_team) and [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team), who Thailand played its first match home against on 24 May and can only be won by a victory goal from a shot 20 yards away. They played a much easier match at the same opponent's home soil, winning 3–0. Thailand won both matches against Chinese Taipei and drew 2–2 both matches against Iraq, allowing them to qualify for the next round as group F winners.
In the last round, Kiatisuk's men shared the same group with [Australia](/wiki/Australia_men%27s_national_soccer_team), [Japan](/wiki/Japan_national_football_team), [Saudi Arabia](/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), [UAE](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) along with previous opponent, Iraq. Again, Thailand was eliminated without winning a match and recorded only two points out of ten matches.

### 2017–present: aiming for continental success

#### Rajevac, Sirisak and Nishino

The Thai team at the [2017 King's Cup](/wiki/2017_King%27s_Cup), wearing black to mourn the death of King [Bhumibol Adulyadej](/wiki/Bhumibol_Adulyadej)

Thai players lining up at the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup)

Since taking over the administration by [Somyot Poompanmoung](/wiki/Somyot_Poompanmoung), FA Thailand aims to drive men's national football team to be one of the leading teams in Asia by which there are concrete 20 years development plans and preparations. After the elimination from World Cup qualifiers, Kiatisuk resigned and Thailand appointed [Milovan Rajevac](/wiki/Milovan_Rajevac) as a coach, thus marked the first non-[Brazilian](/wiki/Brazilians)/German/English team's chief. With the new coach, however, Thailand failed to defend its AFF Championship title in [2018](/wiki/2018_AFF_Championship) when losing Malaysia in the semi-finals by the [away goals rule](/wiki/Away_goals_rule).

Ahead of [2019 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup), Thailand was drawn into group A together with the host [UAE](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), [Bahrain](/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team) and [India](/wiki/India_national_football_team). Rajevac oversaw Thailand in the commencing 1–4 loss to India. The Serbian coach was sacked and his assistant, [Sirisak Yodyardthai](/wiki/Sirisak_Yodyardthai) became the interim coach on 7 January. Sirisak guided Thailand to a 1–0 win over Bahrain and a 1–1 draw with the host UAE, enough to move on to the knockout stage of the AFC Asian Cup for the first time in 47 years. Their success was greeted with congratulation from the FA. Thailand encountered [China](/wiki/China_national_football_team) in the round of sixteen, taking an early lead but eventually lost 2–1 as China make their decisive respond.

After finishing in the fourth place of [2019 King's Cup](/wiki/2019_King%27s_Cup) and losing the rival Vietnam in that tournament, Sirisak had resigned and FA Thailand appointed the Japanese coach [Akira Nishino](/wiki/Akira_Nishino_(footballer)), who had brought Japan to the round of 16 of [2018 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup), for replacement. This was the first-ever Asian coach becoming Thailand's head coach. The team was drawn into group G of [the second round of 2022 FIFA World Cup qualification](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_AFC_second_round) with other three Southeast Asian rivals: Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia; along with [United Arab Emirates](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team). Despite defeating Indonesia 3–0 and UAE 2–1, Thailand failed to revenge Vietnam when getting goalless draws in both legs, while losing Malaysia 1–2 in Bukit Jalil. With these results, Thailand could only get the third place in group G after five qualifying matches. After a one-year disruption due to [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), Thailand and other teams in group G had to play their remaining matches in Dubai, UAE. However, the team suffered a huge loss of key players when [Chanathip Songkrasin](/wiki/Chanathip_Songkrasin) was injured, while [Teerasil Dangda](/wiki/Teerasil_Dangda) and [Theerathon Bunmathan](/wiki/Theerathon_Bunmathan) refused to participate the qualification due to various reasons. Without these three players, Thailand showed a poor performance in [Dubai](/wiki/Dubai) - drawn the bottom place team Indonesia 2–2, then lost the UAE 1–3 and Malaysia 0–1, respectively; which eventually pushed the team down to the fourth place of the group G. Nishino did not come back to Thailand to explain the team's failure, but unilaterally returning to Japan, which made FA Thailand appoint [Anurak Srikerd](/wiki/Anurak_Srikerd) as the caretaker and consider sacking Nishino in upcoming days. On 29 July 2021, shortly after Nishino came back to Thailand, FA Thailand decided to terminate the contract with Nishino.

#### Fruitful victories under Alexandré Pölking

On 28 September 2021, Brazilian [Alexandré Pölking](/wiki/Alexandr%C3%A9_P%C3%B6lking) was appointed as the head coach of the Thailand national team, replacing [Akira Nishino](/wiki/Akira_Nishino_(footballer)). Pölking's first task was the [2020 AFF Championship](/wiki/2020_AFF_Championship) in December 2021 held in Singapore. Between 5 December 2021 and 1 January 2022, Polking accomplished the very task as he managed the War Elephants to win 6–2 on aggregate after being held to a 2–2 second-leg draw by Indonesia, guiding Thailand to win the AFF Championship for the sixth time. In the [2022 AFF Championship](/wiki/2022_AFF_Championship), Thailand defended their title on 16 January 2023, winning the tournament by a 3–2 aggregate score in the [two-legged final](/wiki/2022_AFF_Championship_Final) against [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) to secure their seventh title.

In October 2023, Thailand embarked on a European tour playing two friendlies against [Georgia](/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team) and [Estonia](/wiki/Estonia_national_football_team) and with star players like [Chanathip Songkrasin](/wiki/Chanathip_Songkrasin), [Nicholas Mickelson](/wiki/Nicholas_Mickelson) and [Ekanit Panya](/wiki/Ekanit_Panya) injured and club team refused to release [Teerasil Dangda](/wiki/Teerasil_Dangda) and [Theerathon Bunmathan](/wiki/Theerathon_Bunmathan), [Supachok Sarachat](/wiki/Supachok_Sarachat), [Sarach Yooyen](/wiki/Sarach_Yooyen), [Pansa Hemviboon](/wiki/Pansa_Hemviboon) for international duties, Thailand have to relies on inexperienced international players and also giving the opportunity for new young players a chance to touched up for the future international matches. On 12 October 2023, Thailand suffered their worst defeat in the 21st century losing against Georgia 8–0 at the [Mikheil Meskhi Stadium](/wiki/Mikheil_Meskhi_Stadium). In the next match against Estonia on 17 October, [Jakkapan Praisuwan](/wiki/Jakkapan_Praisuwan) equalised it for Thailand for a hard fought 1–1 draw at the [Lilleküla Stadium](/wiki/Lillek%C3%BCla_Stadium).

#### 2023 AFC Asian Cup

Thailand vs. [Japan](/wiki/Japan_national_football_team) on New Year’s Day Match at Toyo Tires Cup in 2024

After a hurried attempt, Japanese manager [Masatada Ishii](/wiki/Masatada_Ishii) was announced as Thailand's temporarily new head coach, with a contract lasted until the end of the [2023 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup) as he faced a daunting prospect of reviving the lacklustre Thai side to reach continental level. His first match, a friendly against his homeland Japan, saw Thailand thrashed 5–0 in the second half after an impressive first half display, increased negative feelings among Thai fans as the War Elephants faced a huge challenge in [group F](/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_F) at the Asian Cup, with Saudi Arabia, [Kyrgyzstan](/wiki/Kyrgyzstan_national_football_team) and Oman on the same boat.

However, Thailand started their Asian Cup campaign with a 2-0 win against Kyrgyzstan, marking Thailand's first win in an Asian Cup opening match. Thailand's next match was against Oman, who performed well over the past three years and whose manager, [Branko Ivanković](/wiki/Branko_Ivankovi%C4%87), who had never lost to Thailand in his coaching career. However, Thailand resolutely defended throughout the match, resulting in a scoreless tie, giving Thailand four points and an early progression to the knockout stage. Thailand would then go on to produce its most famous game in their modern Asian Cup history against Saudi Arabia in the final group stage match, including a famous penalty save by [Saranon Anuin](/wiki/Saranon_Anuin) resulting in another scoreless draw, ending their losing streak that started in 2012 to the same opponent and, for the first time ever, Thailand gained a shutout record in the Asian Cup group stage. However, Thailand failed to end their knockout stage losing streak record in the Asian Cup, losing 2–1 to [Uzbekistan](/wiki/Uzbekistan_national_football_team) in the round of 16 to equal their 2019 run. Despite this exit, Thailand's performance in the tournament earned the team significant praise, and Masatada Ishii was appointed on a permanent basis, with the aim to help Thailand to qualify for the [third round of the 2026 World Cup qualification](/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) and the [2027 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2027_AFC_Asian_Cup).

#### 2026 FIFA World Cup qualifying

The [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification](/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round) saw Thailand drawn in group C against [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea_national_football_team), [China](/wiki/China_national_football_team) and [Singapore](/wiki/Singapore_national_football_team), with the target to be winning the second place to advance to the final round. However, Thailand would get off to a disastrous start, flopping at home to China 2–1 despite opened the scoring by [Sarach Yooyen](/wiki/Sarach_Yooyen). This home loss ultimately sealed Pölking's fate, despite a 3–1 away victory over Singapore which moved Thailand up to second place; with the Brazilian sacked for failing to meet expectations.

After the 2023 AFC Asian Cup, Thailand played two more qualifiers, both against South Korea, in March 2024. On 21 March 2024, Thailand managed to earn a surprise 1–1 draw in front of 64,912 fans in [Seoul](/wiki/Seoul), but would later lose 3–0 at home to the same opponents. Thailand would end their qualification campaign in June undefeated, notably with a 1–1 draw away to China, before managing to overcome Singapore 3–1; yet their inability to convert scoring opportunities, as well as giving up a late goal in their draw with China, sealed Thailand's fate. The team did not advance to the next phase and finished behind China based on head-to-head results, as both teams ended with the same goal difference and points total.

## Team image

### Colours

Thailand national team vintage [1968 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Football_at_the_1968_Summer_Olympics) shirt

In older days, the primary kits worn are all red.

Thailand national team used to play with a kit made by local provider [FBT](/wiki/FBT_(company)). This contract lasted until June 2007.

In July 2007, [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) became kit providers, and from October that year, the team played in an all-yellow home kit in honour of King [Bhumibol Adulyadej](/wiki/Bhumibol_Adulyadej)'s 80th birthday (yellow being the royal color), having used two other yellow kits in friendlies against [China](/wiki/China_national_football_team) on 16 May 2007 and [Qatar](/wiki/Qatar_national_football_team) on 2 July 2007.

From October 2012 through 2016, Nike was replaced by [Grand Sport](/wiki/Grand_Sport_Group) in a deal worth 96M baht (3.1M USD). The new home kit of Thailand reverted to all-red and the away kit to all-blue. However, the order was reversed from the [2014 AFF Championship](/wiki/2014_AFF_Championship) onward.

In September 2016, the national team signed a four-year contract with [Warrix Sports](/wiki/Warrix_Sports) to be their kit provider from 2017. On 4 January 2017, the new provider introduced a new pair of Thailand kits that was all black home and all white away, honouring their late [King Bhumibol](/wiki/Bhumibol_Adulyadej) for a year after his passing, with black and white being the traditional Thai colors of mourning.

In March 2018, Warrix returned Thailand to the all-blue first, all-red second kits with an addition of a white-black third kit.

In December 2018, a new, darker version of blue, red kits and an all white third kit were presented for the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) campaign and the rest of 2019. For the [2019 King's Cup](/wiki/2019_King%27s_Cup) in May, Warrix released the kit consisting of a yellow shirt with white shorts and socks – yellow reportedly being the favorite color of the newly crowned [King Maha Vajiralongkorn](/wiki/King_Maha_Vajiralongkorn).

Thailand wore black again in November 2025 after the passing of [Queen Sirikit](/wiki/Sirikit).

### Rivalries

#### Notable rivalries

Thailand has rivalries with [Myanmar](/wiki/Myanmar_national_football_team), [Singapore](/wiki/Singapore_national_football_team), [Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team), [Indonesia](/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team), and [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team). These rivalries are rooted in geographical proximity.

[Malaysia](/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team) is Thailand's most frequently faced opponent, the two teams having played each other 107 times. Before Malaysia fell into [football scandal](/wiki/1994_Malaysian_football_scandal) that weakened the country's football development from the 1990s to 2018, Malaysia was Thailand's most annoying and difficult opponent in the region. Despite the football scandal, Thailand have not defeated the Malaysians on their home turf since 1971. Thailand have better records in international football competitions than the Malaysians.

[Indonesia](/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team) has met Thailand in four finals of the [AFF Championship](/wiki/AFF_Championship) at [2000](/wiki/2000_AFF_Championship), [2002](/wiki/2002_AFF_Championship), [2016](/wiki/2016_AFF_Championship), [2020](/wiki/2020_AFF_Championship) and Thailand all triumphed at the expense of Indonesia. It's been said that while Thailand was able to elevate its position to become a more serious Asian competitor, Indonesia fell into mismanagement and matches between two teams also began to lose its importance.

The rivalry between Thailand and [Singapore](/wiki/Singapore) is a newer one and its importance can be emphasized by the domination of both countries in the AFF Championship with Thailand winning five times and Singapore winning four. Up until 2012, Singapore and Thailand have been the more dominant forces in Southeast Asian football. Football development in both countries have been different with Thailand relies mostly on its own domestically developed players while Singapore has been reliant on naturalized players.

[Thailand's rivalry with Vietnam](/wiki/Thailand%E2%80%93Vietnam_football_rivalry) has developed differently over time. During the time of [South Vietnam](/wiki/South_Vietnam_national_football_team) and [North Vietnam](/wiki/North_Vietnam_national_football_team), Thailand had a poorer performance, winning only 5 of 28 matches against the South Vietnamese. However, when [Vietnam](/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) rejoined international football in 1991, Thailand established a period of dominance until 2016, winning 14 out of 20 matches. Since late 2017, the rivalry has become more closely contested, with Thailand recording 3 wins, 4 draws, and 3 losses in their last 10 meetings (as of 2025). Overall, the record since 1956 remains relatively balanced for both sides.

When [Myanmar](/wiki/Myanmar_national_football_team) was still a football power, it was Thailand's first-ever rival, owned by the history of the [Burmese–Siamese wars](/wiki/Burmese%E2%80%93Siamese_wars) which led to a nationalist fervor among Thai fans with its desire to beat the Burmese. But with Myanmar weakened following the reign of [Ne Win](/wiki/Ne_Win) and junta, Thailand improved and since 1983, holds an undefeated streak over its western rival. The rivalry today only serves mostly in the memoir of Burmese fans who are nostalgic to an era when Myanmar was still a leading football power, while for some Thai fans, they have more important opponents to concentrate on.

### Home ground

Most of Thailand home matches took place in [Rajamangala National Stadium](/wiki/Rajamangala_Stadium) in [Bang Kapi District](/wiki/Bang_Kapi_District) of [Bangkok](/wiki/Bangkok). Built for the [1998 Asian Games](/wiki/1998_Asian_Games), the stadium is the largest sporting facility in Thailand with a capacity of 49,749, all seated. International matches are also occasionally played at [Supachalasai Stadium](/wiki/Supachalasai_Stadium), [700th Anniversary Stadium](/wiki/700th_Anniversary_Stadium), [80th Birthday Stadium](/wiki/80th_Birthday_Stadium), [Thammasat Stadium](/wiki/Thammasat_Stadium), [Chang Arena](/wiki/Chang_Arena), [BG Stadium](/wiki/BG_Stadium) and [Thunderdome Stadium](/wiki/Thunderdome_Stadium).

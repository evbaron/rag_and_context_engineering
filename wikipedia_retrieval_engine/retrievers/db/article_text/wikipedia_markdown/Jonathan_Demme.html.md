**Robert Jonathan Demme** ([/ˈdɛmi/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*DEM-ee*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); February 22, 1944 – April 26, 2017) was an American filmmaker. His career of directing, producing, and screenwriting spanned more than 30 years and 70 feature films, documentaries, and television productions. In addition to being an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) and a [Directors Guild of America Award](/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_America_Award) winner, he received nominations for a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award), and three [Independent Spirit Awards](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award).

Beginning his career under [B-movie](/wiki/B-movie) producer [Roger Corman](/wiki/Roger_Corman), Demme made his directorial debut with the 1974 [women-in-prison](/wiki/Women_in_prison_film) film *[Caged Heat](/wiki/Caged_Heat)*, before becoming known for his casually humanist films such as *[Melvin and Howard](/wiki/Melvin_and_Howard)* (1980), *[Swing Shift](/wiki/Swing_Shift_(film))* (1984), *[Something Wild](/wiki/Something_Wild_(1986_film))* (1986), and *[Married to the Mob](/wiki/Married_to_the_Mob)* (1988). His 1991 psychological horror film *[The Silence of the Lambs](/wiki/The_Silence_of_the_Lambs_(film))*, based on the [novel of the same title](/wiki/The_Silence_of_the_Lambs_(novel)), won five [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards), including [Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) and [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture).

His subsequent films earned similar acclaim, notably the [HIV/AIDS](/wiki/HIV/AIDS)-themed drama *[Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia_(film))* (1993), the supernatural [Gothic horror](/wiki/Gothic_film) *[Beloved](/wiki/Beloved_(1998_film))* (1998), the conspiracy thriller *[The Manchurian Candidate](/wiki/The_Manchurian_Candidate_(2004_film))* (2004), and the independent drama *[Rachel Getting Married](/wiki/Rachel_Getting_Married)* (2008). Demme also directed numerous concert films such as *[Stop Making Sense](/wiki/Stop_Making_Sense)* (1984), *[Neil Young: Heart of Gold](/wiki/Neil_Young:_Heart_of_Gold)* (2006), and *[Justin Timberlake + The Tennessee Kids](/wiki/Justin_Timberlake_%2B_The_Tennessee_Kids)* (2016), and worked on several television series as both a producer and director. *Stop Making Sense*, *The Silence of the Lambs*, and *Philadelphia* have been inducted into the [National Film Registry](/wiki/National_Film_Registry).

## Early life

Demme was born on February 22, 1944, in [Baldwin](/wiki/Baldwin_(hamlet),_New_York), New York, the son of Dorothy Louise ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Rogers) and Robert Eugene Demme, a public relations executive. He was raised in [Rockville Centre, New York](/wiki/Rockville_Centre,_New_York) and [Miami](/wiki/Miami), where he graduated from [Southwest Miami High School](/wiki/Southwest_Miami_High_School) before attending the [University of Florida](/wiki/University_of_Florida).

## Career

### Early films

Demme broke into feature film working for [exploitation film](/wiki/Exploitation_film) producer [Roger Corman](/wiki/Roger_Corman) early in his career, co-writing and producing *[Angels Hard as They Come](/wiki/Angels_Hard_as_They_Come)* (1971), a motorcycle movie very loosely based on *[Rashomon](/wiki/Rashomon)*, and *[The Hot Box](/wiki/The_Hot_Box)* (1972). He then moved on to directing three films for Corman's studio [New World Pictures](/wiki/New_World_Pictures): *[Caged Heat](/wiki/Caged_Heat)* (1974), *[Crazy Mama](/wiki/Crazy_Mama)* (1975), and *[Fighting Mad](/wiki/Fighting_Mad_(1976_film))* (1976). After *Fighting Mad*, Demme directed the comedy film *[Handle with Care](/wiki/Handle_with_Care_(1977_film))* (originally titled *Citizens Band*, 1977) for [Paramount Pictures](/wiki/Paramount_Pictures). The film was well received by critics, but received little promotion, and performed poorly at the box office. He also directed a 1978 episode of *Columbo*.

Demme's next film, *[Melvin and Howard](/wiki/Melvin_and_Howard)* (1980), did not get a [wide release](/wiki/Wide_release), but received a groundswell of critical acclaim and film award recognition, including Academy Award nominations, winning two of its three nominations ([Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress) – [Mary Steenburgen](/wiki/Mary_Steenburgen), and [Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay) – [Bo Goldman](/wiki/Bo_Goldman)). This acclaim led to the signing of Demme to direct the [Goldie Hawn](/wiki/Goldie_Hawn) and [Kurt Russell](/wiki/Kurt_Russell) [star vehicle](/wiki/Star_vehicle) *[Swing Shift](/wiki/Swing_Shift_(film))* (1984). Intended as a prestige picture for [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) as well as a major commercial vehicle for Demme, it instead became a troubled production due to the conflicting visions of Demme and star Hawn. Demme ended up renouncing the finished product, and when the film was released in May 1984, it was generally panned by critics and neglected by moviegoers. After *Swing Shift*, Demme stepped back from Hollywood to make the [Talking Heads](/wiki/Talking_Heads) [concert film](/wiki/Concert_film) *[Stop Making Sense](/wiki/Stop_Making_Sense)* (also 1984) which won the [National Society of Film Critics](/wiki/National_Society_of_Film_Critics) Award for best documentary; the action-romantic comedy *[Something Wild](/wiki/Something_Wild_(1986_film))* (1986); a film-version of the stage production *[Swimming to Cambodia](/wiki/Swimming_to_Cambodia)* (1987), by monologist [Spalding Gray](/wiki/Spalding_Gray); and the comedy *[Married to the Mob](/wiki/Married_to_the_Mob)* (1988).

Demme formed his production company, Clinica Estetico, with producers [Edward Saxon](/wiki/Edward_Saxon) and [Peter Saraf](/wiki/Peter_Saraf) in 1987. They were based out of New York City for fifteen years.

Further information on the 1981 film presentation: [Made in Texas](/wiki/Made_in_Texas)

### Later films

Demme won the Academy Award for *[The Silence of the Lambs](/wiki/The_Silence_of_the_Lambs_(film))* (1991)—one of only three films to win all the major categories (Best Picture, Best Director, Best Screenplay, Best Actor, and Best Actress). Inspired by his friend [Juan Suárez Botas](/wiki/Juan_Su%C3%A1rez_Botas)'s illness with [AIDS](/wiki/HIV/AIDS) and fueled by his own moral convictions, Demme then used his influence to make *[Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia_(film))* (1993), one of the first major films to address the [AIDS crisis](/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_the_United_States) and which garnered star [Tom Hanks](/wiki/Tom_Hanks) his first [Best Actor Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor). He also co-directed (with his nephew [Ted](/wiki/Ted_Demme)) the music video for [Bruce Springsteen](/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen)'s [Best Song Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song)-winning "[Streets of Philadelphia](/wiki/Streets_of_Philadelphia)" from the film's soundtrack. Jonathan used several of the same actors for both movies.

Subsequently, his films included an adaptation of [Toni Morrison](/wiki/Toni_Morrison)'s *[Beloved](/wiki/Beloved_(1998_film))* (1998), and remakes of two films from the 1960s: *[The Truth About Charlie](/wiki/The_Truth_About_Charlie)* (2002), based on *[Charade](/wiki/Charade_(1963_film))*, that starred [Mark Wahlberg](/wiki/Mark_Wahlberg) in the [Cary Grant](/wiki/Cary_Grant) role; and *[The Manchurian Candidate](/wiki/The_Manchurian_Candidate_(2004_film))* (2004), with [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington) and [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep). Demme's documentary film *[Man from Plains](/wiki/Man_from_Plains)* (2007), a documentary about former U.S. President [Jimmy Carter](/wiki/Jimmy_Carter)'s promotional tour publicizing his book *[Palestine: Peace Not Apartheid](/wiki/Palestine:_Peace_Not_Apartheid)*, had its premiere at the [Venice Film Festival](/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival) and [Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival).

His art-house hit *[Rachel Getting Married](/wiki/Rachel_Getting_Married)* (2008) was compared by many critics to Demme's films of the late 1970s and 1980s. It was included in many 2008 "best of" lists, and received numerous awards and nominations, including an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nomination for [Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress) by lead [Anne Hathaway](/wiki/Anne_Hathaway). In 2010, Demme made his first foray into theater, directing *Family Week*, a play by [Beth Henley](/wiki/Beth_Henley). The play was produced by [MCC Theater](/wiki/MCC_Theater) and co-starred [Rosemarie DeWitt](/wiki/Rosemarie_DeWitt) and [Kathleen Chalfant](/wiki/Kathleen_Chalfant).

At one time, Demme was signed on to direct, produce, and write an adaptation of [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King)'s sci-fi novel *[11/22/63](/wiki/11/22/63)*, but later left the project due to disagreements with King on what should be included in the script.

He returned to the concert documentary format with *[Justin Timberlake + the Tennessee Kids](/wiki/Justin_Timberlake_%2B_the_Tennessee_Kids)* (2016), which he described as a "performance film, but also a portrait of an artist at a certain moment in the arc of his career", and his last project was a history of rock & roll for the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame) compiled from footage from Hall of Fame induction ceremonies set to debut in summer 2017.

Demme directed music videos for artists such as [Suburban Lawns](/wiki/Suburban_Lawns), [New Order](/wiki/New_Order_(band)), [KRS-One](/wiki/KRS-One)'s H.E.A.L. project and [Bruce Springsteen](/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen). He also produced a compilation of [Haitian music](/wiki/Music_of_Haiti) called *Konbit: Burning Rhythms of Haiti* that was released in 1989. ([Lou Reed](/wiki/Lou_Reed) selected *Konbit...* as one of his 'picks of 1989').

Demme was on the board of directors at [Jacob Burns Film Center](/wiki/Jacob_Burns_Film_Center) in [Pleasantville, New York](/wiki/Pleasantville,_New_York). In addition to his role on the board, he curated and hosted a monthly series called *Rarely Seen Cinema*.

## Style

Throughout 1986–2004, Demme was known for his dramatic close-ups in films. This style of close-ups involves the character looking directly into the camera during crucial moments. According to Demme, this was done to put the viewer into the character's shoes. Beginning with *[Rachel Getting Married](/wiki/Rachel_Getting_Married)* (2008), Demme adopted a documentary style of filmmaking.

He was known for his use of recurring supporting players, including [Charles Napier](/wiki/Charles_Napier_(actor)), [Harry Northup](/wiki/Harry_Northup), [Tracey Walter](/wiki/Tracey_Walter), [Ann Dowd](/wiki/Ann_Dowd), [LisaGay Hamilton](/wiki/LisaGay_Hamilton), [Kimberly Elise](/wiki/Kimberly_Elise), Paul Lazar, [Ron Vawter](/wiki/Ron_Vawter), [Dean Stockwell](/wiki/Dean_Stockwell), [Obba Babatundé](/wiki/Obba_Babatund%C3%A9), [Ted Levine](/wiki/Ted_Levine), [Paul Le Mat](/wiki/Paul_Le_Mat), [Mary Steenburgen](/wiki/Mary_Steenburgen), [Jason Robards](/wiki/Jason_Robards), [Scott Glenn](/wiki/Scott_Glenn), and his former producer [Roger Corman](/wiki/Roger_Corman), as well as casting musicians and bands in roles. These included [Sister Carol](/wiki/Sister_Carol), [Chris Isaak](/wiki/Chris_Isaak), [Tunde Adebimpe](/wiki/Tunde_Adebimpe), [the Feelies](/wiki/The_Feelies), [Charles Aznavour](/wiki/Charles_Aznavour), Steve Scales, [the Flirtations](/wiki/The_Flirtations_(group)), [Manno Charlemagne](/wiki/Manno_Charlemagne), [Bernie Worrell](/wiki/Bernie_Worrell), [David Johansen](/wiki/David_Johansen), [Beau Sia](/wiki/Beau_Sia), [Q Lazzarus](/wiki/Q_Lazzarus), [Robyn Hitchcock](/wiki/Robyn_Hitchcock), and [Rick Springfield](/wiki/Rick_Springfield). In addition to Corman, Demme cast a number of other fellow directors in cameos, including [John Sayles](/wiki/John_Sayles), [Agnès Varda](/wiki/Agn%C3%A8s_Varda), [George A. Romero](/wiki/George_A._Romero), [Sidney Lumet](/wiki/Sidney_Lumet), and [John Waters](/wiki/John_Waters). Many of these performers received opening credits billing in films they appeared in, despite sometimes having only one or two lines.

Writer/director [Paul Thomas Anderson](/wiki/Paul_Thomas_Anderson) has paid homage to Demme in his films and has cited him as a major influence in his work. In an interview, Anderson jokingly stated that the three filmmakers who inspired him the most are "Jonathan Demme, Jonathan Demme and Jonathan Demme." Other directors such as [Alexander Payne](/wiki/Alexander_Payne) and [Wes Anderson](/wiki/Wes_Anderson) have been known to be inspired by his close-ups in their own work.

## Political activism

Demme was involved in various political projects. In 1981, he directed a series of commercials for the liberal advocacy group [People for the American Way](/wiki/People_for_the_American_Way). The [spots](/wiki/Television_advertisement), titled "Eggs", "Music", and "Sports", were produced by [Norman Lear](/wiki/Norman_Lear) and featured [Muhammad Ali](/wiki/Muhammad_Ali), [Carol Burnett](/wiki/Carol_Burnett), and [Goldie Hawn](/wiki/Goldie_Hawn) celebrating [Freedom of Expression](/wiki/Freedom_of_speech_in_the_United_States). In 1985, he directed a video for [Artists United Against Apartheid](/wiki/Artists_United_Against_Apartheid). The short, featured various international musicians including [Afrika Bambaataa](/wiki/Afrika_Bambaataa), [Rubén Blades](/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Blades), [Jimmy Cliff](/wiki/Jimmy_Cliff), [Herbie Hancock](/wiki/Herbie_Hancock), [Little Steven](/wiki/Steven_Van_Zandt), [Run–D.M.C.](/wiki/Run%E2%80%93D.M.C.), and [Bruce Springsteen](/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen), calling for a boycott of the South African luxury resort [Sun City](/wiki/Sun_City,_North_West) during [Apartheid](/wiki/Apartheid). His documentary *Haiti Dreams of Democracy* (1988) captured Haiti's era of democratic rebuilding after dictatorship, while his documentary *[The Agronomist](/wiki/The_Agronomist)* (2008) profiled Haitian journalist and human rights activist [Jean Dominique](/wiki/Jean_Dominique). Demme spent six years on the documentary *I'm Carolyn Parker* (2011), which highlighted rebuilding efforts in [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans) [Lower Ninth Ward](/wiki/Lower_Ninth_Ward) after [Hurricane Katrina](/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina).

## Personal life

Demme was married twice. His first marriage to Evelyn Purcell ended in divorce. In 1987, he married artist Joanne Howard, with whom he had three children. He was the uncle of film director [Ted Demme](/wiki/Ted_Demme), who died in 2002. Demme's cousin was the [Rev. Robert Wilkinson Castle Jr.](/wiki/Robert_W._Castle), an Episcopal priest who appeared in some of Demme's films.

Demme was a member of the steering committee of the Friends of the [Apollo Theater, Oberlin, Ohio](/wiki/Apollo_Theatre_(Oberlin,_Ohio)), along with [Danny DeVito](/wiki/Danny_DeVito) and [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman). In 2013, he returned to Oberlin as part of an alumni reunion during the class of 2013 graduation ceremony and received the award for Honorary Doctor of Fine Arts.

In 2009, Demme signed a petition in support of director [Roman Polanski](/wiki/Roman_Polanski), who had been detained while traveling to a film festival in relation to his 1977 [sexual abuse charges](/wiki/Roman_Polanski_sexual_abuse_case), which the petition argued would undermine the tradition of film festivals as a place for works to be shown "freely and safely", and that arresting filmmakers traveling to neutral countries could open the door "for actions of which no-one can know the effects."

Demme was an avid collector and devotee of [Haitian art](/wiki/Haitian_art), in particular of [Hector Hyppolite](/wiki/Hector_Hyppolite) - so much so that he called it "an addiction". In 2014, he held an auction in Philadelphia selling thousands from his collection, much of which was donated to a cultural center in [Port-au-Prince](/wiki/Port-au-Prince).

## Death

Demme died at his home in [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan) on April 26, 2017, from complications from [esophageal cancer](/wiki/Esophageal_cancer) and [heart disease](/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease); he was 73.

> "I am heart-broken to lose a friend, a mentor, a guy so singular and dynamic you'd have to design a hurricane to contain him. Jonathan was as quirky as his comedies and as deep as his dramas. He was pure energy, the unstoppable cheerleader for anyone creative. Just as passionate about music as he was about art, he was and will always be a champion of the soul. JD, most beloved, something wild, brother of love, director of the lambs. Love that guy. Love him so much."

— [Jodie Foster](/wiki/Jodie_Foster)'s statement following Demme's death.

Director [Brady Corbet](/wiki/Brady_Corbet) dedicated his 2018 film *[Vox Lux](/wiki/Vox_Lux)* to Demme's memory, as did [Luca Guadagnino](/wiki/Luca_Guadagnino) with his 2018 film *[Suspiria](/wiki/Suspiria_(2018_film))* and Paul Thomas Anderson with his 2017 film *[Phantom Thread](/wiki/Phantom_Thread)* starring [Daniel Day-Lewis](/wiki/Daniel_Day-Lewis). Demme is thanked in the credits of [Spike Lee](/wiki/Spike_Lee)'s 2020 concert film *[American Utopia](/wiki/American_Utopia_(film))* starring [David Byrne](/wiki/David_Byrne). The album *[A Beginner's Mind](/wiki/A_Beginner%27s_Mind)* by musicians [Sufjan Stevens](/wiki/Sufjan_Stevens) and [Angelo De Augustine](/wiki/Angelo_De_Augustine) is dedicated to Demme, with one of its songs, "Cimmerian Shade", mentioning him and referencing *The Silence of the Lambs* within its lyrics.

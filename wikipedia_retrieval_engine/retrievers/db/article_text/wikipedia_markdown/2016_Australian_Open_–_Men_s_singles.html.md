Main article: [2016 Australian Open](/wiki/2016_Australian_Open)

Defending champion [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic) defeated [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray) in a rematch of the previous year's final, 6–1, 7–5, 7–6(7–3) to win the men's singles tennis title at the [2016 Australian Open](/wiki/2016_Australian_Open). It was his record-equaling sixth [Australian Open](/wiki/Australian_Open) men's singles title (tying [Roy Emerson](/wiki/Roy_Emerson)) and eleventh [major](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments) title overall. This was the fourth Australian Open final between [the pair](/wiki/Djokovic%E2%80%93Murray_rivalry). Murray was the second man in the [Open Era](/wiki/Open_Era) to lose five finals at the same major (after [Ivan Lendl](/wiki/Ivan_Lendl) at the [US Open](/wiki/US_Open_(tennis))), and is the only one to have the distinction without having won the title.

This marked the final professional appearance of former [world No. 1](/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_singles_players), two-time major champion and [2005 finalist](/wiki/2005_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt); he lost to [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer) in the second round.

This marked the 65th consecutive major appearance for Federer, setting a new record after surpassing [Wayne Ferreira](/wiki/Wayne_Ferreira)’s previous record of 54 consecutive major appearances ([Feliciano López](/wiki/Feliciano_L%C3%B3pez) later broke Federer’s record and set the current record at 79 consecutive major appearances from 2002 to 2022).

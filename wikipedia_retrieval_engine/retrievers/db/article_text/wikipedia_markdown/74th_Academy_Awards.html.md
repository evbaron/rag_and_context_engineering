# 74th Academy Awards

The **74th Academy Awards** ceremony, presented by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) (AMPAS), took place on March 24, 2002, at the [Kodak Theatre](/wiki/Kodak_Theatre) in [Hollywood](/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), Los Angeles. During the ceremony, AMPAS presented [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards) (commonly referred to as Oscars) in 24 categories honoring [films released in 2001](/wiki/2001_in_film). The ceremony, televised in the United States by [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company), was produced by [Laura Ziskin](/wiki/Laura_Ziskin) and directed by Louis J. Horvitz. Actress [Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg) hosted the show for the fourth time. She first hosted the [66th ceremony](/wiki/66th_Academy_Awards) held in 1994 and had last hosted the [71st ceremony](/wiki/71st_Academy_Awards) in 1999. Three weeks earlier, in a ceremony held at the [Regent Beverly Wilshire Hotel](/wiki/Beverly_Wilshire_Hotel) in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California), on March 2, the [Academy Awards for Technical Achievement](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Technical_Achievement) were presented by host [Charlize Theron](/wiki/Charlize_Theron).

*[A Beautiful Mind](/wiki/A_Beautiful_Mind_(film))* won four awards, including [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture). Other winners included *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* with four awards, *[Black Hawk Down](/wiki/Black_Hawk_Down_(film))* and *[Moulin Rouge!](/wiki/Moulin_Rouge!)* with two, and *[The Accountant](/wiki/The_Accountant_(2001_film))*, *[For the Birds](/wiki/For_the_Birds_(film))*, *[Gosford Park](/wiki/Gosford_Park)*, *[Iris](/wiki/Iris_(2001_film))*, *[Monster's Ball](/wiki/Monster%27s_Ball)*, *[Monsters, Inc.](/wiki/Monsters,_Inc.)*, *[Murder on a Sunday Morning](/wiki/Murder_on_a_Sunday_Morning)*, *[No Man's Land](/wiki/No_Man%27s_Land_(2001_film))*, *[Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Pearl_Harbor_(film))*, *[Shrek](/wiki/Shrek)*, *[Thoth](/wiki/Thoth_(film))*, and *[Training Day](/wiki/Training_Day)* with one. Despite a record length of four hours and twenty-three minutes, the telecast garnered nearly 42 million viewers in the United States.

## Winners and nominees

The nominees for the 74th Academy Awards were announced on February 12, 2002, at the [Samuel Goldwyn Theater](/wiki/Samuel_Goldwyn_Theater) in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California), by [Frank Pierson](/wiki/Frank_Pierson), president of the academy, and the actress [Marcia Gay Harden](/wiki/Marcia_Gay_Harden). *[The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings:_The_Fellowship_of_the_Ring)* earned the most nominations with thirteen. It was the seventh film to earn that many nominations. *[A Beautiful Mind](/wiki/A_Beautiful_Mind_(film))* and *[Moulin Rouge!](/wiki/Moulin_Rouge!)* tied for second place with eight apiece.

The winners were announced during the awards ceremony on March 24, 2002. By virtue of its latest Best Picture victory for *A Beautiful Mind*, [DreamWorks](/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures) became the second film studio to release three consecutive Best Picture winners; the studio had previously released *[American Beauty](/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film))* and *[Gladiator](/wiki/Gladiator_(2000_film))*. [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington) was the second African-American to win the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor), following [Sidney Poitier](/wiki/Sidney_Poitier) for 1963's *[Lilies of the Field](/wiki/Lilies_of_the_Field_(1963_film))*. [Halle Berry](/wiki/Halle_Berry) became the first, and as of 2026, only, African-American to win the [Academy Award for Best Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress). Nominated for their performances as the [title character](/wiki/Iris_Murdoch) in *[Iris](/wiki/Iris_(2001_film))*, Best Actress nominee [Judi Dench](/wiki/Judi_Dench) and [Best Supporting Actress](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actress) nominee [Kate Winslet](/wiki/Kate_Winslet) became the second pair of actresses nominated for portraying the same character in the same film, following Best Actress nominee Winslet and Best Supporting Actress nominee [Gloria Stuart](/wiki/Gloria_Stuart) as Rose in 1997's *[Titanic](/wiki/Titanic_(1997_film))*.

## Ceremony information

[Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg) hosted the 74th Academy Awards.

The Academy wanted to find a new venue for the festivities amid limited seating and rehearsal time concerns with the [Dorothy Chandler Pavilion](/wiki/Dorothy_Chandler_Pavilion). In addition, problems arose regarding staging the Oscars at the [Shrine Auditorium](/wiki/Shrine_Auditorium) because there was difficulty of directing guests from the auditorium where the main event took place to the adjacent Exhibition Hall for the Governor's Ball. In August 1997, AMPAS and Canadian development firm [TrizecHahn](/wiki/The_Hahn_Company) went into negotiations over the development of an entertainment complex located on the corner of [Hollywood Boulevard](/wiki/Hollywood_Boulevard) and [Highland Avenue](/wiki/Highland_Avenue_(Los_Angeles)) adjacent to the [Mann's Chinese Theatre](/wiki/Grauman%27s_Chinese_Theatre). Seven months later, both the Academy and TrizecHahn agreed on a twenty-year lease that allowed for the ceremony to be staged at a new venue, which would later be called the [Kodak Theatre](/wiki/Kodak_Theatre), located within the property which was also situated near the [Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel](/wiki/Hollywood_Roosevelt_Hotel), the site of the [inaugural awards ceremony](/wiki/1st_Academy_Awards) in 1929. This was the first time the ceremony was held in Hollywood since the [32nd ceremony](/wiki/32nd_Academy_Awards) took place at the [Pantages Theatre](/wiki/Pantages_Theatre_(Hollywood)) in 1960.

In view of the return of the Oscars to Hollywood, the Academy hired film producer and [Sony Pictures Entertainment](/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Entertainment) chairman [Laura Ziskin](/wiki/Laura_Ziskin) in September 2001 to oversee production of the telecast. Pierson explained the decision to hire Ziskin saying, "This show is one of the most difficult—if not the most difficult—producing jobs in show business. Laura Ziskin brings intelligence, experience and wit expressed in everything she has done." This marked the first occurrence that a woman produced the Oscars solo. Four months later, [Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg) was selected as host of the 2002 ceremony. In an article in the *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)*, Ziskin justified her choice of Goldberg commenting that she has "great warmth, with humor, humanity and social conscience, all qualities that I feel are essential for this year's show. I look forward to collaborating with Whoopi to put on a meaningful and entertaining evening."

Furthermore, the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks) affected the telecast and its surrounding events. Despite speculation and suggestions that the festivities be postponed or canceled, AMPAS President Pierson wrote in a *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* column refusing to take such action stating that it would send the message that "[the terrorists have won](/wiki/The_terrorists_have_won)". However, due to security concerns, the Academy announced that red carpet bleacher seats would now be limited on a reservation basis based on a random selection and a [background check](/wiki/Background_check).

On Oscar night, [Tom Cruise](/wiki/Tom_Cruise) opened the show and stated that it was the job of filmmakers to make films during troubling times. In addition, later in the evening Goldberg introduced a "New York icon" to the stage and filmmaker and director [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen), who had previously never attended a ceremony, made a surprise appearance. Allen was greeted with a hearty standing ovation from audience members including [Baz Luhrmann](/wiki/Baz_Luhrmann), [Ron Howard](/wiki/Ron_Howard), [Jennifer Connelly](/wiki/Jennifer_Connelly), [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington), and [Ethan Hawke](/wiki/Ethan_Hawke). He explained that the September 11 attacks influenced him to attend the Oscars so that he could represent the city he deeply admired and urged filmmakers to continue to film in New York City. Allen then presented a film montage directed by fellow New Yorker and screenwriter [Nora Ephron](/wiki/Nora_Ephron) saluting New York City in film.

Several other people participated in the production of the ceremony. Actors [Donald Sutherland](/wiki/Donald_Sutherland) and [Glenn Close](/wiki/Glenn_Close) served as announcers during the show. The orchestra, led by film composer and musical supervisor [John Williams](/wiki/John_Williams), performed selections of film scores during a montage saluting film composers produced by [Kyle Cooper](/wiki/Kyle_Cooper). Filmmaker [Errol Morris](/wiki/Errol_Morris) filmed a vignette featuring several famous people discuss movie memories. Director [Penelope Spheeris](/wiki/Penelope_Spheeris) produced a montage saluting 60 years of Oscar-winning documentary feature films. [Cirque du Soleil](/wiki/Cirque_du_Soleil) performed a dance number inspired by movies and visual effects.

### Introduction of Best Animated Feature award

Beginning with this ceremony, AMPAS introduced a new competitive award that would honor [animated](/wiki/Animation) feature films. According to Academy communications director John Pavlik, the film must be at least 70 minutes in length, have a significant amount of animated characters, and be at least 75 percent animated in order to be qualified for consideration. A minimum of eight qualifying films must be released within the calendar year to permit a slate of three nominees. If the number of films exceeds twelve, the nominee roster increases to five. Prior to the introduction of this category, three [Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures) films (1937's *[Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs](/wiki/Snow_White_and_the_Seven_Dwarfs_(1937_film))*, 1988's *[Who Framed Roger Rabbit](/wiki/Who_Framed_Roger_Rabbit)*, and 1995's *[Toy Story](/wiki/Toy_Story)*) were all given [Special Achievement Academy Awards](/wiki/Special_Achievement_Academy_Award).

### Box office performance of nominated films

At the time of the nominations announcement on February 12, the combined gross of the five Best Picture nominees at the US box office was $484 million, with an average of $96.9 million per film. *The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring* was the highest earner among the Best Picture nominees with $271 million in domestic box office receipts. The film was followed by *A Beautiful Mind* ($113 million), *Moulin Rouge!* ($57.1 million), *Gosford Park* ($22.2 million), and finally *In the Bedroom* ($19.5 million).

Of the top 50 grossing movies of the year, 46 nominations went to 14 films on the list. Only *The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring* (2nd), *Shrek* (3rd), *Monsters, Inc.* (4th), *A Beautiful Mind* (15th), *Black Hawk Down* (25th), *Jimmy Neutron: Boy Genius* (27th), *Training Day* (29th), *Bridget Jones's Diary* (31st), *Ali* (41st), and *Moulin Rouge!* (44th) were nominated for Best Picture, Best Animated Feature, or any of the directing, acting, or screenwriting awards. The other top-50 box office hits that earned nominations were *Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone* (1st), *Pearl Harbor* (7th), *Vanilla Sky* (19th), and *AI: Artificial Intelligence* (28th).

### Critical reviews

The show received a mixed reception from media publications. Some media outlets were more critical of the show. Television critic Robert Bianco of *[USA Today](/wiki/USA_Today)* complained that the awards ceremony was "intensely narcissistic and characteristically, almost unrelievedly, dull." Columnist Matthew Gilbert of *[The Boston Globe](/wiki/The_Boston_Globe)* bemoaned that "TV's most-watched slug crawled back into town last night." He also sniped, "As usual, the technical awards formed a [Bermuda Triangle](/wiki/Bermuda_Triangle) in the middle of the show, and the film-clip fests and production numbers numbed our brains." *[The Sacramento Bee](/wiki/The_Sacramento_Bee)*'s Rick Kishman lamented that "It was the first time both best-acting Oscars went to African Americans...yet viewers had to fight hours and hours of boredom to care." He also quipped that the excessive amount of montage and tributes dragged down the proceedings.

Other media outlets received the broadcast more positively. *[Orange County Register](/wiki/Orange_County_Register)* film critic Henry Sheehan praised Goldberg's performance as hosting writing that her "ensuing entrance a la *Moulin Rouge* was a comparative triumph and her boom-boom-boom succession of jokes put the show right on track." Television columnist Joanne Ostrow of *[The Denver Post](/wiki/The_Denver_Post)* raved, "The nearly five-hour telecast was stunning, historic, slick, efficient, and helped along by some knockout clothes." She also commented that Washington and Berry's acceptance speeches and the Sidney Poitier tribute added to the historic and emotional mood of the festivities. John Levesque of the *[Seattle Post-Intelligencer](/wiki/Seattle_Post-Intelligencer)* commended producer Ziskin for producing "the best Oscar telecast this TV watcher can remember." In addition, he wrote that "It was clear the 74th Academy Awards ceremony was something special: fresh, crisp, different from its predecessors."

### Ratings and reception

The American telecast on ABC drew in an average of 41.82 million people over its length, which was a 3% decrease from the [previous year's ceremony](/wiki/73rd_Academy_Awards). The show also earned lower [Nielsen ratings](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings) compared to the previous ceremony with 25.54% of households watching over a 40.34 share. In addition, it garnered a lower 18–49 demo rating with a 16.13 rating over a 36.46 share among viewers in that demographic.

In July 2002, the ceremony presentation received seven nominations at the [54th Primetime Emmys](/wiki/54th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards). Two months later, the ceremony won one of those nominations for Debra Brown's choreography during the telecast.

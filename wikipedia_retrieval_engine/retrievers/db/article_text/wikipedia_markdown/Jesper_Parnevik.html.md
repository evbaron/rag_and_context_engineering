**Jesper Bo Parnevik** (Swedish pronunciation: [[ˈjɛ̌sːpɛr ˈpɑ̂ːɳɛˌviːk]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Swedish); born 7 March 1965) is a Swedish [professional golfer](/wiki/Professional_golfer). He spent 38 weeks in the top 10 of the [Official World Golf Ranking](/wiki/Official_World_Golf_Ranking) in 2000 and 2001.

## Early life

Parnevik was born in [Botkyrka](/wiki/Botkyrka), [Stockholm County](/wiki/Stockholm_County), and is the son of Swedish entertainer [Bosse Parnevik](/wiki/Bosse_Parnevik) and his wife Gertie (b. 1940). He grew up in [Åkersberga](/wiki/%C3%85kersberga).

Parnevik became a member of the first group of students in Sweden to combine studying with golf training at the Swedish upper secondary sports school in [Danderyd](/wiki/Danderyd_Municipality) outside Stockholm. At age 15, Parnevik spent 10 days in [Myrtle Beach, South Carolina](/wiki/Myrtle_Beach,_South_Carolina), and developed an appreciation for life in the United States; he later moved to [Palm Beach County, Florida](/wiki/Palm_Beach_County,_Florida), to attend [Palm Beach Junior College](/wiki/Palm_Beach_Junior_College) in [Lake Worth](/wiki/Lake_Worth,_Florida) on a golf scholarship.

## Amateur career

Parnevik was a member of the team representing Sweden at the [1984](/wiki/1984_Eisenhower_Trophy) and [1986](/wiki/1986_Eisenhower_Trophy) [Eisenhower Trophy](/wiki/Eisenhower_Trophy). He was also part of the Swedish team finishing second, after losing in the final against [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland), at the [1985](/wiki/1985_European_Amateur_Team_Championship) [European Amateur Team Championship](/wiki/European_Amateur_Team_Championship) on home soil in [Halmstad](/wiki/Halmstad_Golf_Club), Sweden, where Parnevik won individually at the initial qualifying stroke-play competition.

## Professional career

In 1986, Parnevik turned professional. After winning the [Swedish Golf Tour](/wiki/Swedish_Golf_Tour) Order of Merit in [1988](/wiki/1988_Swedish_Golf_Tour), including a runner-up finish to [Vijay Singh](/wiki/Vijay_Singh) at the 1988 Swedish PGA Championship, he scored four wins on the [European Tour](/wiki/European_Tour). His breakthrough came when he out-dueled [Payne Stewart](/wiki/Payne_Stewart) at the [1993](/wiki/1993_European_Tour) [Scottish Open](/wiki/Scottish_Open_(golf)) at [Gleneagles](/wiki/Gleneagles_Hotel) King's Course.

With victories in 1995 and 1998, Parnevik became the first Swede to win twice on the European Tour on home soil. At his first victory at the [Scandinavian Masters](/wiki/Scandinavian_Masters) in [1995](/wiki/1995_European_Tour) at [Barsebäck Golf & Country Club](/wiki/Barseb%C3%A4ck_Golf_%26_Country_Club), he played 72 holes competition and 18 holes pro-am without a bogey, but with one double-bogey.

### PGA Tour

Based in Florida, he joined the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour) in the mid-90s, subsequently winning five events. His playing career also includes three [Ryder Cup](/wiki/Ryder_Cup) appearances (1997, 1999, and 2002) and two runner-up finishes in [The Open](/wiki/The_Open_Championship) (1994 and 1997). His career best [world ranking](/wiki/Official_World_Golf_Ranking) of seventh, which he attained on 14 May 2000, was the highest world ranking achieved by a [Swedish golfer](/wiki/List_of_Swedish_professional_golfers) until [Henrik Stenson](/wiki/Henrik_Stenson) reached the top five in February 2007.

In late 2000, Parnevik underwent hip surgery at the persuasion of [Greg Norman](/wiki/Greg_Norman), who had undergone similar surgery.

Parnevik finished second in the [Valero Texas Open](/wiki/Valero_Texas_Open) in October 2007, losing to [Justin Leonard](/wiki/Justin_Leonard) in a playoff. In 2009, after a tie for 17th at the [SAS Masters](/wiki/SAS_Masters) in Sweden, Parnevik underwent further hip surgery in [Vail, Colorado](/wiki/Vail,_Colorado), United States, which cut short his 2009 season on the PGA Tour. He also cited the hip injury as the reason for his withdrawal from the [PGA Tour Qualifying Tournament](/wiki/PGA_Tour_Qualifying_Tournament) ("Q-School") in December 2009.

In 2010, Parnevik missed cuts in his first three tournaments and had to withdraw again after a first round 68 at the [Northern Trust Open](/wiki/Northern_Trust_Open) because of an ailing back. He had emergency surgery where it was discovered that he had a broken [lumbar vertebrae](/wiki/Lumbar_vertebrae) that could put his career in jeopardy.

Parnevik played the 2011 PGA Tour season on a fully exempt status for his Top 50 All-Time Earnings ranking. He was allowed to reuse this one-time exemption after being able to play only five tournaments in 2010. He played the 2012 and 2013 seasons on a Medical Extension after suffering a severe injury to his right hand in a boating accident. Despite his limited play, Parnevik made news in 2013 after bogeying the 18th hole at the [RBC Heritage](/wiki/RBC_Heritage), which allowed a record-tying 91 players to make the 36 hole cut.

Plagued by injuries for years, Parnevik staged a comeback on the [Champions Tour](/wiki/Champions_Tour) when he became eligible in 2015. He started [2016](/wiki/2016_Champions_Tour) with a tie for third at the [Chubb Classic](/wiki/Chubb_Classic), losing out to former [world number one](/wiki/List_of_World_Number_One_male_golfers) golfers [Bernhard Langer](/wiki/Bernhard_Langer) and [Fred Couples](/wiki/Fred_Couples). He got his first win on the Champions Tour in the same year at the [Insperity Invitational](/wiki/Insperity_Invitational).

### Style

Parnevik is known for his distinctive and [eccentric](/wiki/Eccentricity_(behavior)) taste in clothes and fashion as well as his playing achievements, a fashion sense that led golf writer [Dan Jenkins](/wiki/Dan_Jenkins) to describe him as "the last guy to climb out of the clown car at the circus". His on-course trademark was the upturned bill on his baseball cap. While on the European [Ryder Cup](/wiki/Ryder_Cup) team, he received a customized team cap with the Ryder Cup logo on the bottom of the bill instead of the front, so that it could be seen with the bill turned up. He has since stopped wearing the flipped-bill hat, opting for strawhats and visors but retains a flamboyant sense of fashion, such as [disco](/wiki/Disco)-style purple trousers and other golf apparel designed by [Johan Lindeberg](/wiki/Johan_Lindeberg). He has been politely described as "eclectic", and has been known to change outfits at the halfway stage of a round of tournament golf. Beginning in 2006, one of his new on-course fashion statements was a necktie worn under a vest. Puma Golf signed Jesper Parnevik in 2014 and he gets credited for his influence on their LUX Golf Appearel Line. Since wearing Puma Clothes he once again wears a custom made and more modern looking flipped bill hat. He is also known to eat volcanic dust as a dietary supplement. Parnevik's nickname on tour is "Spaceman".

## Awards and honors

- In 1998, Parnevik was awarded honorary member of the PGA of Sweden.
- He was awarded the 1999 [Swedish Golfer of the Year](/wiki/Swedish_Golfer_of_the_Year).
- In 2003, the three Swedish teammates of the victorious European Ryder Cup team the previous year, [Niclas Fasth](/wiki/Niclas_Fasth), [Pierre Fulke](/wiki/Pierre_Fulke) and Parnevik, was each, by the [Swedish Golf Federation](/wiki/Swedish_Golf_Federation), awarded the Golden Club, the highest award for contributions to Swedish golf, as the 30th, 31st and 32nd recipients.
- In 2023, he was inducted into the Swedish Golf Hall of Fame.

## Personal life

Parnevik has a waterfront home in [Tequesta, Florida](/wiki/Tequesta,_Florida). He is married to Mia Parnevik (née Sandsten 1968), and they have three daughters and one son. His oldest daughter [Peg Parnevik](/wiki/Peg_Parnevik) has a career as a pop singer. Another daughter, Penny, is married to former [NHL](/wiki/NHL) ice hockey player [Douglas Murray](/wiki/Douglas_Murray_(ice_hockey)). His son Phoenix is named after Parnevik's first PGA Tour win at the 1998 Phoenix Open.

Parnevik is credited with having introduced fellow professional golfer [Tiger Woods](/wiki/Tiger_Woods) to Swedish [au pair](/wiki/Au_pair) [Elin Nordegren](/wiki/Elin_Nordegren) (previously employed by Parnevik), whom Woods eventually married. Parnevik subsequently stated in 2009 that he regretted his responsibility for this introduction after reports of Woods' infidelity surfaced that year.

## In popular culture

- Parnevik made a cameo in the 2007 comedy, *[Who's Your Caddy](/wiki/Who%27s_Your_Caddy)*, in which he plays himself.
- Parnevik and his family starred in a reality TV show, *[Parneviks](/wiki/Parneviks)*, which aired on [TV3](/wiki/TV3_(Sweden)) in Sweden for four seasons from 2015 to 2018, giving insights into his life in Florida.

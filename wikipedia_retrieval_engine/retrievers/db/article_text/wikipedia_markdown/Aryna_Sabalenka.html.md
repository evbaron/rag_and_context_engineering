In this name that follows [East Slavic naming customs](/wiki/East_Slavic_naming_customs), the [patronymic](/wiki/Patronymic) is  *Siarhiejeŭna* and the [family name](/wiki/Surname) is  *Sabalenka*.

**Aryna Siarhiejeŭna Sabalenka** (born 5 May 1998) is a Belarusian professional [tennis](/wiki/Tennis) player. She is the current world No. 1 in women's [singles](/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players) by the [WTA](/wiki/Women%27s_Tennis_Association) and is a former No. 1 in [doubles](/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_doubles_tennis_players). Sabalenka has won 24 [WTA Tour](/wiki/WTA_Tour)-level singles titles, including four [majors](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments) – two each at the [Australian Open](/wiki/Australian_Open) and the [US Open](/wiki/US_Open_(tennis)) – as well as 11 [WTA 1000](/wiki/WTA_1000) events. She has also won six doubles titles, including the [2019 US Open](/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) and [2021 Australian Open](/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles), both with [Elise Mertens](/wiki/Elise_Mertens).

Sabalenka came to prominence in 2017 when, together with [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich), they led the [Belarus Fed Cup team](/wiki/Belarus_Fed_Cup_team) to a runner-up finish even though both were ranked outside the top 75 at the time. She finished 2018 and 2019 ranked world No. 11 in singles. Following two major singles semifinal appearances in 2021, Sabalenka rose to world No. 2 but struggled to maintain that success in 2022 consistently. In 2023, she won her first major singles title at the [Australian Open](/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), finished runner-up at the [US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), and obtained the world No. 1 ranking for the first time.

In 2024, Sabalenka defended her [Australian Open](/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) title and won a third major singles title at the [US Open](/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), finishing as the year-end No. 1 for the first time. In 2025, she defended her title at the [US Open](/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) and was runner-up at the [Australian](/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) and [French](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) Opens, holding the No. 1 position for the entire season. In 2026, she reached an eighth major singles final at the [Australian Open](/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) and completed the [Sunshine Double](/wiki/Sunshine_Double) (winning [Indian Wells](/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) and [Miami](/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) back-to-back). Also in 2026, Sabalenka won the [Laureus World Sports Award](/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award) for [Sportswoman of the Year](/wiki/Laureus_World_Sports_Award_for_Sportswoman_of_the_Year).

Sabalenka began playing doubles regularly in 2019. With Mertens as her partner, she found instant success by winning the [Sunshine Double](/wiki/Sunshine_Double) ([Indian Wells](/wiki/2019_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) and [Miami](/wiki/2019_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles)) that same year. They won the [US Open](/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) later in the season, and Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Finals](/wiki/2019_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Doubles) for the first time. After she and Mertens won their second major title at the [Australian Open](/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) in 2021, Sabalenka became the world No. 1 in the discipline, after which she shifted to playing exclusively singles.

## Early life and background

Sabalenka was born in [Minsk](/wiki/Minsk), the capital of [Belarus](/wiki/Belarus). Her father, Sergey (died 2019), was an [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) player. Sabalenka started playing tennis by chance. She said, "One day, my dad was just driving me somewhere in the car, and he saw tennis courts on the way. So he took me to the courts. I liked it and enjoyed it, and that's how it was. That's how it started." She began training at the National Tennis Academy in Minsk when it opened in 2014. In 2015, the Belarusian Tennis Federation persuaded Sabalenka and her team to focus on playing low-level professional events instead of junior tournaments, even though she was still eligible to compete at the junior level.

## Career

### Juniors

Sabalenka had a late start on the [ITF Junior Circuit](/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit), instead competing on the U14 and U16 Tennis Europe tours at a younger age. She did not compete in the main draw of any ITF events until 2013 at the low-level Grade-4 Tallink Cup in Estonia at the age of 15. She never played in the junior Grand Slam tournaments or any other high-level Grade-A and Grade-1 events. Without the higher point levels from these bigger tournaments, she had a career-high ranking of just No. 225. Sabalenka won her first ITF title in doubles at the lowest-level Grade-5 Alatan Tour Cup in Belarus in late 2013 with compatriot [Vera Lapko](/wiki/Vera_Lapko) as her partner. In 2014, she excelled at Grade 4 events. She reached her first singles final at the Estonian Junior Open in June and won her first singles title at the MTV Total Junior Cup in Finland in October. At the end of the season, Sabalenka defended her Alatan Tour Cup doubles title, this time with compatriot Nika Shytkouskaya, and also won the singles title. She only played in one tournament in 2015, the European Junior Championships. As a Grade B1 event, this was the highest-level junior tournament she played in. She lost in the second round to top seed [Markéta Vondroušová](/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1).

### 2012–2016: Professional & Fed Cup debut

Sabalenka began playing on the [ITF Women's Circuit](/wiki/ITF_Women%27s_World_Tennis_Tour) in 2012, even before she competed on the [ITF Junior Circuit](/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit). Her first five tournaments were in her hometown of Minsk and spread out over two years, but she did not win a main draw match in any of them. She won her first professional match at the very end of 2014 in Istanbul. The following season in October, she won her first two titles in back-to-back weeks in [Antalya](/wiki/Antalya), both at the $10k level. Sabalenka also won a $25k title the last week of the year. This title put her into the top 300 WTA rankings for the first time at the start of 2016. That year, she made her Fed Cup debut in April, losing her only match. She also won her two biggest titles to date at the $50k level. The first in [Tianjin](/wiki/2016_Tianjin_Health_Industry_Park) put her into the top 200 in May and the second in [Toyota](/wiki/2016_Dunlop_World_Challenge) in November helped her finish the year ranked at No. 137 in the world.

### 2017: WTA 125 title, top 100

Sabalenka at the [2017 Washington Open](/wiki/2017_Citi_Open)

Despite some early season success in the Fed Cup, Sabalenka had a quiet start to the year. She played in her first WTA Tour main draw in February as a qualifier at the [Dubai Open](/wiki/2017_Dubai_Tennis_Championships); however, she did not win her first WTA Tour match until [Wimbledon](/wiki/2017_Wimbledon_Championships) in July. In her Grand Slam debut, she again reached the main draw through qualifying and defeated [Irina Khromacheva](/wiki/Irina_Khromacheva) in the opening round. Sabalenka followed up this achievement with another win at the [Washington Open](/wiki/2017_Citi_Open) over No. 34 [Lauren Davis](/wiki/Lauren_Davis), the 2016 runner-up and the highest-ranked player she had defeated at the time.

After losing in qualifying at the [US Open](/wiki/2017_US_Open_(tennis)), Sabalenka reached her first ever WTA semifinal at the [Tashkent Open](/wiki/2017_Tashkent_Open), defeating third seed and world No. 53, [Tatjana Maria](/wiki/Tatjana_Maria), along the way. A few weeks later, she entered the [Tianjin Open](/wiki/2017_Tianjin_Open) as the 119th-ranked player in the world, but managed to reach her first WTA tournament final. There, she faced her childhood idol [Maria Sharapova](/wiki/Maria_Sharapova), but ultimately lost in two tight sets. With this performance, she rose to No. 76 in the rankings, entering the top 100 for the first time. After losing a tight Fed Cup final to the [United States](/wiki/United_States_Fed_Cup_team), Sabalenka finished the season by winning the biggest title of her career at the time at the [Mumbai Open](/wiki/2017_Mumbai_Open), a [WTA 125](/wiki/WTA_125_tournaments) event. The title cemented her at No. 78 at the end of the year.

### 2018: Newcomer of the Year, Premier 5 title

Sabalenka at the [2018 Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships)

After playing relatively few WTA events in 2017, Sabalenka used her higher ranking to play exclusively on the WTA Tour 2018. She reached two quarterfinals to begin the year, but lost her opening-round match at the [Australian Open](/wiki/2018_Australian_Open) to top-ranked Australian and world No. 18 [Ashleigh Barty](/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty). She then won her first matches at a [Premier](/wiki/WTA_Premier_tournaments) tournament with a third-round appearance at the [Indian Wells Open](/wiki/2018_BNP_Paribas_Open) before the early-year hardcourt season came to a close, including a victory over No. 19, [Svetlana Kuznetsova](/wiki/Svetlana_Kuznetsova).

Sabalenka began the clay-court season by reaching a second career final at the [Ladies Open Lugano](/wiki/2018_Ladies_Open_Lugano), where she finished runner-up to No. 20 [Elise Mertens](/wiki/Elise_Mertens). This success put her in the top 50 for the first time. However, she did not win another match for the rest of the clay-court season; this included a first-round loss to No. 22 [Kiki Bertens](/wiki/Kiki_Bertens) at the [French Open](/wiki/2018_French_Open). Sabalenka had stronger results on grass, playing in tune-ups during each of the three weeks before Wimbledon. She made it to the quarterfinals at the [Rosmalen Grass Court Championships](/wiki/2018_Rosmalen_Grass_Court_Championships) and the final at the Premier level [Eastbourne International](/wiki/2018_Eastbourne_International). At the latter event, she won five consecutive three-set matches, including three over top 20 opponents and her first top-ten victory against defending champion and world No. 7 [Karolína Plíšková](/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1). Sabalenka lost the final to world No. 2 [Caroline Wozniacki](/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki). For the third consecutive Grand Slam event, she went out in the first round at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2018_Wimbledon_Championships).

During the North American hardcourt summer season, Sabalenka rose through the rankings. At the two Premier 5 tournaments, she reached the third round at the [Canadian Open](/wiki/2018_Rogers_Cup) and the semifinals at the [Cincinnati Open](/wiki/2018_Western_%26_Southern_Open). In the former, she avenged her previous loss to world No. 2, Wozniacki, for the biggest win of her career, hitting 64 winners during the match. In the latter, she recorded two more top-ten wins over No. 8 Plíšková and No. 5 [Caroline Garcia](/wiki/Caroline_Garcia) before losing to world No. 1, [Simona Halep](/wiki/Simona_Halep). Just a week later, Sabalenka won her first WTA Tour title at the Premier-level [Connecticut Open](/wiki/2018_Connecticut_Open) with wins over world No. 9, [Julia Görges](/wiki/Julia_G%C3%B6rges), in the semifinal, and [Carla Suárez Navarro](/wiki/Carla_Su%C3%A1rez_Navarro) in the final. Playing a fourth consecutive week, she closed out this part of the season with her best result at a Grand Slam tournament to date, making it to the fourth round at the [US Open](/wiki/2018_US_Open_(tennis)). In particular, she upset world No. 5, [Petra Kvitová](/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1), in the third round before losing to the eventual champion, [Naomi Osaka](/wiki/Naomi_Osaka). She was the only player to win a set against Osaka in the tournament.

After the US Open, Sabalenka earned her first No. 1 seed at the [Tournoi de Québec](/wiki/2018_Coupe_Banque_Nationale) but lost her opening match. Nonetheless, she followed this up by winning the Premier 5 level [Wuhan Open](/wiki/2018_Wuhan_Open), her biggest title so far. During the event, she upset No. 6 [Elina Svitolina](/wiki/Elina_Svitolina) in the second round and did not drop a set in her last four matches. The following week, Sabalenka reached the quarterfinals of the [China Open](/wiki/2018_China_Open_(tennis)), a run that included a win over defending champion and No. 4, Caroline Garcia, for her eighth top-ten victory of the season. This success in China helped her climb to No. 11 in the world. At the end of the season, Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Elite Trophy](/wiki/2018_WTA_Elite_Trophy), where she was grouped with Garcia and Ashleigh Barty. She defeated Barty to open the group but lost to Garcia in the group's final match. Barty, having defeated Garcia with fewer games lost, advanced out of the group through the tiebreak criteria to end Sabalenka's season. Nonetheless, she was named the [WTA Newcomer of the Year](/wiki/WTA_Newcomer_of_the_Year) for her excellent performance in her first full year on the WTA Tour.

### 2019: Singles top 10, doubles No. 2

#### Singles: Elite Trophy, three titles

Although Sabalenka once again struggled at the Grand Slam tournaments, she ultimately finished the year with the same year-end ranking as in 2018 on the strength of three titles, all in China. She began the season by winning her third career WTA title at the [Shenzhen Open](/wiki/2019_WTA_Shenzhen_Open), defeating [Alison Riske](/wiki/Alison_Riske) in the final in a tight three-set match. Due to rain delays in the earlier rounds, she needed to play both the semifinal and the final on the last day of the tournament. However, she could not build on this success in the rest of the first half of the year. Sabalenka lost to 17-year-old [Amanda Anisimova](/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova) in straight sets at both the [Australian Open](/wiki/2019_Australian_Open) and the [French Open](/wiki/2019_French_Open) in the third and second rounds, respectively. She had been considered the third-leading favourite for the title at the Australian Open. Nonetheless, she made her top-10 debut following the event. Sabalenka fared worse at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships), losing her opening match to No. 139 [Magdaléna Rybáriková](/wiki/Magdal%C3%A9na_Ryb%C3%A1rikov%C3%A1). In-between the Grand Slam tournaments, Sabalenka's best result was a semifinal loss to No. 8, Kiki Bertens, at the Premier-level [St. Petersburg Trophy](/wiki/2019_St._Petersburg_Ladies%27_Trophy) in February. She also made the fourth round at the [Indian Wells Open](/wiki/Indian_Wells_Open). Her best result on clay was a semifinal at the [Internationaux de Strasbourg](/wiki/2019_Internationaux_de_Strasbourg) in May.

Sabalenka had a better second half of the season. In her first tournament following Wimbledon, she finished runner-up to [Zheng Saisai](/wiki/Zheng_Saisai) at the [Silicon Valley Classic](/wiki/2019_Silicon_Valley_Classic), a Premier-level event. She did not perform well at either Premier 5 tournament in August or the US Open, losing in the second round at the last Grand Slam tournament of the year. Sabalenka returned to China following the US Open, and produced three strong results in four events. After a quarterfinal at the [Zhengzhou Open](/wiki/2019_Zhengzhou_Open), she defended her title at the Premier 5 [Wuhan Open](/wiki/2019_Wuhan_Open). During the event, she defeated No. 8 Kiki Bertens in the third round and No. 1 Ashleigh Barty in the semifinals, her first victory over a current world No. 1 player. She won the final over [Alison Riske](/wiki/Alison_Riske). At the end of the season, Sabalenka qualified for the [WTA Elite Trophy](/wiki/2019_WTA_Elite_Trophy) for the second consecutive year. She swept her round-robin group of [Maria Sakkari](/wiki/Maria_Sakkari) and her doubles partner Elise Mertens. In the knockout rounds, Sabalenka defeated [Karolína Muchová](/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Muchov%C3%A1) and Bertens for her fifth career title, and her third title of the year in China.

#### Doubles: US Open title, Sunshine Double

Mertens and Sabalenka at the 2019 French Open

Sabalenka started the year ranked No. 73 in doubles. She began partnering with Elise Mertens in January, when the pair lost to top seeds [Barbora Krejčíková](/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) and [Kateřina Siniaková](/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1) in the third round of the [Australian Open](/wiki/2019_Australian_Open). They had their breakthrough in March at the two Premier Mandatory tournaments. In only their second tournament together, Sabalenka and Mertens won the [Indian Wells Open](/wiki/2019_Indian_Wells_Masters). They defeated three of the top five seeds in the event, including second seeds [Tímea Babos](/wiki/T%C3%ADmea_Babos) and [Kristina Mladenovic](/wiki/Kristina_Mladenovic) in the first round and top seeds Krejčíková and Siniaková in the final. The pair matched this success at their next event by winning the [Miami Open](/wiki/2019_Miami_Open) to complete the Sunshine Double. They defeated three of the top six seeds, including third seeds [Hsieh Su-wei](/wiki/Hsieh_Su-wei) and [Barbora Strýcová](/wiki/Barbora_Str%C3%BDcov%C3%A1) in the second round. They won the final against sixth seeds [Samantha Stosur](/wiki/Samantha_Stosur) and [Zhang Shuai](/wiki/Zhang_Shuai). With these two titles, Sabalenka rose to No. 21 in the world.

Sabalenka and Mertens continued to partner together throughout the year, entering eleven events before the year-end championships. Sabalenka did not have much success in the Grand Slam singles events, but she produced much better results in doubles. Sabalenka and Mertens reached the semifinals at the [French Open](/wiki/2019_French_Open), losing to second seeds Babos and Mladenovic. They then reached the quarterfinals at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships), losing to third seeds Hsieh and Strýcová. These were Sabalenka's first two appearances in at least the quarterfinals at a Grand Slam event. Sabalenka and Mertens had their best result of the year at the [US Open](/wiki/2019_US_Open_(tennis)). As the fourth seed at the event, they made it to the final without playing another top-ten-seeded team. In the final, they faced eighth seeds [Victoria Azarenka](/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka) and Ashleigh Barty, the latter of whom had won the title a year earlier with [CoCo Vandeweghe](/wiki/CoCo_Vandeweghe). Sabalenka and Mertens defeated Azarenka and Barty in straight sets for their first Grand Slam title in any discipline. With this title, Sabalenka made her top 10 debut in doubles at No. 6 in the world.

Sabalenka and Mertens made one more final during the year, finishing runner-up at the [Wuhan Open](/wiki/2019_Wuhan_Open), where Sabalenka won the singles title. Their three big titles helped them win the Race to Shenzhen and qualify for the [WTA Finals](/wiki/2019_WTA_Finals) as the top seeds. Before the event, Sabalenka and Mertens moved up to No. 2 and No. 3 in the rankings, respectively, behind only world No. 1, Barbora Strýcová. At the WTA Finals, the pair were placed in a round robin group with third seeds Babos and Mladenovic, fifth seeds [Chan Hao-ching](/wiki/Chan_Hao-ching) and [Latisha Chan](/wiki/Latisha_Chan), as well as eighth seeds [Anna-Lena Grönefeld](/wiki/Anna-Lena_Gr%C3%B6nefeld) and [Demi Schuurs](/wiki/Demi_Schuurs). In their opening match, they were upset by Grönefeld and Schuurs in a match tiebreak. After defeating the Chan sisters, Sabalenka and Mertens lost to Babos and Mladenovic in another match tiebreak. They did not advance out of their group.

### 2020: Three tour titles, top 10

Sabalenka continued her late-season surge by reaching the semifinals in Adelaide. She came back from a final set deficit against Hsieh Su-wei before sliding past Bernarda Pera and shocking second seed Simona Halep, whom she had never won a set against before, in straight sets. She then lost in straight sets to eventual runner-up [Dayana Yastremska](/wiki/Dayana_Yastremska). Despite her excellent start to the season, she was upset by Carla Suárez Navarro in two tie-breakers in the first round of the Australian Open. She enjoyed moderate success in doubles, with Mertens reaching the quarterfinals before losing to the Chan sisters. Her next tournament was the [Dubai Tennis Championships](/wiki/2020_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), where she made the quarterfinals with wins over Maria Sakkari and doubles partner Mertens. There she faced Simona Halep. Despite taking the first set, she could not repeat her earlier upset, falling to the eventual champion in three sets. She rebounded at the [Qatar Open](/wiki/2020_Qatar_Total_Open), reaching the final with wins over [Anett Kontaveit](/wiki/Anett_Kontaveit), Maria Sakkari, Zheng Saisai and Svetlana Kuznetsova. In the final, she defeated Petra Kvitová in straight sets to claim her third Premier-5 title. After tennis resumed in August due to [COVID-19](/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_sports#Tennis), she was the second seed in [Lexington](/wiki/2020_Top_Seed_Open), where she survived [Madison Brengle](/wiki/Madison_Brengle) in three sets but then fell in a three set thriller to 16-year-old [Coco Gauff](/wiki/Coco_Gauff). Her results continued to disappoint as she seeded fifth and crashed out in the second round of Cincinnati and New York to [Jessica Pegula](/wiki/Jessica_Pegula) and a resurgent Victoria Azarenka. She had moderate success in doubles, reaching the quarterfinals of both events. Her results in singles began to improve on clay as she reached the semifinals in Strasbourg and the third round of the [French Open](/wiki/2020_French_Open) losing to Elina Svitolina and [Ons Jabeur](/wiki/Ons_Jabeur), respectively. That was Sabalenka's last loss of the season. In Ostrava, she came from behind in the decider down to avenge her Lexington loss to Gauff and lost the first ten games of her quarterfinal match against [Sara Sorribes Tormo](/wiki/Sara_Sorribes_Tormo) before winning the next twelve to win. In the final, she avenged her US Open loss to Azarenka, defeating her compatriot in straight sets. She then also took the Linz title by beating Elise Mertens in the final. This gave Sabalenka the first year-end top-10 finish in her career.

### 2021: Doubles No. 1, singles No. 2

Sabalenka at the [2021 French Open](/wiki/2021_French_Open)

Sabalenka entered 2021 on a nine-match winning streak and participated in her first tournament at the [Abu Dhabi Open](/wiki/2021_Abu_Dhabi_Women%27s_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as the fourth seed. She defeated [Polona Hercog](/wiki/Polona_Hercog) in straight sets, coming back from a 5–2 deficit in the first set, and then defeated [Ajla Tomljanović](/wiki/Ajla_Tomljanovi%C4%87) and Ons Jabeur to reach the quarterfinals, similarly in straight sets. She beat Elena Rybakina in the quarterfinals, where she dropped her first set of the week before defeating Maria Sakkari in straight sets. In the final, Sabalenka defeated first-time finalist [Veronika Kudermetova](/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova) in straight sets, losing just four games in total. The run to the title at Abu Dhabi extended her winning streak to 15 matches and catapulted her to a new ranking of No. 7.

Sabalenka went into the Australian Open looking to make a Grand Slam singles quarterfinal for the first time in her career. In three sets, she was defeated in the fourth round by 23-time Grand Slam champion [Serena Williams](/wiki/Serena_Williams). Sabalenka won the [doubles competition](/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_doubles) with Elise Mertens. By winning the title, Sabalenka ascended to world No. 1 in the doubles rankings for the first time in her career on 22 February 2021.

As defending champion at the [Qatar Open](/wiki/2021_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) in Doha, and after receiving a first-round bye, Sabalenka was defeated in her first match by eventual finalist [Garbiñe Muguruza](/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza) in three sets. At the [Dubai Tennis Championships](/wiki/2021_Dubai_Tennis_Championships), in her first tournament since becoming No. 1 in doubles, Sabalenka and Mertens received a bye in the first round. They lost their opening match to Jessica Pegula and [Bethanie Mattek-Sands](/wiki/Bethanie_Mattek-Sands). Sabalenka cruised through to the quarterfinals in the singles event, defeating 15th seed Anett Kontaveit along the way, before losing to Muguruza for the second time in as many weeks, again in three sets.

Sabalenka won the title at the [Madrid Open](/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open), facing Ashleigh Barty in the championship match. It was a rematch of the [2021 Porsche Tennis Grand Prix](/wiki/2021_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix) final, two week earlier, as Sabalenka faced her vanquisher, world No. 1 Ashleigh Barty. As a result of her fourth WTA 1000 title, she entered the top 5 rankings in singles at world No. 4.

Sabalenka and compatriot Victoria Azarenka won the doubles event at the [German Open](/wiki/2021_WTA_German_Open), defeating the top-seeded pair of Demi Schuurs and [Nicole Melichar](/wiki/Nicole_Melichar).

Seeded second at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships), Sabalenka reached her first Grand Slam quarterfinal and semifinal, defeating 18th seed Elena Rybakina and 21st seed Ons Jabeur in straight sets, respectively. She then lost her semifinal match against Karolína Plíšková in three sets. As a result of her performance at Wimbledon, she reached a career-high ranking in the world No. 3 in the WTA singles rankings. Sabalenka became the third Belarusian woman to reach the last four of Wimbledon following [Natasha Zvereva](/wiki/Natasha_Zvereva) in 1998 and Victoria Azarenka in 2011 and 2012.

Sabalenka continued her season at the [Canadian Open](/wiki/2021_National_Bank_Open) in Montreal. She reached the semifinals but lost to Karolína Plíšková, in straight sets. She then lost her opening-round match against Paula Badosa at the [Cincinnati Open](/wiki/2021_Western_%26_Southern_Open). Despite the loss, Sabalenka reached a career-high ranking of world No. 2.

At the [US Open](/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka reached her second consecutive (and second overall) major semifinal following wins over [Danielle Collins](/wiki/Danielle_Collins), Elise Mertens, and Barbora Krejčíková. In the semifinal, she lost to [Leylah Fernandez](/wiki/Leylah_Fernandez) in three sets.

Due to a positive COVID-19 test, Sabalenka was not able to play at [Indian Wells](/wiki/2021_BNP_Paribas_Open).

### 2022: WTA Finals runner-up, US Open semifinal

Sabalenka began her season at the [2022 Adelaide International 1](/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_1) as the second seed. After a first-round bye, Sabalenka lost in the second round in straight sets to [Kaja Juvan](/wiki/Kaja_Juvan). Sabalenka served 18 double faults to five aces. Next, Sabalenka received a wildcard to enter the Adelaide International 2 and was seeded top, but lost her first round to a qualifier, world No. 93 [Rebecca Peterson](/wiki/Rebecca_Peterson), in three sets. Once again, Sabalenka struggled with her serve, recording 21 double faults for the match.

She entered the [Australian Open](/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the second seed.
Sabalenka's serving struggles continued, but she managed to dig out three-set wins against world No. 128 [Storm Sanders](/wiki/Storm_Sanders), No. 100 [Wang Xinyu](/wiki/Wang_Xinyu), and No. 41 [Markéta Vondroušová](/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1) to advance to the fourth round for the second time in her career. She faced off against giant-killer [Kaia Kanepi](/wiki/Kaia_Kanepi), who had defeated the 16th seed [Angelique Kerber](/wiki/Angelique_Kerber) in the first round. Kanepi prevailed in a tight three-set match, which ended in a third-set super-tiebreak. Sabalenka managed to save four match points during the match, but also served 15 double faults to Kanepi's four.

She found her form, reaching the quarterfinals at the [Qatar Ladies Open](/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open), beating [Alizé Cornet](/wiki/Aliz%C3%A9_Cornet) and [Jil Teichmann](/wiki/Jil_Teichmann), before losing to the eventual champion [Iga Świątek](/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek) in the semifinals. Following early exits in [Indian Wells](/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), [Miami](/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) and [Charleston](/wiki/2022_Credit_One_Charleston_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), she reached her first final of 2022 at the [Stuttgart Open](/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix), defeating [Bianca Andreescu](/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu), world No. 6 [Anett Kontaveit](/wiki/Anett_Kontaveit) and [Paula Badosa](/wiki/Paula_Badosa), the new world No. 2, eventually losing to world No. 1, Świątek, again. Entering as the defending champion at the [Madrid Open](/wiki/Madrid_Open_(tennis)), Sabalenka was knocked out by Amanda Anisimova in the first round. At the [Italian Open](/wiki/Italian_Open_(tennis)), after defeating [Zhang Shuai](/wiki/Zhang_Shuai), Amanda Anisimova, and Jessica Pegula, she lost to Świątek in the semifinals for the third time in 2022. At the [French Open](/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka lost in the third round to [Camila Giorgi](/wiki/Camila_Giorgi) in three sets.

Sabalenka began the grass-court season at the [Libéma Open](/wiki/2022_Lib%C3%A9ma_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the top seed, where she made the final, losing to [Ekaterina Alexandrova](/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova). She then entered the [German Open](/wiki/2022_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) as the third seed but lost in the first round to Veronika Kudermetova. Due to Wimbledon's [ruling on Russian and Belarusian players](/wiki/Wimbledon_ban_on_Russian_and_Belarusian_players), Sabalenka was banned from participating in the event due to Russia's invasion of Ukraine, cutting her grass season short.

She opened the US hardcourt swing in [Silicon Valley Classic](/wiki/2022_Silicon_Valley_Classic_%E2%80%93_Singles), losing to [Daria Kasatkina](/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina) in the quarterfinals. She then played the [Canadian Open](/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), losing to [Coco Gauff](/wiki/Coco_Gauff) in the third round. Her best result since Stuttgart came at [Cincinnati Open](/wiki/2022_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), where as the sixth seed, she made the semifinals, defeating [Anna Kalinskaya](/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya), [Shelby Rogers](/wiki/Shelby_Rogers), and Zhang Shuai before losing to eventual champion Caroline Garcia in three sets. She then made her best run of the season at the [US Open](/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles). Seeded sixth, she defeated [Catherine Harrison](/wiki/Catherine_Harrison_(tennis)), Kaia Kanepi, [Clara Burel](/wiki/Clara_Burel), 19th seed Danielle Collins, and 22nd seed & former world No. 1 Karolína Plíšková to reach the semifinals, matching her previous best result from 2021. In her second-round match against Kanepi, Sabalenka came back from a set and behind in the second to win in three sets, saving two match points in the process. In the semifinals, she was defeated by world No. 1, Iga Świątek, for the fourth time this season.

At the [San Diego Open](/wiki/2022_San_Diego_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka defeated [Sloane Stephens](/wiki/Sloane_Stephens) in three sets before losing to [Donna Vekić](/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87) in the quarterfinals. In receipt of a first-round bye in [Guadalajara](/wiki/2022_Guadalajara_Open_Akron_%E2%80%93_Singles), she lost to [Liudmila Samsonova](/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova) in the second round. However, she managed to qualify for the [WTA Finals](/wiki/2022_WTA_Finals) for a second straight year. There, she reached the final, defeating world No. 3 Jessica Pegula and No. 2 Ons Jabeur in the round-robin stage, and No. 1 Iga Świątek in the semifinals, but lost to Caroline Garcia in straight sets. She became the fourth woman to defeat the top 3 players at the same tournament, joining [Steffi Graf](/wiki/Steffi_Graf) (1999 French Open), Serena Williams (2002 Miami Open), and [Venus Williams](/wiki/Venus_Williams) (2008 WTA Finals).

### 2023: Australian Open champion, No. 1

See also: [2023 Aryna Sabalenka tennis season](/wiki/2023_Aryna_Sabalenka_tennis_season)

Sabalenka at the [2023 US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_(tennis))

Sabalenka entered [Adelaide 1](/wiki/2023_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the second seed. She reached her first final of the season by defeating [Liudmila Samsonova](/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova), Markéta Vondroušová, [Irina-Camelia Begu](/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu) en route. Then she defeated Czech teenage qualifier [Linda Nosková](/wiki/Linda_Noskov%C3%A1) to win her first title since Madrid 2021 and 11th career title without dropping a single set.

Sabalenka entered the [Australian Open](/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the fifth seed and one of the title contenders. She defeated [Tereza Martincová](/wiki/Tereza_Martincov%C3%A1), Shelby Rogers, former doubles partner and 26th seed Elise Mertens, and the previous week's Adelaide 2 champion and world no. 10 [Belinda Bencic](/wiki/Belinda_Bencic) to reach her first-ever Australian Open quarterfinal. She then beat Donna Vekić to reach her fourth Grand Slam semifinal, and won her tenth consecutive match by beating [Magda Linette](/wiki/Magda_Linette) in the semifinal to make her first ever major final. In the final, she defeated reigning Wimbledon champion [Elena Rybakina](/wiki/Elena_Rybakina) in three sets to win her first major title. She became the second Belarusian to ever win a major singles title, and the first since Victoria Azarenka a decade earlier. After winning the title in Melbourne, Sabalenka rose back to No. 2 in the WTA rankings.

In [Dubai](/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka defeated [Lauren Davis](/wiki/Lauren_Davis) and [Jeļena Ostapenko](/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko) to reach the quarterfinals, where she fell to the eventual champion Barbora Krejčíková in three sets. She then participated in [Indian Wells](/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), where she defeated [Evgeniya Rodina](/wiki/Evgeniya_Rodina) and took revenge on Krejčíková for her last defeat in three sets. Then she beat Coco Gauff and Maria Sakkari to reach her first Indian Wells final, where she lost to Elena Rybakina in a rematch of the 2023 Australian Open final. In [Miami](/wiki/2023_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka defeated [Shelby Rogers](/wiki/Shelby_Rogers), [Marie Bouzková](/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1), and Krejčíková each in straight sets to reach the quarterfinals, where she was defeated by [Sorana Cîrstea](/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea).

Sabalenka started her clay court season in [Stuttgart](/wiki/2023_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles), where she defeated Krejčíková, [Paula Badosa](/wiki/Paula_Badosa), and [Anastasia Potapova](/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) to reach her fourth final of the year. In the final, a rematch of [last year's final](/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles), she was defeated by world No. 1, Iga Świątek, in straight sets for the second consecutive year. In [Madrid](/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka reached her second final at this tournament, defeating Sorana Cîrstea, [Camila Osorio](/wiki/Camila_Osorio), [Mirra Andreeva](/wiki/Mirra_Andreeva), [Mayar Sherif](/wiki/Mayar_Sherif), and Maria Sakkari, dropping just one set en route to the final. In the final, she once again faced Świątek, and won her third title of the year in three sets. In [Rome](/wiki/2023_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka suffered her first early defeat of the year, losing in straight sets to former Grand Slam champion [Sofia Kenin](/wiki/Sofia_Kenin). At the [French Open](/wiki/2023_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), she reached the semifinals without dropping a set, defeating [Marta Kostyuk](/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk), [Iryna Shymanovich](/wiki/Iryna_Shymanovich), [Kamilla Rakhimova](/wiki/Kamilla_Rakhimova), Sloane Stephens, and Elina Svitolina. In the semifinals, she lost to Karolína Muchová in three sets, despite leading 5–2 and holding match point.

Sabalenka began her grass court season in [Berlin](/wiki/2023_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), defeating [Vera Zvonareva](/wiki/Vera_Zvonareva) before losing to Veronika Kudermetova. Sabalenka next competed at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), where she reached her fourth straight major semifinal. Sabalenka defeated [Panna Udvardy](/wiki/Panna_Udvardy), [Varvara Gracheva](/wiki/Varvara_Gracheva), [Anna Blinkova](/wiki/Anna_Blinkova), Ekaterina Alexandrova, and [Madison Keys](/wiki/Madison_Keys), before losing to Ons Jabeur, despite leading by a set and a break.

Her next tournament was [Montréal](/wiki/2023_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), where she defeated [Petra Martić](/wiki/Petra_Marti%C4%87), before losing in three sets to eventual runner-up Liudmila Samsonova. She next reached the semifinals in [Cincinnati](/wiki/2023_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), defeating [Ann Li](/wiki/Ann_Li_(tennis)), [Daria Kasatkina](/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina), and Ons Jabeur, before falling once again in three sets to Karolína Muchová. At the [US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka reached the semifinals by defeating [Maryna Zanevska](/wiki/Maryna_Zanevska), [Jodie Burrage](/wiki/Jodie_Burrage), [Clara Burel](/wiki/Clara_Burel), Daria Kasatkina, and [Zheng Qinwen](/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen) in straight sets, dropping just 21 games in all five matches. Following these wins, Sabalenka became the first player since [Serena Williams](/wiki/Serena_Williams) in [2016](/wiki/2016_WTA_Tour) to reach the semifinals of all four major events in one year. She faced Madison Keys in the semifinals, recovering from a set and break deficit and serving to stay in the match four times in the final two sets. In her second major final of the year, Sabalenka faced Coco Gauff, and lost in three sets. Due to the loss of No. 1 Iga Świątek in the fourth round, Sabalenka became, for the first time, the world No. 1 tennis player at the conclusion of the tournament. Sabalenka became the 29th player to be ranked No. 1 in the world on the WTA Tour, and the second Belarusian, after Victoria Azarenka. She was also just the eighth female player to have been ranked No. 1 in the Open Era in both singles and doubles at some point in their careers.

After the US Open, Sabalenka reached the quarterfinals of the [China Open](/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), losing to Rybakina. At the [WTA Finals](/wiki/2023_WTA_Finals), she defeated Rybakina and Sakkari and lost to Pegula in the group stage. Sabalenka then lost in the semifinals to Świątek, who would go on to win the event. As a result, Świątek reclaimed the world No. 1 ranking and Sabalenka finished as year-end No. 2. In December, Sabalenka was named the ITF World Champion.

### 2024: Two major titles, year-end No. 1

See also: [2024 Aryna Sabalenka tennis season](/wiki/2024_Aryna_Sabalenka_tennis_season)

Sabalenka at the [2024 US Open](/wiki/2024_US_Open_(tennis))

In January, Sabalenka reached the final of the [Brisbane International](/wiki/2024_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), defeating Victoria Azarenka in the semifinals, before losing the championship match to [Elena Rybakina](/wiki/Elena_Rybakina).

Sabalenka won the [Australian Open](/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) without losing a set and became the first woman to successfully defend her title there since Victoria Azarenka in 2013. She defeated 12th seed Zheng Qinwen in the final, having overcome [Ella Seidel](/wiki/Ella_Seidel), [Brenda Fruhvirtová](/wiki/Brenda_Fruhvirtov%C3%A1), 28th seed [Lesia Tsurenko](/wiki/Lesia_Tsurenko), Amanda Anisimova, ninth seed Barbora Krejčíková and fourth seed [Coco Gauff](/wiki/Coco_Gauff) in the earlier rounds.

She reached the quarterfinals at [Stuttgart](/wiki/2024_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix), where she was eliminated by Markéta Vondroušová. As the defending champion in [Madrid](/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), she defeated Magda Linette, [Robin Montgomery](/wiki/Robin_Montgomery), [Danielle Collins](/wiki/Danielle_Collins) and Mirra Andreeva, before edging an epic three-set semifinal victory over world No. 4, Elena Rybakina. In total, she played four three-set matches to reach the final, dropping 60 games in the process, the most to reach the women's singles final in Madrid. In a rematch of the previous year's final, she faced Iga Świątek. Sabalenka had three championship points during the match, but eventually lost. In [Rome](/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), she saved three match points in her fourth-round match against Elina Svitolina and endured a lower-back injury before defeating her opponent in three sets. In the quarterfinal, she earned her third WTA top-10 win of the year by defeating Jeļena Ostapenko. It was followed by a semifinal victory over [Danielle Collins](/wiki/Danielle_Collins), before Sabalenka fell again to Iga Świątek in the final, this time in straight sets.

She entered the [French Open](/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) as the second seed, and defeated Erika Andreeva, [Moyuka Uchijima](/wiki/Moyuka_Uchijima), Paula Badosa and 22nd seed Emma Navarro in straight sets. In the quarterfinal, she was hampered by stomach issues and lost to Mirra Andreeva in three sets. With this loss, she fell in the WTA ranking to No. 3.

Sabalenka suffered a right shoulder injury that forced her to retire during her quarterfinal match against [Anna Kalinskaya](/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya) at the [Berlin Ladies Open](/wiki/2024_Berlin_Ladies_Open) in June and she withdrew from the [2024 Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships), having failed to recover in time to play. She won the [Cincinnati Open](/wiki/2024_Cincinnati_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) in August, with victories over Iga Świątek in the semifinals and [Jessica Pegula](/wiki/Jessica_Pegula) in the final.

In September, Sabalenka won the [US Open](/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), overcoming qualifier [Priscilla Hon](/wiki/Priscilla_Hon), [Lucia Bronzetti](/wiki/Lucia_Bronzetti), 29th seed Ekaterina Alexandrova, former doubles partner Elise Mertens, Olympic champion and seventh seed Zheng Qinwen and 13th seed Emma Navarro, before defeating sixth seed Jessica Pegula in straight sets in the final to claim her third major title.

Sabalenka lifting the 2024 US Open singles trophy

Sabalenka continued her great form at the [China Open](/wiki/2024_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) in Beijing, where she defeated [Mananchaya Sawangkaew](/wiki/Mananchaya_Sawangkaew), [Ashlyn Krueger](/wiki/Ashlyn_Krueger) and Madison Keys all in straight sets to increase her winning streak to 15 matches. Sabalenka lost in the quarterfinals to Karolína Muchová in three sets.

At the [Wuhan Open](/wiki/2024_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), where she won back-to-back in 2018 and 2019, before the tournament was suspended due to COVID-19 from 2020 until 2023, Sabalenka defeated Kateřina Siniaková in straight sets in her opening match. In the next round, she came back from a set down against [Yulia Putintseva](/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva) to advance to the quarterfinals, where she defeated [Magdalena Fręch](/wiki/Magdalena_Fr%C4%99ch). Sabalenka faced fourth seed Coco Gauff in the semifinals, mounting a comeback from a set and a break down to the reach final for the third time. In the final, she defeated fifth seed Zheng Qinwen in three sets to lift her third Wuhan Open title and her second WTA 1000 for the 2024 season.

After 11 months off the top in October, she regained the WTA world No.1 ranking. Following wins against Zheng Qinwen and Jasmine Paolini in her first two group matches at the [WTA Finals](/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles), combined with Świątek's loss to Coco Gauff, Sabalenka for the first time would finish the year as No. 1. Sabalenka lost her last group match to Elena Rybakina and was defeated by Gauff in the semifinals.

In December, Sabalenka was named [WTA Player of the Year](/wiki/WTA_Awards#Player_of_the_Year).

### 2025: US Open title, year-end No. 1

See also: [2025 Aryna Sabalenka tennis season](/wiki/2025_Aryna_Sabalenka_tennis_season)

Sabalenka started 2025 by winning the [Brisbane International](/wiki/2025_Brisbane_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), defeating [Renata Zarazúa](/wiki/Renata_Zaraz%C3%BAa), Yulia Putintseva, [Marie Bouzková](/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1), and Mirra Andreeva to reach the final, where she came back from a set down against [Polina Kudermetova](/wiki/Polina_Kudermetova) to claim the 18th singles title of her career.

Sabalenka started her quest for a three-peat at the [Australian Open](/wiki/Australian_Open) by defeating former world No. 3, Sloane Stephens, in straight sets in the first round. In the second and third rounds, she defeated [Jéssica Bouzas Maneiro](/wiki/J%C3%A9ssica_Bouzas_Maneiro) and Clara Tauson, respectively, both matches ending in straight sets. Sabalenka then continued her journey at the Australian Open by defeating Mirra Andreeva, [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova), and Paula Badosa to advance to the final for the third time in a row. In the final, Sabalenka lost in three sets to Madison Keys, who won her first major title.

At the [Qatar Ladies Open](/wiki/2025_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open) she lost in the first round to [Ekaterina Alexandrova](/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova) in three sets.
At the [Dubai Championships](/wiki/2025_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka defeated Veronika Kudermetova in straight sets. In the round of 16, she faced Clara Tauson, whom she played at the Australian Open earlier in the year but this time Sabalenka lost in straight sets.
After her loss in Dubai, Sabalenka played at [Indian Wells](/wiki/2025_BNP_Paribas_Open) where she defeated [McCartney Kessler](/wiki/McCartney_Kessler) in straight sets. She then defeated Bronzetti and in the fourth round, Sabalenka took on lucky loser [Sonay Kartal](/wiki/Sonay_Kartal) and recorded again a straight set win. She advanced to the semifinals after defeating Liudmila Samsonova. Her next opponent was Madison Keys, who defeated her at the Australian Open earlier in the year, but this time Sabalenka took her revenge to reach the final, where she lost to Mirra Andreeva in three sets.

Sabalenka then played at the [Miami Open](/wiki/2025_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles). In the second round, Sabalenka defeated [Viktoriya Tomova](/wiki/Viktoriya_Tomova) in straight sets to advance to the third round. Next Sabalenka faced [Elena-Gabriela Ruse](/wiki/Elena-Gabriela_Ruse) and won the match as Ruse retired injured. In the fourth round, Sabalenka defeated defending champion Danielle Collins. She continued with straight-set victories over Qinwen Zheng and [Jasmine Paolini](/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini) to reach the final. There, Sabalenka defeated Pegula in straight sets to claim her first Miami Open title.

Sabalenka at a 2025 Miami Open press conference

At the [Stuttgart Open](/wiki/2025_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix) Sabalenka was supposed to face [Anastasia Potapova](/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) but Potapova withdrew after winning her first match. Sabalenka defeated [Elise Mertens](/wiki/Elise_Mertens) in the quarterfinals and fifth seed [Jasmine Paolini](/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini) in the semifinals to reach her fifth final of the season. She lost the championship match for the fourth time at the tournament to unseeded [Jeļena Ostapenko](/wiki/Je%C4%BCena_Ostapenko).

In the second round of the [Mutua Madrid Open](/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), Sabalenka defeated [Anna Blinkova](/wiki/Anna_Blinkova) in straight sets. She came back from one set down against Elise Mertens to advance to the fourth round. Following straight sets victories over [Peyton Stearns](/wiki/Peyton_Stearns) and [Marta Kostyuk](/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk) she advanced to the semifinals, where she defeated [Elina Svitolina](/wiki/Elina_Svitolina) to reach her fourth Madrid final. With her win over [Coco Gauff](/wiki/Coco_Gauff) in the final, Sabalenka claimed her third Madrid Open title and became the third woman to surpass 11,000 points in the WTA ranking.

In [Rome](/wiki/2025_Italian_Open), Sabalenka won her second-round match against [Anastasia Potapova](/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) in straight sets. In the third round against [Sofia Kenin](/wiki/Sofia_Kenin) she came back from a set down to win. In the fourth round she faced [Marta Kostyuk](/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk), whom she had played earlier in Madrid; Sabalenka defeated Kostyuk in straight sets to advance to the quarterfinal, where she lost to Zheng Qinwen.

At the [French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open), Sabalenka defeated in straight sets [Kamilla Rakhimova](/wiki/Kamilla_Rakhimova), [Jil Teichmann](/wiki/Jil_Teichmann), [Olga Danilović](/wiki/Olga_Danilovi%C4%87) and [Amanda Anisimova](/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova) to reach the quarterfinals where she faced Zheng Qinwen and avenged her Italian Open loss to this player. On 28 December 2025, Sabalenka (women's singles world No. 1) and [Nick Kyrgios](/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios) (men's singles world No. 671) contested an exhibition match promoted as the *[Battle of the Sexes](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Sexes_(tennis))* at the [Coca-Cola Arena](/wiki/Coca-Cola_Arena) in [Dubai](/wiki/Dubai). ## National representation

### Fed Cup

#### Early appearances

Sabalenka represented Belarus at the Junior Fed Cup in 2014, with the team finishing in sixth place. She then made her senior [Fed Cup](/wiki/Fed_Cup) debut for [Belarus](/wiki/Belarus_Fed_Cup_team) in April 2016, losing a [dead rubber](/wiki/Dead_rubber) doubles match against [Russia](/wiki/Russia_Fed_Cup_team). Nonetheless, the Belarusian team led by [Victoria Azarenka](/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka) and [Aliaksandra Sasnovich](/wiki/Aliaksandra_Sasnovich) won the tie to qualify for the top-tier World Group the following season for the first time in their history.

#### 2017: Surprise runner-up in World Group debut

The Belarus Fed Cup team made their debut in the World Group and ultimately reached the final, despite being the underdogs in all three ties. Little was expected from the team because they were without their veteran leader Azarenka, who missed the first two ties on maternity leave and the last because of a custody battle. Without her, Belarus was led by Sabalenka and Sasnovich, neither of whom had ever been ranked above No. 76 by the time of the final. However, they did have the advantage of playing all of their ties at home in Minsk.

The ties in the quarterfinals against the [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_Fed_Cup_team) in February and the semi-finals against [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland_Fed_Cup_team) in April both played out in the same way. While Sabalenka lost her opening matches to their opponents' respective top-ranked players of [Kiki Bertens](/wiki/Kiki_Bertens) and [Timea Bacsinszky](/wiki/Timea_Bacsinszky), Sasnovich was able to give Belarus a 2–1 lead in each instance. Sabalenka then clinched both ties, with wins over [Michaëlla Krajicek](/wiki/Micha%C3%ABlla_Krajicek) and No. 54 [Viktorija Golubic](/wiki/Viktorija_Golubic), respectively. She was only ranked No. 125 at the time of the semifinal, with no career tour match wins outside of Fed Cup.

> "I've never felt so much emotion in a match. When you play at home and you are down 0–1 and you have to win and you fight with yourself... I just started crying because it was such an important match."

— Sabalenka on her Fed Cup rubber win over Stephens

On the opening day of the final against the [United States](/wiki/United_States_Fed_Cup_team), Sabalenka upset the reigning US Open champion and world No. 13, [Sloane Stephens](/wiki/Sloane_Stephens), to level the tie after Sasnovich lost her first rubber to No. 10, [CoCo Vandeweghe](/wiki/CoCo_Vandeweghe). The next day began with Sabalenka losing to Vandeweghe, before Sasnovich again levelled the tie by defeating Stephens. Sabalenka and Sasnovich were then selected for the decisive doubles rubber for the Fed Cup crown, but the duo were comprehensively defeated by Vandeweghe and [Shelby Rogers](/wiki/Shelby_Rogers).

Despite finishing as runner-up, Belarus's Fed Cup success helped popularize women's tennis in Belarus, and vaulted Sabalenka and Sasnovich into international prominence. Sasnovich said, "When we played the quarterfinals and semi-finals in Minsk, a lot of people were coming to see our matches. They finally saw tennis in life, and it's like a popularization... I want my country to improve even more in tennis, because I think we can have even more from Belarus."

#### 2018–19: Avoiding demotion, another semifinal

Belarus was unable to repeat their 2017 Fed Cup success in 2018. Their quarterfinal tie was held in Minsk against [Germany](/wiki/Germany_Fed_Cup_team). Although Sabalenka won both of her singles rubbers, Sasnovich and [Vera Lapko](/wiki/Vera_Lapko) lost each of theirs to set up a decisive doubles rubber. Sabalenka and doubles specialist [Lidziya Marozava](/wiki/Lidziya_Marozava) were selected for the match, with Sabalenka playing on short rest directly after her last singles match. After taking the first set against [Anna-Lena Grönefeld](/wiki/Anna-Lena_Gr%C3%B6nefeld) and [Tatjana Maria](/wiki/Tatjana_Maria), they ultimately lost the rubber and the tie.

Their next tie was again contested in Minsk as part of the [World Group play-offs](/wiki/2018_Fed_Cup_World_Group_play-offs), with [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovakia_Fed_Cup_team) competing to take Belarus's place in the World Group the following season. Sabalenka and Sasnovich each split their two singles rubbers, with Sabalenka being upset by [Viktória Kužmová](/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Ku%C5%BEmov%C3%A1). Doubles specialists Lapko and Marozava were chosen for the final rubber and the pair won the match to keep Belarus in the World Group for 2019.

In the [2019 Fed Cup](/wiki/2019_Fed_Cup), Belarus were drawn against Germany in the quarterfinals for the second consecutive year. After Sasnovich won the opening rubber against Maria, Sabalenka won both of her singles rubbers against [Andrea Petkovic](/wiki/Andrea_Petkovic) and [Laura Siegemund](/wiki/Laura_Siegemund) to clinch the tie. They advanced to face [Australia](/wiki/Australia_Fed_Cup_team) in the semi-finals. Only two players from each team participated: Sabalenka and Azarenka for Belarus, and Ashleigh Barty and [Samantha Stosur](/wiki/Samantha_Stosur) for Australia. Both Sabalenka and Azarenka defeated Stosur, but lost to Barty. In the decisive doubles rubber, Barty and Stosur won in three sets to eliminate Belarus.

## Rivalries

### Elena Rybakina

Main article: [Rybakina–Sabalenka rivalry](/wiki/Rybakina%E2%80%93Sabalenka_rivalry)

Sabalenka and [Elena Rybakina](/wiki/Elena_Rybakina) have played each other 17 times since 2019, with Sabalenka leading their head-to-head 10–7 whereas Rybakina leads the finals 4–2. Both players are known for their aggressive baseline play, resulting in fast-paced and intense matches.

They have faced off in two major finals with the [2023 Australian Open](/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_-_Women%27s_singles) won by Sabalenka, and the [2026 Australian Open](/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_-_Women%27s_singles) won by Rybakina.

Their other notable encounters include the [2023 BNP Paribas Open](/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) finals,  [2024 Madrid Open](/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) semifinal, [2025 WTA Finals](/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) finals, and the [2026 BNP Paribas Open](/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final.

### Iga Świątek

Sabalenka and [Iga Świątek](/wiki/Iga_%C5%9Awi%C4%85tek) have met 13 times since 2021, with Świątek in control of the head-to-head at 8–5. Their rivalry has been seen as becoming one of the greatest in women's tennis. Their most praised matches include the [2024 Madrid Open](/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final, where Świątek defeated defending champion Sabalenka in three sets in 3 hours and 11 minutes, saving three championship points in the process, and the [2023 Madrid Open](/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final, where Sabalenka won in three sets in 2 hours and 25 minutes against Świątek to claim her second Madrid title. Sabalenka and Świątek played each other at the [2025 French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), which Sabalenka won in three sets.

### Coco Gauff

Sabalenka and [Coco Gauff](/wiki/Coco_Gauff) have met 13 times since 2020, with Sabalenka edging the head-to-head at 7–6. They have played each other multiple times at [majors](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)), the first being the [2023 US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final. Sabalenka lost that match despite winning the first set. Sabalenka defeated Gauff at the [2024 Australian Open](/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) semifinals on her way to defending the title. Sabalenka and Gauff faced off again in the final of the [2025 French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open), with Gauff overtaking Sabalenka for her first French Open Major Championship win and second Grand Slam overall. Sabalenka would avenge that loss by knocking Gauff out of the [2025 WTA Finals](/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) where Gauff was the defending champion. Sabalenka would meet Gauff for the first time in 2026 in the [Miami](/wiki/2026_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) finals, where Sabalenka battled through Gauff in three sets to win Miami for the second time, as well as the [Sunshine Double](/wiki/Sunshine_Double).

### Jessica Pegula

Sabalenka has developed a fairly storied rivalry with [Jessica Pegula](/wiki/Jessica_Pegula). Their consistency on hard courts leads to them frequently facing each other in the latter stages of events. The two have met 12 times since 2020, with Sabalenka controlling the head-to-head 9–3. Pegula would win their first meeting in the third round of the [2020 Western & Southern Open](/wiki/2020_Western_%26_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) in three sets. They would face off at the [2024 US Open](/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final in which Sabalenka would win 7–5, 7–5 to earn her first US Open title. The two would have a rematch of their US Open final in the [2025 US Open](/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) semifinals, where Sabalenka would win a close three-set match 4–6, 6–3, 6–4 on her way to defending her US Open title. Pegula would snap Sabalenka’s 20-match winning streak as well as her 2025 tiebreak record in their following match in [Wuhan](/wiki/2025_Wuhan_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles), before pulling out a three-set win 2–6, 6–4, 7–6(2). They would face each other once more in the Riyadh [2025 WTA Finals](/wiki/2025_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles), where Sabalenka defeated Pegula in three sets.

### Jasmine Paolini

Sabalenka has developed a recurring rivalry with [Jasmine Paolini](/wiki/Jasmine_Paolini), which she leads 6–2. ## Playing style

Sabalenka serving at the [2019 Sydney International](/wiki/2019_Sydney_International_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles)

Sabalenka is an [aggressive baseliner](/wiki/Aggressive_baseliner) whose game is characterised by a powerful [serve](/wiki/Serve_(tennis)) and equally powerful [groundstrokes](/wiki/Groundstroke). Her game is based around taking control of points, with a knack for turning defensive positions into offensive opportunities. Due to her aggression, she usually hits a high number of both winners and unforced errors off her groundstrokes, which are hit with relentless pace and depth. At the [2024 US Open](/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), she struck her forehand at an average speed of 129 km/h (80 mph). Sabalenka's big serve is central to her game: in 2024 she won 62.7% of points on her serve, ranking fifth among the players on tour, and won 78.5% of her service games.

Sabalenka's improved consistency on serve was the key which allowed her to ascend to the top of the WTA rankings. In the past, she struggled with an inconsistent serve, leading to a high double fault count; she served 166 double faults in 2020, the most of any player. She notably suffered from the [yips](/wiki/Yips) from the [2021 WTA Finals](/wiki/2021_WTA_Finals) and into 2022, serving 152 double faults in 11 matches, an average of 14 double faults a match. Following the [2022 Canadian Open](/wiki/2022_Canadian_Open_(tennis)), she hired biomechanics expert Gavin Macmillan to revamp her serve. Sabalenka served a total of 440 double faults in 2022, but her serve quickly saw improvements in 2023 under Macmillan's guidance, and has since become a reliable weapon.

As Sabalenka's game has matured, she has also developed better shot selection and more variety. When she first arrived on tour, she was sometimes described as a player who hit everything as hard as she could. In the summer of 2018, her coach [Dmitry Tursunov](/wiki/Dmitry_Tursunov) said her game was already evolving, and, "The major thing is she stopped trying [to] hit a winner with every shot." Sabalenka has become increasingly willing to experiment with variety on her groundstrokes, and with finishing more points at the net. During the 2024 clay season, she began to incorporate more [drop shots](/wiki/Drop_shot) into her game, which pair well with her power from the baseline.

Sabalenka is primarily known for her success on [hardcourt](/wiki/Hardcourt). Nineteen of her twenty-two singles titles, including all four of her major titles, have been won on hardcourt, which suits her game due to its consistent bounce and fast-paced play. However, she is also recognised as a consistent contender in the late stages of [grass](/wiki/Grass_court) and [clay court](/wiki/Clay_court) tournaments. She has been particularly successful on faster clay courts, such as the [Madrid Open](/wiki/Madrid_Open_(tennis)), where she won the title in [2021](/wiki/2021_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), [2023](/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles), and [2025](/wiki/2025_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles). She also reached her first major final on clay at the [French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) in 2025.

Sabalenka has become increasingly notable for her ability to win tiebreaks. In 2025, she set an [Open Era](/wiki/Open_Era) record for most tiebreaks won by a woman in a single season, winning 22 out of 25 played. This included a streak of nineteen consecutive tiebreaks won between February and October. In 2026, Sabalenka won her twentieth consecutive tiebreak. While Sabalenka is known for her clutch factor in tiebreaks, she has also lost a large number of closely-contested prominent matches, such as the [2025 French Open](/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final and [2026 Australian Open](/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles) final, and holds a 22–20 (52%) record in singles finals overall.

Sabalenka frequently accompanies her shots with loud [grunting](/wiki/Grunting_in_tennis). She said, "Honestly, I don't even hear myself when I am playing." She expressed her hope that her grunting does not disturb her opponents. At the [Australian Open](/wiki/Australian_Open) in 2018, the crowd mocked her habit in a match against Ashleigh Barty. She is an expressive personality on court.

## Coaches

Sabalenka had worked with Khalil Ibrahimov for two years up until early 2018. At this point, she began working with Swedish former professional tennis players [Magnus Norman](/wiki/Magnus_Norman) and [Magnus Tideman](/wiki/Magnus_Tideman). [Dmitry Tursunov](/wiki/Dmitry_Tursunov) became her primary coach in time for the grass court season in 2018. Sabalenka briefly split with Tursunov after the 2019 US Open. Although they reunited later in the year, she made the split permanent at the end of the season. Sabalenka briefly worked with [Dieter Kindlmann](/wiki/Dieter_Kindlmann) before switching coaches to her longtime hitting partner and compatriot [Anton Dubrov](/wiki/Anton_Dubrov), aged 25 at the time. In 2025, Belarusian former professional tennis player and 10-time [major](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments) doubles champion [Max Mirnyi](/wiki/Max_Mirnyi) joined Sabalenka's coaching team as a consultant.

As of 2025, her coaching team includes Dubrov; Mirnyi; fitness trainer Jason Stacy, a martial arts expert; and hitting partner [Andrei Vasilevski](/wiki/Andrei_Vasilevski_(tennis)).

## Sponsorships

Sabalenka sponsored by [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) for sportswear and [Wilson](/wiki/Wilson_Sporting_Goods) for racquets

Sabalenka has been endorsed by [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) for apparel and shoes since the beginning of her professional career. Nike began to design custom garments for Sabalenka starting from the [2023 US Open](/wiki/2023_US_Open_(tennis)). She is also endorsed by [Wilson](/wiki/Wilson_Sporting_Goods), specifically using the Wilson Blade range of racquets.

In June 2025, Sabalenka became a global ambassador and minority shareholder of IM8, a health‑supplement brand co‑founded by former footballer [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham).

## Personal life

Sabalenka has a tiger tattoo on her left arm that has earned her the nickname "The Tiger", which she has used to refer to herself. She studied at the [Belarusian State University](/wiki/Belarusian_State_University) in a sports-related program. Her tennis idols growing up were [Serena Williams](/wiki/Serena_Williams) and [Maria Sharapova](/wiki/Maria_Sharapova). Her father Sergey, a former ice hockey player, died suddenly in 2019 at age 43. He had [meningitis](/wiki/Meningitis).

Sabalenka's ex-boyfriend, [Konstantin Koltsov](/wiki/Konstantin_Koltsov), died of an apparent suicide in March 2024 at age 42. She became engaged to Brazilian entrepreneur Georgios Frangulis on March 3, 2026.

### Political views

In August 2020, during the [2020–2021 Belarusian protests](/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_Belarusian_protests), Sabalenka criticised the government of [Alexander Lukashenko](/wiki/Alexander_Lukashenko) for its dispersal of peaceful protests and called for no violence, but was criticised by members of the [Belarusian opposition](/wiki/Belarusian_opposition) for not using her platform to advocate the protest movement's goals. She separately praised the Belarusian government for "everything that is done for the country in general and for sports in particular". In 2020, during widespread protests after the disputed election, Sabalenka signed an open letter that said that sport should remain outside of politics. Belarusian media considered the letter supportive of Lukashenko, as it followed an open letter by Belarusian sporting figures demanding the election be invalidated.

Following the start of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Sabalenka expressed support for the Ukrainian people and the use of Ukraine-coloured ribbons: "I feel like people need our support. I just hope they understand that we're all really concerned. I think even 'sad' isn't even the right word." In January 2023, with regard to the war and the banning of Russian and Belarusian players from the [2022 Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships), Sabalenka said, "I just understand that it's not my fault." Her comments on the Russian invasion of Ukraine and the situation of Ukrainian tennis players were criticised by [Lesia Tsurenko](/wiki/Lesia_Tsurenko)'s coach Nikita Vlasov and former Ukrainian player [Sergiy Stakhovsky](/wiki/Sergiy_Stakhovsky) for lacking substance and compassion. Sabalenka later indicated support for ending the war in Ukraine, saying: "If I could stop the war I would do it but unfortunately that doesn't lie in my hands. I just hope for peace."

During a press conference at the [2023 French Open](/wiki/2023_French_Open), when asked about the war by Ukrainian journalist [Daria Meshcheriakova](/wiki/Daria_Meshcheriakova), she said: "Nobody in this world, Russian athletes or Belarusian athletes, support the war. Nobody. How can we support the war? Nobody, normal people will never support it." She later said: "I don't want my country to be in any conflict, I don't support war, meaning I don't support Lukashenko right now."

### Television and film

Sabalenka appears in the tennis docuseries *[Break Point](/wiki/Break_Point_(2023_TV_series))*, which premiered on Netflix on 13 January 2023.

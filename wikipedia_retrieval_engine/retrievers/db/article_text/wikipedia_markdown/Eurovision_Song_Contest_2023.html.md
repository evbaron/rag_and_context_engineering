The **Eurovision Song Contest 2023** was the 67th edition of the [Eurovision Song Contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest). It consisted of two semi-finals on 9 and 11 May and a final on 13 May 2023, held at [M&S Bank Arena Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_Arena) in [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool), United Kingdom, and presented by [Alesha Dixon](/wiki/Alesha_Dixon), [Hannah Waddingham](/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham), and [Julia Sanina](/wiki/Julia_Sanina), with [Graham Norton](/wiki/Graham_Norton) joining for the final. It was organised by the [European Broadcasting Union](/wiki/European_Broadcasting_Union) (EBU) and host broadcaster the [British Broadcasting Corporation](/wiki/BBC) (BBC), which staged the event on behalf of the [Public Broadcasting Company of Ukraine](/wiki/Suspilne) (UA:PBC), which had won the [2022 contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022) for [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022) with the song "[Stefania](/wiki/Stefania_(song))" by [Kalush Orchestra](/wiki/Kalush_Orchestra) but was unable to stage the event due to the [Russian invasion of the country](/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_(2022%E2%80%93present)).

Broadcasters from thirty-seven countries participated in the contest, three fewer than in 2022. [Bulgaria](/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), [Montenegro](/wiki/Montenegro_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), and [North Macedonia](/wiki/North_Macedonia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) opted not to participate, primarily due to the economic impact of the [2021–2023 global energy crisis](/wiki/Global_energy_crisis_(2021%E2%80%932023)).

The winner was [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) with the song "[Tattoo](/wiki/Tattoo_(Loreen_song))", performed by [Loreen](/wiki/Loreen) and written by her with [Jimmy Thörnfeldt](/wiki/Jimmy_Th%C3%B6rnfeldt), [Jimmy Jansson](/wiki/Jimmy_Jansson), [Moa Carlebecker](/wiki/Cazzi_Opeia), [Peter Boström](/wiki/Peter_Bostr%C3%B6m), and [Thomas G:son](/wiki/Thomas_G:son). [Finland](/wiki/Finland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), [Israel](/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), [Italy](/wiki/Italy_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), and [Norway](/wiki/Norway_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) completed the top five. Sweden won the combined vote and jury vote, and finished second to Finland in the televote. Loreen became the second performer to win the contest twice, after Irish singer [Johnny Logan](/wiki/Johnny_Logan_(singer)); it was also the seventh win for Sweden, tying [Ireland](/wiki/Ireland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest)'s record for the most Eurovision victories.

The EBU reported that the contest had a television audience of 162 million viewers in 38 European markets, an increase of a million viewers from the previous edition. A total of 15.6 million viewers watched the contest online on [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) and [TikTok](/wiki/TikTok). The broadcast of the contest won the [British Academy Television Award for Best Live Event](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Live_Event), and Waddingham received a [British Academy Television Award for Best Entertainment Performance](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Entertainment_Performance) nomination for her role as a co-presenter.

## Location

[M&S Bank Arena Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_Arena) – host venue of the 2023 contest

[St George's Hall](/wiki/St_George%27s_Hall,_Liverpool) – host venue for the allocation draw and the opening ceremony of the 2023 contest

Location of host venue (red) and other contest-related sites and events (blue)

The 2023 contest was held in [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool), United Kingdom. It was the ninth time that the United Kingdom had hosted the contest, having previously done so in [1960](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1960), [1963](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1963), [1968](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1968), [1972](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1972), [1974](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1974), [1977](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1977), [1982](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1982), and [1998](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1998). The selected venue was the 11,000-seat [M&S Bank Arena Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_Arena) a multi-purpose indoor arena located in the [ACC Liverpool](/wiki/ACC_Liverpool) complex. The "Turquoise Carpet" event, where the contestants and their delegations were presented before accredited press and fans, took place outside the [Walker Art Gallery](/wiki/Walker_Art_Gallery) on 7 May 2023, followed by the Opening Ceremony at [St George's Hall](/wiki/St_George%27s_Hall,_Liverpool).

In conjunction with the contest, Liverpool held a cultural festival called "EuroFest", which featured collaborations between British and Ukrainian artists. The [Pier Head](/wiki/Pier_Head) was the location of the Eurovision Village, where a stage hosted performances by Ukrainian artists, local artists, current and previous Eurovision entrants, and other groups. It also held screenings of the three live shows. Entry to the Village was free of charge except during the final. The EuroClub, which took place at Camp and Furnace, hosted the official after-parties and private performances by contest participants.

### Host country selection

Further information: [Russia–Ukraine relations in the Eurovision Song Contest § 2023 contest](/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Ukraine_relations_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest#2023_contest)

The [2022 contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022) was won by [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022) with the song "[Stefania](/wiki/Stefania_(song))" by [Kalush Orchestra](/wiki/Kalush_Orchestra), which, according to Eurovision tradition, made Ukraine the presumptive host of the 2023 contest. The country had hosted the contest twice before, in [2005](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005) and [2017](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2017), both times in [Kyiv](/wiki/Kyiv). Between May and June 2022, the Ukrainian government and [UA:PBC](/wiki/Suspilne), the nation's public broadcaster, discussed hosting the contest with the EBU. The chairman of UA:PBC, Mykola Chernotytskyi, Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelenskyy), and other Ukrainian politicians expressed their willingness to host the event, and an organising committee was formed.

Despite this, the EBU announced on 17 June 2022 that the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) meant that UA:PBC could not give the security and operations guarantees required to host the contest, and that the event could therefore not be held in Ukraine. The EBU then entered discussions with the [BBC](/wiki/BBC), the 2022 runner-up, and on 25 July announced that the 2023 contest would be hosted in the United Kingdom. It was the first time since [1980](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1980) that the contest was not hosted by the previous edition's winning country.

The decision not to host in Ukraine was not taken lightly and was initially met with disappointment. UA:PBC published a statement in which Chernotytskyi requested further talks with the EBU, and [Oleh Psiuk](/wiki/Oleh_Psiuk) of Kalush Orchestra published an open letter criticising the decision, co-signed by Ukraine's previous Eurovision winners, [Ruslana](/wiki/Ruslana) and [Jamala](/wiki/Jamala), as well as Ukraine's minister of culture [Oleksandr Tkachenko](/wiki/Oleksandr_Tkachenko_(journalist)). This stance was supported by [Boris Johnson](/wiki/Boris_Johnson), who was the British prime minister at the time, [Nadine Dorries](/wiki/Nadine_Dorries), who was the British culture secretary at the time, the Polish broadcaster [Telewizja Polska](/wiki/Telewizja_Polska), and Poland's deputy prime minister and minister of culture [Piotr Gliński](/wiki/Piotr_Gli%C5%84ski). The announcement on 25 July that the BBC would host the contest was supported by UA:PBC.

The host city bidding process ran from 25 July to 7 October 2022, with candidates judged against a set of criteria to demonstrate that they could host an event on the scale of the Eurovision Song Contest. During the first stage of the process, the BBC received expressions of interest from 20 UK cities and towns, seven of which were longlisted on 12 August 2022: [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), [Glasgow](/wiki/Glasgow), [Leeds](/wiki/Leeds), [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool), [Manchester](/wiki/Manchester), [Newcastle](/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne), and [Sheffield](/wiki/Sheffield). These cities had until 8 September to develop their bids in detail for evaluation by the BBC, which also conducted visits to the cities throughout the month. On 27 September, Glasgow and Liverpool were announced to have made the shortlist, and on 7 October, the EBU and the BBC announced Liverpool as the host city.

Eligibility for potential participation in the Eurovision Song Contest requires a national broadcaster with [active EBU membership](/wiki/European_Broadcasting_Union#Members) capable of receiving the contest via the [Eurovision network](/wiki/Eurovision_(network)) and broadcasting it live nationwide. The EBU issued an invitation to participate in the contest to all active members. Associate member [Australia](/wiki/Australia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) did not need an invitation for the 2023 contest, as it had previously been granted permission to participate until at least this year.

On 20 October 2022, the EBU announced that 37 countries would participate in the 2023 contest – the lowest number of participating countries in a single edition since [2014](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014) – with [Bulgaria](/wiki/Bulgaria_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), [Montenegro](/wiki/Montenegro_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) and [North Macedonia](/wiki/North_Macedonia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), which had participated in the [2022 contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022), opting not to participate in 2023 for financial reasons. This was also the first contest where the [Czech Republic](/wiki/Czech_Republic_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) participated under [its shortened English name of Czechia](/wiki/Name_of_the_Czech_Republic).

The contest featured four representatives who also previously performed as lead vocalists for the same country. Two of them had competed in [2012](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2012): [Loreen](/wiki/Loreen) won that year's contest representing [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2012), while [Pasha Parfeni](/wiki/Pasha_Parfeni) represented [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2012) that year and later provided backing vocals for [Moldova in 2013](/wiki/Moldova_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013). Also returning as lead artists were [Marco Mengoni](/wiki/Marco_Mengoni), who had represented [Italy in 2013](/wiki/Italy_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013), and [Monika Linkytė](/wiki/Monika_Linkyt%C4%97), who had represented [Lithuania in 2015](/wiki/Lithuania_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015) alongside [Vaidas Baumila](/wiki/Vaidas_Baumila). In addition, [Gustaph](/wiki/Gustaph_(singer)) had previously provided backing vocals for [Belgium in 2018](/wiki/Belgium_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018) and [in 2021](/wiki/Belgium_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021), and [Iru](/wiki/Iru_Khechanovi) had won Junior Eurovision for [Georgia in 2011](/wiki/Georgia_in_the_Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2011) as a member of [Candy](/wiki/Candy_(Georgian_band)).

### Other countries

Several EBU member broadcasters made statements confirming non-participation prior to the publication of the official 2023 participants list. The management board of Bulgarian broadcaster [BNT](/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Television), at a meeting on 7 September 2022, decided not to participate in the 2023 contest, citing an expected increase in participation fees; this was later publicly confirmed in several Bulgarian news outlets on 19 October. The Montenegrin broadcaster [RTCG](/wiki/Radio_and_Television_of_Montenegro) and the Macedonian broadcaster [MRT](/wiki/Macedonian_Radio_Television) also publicly confirmed on 13 and 14 October 2022 respectively that they would not participate in the contest, citing financial contraints. Both RTCG and MRT however confirmed their intentions to broadcast the 2023 contest. Active EBU member broadcasters in [Andorra](/wiki/Andorra_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), [Luxembourg](/wiki/Luxembourg_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) and [Slovakia](/wiki/Slovakia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) also confirmed non-participation prior to the announcement of the participants list by the EBU.

A potential return for [Monaco](/wiki/Monaco_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) to the contest in 2023 – in what would have been its first participation since [2006](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006) – was first discussed in November 2021, when it was reported that part of the Monégasque state budget had been reserved for participation in the 2023 contest. However, these plans were curtailed due to the delay in the launch of a new Monégasque public television channel, [TVMonaco](/wiki/TVMonaco), which commenced broadcasts in September 2023 instead of the initially outlined period of late 2022. [Monaco Media Diffusion](/wiki/Monaco_Media_Diffusion), the current EBU member broadcaster for Monaco, subsequently confirmed on 5 September 2022 that the country would not participate in the 2023 event.

Discussions were also reported between the EBU and Kazakh broadcaster [Khabar Agency](/wiki/Khabar_Agency), an associate member of the EBU, which would have led to Kazakhstan being invited to participate in the contest for the first time. Kazakhstan has [participated](/wiki/Kazakhstan_in_the_Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest) in the [Junior Eurovision Song Contest](/wiki/Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest) since [2018](/wiki/Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018), with television producer Zhan Mukanov stating that "there is every chance [for Kazakhstan] to enter the adult Eurovision next year" and that the country's participation in the [2022 Junior contest](/wiki/Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022) would have a "significant impact" on its chances of debuting in the adult event. The country, however, did not appear on the final list of participants.

## Production

Exterior of the Liverpool Arena during the Eurovision event weeks

The Eurovision Song Contest 2023 was produced by the British national broadcaster [British Broadcasting Corporation](/wiki/BBC) (BBC). The Ukrainian public broadcaster [UA:PBC](/wiki/Suspilne) worked with the BBC to develop and implement Ukrainian elements for the live shows, including theme artwork, background music, selection of presenters, and opening and interval acts. The three shows were produced by BBC Studios Entertainment Productions and BBC Studios Music Productions, part of the BBC's commercial subsidiary [BBC Studios](/wiki/BBC_Studios).

The senior production team consisted of [Martin Green](/wiki/Martin_Green_(producer)) as managing director, Rachel Ashdown as lead commissioner, Andrew Cartmell as executive producer, Lee Smithurst as head of show, Twan van de Nieuwenhuijzen as head of contest, and James O'Brien as executive in charge of production. Additional production personnel included multi-camera directors Nikki Parsons, Richard Valentine and Ollie Bartlett, lead creative director Dan Shipton, music director Kojo Samuel, stage designer Julio Himede, head of sound Robert Edwards, and lighting designer Tim Routledge. The Ukrainian consultation team was led by Oksana Skybinska, Tetiana Semenova, and Herman Nenov. Background music for the shows was composed by Mykhailo Nekrasov.

The budget was contributed to by [Liverpool City Council](/wiki/Liverpool_City_Council) and the [Liverpool City Region Combined Authority](/wiki/Liverpool_City_Region_Combined_Authority) ([£](/wiki/Pound_sterling)2 million each), the [British government](/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom) (£10 million), and the BBC (£8 million to £17 million). The overall budget was not made public, but was estimated to be at around £24.7 million ([€](/wiki/Euro)28.3 million), including expenditures by the host city.

### Visual design

The graphic design of the 2023 contest on display in Liverpool

On 7 October 2022, along with the host city announcement, the EBU revealed the generic logo for the 2023 contest. The Eurovision heart, which typically has the flag of the host country placed in its centre, contained the [Ukrainian flag](/wiki/Flag_of_Ukraine) for this year to reflect the country's win the previous year. The 'Song Contest' text was accompanied below by 'United Kingdom' and further down by 'Liverpool 2023'.

The theme art and slogan for the contest, "United by Music", was unveiled on 31 January 2023. Designed by London-based brand consultancy [Superunion](/wiki/Superunion) and Ukrainian production company [Starlight Media](/wiki/Starlight_Media), the artwork was built around a string of two-dimensional hearts resembling an [electrocardiogram](/wiki/Electrocardiography), representing response to rhythm and sound, while the colours were inspired by those of the Ukrainian and [British](/wiki/Union_Jack) flags. The typeface, Penny Lane, was inspired by 20th-century Liverpool street signs and the city's musical heritage.

### Stage design

The stage in the arena

The stage design for the 2023 contest was revealed on 2 February 2023. Designed by New York-based set designer Julio Himede, the design was based on "the principles of togetherness, celebration and community", taking inspiration from a wide [hug](/wiki/Hug) and the "cultural aspects and similarities between Ukraine, the UK and specifically Liverpool". The stage was 450 m2 (4,800 sq ft), with 220 m2 (2,400 sq ft) of independently rotating LED screens, over 700 LED floor tiles and more than 1,500 metres (4,900 ft) of LED lights. [King Charles III](/wiki/Charles_III) and [Queen Camilla](/wiki/Queen_Camilla) (whose [coronations](/wiki/Coronation_of_Charles_III_and_Camilla) were held the week before the contest) inaugurated the stage on 26 April, during an official visit to Liverpool.

### Postcards

The "postcards" were 40-second video introductions shown on television whilst the stage is being prepared for the next entry. Filmed between February and April 2023 and directed by Tom Cook, with Carlo Massarella and Jane McGoldrick serving as executive producers, the postcards were based on the "United by Music" theme of the contest. Making use of 360° drone technology, each postcard began in a selected location in Ukraine, then one in the United Kingdom, before moving to the artist's country of origin, where the artist took part in an activity of their choice. The three locations appearing in each postcard were connected by a singular theme. Each postcard was bookended with the "[little planet effect](/wiki/Stereographic_projection#Photography)", which symbolised the interconnections between people. The postcards were produced by London-based production company [Windfall Films](/wiki/Argonon#Windfall_Films) and Ukrainian production company 23/32, with background music composed by [Dmytro Shurov](/wiki/Dmytro_Shurov). ### Vocal rules

For the third year in a row, delegations had the option to use pre-recorded backing vocals, though each delegation could still use live backing singers—whether on or off stage—or a combination of live and recorded backing vocals. However, all lead vocals and lead dubs performing the melody of the song must still be live. The contest's executive supervisor [Martin Österdahl](/wiki/Martin_%C3%96sterdahl) later stated that the use of pre-recorded backing vocals would continue to be permitted for the foreseeable future.

### Presenters

Presenters as they appeared in the final, from left to right: [Alesha Dixon](/wiki/Alesha_Dixon), [Julia Sanina](/wiki/Julia_Sanina), [Hannah Waddingham](/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham) and [Graham Norton](/wiki/Graham_Norton)

British singer [Alesha Dixon](/wiki/Alesha_Dixon), British actress [Hannah Waddingham](/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham), and Ukrainian singer [Julia Sanina](/wiki/Julia_Sanina) were announced as the presenters for the 2023 contest on 22 February 2023, and they hosted all three shows of the event; Irish television presenter [Graham Norton](/wiki/Graham_Norton) joined them for the final. Norton has served as the BBC's commentator for the contest since [2009](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009), and had previously co-hosted both editions of the [Eurovision Dance Contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Dance_Contest) in [2007](/wiki/Eurovision_Dance_Contest_2007) and [2008](/wiki/Eurovision_Dance_Contest_2008), as well as *[Eurovision Song Contest's Greatest Hits](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest%27s_Greatest_Hits)* in 2015.

The "Turquoise Carpet" and Opening Ceremony events were hosted by [Timur Miroshnychenko](/wiki/Timur_Miroshnychenko) (who had co-hosted the [2017 contest](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2017)) and [Sam Quek](/wiki/Sam_Quek), with [Richie Anderson](/wiki/Richie_Anderson_(presenter)) providing off-screen commentary. Miroshnychenko also moderated the contest's press conferences, along with Jermaine Foster and Mariia Vynogradova.

## Format

### Voting system and contest structure

See also: [Voting at the Eurovision Song Contest](/wiki/Voting_at_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest)

Presenters [Julia Sanina](/wiki/Julia_Sanina) and [Hannah Waddingham](/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham) announcing the semi-final qualifiers. The contest's executive supervisor, [Martin Österdahl](/wiki/Martin_%C3%96sterdahl), is seen in the background.

On 22 November 2022, the EBU announced changes to the voting system for the 2023 contest. The results of the semi-finals would be determined solely by televoting, as was the case between [2004](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004) and [2007](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2007), while the results of the final would be determined by a combination of national juries and televoting, as has been the case since the [2009](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2009) final. In the event that a country cannot deliver a televoting result in a semi-final, a backup jury result would be used instead. In the final, in the event that a country cannot deliver a televoting result, an aggregated result calculated on the basis of countries with similar voting patterns would be used. If a country's jury is disqualified, the televoting points from that country would be doubled and used as a substitute for that country's jury points in the final. The procedure of using calculated points would remain as a last resort in the event that a country cannot deliver a valid jury or televoting result. Viewers from non-participating countries would also be able to vote in all shows, with their votes being aggregated and presented as one individual set of points under "[Rest of the World](/wiki/Rest_of_the_World_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest)". Those viewers would be able to cast votes via an online platform, which requires ownership of a [credit](/wiki/Credit_card) or [debit card](/wiki/Debit_card) for verification.

On 8 May 2023, a change to the semi-final qualifiers announcement format was revealed, where the acts would be on stage to anticipate the announcement of the finalists instead of sitting in the [green room](/wiki/Green_room), similar to *[The X Factor](/wiki/The_X_Factor)*. This format was trialled during a dress rehearsal for the first semi-final, before being dropped on the same day due to negative responses.

London-based production company ModestTV was commissioned to produce the broadcast of the ceremony.

## Contest overview

### Semi-final 1

[Rita Ora](/wiki/Rita_Ora) performed as an interval act in the first semi-final.

The first semi-final took place on 9 May 2023 at 20:00 [BST](/wiki/British_Summer_Time) (21:00 [CEST](/wiki/Central_European_Summer_Time)). Fifteen countries participated in this semi-final, with the running order published on 22 March 2023. Finland won the most points, followed by Sweden, Israel, Czechia, Moldova, Norway, Switzerland, Croatia, Portugal, and Serbia. The countries that failed to reach the final were Latvia, Ireland, the Netherlands, Azerbaijan, and Malta. All the countries competing in this semi-final were eligible to vote, plus [France](/wiki/France_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), [Germany](/wiki/Germany_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) and [Italy](/wiki/Italy_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), as well as non-participating countries under an aggregated "Rest of the World" vote.

This semi-final was opened by a dance sketch set to "[Together in Electric Dreams](/wiki/Together_in_Electric_Dreams)", preceded by a pre-recorded segment featuring [Paul Hollywood](/wiki/Paul_Hollywood), [King Charles III](/wiki/Charles_III), [Queen Camilla](/wiki/Queen_Camilla), [Sister Sister](/wiki/Sister_Sister_(drag_queen)), [Ricky Tomlinson](/wiki/Ricky_Tomlinson), [Nikita Kuzmin](/wiki/Nikita_Kuzmin) and [Paul O'Grady](/wiki/Paul_O%27Grady) in a posthumous appearance. This was followed by co-presenter [Julia Sanina](/wiki/Julia_Sanina) performing "Mayak" with her husband and fellow [The Hardkiss](/wiki/The_Hardkiss) member Valeriy Bebko. The interval acts included [Alyosha](/wiki/Olena_Topolia) ([Ukraine 2010](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2010)) and [Rebecca Ferguson](/wiki/Rebecca_Ferguson_(singer)) performing "[Ordinary World](/wiki/Ordinary_World_(song))"; and [Rita Ora](/wiki/Rita_Ora) performing a medley of "[Ritual](/wiki/Ritual_(Ti%C3%ABsto,_Jonas_Blue_and_Rita_Ora_song))", "[Anywhere](/wiki/Anywhere_(Rita_Ora_song))", "[I Will Never Let You Down](/wiki/I_Will_Never_Let_You_Down)", and "[Praising You](/wiki/Praising_You)". The French, German, and Italian artists were then interviewed, and clips of their competing songs were played.

### Semi-final 2

[Mariya Yaremchuk](/wiki/Mariya_Yaremchuk) and [Zlata Dziunka](/wiki/Zlata_Dziunka) performed as part of an interval act in the second semi-final.

The second semi-final took place on 11 May 2023 at 20:00 BST (21:00 CEST). Sixteen countries participated in this semi-final, with the running order published on 22 March 2023. Australia won the most points, followed by Austria, Poland, Lithuania, Slovenia, Armenia, Cyprus, Belgium, Albania, and Estonia. The countries that failed to reach the final were Iceland, Georgia, Greece, Denmark, Romania, and San Marino. All the countries competing in this semi-final were eligible to vote, plus [Spain](/wiki/Spain_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023) and the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2023), as well as non-participating countries under an aggregated "Rest of the World" vote.

This semi-final featured a pre-recorded [spoken word](/wiki/Spoken_word) piece on the history of the contest by actor [Luke Evans](/wiki/Luke_Evans) during a break between the competing performances, while the interval acts included "Music Unites Generations", a medley of Ukrainian musical works performed by [Mariya Yaremchuk](/wiki/Mariya_Yaremchuk) ([Ukraine 2014](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014)), Otoy, and [Zlata Dziunka](/wiki/Zlata_Dziunka) (represented [Ukraine in Junior Eurovision 2022](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Junior_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022)); and a dance sketch choreographed by [Jason Gilkison](/wiki/Jason_Gilkison) and performed by [drag](/wiki/Drag_(entertainment)) acts Miss Demeanour, Miss Mercedes Bends, and [Tomara Thomas](/wiki/Tomara_Thomas), along with the Podilya dance ensemble. Titled "Be Who You Wanna Be", the second interval act was set to a medley of "[Free Yourself](/wiki/Free_Yourself_(Jessie_Ware_song))", "[Free Your Mind](/wiki/Free_Your_Mind_(song))", "[Free](/wiki/Free_(Ultra_Nat%C3%A9_song))" and the [2018 Australian entry](/wiki/Australia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018) "[We Got Love](/wiki/We_Got_Love_(Jessica_Mauboy_song))". The British, Spanish and Ukrainian artists were then interviewed, and clips of their competing songs were played.

### Final

[Duncan Laurence](/wiki/Duncan_Laurence) performed together with the guest artists and the presenters as part of an interval act in the final. [Ruslana](/wiki/Ruslana) can be seen on the LED background, in a pre-recorded appearance from the [Golden Gate](/wiki/Golden_Gate,_Kyiv) in [Kyiv](/wiki/Kyiv).

The final took place on 13 May 2023 at 20:00 BST (21:00 CEST). Twenty-six countries participated in the final, with the jury and televote of all thirty-seven participating countries, as well as non-participating countries under an aggregated "Rest of the World" online vote, eligible to vote. The running order for the final was published on 12 May 2023. Sweden won the contest with the song "[Tattoo](/wiki/Tattoo_(Loreen_song))", performed by [Loreen](/wiki/Loreen) and written by her along with [Jimmy Jansson](/wiki/Jimmy_Jansson), [Jimmy "Joker" Thörnfeldt](/wiki/Jimmy_Th%C3%B6rnfeldt), [Moa "Cazzi Opeia" Carlebecker](/wiki/Cazzi_Opeia), [Peter Boström](/wiki/Peter_Bostr%C3%B6m), and [Thomas G:son](/wiki/Thomas_G:son). Sweden won with 583 points, also winning the jury vote. Finland came second with 526 points and won the televote, with Israel, Italy, Norway, Ukraine, Belgium, Estonia, Australia and Czechia completing the top ten. Albania, Portugal, Serbia, the United Kingdom, and Germany occupied the bottom five positions.

The final was opened by [Kalush Orchestra](/wiki/Kalush_Orchestra) performing their winning song in [2022](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022), "[Stefania](/wiki/Stefania_(song))", and their latest single "Changes". Among those who appeared in the pre-recorded portion of the opening were Bolt Strings, [Andrew Lloyd Webber](/wiki/Andrew_Lloyd_Webber), [Joss Stone](/wiki/Joss_Stone), [Ballet Black](/wiki/Ballet_Black), [Ms Banks](/wiki/Ms_Banks), and [Catherine, Princess of Wales](/wiki/Catherine,_Princess_of_Wales). This was followed by the flag parade, introducing all twenty-six finalists, accompanied by four former Ukrainian participants performing new spins on their competing entries mixed with British classics: [Go\_A](/wiki/Go_A) with "[Shum](/wiki/Shum_(song))" ([2021](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021)), [Jamala](/wiki/Jamala) with "[1944](/wiki/1944_(song))" (winner in [2016](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2016)), [Tina Karol](/wiki/Tina_Karol) with "Show Me Your Love" ([2006](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006)), and [Verka Serduchka](/wiki/Verka_Serduchka) with "[Dancing Lasha Tumbai](/wiki/Dancing_Lasha_Tumbai)" ([2007](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2007)).

The interval acts included [Sam Ryder](/wiki/Sam_Ryder_(singer)) ([United Kingdom 2022](/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022)) performing his new single "[Mountain](/wiki/Mountain_(Sam_Ryder_song))" with [Roger Taylor](/wiki/Roger_Taylor_(Queen_drummer)) of [Queen](/wiki/Queen_(band)), and "The Liverpool Songbook", a homage to Liverpool's music heritage featuring six former entrants performing their own version of songs from the host city: [Mahmood](/wiki/Mahmood_(singer)) ([Italy 2019](/wiki/Italy_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019) and [2022](/wiki/Italy_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022)) with "[Imagine](/wiki/Imagine_(John_Lennon_song))", [Netta](/wiki/Netta_Barzilai) (winner for [Israel in 2018](/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018)) with "[You Spin Me Round (Like a Record)](/wiki/You_Spin_Me_Round_(Like_a_Record))", [Daði Freyr](/wiki/Da%C3%B0i_Freyr) ([Iceland 2021](/wiki/Iceland_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021)) with "[Whole Again](/wiki/Whole_Again)", [Cornelia Jakobs](/wiki/Cornelia_Jakobs) ([Sweden 2022](/wiki/Sweden_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022)) with "[I Turn to You](/wiki/I_Turn_to_You_(Melanie_C_song))", [Sonia](/wiki/Sonia_(singer)) with her entry for the [United Kingdom in 1993](/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1993), "[Better the Devil You Know](/wiki/Better_the_Devil_You_Know_(Sonia_song))", and [Duncan Laurence](/wiki/Duncan_Laurence) (winner for the [Netherlands in 2019](/wiki/Netherlands_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019)), together with the aforementioned artists, the presenters, and [Ruslana](/wiki/Ruslana) (winner for [Ukraine in 2004](/wiki/Ukraine_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004)) in a pre-recorded appearance from the [Golden Gate](/wiki/Golden_Gate,_Kyiv) in [Kyiv](/wiki/Kyiv), with "[You'll Never Walk Alone](/wiki/You%27ll_Never_Walk_Alone)". The ten qualifying countries were announced in no particular order, and the full results of how each country voted was published after the final had been held.

### Semi-final 2

The ten qualifiers from the second semi-final were determined solely by televoting, with the exception of San Marino which was unable to provide a valid televote result and thus used the votes of its backup jury. All sixteen countries competing in the second semi-final voted, alongside Spain, Ukraine and the United Kingdom, and the aggregated Rest of the World vote. The ten qualifying countries were announced in no particular order, and the full results of how each country voted was published after the final had been held.

The results of the final were determined by televoting and jury voting in all thirty-seven participating countries, plus the Rest of the World aggregate public vote. The announcement of the jury points was conducted by each country individually, with the country's spokesperson announcing their jury's favourite entry that received 12 points, with the remaining points shown on screen. Following the completion of the jury points announcement, the public points were announced as an aggregate by the contest hosts in ascending order starting from the country which received the fewest points from the jury.

#### 12 points

Below is a summary of all 12 points received in the final. In the jury vote, Sweden received the maximum score of 12 points from fifteen countries, with Italy and Israel receiving five sets of 12 points. Belgium received the maximum score from three countries, Australia and Finland were awarded two sets of 12 points each, and Austria, Czechia, Estonia, Slovenia and Ukraine were each being awarded one set of 12 points. In the public vote, Finland received the maximum score of 12 points from eighteen countries, followed by Israel and Ukraine which received four sets of 12 points each. Armenia, Italy and Moldova received two sets of maximum scores each, and Albania, Croatia, Cyprus, Norway, Poland and Slovenia were each awarded one set of 12 points. The winning country Sweden failed to receive any maximum scores from the public vote.

## Broadcasts

All participating broadcasters may choose to have on-site or remote commentators providing insight and voting information to their local audience. While they must broadcast at least the semi-final they are voting in and the final, most broadcasters air all three shows with different programming plans. In addition, some non-participating broadcasters air the contest. The European Broadcasting Union also provided international live streams with no commentary of both semi-finals and the final through their official [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) and [TikTok](/wiki/TikTok) channels. The table below details the broadcasting plans and commentators for the countries that aired the contest. According to the EBU, in total 162 million people watched at least a minute of the television broadcasts, and 15.6 million people watched the online broadcasts. Votes were received from 144 countries, including the 37 competing countries.

Technical issues occurred during the start of the first semi-final, causing most of the on-site commentators to lose connection to their broadcasters for around 15 minutes.

## Reception

### Commercial impact

After winning the 2023 contest, Sweden's entry "[Tattoo](/wiki/Tattoo_(Loreen_song))" became a commercial success. It peaked at number two on the [UK singles chart](/wiki/UK_singles_chart) dated 19 May 2023, and later became the first Eurovision song in 27 years to spend two weeks in the UK top five. In total, it spent four weeks in the UK top ten. It also topped the official charts in ten countries, and reached the top ten in a further 17 countries. The day after the final, "Tattoo" garnered 4,275,290 streams on [Spotify](/wiki/Spotify), thus breaking the record for the most streams achieved by a Eurovision song on a single day, which was previously held by the [2021](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021) winning entry "[Zitti e buoni](/wiki/Zitti_e_buoni)".

Along with "Tattoo", three other entries in the 2023 contest entered the top ten of the UK singles chart dated 19 May 2023, which is a first in the history of the chart: Finland's "[Cha Cha Cha](/wiki/Cha_Cha_Cha_(K%C3%A4%C3%A4rij%C3%A4_song))" at number six, the UK's "[I Wrote a Song](/wiki/I_Wrote_a_Song)" at number nine, and Norway's "[Queen of Kings](/wiki/Queen_of_Kings_(song))" at number ten. On the [*Billboard* Global 200](/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) chart dated 27 May 2023, "Tattoo", "Cha Cha Cha" and "Queen of Kings" entered at numbers 15, 27 and 58, respectively. On the [*Billboard* Global Excl. US](/wiki/Billboard_Global_200#Billboard_Global_Excl._US) chart also dated 27 May 2023, "Tattoo", "Cha Cha Cha" and "Queen of Kings" entered at numbers 7, 13 and 29, respectively, followed by Israel's "[Unicorn](/wiki/Unicorn_(song))" at number 153 and Italy's "[Due vite](/wiki/Due_vite)" at number 174. "Due vite" had previously peaked at number 32 following its win at the [Sanremo Music Festival 2023](/wiki/Sanremo_Music_Festival_2023), which also doubled as the Italian national final.

### Reaction to the results

See also: [List of Eurovision Song Contest winners § Discrepancies between the jury and televote](/wiki/List_of_Eurovision_Song_Contest_winners#Discrepancies_between_the_jury_and_televote)

Sweden's overall victory despite Finland's lead in the televoting sparked controversy among viewers and the live audience. During the jury voting sequence, several occasions when Sweden scored 12 points were disrupted by chanting from Finland's supporters, although the Swedish entrant [Loreen](/wiki/Loreen), following her win, stated that she did not mind and furthermore appreciated their enthusiasm. While the televote winner failed to win overall on three previous occasions, in [2015](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015), [2016](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2016) and [2019](/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019), 2023 was different in that Finland's lead of 133 points in the televote was the largest to date for an entry that did not win, and it also received the full 12 points from 18 different countries in the televoting, while Sweden did not win 12 points from any.

### Broadcasting awards

The 2023 contest was presented with the Changemaker Award at the [International Broadcasting Convention](/wiki/International_Broadcasting_Convention), in recognition of "its contribution to society and culture – celebrating a brand that continues to stay relevant and fresh on a huge scale". The award was received on 17 September 2023 by the contest's executive supervisor Martin Österdahl. The broadcast of the contest received nominations at the [2024 British Academy Television Awards](/wiki/2024_British_Academy_Television_Awards) for [Best Entertainment Performance](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Entertainment_Performance) (for co-presenter [Hannah Waddingham](/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham)) and [Best Live Event Coverage](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_Best_Live_Event), and at the [2024 British Academy Television Craft Awards](/wiki/2024_British_Academy_Television_Craft_Awards) for [Best Director: Multi-Camera](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Craft_Award_for_Best_Director:_Multi-Camera) (for Nikki Parsons, Ollie Bartlett, and Richard Valentine), and [Best Entertainment Craft Team](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Craft_Award_for_Best_Entertainment_Craft_Team) (for Julio Himede, [Tim Routledge](/wiki/Tim_Routledge), Kojo Samuel, Michael Sharp, and Dan Shipton); it won the latter three awards.

## Other awards

In addition to the main winner's trophy, the [Marcel Bezençon Awards](/wiki/Marcel_Bezen%C3%A7on_Awards) and the [You're a Vision Award](/wiki/You%27re_a_Vision_Award) were contested during the Eurovision Song Contest 2023. The [OGAE](/wiki/OGAE), "General Organisation of Eurovision Fans" voting poll also took place before the contest. Eurovision Awards, an end-of-year poll conducted by the contest's official site, returned for a third year with the results determined across eight categories.

### Marcel Bezençon Awards

The [Marcel Bezençon Awards](/wiki/Marcel_Bezen%C3%A7on_Awards), organised since 2002 by Sweden's then-Head of Delegation and 1992 representative [Christer Björkman](/wiki/Christer_Bj%C3%B6rkman), and winner of the 1984 contest [Richard Herrey](/wiki/Herreys), honours songs in the contest's final. The awards are divided into three categories: the Artistic Award, the Composers Award, and the Press Award. The winners were revealed shortly before the Eurovision final on 13 May.

### You're a Vision Award

The [You're a Vision Award](/wiki/You%27re_a_Vision_Award) (a [word play](/wiki/Word_play) of "Eurovision"), established in 2022 by the fansite *Songfestival.be* following the cancellation of the [Barbara Dex Award](/wiki/Barbara_Dex_Award) due to its associated negative connotations, aims to "celebrate the creativity and diversity that embody the Eurovision spirit", with the winner being the one with the most notable outfit. Finland's [Käärijä](/wiki/K%C3%A4%C3%A4rij%C3%A4) won the 2023 award, with Croatia's [Let 3](/wiki/Let_3) and Belgium's [Gustaph](/wiki/Gustaph_(singer)) completing the top three.

## Official album

Cover art of the official album

***Eurovision Song Contest: Liverpool 2023*** is the official [compilation album](/wiki/Compilation_album) of the contest. It was put together by the European Broadcasting Union and was released by [Universal Music Group](/wiki/Universal_Music_Group) digitally on 14 April 2023, in CD format on 28 April 2023, and in vinyl format on 26 May 2023. The album features all 37 entries.

# Chubby Checker

**Chubby Checker** (born **Ernest Evans**; October 3, 1941) is an American [rock and roll](/wiki/Rock_and_roll) singer and dancer. He is known for popularizing many dance styles, including [the Twist](/wiki/Twist_(dance)), with his 1960 hit cover of [Hank Ballard](/wiki/Hank_Ballard) & [The Midnighters](/wiki/The_Midnighters)' R&B song "[The Twist](/wiki/The_Twist_(song))", and the [Pony](/wiki/Pony_(dance)), with his 1961 cover of the song "[Pony Time](/wiki/Pony_Time)". His biggest UK hit, "[Let's Twist Again](/wiki/Let%27s_Twist_Again)", was released one year later (in 1962). That same year, Checker also popularized the song "[Limbo Rock](/wiki/Limbo_Rock)", a previous-year instrumental hit by [the Champs](/wiki/The_Champs) to which he added lyrics and its trademark [Limbo dance](/wiki/Limbo_(dance)). He also introduced other dance styles, such as [the Fly](/wiki/The_Fly_(Chubby_Checker_song)). In September 2008, "The Twist" topped *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))*'s list of the most popular [singles](/wiki/Single_(music)) to have appeared in the [Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) since its debut in 1960, an honor it maintained in an August 2013 update of the list. In 2014, Checker was inducted into the [National Rhythm & Blues Hall of Fame](/wiki/National_Rhythm_%26_Blues_Hall_of_Fame), and he was selected for induction into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame) in 2025.

## Early life

Checker was born Ernest Evans on October 3, 1941, in [Spring Gully, South Carolina](/wiki/Spring_Gully,_South_Carolina). He was raised in [the projects](/wiki/The_projects) of [South Philadelphia](/wiki/South_Philadelphia), where he lived with his parents, Raymond and Eartle Evans, and two brothers. By age 11, Evans formed a street-corner harmony group, and by the time he entered high school, Ernest had learned to play the piano a little at [Settlement Music School](/wiki/Settlement_Music_School). He entertained his classmates by performing vocal impressions of popular entertainers of the day, such as [Jerry Lee Lewis](/wiki/Jerry_Lee_Lewis), [Elvis Presley](/wiki/Elvis_Presley) and [Fats Domino](/wiki/Fats_Domino). One of his classmates and friends at [South Philadelphia High School](/wiki/South_Philadelphia_High_School) was Fabian Forte, who would become a popular performer of the late 1950s and early 1960s as [Fabian](/wiki/Fabian_Forte).

After school, Evans would entertain customers at his various jobs, including Fresh Farm Poultry in the [Italian Market](/wiki/Italian_Market,_Philadelphia) on Ninth Street and at the Produce Market, with songs and jokes. It was his boss at the Produce Market, Anthony Tambone, who gave Evans the nickname "Chubby". The owner of Fresh Farm Poultry, Henry Colt, was so impressed by the boy's performances for the customers that he, along with his colleague and friend Kal Mann, who worked as a songwriter for [Cameo-Parkway Records](/wiki/Cameo-Parkway_Records), arranged for young Chubby to do a private recording for *[American Bandstand](/wiki/American_Bandstand)* host [Dick Clark](/wiki/Dick_Clark). After hearing Chubby's Fats Domino impression, Clark's wife, Barbara, suggested that Chubby be called "Chubby [Checker](/wiki/Checkers)" in homage to him.

## Career

### 1950s–1960s

Checker performing in 1964

In December 1958, Checker privately recorded a novelty single for Clark in which the singer portrayed a school teacher with an unruly classroom of musical performers. The premise allowed Checker to imitate such acts as [Fats Domino](/wiki/Fats_Domino), [The Coasters](/wiki/The_Coasters), [Elvis Presley](/wiki/Elvis_Presley), [Cozy Cole](/wiki/Cozy_Cole), and [The Chipmunks](/wiki/The_Chipmunks), each singing "[Mary Had a Little Lamb](/wiki/Mary_Had_a_Little_Lamb)". Clark sent the song out as his Christmas greeting, and it received such good response that Cameo-Parkway signed Checker to a recording contract. Titled "[The Class](/wiki/The_Class_(song))", the single became Checker's first release, charting at No. 38 in the spring of 1959.

Checker introduced his version of "The Twist" at age 18 in July 1960 in [Wildwood, New Jersey](/wiki/Wildwood,_New_Jersey) at the Rainbow Club. The song went on to top the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) not just once in 1960, but yet again in a separate chart run in late 1961. The first success was attributed to teens, and the unprecedented second number-one Billboard ranking was driven by older audiences following a spirited live performance of the song by Checker on [The Ed Sullivan Show](/wiki/The_Ed_Sullivan_Show), seen by over 10 million viewers. ([Bing Crosby](/wiki/Bing_Crosby)'s "[White Christmas](/wiki/White_Christmas_(song))" had also achieved number one twice on *Billboard*'s earlier chart.)

"[The Twist](/wiki/The_Twist_(song))" had previously peaked at No. 16 on the *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* rhythm and blues chart, in the 1959 version recorded by its author, [Hank Ballard](/wiki/Hank_Ballard), whose band The Midnighters first performed the dance on stage. Checker's "Twist", however, was a nationwide smash, aided by his many appearances on Dick Clark's *[American Bandstand](/wiki/American_Bandstand)*, the top 10 *American Bandstand* ranking of the song, and the teenagers on the show who enjoyed dancing the Twist. The song was so ubiquitous that Checker felt that his critics thought he could only succeed as a dance demonstrator. Checker later lamented: ""The Twist" really ruined my life. I was on my way to becoming a big nightclub performer, and "The Twist" just wiped it out ... It got so out of proportion. No one ever believes I have talent." By 1965 alone, "The Twist" had sold over 15 million copies, and was awarded multiple [gold discs](/wiki/Music_recording_sales_certification) by the [RIAA](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America).

Despite Checker's initial disapproval, he found follow-up success with a succession of up-tempo dance tracks, including "The Hucklebuck" (No. 14), "[The Fly](/wiki/The_Fly_(Chubby_Checker_song))" (No. 7), "Dance the Mess Around" (No. 24), and "[Pony Time](/wiki/Pony_Time)", which became his second No. 1 single. Checker's follow-up "twist" single, "[Let's Twist Again](/wiki/Let%27s_Twist_Again)", won the 1962 [Grammy Award for Best Rock and Roll Recording](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rock_and_Roll_Recording). A 1962 duet with [Dee Dee Sharp](/wiki/Dee_Dee_Sharp), "Slow Twistin'", reached No. 3 on the national charts. Other substantial hits included "[Dancin' Party](/wiki/Dancin%27_Party)", "[Popeye the Hitchhiker](/wiki/Popeye_the_Hitchhiker)", "[Twenty Miles](/wiki/Twenty_Miles)", "[Birdland](/wiki/Birdland_(Chubby_Checker_song))", "[Loddy Lo](/wiki/Loddy_Lo)", and a Christmas duet with [Bobby Rydell](/wiki/Bobby_Rydell), "[Jingle Bell Rock](/wiki/Jingle_Bell_Rock)". "[Limbo Rock](/wiki/Limbo_Rock)" reached No. 2 on 22–29 December 1962, becoming Checker's last top 10 hit. Checker continued to have top 40 singles until 1965, his last being "[Let's Do the Freddie](/wiki/Let%27s_Do_the_Freddie)" (No. 40), a variation on [Freddie and the Dreamers](/wiki/Freddie_and_the_Dreamers)' dance tune "Do the Freddie", with new melody and lyrics. Changes in public tastes, owing mostly to the [British Invasion](/wiki/British_Invasion) and [counterculture era](/wiki/Music_history_of_the_United_States_in_the_1960s#Rock), ended his hit-making career. Checker spent much of the rest of the 1960s touring and recording in Europe.

### 1970s–1990s

"The Twist" was recorded for [Cameo-Parkway Records](/wiki/Cameo-Parkway_Records) and along with the label's other material, became unavailable after the early 1970s because of the company's internal legal disputes. For decades, almost all compilations of Checker's hits consisted of re-recordings. The 1970s saw a shift and resurgence for his career, including a temporary stint as a [disco](/wiki/Disco) artist. Checker continued to be a superstar in Europe with television and records. A dance-floor cover version of [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles)' "[Back in the U.S.S.R.](/wiki/Back_in_the_U.S.S.R.)" released in 1969 on [Buddah Records](/wiki/Buddah_Records), his first chart entry in three years, reached No. 82. It was Checker's last chart appearance until 1982 when he hit No. 91 with "Running".

Checker during a TV interview in 2008

In 1971, Checker at his own insistence recorded a [psychedelic](/wiki/Psychedelic_music) album filled with music he felt was "current" that was initially only released in Europe. Originally named *[Chequered!](/wiki/Chequered!)*, it was renamed over the years in subsequent re-releases as *New Revelation*, *The Other Side Of Chubby Checker*, and sometimes as *Chubby Checker*. The songs were all written by Checker and produced by former [Jimi Hendrix](/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix) producer [Ed Chalpin](/wiki/Ed_Chalpin), but the studio musicians' names are unknown. The album flopped.
Later in the decade, he recorded an album of "audiophile re-creations" of his greatest hits, for producer Stan Shulman.

In 1988, Chubby teamed up with hip-hop trio [The Fat Boys](/wiki/The_Fat_Boys) with their version of *The Twist (Yo, Twist)*: this reached No 2 in the UK.

### 2000s and beyond

Checker had a single at No. 1 on *Billboard's* dance [chart](/wiki/Record_chart) in July 2008 with "Knock Down the Walls". The single also made the top 30 on the Adult Contemporary chart. [Roger Filgate](/wiki/Roger_Filgate) of [Wishbone Ash](/wiki/Wishbone_Ash) is featured on lead guitar.

In 2009, Checker recorded a [public service announcement](/wiki/Public_service_announcement) (PSA) for the [Social Security Administration](/wiki/Social_Security_Administration) to help launch a new campaign to promote recent changes in [Medicare](/wiki/Medicare_(United_States)) law. In the PSA, Checker encourages Americans on Medicare to apply for Extra Help, "A new 'twist' in the law makes it easier than ever to save on your prescription drug plan costs."

On February 25, 2013, Checker released a new single, the ballad "Changes," via [iTunes](/wiki/ITunes); it was posted on YouTube and amassed over 160,000 views. "Changes" was produced by the hill & hifi and reached 43 on the [Mediabase](/wiki/Mediabase) Top 100 AC Chart and 41 on the Gospel Chart. Checker performed it on July 5, 2013, on [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s *[Today](/wiki/Today_(U.S._TV_program))* show. In 2015, Checker joined forces with Howard Perl Entertainment to produce live events, such as "Rock and Roll to The Rescue", a tour designed to raise funds for rescue animals in need nationwide.

In 2025, Checker was selected for induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame; he had been eligible for induction since the Hall's first class in 1986.

In 2002, Checker protested outside of the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame) induction ceremony, over the lack of radio airplay of his hit "[The Twist](/wiki/The_Twist_(song))" and his perception that the Hall of Fame had snubbed him. [Seymour Stein](/wiki/Seymour_Stein), president of the [Rock Hall](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame)'s New York chapter and member of the nomination committee, claimed "I think that Chubby is someone who will be considered. He has in certain years."

In 2013, Checker sued [HP](/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) over a [WebOS](/wiki/WebOS) application using his name. The application, before being pulled in September 2012, was used to unscientifically estimate penis size from shoe size. The district court said that Checker's trademark claim survived HP's motion to dismiss, but his other claims were dismissed per [Section 230](/wiki/Section_230) of the [Communications Decency Act](/wiki/Communications_Decency_Act).

## Film and musical depictions

Checker performed as well as appeared as a version of himself in *[Twist Around the Clock](/wiki/Twist_Around_the_Clock)* (1961) and *[Don't Knock the Twist](/wiki/Don%27t_Knock_the_Twist)* (1962). In both films he provided advice and crucial breaks for the protagonist.

In 1988, Checker appeared as himself performing alongside the Purple People Eater in the [film of the same name](/wiki/Purple_People_Eater_(film)). Checker later appeared as himself in the 1989 *[Quantum Leap](/wiki/Quantum_Leap_(1989_TV_series))* episode entitled "Good Morning, Peoria", where he walks into a radio station in 1959 hoping to have his demo record played on the air. The show's main character, Dr. [Sam Beckett](/wiki/Sam_Beckett) ([Scott Bakula](/wiki/Scott_Bakula)), persuades the station owner to play the song "The Twist", inadvertently teaching Checker himself how to do The Twist.

In 2001, Checker again guest-starred as himself singing "The Twist" in the [fourth season](/wiki/Ally_McBeal_(season_4)) of *[Ally McBeal](/wiki/Ally_McBeal)*. He also performed the track in the [tenth season](/wiki/List_of_Murphy_Brown_episodes#Season_10_(1997–98)) of *[Murphy Brown](/wiki/Murphy_Brown)*.

## Awards

In 2008, Checker's "The Twist" was named the biggest chart hit of all time by *Billboard* magazine. *Billboard* looked at all singles that made the charts between 1958 and 2008. He was also honored by [Settlement Music School](/wiki/Settlement_Music_School) as part of the school's centennial celebration and named to the Settlement 100, a list of notable people connected to the school.

In 2014, Checker was inducted into the [National Rhythm & Blues Hall of Fame](/wiki/National_Rhythm_%26_Blues_Hall_of_Fame).

In 2025, Checker was inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](/wiki/Rock_and_Roll_Hall_of_Fame).

## Personal life

On December 12, 1963, Checker proposed to [Catharina Lodders](/wiki/Catharina_Lodders), a 21-year-old [Dutch](/wiki/Dutch_people) model and [Miss World 1962](/wiki/Miss_World_1962) from [Haarlem](/wiki/Haarlem). Checker said he met Lodders in [Manila](/wiki/Manila) the prior January. The song "[Loddy Lo](/wiki/Loddy_Lo)" is about her. They got married on April 12, 1964, at Temple Lutheran Church in [Pennsauken, New Jersey](/wiki/Pennsauken_Township,_New_Jersey). Their first child, Bianca Johanna Evans, was born in a [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia) hospital on December 8, 1966. Their other two children are Ilka Evans and musician Shan Egan (Evans), lead singer of Funk Church, a band in the [Philadelphia area](/wiki/Philadelphia_metropolitan_area). Checker is also the father of former [WNBA](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) player [Mistie Bass](/wiki/Mistie_Bass).

**Sir Ben Kingsley** (born **Krishna Pandit Bhanji**; 31 December 1943) is a British-Indian actor. He has received [various accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Ben_Kingsley) throughout [his career](/wiki/Ben_Kingsley_on_screen_and_stage) spanning seven decades, including an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award), a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award), a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award), and two [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) as well as nominations for four [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards) and two [Laurence Olivier Awards](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Awards). Kingsley was appointed [Knight Bachelor](/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in 2002 for services to the [British film industry](/wiki/British_film_industry). He was awarded a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 2010 and received the [Britannia Award](/wiki/Britannia_Award) in 2014.

Born to an English mother and an Indian [Gujarati](/wiki/Gujarati_people) father with roots in [Jamnagar](/wiki/Jamnagar), Kingsley began his career in theatre, joining the [Royal Shakespeare Company](/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company) in 1967 and spending the next 15 years appearing mainly on stage. His starring roles included productions of *[As You Like It](/wiki/As_You_Like_It)* (his [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) debut for the company at the [Aldwych Theatre](/wiki/Aldwych_Theatre) in 1967), *[Much Ado About Nothing](/wiki/Much_Ado_About_Nothing)*, *[Richard III](/wiki/Richard_III_(play))*, *[The Tempest](/wiki/The_Tempest)*, *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)*, *[Hamlet](/wiki/Hamlet)* and *[The Merry Wives of Windsor](/wiki/The_Merry_Wives_of_Windsor)*. Also known for his television roles, he received four [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nominations for his performances in *[Murderers Among Us: The Simon Wiesenthal Story](/wiki/Murderers_Among_Us:_The_Simon_Wiesenthal_Story)* (1989), *[Joseph](/wiki/Joseph_(1995_film))* (1995), *[Anne Frank: The Whole Story](/wiki/Anne_Frank:_The_Whole_Story)* (2001), and *[Mrs. Harris](/wiki/Mrs._Harris)* (2006).

In film, Kingsley is principally known for his starring role as [Mahatma Gandhi](/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) in [Richard Attenborough](/wiki/Richard_Attenborough)'s *[Gandhi](/wiki/Gandhi_(film))* (1982), for which he won the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) and [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role). For his portrayal of [Itzhak Stern](/wiki/Itzhak_Stern) in [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s *[Schindler's List](/wiki/Schindler%27s_List)* (1993), he received a [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role) nomination. He was Oscar-nominated for *[Bugsy](/wiki/Bugsy)* (1990), *[Sexy Beast](/wiki/Sexy_Beast)* (2000), and *[House of Sand and Fog](/wiki/House_of_Sand_and_Fog_(film))* (2003). His other notable films include *[Maurice](/wiki/Maurice_(1987_film))* (1987), *[Sneakers](/wiki/Sneakers_(1992_film))* (1992), *[Dave](/wiki/Dave)* (1993), *[Searching for Bobby Fischer](/wiki/Searching_for_Bobby_Fischer)* (1993), *[Death and the Maiden](/wiki/Death_and_the_Maiden_(film))* (1994), *[Twelfth Night](/wiki/Twelfth_Night_(1996_film))* (1996), *[Tuck Everlasting](/wiki/Tuck_Everlasting_(2002_film))* (2002), *[Lucky Number Slevin](/wiki/Lucky_Number_Slevin)* (2006), *[Elegy](/wiki/Elegy_(film))* (2008), *[Shutter Island](/wiki/Shutter_Island_(film))* (2010), and *[Hugo](/wiki/Hugo_(film))* (2011).

Kingsley has played the character of [Trevor Slattery](/wiki/Trevor_Slattery) in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) for over a decade, appearing in the films *[Iron Man 3](/wiki/Iron_Man_3)* (2013) and *[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings](/wiki/Shang-Chi_and_the_Legend_of_the_Ten_Rings)* (2021), the short film *[All Hail the King](/wiki/All_Hail_the_King)* (2014), and the [Disney+](/wiki/Disney%2B) series *[Wonder Man](/wiki/Wonder_Man_(TV_series))* (2026–present). He also acted in the blockbusters *[Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time](/wiki/Prince_of_Persia:_The_Sands_of_Time_(film))* (2010), and *[Ender's Game](/wiki/Ender%27s_Game_(film))* (2013). Kingsley provided his voice in the films *[The Boxtrolls](/wiki/The_Boxtrolls)* (2014) and *[The Jungle Book](/wiki/The_Jungle_Book_(2016_film))* (2016).

## Early life, education and ancestry

Kingsley was born Krishna Pandit Bhanji on 31 December 1943, in [Snainton, North Riding of Yorkshire](/wiki/Snainton). His mother, Anna Lyna Mary (*née* Goodman) (1914–2010), was an English actress and model, and she later gave birth to a second son named Sadrudin Bhanji, who later practised as a [psychiatrist](/wiki/Psychiatrist) in [Devon](/wiki/Devon). She was born out of wedlock and "was loath to speak of her background". His father, Rahimtulla Harji Bhanji (1914–1968), was born in [Zanzibar](/wiki/Zanzibar) (now part of [Tanzania](/wiki/Tanzania)) to a family originating from the [Indian](/wiki/India) city of [Jamnagar](/wiki/Jamnagar), of [Khoja](/wiki/Khoja) [Gujarati](/wiki/Gujarati_people) descent.

Kingsley grew up in [Pendlebury, Lancashire](/wiki/Pendlebury,_Lancashire). Although his father was a [Gujarati](/wiki/Gujarat) Khoja who practised [Isma'ili Shia Islam](/wiki/Isma%27ili_Shia_Islam), Kingsley was not raised in his father's faith; he is a [Quaker](/wiki/Quaker). He was educated at [Manchester Grammar School](/wiki/Manchester_Grammar_School), where one of his classmates was actor [Robert Powell](/wiki/Robert_Powell). Kingsley went on to study at [De La Salle College](/wiki/Pendleton_College) in [Salford](/wiki/City_of_Salford), which later became home to The Ben Kingsley Theatre. While at college, he became involved in amateur dramatics in [Manchester](/wiki/Manchester), making his professional stage debut on graduation, aged 23.

Kingsley's paternal grandfather, Harji Bhanji, was a successful [spice trader](/wiki/Spice_trade) who had moved from India to the [Sultanate of Zanzibar](/wiki/Sultanate_of_Zanzibar), where Kingsley's father lived until moving to the United Kingdom at the age of 14. Kingsley's maternal grandfather was believed by the family to have been of Russian or German-Jewish descent, while his maternal grandmother was English and worked in the garment district of [London's East End](/wiki/London%27s_East_End). Kingsley stated in 1994, "I'm not Jewish, and though there might be some Russian-Jewish heritage way back on my mother's side, the thread is so fine there's no real evidence."

## Career

### 1967–1981: Stage work and early career

After graduating, in 1966, Kingsley was approached by music producer and manager [Dick James](/wiki/Dick_James). James, who was the publisher of [The Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles), offered to mould Kingsley into a pop star. Kingsley declined James' offer, and instead chose to join the [Royal Shakespeare Company](/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company) (RSC) in 1967 after an audition before [Trevor Nunn](/wiki/Trevor_Nunn). Devoting himself almost exclusively to stage work for the next 15 years, he made his [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) debut for the company at the [Aldwych Theatre](/wiki/Aldwych_Theatre) in 1967 in a production of *[As You Like It](/wiki/As_You_Like_It)*. Further productions for the RSC included *[Much Ado About Nothing](/wiki/Much_Ado_About_Nothing)*, *[Richard III](/wiki/Richard_III_(play))*, *[The Tempest](/wiki/The_Tempest)*, *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)* (starring in [Peter Brook](/wiki/Peter_Brook)'s [acclaimed 1970 RSC production](/wiki/RSC_production_of_A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream_(1970)) as Demetrius), *[Hamlet](/wiki/Hamlet)* and *[The Merry Wives of Windsor](/wiki/The_Merry_Wives_of_Windsor)*.

In the 1960s, Kingsley changed his name to Ben Kingsley, fearing that a foreign name would hamper his career. He told the *[Radio Times](/wiki/Radio_Times)*, "As soon as I changed my name, I got the jobs. I had one audition as Krishna Bhanji and they said, 'Beautiful audition but we don't quite know how to place you in our forthcoming season.' I changed my name, crossed the road, and they said when can you start?" In 1971 Kingsley made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) debut playing Demetrius in the revival of [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare)'s *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)* acting with [Patrick Stewart](/wiki/Patrick_Stewart), [Frances de la Tour](/wiki/Frances_de_la_Tour) and [Martin Best](/wiki/Martin_Best). He played Mosca in [Peter Hall](/wiki/Peter_Hall_(director))'s 1977 production of Ben Jonson's *[Volpone](/wiki/Volpone)* for the [Royal National Theatre](/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre). In 1981 he returned to Broadway playing the [title role](/wiki/Edmund_Kean) in the Raymond Fitzsimmons play *[Edmund Kean](/wiki/Edmund_Kean)* (1983). He played [Willy Loman](/wiki/Willy_Loman) in a 1982 [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney) production of the [Arthur Miller](/wiki/Arthur_Miller) play *[Death of a Salesman](/wiki/Death_of_a_Salesman)* opposite [Mel Gibson](/wiki/Mel_Gibson).

Kingsley began his transition to film roles early on, making his feature film debut playing a supporting role in the British action thriller *[Fear Is the Key](/wiki/Fear_Is_the_Key_(film))* in 1972. Kingsley continued to play small roles in both film and television, including a role as Ron Jenkins on the soap opera *[Coronation Street](/wiki/Coronation_Street)* from 1966 to 1967 and regular appearances as a defence counsel in the long-running British legal programme *[Crown Court](/wiki/Crown_Court_(TV_series))*. In 1974 he played Thidias in a taped performance of the [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare) play *[Antony and Cleopatra](/wiki/Antony_and_Cleopatra_(1974_TV_drama))* with the [Royal Shakespeare Company](/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company). He acted alongside [Patrick Stewart](/wiki/Patrick_Stewart) and [Tim Pigott-Smith](/wiki/Tim_Pigott-Smith). In 1975, he starred as [Dante Gabriel Rossetti](/wiki/Dante_Gabriel_Rossetti) in the historical drama *[The Love School](/wiki/The_Love_School)* and appeared in the TV miniseries *[Dickens of London](/wiki/Dickens_of_London)* the following year.

### 1982–1998: Transition to film and television

A turning point in Kingsley's career came with the historical biographical epic drama film *[Gandhi](/wiki/Gandhi_(film))* (1982), directed by [Richard Attenborough](/wiki/Richard_Attenborough), in which Kingsley played the titular role of the [anti-colonialist](/wiki/Anti-colonialist) activist and peacemaker [Mahatma Gandhi](/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi). The film was a critical and financial success with film critic [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) of *[The Chicago Sun-Times](/wiki/The_Chicago_Sun-Times)* praising the casting of Kingsley in the lead role writing, "makes the role so completely his own that there is a genuine feeling that the spirit of Gandhi is on the screen. Kingsley's performance is powerful without being loud or histrionic; he is almost always quiet, observant, and soft-spoken on the screen, and yet his performance comes across with such might that we realize, afterward, that the sheer moral force of Gandhi must have been behind the words." Kingsley went on to win numerous accolades for his performance including the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor), the [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), and the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama) for his performance.

Kingsley in Sweden (1983)

Following the success of the film *Gandhi*, Kingsley appeared in a variety of films, including the 1983 British drama film *[Betrayal](/wiki/Betrayal_(1983_film))*, which was an adaptation of the [1978 play of the same title](/wiki/Betrayal_(play)) by [Harold Pinter](/wiki/Harold_Pinter) and also features actors, [Jeremy Irons](/wiki/Jeremy_Irons) and [Patricia Hodge](/wiki/Patricia_Hodge). For this performance, Kingsley won the [Evening Standard British Film Awards](/wiki/Evening_Standard_British_Film_Awards#1983_Winners).

In 1985, Kingsley appeared in the [John Irvin](/wiki/John_Irvin) directed British drama *[Turtle Diary](/wiki/Turtle_Diary)*, which co-starred [Glenda Jackson](/wiki/Glenda_Jackson). This film was based on the [1975 novel of the same title](/wiki/Turtle_Diary_(novel)) and was adapted for the screen by [Harold Pinter](/wiki/Harold_Pinter). Sheila Benson of *[The Los Angeles Times](/wiki/The_Los_Angeles_Times)* praised their performances writing, "No filmic cliches, no swelling musical score; these are no "littul peeple" who melt into each other's arms, but blessedly real people, who get exhausted and don't talk all the time." During that same year, he appeared in the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) adaptation of *[Silas Marner](/wiki/Silas_Marner)* as the titular character.

In 1987, Kingsley acted in the [Merchant-Ivory](/wiki/Merchant-Ivory) costume drama *[Maurice](/wiki/Maurice_(1987_film))*. This film was adapted from the [1971 novel of the same title](/wiki/Maurice_(novel)) by [E. M. Forster](/wiki/E._M._Forster) and it also features the actors [Hugh Grant](/wiki/Hugh_Grant), [Rupert Graves](/wiki/Rupert_Graves), [Simon Callow](/wiki/Simon_Callow), and [Denholm Elliott](/wiki/Denholm_Elliott). The following year, Kingsley played multiple roles including the Russian composer [Dmitri Shostakovich](/wiki/Dmitri_Shostakovich) in *[Testimony](/wiki/Testimony_(1988_film))*, Basil Pascali in *[Pascali's Island](/wiki/Pascali%27s_Island_(film))* and [Dr. John Watson](/wiki/Dr._John_Watson) in *[Without a Clue](/wiki/Without_a_Clue)*.

Kingsley at the [Venice Film Festival](/wiki/1990_Venice_Film_Festival) (1990)

In 1991, Kingsley was nominated for an [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) for his portrayal of the organised crime figure [Meyer Lansky](/wiki/Meyer_Lansky) in *[Bugsy](/wiki/Bugsy)*. Shortly thereafter, he played the supporting character of Cosmo in the thriller film *[Sneakers](/wiki/Sneakers_(1992_film))* (1992), Vice-President Gary Nance in *[Dave](/wiki/Dave_(film))* (1993) and the chess teacher [Bruce Pandolfini](/wiki/Bruce_Pandolfini) in *[Searching for Bobby Fischer](/wiki/Searching_for_Bobby_Fischer)* (1993).

In 1993, Kingsley portrayed the [Holocaust](/wiki/Holocaust) survivor [Itzhak Stern](/wiki/Itzhak_Stern) alongside [Liam Neeson](/wiki/Liam_Neeson) as [Oskar Schindler](/wiki/Oskar_Schindler) in [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s historical drama film, *[Schindler's List](/wiki/Schindler%27s_List)*. The film was a critical and commercial success, and Kingsley received a nomination for [BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Supporting Role](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role). Todd McCarthy of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* wrote of his performance, "Kingsley must act within much more rigid constraints as his trusted accountant Stern, a man who feels he must never make a misstep. Role is reminiscent of [Alec Guinness](/wiki/Alec_Guinness)' deluded Col. Nicholson in *[The Bridge on the River Kwai](/wiki/The_Bridge_on_the_River_Kwai)*; in his compulsion to do a perfect job for Schindler, he often seems to forget that he's working for the enemy."

In 1994, Kingsley starred alongside [Sigourney Weaver](/wiki/Sigourney_Weaver) in [Roman Polanski](/wiki/Roman_Polanski)'s *[Death and the Maiden](/wiki/Death_and_the_Maiden_(1994_film))*, having previously acted with her in *Dave*. Two years later, Kingsley portrayed [Feste](/wiki/Feste) in *[Twelfth Night](/wiki/Twelfth_Night_(1996_film))*, a film adaptation of the [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare) play, which also features the actors [Helena Bonham Carter](/wiki/Helena_Bonham_Carter), [Nigel Hawthorne](/wiki/Nigel_Hawthorne), and [Richard E. Grant](/wiki/Richard_E._Grant). In a mixed review, Todd McCarthy of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* noted his performance as a highlight writing, "Ben Kingsley brings some nice readings to his rather mysterious role of Feste, the commentator on the convoluted proceedings."

In 1997, Kingsley provided a voice in the video game *[Ceremony of Innocence](/wiki/Ceremony_of_Innocence)*. The following year, he was the head of the jury at the [48th Berlin International Film Festival](/wiki/48th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival) and starred in the family film *[Spooky House](/wiki/Spooky_House)*, saying he had chosen a role in a lighter film after acting in roles that left him feeling traumatised.

### 1999–present: Further success

Kingsley took on the role of Don Logan, a violent psychopath and recruiter for London's underworld, in [Jonathan Glazer](/wiki/Jonathan_Glazer)'s *[Sexy Beast](/wiki/Sexy_Beast)* (2000), a [psychological](/wiki/Psychological_film) [black comedy](/wiki/Black_comedy) [crime film](/wiki/Crime_film) acting with [Ray Winstone](/wiki/Ray_Winstone) and [Ian McShane](/wiki/Ian_McShane). Critic [Peter Bradshaw](/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) of *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* praised his performance writing, "The role of Don Logan is perfectly suited to Ben Kingsley's gifts for control and stillness. There is something a little baroque and stylised about his approach – it is arguably a little actorly and unlike the behaviour of any real villain. But it is a very funny, intelligent performance nonetheless, beautifully scripted and acted, and Kingsley tops it off with a bravura show of pure sociopathic cunning". Kingsley's role as Logan earned him another Academy Award nomination for [Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor). A year later, he won a [Crystal Globe](/wiki/Crystal_Globe_(Karlovy_Vary_International_Film_Festival)) award for having an outstanding artistic contribution to [world cinema](/wiki/World_cinema) at the [Karlovy Vary International Film Festival](/wiki/Karlovy_Vary_International_Film_Festival). In 2003 he portrayed Colonel Massoud Amir Behrani in the [Vadim Perelman](/wiki/Vadim_Perelman) directed *[House of Sand and Fog](/wiki/House_of_Sand_and_Fog_(film))* acting opposite [Jennifer Connelly](/wiki/Jennifer_Connelly) and [Shohreh Aghdashloo](/wiki/Shohreh_Aghdashloo). Critic Owen Gleiberman of *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* wrote of his performance, "Kingsley, carrying his body like armor, sculpting each line into a bitter dart of pride, plays fierceness with a powerful tug of sorrow." For his role he earned nominations for the [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor), [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama), and [Independent Spirit Award](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award_for_Best_Male_Lead) for Best Actor. The following year he played a supporting role as Benjamin O'Ryan in the psychological thriller *[Suspect Zero](/wiki/Suspect_Zero)* (2004). Although the film received negative reviews from critics, reservations were made for Kingsley's performance.

In July 2006, Kingsley received an [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Limited Series or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie) nomination for his performance in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) television film *[Mrs. Harris](/wiki/Mrs._Harris)* (2005), in which he played famed [cardiologist](/wiki/Cardiologist) [Herman Tarnower](/wiki/Herman_Tarnower), who was murdered by his jilted lover, [Jean Harris](/wiki/Jean_Harris) played by [Annette Bening](/wiki/Annette_Bening). Later that year, he made a cameo appearance in an episode of *[The Sopranos](/wiki/The_Sopranos)* titled "[Luxury Lounge](/wiki/Luxury_Lounge)". Kingsley plays himself in the episode as [Chris](/wiki/Christopher_Moltisanti) and [Little Carmine](/wiki/Little_Carmine) pitch him the role of a mob boss in the film [*Cleaver*](/wiki/Cleaver_(The_Sopranos)), which he turns down. In 2007, Kingsley appeared as a [Polish American](/wiki/Polish_American) mobster in the [Mafia comedy](/wiki/Mafia_comedy) *[You Kill Me](/wiki/You_Kill_Me)*, and a hitman in *[War, Inc.](/wiki/War,_Inc.)* The following year he acted in the romantic drama *[Elegy](/wiki/Elegy_(film))* (2008) directed by [Isabel Coixet](/wiki/Isabel_Coixet). He starred alongside [Penélope Cruz](/wiki/Pen%C3%A9lope_Cruz), [Peter Sarsgaard](/wiki/Peter_Sarsgaard), [Patricia Clarkson](/wiki/Patricia_Clarkson), and [Dennis Hopper](/wiki/Dennis_Hopper). Critic [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) wrote of the film and his performance, "Ben Kingsley, who can play just about any role, seems to be especially effective playing slimy intellectuals. "Elegy" is a film that could have been made for him, although by the time it's over, Penélope Cruz has slipped away with it, and transformed Kingsley's character in the process. It's nicely done." Kingsley received a nomination for the [London Film Critics Circle Award](/wiki/London_Film_Critics_Circle_Award) for Best British Actor of the Year.

The years 2010 and 2011 contained several big roles for Kingsley. In 2010, he worked voicing a character named Sabine in the [Lionhead Studios](/wiki/Lionhead_Studios) game *[Fable III](/wiki/Fable_III)* and starred alongside [Leonardo DiCaprio](/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio) in *[Shutter Island](/wiki/Shutter_Island_(film))* (2010), directed by [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese). That same year, Kingsley made his [Bollywood](/wiki/Bollywood) debut in the thriller *[Teen Patti](/wiki/Teen_Patti_(film))* (2010). The following year he appeared in Scorsese's next film, the children's adventure film *[Hugo](/wiki/Hugo_(film))* (2011), playing the French illusionist [Georges Méliès](/wiki/Georges_M%C3%A9li%C3%A8s). Kingsley's portrayal of Méliès also earned him a [Saturn Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Saturn_Award_for_Best_Actor). Kingsley also signed on to the sci-fi romance feature *Broken Dream*. The feature, by [Neil Jordan](/wiki/Neil_Jordan) and [John Boorman](/wiki/John_Boorman), was later scrapped.

Kingsley at the [Santa Barbara International Film Festival](/wiki/Santa_Barbara_International_Film_Festival) (2012)

In 2013, Kingsley appeared as the villain [Trevor Slattery](/wiki/Trevor_Slattery) in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) film *[Iron Man 3](/wiki/Iron_Man_3)* with [Robert Downey Jr.](/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr.)*,* and as the hero [Mazer Rackham](/wiki/Mazer_Rackham) in the science-fiction action adventure film *[Ender's Game](/wiki/Ender%27s_Game_(film))* with [Harrison Ford](/wiki/Harrison_Ford) and [Asa Butterfield](/wiki/Asa_Butterfield). A year later he played the Hebrew slave [Nun](/wiki/Nun_(biblical_figure)) in [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott)'s *[Exodus: Gods and Kings](/wiki/Exodus:_Gods_and_Kings)* and Merenkahre, a [simulacrum](/wiki/Simulacrum) of an Egyptian pharaoh and father of Ahkmenrah, in [Shawn Levy](/wiki/Shawn_Levy)'s *[Night at the Museum: Secret of the Tomb](/wiki/Night_at_the_Museum:_Secret_of_the_Tomb)* with [Ben Stiller](/wiki/Ben_Stiller), [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams), [Owen Wilson](/wiki/Owen_Wilson), [Rami Malek](/wiki/Rami_Malek) and [Dan Stevens](/wiki/Dan_Stevens). That same year, Kingsley would also reprise his role as Slattery in the direct-to-video short film *[All Hail the King](/wiki/All_Hail_the_King)*.

In 2015, Kingsley portrayed a [Sikh](/wiki/Sikh) driving instructor in the film *[Learning to Drive](/wiki/Learning_to_Drive_(film))*. He voiced [Bagheera](/wiki/Bagheera) in the live-action adaptation of [Jon Favreau](/wiki/Jon_Favreau)'s *[The Jungle Book](/wiki/The_Jungle_Book_(2016_film))* (2016), a remake of the original [1967 film](/wiki/The_Jungle_Book_(1967_film)) shared cast with [Bill Murray](/wiki/Bill_Murray), [Idris Elba](/wiki/Idris_Elba), [Lupita Nyong'o](/wiki/Lupita_Nyong%27o), [Scarlett Johansson](/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson) and [Christopher Walken](/wiki/Christopher_Walken). Kingsley also recorded [Yogananda](/wiki/Paramahansa_Yogananda)'s *[Autobiography of a Yogi](/wiki/Autobiography_of_a_Yogi)* in book-on-tape format. In 2018, he narrated Amazon Prime's documentary *[All or Nothing: Manchester City](/wiki/All_or_Nothing:_Manchester_City)* which followed [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City)'s record breaking [2017–18 Premier League](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Premier_League) campaign. and served as the voice of General Woundwort in the BBC adaptation of *[Watership Down](/wiki/Watership_Down_(2018_TV_series))*.

In 2021, Kingsley reprised his role as Trevor Slattery in the film *[Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings](/wiki/Shang-Chi_and_the_Legend_of_the_Ten_Rings)*. Two years later, he also acted in [Wes Anderson](/wiki/Wes_Anderson)'s *[The Wonderful Story of Henry Sugar](/wiki/The_Wonderful_Story_of_Henry_Sugar_(film))*, a film adaptation of a [short story](/wiki/The_Wonderful_Story_of_Henry_Sugar_and_Six_More) by [Roald Dahl](/wiki/Roald_Dahl), and starred opposite [Ralph Fiennes](/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes), [Dev Patel](/wiki/Dev_Patel) and [Benedict Cumberbatch](/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch). This film went on to win the [Academy Award for Best Live Action Short Film](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Live_Action_Short_Film). In 2025, Kingsley played Ibrahim Arif in a film adaptation of [Richard Osman](/wiki/Richard_Osman)'s multi-award-winning *[Thursday Murder Club](/wiki/Thursday_Murder_Club)* series in an undisclosed role alongside [Helen Mirren](/wiki/Helen_Mirren), [Pierce Brosnan](/wiki/Pierce_Brosnan), [Celia Imrie](/wiki/Celia_Imrie) and [Jonathan Pryce](/wiki/Jonathan_Pryce) .

Kingsley reprised his role of Trevor Slattery in *[Wonder Man](/wiki/Wonder_Man_(TV_series))*, released on 27 January 2026. He will next star as [Robert Dinwiddie](/wiki/Robert_Dinwiddie) in *[Young Washington](/wiki/Young_Washington)*, [Terrence Malick](/wiki/Terrence_Malick)'s epic biblical drama *[The Way of the Wind](/wiki/The_Way_of_the_Wind)*, [Renny Harlin](/wiki/Renny_Harlin)'s disaster horror film *[Deep Water](/wiki/Deep_Water_(2026_film))*, [Simon West](/wiki/Simon_West)'s historical spy action-adventure film *[Fortitude](/wiki/Fortitude_(upcoming_film))*, and the action-adventure film *[Sonic the Hedgehog 4](/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_4_(film))*.

## Personal life

Kingsley (third left) and the cast of *[The Children's Monologues](/wiki/The_Children%27s_Monologues)* at the [Old Vic Theatre](/wiki/Old_Vic_Theatre) (2010)

### Marriage and family

Kingsley has been married four times and has four children: Thomas Bhanji and artist Jasmin Bhanji, with his first wife, actress [Angela Morant](/wiki/Angela_Morant), and Edmund Kingsley and [Ferdinand Kingsley](/wiki/Ferdinand_Kingsley), both of whom became actors, with his second wife, theatrical director [Alison Sutcliffe](/wiki/Alison_Sutcliffe). He divorced his third wife Alexandra Christmann in 2005, having been "deeply, deeply shocked" after pictures of her kissing another man surfaced on the internet. On 3 September 2007, Kingsley married [Brazilian](/wiki/Brazil) actress [Daniela Lavender](/wiki/Daniela_Lavender) at Eynsham Hall in [North Leigh, Oxfordshire](/wiki/North_Leigh).

### Charity

Kingsley appeared in a production of *[The Children's Monologues](/wiki/The_Children%27s_Monologues)* in 2010 on stage in [London](/wiki/London) alongside [Benedict Cumberbatch](/wiki/Benedict_Cumberbatch), [Tom Hiddleston](/wiki/Tom_Hiddleston), [Gemma Arterton](/wiki/Gemma_Arterton), and [Eddie Redmayne](/wiki/Eddie_Redmayne). It was performed on behalf of [Dramatic Need](/wiki/Dramatic_Need), a charity that sends international arts professionals (such as musicians, artists, and actors) to host workshops in underprivileged and rural communities in Africa.

## Acting credits and accolades

Main articles: [Ben Kingsley on screen and stage](/wiki/Ben_Kingsley_on_screen_and_stage) and [List of awards and nominations received by Ben Kingsley](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Ben_Kingsley)

Kingsley's handprints at [Leicester Square](/wiki/Leicester_Square), London

Kingsley won an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) in the [Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor) category for *Gandhi*, and has been nominated three more times: [Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) for *Bugsy* and *Sexy Beast*, and Best Actor for *[House of Sand and Fog](/wiki/House_of_Sand_and_Fog_(film))* (2003). In 1984, Kingsley won a Grammy Award for Best Spoken Word or Nonmusical Recording for *The Words of Gandhi*, received an [honorary degree](/wiki/Honorary_degree) from the [University of Salford](/wiki/University_of_Salford), and was awarded the Indian civilian honour [Padma Shri](/wiki/Padma_Shri).
He was made a [Knight Bachelor](/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in the [2002 New Year Honours](/wiki/2002_New_Year_Honours) for services to the British film industry. The award was announced on 31 December 2001, which happened to be Kingsley's 58th birthday. After being [knighted](/wiki/Knight) by Queen [Elizabeth II](/wiki/Elizabeth_II) at [Buckingham Palace](/wiki/Buckingham_Palace), Kingsley stated:

> I told the Queen that winning an Oscar pales into insignificance—this is insurmountable. I'm fascinated by the [ancient](/wiki/Ancient), by [mythology](/wiki/Mythology), by these islands and their tradition of story telling. I feel that I am a story teller and to receive a [knighthood](/wiki/Knighthood) is really recognition of that.

His demand to be called 'Sir' in film and TV show credits was documented by the BBC, to some criticism. Co-star [Penélope Cruz](/wiki/Pen%C3%A9lope_Cruz) was reportedly unsure what to call him during the filming of *[Elegy](/wiki/Elegy_(film))* as someone had told her she needed to refer to him as "Sir Ben". One day it slipped out as such, and she called him that for the remainder of the shoot. Kingsley has denied accusations that he prefers to be referred to by his title, saying, "If I've ever insisted on being called 'Sir' by colleagues on a film set then I am profoundly sorry. I don't remember ever doing that and I tend not to forget."
In May 2010, Kingsley was awarded a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame). In April 2013, Kingsley was honoured with the Fellowship Award at [The Asian Awards](/wiki/The_Asian_Awards) in London.

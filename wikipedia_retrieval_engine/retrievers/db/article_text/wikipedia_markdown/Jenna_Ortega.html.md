# Jenna Ortega

## Infobox

- **Born:** Jenna Marie Ortega ( 2002-09-27 ) September 27, 2002 (age 23) Rancho Mirage, California, U.S.
- **Occupation:** Actress
- **Years active:** 2012–present

**Jenna Marie Ortega** (born September 27, 2002) is an American actress. Known for her work in [horror](/wiki/Horror_film), Ortega has been dubbed "[Gen Z](/wiki/Gen_Z)'s [scream queen](/wiki/Scream_queen)" by media publications. She has also been featured on the [Power 100](/wiki/Power_100) list from *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* in 2023 and the [*Forbes* 30 Under 30](/wiki/Forbes_30_Under_30) list in 2024.

Ortega began acting professionally at age nine and received recognition for her role as a younger version of [Jane](/wiki/Jane_Villanueva) in [The CW](/wiki/The_CW) comedy-drama series *[Jane the Virgin](/wiki/Jane_the_Virgin)* (2014–2019). She then played a leading role as [Harley Diaz](/wiki/List_of_Stuck_in_the_Middle_characters) in the [Disney Channel](/wiki/Disney_Channel) series *[Stuck in the Middle](/wiki/Stuck_in_the_Middle_(TV_series))* (2016–2018), for which she won an [Imagen Award](/wiki/Imagen_Award). She expanded into more mature projects afterwards, playing [Ellie Alves](/wiki/Ellie_Alves) in the thriller series *[You](/wiki/You_(TV_series))* (2019) and co-starring in the horror-comedy film *[The Babysitter: Killer Queen](/wiki/The_Babysitter:_Killer_Queen)* (2020), both for [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix). She received praise for her performance as a traumatized high school student in the drama film *[The Fallout](/wiki/The_Fallout_(film))* (2021).

Ortega rose to wider prominence as a scream queen after co-starring in the successful slasher films *[Scream](/wiki/Scream_(2022_film))* (2022), *[X](/wiki/X_(2022_film))* (2022), and *[Scream VI](/wiki/Scream_VI)* (2023). She gained international recognition and critical plaudits for her portrayal of [Wednesday Addams](/wiki/Wednesday_Addams) in the Netflix horror comedy series *[Wednesday](/wiki/Wednesday_(TV_series))* (2022–present), for which she [received nominations](/wiki/List_of_roles_and_awards_of_Jenna_Ortega) at the [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actress_%E2%80%93_Television_Series_Musical_or_Comedy), [Primetime Emmy](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series), and [Screen Actors Guild](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Female_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) awards. She has since starred in the blockbuster fantasy film *[Beetlejuice Beetlejuice](/wiki/Beetlejuice_Beetlejuice)* (2024) and the ill-received thriller film *[Hurry Up Tomorrow](/wiki/Hurry_Up_Tomorrow_(film))* (2025). Aside from acting, Ortega has been noted for her goth glam style in addition to supporting various charitable causes.

## Early life

Jenna Marie Ortega was born on September 27, 2002, in [Rancho Mirage, California](/wiki/Rancho_Mirage,_California). Her father, Edward Ortega, a former sheriff who works at a California [district attorney](/wiki/District_attorney)'s office, is of [Mexican](/wiki/Mexicans) descent, and her mother, Natalie Ortega, an [emergency room](/wiki/Emergency_room) nurse, is of Mexican and [Puerto Rican](/wiki/Puerto_Ricans) descent. She is the fourth of their six children. Ortega's maternal great-grandmother was an undocumented immigrant from [Sinaloa](/wiki/Sinaloa), Mexico, and her maternal grandfather relocated from Puerto Rico to [the Bronx](/wiki/The_Bronx), [New York](/wiki/New_York_(state)), before moving to the [Western United States](/wiki/Western_United_States). Ortega grew up in [La Quinta, California](/wiki/La_Quinta,_California), and has described her childhood self as "loud and extroverted".

Ortega wanted to act from the age of six after watching [Dakota Fanning](/wiki/Dakota_Fanning) in the 2004 thriller film [*Man on Fire*](/wiki/Man_on_Fire_(2004_film)). Ortega was unable to understand how a young child could perform in a way that could scare her so much; she analyzed the film several times and decided she wanted to be "the Puerto Rican version of [Fanning]". Ortega later said that acting served as an escape from the pressure she placed on herself due to being a "very existential" and worrisome child. She begged her mother for three years to let her pursue that career path. Her mother attempted to distract her with activities including soccer and schoolwork. Soccer almost convinced Ortega to quit pursuing acting. Ortega's mother bought her a monologue book when she was nine and posted a video of her performing online. A casting director saw it and signed her to an agency.

Ortega's mother began driving her to [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) as often as five days per week for auditions, a journey that could take six hours round trip. She struggled to land roles, because few parts existed for [Latinas](/wiki/Latinas) and she "didn't look [a certain] way". Such rejection challenged her self-esteem. She considered dyeing her hair blonde in an attempt to book more roles. For the first year, with no connections in the film industry, Ortega only auditioned for TV commercials. She managed to secure parts in 12 national campaigns, including three for [McDonald's](/wiki/McDonald%27s). During the school week, she would act in Los Angeles for a few days and subsequently return home to attend school. She attended public school, including Amelia Earhart Elementary School and [John Glenn Middle School](/wiki/John_Glenn_Middle_School). She stopped attending public school during eighth grade to act in [Disney](/wiki/Disney) productions and got an apartment in Los Angeles after booking a role in *[Stuck in the Middle](/wiki/Stuck_in_the_Middle_(TV_series))* (2016–2018). During that time, Ortega acted in Los Angeles during the [workweek](/wiki/Workweek) and returned home on weekends. Ortega has said that her early teenage years were "full of tension and fear" due to being terrified of making mistakes while working.

## Career

### 2012–2017: Early acting roles and Disney

Ortega made her acting debut in the sitcom *[Rob](/wiki/Rob_(TV_series))* (2012) and later made a guest appearance in the *[CSI: NY](/wiki/CSI:_NY)* episode "[Unspoken](/wiki/Unspoken_(CSI:_NY))". In 2013, she made her film debut in an uncredited role as the vice president's daughter in the superhero film *[Iron Man 3](/wiki/Iron_Man_3)*. In the same year, Ortega appeared in the horror film *[Insidious: Chapter 2](/wiki/Insidious:_Chapter_2)* as a part of the supporting cast. From 2014 to 2019, Ortega had a recurring role on [The CW](/wiki/The_CW) comedy television series *[Jane the Virgin](/wiki/Jane_the_Virgin)* as a younger version of [Jane Villanueva](/wiki/Jane_Villanueva), who is portrayed by [Gina Rodriguez](/wiki/Gina_Rodriguez) as an adult. Declan Gallagher of *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* commended Ortega's performance, writing that she avoided "the trappings of a typical child actor". She also starred in the [direct-to-video](/wiki/Direct-to-video) comedy film *[The Little Rascals Save the Day](/wiki/The_Little_Rascals_Save_the_Day)* (2014) and the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) sitcom *[Richie Rich](/wiki/Richie_Rich_(2015_TV_series))* (2015). Ortega appeared in the comedy-drama film *[After Words](/wiki/After_Words_(film))* (2015) as Anna Chapa, the daughter of a [male escort](/wiki/Male_escort). The *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* praised her as "adorable", whereas *[The Arizona Republic](/wiki/The_Arizona_Republic)* found her "horribly cloying".

From 2016 to 2018, Ortega led the [Disney Channel](/wiki/Disney_Channel) sitcom *Stuck in the Middle* as Harley Diaz, an aspiring inventor who is the middle child of the seven siblings. Ortega drew inspiration from her own family and relationships with her siblings; she felt her experience starring on the show was akin to [reality television](/wiki/Reality_television). [Common Sense Media](/wiki/Common_Sense_Media) praised Ortega's performance, saying she "commands this role, pleading her case as a long-suffering middle kid while finding the humor in the many calamities that befall her efforts to shine". Ortega garnered three [Imagen Award](/wiki/Imagen_Award) nominations for Best Young Actor – Television, including a win in 2018. She also voiced Princess Isabel in *[Elena and the Secret of Avalor](/wiki/Elena_and_the_Secret_of_Avalor)* (2016) and *[Elena of Avalor](/wiki/Elena_of_Avalor)* (2016–2020).

### 2018–2021: Transition to mature roles

In 2018, Ortega starred in the film *Saving Flora* in the lead role of Dawn, a circus owner's daughter. After *Stuck in the Middle* ended in 2018, she aimed to act in more mature projects but found people assumed Disney roles were "all you can do, or all you were meant for". She considered quitting acting several times; she said that she "was too old for the young roles and too young for the older roles" and that she thought it was "a good time" to withdraw from the career due to starting high school, as well as needing to "prove [her]self" to casting directors unfamiliar with her. In 2018, she was cast as [Ellie Alves](/wiki/Ellie_Alves) in [the second season](/wiki/You_season_2) of the Netflix thriller series *[You](/wiki/You_(TV_series))* and decided to continue acting due to enjoying the experience. Released in late 2019, the second season, like the show's first season, received positive reviews; *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)*'s Robyn Bahr noted Ortega as "a standout performer". Ortega was slated to return for [the third](/wiki/You_season_3) and [fourth](/wiki/You_season_4) seasons but was unable to do so due to scheduling conflicts.

Ortega in 2020

Ortega played Phoebe, the love interest of [Judah Lewis](/wiki/Judah_Lewis)'s character, in Netflix's horror film *[The Babysitter: Killer Queen](/wiki/The_Babysitter:_Killer_Queen),* which was released in September 2020 to negative critical reviews. She also voiced a travel vlogger named Brooklynn in the Netflix animated show *[Jurassic World Camp Cretaceous](/wiki/Jurassic_World_Camp_Cretaceous)* (2020–2022). In 2021, she made her writing debut with the book *It's All Love: Reflections for Your Heart & Soul*, which contains a series of quotes and statements regarding faith and love. She starred in the Netflix comedy movie *[Yes Day](/wiki/Yes_Day)* (2021), portraying a stubborn teenager who wants more independence from her parents. It was released in March 2021 to mixed reviews. [IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire) lauded Ortega as "very capable", and the [Associated Press](/wiki/Associated_Press) referred to her as "an impressively poised young actor".

Ortega had a leading role in the high-school drama film *[The Fallout](/wiki/The_Fallout_(film))*, starring as a student who is navigating her emotional trauma after a [school shooting](/wiki/School_shooting). To prepare for the role, she looked at photographs and watched videos and interviews about school shootings; processing such events afterward "kind of came naturally". She also participated in the [March for Our Lives](/wiki/March_for_Our_Lives) movement, which led [demonstrations](/wiki/Political_demonstration) in support of [U.S. gun control legislation](/wiki/Gun_law_in_the_United_States). *The Fallout* premiered at [South by Southwest](/wiki/South_by_Southwest) on March 17, 2021, and was released on [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max) on January 27, 2022. The film received a positive response from critics, and Ortega's acting was applauded. [Richard Roeper](/wiki/Richard_Roeper) of the *[Chicago Sun-Times](/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times)* described her performance as "grounded and deeply moving", while *The Hollywood Reporter* wrote that her "beautifully nuanced turn understands the nothing-to-look-at-here façade and the chinks in the armor".

### 2022–2023: Mainstream breakthrough

In the slasher film [*Scream*](/wiki/Scream_(2022_film)) (2022)—the fifth film in the [*Scream* franchise](/wiki/Scream_(franchise))—Ortega played [Tara Carpenter](/wiki/Tara_Carpenter), on which she stated: "I don't even think there are words in the English language to correctly express how happy, excited and nervous I am for this journey". Directors [Matt Bettinelli-Olpin](/wiki/Matt_Bettinelli-Olpin) and [Tyler Gillett](/wiki/Tyler_Gillett) cast Ortega because of her ability to play both horror and comic tones, and for the sound of her scream. She felt nervous about joining a well-known franchise, wanting to respect its legacy while avoiding derivation. She said that working on the film taught her the importance of cast chemistry to the natural flow of the production process. *Scream* was a critical and commercial success, becoming the [28th-highest-grossing film of 2022](/wiki/2022_in_film). According to *[The A.V. Club](/wiki/The_A.V._Club)*, Ortega demonstrated "incredible" resilience and resolve in her role. She also won the [MTV Movie Award for Most Frightened Performance](/wiki/MTV_Movie_Award_for_Most_Frightened_Performance).

Ortega later appeared in the [Foo Fighters](/wiki/Foo_Fighters)–led horror film *[Studio 666](/wiki/Studio_666)* (2022). Reviewers said she was underused. She then starred in the slasher film [*X*](/wiki/X_(2022_film)) (2022), which was directed and written by [Ti West](/wiki/Ti_West), who said Ortega was "fearless in her commitment". Ortega signed onto the project because of its script, calling it "the most outrageous thing I've ever read", and the opportunity to work with West. *X* became Ortega's best-reviewed film on review aggregator [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes); *[The Australian](/wiki/The_Australian)* dubbed her and co-star [Mia Goth](/wiki/Mia_Goth) "scene-stealers". The comedy-horror *[American Carnage](/wiki/American_Carnage)* was Ortega's final film release of 2022. *The A.V. Club* said she was continuing "her streak as the new it-girl of horror with a stubborn punk persona that masks her care for other people", while *[IGN](/wiki/IGN)* lauded her "no-shits-given shell".

Ortega promoting *[Wednesday](/wiki/Wednesday_(TV_series))* in 2022

In May 2021, Ortega was cast as [Wednesday Addams](/wiki/Wednesday_Addams) in the Netflix comedy-horror series *[Wednesday](/wiki/Wednesday_(TV_series))* (2022), which she called a "new chapter" in her career. She auditioned through a [Zoom](/wiki/Zoom_(software)) call with director [Tim Burton](/wiki/Tim_Burton), during which she read a four-page monologue. The casting team felt her portrayal offered the character the necessary empathy. She was initially hesitant to accept the part because she wanted to focus on film acting and feared working on another television series would prevent her from booking the roles she wanted. To prepare for the role, Ortega underwent "the most physical transformation I've ever done", cutting her hair and dying it black, as well as altering her mannerisms, style of speaking, and facial expressions. She also learned to play cello and speak German, read the original *[Addams Family](/wiki/Addams_Family)* comic, and watched [the 1960s television adaptation](/wiki/The_Addams_Family_(1964_TV_series)). Ortega called the production of *Wednesday*'s first season her "most overwhelming job" in 2022, and spoke about being in a constant state of confusion and stress over the direction of the series and character. She said that she had never "had to put my foot down on a set in the way that I had to on *Wednesday*", believing that the initial writing was nonsensical from a character standpoint and did not suit Wednesday's personality. While filming some scenes, Ortega would change her lines without informing the crew.

Upon *Wednesday*'s release, critics widely praised Ortega's performance; [CNN](/wiki/CNN) said that she sets the series apart from similar spin-offs, being "relentlessly strange, a portrait in unblinking intensity and oddly endearing all at once". *Wednesday* became one of [Netflix's most-watched shows](/wiki/List_of_most-watched_Netflix_original_programming), receiving over 1 billion viewing hours within a month. Ortega's performance received nominations for a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) and a [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award). She was also nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) for [Outstanding Lead Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Outstanding_Lead_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series), becoming the category's second-youngest nominee. Journalists have credited the show with garnering Ortega international recognition. She later spoke about her discomfort with becoming well known for her work on the show, having had a creatively unpleasant experience filming it.

In March 2023, Ortega hosted an episode of [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s [sketch comedy](/wiki/Sketch_comedy) series *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*. Ortega reprised her role as Tara Carpenter in *[Scream VI](/wiki/Scream_VI)*. The film was released in March 2023 and grossed over $169 million on a budget of $33–35 million. *[Deadline Hollywood](/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)* attributed its box-office success partially to Ortega's star power. In a review, *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))*'s [Owen Gleiberman](/wiki/Owen_Gleiberman) praised her "surly spunk", and *[Slant Magazine](/wiki/Slant_Magazine)* said she "fantastically embodie[d] the role". In November 2023, Ortega exited the *Scream* franchise after her co-star [Melissa Barrera](/wiki/Melissa_Barrera) was fired due to her [pro-Palestinian comments](/wiki/International_reactions_to_the_Gaza_war#Celebrities,_other_personalities) on the [Gaza war](/wiki/Gaza_war); she stated a subsequent film did not appear to be the "right move" due to the changing cast and crew. Earlier reports suggested that Ortega left the franchise because of scheduling conflicts with the filming of *Wednesday*'s second season and an unsuccessful request for seven-figure pay. In the crime thriller film *[Finestkind](/wiki/Finestkind)* (2023), Ortega played Mabel, a drug dealer's daughter who is seeking her own path in life. The film premiered at the [Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/Toronto_International_Film_Festival) to negative reviews. Many journalists said Ortega had been miscast.

### 2024–present: Continued film roles

Ortega at the *[Beetlejuice Beetlejuice](/wiki/Beetlejuice_Beetlejuice)* film premiere at the [2024 Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/2024_Venice_International_Film_Festival)

Ortega began 2024 with a starring role in the drama *[Miller's Girl](/wiki/Miller%27s_Girl),* opposite [Martin Freeman](/wiki/Martin_Freeman). The film tells the story of Cairo Sweet (Ortega), a high schooler whose writing ability captures her teacher's interest, which leads to a complicated relationship between the two. She called her character "the most complex character that I've ever played" and described the material as risky since she thought it would provoke discussions on topics people may find disturbing. *[RogerEbert.com](/wiki/RogerEbert.com)*'s [Christy Lemire](/wiki/Christy_Lemire) found Ortega "magnetic enough" to maintain the viewer's interest, although she thought the character's motives were predictable and illogical; Mark Kennedy of the Associated Press dubbed the film "a pointless, awkward turn" in her career. Ortega appeared in the romantic drama *[Winter Spring Summer or Fall](/wiki/Winter_Spring_Summer_or_Fall)*, which premiered at the 2024 [Tribeca Festival](/wiki/Tribeca_Festival). Director Tiffany Paulsen described Ortega as the casting team's first choice for the role of Remi Aguilar, a driven genius who falls in love with a rebellious musician. She later starred in the music video for [Sabrina Carpenter](/wiki/Sabrina_Carpenter)'s song "[Taste](/wiki/Taste_(Sabrina_Carpenter_song))".

Ortega starred in Burton's fantasy film *[Beetlejuice Beetlejuice](/wiki/Beetlejuice_Beetlejuice)* (2024)—a sequel to *[Beetlejuice](/wiki/Beetlejuice)* (1988)—playing Astrid Deetz, the cynical teenage daughter of Lydia Deetz. It opened the [81st Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/81st_Venice_International_Film_Festival) and received generally positive reviews. *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* said Ortega was an "unimpeachable" casting choice and commended the emotions she conveyed using a "world-class deadpan stare". In a more negative review, *[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)* stated that she "can't help but seem a little bland and strait-laced" acting opposite [Michael Keaton](/wiki/Michael_Keaton) as Betelgeuse. The film has grossed $451 million worldwide; publications such as *[Boxoffice Pro](/wiki/Boxoffice_Pro)* and *[TheWrap](/wiki/TheWrap)* credited Ortega for attracting younger audiences, particularly members of [Generation Z](/wiki/Generation_Z).

In the dark comedy *[Death of a Unicorn](/wiki/Death_of_a_Unicorn)* (2025), [Paul Rudd](/wiki/Paul_Rudd) and Ortega starred as a father and daughter who accidentally kill a [unicorn](/wiki/Unicorn), leading to brutal consequences. Ortega said she was drawn to the project because of its original and unique script. The film received mixed reviews. [Peter Travers](/wiki/Peter_Travers) of [ABC News](/wiki/ABC_News_(United_States)) said Ortega "takes this horror comedy into human territory and gives it a beating heart", bringing energy to the story despite its perceived shortcomings. Another review in *IGN* described her portrayal as a "wonderfully lived-in performance" of a withdrawn youth, praising her ability to "inject great thought and subtext into any scenario". She next featured in [Trey Edward Shults](/wiki/Trey_Edward_Shults)' *[Hurry Up Tomorrow](/wiki/Hurry_Up_Tomorrow_(film))*, co-starring alongside [Abel Tesfaye](/wiki/Abel_Tesfaye) and [Barry Keoghan](/wiki/Barry_Keoghan). Ortega played Anima, a troubled young woman who pulls Tesfaye's character into an existential odyssey. The film was panned by critics, but Ortega's performance was generally praised. Clint Worthington of *RogerEbert.com* felt that she did "her best to uplift her half of the movie", while [Frank Scheck](/wiki/Frank_Scheck) of *The Hollywood Reporter* commended her "intense" energy. *IndieWire*'s Charles Bramesco, however, dismissed her performance as "imitative and hollow".

Ortega at the 2026 Sundance Film Festival

Ortega reprised her role as Wednesday in the second season of *Wednesday*. She became a producer, which she described as a "natural progression" due to the first season's collaborative nature, though she said her new role made her feel "more empowered to speak my mind". Ortega was involved with writing, design, and casting, where she discussed scripts, oversaw [prosthetics](/wiki/Prosthetic_makeup), and observed auditions and chemistry reads. She stated the experience was "a great education" where she sought to immerse herself and absorb as much as possible. Upon the season's 2025 release, Ortega's performance continued to received positive reviews. *RogerEbert.com* described her portrayal as "expertly calibrated between menacing and inquisitive", and *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* said her "charisma could power a thousand hearses". She also received her second Golden Globe and Actor Award nominations. In 2026, she starred opposite [Natalie Portman](/wiki/Natalie_Portman) in *[The Gallerist](/wiki/The_Gallerist_(film))*.

#### Upcoming projects

Ortega is slated portray the titular character in *[Klara and the Sun](/wiki/Klara_and_the_Sun_(film)),* directed by [Taika Waititi](/wiki/Taika_Waititi), and appear in [J. J. Abrams](/wiki/J._J._Abrams)'s *[The Great Beyond](/wiki/The_Great_Beyond_(film))*, for which she was given creative control over her character's appearance.

Ortega is also attached to co-star and produce *Single Female,* a remake of the 1992 thriller film *[Single White Female](/wiki/Single_White_Female),* opposite [Taylor Russell](/wiki/Taylor_Russell).

## Other ventures

### Activism and philanthropy

Ortega has used her platform to support various causes, including *Pride Over Prejudice*—a [DoSomething](/wiki/DoSomething) campaign that advocates for immigrants and refugees—the National Bullying Prevention Center, the [AIDS Healthcare Foundation](/wiki/AIDS_Healthcare_Foundation), the [Geena Davis Institute on Gender in Media](/wiki/Geena_Davis_Institute_on_Gender_in_Media), [Planned Parenthood](/wiki/Planned_Parenthood), and the [Joint United Nations Programme on HIV and AIDS](/wiki/Joint_United_Nations_Programme_on_HIV_and_AIDS). She is pro-immigration and anti-discrimination, telling *[Teen Vogue](/wiki/Teen_Vogue)*: "It's important to embrace your culture today because there are so many different ethnicities in America. At the end of the day, you are you. You've got to stay true to yourself, and you can't change yourself in order to fit in or to make someone else feel comfortable." Ortega is also an advocate for [women's rights](/wiki/Women%27s_rights).

In 2016, Ortega organized a meet-and-greet event for fans to raise money for a young girl with cancer. At the 2018 [Radio Disney Music Awards](/wiki/Radio_Disney_Music_Awards), Ortega wore a jacket displaying the words "I Do Care and U Should Too" in response to the clothing [First Lady of the United States](/wiki/First_Lady_of_the_United_States) [Melania Trump](/wiki/Melania_Trump) wore on a visit to see immigrant children who were being housed without their parents. Trump's jacket read, "I Really Don't Care, Do U?". Ortega's protest gained significant media coverage; she told the Associated Press that Trump's apparel displayed a lack of judgement and as the first lady, she ought to show concern for migrant children. Ortega later said: "We should all care about each other and our country ... we are one country under God". In 2019, Ortega appeared at numerous [We Day](/wiki/We_Day) benefit concerts across the U.S. and [Canada](/wiki/Canada) to raise funds for [WE Charity](/wiki/WE_Charity).

In 2023, Ortega, as part of a group called [Artists4Ceasefire](/wiki/Artists4Ceasefire), signed a letter urging the [United States Congress](/wiki/United_States_Congress) and President [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) to call for an immediate ceasefire in the [Gaza Strip](/wiki/Gaza_Strip) during the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict). She later condemned the [Tel al-Sultan attack](/wiki/Tel_al-Sultan_attack) and emphasized the need for a ceasefire. In 2026, she denounced the [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement) (ICE) following the killings of [Renée Good](/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good) and [Alex Pretti](/wiki/Killing_of_Alex_Pretti).

### Endorsements

In 2020, Ortega was named a brand ambassador for the cosmetics manufacturer [Neutrogena](/wiki/Neutrogena) and was the face of its "My Quinceañera Journey" campaign. In 2023, she became an ambassador for the sportswear company [Adidas](/wiki/Adidas) and the luxury fashion brand [Dior](/wiki/Dior). She was the face of Adidas Sportswear, the company's first new line in 50 years, as well as one of the faces of the #DareInGrisDior campaign. In 2025, she was announced as a global beauty ambassador for Dior. Ortega starred alongside [Danny Ramirez](/wiki/Danny_Ramirez), Olivia Negron, and [Patricia Mauceri](/wiki/Patricia_Mauceri) in an advertisement for [Doritos](/wiki/Doritos) that aired at the [Super Bowl LVIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII) in February 2024.

## Artistry

[Megan Park](/wiki/Megan_Park), who directed Ortega in *The Fallout*, said that she knows "when to give her all and when to hold back" while acting, adding that "to have that understanding of herself as a performer at such a young age" is extremely rare. Burton compared Ortega's acting style to [silent film](/wiki/Silent_film) acting, saying she expresses emotion through her eyes without words. Ortega says she tries not to be more prepared than necessary for a scene because "you never know what you're going to say next or what your next facial expression is going to be" in real life. She has stated that she finds it natural for actors to assimilate into their characters' environment, for instance, using sarcasm and rewatching films in preparation for *Wednesday*.

The [Dodge College](/wiki/Dodge_College_of_Film_and_Media_Arts) professor and agent Joe Rosenberg describes Ortega as an actress who has "a feel for the right project at the right time" and is "savvy" with her role choices. Ortega feels taking more varied acting roles is important to her to avoid being typecast; she told *[Entertainment Tonight](/wiki/Entertainment_Tonight)* in 2021 that she did not want to be "pigeonholed as an actor" and that her desire to accept the most diverse roles possible made the transition to mature roles easier for her. Ortega stated that she looks for projects that will have a "forever effect" for her, such as *The Fallout*. She has said she feels most comfortable starring in horror works, describing such projects as "very therapeutic". In 2023, Ortega said she is mindful of not becoming overly associated with violent films and feels "very conflicted" about her interests, because there is "a part of me that always feels like the girl in the [Coachella Valley](/wiki/Coachella_Valley)".

## Media image

Media publications have described Ortega's style as goth glam.

Commenting on Ortega's off-screen persona, *Who What Wear* wrote in 2022 that she is "poised, mature, and wise beyond her years" while providing "an essence of relatability". A 2023 *[Harper's Bazaar](/wiki/Harper%27s_Bazaar)* story describes her as "quiet and contemplative", while *[Vanity Fair](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine))* wrote that she has a "black-hole persona" but is "much warmer and more cheerful than you'd expect". Ortega stated that the media's public persona of her is inaccurate and that she can feel "incredibly misunderstood". Ortega has been noted for her work in horror and has been described as a horror icon by journalists. Media publications have dubbed her "Gen Z's scream queen", and the [Hollywood Foreign Press Association](/wiki/Hollywood_Foreign_Press_Association) called her the "scream queen for the 21st century". Ortega attributes her "instinctive" scream and "really ugly crying face" for the positive reception her horror work has received from audiences.

In 2018, *The Hollywood Reporter* named Ortega one of "Hollywood's Top 30 Stars Under Age 18". The magazine later referred to her as the "Next Big Thing" in 2022 and "The Gen Z Obsession" in 2024. In 2023, Ortega appeared on *Variety*'s "Young Hollywood Impact Report", a list of breakthrough performers, and *The Hollywood Reporter*'s [Power 100](/wiki/Power_100), a list of the most powerful women in entertainment. *[Complex](/wiki/Complex_(magazine))* also listed her among the best actors in their 20s. *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* included her on the 2024 edition of its [30 Under 30](/wiki/30_Under_30) in the Hollywood & Entertainment category.

Ortega has also been called a style icon by *Harper's Bazaar*. Her initial red-carpet style featured bright colors, patterned clothing, and many accessories. Enrique Melendez, her stylist, dressed Ortega in [RED Valentino](/wiki/Valentino_(fashion_house)) and [Thom Browne](/wiki/Thom_Browne), wanting her wardrobe to be age-appropriate, fashionable, and stylistically interesting. They later tried to move away from the "feminine and flirty and frilly" style of other Disney actresses. After her role in *You*, Ortega began wearing heavier makeup and darker clothing. Media publications said her later style borrowed from Wednesday's while incorporating [high fashion](/wiki/High_fashion) and described it as goth glam. During the press tour for *Wednesday*, Vogue and *WWD* highlighted her choice of [Miista](/wiki/Miista) boots as part of that evolving aesthetic. Ortega said that playing Wednesday has "changed my taste a lot" and she has "a hard time getting her off of me, at least clothing-wise". *[The New York Observer](/wiki/The_New_York_Observer)* and *Harper's Bazaar* credited her with popularizing "Wednesdaycore", a high-fashion interpretation of gothic style. In 2025, *[Vogue](/wiki/Vogue_(magazine))* listed her among the best dressed of the year; the magazine stated that she is noted for "[l]eading the goth resurgence in dramatic silhouettes, strange textures, monochromatic palettes, bleached brows, and smudgy, undone glam". Ortega cited [Siouxsie Sioux](/wiki/Siouxsie_Sioux) as an inspiration for her aesthetic saying: "I love everything about her and her presence, she’s always been quite impactful for me."

Ortega is active on [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram) but has described social media overall as being a "comparing game" and "very manipulative". She has said she became fearful of her comments on social media being misinterpreted following the release of *Wednesday*, since her sarcasm and dry humor are easily misconstrued, adding: "[People] see your vulnerability and twist it in a way that you don't always expect." As a minor, Ortega was sent explicit images of herself that were [AI-generated](/wiki/Deepfake), which later led to her deleting her [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter) account.

## Personal life

Ortega stated that she would like to be a "private person" and not discuss her romantic life publicly, believing that it distracts audiences from her work. She has described the concept of [celebrity](/wiki/Celebrity) as "absolutely ridiculous". Ortega has said that her friends and family give her a sense of safety that helps her deal with the more challenging parts of her career. She states that growing up in an industry dominated by adults forced her to mature faster than normal.

Ortega stated in 2025 that she has [anxiety](/wiki/Anxiety_disorder) and [obsessive–compulsive disorder](/wiki/Obsessive%E2%80%93compulsive_disorder).

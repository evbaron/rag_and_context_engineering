The **River Marden** is a small [tributary](/wiki/Tributary) of the [River Avon](/wiki/River_Avon,_Bristol) in England. It flows from the hills surrounding [Calne](/wiki/Calne) and meets the Avon about a mile upstream of [Chippenham](/wiki/Chippenham). The river has a mean flow of 43 cubic feet per second (1.2 m3/s).

## Course

The Marden in the grounds of Blackland House, upstream of Calne

The Marden rises just north of the valley of Ranscombe Bottom near [Calstone Wellington](/wiki/Calstone_Wellington) in [Wiltshire](/wiki/Wiltshire). It then flows in a north-west direction through the [Blackland](/wiki/Blackland,_Wiltshire) area, where it forms a small ornamental lake at Blackland House, and on to [Quemerford](/wiki/Quemerford), where it is joined on the right bank by the Rivers Brook. In Calne, the Abberd Brook joins on the right. The river turns in a westerly direction and is joined by the overspill from Bowood Lake, part of the [Bowood House](/wiki/Bowood_House) estate, on the left at Studleybrook Farm. The river is then joined by the combined Fisher's and Cowage brooks before turning to the north-west, past the village of [Stanley](/wiki/Stanley,_Wiltshire). 1.5 miles (2.4 km) later it joins the Bristol Avon to the north-east of [Chippenham](/wiki/Chippenham).

## History

The [Domesday survey](/wiki/Domesday_Book) of England in 1086 records four [watermills](/wiki/Watermill) on the Marden at Calne. In the 18th century four [fulling](/wiki/Fulling) mills are recorded and one of these, Upper Mill, became a paper mill in 1768 and continued in operation until 1860. Hassell's Mill at [Studley](/wiki/Studley,_Wiltshire) remained in operation until 1960.

A branch of the [Wilts & Berks Canal](/wiki/Wilts_%26_Berks_Canal), opened in 1810, paralleled the course of the Marden west of Calne. The canal was closed in 1914 following the collapse into the river of the Stanley aqueduct in 1901. Traces of the canal and its towpath survive.

## Hydrology

The [Environment Agency](/wiki/Environment_Agency) gauging station at Stanley has measured the mean flow of the river as 43 cubic feet per second (1.2 m3/s), with a maximum recorded flow of 1,529 cubic feet per second (43.3 m3/s) on 30 October 2000 and a minimum of 3.9 cubic feet per second (0.11 m3/s) on 21 August 1976.

**Luther Burbank Home and Gardens** is a city park containing the former home, greenhouse, gardens, and grave of noted American [horticulturist](/wiki/Horticulture) [Luther Burbank](/wiki/Luther_Burbank) (1849–1926). It is located at the intersection of Santa Rosa Avenue and Sonoma Avenue in [Santa Rosa, California](/wiki/Santa_Rosa,_California), in the [United States](/wiki/United_States). The park is open daily without charge; a fee is charged for guided tours. It is designated as a [National Historic Landmark](/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark) as well as a [California Historical Landmark (#234)](/wiki/California_Historical_Landmark).

## History

Luther Burbank Gardens in 2006

Burbank lived in Santa Rosa for more than 50 years, and performed the bulk of his life's work at this location. From 1884 to 1906 he lived in this park's [Greek Revival](/wiki/Greek_Revival_architecture) house; he then moved across Tupper street to a house that no longer exists. After Burbank's death in 1926, his widow Elizabeth moved back to the house, where she remained until her death in 1977.

Burbank, a native of [Massachusetts](/wiki/Massachusetts), was a nationally known figure who was responsible for creating many new varieties of plants. He is credited with introducing more than 250 new varieties of [fruit](/wiki/Fruit), including a large number of [plum](/wiki/Plum) varieties that are widely used in agriculture. A portion of the property was declared a [National Historic Landmark](/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark) in 1964. The site is included in the [Santa Rosa historic landmarks](/wiki/Santa_Rosa_historic_landmarks) and the Sonoma County Historical Society list of landmarks.

## Gardens

The gardens include many of Burbank's horticultural introductions, with collections of [cactus](/wiki/Cactus), [fruit](/wiki/Fruit) trees, ornamental [grasses](/wiki/Grass), medicinal [herbs](/wiki/Herb), [roses](/wiki/Rose), and [walnuts](/wiki/Walnut). Most plants are labeled with botanic and common names. The garden's greenhouse was designed and built by Burbank in 1889; Burbank's grave is nearby, underneath a [Cedar of Lebanon](/wiki/Cedar_of_Lebanon).

# Stone of Morphie

The Stone of Morphie

The **Stone of Morphie** (sometimes known as the **Stone of Morphy**) is a [standing stone](/wiki/Standing_stone) about 700 metres west of the Coast Highway ([A92 road](/wiki/A92_road)) bridge of the [River North Esk](/wiki/River_North_Esk) and 400 metres east of the historic [Mill of Morphie](/wiki/Mill_of_Morphie) in [Aberdeenshire](/wiki/Aberdeenshire), [Scotland](/wiki/Scotland). The stone is approximately 3.5 metres high and is unshaped and uninscripted; the base of this stone measures approximately 70 by 100 centimetres.

The [RCAHMS](/wiki/RCAHMS) record number for the Stone of Morphie is NO76SW 6 7169 6273. According to RCAHMS, the stone is "Traditionally said to mark the grave of a son of [Camus](/wiki/Camus_(folklore)), killed in a battle between the Scots and the Danes". Local tradition claims the site as an alternative burial site for a leader of a [Viking](/wiki/Viking) army that was decimated by the Scots army at the apocryphal [Battle of Barry](/wiki/Battle_of_Barry) in 1010 AD. The date and mention of this battle rests with [Boece](/wiki/Boece).

## Etymology

The name *Morphie* may be of [Brittonic](/wiki/Common_Brittonic) origin, and derived from an element consanguineous to [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh_language) *morfa*, meaning "a sea-plain".

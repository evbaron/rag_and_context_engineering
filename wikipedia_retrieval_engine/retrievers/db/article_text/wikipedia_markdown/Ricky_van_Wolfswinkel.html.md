In this [Dutch name](/wiki/Dutch_name), the [surname](/wiki/Surname) is  *van Wolfswinkel*, not  *Wolfswinkel*.

**Ricky van Wolfswinkel** (Dutch pronunciation: [[ˈrɪki vɑɱ ˈʋɔl(ə)fsˌʋɪŋkəl]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch); born 27 January 1989) is a Dutch professional [footballer](/wiki/Association_football) who plays as a [striker](/wiki/Striker_(association_football)) for [Eredivisie](/wiki/Eredivisie) club [Twente](/wiki/FC_Twente). He has played top-flight football in the Netherlands, Portugal, England, France, Spain and Switzerland and has been [capped](/wiki/Cap_(sport)) twice by the [Netherlands national team](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team).

## Club career

### Vitesse

Van Wolfswinkel started his football career at the D-pupils of [Vitesse Arnhem](/wiki/Vitesse_Arnhem). In the A-juniors, he became top scorer in the national first division, winning a prize awarded by the [Royal Dutch Football Association](/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Football_Association) (KNVB) for the feat. This prize was presented by [Wesley Sneijder](/wiki/Wesley_Sneijder) in 2007. On 5 April 2008, Van Wolfswinkel made his debut for Vitesse's first team in a match against [Sparta Rotterdam](/wiki/Sparta_Rotterdam), in which he was the substitute in the 85th minute for [Santi Kolk](/wiki/Santi_Kolk). Against [De Graafschap](/wiki/De_Graafschap), in the [2008–09](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Eredivisie) season, he was in the starting lineup for the first time. In his third match that season, against Sparta Rotterdam, he scored his first [Eredivisie](/wiki/Eredivisie) goal.

### Utrecht

[![](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Ricky_van_Wolfswinkel.ogv/250px--Ricky_van_Wolfswinkel.ogv.jpg)](//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/Ricky_van_Wolfswinkel.ogv)

Van Wolfswinkel being interviewed following Utrecht's qualification for the Europa League

On 29 May 2009, Van Wolfswinkel announced his departure from Vitesse, subsequently signing a three-year contract with [FC Utrecht](/wiki/FC_Utrecht). He began the [2010–11](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Eredivisie) league campaign with a goal in a 3–1 loss against [Feyenoord](/wiki/Feyenoord). On 22 August 2010, he scored two goals against [Willem II](/wiki/Willem_II_(football_club)) resulting in a 3–0 win for Utrecht. On 26 August 2010, Van Wolfswinkel scored a hat-trick against [Celtic](/wiki/Celtic_F.C.) in the [UEFA Europa League](/wiki/UEFA_Europa_League); Utrecht went on to win the match 4–0. On 3 October 2010, Van Wolfswinkel scored two penalties against [Ajax](/wiki/AFC_Ajax) to gain a 2–1 away win over the Dutch runners-up.

### Sporting CP

On 3 June 2011, Van Wolfswinkel moved to [Sporting CP](/wiki/Sporting_Clube_de_Portugal), signing a five-year contract on a €5.4 million deal; Sporting CP was given financial aid by [Sporting Portugal Fund](/wiki/Sporting_Portugal_Fund) and [Quality Sports Investments](/wiki/Quality_Sports_Investments). He scored his first goal for Sporting on 10 September 2011 against [Paços de Ferreira](/wiki/F.C._Pa%C3%A7os_de_Ferreira) in a 3–2 win. He was the [SJPF Player of the Month](/wiki/SJPF_Player_of_the_Month) for September 2011. Overall, he scored a total of 25 goals during the [2011–12](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Sporting_Clube_de_Portugal_season) season, including a hat-trick in the final game, a 3–2 win over [Braga](/wiki/S.C._Braga) at the [Estádio José Alvalade](/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Jos%C3%A9_Alvalade) on 12 May 2012.

In his second season in [Lisbon](/wiki/Lisbon), Van Wolfswinkel recorded another 14 league goals in 30 games, although his team missed out on European competition by finishing 7th. He scored a hat-trick, again in a 3–2 win over Braga, on 1 April 2013.

### Norwich City

On 22 March 2013, it was announced that Van Wolfswinkel had agreed a deal to join English club [Norwich City](/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.) for the [2013–14](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Norwich_City_F.C._season) season, a transfer worth €10 million, plus up to €2 million in bonuses. He joined the Canaries on 1 July on a four-year deal. He stated that the famous Netherlands player [Johan Neeskens](/wiki/Johan_Neeskens) had urged him to join Norwich, saying, "Ricky, you have to go. It's a great club for you. If you have a great feeling, do it."

He scored on his league debut on 17 August, in a 2–2 home draw for Norwich against [Everton](/wiki/Everton_F.C.). After the match, he said in an interview that [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) and [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) international [Robin van Persie](/wiki/Robin_van_Persie) also recommended Norwich to him. Van Persie was reported to have told Van Wolfswinkel that Norwich are "a difficult team to beat" and that the "crowd always get really behind them."

Van Wolfswinkel was involved in controversy in the 0–0 home draw against [Cardiff City](/wiki/Cardiff_City_F.C.) on 26 October 2013. After teammate [Alexander Tettey](/wiki/Alexander_Tettey) was injured, Cardiff goalkeeper [David Marshall](/wiki/David_Marshall_(Scottish_footballer)) sportingly put the ball out of play. Van Wolfswinkel quickly threw the ball to [Leroy Fer](/wiki/Leroy_Fer), who kicked the ball into the goal. This caused outrage amongst the Cardiff players, who started shoving both Van Wolfswinkel and Fer. The goal was disallowed despite it not technically being a violation of any rules. In February 2014, he was alleged to have been kicked by [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.)'s [Yaya Touré](/wiki/Yaya_Tour%C3%A9), who was investigated, and Van Wolfswinkel welcomed [The FA](/wiki/The_FA)'s verdict that Touré had done no wrong.

Van Wolfswinkel failed to score for the remainder of the season, making 25 Premier League appearances in total, while Norwich were relegated to the [Championship](/wiki/Football_League_Championship) after finishing with only 33 points.

#### Loans

On 5 August 2014, Van Wolfswinkel joined [Saint-Étienne](/wiki/AS_Saint-%C3%89tienne) of the French [Ligue 1](/wiki/Ligue_1) on a season-long loan. He stated that his goal was to work hard for the team and to be important for the club. Van Wolfswinkel scored his first goal for the club on 25 September, the first goal in the 1–1 home draw against [Bordeaux](/wiki/Girondins_de_Bordeaux). On 30 November, he scored the second goal as the team won 3–0 in the *[Derby du Rhône](/wiki/Derby_du_Rh%C3%B4ne)* against [Lyon](/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais), his team's first home win over their rivals in 20 years.

Upon his return to Norwich from his loan spell at Saint-Étienne, he scored against [Rotherham United](/wiki/Rotherham_United_F.C.) in the [League Cup](/wiki/Football_League_Cup) second round on 25 August 2015, a 2–1 away win.

On 31 August 2015, Van Wolfswinkel joined [Real Betis](/wiki/Real_Betis) of the Spanish [La Liga](/wiki/La_Liga) on a season-long loan. On 24 September, he made his first appearance in a 2–1 home defeat to [Deportivo La Coruña](/wiki/Deportivo_de_La_Coru%C3%B1a), replacing [Jorge Molina](/wiki/Jorge_Molina_Vidal) in the 59th minute. He scored his first two goals on 15 December in a [Copa del Rey](/wiki/Copa_del_Rey) tie against [Sporting Gijón](/wiki/Sporting_de_Gij%C3%B3n), which finished as a 3–3 draw, and on 19 April 2016, he finally scored his first La Liga goal in a 1–0 home victory over [Las Palmas](/wiki/UD_Las_Palmas).

### Return to Vitesse

On 28 July 2016, Van Wolfswinkel rejoined Vitesse for an undisclosed fee from Norwich. On 6 August, he marked his comeback with a goal in the 21st minute to give Vitesse a 1–0 lead in an eventual 4–1 away victory over [Willem II](/wiki/Willem_II_(football_club)).

He ultimately scored 20 goals in 32 games for the league season, one behind Golden Boot winner [Nicolai Jørgensen](/wiki/Nicolai_J%C3%B8rgensen). This included a hat-trick on 8 April 2017 in a 4–2 win over [Heerenveen](/wiki/Sc_Heerenveen) at the [GelreDome](/wiki/GelreDome).
He scored two goals in the final of the [KNVB Beker](/wiki/KNVB_Beker) against AZ on 30 April 2017 to lead the club to the title for the first time in its 125-year history.

### Basel

On 14 June 2017, it was announced that van Wolfswinkel would join Swiss side [Basel](/wiki/FC_Basel) on a three-year deal. He joined Basel's first team for their [2017–18 season](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_FC_Basel_season) under head coach [Raphaël Wicky](/wiki/Rapha%C3%ABl_Wicky). After playing in five test games van Wolfswinkel played his first team league debut for his new club on 22 July in the 2–0 away defeat against [Young Boys](/wiki/BSC_Young_Boys) in the [Stade de Suisse](/wiki/Stade_de_Suisse,_Wankdorf). He scored his first goal for his new club in the next match in the 3–1 home win in the [St. Jakob-Park](/wiki/St._Jakob-Park) against [Luzern](/wiki/FC_Luzern) on 30 July. On 10 August, van Wolfswinkel scored twice as Basel beat [Grasshoppers](/wiki/Grasshopper_Club_Z%C3%BCrich) 3–2; his first double strike for the club meant that he scored four times in his first four league games for the club.

Van Wolfswinkel injured himself during the [Champions League group stage](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_UEFA_Champions_League_group_stage#Group_A) home game against [Benfica](/wiki/S.L._Benfica) on 27 September. The game was won 5–0 and van Wolfswinkel had netted the third goal. However, the injury (a metatarsal fracture) meant that van Wolfswinkel could not play again in the first half of the season. He returned to the team after three months on 17 December in the 2–0 away win against Grasshoppers being substituted in five minutes from time.

Under trainer [Marcel Koller](/wiki/Marcel_Koller) Basel won the [Swiss Cup in the 2018–19 season](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Swiss_Cup). In the first round Basel beat FC Montlingen 3–0, in the second round [Echallens Région](/wiki/FC_Echallens) 7–2 and in the round of 16 [Winterthur](/wiki/FC_Winterthur) 1–0. In the quarter-finals [Sion](/wiki/FC_Sion) were defeated 4–2 after extra time and in the semi-finals [Zürich](/wiki/FC_Z%C3%BCrich) were defeated 3–1. All these games were played away from home. The final was held on 19 May 2019 in the [Stade de Suisse Wankdorf Bern](/wiki/Stade_de_Suisse) against [Thun](/wiki/FC_Thun). Striker [Albian Ajeti](/wiki/Albian_Ajeti) scored the first goal, [Fabian Frei](/wiki/Fabian_Frei) the second for Basel, then [Dejan Sorgić](/wiki/Dejan_Sorgi%C4%87) netted a goal for Thun, but the result remained 2–1 for Basel. Van Wolfswinkel played in five cup games and scored twice.

In August 2019, van Wolfswinkel underwent surgery for a brain aneurysm detected during a routine scan for concussion. After some complications and an injury in training, he returned to the squad in June the following year. His contract expired on 31 August 2020, but a week later, he signed for another two years. During their [2020–21 season](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FC_Basel_season) under head coach [Ciriaco Sforza](/wiki/Ciriaco_Sforza) made 25 league appearances, mainly as substitute. After the season van Wolfswinkel left the club. Between the years 2017 and 2020 he played a total of 148 games for Basel scoring a total of 54 goals. 88 of these games were in the [Swiss Super League](/wiki/Swiss_Super_League), 11 in the [Swiss Cup](/wiki/Swiss_Cup), 16 in the UEFA competitions (Champions League and Europa League) and 33 were friendly games. He scored 28 goals in the domestic league, 4 in the cup, 6 in the European games and the other 16 were scored during the test games.

### Twente

On 21 July 2021, Van Wolfswinkel signed a two-year contract with an option for a further season with [Twente](/wiki/FC_Twente), returning to the [Eredivisie](/wiki/Eredivisie). He scored on his competitive debut on 14 August, opening the scoring in a 2–1 league defeat away to [Fortuna Sittard](/wiki/Fortuna_Sittard). He finished the [2021–22](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_FC_Twente_season) campaign as Twente's leading scorer with 17 goals in 35 appearances, contributing to a fourth-place league finish and qualification for European play-offs.

On 27 March 2025, Van Wolfswinkel signed a one-year extension through 2026. He scored his 100th Eredivisie goal on 13 September 2025 against [NAC Breda](/wiki/NAC_Breda), becoming the 67th player to reach the milestone; per [Opta](/wiki/Opta_Sports), the span between his first and hundredth Eredivisie goals—16 years and 358 days—was the longest on record.

## International career

Van Wolfswinkel played several matches for the Netherlands' national youth sides in various age groups and in August 2010 was called up for the [senior national team](/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) for a friendly against [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team), during which he made his senior debut. He earned his second full international cap against [Indonesia](/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team) in June 2013.

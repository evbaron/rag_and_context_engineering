The **1998 NFL draft** was the procedure by which [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League) teams selected amateur [college football](/wiki/College_football) players. It is officially known as the NFL Annual Player Selection Meeting. The [draft](/wiki/NFL_draft) was held April 18–19, [1998](/wiki/1998_NFL_season), at [the Theater at Madison Square Garden](/wiki/The_Theater_at_Madison_Square_Garden) in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City). The league also held a [supplemental draft](/wiki/NFL_draft#Supplemental_draft) after the regular draft and before the regular season.

Before the draft, there was much debate in the media on if the [Indianapolis Colts](/wiki/1998_Indianapolis_Colts_season) would select [Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning) or [Ryan Leaf](/wiki/Ryan_Leaf) with the first overall pick. Both were considered excellent prospects and future franchise quarterbacks: Leaf was considered to have more upside and a stronger throwing arm, whereas Manning was considered a polished prospect who was NFL ready and more mature.

On the day of the draft, the Colts selected Manning due to Leaf's disdain for Indianapolis, with Leaf being selected second overall by the [San Diego Chargers](/wiki/1998_San_Diego_Chargers_season).

Manning went on to be a five-time [Most Valuable Player (MVP) award](/wiki/National_Football_League_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) winner (the most of any player in NFL history), a two-time [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl) champion ([in 2006 with the Colts](/wiki/2006_Indianapolis_Colts_season) and [in 2015 with the Denver Broncos](/wiki/2015_Denver_Broncos_season)), and was inducted into the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in [2021](/wiki/2021_NFL_season), while Leaf was out of the NFL by [2002](/wiki/2002_Seattle_Seahawks_season) after having earned a 4–17 record, and is considered one of the biggest draft busts in NFL history.

[Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning), taken first overall, won two Super Bowls and is considered one of the greatest quarterbacks of all time.

[Charles Woodson](/wiki/Charles_Woodson) was a nine-time [Pro Bowl](/wiki/Pro_Bowl) selection, four-time [All-Pro](/wiki/All-Pro), voted to the [NFL 2000s All-Decade Team](/wiki/NFL_2000s_All-Decade_Team) and inducted into the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in 2021

[Randy Moss](/wiki/Randy_Moss) is considered one of the greatest wide receivers of all time and was inducted into the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in 2018

Considered one of the best guards ever, [Alan Faneca](/wiki/Alan_Faneca) was a nine-time [Pro Bowl](/wiki/Pro_Bowl) selection, a six-time All-Pro, voted to the [NFL 2000s All-Decade Team](/wiki/NFL_2000s_All-Decade_Team) and inducted into the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in 2021

Receiver [Hines Ward](/wiki/Hines_Ward) won two Super Bowls with Pittsburgh, winning the [Super Bowl XL](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XL) [MVP](/wiki/Super_Bowl_MVP) award

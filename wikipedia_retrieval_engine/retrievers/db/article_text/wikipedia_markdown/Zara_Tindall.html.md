**Zara Anne Elizabeth Tindall** ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Phillips**; born 15 May 1981) is a British [equestrian](/wiki/Equestrianism), [Olympian](/wiki/Olympic_Games), and member of the [British royal family](/wiki/British_royal_family). She is the daughter of [Anne, Princess Royal](/wiki/Anne,_Princess_Royal), and [Captain Mark Phillips](/wiki/Captain_Mark_Phillips), and the eldest niece of [King Charles III](/wiki/King_Charles_III). At birth she was sixth in the [line of succession to the British throne](/wiki/Line_of_succession_to_the_British_throne) during the reign of her maternal grandmother, [Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II), and as of 2026 is 22nd.

Tindall won the individual eventing world title at [Aachen](/wiki/Aachen) in 2006 and was later named [BBC Sports Personality of the Year](/wiki/BBC_Sports_Personality_of_the_Year). She married former [rugby union](/wiki/Rugby_union) player [Mike Tindall](/wiki/Mike_Tindall) in 2011, and the couple have three children. The following year she carried the [Olympic flame](/wiki/Olympic_flame) at [Cheltenham Racecourse](/wiki/Cheltenham_Racecourse) on her horse Toytown, and went on to win a [team silver medal in eventing at the 2012 London Summer Olympics](/wiki/Equestrian_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_eventing), presented to her by her mother.

## Early life and education

Zara Anne Elizabeth Phillips was born at 8:15 pm on 15 May 1981 at [St Mary's Hospital, London](/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Hospital,_London). She was [baptised](/wiki/Baptised) on 27 July in the private chapel at Windsor Castle. Her first name was suggested by her uncle, [Charles](/wiki/Charles_III), the then [Prince of Wales](/wiki/Prince_of_Wales). Her godparents are her maternal uncle, [Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor](/wiki/Andrew_Mountbatten-Windsor); the [Countess of Lichfield](/wiki/Leonora_Anson,_Countess_of_Lichfield); Helen, Lady Stewart, the wife of [Sir Jackie Stewart](/wiki/Sir_Jackie_Stewart); [Andrew Parker Bowles](/wiki/Andrew_Parker_Bowles); and [Hugh Thomas](/wiki/Hugh_Thomas_(equestrian)). She has an elder brother, [Peter](/wiki/Peter_Phillips), and two younger half-sisters, Felicity Wade (née Tonkin), from her father's affair with Heather Tonkin; and Stephanie Phillips, from his second marriage to [Sandy Pflueger](/wiki/Sandy_Pflueger).

Phillips attended Beaudesert Park School in [Stroud](/wiki/Stroud_(district)), [Gloucestershire](/wiki/Gloucestershire), and [Port Regis School](/wiki/Port_Regis_School) in [Shaftesbury](/wiki/Shaftesbury), [Dorset](/wiki/Dorset), before following other members of the royal family in attending [Gordonstoun School](/wiki/Gordonstoun_School) in [Moray](/wiki/Moray), Scotland. During her school years, she excelled in a range of sports and represented her schools in [hockey](/wiki/Field_hockey), [athletics](/wiki/Sport_of_athletics), and [gymnastics](/wiki/Gymnastics). She later studied at the [University of Exeter](/wiki/University_of_Exeter) and qualified as a [physiotherapist](/wiki/Physiotherapist).

## Equestrianism

Tindall in 2007

After leaving university, Phillips began to pursue an equestrian career, following in the footsteps of her parents. In June 2003, she announced that she had secured a sponsorship agreement with [Cantor Index](/wiki/Cantor_Index), a [spread-betting](/wiki/Spread_betting) company, to support the costs of her competitive career. Later that year, she finished as runner-up at the [Burghley Horse Trials](/wiki/Burghley_Horse_Trials) in her first four-star event. Phillips missed the [2004 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics) in Athens after her horse was injured during training.

Riding [Toytown](/wiki/Toytown_(horse)), Phillips won individual and team gold medals at the 2005 [European Eventing Championship](/wiki/European_Eventing_Championship) at Blenheim. She went on to win individual gold and team silver at the [2006 FEI World Equestrian Games](/wiki/2006_FEI_World_Equestrian_Games) in [Aachen](/wiki/Aachen), Germany, becoming [Eventing World Champion](/wiki/Eventing_World_Championship) until 2010. Following her success in Germany, she was voted [BBC Sports Personality of the Year](/wiki/BBC_Sports_Personality_of_the_Year_Award), an award previously won by her mother in 1971. She was appointed [Member of the Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (MBE) in the [2007 New Year Honours](/wiki/2007_New_Year_Honours) for services to equestrianism. At the 2007 European Eventing Championships in Italy, she won team gold but did not defend her individual title in the show‑jumping phase.

The [British Olympic Association](/wiki/British_Olympic_Association) selected Phillips and Toytown for the 2008 Olympic Games in Hong Kong; however, Toytown sustained an injury during training and she withdrew from the team. On 25 October 2008, Phillips fell from her horse, Tsunami II, at the 15th fence of a cross‑country event in [Pau](/wiki/Pau,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Atlantiques), France, breaking her right collarbone. Tsunami II broke her neck after tipping over a hedge and was [put down](/wiki/Animal_euthanasia). In July 2010, [Musto](/wiki/Musto_(company)) launched a range of equestrian clothing designed by Phillips, named ZP176 after her team number when she first represented Great Britain.

Tindall competing at the 2012 Summer Olympics in London

Phillips competed at the [2012 London Olympic Games](/wiki/2012_London_Olympic_Games) on High Kingdom, winning a silver medal in the team event. She finished second at the 2013 [Luhmühlen Horse Trials](/wiki/Luhm%C3%BChlen_Horse_Trials) on High Kingdom, and at the 2014 World Equestrian Games she was part of the British team that won team silver. She stopped using her maiden name in March 2016 and competed as Zara Tindall for the first time during her unsuccessful attempt to qualify for the [2016 Rio Olympic Games](/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics). In 2017, Tindall finished third at the [Kentucky Three-Day Event](/wiki/Kentucky_Three-Day_Event) on High Kingdom, who retired from competition in 2018. In January 2020, she became a non‑executive director of [Cheltenham Racecourse](/wiki/Cheltenham_Racecourse). In August 2020, she was selected for her first appearance on the British team at the [FEI Nations Cup](/wiki/FEI_Nations_Cup) in Le Pin au Haras, France. In October 2021, she competed at the Maryland 5 Star at [Fair Hill](/wiki/Fair_Hill,_Maryland), finishing 11th on Class Affair. In May 2022, she won the advanced class at the Chatsworth Horse Trials, again riding Class Affair.

## Charity work

Tindall frequently supports and attends events for a range of charitable causes, particularly those connected with spinal injuries, equestrianism, and children's welfare. In 2005, she auctioned an evening gown worn at the London premiere of the film *[Seabiscuit](/wiki/Seabiscuit_(film))* to raise funds for tsunami relief. She later undertook a visit to New Zealand in her role as patron of The Catwalk Trust. From 1998 to 2005, she served as president of Club 16–24, an organisation that encourages young people to take an interest in horse racing. She is associated with Inspire, a Salisbury‑based medical research charity that supports people with spinal cord injuries, and is patron of Lucy Air Ambulance for Children, the UK's first dedicated air transfer service for critically ill infants and children.

Tindall has appeared at events for [The Caudwell Charitable Trust](/wiki/John_Caudwell), which supports children with special needs, disabilities, and serious illnesses. In 2006, she took part in a charity day for Cantor Index, 658 of whose employees were killed in the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks). The following year, she became patron of the Mark Davies Injured Riders Fund. For [Sport Relief 2008](/wiki/Sport_Relief), she posed for a portrait by the artist [Jack Vettriano](/wiki/Jack_Vettriano). In 2009, she attended a celebrity poker tournament in [Monaco](/wiki/Monaco) in aid of [Darfur](/wiki/Darfur), Sudan, and in October 2010 she attended a similar event in London in aid of [Cancer Research UK](/wiki/Cancer_Research_UK), of which she is patron. In 2011, she auctioned another evening gown in aid of the [Christchurch earthquake](/wiki/Christchurch_earthquake) appeal, raising £22,000. In 2013, she visited Stroud Maternity Ward to mark its 60th anniversary, and in 2014 she lent her support to the #bringbackourgirls campaign. In April 2020, Tindall participated in the Equestrian Relief initiative to help provide additional [personal protective equipment](/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment) for [National Health Service](/wiki/National_Health_Service) workers.

## Other activities

In June 2015, Tindall launched an equestrian-themed jewellery collection, named "Zara Phillips Collection", in collaboration with Australian designer John Calleija.
On 17 September 2022, during the period of [official mourning for Queen Elizabeth II](/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II), Tindall joined her brother and six cousins to mount [a 15-minute vigil](/wiki/Vigil_of_the_Princes) around the coffin of the Queen, as it [lay in state](/wiki/Lay_in_state) at [Westminster Hall](/wiki/Westminster_Hall). On 19 September, with her husband Mike and daughter Mia, she joined other family members at the state funeral.

## Personal life

Tindall with her husband in 2012

Phillips met [rugby union](/wiki/Rugby_union) player [Mike Tindall](/wiki/Mike_Tindall), who was then playing for the [England national team](/wiki/England_national_rugby_union_team), during the squad's Rugby World Cup-winning campaign in Australia in 2003. The couple became engaged in 2010. As required at the time by the [Royal Marriages Act 1772](/wiki/Royal_Marriages_Act_1772), the Queen gave her consent to the marriage at a meeting of the [Privy Council](/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom) on 10 May 2011. A celebration was held on the royal yacht [*Britannia*](/wiki/HMY_Britannia) prior to the wedding.

They married on 30 July 2011 at the [Canongate Kirk](/wiki/Canongate_Kirk) in Edinburgh, Scotland, with around 400 guests in attendance. Her [off-the-rack](/wiki/Off-the-rack) ivory silk dress, designed by [Stewart Parvin](/wiki/Stewart_Parvin), featured "a chevron-pleated bodice, a dropped waist, and a 'cathedral-length' train". The [Meander Tiara](/wiki/Elizabeth_II%27s_jewels#Meander_Tiara) was lent to her to secure the veil. A reception followed at the [Palace of Holyroodhouse](/wiki/Holyrood_Palace).

Tindall, and her husband [Mike](/wiki/Mike_Tindall), at the National Service of Thanksgiving for the [Platinum Jubilee of Elizabeth II](/wiki/Platinum_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II) in 2022

The Tindalls lived in [Cheltenham](/wiki/Cheltenham), Gloucestershire, before moving to Aston Farm, a seven-bedroom property adjoining the Princess Royal's [Gatcombe Park](/wiki/Gatcombe_Park) estate. Their first child, Mia Grace, was born on 17 January 2014 at [Gloucestershire Royal Hospital](/wiki/Gloucestershire_Royal_Hospital) and was 16th, later 23rd in the line of succession. A second daughter, Lena Elizabeth, was born on 18 June 2018 at Stroud Maternity Hospital and was 19th, later 24th, in the line of succession. Their son, Lucas Philip, was born on 21 March 2021 at the family home on the Gatcombe Park estate and was 22nd, later 25th, in the line of succession. Tindall is a godmother to [Prince George of Wales](/wiki/Prince_George_of_Wales), the son of her cousin [William, Prince of Wales](/wiki/William,_Prince_of_Wales).

In December 2000, before her marriage, Phillips was involved in a serious car crash near [Bourton-on-the-Water](/wiki/Bourton-on-the-Water), escaping injury after overturning her [Land Rover](/wiki/Land_Rover). In January 2020, she received a six‑month driving ban after accumulating 12 points on her licence, and was fined [victim surcharge](/wiki/Victim_surcharge) of £151".

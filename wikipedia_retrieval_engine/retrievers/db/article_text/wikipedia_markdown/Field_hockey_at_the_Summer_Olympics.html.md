# Field hockey at the Summer Olympics

## Infobox

- **IOC Code:** HOC
- **Governing body:** FIH
- **Events:** 2 (men: 1; women: 1)

[Field hockey](/wiki/Field_hockey) made its [debut](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1908_Summer_Olympics) at the [Modern Olympic Games](/wiki/Modern_Olympic_Games) as a men's competition in the [1908 Games](/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics) in [London](/wiki/London). It was removed from the Olympic schedule of the [Summer Olympic Games](/wiki/Summer_Olympic_Games) for the [1924 Paris Games](/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics) and was reintroduced in the [1928 Amsterdam Games](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics). The Women's field hockey was [introduced](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament) into the Olympic programme at the [1980 Moscow Olympics](/wiki/1980_Moscow_Olympics).

The [International Hockey Federation](/wiki/International_Hockey_Federation) (FIH) was founded in 1924 and governs the field hockey events. Until the [1988 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics), the tournaments were invitational, but a qualification system was introduced since the [1992 Barcelona Olympics](/wiki/Field_Hockey_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics). The last edition at [Paris](/wiki/2024_Paris_Olympics) was the 25th occurrence of the [men's event](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament) and 12th consecutive occurrence of [women's event](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament) in the Olympics.

India holds the record for the most appearances in the Olympic men's competitions (22) while Australia and Netherlands hold the same record in women's competitions (11). India leads the medal tally for the men's events with 13 medals while the Netherlands heads the women's events with 10 medals.

[Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) won the first two editions of the men's event in [1908](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1908_Summer_Olympics) and [1920](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics). [India](/wiki/India_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) won the gold medal in seven out of eight Olympics from [1928](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics) to [1964](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) with [Pakistan](/wiki/Pakistan_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) winning three gold and silver medals each between the 1956 and [1984 Games](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics). The matches are played on [artificial turf](/wiki/Artificial_turf) since [1976](/wiki/1976_Summer_Olympics). Since the late 1980s, European nations have dominated the field hockey events with [Germany](/wiki/Germany_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) and [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) having won three gold medals each in the men's event. In the women's event, [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) and [Australia](/wiki/Australia_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) have been dominant, having won five and three gold medals respectively since its introduction in 1980.

## History

[Field hockey](/wiki/Field_hockey) was introduced at the [Modern Olympic Games](/wiki/Modern_Olympic_Games) as a men's competition at the [1908 Games](/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics) in [London](/wiki/London). [Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) won the first two editions in [1908](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1908_Summer_Olympics) (as [England](/wiki/England_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team)) and [1920](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics). It was removed from the Olympic schedule of the [Summer Olympic Games](/wiki/Summer_Olympic_Games) for the [1924 Paris Games](/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics) because of the lack of an international sporting structure. The [International Hockey Federation](/wiki/International_Hockey_Federation) (FIH) was founded in Paris that year as a response to the same. Men's field hockey was added to the Olympics for the next Olympic Games in [1928 Games](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) held at [Amsterdam](/wiki/Amsterdam).

[Indian team](/wiki/India_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) that won the gold at the [1936 Berlin Olympics](/wiki/India_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics)

Starting in [1928](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics), [India](/wiki/India_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) won the gold medal in seven out of eight Olympics till [1964](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) including six consecutive gold medals from the [1928 Olympics](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) to [1956](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1956_Summer_Olympics). [Pakistan](/wiki/Pakistan_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) won its first gold medal in [1960](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1960_Summer_Olympics) and won three gold and silver medals each in a run lasting from 1956 to [1984](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics). [West Germany](/wiki/West_Germany_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) won the gold medal in the [1972 Munich Olympics](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1972_Summer_Olympics), for the first gold medal by a non-Asian country since 1928. Olympic field hockey games were first played on [artificial turf](/wiki/Artificial_turf) at the [1976 Montreal Games](/wiki/1976_Summer_Olympics) and was won by [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team). The domination of both the Asian countries came to an end in the late 1980s with India winning its last gold medal in the competition in [1980](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics) and Pakistan in 1984.

Though the International Federation of Women’s Hockey Associations (IFWHA) was founded way back in 1927, [Women's field hockey](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament) made its debut at the [Summer Olympics](/wiki/Summer_Olympics) only at the [1980 Moscow Olympics](/wiki/1980_Moscow_Olympics) and was won by [Zimbabwe](/wiki/Zimbabwe_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team). On 23 April 1983, women’s hockey programme was taken over by FIH and the IFWHA was dissolved. Great Britain won its first gold medal in the [men's event](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament) since 1920 in the [1988 Seoul Olympics](/wiki/1988_Seoul_Olympics).

[Netherlands women's team](/wiki/Netherlands_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) after winning gold at the [2012 Olympics](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament)

Until the 1988 Olympics, the tournament was invitational but FIH introduced a qualification system starting from the [1992 Barcelona Olympics](/wiki/Field_Hockey_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics). Since the 1990s, European countries have been dominant in the men's sport with the only exceptions being the wins by [Australia](/wiki/Australia_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in [2004](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament) and [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in [2016](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament). Both [Germany](/wiki/Germany_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) and [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) have won three gold medals each, with the only other European winner being [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in [2020](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament). In the women's events, [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) and [Australia](/wiki/Australia_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) have been dominant, having won five and three gold medals respectively since its introduction to the Olympic programme in [1980](/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics). The only other winners of the women's event were [Spain](/wiki/Spain_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in [1992](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), [Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in [2004](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament), and [Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in [2016](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament).

The last edition of both the [men's](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_tournament) and [women's events](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_tournament) were held as a part of the [2024 Paris Olympics](/wiki/2024_Paris_Olympics) with Netherlands winning both the events. India holds the record for the most appearances in the Olympic men's competitions (22) and [Spain](/wiki/Spain_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) has appeared in the most Olympic men's competitions (19) without winning the men's gold medal with the best place finishes being the silver medal won in [1980](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics), [1996](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics), and [2008](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics). Australia and Netherlands hold the record for the most appearances in the Olympic women's competitions (11). India leads the medal tally for the men's events with 13 medals including eight gold medals. Netherlands heads the women's events with 10 medals including five gold medals and also leads the overall medal tally with 20 medals (eight gold, six silver and six bronze).

[India](/wiki/India_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) against [Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) in the final of the [1948 Olympics](/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_1948_Summer_Olympics)

### Men

- Most appearances :  [India](/wiki/India_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) (22)
- Most consecutive appearances :  India (18, [1928 Amsterdam](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) – [2004 Athens](/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics))
- Most medals :  India (13)
- Most titles :  India (8)
- Longest winning streak : 30 matches  (India, [1928 Amsterdam](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) – [1960 Rome](/wiki/1960_Summer_Olympics))
- Most goals scored in a single tournament :  India (43, [1980 Moscow](/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics))
- Fewest goals conceded in a single tournament:  India (nil, ([1928 Amsterdam](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics), [1956 Melbourne](/wiki/1956_Summer_Olympics))
- Biggest margin of victory :  India 24–1 [United States](/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) ([1932 Los Angeles](/wiki/1932_Summer_Olympics))
- Biggest margin of victory at an Olympic final :  India 8–1 [Germany](/wiki/Germany_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team) ([1936 Berlin](/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics))
- Most appearances by a player:  [Teun de Nooijer](/wiki/Teun_de_Nooijer) ([Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team)),  [Ric Charlesworth](/wiki/Ric_Charlesworth) ([Australia](/wiki/Australia_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team)),  [David Alegre](/wiki/David_Alegre) and [Pol Amat](/wiki/Pol_Amat) ([Spain](/wiki/Spain_men%27s_national_field_hockey_team)) (5 appearances)
- Most goals scored by a player in a match :  [Roop Singh](/wiki/Roop_Singh) (India, 10 goals vs United States at [1932 Los Angeles](/wiki/1932_Summer_Olympics))
- Most goals scored by a player in an Olympic final :  [Balbir Singh Sr.](/wiki/Balbir_Singh_Sr.) (India, 5 goals vs Netherlands at [1952 Helsinki](/wiki/1952_Summer_Olympics))

### Women

- Most appearances :  [Australia](/wiki/Australia_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) and  [Netherlands](/wiki/Netherlands_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team) (11)
- Most consecutive appearances :  Australia and  Netherlands (11, [1980 Moscow](/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics) – [2024 Paris](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics))
- Most medals :  Netherlands (10)
- Most titles :  Netherlands (5)
- Most goals scored :  Netherlands (192)
- Most goals scored in a single tournament :  Netherlands (29, [2020 Tokyo](/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics))
- Most appearances by a player:  [Natascha Keller](/wiki/Natascha_Keller) ([Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_field_hockey_team), 5 appearances)
- Most goals by a player:  [Maartje Paumen](/wiki/Maartje_Paumen) (Netherlands, 19 goals)
- Most goals scored by a player in a single tournament:  [Maartje Paumen](/wiki/Maartje_Paumen) (Netherlands, 11 goals at [2008 Beijing](/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics))

Not to be confused with the southern constellation of [Sagittarius](/wiki/Sagittarius_(constellation)). For other uses, see [Sagitta (disambiguation)](/wiki/Sagitta_(disambiguation)).

**Sagitta** is a dim but distinctive [constellation](/wiki/Constellation) in the northern sky. Its name is [Latin](/wiki/Latin) for 'arrow', not to be confused with the significantly larger constellation [Sagittarius](/wiki/Sagittarius_(constellation)) 'the archer'. It was included among the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer [Ptolemy](/wiki/Ptolemy), and it remains one of the [88 modern constellations](/wiki/88_modern_constellations) defined by the [International Astronomical Union](/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union). Although it dates to antiquity, Sagitta has no star brighter than 3rd [magnitude](/wiki/Apparent_magnitude) and has the third-smallest area of any constellation.

[Gamma Sagittae](/wiki/Gamma_Sagittae) is the constellation's brightest star, with an apparent magnitude of 3.47. It is an aging [red giant](/wiki/Red_giant) star 90% as massive as the Sun that has cooled and expanded to a radius 54 times greater than it. [Delta](/wiki/Delta_Sagittae), [Epsilon](/wiki/Epsilon_Sagittae), [Zeta](/wiki/Zeta_Sagittae), and [Theta Sagittae](/wiki/Theta_Sagittae) are each [multiple](/wiki/Star_system) stars whose components can be seen in small telescopes. [V Sagittae](/wiki/V_Sagittae) is a [cataclysmic variable](/wiki/Cataclysmic_variable)—a [binary star system](/wiki/Binary_star) composed of a [white dwarf](/wiki/White_dwarf) accreting mass of a donor star that is expected to go [nova](/wiki/Nova) and briefly become the most [luminous](/wiki/Luminosity) star in the [Milky Way](/wiki/Milky_Way) and one of the brightest stars in our sky around the year 2083. Two star systems in Sagitta are known to have Jupiter-like planets, while a third—[15 Sagittae](/wiki/15_Sagittae)—has a [brown dwarf](/wiki/Brown_dwarf) companion.

## History

Sagitta can be seen above [Aquila](/wiki/Aquila_(constellation)) in this plate from *[Urania's Mirror](/wiki/Urania%27s_Mirror)* (1825).

The [ancient Greeks](/wiki/Classical_Greece) called Sagitta *Oistos* 'the arrow', and it was one of the 48 constellations described by [Ptolemy](/wiki/Ptolemy). It was regarded as the weapon that [Hercules](/wiki/Hercules) used to kill the eagle (*Aquila*) of [Jove](/wiki/Jupiter_(mythology)) that perpetually gnawed [Prometheus](/wiki/Prometheus)' liver. Sagitta is located beyond the north border of [Aquila](/wiki/Aquila_(constellation)), the Eagle. An amateur naturalist, polymath [Richard Hinckley Allen](/wiki/Richard_Hinckley_Allen) proposed that the constellation could represent the arrow shot by Hercules towards the adjacent [Stymphalian birds](/wiki/Stymphalian_birds) (which feature in [Hercules' sixth labour](/wiki/Labours_of_Hercules)) who had claws, beaks, and wings of iron, and who lived on human flesh in the marshes of [Arcadia](/wiki/Arcadia_(ancient_region))—denoted in the sky by the constellations Aquila the Eagle, [Cygnus 'the Swan'](/wiki/Cygnus_(constellation)), and [Lyra 'the Vulture'](/wiki/Lyra)—and still lying between them, whence the title [Herculea](/wiki/Herculea). Greek scholar [Eratosthenes](/wiki/Eratosthenes) claimed it as the arrow with which [Apollo](/wiki/Apollo) exterminated the [Cyclopes](/wiki/Cyclopes). The Romans named it Sagitta. In Arabic, it became *al-sahm* 'arrow', though this name became *Sham* and was transferred to [Alpha Sagittae](/wiki/Alpha_Sagittae) only. The Greek name has also been mistranslated as *hó istos* 'the loom' and thus in Arabic *al-nawl*. It was also called *al-'anaza* 'pike/javelin'.

## Characteristics

The four brightest stars make up an arrow-shaped [asterism](/wiki/Asterism_(astronomy)) located due north of the bright star [Altair](/wiki/Altair). Covering 79.9 square degrees and hence 0.194% of the sky, Sagitta ranks 86th of the [88 modern constellations](/wiki/88_modern_constellations) by area. Only [Equuleus](/wiki/Equuleus) and [Crux](/wiki/Crux) are smaller. Sagitta is most readily observed from the late spring to early autumn to northern hemisphere observers, with midnight [culmination](/wiki/Culmination) occurring on 17 July. Its position in the [Northern Celestial Hemisphere](/wiki/Northern_Celestial_Hemisphere) means that the whole [constellation](/wiki/Constellation) is visible to observers north of [69°S](/wiki/69th_parallel_south). Sagitta is bordered by [Vulpecula](/wiki/Vulpecula) to the north, [Hercules](/wiki/Hercules_(constellation)) to the west, Aquila to the south, and [Delphinus](/wiki/Delphinus_(constellation)) to the east. The three-letter abbreviation for the constellation, as adopted by the [International Astronomical Union](/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) in 1922, is "Sge"; American astronomer [Henry Norris Russell](/wiki/Henry_Norris_Russell), who devised the code, had to resort to using the [genitive](/wiki/Genitive_case) form of the name to come up with a letter to include ('e') that was not in the name of the constellation Sagittarius. The official constellation boundaries, as set by Belgian astronomer [Eugène Delporte](/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Joseph_Delporte) in 1930, are defined by a polygon of twelve segments (*illustrated in infobox*). In the [equatorial coordinate system](/wiki/Equatorial_coordinate_system), the [right ascension](/wiki/Right_ascension) coordinates of these borders lie between 18h 57.2m  and 20h 20.5m , while the [declination](/wiki/Declination) coordinates are between 16.08° and 21.64°.

## Features

### Stars

See also: [List of stars in Sagitta](/wiki/List_of_stars_in_Sagitta)

Celestial cartographer [Johann Bayer](/wiki/Johann_Bayer) gave [Bayer designations](/wiki/Bayer_designation) to eight stars, labelling them Alpha to Theta. English astronomer [John Flamsteed](/wiki/John_Flamsteed) added the letters x, mistaken as [Chi](/wiki/Chi_(letter)) (χ), y and z to 13, 14, and 15 Sagittae in his *Catalogus Britannicus*. All three were dropped by later astronomers [John Bevis](/wiki/John_Bevis) and [Francis Baily](/wiki/Francis_Baily).

#### Bright stars

Ptolemy saw the constellation's brightest star [Gamma Sagittae](/wiki/Gamma_Sagittae) as marking the arrow's head, while Bayer saw Gamma, Eta, and Theta as depicting the arrow's shaft. Gamma Sagittae is a [red giant](/wiki/Red_giant) of spectral type M0 III, and magnitude 3.47. It lies at a distance of 258±4 [light-years](/wiki/Light-years) from Earth. With around 90% of the Sun's mass, it has a radius 54 times that of the Sun and is 575 times as bright. It is most likely on the [red-giant branch](/wiki/Red-giant_branch) of its [evolutionary](/wiki/Stellar_evolution) lifespan, having exhausted its core hydrogen and now burning it in a surrounding shell.

[Delta Sagittae](/wiki/Delta_Sagittae) is the second-brightest star in the constellation and is a binary. Delta and Zeta depicted the spike according to Bayer. The Delta Sagittae system is composed of a [red supergiant](/wiki/Red_supergiant) of spectral type M2 II that has 3.9 times the Sun's mass and 152 times its radius and a blue-white B9.5V [main sequence](/wiki/Main_sequence) star that is 2.9 times as massive as the Sun. The two orbit each other every ten years. [Zeta Sagittae](/wiki/Zeta_Sagittae) is a triple star system, approximately 326 light-years from Earth. The primary and secondary are A-type stars.

In his *Uranometria*, Bayer depicted Alpha, [Beta](/wiki/Beta_Sagittae), and [Epsilon Sagittae](/wiki/Epsilon_Sagittae) as the fins of the arrow. Also known as Sham, Alpha is a yellow [bright giant](/wiki/Bright_giant) star of [spectral class](/wiki/Stellar_classification) G1 II with an [apparent magnitude](/wiki/Apparent_magnitude) of 4.38, which lies at a distance of 382±8 light-years from Earth. Four times as massive as the Sun, it has swollen and brightened to 21 times the Sun's radius and 340 times its [luminosity](/wiki/Luminosity). Also of magnitude 4.38, Beta is a G-type [giant](/wiki/Giant_star) located 420±10 light-years distant from Earth. Estimated to be around 129 million years old, it is 4.33 times as massive as the Sun, and has expanded to roughly 27 times its radius. Epsilon Sagittae is a [double star](/wiki/Double_star) whose component stars can be seen in a small telescope. With an apparent magnitude of 5.77, the main star is a 331-million-year-old yellow giant of spectral type G8 III around 3.09 times as massive as the Sun, that has swollen to 18.37+0.65
−0.88 its radius. It is 580±10 light-years distant. The visual companion of magnitude 8.35 is 87.4 [arcseconds](/wiki/Arcseconds) distant, but is an unrelated [blue supergiant](/wiki/Blue_supergiant) around 7,000 light-years distant from Earth.

[Eta Sagittae](/wiki/Eta_Sagittae) is an orange giant of spectral class K2 III with a magnitude of 5.09. Located 155.9±0.9 light-years from Earth, it has a 61.1% chance of being a member of the [Hyades](/wiki/Hyades_(star_cluster))–[Pleiades](/wiki/Pleiades) stream of stars that share a [common motion through space](/wiki/Stellar_kinematics). [Theta Sagittae](/wiki/Theta_Sagittae) is a double star system, with components 12 arcseconds apart visible in a small telescope. At magnitude 6.5, the brighter is a yellow-white main sequence star of spectral type F3 V, located 146.1±0.2 light-years from Earth. The 8.8-magnitude fainter companion is a main sequence star of spectral type G5 V. A 7.4-magnitude orange giant of spectral type K2 III is also visible 91[″](/wiki/Second_of_arc) from the binary pair, located 842±9 light-years away.

#### Variable stars

[James Webb Space Telescope](/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope) image of [WR 124](/wiki/WR_124) in Sagitta. [NIRCam](/wiki/NIRCam) and [MIRI](/wiki/Mid-Infrared_Instrument) composite

Variable stars are popular targets for amateur astronomers, their observations providing valuable contributions to understanding star behaviour. [R Sagittae](/wiki/R_Sagittae) is a member of the rare [RV Tauri variable](/wiki/RV_Tauri_variable) class of star. It ranges in magnitude from 8.2 to 10.4. It is around 8,100 light-years distant. It has a radius 61.2+12.4
−9.9 times that of the Sun, and is 2,329+744
−638 as luminous, yet most likely is less massive than the Sun. An aging star, it has moved on from the [asymptotic giant branch](/wiki/Asymptotic_giant_branch) of stellar evolution and is on its way to becoming a [planetary nebula](/wiki/Planetary_nebula). [FG Sagittae](/wiki/FG_Sagittae) is a "born again" star, a highly luminous star around 4,000 light-years distant from Earth. It reignited fusion of a helium shell shortly before becoming a white dwarf, and has expanded first to a blue supergiant and then to a K-class [supergiant](/wiki/Supergiant) in less than 100 years. It is surrounded by a faint (visual magnitude 23) planetary nebula, Henize 1–5, that formed when FG Sagittae first left the asymptotic giant branch.

[S Sagittae](/wiki/S_Sagittae) is a [classical Cepheid](/wiki/Classical_Cepheid) that varies from magnitude 5.24 to 6.04 every 8.38 days. It is a yellow-white supergiant that pulsates between spectral types F6 Ib and G5 Ib. Around 6 or 7 times as massive and 3,500 times as luminous as the Sun, it is located around 5,100 light-years from Earth. [HD 183143](/wiki/HD_183143) is a remote highly luminous star around 7,900 light-years away, that has been classified as a blue [hypergiant](/wiki/Hypergiant). [Infrared](/wiki/Infrared) bands of ionised [buckminsterfullerene](/wiki/Buckminsterfullerene) molecules have also been found in its spectrum. [WR 124](/wiki/WR_124) is a [Wolf–Rayet star](/wiki/Wolf%E2%80%93Rayet_star) moving at great speed surrounded by a nebula of ejected gas.

[U Sagittae](/wiki/U_Sagittae) is an eclipsing binary that varies between magnitudes 6.6 and 9.2 over 3.4 days, making it a suitable target for enthusiasts with small telescopes. There are two component stars—a blue-white star of spectral type B8 V and an ageing star that has cooled and expanded into a yellow subgiant of spectral type G4 III-IV. They orbit each other close enough that the cooler subgiant has filled its [Roche lobe](/wiki/Roche_lobe) and is passing material to the hotter star, and hence it is a [semidetached binary](/wiki/Semidetached_binary) system. The system is 900±10 light-years distant. Near U Sagittae is [X Sagittae](/wiki/X_Sagittae), a [semiregular variable](/wiki/Semiregular_variable) ranging between magnitudes 7.9 and 8.4 over 196 days. A [carbon star](/wiki/Carbon_star), X Sagittae has a surface temperature of 2,576 [K](/wiki/Kelvin).

Located near 18 Sagittae is [V Sagittae](/wiki/V_Sagittae), the prototype of the [V Sagittae variables](/wiki/V_Sagittae_variable), [cataclysmic variables](/wiki/Cataclysmic_variable) that are also [super soft X-ray sources](/wiki/Super_soft_X-ray_source). It is expected to become a [luminous red nova](/wiki/Luminous_red_nova) when the two stars merge around the year 2083, and briefly become the most luminous star in the [Milky Way](/wiki/Milky_Way) and one of the brightest stars in Earth's sky. [WZ Sagittae](/wiki/WZ_Sagittae) is another cataclysmic variable, composed of a [white dwarf](/wiki/White_dwarf) that has about 85% the mass of the Sun, and low-mass star companion that has been calculated to be a [brown dwarf](/wiki/Brown_dwarf) of spectral class L2 that is only 8% as massive as the Sun. Normally a faint object dimmer than magnitude 15, it flared up in 1913, 1946 and 1978 to be visible in binoculars. The [black widow pulsar](/wiki/Black_widow_pulsar) (B1957+20) is the second [millisecond pulsar](/wiki/Millisecond_pulsar) ever discovered. It is a massive [neutron star](/wiki/Neutron_star) that is [ablating](/wiki/Ablation) its brown dwarf-sized companion which causes the pulsar's radio signals to [attenuate](/wiki/Attenuation) as they pass through the outflowing material.

#### Stars with exoplanets

[Messier 71](/wiki/Messier_71) globular cluster

[HD 231701](/wiki/HD_231701) is a [yellow-white main sequence star](/wiki/F-type_main_sequence_star) hotter and larger than the Sun, with a [Jupiter](/wiki/Jupiter)-like [planet](/wiki/Planet) that was discovered in 2007 by the [radial velocity technique](/wiki/Methods_of_detecting_extrasolar_planets#Radial_velocity). The planet orbits at a distance of 0.57 [AU](/wiki/Astronomical_unit) from the star with a period of 141.6 days. It has a mass of at least 1.13 Jupiter masses.

HAT-P-34 is a star 1.392±0.047 times as massive as the Sun with 1.535+0.135
−0.102 times its radius and 3.63+0.75
−0.51 times its luminosity. With an apparent magnitude of 10.4, it is 819±9 light-years distant. A planet 3.328±0.211 times as massive as Jupiter was discovered transiting it in 2012. With a period of 5.45 days and a distance of 0.06 AU from its star, it has an estimated surface temperature of 1,520±60 K.

[15 Sagittae](/wiki/15_Sagittae) is a [solar analog](/wiki/Solar_analog)—a star similar to the Sun, with 1.08±0.04 times its mass, 1.115±0.021 times its radius and 1.338±0.03 times its luminosity. It has an apparent magnitude of 5.80. It has an L4 brown dwarf substellar companion that is around the same size as Jupiter but 69 times as massive with a surface temperature of between 1,510 and 1,850 K, taking around 73.3 years to complete an orbit around the star. The system is estimated to be 2.5±1.8 billion years old.

### Deep-sky objects

The band of the Milky Way and the [Great Rift](/wiki/Great_Rift_(astronomy)) within it pass through Sagitta, with Alpha, Beta and Epsilon Sagittae marking the Rift's border. Located between Beta and Gamma Sagittae is [Messier 71](/wiki/Messier_71), a very loose [globular cluster](/wiki/Globular_cluster) mistaken for some time for a dense [open cluster](/wiki/Open_cluster). At a distance of about 13,000 light-years from Earth, it was first discovered by the French astronomer [Philippe Loys de Chéseaux](/wiki/Philippe_Loys_de_Ch%C3%A9seaux) in the year 1745 or 1746. The loose globular cluster has a mass of around 53,000 [M☉](/wiki/Solar_mass) and a [luminosity](/wiki/Luminosity#In_astronomy) of approximately 19,000 [L☉](/wiki/Solar_luminosity).

There are two notable [planetary nebulae](/wiki/Planetary_nebula) in Sagitta: [NGC 6886](/wiki/NGC_6886) is composed of a hot central post-AGB star that has 55% of the Sun's mass yet 2,700±850 times its luminosity, with a surface temperature of 142,000 K, and surrounding nebula estimated to have been expanding for between 1,280 and 1,600 years, The nebula was discovered by [Ralph Copeland](/wiki/Ralph_Copeland) in 1884. The [Necklace Nebula](/wiki/Necklace_Nebula)—originally a close binary, one component of which swallowed the other as it expanded to become a giant star. The smaller star remained in orbit inside the larger, whose rotation speed increased greatly, resulting in it flinging its outer layers off into space, forming a ring with knots of bright gas formed from clumps of stellar material. It was discovered in 2005 and is around 2 light-years wide. It has a size of 0.35[′](/wiki/Minute_of_arc). Both nebulae are around 15,000 light-years from Earth.

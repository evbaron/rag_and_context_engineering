# Artstetten Castle

## Infobox

- **Location:** Artstetten-Pöbring, Austria

**Artstetten Castle** ([German](/wiki/German_language): *Schloss Artstetten*, pronounced [[ˌʃlɔs ˈaʁtʃtɛtn̩]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:De-Schloss_Artstetten.ogg)) is a historic *[Schloss](/wiki/Schloss)* near the [Wachau](/wiki/Wachau) valley in [Lower Austria](/wiki/Lower_Austria), in the municipality of [Artstetten-Pöbring](/wiki/Artstetten-P%C3%B6bring). It is the final resting place of [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria) and [Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg](/wiki/Sophie,_Duchess_of_Hohenberg), whose assassinations were the immediate cause of [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I).

## History

Artstetten Castle was owned by a number of families over the centuries until it was purchased in 1823 by Emperor [Francis I of Austria](/wiki/Francis_I_of_Austria). In 1852, [Archduke Franz Karl of Austria](/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Karl_of_Austria) became the owner, then he gave it in 1861 to his third son, [Archduke Karl Ludwig of Austria](/wiki/Archduke_Karl_Ludwig_of_Austria). In 1889, the property was given to [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria), who made extensive renovations to the castle.

Formerly used as a summer residence by members of the [Habsburg dynasty](/wiki/House_of_Habsburg-Lorraine), the castle is now the final resting place of Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his [morganatic](/wiki/Morganatic) wife [Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg](/wiki/Sophie,_Duchess_of_Hohenberg), who were [assassinated in 1914](/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand). Emperor [Franz Joseph I](/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria) refused to allow them to be interred in the traditional [Imperial Crypt](/wiki/Imperial_Crypt) at the [Capuchin Church, Vienna](/wiki/Capuchin_Church,_Vienna), because of their [morganatic marriage](/wiki/Morganatic_marriage). It also houses the Archduke Franz Ferdinand Museum.

After [Konopiště Castle](/wiki/Konopi%C5%A1t%C4%9B) was confiscated by [Czechoslovakia](/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic) in 1921 as part of the confiscation of Habsburg properties under the [Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye](/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_(1919)), Arstetten became the [Hohenberg family](/wiki/Hohenberg_family)'s primary residence. The castle remains their private property, and parts of the castle are open to the public for visits.

After the *[Anschluss](/wiki/Anschluss)* in 1938, [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) deported the Hohenbergs to [Dachau concentration camp](/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp) for their [anti-Nazi](/wiki/Anti-fascism) views and confiscated the castle. After the [Vienna offensive](/wiki/Vienna_offensive), the [Soviet occupation authorities](/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Austria) allowed the residents of Arstetten to elect [Duke Maximilian](/wiki/Maximilian,_Duke_of_Hohenberg) as their mayor, and the [Republic of Austria](/wiki/Austria) formally returned the castle to the family in 1949, being unable to retain ownership as the Hohenbergs were not covered by the [Habsburg Law](/wiki/Habsburg_Law).

After the death of [Franz, Duke of Hohenberg](/wiki/Franz,_Duke_of_Hohenberg) in 1977, the property was deeded to his eldest child, Princess Anita of Hohenberg. The Anita Hohenberg Trust was set up in 2003, and she and her family manage the property.

## Coin

[Artstetten coin](/wiki/Euro_gold_and_silver_commemorative_coins_(Austria)#2004_coinage) (obverse)

The castle was selected as a main motif for a high-value [euro collectors' coin](/wiki/Euro_gold_and_silver_commemorative_coins), the [Austrian 10 euro Castle of Artstetten commemorative coin](/wiki/Euro_gold_and_silver_commemorative_coins_(Austria)#2004_coinage), minted on 13 October 2004.

The coin's [obverse](/wiki/Obverse) shows the castle of Artstetten standing above the [Danube River](/wiki/Danube) on the threshold to the region of [Wachau](/wiki/Wachau). The reverse shows the entrance to the [crypt](/wiki/Crypt) of the Hohenberg family. There are two portraits on the left, showing Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his wife, Sophie.

## Burials

[Sarcophagus](/wiki/Sarcophagus) of Franz Ferdinand, with his wife's sarcophagus on the right

Notable burials in the castle's crypt include:

- [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria) (1863 – 1914), heir presumptive to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife [Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg](/wiki/Sophie,_Duchess_of_Hohenberg) (1868 – 1914), both assassinated by [Gavrilo Princip](/wiki/Gavrilo_Princip) in [Sarajevo](/wiki/Sarajevo) on 28 June 1914
- Stillborn third son of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie (1908)
- [Prince Ernst of Hohenberg](/wiki/Prince_Ernst_of_Hohenberg) (1904 – 1954), second son of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie
  - Marie-Thérèse Wood (1910 – 1985), wife of Ernst
- [Maximilian, 1st Duke of Hohenberg](/wiki/Maximilian,_Duke_of_Hohenberg) (1902 – 1962), eldest son of Franz Ferdinand and Sophie
  - Countess Maria von Waldburg zu Wolfegg und Waldsee, wife of Maximilian
- [Franz, 2nd Duke of Hohenberg](/wiki/Franz,_Duke_of_Hohenberg) (1927 – 1977), eldest son of Maximilian
  - [Princess Elisabeth, Duchess of Hohenberg](/wiki/Princess_Elisabeth,_Duchess_of_Hohenberg) (1922 – 2011), wife of Franz and a member of the [Grand Ducal Family of Luxembourg](/wiki/Grand_Ducal_Family_of_Luxembourg)
- [Georg, 3rd Duke of Hohenberg](/wiki/Georg,_Duke_of_Hohenberg) (1929 – 2018), second son of Maximilian

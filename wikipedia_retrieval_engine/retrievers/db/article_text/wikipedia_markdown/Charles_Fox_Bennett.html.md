**Charles James Fox Bennett** (June 11, 1793 – December 5, 1883) was a merchant and politician who successfully fought attempts to take [Newfoundland](/wiki/Colony_of_Newfoundland) into [Canadian Confederation](/wiki/Canadian_Confederation). Bennett was a successful businessman and one of the colony's richest residents with interests in the fisheries, distillery, brewery industry, and shipbuilding. His brother, [Thomas Bennett](/wiki/Thomas_Bennett_(magistrate)), a magistrate and member of Newfoundland's first House of Assembly, was a partner in the business.

Bennett became involved in politics in the 1840s as a leader of the colony's [Anglican](/wiki/Anglicanism) community and an opponent of [responsible government](/wiki/Responsible_government#Canada), an argument he lost when an alliance of [Catholics](/wiki/Catholics) and non-Anglican Protestants persuaded the [Colonial Office](/wiki/Colonial_Office) to grant Newfoundland self-government.

In the 1860s, he led the [Anti-Confederation Party](/wiki/Anti-Confederation_Party) opposing the proposals by Sir [Frederick Carter](/wiki/Frederick_Carter) to join Canada. Bennett's party defeated Carter's Conservatives on the Confederation issue in the [1869 election](/wiki/1869_Newfoundland_general_election), allowing Bennett to form a government in 1870. However, as [Premier](/wiki/Premier_of_Newfoundland_and_Labrador) he was unable to keep his party united, and in 1874, resigned. This allowed Carter to return to power. The issue of Confederation had become a moot point and would not be seriously raised again until the [Great Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression).

Bennet also commissioned extensive mineral surveys along the coasts, and in the 1860s developed the prosperous copper mine at [Tilt Cove](/wiki/Tilt_Cove) in [Notre Dame Bay](/wiki/Notre_Dame_Bay).

Bennett's anti-Confederates reformed themselves into the colony's [Liberal Party](/wiki/Liberal_parties_in_pre-confederation_Newfoundland).

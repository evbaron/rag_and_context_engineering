**Walt Disney Imagineering Research & Development, Inc.**—commonly referred to as **Walt Disney Imagineering**, **Imagineering**, **or WDI**—is the [research and development](/wiki/Research_and_development) arm of [the Walt Disney Company](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company), responsible for the creation, design, and construction of [Disney theme parks](/wiki/Disney_Experiences) and attractions worldwide. The company also operates [Disney Live Entertainment](/wiki/Disney_Live_Entertainment) and [the Muppets Studio](/wiki/The_Muppets_Studio) and manages Disney's properties, from [Walt Disney Studios](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_(Burbank)) in Burbank to [New Amsterdam Theatre](/wiki/New_Amsterdam_Theatre) and [Times Square Studios](/wiki/Times_Square_Studios) Ltd. in New York City.

Founded on December 16, 1952 by [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) to oversee the production of [Disneyland Park](/wiki/Disneyland), it was originally known as **Walt Disney, Inc.**, then [WED Enterprises](/wiki/Retlaw_Enterprises), from the initials of "Walter Elias Disney", Disney's full name. Headquartered in [Glendale, California](/wiki/Glendale,_California), Imagineering is composed of "Imagineers", who are illustrators, architects, engineers, lighting designers, show writers and graphic designers.

The term "Imagineering", a [portmanteau](/wiki/Portmanteau), was introduced in the 1940s by [Alcoa](/wiki/Alcoa) to describe its blending of imagination and engineering, and used by [Union Carbide](/wiki/Union_Carbide) in an in-house magazine in 1957, with an article by Richard F. Sailer called "BRAINSTORMING IS IMAGination engINEERING". Disney filed for a trademark for the term in 1989, claiming first use of the term in 1962. Imagineering is a [registered trademark](/wiki/Registered_trademark_symbol) of [Disney Enterprises, Inc.](/wiki/List_of_assets_owned_by_The_Walt_Disney_Company#Corporate)

## History

### WED Enterprises

Walt Disney, Inc. (WDI) was formed by [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) on December 16, 1952, with an engineering division tasked with designing [Disneyland](/wiki/Disneyland). In light of objections from his brother [Roy O. Disney](/wiki/Roy_O._Disney) as well as those of potential stockholders, WDI was renamed *WED Enterprises* in 1953 based on Disney's initials. In 1961, WED moved into the [Grand Central Business Park](/wiki/Grand_Central_Airport_(California)). [WED Enterprises](/wiki/Retlaw_Enterprises)'s theme park design and architectural group became so integral to the Disney studio's operations that Walt Disney Productions bought it on February 5, 1965, along with the WED Enterprises name.

### Imagineering

The unit was renamed as of January 1986 to Walt Disney Imagineering. In 1996, [Disney Development Company](/wiki/Disney_Development_Company), the Disney conglomerate's real estate development subsidiary, merged into Imagineering.

Imagineering created [Disney Fair](/wiki/Disney_Theatrical_Group#Background), a U.S. traveling attraction, which premiered in September 1996. With poor attendance, the fair was pulled after a few stops. Disney Entertainment Projects (Asia Pacific), Inc., a new Disney Asian Pacific subsidiary, selected a renamed fair called DisneyFest as its first project, taking it to Singapore to open there on October 30, 1997.

By 1997, Imagineers were in several buildings in Grand Central Business Park when Disney purchased the park. In September 1999, Disney Imagineering announced the Grand Central Creative Campus redesign of the industrial park with a new office-studio complex anchored by Disney Imagineering. Some of the buildings were demolished to make way for new buildings. The additional space would be for sound stages, production facilities and offices.

As part of [the Walt Disney Company](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company)'s March 2018 strategic reorganization, Walt Disney Parks and Resorts merged with Disney Consumer Products and Interactive Media segments into [Disney Parks, Experiences and Products](/wiki/Disney_Experiences) (DPEP), giving Disney Imagineering oversight of merchandise, games and publishing development.

In July 2021, DPEP chairman [Josh D'Amaro](/wiki/Josh_D%27Amaro) announced that approximately 2,000 DPEP positions would be transferred over the next couple of years to the [Lake Nona](/wiki/Lake_Nona,_Orlando,_Florida) area of [Orlando, Florida](/wiki/Orlando,_Florida). In November 2021, it was reported that as many as 90% of the positions to be transferred are Walt Disney Imagineering positions, as DPEP relocates Imagineering's headquarters from Glendale to a new 60-acre corporate campus in Lake Nona. The Imagineering positions that were reportedly exempt from relocation were those dedicated to Disneyland or certain international parks. The relocation was reportedly motivated in part by $570 million in [tax breaks](/wiki/Tax_incentive) from the state of Florida, as well as Florida's business-friendly climate, lower [cost of living](/wiki/Cost_of_living), and lack of a [state income tax](/wiki/State_income_tax). This relocation was cancelled in May 2023 amidst [a feud](/wiki/Disney_v._DeSantis) with Florida governor [Ron DeSantis](/wiki/Ron_DeSantis).

## Principles

WED Enterprises engineers examining a model for [Space Mountain](/wiki/Space_Mountain_(Magic_Kingdom)) at [Magic Kingdom](/wiki/Magic_Kingdom) in 1973

New concepts and improvements are often created to fulfill specific needs. For instance, the [Soarin' Over California](/wiki/Soarin%27_Over_California) ride system was designed to help guests experience the sensation of flight. During development, Imagineer Mark Sumner found an [erector set](/wiki/Erector_Set) in his attic, which quickly inspired the solution to create this experience. The ride effectively simulates [hang gliding](/wiki/Hang_gliding). One of Imagineering's techniques, "blue sky speculation", is a process in which ideas are generated without limitations. Imagineers may develop a bold idea in extreme detail, initially disregarding budgetary or physical constraints. It can take up to five years for an idea to turn into a finished attraction. The company considers this the beginning of a design process, believing, "if it can be dreamt, it can be built".

WDI partnered with the [Khan Academy](/wiki/Khan_Academy) to create a series of online video classes called [Imagineering in a Box](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/hass-storytelling/imagineering-in-a-box), to allow students to "explore different aspects of theme park design, from characters to ride development..." The classes, which are organized into three main categories (*Creating Worlds*, *Designing Attractions*, and *Bringing Characters to Life*), are presented by WDI employees using multimedia lessons and exercises.

### The Imagineering Pyramid

Past Disney Imagineers have shared 15 principles of Disney Imagineering, divided into five tiers. These are:

Tier 1: Foundations of Imagineering

 1. It All Begins with a Story

 2. Creative Intent

 3. Attention to Detail

 4. Theming

 5. Long, Medium, and Close Shots

Tier 2: Wayfinding

 6. Wienies

 7. Transitions

 8. [Storyboards](/wiki/Storyboards)

 9. Pre-Shows and Post-Shows

Tier 3: Visual Communication

 10. [Forced Perspective](/wiki/Forced_Perspective)

 11. "Read"-ability

 12. Kinetics

Tier 4: Making It Memorable

 13. The "[It's a Small World](/wiki/It%27s_a_Small_World)" Effect

 14. [Hidden Mickeys](/wiki/Hidden_Mickey)

Tier 5: Walt’s Cardinal Rule

 15. Plussing

[Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) coined the term "plussing" for the process of constantly striving to perfect Imagineering work. Disney believed that there is always room for innovation and improvement, stating, "Disneyland will never be completed as long as there's imagination left in the world." This sometimes takes the form of returning to previously abandoned ideas, such as the rework of the Museum of the Weird wax museum concept into the present [The Haunted Mansion](/wiki/The_Haunted_Mansion).

### The Art of the Show

Disney theme parks are storytelling and visual experiences, also known as “The Art of the Show.” The use of theming, atmosphere, and attention to detail are essential in the Disney experience. Creative director [John Hench](/wiki/John_Hench) noted the similarities between theme park design and film making, such as the use of techniques, including [forced perspective](/wiki/Forced_perspective). One example of forced perspective is [Cinderella Castle](/wiki/Cinderella_Castle) in [Magic Kingdom](/wiki/Magic_Kingdom) at [Walt Disney World](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World). The scale of architectural elements is much smaller in the upper reaches of the castle compared to the foundation, making it seem significantly taller than its actual height of 189 feet.

The attraction, [Pirates of the Caribbean](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean_(attraction)), evokes a “rollicking buccaneer adventure,” according to Hench. In contrast, the [Disney Cruise Line](/wiki/Disney_Cruise_Line) ships create an elegant seafaring atmosphere. Minor details in theme park shops and restaurants are crucial; these include the menus, names of the dishes and the [Cast Members](/wiki/Cast_Members)’ costumes. When guests walk down the area of [Main Street, U.S.A.](/wiki/Main_Street,_U.S.A.), they are likely to notice a bakery fragrance, reminiscent of [suburban America](/wiki/Middle_America_(United_States)) in the 1900s. In addition to theme parks, Imagineering has devised retail stores, galleries, and hotels which have "stories" and create a specific mood. For instance, the [Disney's Contemporary Resort](/wiki/Disney%27s_Contemporary_Resort) features an [A-frame](/wiki/A-frame) structure, modern décor and futuristic features including a quiet [monorail](/wiki/Monorail) in the lobby. These details reinforce the hotel's contemporary nature.

In 2010, Disney Educational Products produced a series of videos called *The Science of Disney Imagineering*. Each video was presented by Imagineer Asa Kalama and focused on a different science subject. Each video featured at least one Disney attraction, to show how science was used in them. These science subjects include [Gravity](/wiki/Gravity), [Trajectory](/wiki/Trajectory), [Levers](/wiki/Levers) & [Pulleys](/wiki/Pulleys), [Fluids](/wiki/Fluids), [Energy](/wiki/Energy), [Design](/wiki/Design) & [Models](/wiki/Physical_model), [Magnetism](/wiki/Magnetism), [Motion](/wiki/Motion_(physics)), [Animal Adaptations: Communication](/wiki/Animal_communication), [Friction](/wiki/Friction), and [Electricity](/wiki/Electricity).

### Mickey's Ten Commandments

In 1991, [Marty Sklar](/wiki/Marty_Sklar) (then-president of Imagineering) presented ten commandments attributed to Mickey Steinberg (the vice president of Imagineering). They are:

1. Know your audience
2. Wear your guest's shoes (don't forget the human factors; try to experience the parks from the guests' point of view)
3. Organize the flow of people and ideas (ensure experiences tell a story that is organized and logically laid out)
4. Create a "Wienie" (Walt Disney's term for a "visual magnet")
5. Communicate with visual literacy (use a dominant color or shape or building to reinforce a theme)
6. Avoid overload—create turn-ons (do not offer too much detailed information)
7. Tell one story at a time (put one 'big idea' in each show so guests leave with a clear understanding of the theme)
8. Avoid contradictions—maintain identity (avoid irrelevant or contradicting elements; make sure the audience has a clear idea of what is being said)
9. For every ounce of treatment, provide a ton of treat (take advantage of the distinction of the theme park, which is that it encourages active participation, compared to passive entertainment)
10. Keep it up (do not become complacent or allow things to run down)

## Innovation

[Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney) in 1966, inspecting plastic heads for use in the Disneyland iteration of [Pirates of the Caribbean](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean_(attraction))

Imagineering has been granted over 300 patents in areas such as advanced audio systems, [fiber optics](/wiki/Fiber_optics), interactive technology, live entertainment, ride systems and special effects. Imagineering pioneered technological advances such as the [Circle-Vision 360°](/wiki/Circle-Vision_360%C2%B0) film technique and the [FastPass](/wiki/FastPass) virtual queuing system.

### Audio-Animatronics

Main article: [Audio-Animatronics](/wiki/Audio-Animatronics)

Imagineering is known for its development of Audio-Animatronics, a form of robotics, used in shows and theme park attractions that animate objects in [three dimension (3D)](/wiki/3D_film). The idea originated from Walt Disney's fascination with a mechanical bird that he purchased in [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans), which later led to the development of the attraction, [The Enchanted Tiki Room](/wiki/The_Enchanted_Tiki_Room). The attraction, which featured singing Audio-Animatronic birds, was the first to use this technology. In the [1964 World's Fair](/wiki/1964_World%27s_Fair), a 3D figure of [Abraham Lincoln](/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) was represented. The animated Lincoln delivered part of the [Gettysburg Address](/wiki/Gettysburg_Address) for the "[Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln](/wiki/Great_Moments_with_Mr._Lincoln)" exhibit, the first human Audio-Animatronic figure.

Today, Audio-Animatronics are featured in many attractions, including [Pirates of the Caribbean](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean_(attraction)), [The Haunted Mansion](/wiki/The_Haunted_Mansion), [The Hall of Presidents](/wiki/The_Hall_of_Presidents), [Country Bear Musical Jamboree](/wiki/Country_Bear_Jamboree), [*Star Wars*: Rise of the Resistance](/wiki/Star_Wars:_Rise_of_the_Resistance), and [Tiana's Bayou Adventure](/wiki/Tiana%27s_Bayou_Adventure). Some Audio-Animatronics figures can interact with park guests, such as [Mr. Potato Head](/wiki/List_of_Toy_Story_characters#Mr._Potato_Head) at [Toy Story Mania!](/wiki/Toy_Story_Mania!).

### WEDway

[WEDway](/wiki/WEDway) is a [people mover](/wiki/People_mover) system using [linear induction motor](/wiki/Linear_motor) (LIM) technology to propel vehicles along a pair of steel rails. This system was developed in the company's early years. The system is in operation at [Walt Disney World Resort](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World_Resort)'s [Magic Kingdom](/wiki/Magic_Kingdom) and [George Bush Intercontinental Airport](/wiki/George_Bush_Intercontinental_Airport) in [Houston](/wiki/Houston), [Texas](/wiki/Texas), [United States](/wiki/United_States). From 1967 to 1995, [Disneyland](/wiki/Disneyland) utilized a version of this system which had rubber wheels placed every 9 feet along the guide-way.

## Other projects

Former creative director [John Hench](/wiki/John_Hench) designed the "Tower of Nations" for the opening and closing ceremony of the [1960 Winter Olympics](/wiki/1960_Winter_Olympics), whilst Walt Disney served as Pageantry Committee chairman.

Imagineering has collaborated with [Disney Consumer Products](/wiki/Disney_Consumer_Products) on a number of projects for [Disney Stores](/wiki/Disney_Store). The first store, in [Glendale](/wiki/Glendale,_California), was designed and constructed by a group of architectural Imagineers. Imagineering developed the now-defunct Walt Disney Gallery at the Main Place Mall in [Santa Ana, California](/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California), and a [Roman](/wiki/Roman_Empire) themed Disney Store at [The Forum Shops at Caesars](/wiki/The_Forum_Shops_at_Caesars) in [Las Vegas](/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley). After the purchase of the Disney Stores by [The Children's Place](/wiki/The_Children%27s_Place) in 2004, Imagineering helped design an exclusive chain of flagship stores, called [World of Disney](/wiki/World_of_Disney). These are located in resorts, [Lake Buena Vista, Florida](/wiki/Lake_Buena_Vista,_Florida) and [Anaheim, California](/wiki/Anaheim,_California), as well as New York City. Another flagship store arrived at [Disneyland Paris](/wiki/Disneyland_Paris) in 2012. An overhaul of [Disney Stores](/wiki/Disney_Store) was planned in 2009 with the help of [Apple](/wiki/Apple_Inc.) CEO [Steve Jobs](/wiki/Steve_Jobs). Disney hoped to transition from a traditional [retail](/wiki/Retail) model to an [interactive](/wiki/Interactive_media) entertainment hub.

In the 1990s, Imagineering designed the 24,000 square feet (2,200 m2) [Club Disney](/wiki/Club_Disney) interactive family fun center in [Thousand Oaks, California](/wiki/Thousand_Oaks,_California). Although now closed, it was the first of several location-based entertainment (LBE) venues. In 1998, [DisneyQuest](/wiki/DisneyQuest), an 80,000 square feet (7,400 m2) high-tech, virtual reality arcade was launched at [Disney Springs](/wiki/Disney_Springs) in [Lake Buena Vista, Florida](/wiki/Lake_Buena_Vista,_Florida). Another DisneyQuest in [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago) was launched a year later. In 2007, Imagineering oversaw design and construction of ships, [Disney Dream](/wiki/Disney_Dream) and [Disney Fantasy](/wiki/Disney_Fantasy). They also helped design exhibitions for the [Autry Museum of Western Heritage](/wiki/Autry_National_Center) in [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California) and developed the Encounter Restaurant, which is located at the top of [Theme Building](/wiki/Theme_Building) in [Los Angeles International Airport](/wiki/Los_Angeles_International_Airport). Moreover, they provided exhibits for the [Port Discovery](/wiki/Port_Discovery_(museum)) children's museum at the [Inner Harbor](/wiki/Inner_Harbor) in [Baltimore](/wiki/Baltimore), [Maryland](/wiki/Maryland), as well as the "Below Deck" sound show depicting [Blackbeard](/wiki/Blackbeard)'s final battle as part of the Pirate and Treasure Museum in [St. Augustine, Florida](/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida).

From 1995 to 1997, Imagineering oversaw the renovation of the [New Amsterdam Theatre](/wiki/New_Amsterdam_Theatre) in New York City. Imagineering also remodeled the [Times Square Studios](/wiki/Times_Square_Studios) in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) in 1996, following the acquisition of [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company). In 1997, Disney purchased the [California Angels](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Angels_of_Anaheim) and renamed the team to Anaheim Angels. Shortly after, Imagineering renovated the [Anaheim Stadium](/wiki/Angel_Stadium_of_Anaheim). Imagineering worked with a charity, [For Inspiration and Recognition of Science and Technology](/wiki/For_Inspiration_and_Recognition_of_Science_and_Technology), to create the teaser video and the story, as well as the theming of the 2016 [FIRST Robotics Competition](/wiki/FIRST_Robotics_Competition), [FIRST Stronghold](/wiki/FIRST_Stronghold).

## Corporate locations

Map of [Grand Central Creative Campus](/wiki/Grand_Central_Airport_(California)) in Glendale, California

Since the 1960s, Imagineering's headquarters have been located in nondescript office buildings on the site of the former [Grand Central Airport](/wiki/Grand_Central_Airport_(United_States)) in [Glendale, California](/wiki/Glendale,_California), about two miles (3.2 km) east of [Disney's corporate headquarters](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Studios_(Burbank)) in [Burbank](/wiki/Burbank,_California). Many of these employees were set to be relocated to a new facility in [Lake Nona, Orlando, Florida](/wiki/Lake_Nona,_Orlando,_Florida), however the move was canceled in May 2023 amid employee objections and disputes with Florida's state government. There are field offices at [Epcot](/wiki/Epcot) and [Disney's Hollywood Studios](/wiki/Disney%27s_Hollywood_Studios) at the [Walt Disney World Resort](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World_Resort). There are also offices located at:

- Walt Disney Imagineering Disneyland Field Office, [Disneyland Resort](/wiki/Disneyland_Resort)
- Tokyo Disney Resort Administration Building, [Tokyo Disney Resort](/wiki/Tokyo_Disney_Resort)
- Walt Disney Imagineering Hong Kong Site Office, [Hong Kong Disneyland Resort](/wiki/Hong_Kong_Disneyland_Resort)
- Walt Disney Imagineering Germany GmbH, [Meyer Werft](/wiki/Meyer_Werft) [Papenburg, Germany](/wiki/Papenburg,_Germany)

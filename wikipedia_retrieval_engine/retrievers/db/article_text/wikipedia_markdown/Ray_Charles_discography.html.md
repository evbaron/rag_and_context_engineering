# Ray Charles discography

The discography of American musician [Ray Charles](/wiki/Ray_Charles) consists of 54 studio albums, 7 live albums, 39 compilations albums, and 127 singles.

## Overview

Ray Charles first appeared on a *Billboard* chart in 1949, when — as part of the Maxin Trio with G.D. McKee and Milton Garred — he charted his first single with "Confession Blues." It reached No. 2 on the R&B chart, then called the Most-Played Juke Box Race Records. In 1952, Charles signed with [Atlantic Records](/wiki/Atlantic_Records) and went on to top *Billboard*'s pop, country, R&B, jazz, and dance charts.

In the early 1960s, after leaving Atlantic Records to sign with [ABC–Paramount](/wiki/ABC_Records), he negotiated for ownership of his own master recordings. He also established his own labels. [Tangerine](/wiki/Tangerine_Records_(1962)) came first, which evolved into CrossOver Records. As a songwriter, Charles penned nearly 200 songs. He also operated his own publishing companies, Tangerine Music and Racer Music.

On the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100), Charles had 80 hits between 1958 (the year the chart began) and 1990, when "[I'll Be Good to You](/wiki/I%27ll_Be_Good_to_You)" became his last Hot 100 single, reaching No. 18. His biggest hit, "[I Can't Stop Loving You](/wiki/I_Can%27t_Stop_Loving_You)", spent five weeks at No. 1 in 1962. He also topped the Hot 100 with the singles "[Hit the Road Jack](/wiki/Hit_the_Road_Jack)" and "[Georgia on My Mind](/wiki/Georgia_on_My_Mind)". Among all acts, he charted the third-most singles on the Hot 100; only [Elvis Presley](/wiki/Elvis_Presley) (with 108) and [James Brown](/wiki/James_Brown) (91) had more.

On [*Billboard*'s R&B chart](/wiki/Hot_R%26B/Hip-Hop_Songs), Charles had 86 hits, including 11 chart-toppers such as "[I've Got a Woman](/wiki/I_Got_a_Woman)", "[What'd I Say (Part 1)](/wiki/What%27d_I_Say_(song))", "Hit the Road Jack", and "[You Are My Sunshine](/wiki/You_Are_My_Sunshine)."

Charles also reached No. 1 on the [Hot Country Songs](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) chart in 1985 with "[Seven Spanish Angels](/wiki/Seven_Spanish_Angels)", a duet with [Willie Nelson](/wiki/Willie_Nelson). The single appears on Charles' duets album, *[Friendship](/wiki/Friendship_(Ray_Charles_album))*, which reached No. 1 on Top Country Albums. His 1962 album, *[Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music](/wiki/Modern_Sounds_in_Country_and_Western_Music)*, became his first album to top the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200).

In 1990, Charles had a No. 1 on the [Hot Dance Club Songs](/wiki/Dance_Club_Songs) chart with "I'll Be Good to You", a collaboration with [Quincy Jones](/wiki/Quincy_Jones) and [Chaka Khan](/wiki/Chaka_Khan).

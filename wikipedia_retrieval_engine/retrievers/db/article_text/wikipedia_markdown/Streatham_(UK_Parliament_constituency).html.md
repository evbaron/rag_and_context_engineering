# Streatham (UK Parliament constituency)

## Infobox

- **County:** Greater London
- **Electorate:** 71,913 (December 2010) [ 1 ]
- **Major settlements:** Clapham Common, Streatham Hill, Brixton Hill, Streatham South
- **Seats:** One
- **Created from:** Wandsworth
- **Replaced by:** Clapham and Brixton Hill, Streatham and Croydon North

**Streatham** was a [constituency](/wiki/List_of_United_Kingdom_Parliament_constituencies) represented in the [House of Commons](/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_the_United_Kingdom) of the [UK Parliament](/wiki/UK_Parliament).

In the [2016 EU referendum](/wiki/2016_EU_referendum), Streatham was estimated to have [voted](/wiki/Results_of_the_2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum_by_constituency) to remain in the European Union by 79%. This was the second highest remain vote in the United Kingdom, behind [Vauxhall](/wiki/Vauxhall_(UK_Parliament_constituency)).

Further to the completion of the [2023 review of Westminster constituencies](/wiki/2023_review_of_Westminster_constituencies), the seat was abolished. The four southern wards, comprising the town of [Streatham](/wiki/Streatham), were included in the new constituency of [Streatham and Croydon North](/wiki/Streatham_and_Croydon_North). Northern areas ([Brixton Hill](/wiki/Brixton_Hill_(ward)), [Clapham Common](/wiki/Clapham_Common_(ward)), [Thornton](/wiki/Thornton_(ward)), and [Tulse Hill](/wiki/Tulse_Hill_(ward))) form part of the new constituency of [Clapham and Brixton Hill](/wiki/Clapham_and_Brixton_Hill).

### Summary

Streatham was a long constituency comprising the south-west portion of the [London Borough of Lambeth](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Lambeth). The town of [Streatham](/wiki/Streatham) constituted the four wards in the southern half of the constituency. At its north-western tip the seat included half of [Clapham Common](/wiki/Clapham_Common); the north-east takes in part of [Brixton](/wiki/Brixton) which was shared with neighbouring [Vauxhall](/wiki/Vauxhall_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) and [Dulwich and West Norwood](/wiki/Dulwich_and_West_Norwood).

The northern boundary followed Clapham Park Road, Acre Lane, and Coldharbour Lane through [Clapham](/wiki/Clapham) and [Brixton](/wiki/Brixton) to [Lambeth Town Hall](/wiki/Lambeth_Town_Hall). The north-eastern boundary generally followed Effra Road and Tulse Hill, but ran east of the main road to include the part of the Tulse Hill estate and the Cressingham Gardens estate west of [Brockwell Park](/wiki/Brockwell_Park). The boundary skirted the Tulse Hill district centre, following Hardel Rise, Christchurch Road and Norwood Road, and then ran along Leigham Vale and Leigham Court Road. The southern and western constituency boundaries followed [Lambeth](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Lambeth)'s borough boundaries with [Croydon](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Croydon), [Merton](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Merton) and [Wandsworth](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Wandsworth).

### Historic boundaries

The constituency of Streatham was contested under this name at the [1918 general election](/wiki/1918_United_Kingdom_general_election), when it approximately followed the historic parish boundaries of Streatham, including a substantial part of [Balham](/wiki/Balham), a 19th-century founded and primarily urban parish by that time.

The constituency was carved out of the former constituency of [Wandsworth](/wiki/Wandsworth_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) in the same way as [Putney](/wiki/Putney_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), [Wandsworth Central](/wiki/Wandsworth_Central) and [Balham and Tooting](/wiki/Balham_and_Tooting) under the [Representation of the People Act 1918](/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_1918), the fourth major UK reform, that settled upon single member constituencies, and roughly equal electorates.

The 1918 boundaries remained unchanged until the 1965 changes to [Greater London](/wiki/Greater_London) [local government](/wiki/Local_government_of_England) became reflected in the parliamentary constituencies, at the [February 1974 general election](/wiki/February_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election). This resulted in a net reduction in the size of the area. The western district [Streatham Park](/wiki/Streatham_Park) (location of the Streatham Conservative Club) and the remainder of [Furzedown](/wiki/Furzedown) ward went into the [Tooting](/wiki/Tooting_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) seat.

The rest of the constituency, including the town of [Streatham](/wiki/Streatham) has since 1965 been in the [London Borough of Lambeth](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Lambeth). Three other [constituencies](/wiki/Borough_constituency) covered Lambeth from 1974, Vauxhall, Norwood and Lambeth Central. The [Clapham](/wiki/Clapham_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) constituency was abolished as part of the 1974 changes. The [Clapham Park](/wiki/Clapham_Park) area and [Hyde Farm](/wiki/Hyde_farm_estate) (commonly thought of as part of Balham) came into the Streatham seat, whereas the rest of Clapham went into the [Vauxhall](/wiki/Vauxhall_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) seat creating an enduring split.

On abolition of Lambeth Central at the 1983 election, the constituency gained much of southern Brixton. Following further population decline, Lambeth was paired with Southwark in the next boundary review, and from the 1997 election, Streatham constituency gained areas around [Tulse Hill](/wiki/Tulse_Hill) from the former [Norwood](/wiki/Norwood_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) constituency, the rest of which became part of [Dulwich and West Norwood](/wiki/Dulwich_and_West_Norwood).

## History

### Local government results

The constituency shared boundaries with the [Streatham electoral division](/wiki/Streatham_(electoral_division)) for election of councillors to the [Greater London Council](/wiki/Greater_London_Council_leadership_of_Ken_Livingstone) at elections in 1973, 1977 and 1981.

The local government [wards](/wiki/Ward_(politics)) in the constituency are currently represented by 20 [Labour](/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK)) councillors, 1 [Conservative](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)) councillor and 3 [Green](/wiki/Green_Party_of_England_and_Wales) councillors, including [Jonathan Bartley](/wiki/Jonathan_Bartley), the leader of the opposition on [Lambeth Council](/wiki/Lambeth_Council), and former London MEP [Scott Ainslie](/wiki/Scott_Ainslie).

Nine [Liberal Democrat](/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_(UK)) councillors previously represented the wards of Streatham Hill, Streatham Wells and St Leonard's, with one additional councillor elected for Clapham Common in 2010. All Streatham wards had been represented by the Liberal Democrats from 1990 to 2014, before Labour subsequently gained seven seats from them at the [2014 council elections](/wiki/2014_Lambeth_London_Borough_Council_election). The Liberal Democrats were unsuccessful in gaining any seats back at the [2018 local elections](/wiki/2018_Lambeth_London_Borough_Council_election). In a 2019 Thornton Ward by-election, the Lib Dems came within 19 votes of winning the seat.

In 2018, the Conservatives held one seat and lost two to Labour in Clapham Common by a very narrow margin, whilst the Greens took the other two seats from Labour in St Leonard's.

### Political history

Streatham was for a few decades solidly Tory [suburbia](/wiki/Suburbia) overall: the Conservatives won Streatham when Labour gained large majorities in 1945 and 1966, and it was the only seat in the former LCC area (Inner London) apart from the [Chelsea](/wiki/Chelsea,_London)/[Kensington](/wiki/Kensington)/[Westminster](/wiki/Westminster)/[City](/wiki/City_of_London) central core to remain consistently Conservative. More recently, demographic and voting pattern changes combined with unfavourable boundary changes converted Streatham into a [marginal seat](/wiki/Marginal_seat), then into a mid-ranking [safe](/wiki/Safe_seat) Labour seat.

Streatham has modestly swung against the Conservative Party since the 1980s, even more than other similar seats in South London (such as [Croydon North](/wiki/Croydon_North_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), [Dulwich and West Norwood](/wiki/Dulwich_and_West_Norwood), [Lewisham East](/wiki/Lewisham_East_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) and [West](/wiki/Lewisham_West_(UK_Parliament_constituency))).

The Conservative Party lost Streatham in 1992, despite winning the general election that year with a small majority, having held it since 1918. The Conservative candidate was beaten into third place by a Liberal Democrat in 2001, and there were [swings](/wiki/Swing_(politics)) from Labour to the Liberal Democrats at the two subsequent general elections. In 2010, when the Labour incumbent, [Keith Hill](/wiki/Keith_Hill_(politician)), retired and [Chuka Umunna](/wiki/Chuka_Umunna) stood for the party, there was a serious Liberal Democrat campaign resulting in their best showing to date in the seat, but there was also a marginal increase in the Conservative vote share and Umunna was elected. The 2015 result was the re-election of Umunna, which made the seat the 96th safest of Labour's 232 seats by percentage of majority.

In 2015 the Conservatives moved into second place with a sharp increase in numerical vote share, and remained there in 2017. However, in 2019, they reverted to third place behind the Liberal Democrats.

### 2016 European Union referendum

Main article: [2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum](/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum)

In the 2016 referendum on European Union membership, Streatham is estimated to have posted the [highest proportion of support for Remain](/wiki/Results_of_the_2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum_by_constituency) of any constituency, at 79.5%.

## Constituency profile

Among the most ethnically diverse constituencies, Streatham - which covered parts of Clapham, Brixton, Tulse Hill and Streatham itself - was in the south [London borough of Lambeth](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Lambeth). Only 58.2% of residents were white and it had among the most mixed race and black residents in the country, according to the 2011 Census. It also had Polish, Portuguese and Hispanic communities.

The bulk of residents were aged 25–44, with relatively few pensioners. Although it is a residential area, it is more popular with young workers than families, having good transport links into central London. Many residents rent, and there is a large social housing sector. Commercially, [Streatham High Road](/wiki/Streatham_High_Road) is home to over 400 businesses, whilst a £26m ice rink and leisure centre opened in November 2013, part of continuing investment. The population is highly qualified and a high percentage are in full-time work.

As of 2016, at just over 6% of the population, Streatham (which is located in the [London Borough of Lambeth](/wiki/London_Borough_of_Lambeth)), had the largest proportion of [LGBT+](/wiki/LGBT%2B) people in the country.

**Georges Jean Raymond Pompidou** ([/ˈpɒmpɪduː/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*POMP-id-oo*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); French: [[ʒɔʁʒ(ə) pɔ̃pidu]](/wiki/Help:IPA/French) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:Fr-Georges-Pompidou.ogg); 5 July 1911 – 2 April 1974) was [President of France](/wiki/President_of_France) from 1969 until his death in 1974. He had previously served from 1962 to 1968 as [Prime Minister of France](/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_France) under President [Charles de Gaulle](/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle), with whom he was closely associated throughout his career.

In the context of the strong growth of the last years of the *[Trente Glorieuses](/wiki/Trente_Glorieuses)*, Pompidou continued De Gaulle's policy of modernisation, which was symbolised by the presidential use of the [Concorde](/wiki/Concorde), the creation of large industrial groups and the launch of the [high-speed train project](/wiki/TGV) (TGV). The government invested heavily in the automobile, [agribusiness](/wiki/Agribusiness), steel, telecommunications, [nuclear](/wiki/Force_de_dissuasion) and aerospace sectors and also created the minimum wage (SMIC) and the Ministry of the Environment.

His foreign policy was pragmatic but in line with the [Gaullist](/wiki/Gaullism) principle of French autonomy within the [Western Bloc](/wiki/Western_Bloc). It was marked by a warming of relations with [Richard Nixon](/wiki/Richard_Nixon)'s [United States](/wiki/United_States), close relations with [Leonid Brezhnev](/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev)'s [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union), the launch of the '[snake in the tunnel](/wiki/Snake_in_the_tunnel)' and the relaunching of European construction by facilitating the [United Kingdom](/wiki/Accession_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_the_European_Communities)'s entry to the [EEC](/wiki/EEC) in contrast to de Gaulle's opposition. Pompidou died in office in 1974 of [Waldenström's disease](/wiki/Waldenstr%C3%B6m_macroglobulinemia), a rare form of [blood cancer](/wiki/Blood_cancer).

An admirer of [contemporary art](/wiki/Contemporary_art), Pompidou's name remains known worldwide for the [Centre Pompidou](/wiki/Centre_Pompidou), which he initiated and which was inaugurated in 1977; it subsequently spread the name with its branches in [Metz](/wiki/Metz) (France), [Málaga](/wiki/M%C3%A1laga) ([Spain](/wiki/Spain)), [Brussels](/wiki/Brussels) ([Belgium](/wiki/Belgium)) and [Shanghai](/wiki/Shanghai) ([China](/wiki/China)). A Georges Pompidou Museum is also dedicated to him in his hometown.

## Early life

The family of Georges Pompidou was of very modest origins. He was the grandson of farmers of modest means in Cantal on both his father's and his mother's side. His case is thus often cited as a typical example of [social mobility](/wiki/Social_mobility) in the [Third Republic](/wiki/French_Third_Republic) because of public schooling.

Georges Jean Raymond Pompidou was born on 5 July 1911 in the commune of [Montboudif](/wiki/Montboudif), in the department of [Cantal](/wiki/Cantal), in south-central [France](/wiki/France). After his *[hypokhâgne](/wiki/Hypokh%C3%A2gne)* at Lycée Pierre-de-Fermat and his *[khâgne](/wiki/Kh%C3%A2gne)* at [Lycée Louis-le-Grand](/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Louis-le-Grand), where he befriended the future [Senegalese](/wiki/Senegal) poet and [statesman](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/statesman) [Léopold Sédar Senghor](/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_S%C3%A9dar_Senghor), Pompidou attended the [École Normale Supérieure](/wiki/%C3%89cole_Normale_Sup%C3%A9rieure) from which he graduated with a degree of *[agrégation](/wiki/Agr%C3%A9gation)* in literature.

He first taught literature at the [lycée Henri IV](/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Henri_IV) in [Paris](/wiki/Paris) until he was hired in 1953 by [Guy de Rothschild](/wiki/Guy_de_Rothschild) to work at [Rothschild](/wiki/Rothschild_%26_Co). In 1956, he was appointed the bank's general manager, a position that he held until 1962. Later, he was hired by [Charles de Gaulle](/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) to manage the [Anne de Gaulle](/wiki/Anne_de_Gaulle) Foundation for [Down syndrome](/wiki/Down_syndrome) (de Gaulle's youngest daughter, Anne, had Down syndrome).

## Prime minister

Pompidou (upper right) with [US](/wiki/US) [Vice President](/wiki/Vice_President_of_the_United_States) [Hubert Humphrey](/wiki/Hubert_Humphrey), Soviet cosmonaut [Yuri Gagarin](/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin) and [Gemini 4](/wiki/Project_Gemini) astronauts at the 1965 [Paris Air Show](/wiki/Paris_Air_Show)

[Jacques Chirac](/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) served as an aide to Prime Minister Pompidou and recalled:

> The man gave the appearance of being secretive, wily, a little cunning—which he was, to a degree. However, it was primarily his intelligence, culture, and competence that conferred indisputable authority on him and commanded respect.... I remember his untamed eyebrows, his penetrating, very kindly gaze, his perceptive smile, full of humour and mischievousness, his voice with its wonderful low, warm, gravelly tone, and a figure that was both powerful and elegant. Naturally reserved, little given to emotional outbursts, Pompidou did not forge very close ties with his colleagues.

He served as [prime minister of France](/wiki/Prime_minister_of_France) under de Gaulle after [Michel Debré](/wiki/Michel_Debr%C3%A9) resigned, from 14 April 1962 to 10 July 1968, and to this day is the longest serving French prime minister under the [Fifth Republic](/wiki/Fifth_French_Republic). His nomination was controversial because he was not a member of the [National Assembly](/wiki/National_Assembly_of_France). In October 1962, he was defeated in a vote of no-confidence, but de Gaulle dissolved the National Assembly. This was the last time a French government fell to a confidence vote until the [collapse](/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Barnier_government) of [Michel Barnier](/wiki/Michel_Barnier)'s [government](/wiki/Barnier_government) in 2024. The Gaullists won the [legislative election](/wiki/1962_French_legislative_election) and Pompidou was reappointed as prime minister.

In 1964, he was faced with a miners' strike. He led the [1967 legislative campaign](/wiki/1967_French_legislative_election) of the [Union of Democrats for the Fifth Republic](/wiki/Gaullist_Party) to a narrow victory. Pompidou was widely regarded as being responsible for the peaceful resolution of the student uprising of May 1968. His strategy was to break the coalition of students and workers by negotiating with the trade-unions and employers ([Grenelle conference](/wiki/Grenelle_agreements)).

During the [events of May 1968](/wiki/May_1968_events_in_France), disagreements arose between Pompidou and de Gaulle. Pompidou did not understand why the President did not inform him of his departure to [Baden-Baden](/wiki/Baden-Baden) on 29 May. Their relationship, until then very good, would be strained from then on. Pompidou led and won the [1968 legislative campaign](/wiki/1968_French_legislative_election), overseeing a tremendous victory of the Gaullist Party. He then resigned. Nevertheless, in part due to his actions during the May 1968 crisis, he appeared as the natural successor to de Gaulle. Pompidou announced his candidature for the Presidency in January 1969.

In social policy, Pompidou's tenure as prime minister witnessed the establishment of the National Employment Fund in 1963 to counter the negative effects on employment caused by industrial restructuring.

## Presidency

After the failure of the [1969 constitutional referendum](/wiki/1969_French_constitutional_referendum), de Gaulle resigned and Pompidou was elected president of France. In the [general election of 15 June 1969](/wiki/1969_French_presidential_election), he defeated the centrist president of the Senate and acting president [Alain Poher](/wiki/Alain_Poher) by a wide margin (58% to 42%). Though a Gaullist, Pompidou was more pragmatic than de Gaulle, notably facilitating the accession of the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom) to the [European Community](/wiki/European_Community) on 1 January 1973. He embarked on an industrialisation plan and initiated the [Arianespace](/wiki/Arianespace) project, as well as the [TGV](/wiki/TGV) project, and furthered the French civilian nuclear programme. He was sceptical about the "New Society" programme of his prime minister, [Jacques Chaban-Delmas](/wiki/Jacques_Chaban-Delmas). In 1972, he replaced Chaban-Delmas with [Pierre Messmer](/wiki/Pierre_Messmer), a more conservative Gaullist. While the left-wing opposition organised itself and proposed a *Common Programme* before the [1973 legislative election](/wiki/1973_French_legislative_election), Pompidou widened his presidential majority by including Centrist pro-European parties. In addition, he paid special attention to regional and local needs in order to strengthen his political party, the UDR (Union des Democrates pour la Ve République), which he made a central and lasting force in the Gaullist movement.

### Foreign affairs

The United States was eager to restore positive relations with France after de Gaulle's departure from office. New US President [Richard Nixon](/wiki/Richard_Nixon) and his top adviser [Henry Kissinger](/wiki/Henry_Kissinger) admired Pompidou; the politicians were in agreement on most major policy issues. The United States offered to help the French nuclear programme. Economic difficulties, however, arose following the [Nixon Shock](/wiki/Nixon_Shock) and the [1973–1975 recession](/wiki/1973%E2%80%931975_recession), particularly over the role of the American dollar as the medium for world trade.

Pompidou sought to maintain good relations with the newly independent former French colonies in Africa, relying on such powerful figures as [Jacques Foccart](/wiki/Jacques_Foccart) and [René Journiac](/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Journiac) who maintained informal networks with African [strongmen](/wiki/Political_strongman). In 1971, he visited [Mauritania](/wiki/Mauritania), Senegal, [Ivory Coast](/wiki/Ivory_Coast), [Cameroon](/wiki/Cameroon), and [Gabon](/wiki/Gabon). He brought a message of cooperation and financial assistance, but without the traditional paternalism. More broadly, he made an effort to foster closer relations with North African and Middle Eastern countries in order to develop a hinterland including all nations bordering the Mediterranean.

### Modernising Paris

Pompidou's time in office was marked by constant efforts to modernise France's capital city. He spearheaded construction of a modern art museum, the Centre Beaubourg (renamed [Centre Pompidou](/wiki/Centre_Pompidou) after his death), on the edge of the [Marais](/wiki/Le_Marais) area of Paris. Other attempts at modernisation included tearing down the open-air markets at [Les Halles](/wiki/Les_Halles) and replacing them with the shopping mall of the same name, building the [Montparnasse Tower](/wiki/Tour_Montparnasse), and constructing an expressway on the right bank of the Seine.

## Death in office

Main article: [Illness and death of Georges Pompidou](/wiki/Illness_and_death_of_Georges_Pompidou)

The grave of Georges and Claude Pompidou in Orvilliers

[While still in office](/wiki/List_of_heads_of_state_and_government_who_died_in_office), Pompidou died on 2 April 1974 at his apartment, from [Waldenström macroglobulinemia](/wiki/Waldenstr%C3%B6m_macroglobulinemia). Although his illness had not been disclosed and the government officially denied any health problems, there were reports of ill health and speculation of possible cancer during his last year in office. However, even on the day of his death, Pompidou had a visit scheduled from Rwandan President [Grégoire Kayibanda](/wiki/Gr%C3%A9goire_Kayibanda).

His body was buried on 4 April, in the churchyard of [Orvilliers](/wiki/Orvilliers), where he had bought an old [baker](/wiki/Baker)'s house which he turned into a weekend home. The official memorial service for him was held at [Notre-Dame de Paris](/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris) with 3,000 dignitaries in attendance, including foreign heads of state. April 6 was declared a [national day of mourning](/wiki/National_day_of_mourning) and entertainment and cultural events were canceled, theatres and schools closed.

A controversy arose surrounding the secrecy kept over Pompidou's illness, and the political class agreed that future presidents would have to provide reports on the state of their health; however, President [François Mitterrand](/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Mitterrand), who had pledged during his 1981 campaign to publish regular health bulletins, would also conceal the severity of his cancer during his presidency.

## Personal life

Pompidou was married to [Claude Cahour](/wiki/Claude_Pompidou), who would outlive him by more than thirty years. The couple adopted a son, [Alain Pompidou](/wiki/Alain_Pompidou), who went on to serve as a [Member of the European Parliament](/wiki/Member_of_the_European_Parliament) and, later, as president of the [European Patent Office](/wiki/European_Patent_Organisation).

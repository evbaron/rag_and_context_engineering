**Timothy Francis Robbins** (born October 16, 1958) is an American actor, director, producer, and writer. Known for his leading roles in film and television, his accolades include an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award), a [Critics' Choice Movie Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Award), three [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and a [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award) as well as nominations for two [British Academy Film Award](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award) and a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award).

Robbins made his acting debut in *[St. Elsewhere](/wiki/St._Elsewhere)* (1982) before taking supporting roles in *[The Sure Thing](/wiki/The_Sure_Thing)* (1985), *[Top Gun](/wiki/Top_Gun)* (1986), *[Bull Durham](/wiki/Bull_Durham)* (1988), and *[Jungle Fever](/wiki/Jungle_Fever)* (1991). He took leading roles in *[Jacob's Ladder](/wiki/Jacob%27s_Ladder_(1990_film))* (1990), *[The Player](/wiki/The_Player_(1992_film))* (1992), *[The Shawshank Redemption](/wiki/The_Shawshank_Redemption)* (1994), *[The Hudsucker Proxy](/wiki/The_Hudsucker_Proxy)* (1994), *[I.Q.](/wiki/I.Q._(film))* (1994) and *[Nothing to Lose](/wiki/Nothing_to_Lose_(1997_film))* (1997). For his role in the [Clint Eastwood](/wiki/Clint_Eastwood) directed drama *[Mystic River](/wiki/Mystic_River_(film))* (2003) he won the [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor), the [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture), and the [Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Actor in a Supporting Role](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Supporting_Role). He later acted in *[Zathura](/wiki/Zathura:_A_Space_Adventure)* (2005), *[The Lucky Ones](/wiki/The_Lucky_Ones_(film))* (2007), *[Green Lantern](/wiki/Green_Lantern_(film))* (2011), and *[Dark Waters](/wiki/Dark_Waters_(2019_film))* (2019).

As a director, he gained acclaim for the crime drama *[Dead Man Walking](/wiki/Dead_Man_Walking_(film))* (1995), earning him a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director). He also directed the satirical [mockumentary](/wiki/Mockumentary) film *[Bob Roberts](/wiki/Bob_Roberts)* (1992) and the historical drama *[Cradle Will Rock](/wiki/Cradle_Will_Rock)* (1999). On television, Robbins played a [Secretary of State](/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State) in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) comedy *[The Brink](/wiki/The_Brink_(TV_series))* (2015), a philosophy professor in the HBO drama series *[Here and Now](/wiki/Here_and_Now_(2018_TV_series))* (2018), the patriarch of a crime family in the [Hulu](/wiki/Hulu) series *[Castle Rock](/wiki/Castle_Rock_(TV_series))* (2019), the head of an IT department in the [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B) series *[Silo](/wiki/Silo_(TV_series))* (2023–2025). For his role as a Hollywood producer in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) television film *[Cinema Verite](/wiki/Cinema_Verite_(2011_film))* (2011) he was nominated for a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film).

Robbins was in a long romantic relationship with actress [Susan Sarandon](/wiki/Susan_Sarandon) from 1988 to 2009, with whom he has a son. He was married to actress [Gratiela Brancusi](/wiki/Gratiela_Brancusi) from 2017 to 2022. He is known for his extensive liberal activism, including opposition to the [Iraq War](/wiki/Iraq_War).

## Early life and education

Robbins was born in [West Covina, California](/wiki/West_Covina,_California), and raised in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City). He attended [Stuyvesant High School](/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School). His parents were Mary Cecelia ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Bledsoe), a musician, and [Gilbert Lee Robbins](/wiki/Gil_Robbins), a singer, actor, and manager of [The Gaslight Cafe](/wiki/The_Gaslight_Cafe). Robbins has two sisters, Adele and Gabrielle, and a brother, composer [David Robbins](/wiki/David_Robbins_(composer)). He was raised Catholic.

Robbins moved to [Greenwich Village](/wiki/Greenwich_Village) with his family at a young age while his father pursued a career as a member of a folk music group called [the Highwaymen](/wiki/The_Highwaymen_(folk_band)). Robbins started performing in theater at age twelve and joined the drama club at [Stuyvesant High School](/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) (Class of 1976). He spent two years at [SUNY Plattsburgh](/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_at_Plattsburgh) and then returned to California to study at the [UCLA Film School](/wiki/UCLA_Film_School), graduating with a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in [Drama](/wiki/Drama) in 1981.

## Career

### 1980–1999: Breakthrough and stardom

Robbins's acting career began at [Theater for the New City](/wiki/Theater_for_the_New_City), where he spent his teenage years in their Annual Summer [Street Theater](/wiki/Street_Theater) and also played the title role in a musical adaptation of [Antoine de Saint-Exupéry](/wiki/Antoine_de_Saint-Exup%C3%A9ry)'s *[The Little Prince](/wiki/The_Little_Prince)*. After graduation from college in 1981, Robbins founded the [Actors' Gang](/wiki/The_Actors%27_Gang), an experimental theater group, in Los Angeles with actor friends from his college [softball](/wiki/Softball) team, as well as [John Cusack](/wiki/John_Cusack).

In 1982, he appeared as domestic terrorist Andrew Reinhardt in three episodes of the television program *[St. Elsewhere](/wiki/St._Elsewhere)*. He had a small role in the film *[No Small Affair](/wiki/No_Small_Affair)* (1984), starring [Demi Moore](/wiki/Demi_Moore). In 1985, he guest-starred in the second episode of the television series *[Moonlighting](/wiki/Moonlighting_(TV_series))*, "[Gunfight at the So-So Corral](/wiki/List_of_Moonlighting_episodes#ep2)". He also took parts in films, such as the role of frat animal "Mother" in *[Fraternity Vacation](/wiki/Fraternity_Vacation)* (1985) and Lt Sam "Merlin" Wells in the fighter pilot film *[Top Gun](/wiki/Top_Gun)* (1986). He appeared on *[The Love Boat](/wiki/The_Love_Boat)*, as a young version of one of the characters in retrospection about the Second World War. His breakthrough role was as pitcher Ebby Calvin "Nuke" LaLoosh in the [baseball](/wiki/Baseball) film *[Bull Durham](/wiki/Bull_Durham)* (1988), in which he co-starred with [Susan Sarandon](/wiki/Susan_Sarandon) and [Kevin Costner](/wiki/Kevin_Costner).

Robbins's amoral film executive in [Robert Altman](/wiki/Robert_Altman)'s film *[The Player](/wiki/The_Player_(1992_film))* (1992) was described by [Peter Travers](/wiki/Peter_Travers) in *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* as "a classic performance, mining every comic and lethal nuance in the role of his career". He won the [Best Actor Award](/wiki/Prix_d%27interpr%C3%A9tation_masculine) at [Cannes](/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival). He made his directorial and screenwriting debut with *[Bob Roberts](/wiki/Bob_Roberts)* (also 1992), a [mockumentary](/wiki/Mockumentary) about a right-wing [senatorial](/wiki/United_States_Senate) candidate. [Todd McCarthy](/wiki/Todd_McCarthy) in *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* commented that the film is "both a stimulating social satire and, for thinking people, a depressing commentary on the devolution of the American political system". Robbins then starred alongside [Morgan Freeman](/wiki/Morgan_Freeman) in *[The Shawshank Redemption](/wiki/The_Shawshank_Redemption)* (1994), which was based on [Stephen King](/wiki/Stephen_King)'s [novella](/wiki/Rita_Hayworth_and_Shawshank_Redemption).

Tim Robbins at the 2008 Toronto International Film Festival

Robbins has written, produced, and directed several films with strong social content, such as the capital punishment saga *[Dead Man Walking](/wiki/Dead_Man_Walking_(film))* (1995), starring Sarandon and [Sean Penn](/wiki/Sean_Penn). The film earned him an [Oscar](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nomination for [Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director). According to [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) in early 1996: "With this film he leaps far beyond" *Bob Roberts* "and has made that rare thing, a film that is an exercise of philosophy. This is the kind of movie that spoils us for other films, because it reveals so starkly how most movies fall into conventional routine, and lull us with the reassurance that they will not look too hard, or probe too deeply, or make us think beyond the boundaries of what is comfortable".

His next directorial effort was [Depression](/wiki/Great_Depression)-era musical *[Cradle Will Rock](/wiki/Cradle_Will_Rock)* (1999). Robbins has also appeared in mainstream Hollywood thrillers, such as *[Arlington Road](/wiki/Arlington_Road)* (also 1999) as a suspected terrorist and *[Antitrust](/wiki/Antitrust_(film))* (2001) as a malicious computer [tycoon](/wiki/Tycoon), and in comical films such as *[The Hudsucker Proxy](/wiki/The_Hudsucker_Proxy)* (1994), *[Nothing to Lose](/wiki/Nothing_to_Lose_(1997_film))* (1997), and *[High Fidelity](/wiki/High_Fidelity_(film))* (2000). Robbins has also acted in and directed several Actors' Gang theater productions.

### 2000–2009: *Mystic River* and other roles

Robbins won the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) and the [SAG Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild#SAG_Awards) for his work in *[Mystic River](/wiki/Mystic_River_(film))* (2003), as a man [traumatized](/wiki/Psychological_trauma) from having been kidnapped and [raped](/wiki/Rape) as a child. He followed his Oscar-win with roles as a temporarily blind man who is nursed to health by a psychologically wounded young woman in *[The Secret Life of Words](/wiki/The_Secret_Life_of_Words)* (2005) and an [apartheid](/wiki/Apartheid) torturer in *[Catch a Fire](/wiki/Catch_a_Fire_(film))* (2006). As of 2006, he was the tallest [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award)-winning actor at 6 feet 5 inches (1.96 m).

In early 2006, Robbins directed an adaptation of [George Orwell](/wiki/George_Orwell)'s novel *[1984](/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four)*, written by Michael Gene Sullivan of the [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award)-winning [San Francisco Mime Troupe](/wiki/San_Francisco_Mime_Troupe). The production opened at [Actors' Gang](/wiki/The_Actors%27_Gang), at their new location at The Ivy Substation in [Culver City](/wiki/Culver_City), California. In addition to venues around the United States, it has played in [Athens, Greece](/wiki/Athens,_Greece), the Melbourne International Festival in Australia and the [Hong Kong Arts Festival](/wiki/Hong_Kong_Arts_Festival). Robbins was soon considering a film adaptation.

Robbins appeared in *[The Lucky Ones](/wiki/The_Lucky_Ones_(film))*, with co-star [Rachel McAdams](/wiki/Rachel_McAdams) as well as *[City of Ember](/wiki/City_of_Ember)* (both 2008). Robbins next film role was as Senator Hammond, the disapproving father of the film's villain [Hector Hammond](/wiki/Hector_Hammond), in the superhero film *[Green Lantern](/wiki/Green_Lantern_(film))* (2011).

Robbins at the [2012 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2012_Toronto_International_Film_Festival)

Robbins released the album *Tim Robbins & The Rogues Gallery Band* (2010), a collection of songs written over the course of 25 years that he ultimately took on a world tour. He was originally offered the chance to record an album in 1992 after the success of his film *[Bob Roberts](/wiki/Bob_Roberts)*, but he declined because he had "too much respect for the process", having seen his father work so hard as a musician, and because he felt he had nothing to say at the time.

### 2010–present: Streaming projects

Robbins directed two episodes of the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) series [*Treme*](/wiki/Treme_(TV_series)). The series follows the interconnected lives of a group of New Orleanians in the wake of [Hurricane Katrina](/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina). He helmed the episodes "Everything I Do Gonh Be Funky" in Season 2 (2011) and "Promised Land" in Season 3 (2012). Robbins became interested in the show while staying in [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans) during the filming of *[Green Lantern](/wiki/Green_Lantern_(film)).* "I had the unique experience of watching *Treme* with locals. It resonated for me immediately, and it resonated for them as well, because they have seen their town get misinterpreted and represented in ridiculous ways," he told [The Times-Picayune](/wiki/The_Times-Picayune) in 2011. "Something about this show was different for them. I appreciated that. I loved the writing and the actors. I loved the environment it's set in. I watched the whole first season in New Orleans, and got in touch with [David Simon](/wiki/David_Simon) and said, 'If you guys need a director next year, I'd be happy to do an episode.'"

In 2013, he was a member of the jury at the [63rd Berlin International Film Festival](/wiki/63rd_Berlin_International_Film_Festival).

In fall of 2024, Robbins and the [Actors' Gang](/wiki/The_Actors%27_Gang) presented a production of his play 'Topsy Turvy - Ramazuri' at the [Csokonai National Theatre](/wiki/Csokonai_Theatre) in [Debrecen](/wiki/Debrecen).

## Personal life

### Marriages and family

In 1988, Robbins began a relationship with actress [Susan Sarandon](/wiki/Susan_Sarandon), whom he met on the set of *[Bull Durham](/wiki/Bull_Durham)*. They have two sons: John "Jack" Henry (b. May 15, 1989) and [Miles Robbins](/wiki/Miles_Robbins) (b. May 4, 1992). Sarandon, like Robbins, is a [lapsed Catholic](/wiki/Lapsed_Catholic). Robbins's relationship with Sarandon ended in December 2009. Robbins married [Gratiela Brancusi](/wiki/Gratiela_Brancusi) on February 1, 2017. They separated on July 1, 2020. News of the marriage was kept private until Robbins filed for divorce in January 2021. The divorce was finalized in 2022.

Robbins is a lifelong [New York Mets](/wiki/New_York_Mets) fan. "Also, my mother, for my 11th birthday, traveled out one morning to [Queens](/wiki/Queens) to wait in line at [Shea Stadium](/wiki/Shea_Stadium) to get me tickets for the [World Series](/wiki/World_Series). I wound up seeing, on my 11th birthday, the Mets win the World Series. That was one of the greatest things a mother could do for her son."

### Political views

Robbins supported [Ralph Nader](/wiki/Ralph_Nader)'s [2000 presidential campaign](/wiki/Ralph_Nader_2000_presidential_campaign) and appeared on stage in character as Bob Roberts during the "Nader Rocks the Garden" rally at [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden). In December 2007, Robbins campaigned for [Senator](/wiki/United_States_Senate) [John Edwards](/wiki/John_Edwards) in the [2008 U.S. presidential election](/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election). He made critical statements against [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) and the [Democratic Leadership Council](/wiki/Democratic_Leadership_Council) while introducing [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) at a 2016 campaign stop.

Robbins opposed the [2003 invasion of Iraq](/wiki/2003_invasion_of_Iraq). In 2003, a 15th anniversary celebration of *Bull Durham* at the [National Baseball Hall of Fame](/wiki/United_States_Baseball_Hall_of_Fame) was canceled by Hall of Fame President [Dale Petroskey](/wiki/Dale_Petroskey). Petroskey told Robbins that his stance helped to "undermine the U.S. position, which could put our troops in even more danger". *Durham* co-star [Kevin Costner](/wiki/Kevin_Costner) defended Robbins and Sarandon: "I think Tim and Susan's courage is the type of courage that makes our democracy work. Pulling back this invite is against the whole principle about what we fight for and profess to be about."

In 2023, Robbins criticized [COVID-19 lockdowns](/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns), arguing they undermined [freedom of speech](/wiki/Freedom_of_speech) and [freedom of assembly](/wiki/Freedom_of_assembly). Robbins added that his villainous character in the television series *[Silo](/wiki/Silo_(TV_series))*, a "leader who crushes any dissent or protest with swift violence", was inspired by pro-lockdown politicians.

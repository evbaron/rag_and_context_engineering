# Lords of Montpellier

"Lady of Montpellier" redirects here. For the wives of the Lords of Montpellier, see [List of consorts of Montpellier](/wiki/List_of_consorts_of_Montpellier).

The **Lordship of Montpellier** was a feudal jurisdiction in Languedoc with its centre in the city of [Montpellier](/wiki/Montpellier), that existed between 986 and 1344.

## Formation

Ricuin II, the bishop of [Mauguio](/wiki/Mauguio) gave these lands in fief to a knight named Guiu I. He was succeeded by his son William I (985–1025) who received more lands around the city and died without descendants. The inheritance fell to his nephew William II, son of Trudgarda (William's sister) and Bernard.

Much younger than its neighbours in the region such as Nîmes, Narbonne, Béziers or Carcassonne, most of which were created in Roman times, the Lordship of Montpellier was only created in the 11th century. Situated between Spain and Italy, close to the [Via Domitia](/wiki/Via_Domitia) and the port of [Lattes](/wiki/Lattes,_H%C3%A9rault), the city quickly experienced significant economic and cultural development, attracting gilders, goldsmiths, drapers and money changers. It thus became a centre of trade between northern Europe, Spain and the Mediterranean basin.

### French royal domain

In 1344 James III sold the Lordship of Montpellier to King [Philip VI of France](/wiki/Philip_VI_of_France): Montpellier became a possession of the [crown of France](/wiki/French_royal_domain).

In 1364, following his defeat in the [Battle of Cocherel](/wiki/Battle_of_Cocherel), King [Charles II of Navarre](/wiki/Charles_II_of_Navarre) negotaited a peace treaty in which he ceded to the French crown [Mantes](/wiki/Mantes), [Meulan](/wiki/Meulan) and the [county of Longueville](/wiki/County_of_Longueville) in Normandy in exchange for the lordship of Montpellier.

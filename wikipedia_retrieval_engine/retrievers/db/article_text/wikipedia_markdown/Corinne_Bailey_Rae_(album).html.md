***Corinne Bailey Rae*** is the debut studio album by English singer-songwriter [Corinne Bailey Rae](/wiki/Corinne_Bailey_Rae), released on 24 February 2006 by [EMI](/wiki/EMI). The album debuted at number one on the [UK Albums Chart](/wiki/UK_Albums_Chart) and has been certified triple platinum by the [British Phonographic Industry](/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry) (BPI). Four singles were released from the album: "[Like a Star](/wiki/Like_a_Star)", "[Put Your Records On](/wiki/Put_Your_Records_On)", "[Trouble Sleeping](/wiki/Trouble_Sleeping_(song))" and "[I'd Like To](/wiki/I%27d_Like_To)". *Corinne Bailey Rae* has sold over four million copies worldwide.

## Release and promotion

"[Like a Star](/wiki/Like_a_Star)" was released as the album's lead single in a limited-edition format in 2005, peaking at number 34 on the [UK Singles Chart](/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart). When re-released in October 2006, "Like a Star" reached a new peak position of number 32 on the UK chart. The second single, "[Put Your Records On](/wiki/Put_Your_Records_On)", peaked at number two in February 2006. "[Trouble Sleeping](/wiki/Trouble_Sleeping_(song))" was released as the album's third single in May 2006, peaking at number 40 in the UK.

"[I'd Like To](/wiki/I%27d_Like_To)" was released on 12 February 2007 as the fourth and final single. On the same day, the album was re-released with a bonus disc containing some of the B-sides and remixes included on previous singles, as well as three new tracks—one of which, a cover of [Björk](/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rk)'s 1993 song "[Venus as a Boy](/wiki/Venus_as_a_Boy)", only previously appeared on a [covermount](/wiki/Covermount) for *[Q](/wiki/Q_(magazine))* magazine. Several songs from the album appear in the 2006 comedy-drama film *[Venus](/wiki/Venus_(2006_film))* starring [Peter O'Toole](/wiki/Peter_O%27Toole). In the United States, "[Breathless](/wiki/Breathless_(Corinne_Bailey_Rae_song))" was released as a [promotional single](/wiki/Promotional_single) in 2007.

*Corinne Bailey Rae* received generally positive reviews from music critics. At [Metacritic](/wiki/Metacritic), which assigns a [normalised](/wiki/Standard_score) rating out of 100 to reviews from mainstream publications, the album received an [average](/wiki/Weighted_arithmetic_mean) score of 69, based on 13 reviews. While some critics perceived [filler](/wiki/Filler_(media)) as a weakness of the album, Bailey Rae earned positive comparisons to female recording artists such as [Billie Holiday](/wiki/Billie_Holiday), [Norah Jones](/wiki/Norah_Jones), [Erykah Badu](/wiki/Erykah_Badu), [Minnie Riperton](/wiki/Minnie_Riperton), and [Macy Gray](/wiki/Macy_Gray). David Jeffries of [AllMusic](/wiki/AllMusic) called it "pleasingly homegrown, warm, and poignant in parts". *[Los Angeles Times](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* writer Natalie Nichols compared Bailey Rae's music to that of [Sade](/wiki/Sade_(band)) and [Stevie Wonder](/wiki/Stevie_Wonder). Michael Endelman of [Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly) called it "extremely agreeable, a bit predictable, and occasionally irresistible". [MusicOMH](/wiki/MusicOMH) writer Michael Hubbard called the debut "exceptional in every way" and "a breath of fresh air" Preston Jones of [Slant Magazine](/wiki/Slant_Magazine) was more lukewarm, saying "Rae’s amiable competence marks her as a talent worth keeping tabs on, but the strength of *Corinne Bailey Rae* is fleeting, a triumph of mood over tangible substance."

In contrast, Neil Spencer of [The Observer](/wiki/The_Observer) gave the album a scathing review, saying "Beyond her calling card single, 'Like a Star', however, she shows a lack of ambition. The arrangements are generic ([Al Green](/wiki/Al_Green)'s leftovers, mostly), the cooing backing singers bland, and the lyrics pedestrian."

*[Paste](/wiki/Paste_(magazine))* listed the album as the 41st best of 2006.

### Accolades

Bailey Rae received a nomination for [Best UK & Ireland Act](/wiki/MTV_Europe_Music_Award_for_Best_UK_%26_Ireland_Act) at the [2006 MTV Europe Music Awards](/wiki/2006_MTV_Europe_Music_Awards). That same year, she won two [MOBO Awards](/wiki/MOBO_Awards) for Best UK Newcomer and Best UK Female, as well as a [Mojo Award](/wiki/Mojo_Awards) for Best New Act. Bailey Rae won [Outstanding New Artist](/wiki/NAACP_Image_Award_for_Outstanding_New_Artist) at the [2007 NAACP Image Awards](/wiki/38th_NAACP_Image_Awards), and was nominated for [Outstanding Female Artist](/wiki/NAACP_Image_Award_for_Outstanding_Female_Artist) and [Outstanding Album](/wiki/NAACP_Image_Award_for_Outstanding_Album). At the [2007 Grammy Awards](/wiki/2007_Grammy_Awards), Bailey Rae received a nomination for [Best New Artist](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_New_Artist), while "Put Your Records On" was nominated for [Record of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Record_of_the_Year) and [Song of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year). The following year, Bailey Rae was nominated for another Grammy Award for Song of the Year for "Like a Star", and "Put Your Records On" won an [ASCAP Award](/wiki/ASCAP_Award) for Song of the Year.

## Commercial performance

*Corinne Bailey Rae* debuted at number one on the [UK Albums Chart](/wiki/UK_Albums_Chart), selling 108,181 copies in its first week. The album had sold 968,341 copies in the United Kingdom by May 2016, earning a triple platinum certification from the [British Phonographic Industry](/wiki/British_Phonographic_Industry) (BPI) on 22 July 2013.

The album debuted at number 17 on the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) in the United States, selling 40,000 copies in its first week. On the issue dated 3 March 2007, it peaked at number four with 120,000 copies sold. The album was certified platinum by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA) on 5 December 2006, and had sold 1.9 million copies in the US by January 2010. As of October 2009, *Corinne Bailey Rae* had sold over four million copies worldwide.

**Jerome Allen Seinfeld** ([/ˈsaɪnfɛld/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*SYNE-feld*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born April 29, 1954) is an American [stand-up comedian](/wiki/Stand-up_comedian), actor, writer, filmmaker, and television producer specializing in [observational comedy](/wiki/Observational_comedy). Seinfeld gained stardom playing [a fictionalized version of himself](/wiki/Jerry_Seinfeld_(character)) in the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) sitcom *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* (1989–1998), which he co-created and wrote with [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David). Seinfeld earned a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Television Series Musical or Comedy](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Television_Series_Musical_or_Comedy) in 1995. The show is one of the most acclaimed and popular [sitcoms](/wiki/Sitcoms) of all time. He has since created and produced the reality series *[The Marriage Ref](/wiki/The_Marriage_Ref_(U.S._TV_series))* (2010–2011), and created and hosted the web series *[Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee](/wiki/Comedians_in_Cars_Getting_Coffee)* (2012–2019), the latter of which earned him three [Webby Awards](/wiki/Webby_Awards). He also co-produced, co-wrote, and starred in the [DreamWorks](/wiki/DreamWorks_animation) animated film *[Bee Movie](/wiki/Bee_Movie)* (2007) and the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) comedy *[Unfrosted](/wiki/Unfrosted)* (2024).

He has released four standup specials, his first being *[Stand-Up Confidential](/wiki/Stand-Up_Confidential)* (1987), followed by *[I'm Telling You for the Last Time](/wiki/I%27m_Telling_You_for_the_Last_Time)* (1998), *[Jerry Before Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Before_Seinfeld)* (2017) and *[23 Hours to Kill](/wiki/23_Hours_to_Kill)* (2020).

Seinfeld has received [numerous accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Jerry_Seinfeld) including a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and three [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards) as well as nominations for four [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards). In 2004, [Comedy Central](/wiki/Comedy_Central) named him the 12th-greatest stand-up comedian of all time. In 2017, [*Rolling Stone*](/wiki/Rolling_Stone_(magazine)) named him the 7th-greatest stand-up comedian of all time.

Seinfeld has also written three books starting with *[SeinLanguage](/wiki/SeinLanguage)* (1993), followed by the children's book [*Halloween*](/wiki/Halloween_(children%27s_book)) (2002), and the comedic compilation book [*Is This Anything?*](/wiki/Is_This_Anything%3F_(book)) (2020). He is a fan of [coffee](/wiki/Coffee) and [automobiles](/wiki/Automobiles). He practices [transcendental meditation](/wiki/Transcendental_Meditation_technique). He is married to author and philanthropist [Jessica Seinfeld](/wiki/Jessica_Seinfeld), with whom he has three children.

## Early life and education

Jerome Allen Seinfeld was born on April 29, 1954, to a [Jewish](/wiki/Jews) family in [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn), New York City. His father, Kalmen Seinfeld, a sign painter, was from Hungary and collected jokes that he heard while serving in [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). His mother, Betty (born Hosni) and her parents, Selim and Salha Hosni, were [Syrian Jews](/wiki/Syrian_Jews) from [Aleppo](/wiki/Aleppo). Their nationality was stated as [Turkish](/wiki/Turkey) when they immigrated in 1917, as Syria was under the [Ottoman Empire](/wiki/Ottoman_Empire). Seinfeld has an older sister, Carolyn. Salha's mother, Garez Dayan, Seinfeld's great-grandmother, was a member of the Dayan rabbinic family, who claim ancestry back to the [Medieval](/wiki/Middle_Ages) [Exilarchs](/wiki/Exilarch), and from the Exilarchs back to the Biblical [King David](/wiki/David). Seinfeld's second cousin is [alternative metal](/wiki/Alternative_metal) musician and actor [Evan Seinfeld](/wiki/Evan_Seinfeld).

Seinfeld grew up in [Massapequa](/wiki/Massapequa,_New_York), on Long Island, in Nassau County and attended [Massapequa High School](/wiki/Massapequa_High_School). At 16, he spent time [volunteering](/wiki/Kibbutz_volunteer) in [Kibbutz](/wiki/Kibbutz) [Sa'ar](/wiki/Sa%27ar) in Israel. He attended the [State University of New York at Oswego](/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_at_Oswego), and transferred after his second year to [Queens College](/wiki/Queens_College,_City_University_of_New_York) of the [City University of New York](/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) in [Flushing](/wiki/Flushing,_Queens), from which he graduated in 1976 with a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in communications and theater.

## Career

### 1976–1987: Rise to prominence

Seinfeld developed an interest in [stand-up comedy](/wiki/Stand-up_comedy) after brief stints in college productions. He appeared on [open-mic](/wiki/Open_mic) nights at Budd Friedman's Improv Club while attending [Queens College](/wiki/Queens_College,_City_University_of_New_York). After graduation in 1976, he tried out at an open-mic night at New York City's [Catch a Rising Star](/wiki/Catch_a_Rising_Star_(comedy_clubs)), which led to an appearance in a [Rodney Dangerfield](/wiki/Rodney_Dangerfield) [HBO](/wiki/HBO) special. In 1980, he had a small recurring role on the sitcom [*Benson*](/wiki/Benson_(TV_series)), playing Frankie, a mail-delivery boy who had comedy routines that no one wanted to hear. Seinfeld was abruptly fired from the show due to creative differences. Seinfeld said that he was not told he had been fired until he arrived for a read-through session and found that there was no script for him. In January 1981, he performed stand-up on *An Evening at the Improv*. In May, Seinfeld made an appearance on *[The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson](/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Johnny_Carson)*, impressing [Carson](/wiki/Johnny_Carson) and the audience, leading to frequent appearances on that show and others, including *[Late Night with David Letterman](/wiki/Late_Night_with_David_Letterman)*. On September 5, 1987, his first one-hour special *[Stand-Up Confidential](/wiki/Stand-Up_Confidential)* aired live on [HBO](/wiki/HBO).

### 1988–1998: *Seinfeld* and stardom

Main article: [Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)

Seinfeld at the [44th Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/44th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) in 1992

Seinfeld created *The Seinfeld Chronicles* with [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David) in 1988 for [NBC](/wiki/NBC). It was renamed *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)* to avoid confusion with the short-lived teen sitcom *The Marshall Chronicles*. By its third season, *Seinfeld* had become the most watched sitcom on American television. The final episode aired in 1998, and the show has been a popular [syndicated](/wiki/Television_syndication) re-run ever since. NBC offered Seinfeld $110 million—a record $5 million an episode for a 22-episode tenth season—but he declined. Along with Seinfeld, the show starred *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* alumna [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus) and established actors [Michael Richards](/wiki/Michael_Richards) and [Jason Alexander](/wiki/Jason_Alexander). Alexander played George, a caricature of [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David). Seinfeld is the only actor to appear in every episode.

### 1998–2010: Established career

After he ended his sitcom, Seinfeld moved back to New York City and returned to stand-up comedy instead of staying in Los Angeles and furthering his acting career. In 1998, he went on tour and recorded a comedy special, titled *[I'm Telling You for the Last Time](/wiki/I%27m_Telling_You_for_the_Last_Time)*. The process of developing and performing new material at clubs around the world was chronicled in a 2002 documentary, *[Comedian](/wiki/Comedian_(film))*, which also featured fellow comic [Orny Adams](/wiki/Orny_Adams) and was directed by Christian Charles. Seinfeld has written several books, mostly archives of past routines. In the late 1990s, [Apple Computer](/wiki/Apple_Computer) came up with the advertising slogan "[Think different](/wiki/Think_different)" and produced a 60-second commercial to promote the slogan. This commercial showed people who were able to "think differently", such as [Albert Einstein](/wiki/Albert_Einstein), [Mahatma Gandhi](/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi), [Martin Luther King Jr.](/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr.), and many others. It was later cut short to 30 seconds and altered such that Seinfeld was included at the end, although he had not been in the original cut. This shorter version of the commercial aired only once, during the [series finale](/wiki/The_Finale_(Seinfeld)) of *Seinfeld*.

Seinfeld at the [49th Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/49th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) in 1997

In 2004, Seinfeld appeared in two commercial [webisodes](/wiki/Webisode) promoting [American Express](/wiki/American_Express), titled *[The Adventures of Seinfeld & Superman](/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Seinfeld_%26_Superman)*. In these, Seinfeld appeared with a cartoon rendering of [Superman](/wiki/Superman), to whom reference was made in numerous episodes of *Seinfeld* as Seinfeld's hero, voiced by [Patrick Warburton](/wiki/Patrick_Warburton) (character [David Puddy](/wiki/David_Puddy) on *Seinfeld*). The webisodes were directed by [Barry Levinson](/wiki/Barry_Levinson) and aired briefly on television. Seinfeld and "Superman" were also interviewed by [Matt Lauer](/wiki/Matt_Lauer) in a specially recorded interview for the [*Today*](/wiki/Today_(NBC_program)) show. Also that year, Seinfeld appeared at the [National Museum of American History](/wiki/National_Museum_of_American_History) to donate the "puffy shirt" he wore in the *Seinfeld* episode [of the same name](/wiki/The_Puffy_Shirt). He joked it was "the most embarrassing moment in the history of the Smithsonian... a new low," but that he was proud of the honor. In 2006, Seinfeld had a cameo appearance on *Saturday Night Live* as host Julia Louis-Dreyfus' assassin. Louis-Dreyfus in her opening monologue mentioned the "Seinfeld curse". While talking about how ridiculous the "curse" was, a [stage light](/wiki/Stage_lighting_instrument) suddenly fell next to her. The camera moved to a [catwalk](/wiki/Catwalk_(theater)) above the stage where Seinfeld was standing, holding a large pair of bolt cutters. He angrily muttered, "Damn it!" upset that it did not hit her. Louis-Dreyfus continued to say that she is indeed not cursed.

Seinfeld appeared at the [79th Academy Awards](/wiki/79th_Academy_Awards) in 2007 as the presenter for "Best Documentary". Before announcing the nominations, he did a monologue about the unspoken agreement between movie theater owners and movie patrons. On October 4, 2007, Seinfeld made a guest appearance as himself in the *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)* episode "[SeinfeldVision](/wiki/SeinfeldVision)". On February 24, 2008, at the [80th Academy Awards](/wiki/80th_Academy_Awards), Seinfeld appeared as the [voice](/wiki/Voice_acting) of his *[Bee Movie](/wiki/Bee_Movie)* animated character Barry, presenting [Best Animated Short Film](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Animated_Short_Film). Before announcing the nominees, he showed a montage of film clips featuring bees, saying that they were some of his early work (as Barry).

Amidst his spring 2008 tour, Seinfeld performed in his hometown of New York City for a one-night-only show at the [Hammerstein Ballroom](/wiki/Hammerstein_Ballroom) to benefit *Stand Up for a Cure*, a charity aiding [lung cancer](/wiki/Lung_cancer) research at [Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center](/wiki/Memorial_Sloan_Kettering_Cancer_Center). That fall, Seinfeld became the pitchman for [Windows Vista](/wiki/Windows_Vista), as part of a $300-million advertising campaign by Microsoft. The ads, which were intended to create interest for Windows in support of the subsequent "I'm a PC" advertisements, were cut from television after three installments; Microsoft opted to continue with the "I'm a PC" advertisements and run the Seinfeld ads on the Microsoft website as a series of longer advertisements. In 2009, Seinfeld and the entire cast of *Seinfeld* appeared for a reunion in Larry David's HBO series *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)*. The fictional reunion took place in the [seventh season's](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm_Season_7) [finale](/wiki/Seinfeld_(Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)) and starred most of the original cast, including [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus), [Jason Alexander](/wiki/Jason_Alexander), [Michael Richards](/wiki/Michael_Richards), in a multiple-episode arc. Seinfeld appeared on an episode of the [Starz](/wiki/Starz_(TV_channel)) original series [*Head Case*](/wiki/Head_Case_(TV_series)). As was the case in many of his previous guest appearances on sitcoms, he played himself.

In Australia, Seinfeld appeared on a series of advertisements for the [Greater Building Society](/wiki/Greater_Building_Society), a [building society](/wiki/Building_society) based in [New South Wales](/wiki/New_South_Wales) and southeastern [Queensland](/wiki/Queensland). His appearance in these ads was highly publicized and considered a coup for the society, being the third time Seinfeld had appeared in a television commercial. The advertisements were filmed in Cedarhurst, [Long Island](/wiki/Long_Island), with the street designed to emulate Beaumont Street in [Hamilton](/wiki/Hamilton,_New_South_Wales), where the Greater's head offices are located. Seinfeld also wrote the scripts for the 15 advertisements that were filmed. The ads largely aired in the Northern New South Wales television market, where the society has most of its branches. Seinfeld was the first guest on [Jay Leno](/wiki/Jay_Leno)'s talk show *[The Jay Leno Show](/wiki/The_Jay_Leno_Show)*, which premiered on September 14, 2009. Seinfeld was featured on *Saturday Night Live*'s [Weekend Update](/wiki/Weekend_Update) sketch to do the "Really!?!" segment with [Seth Meyers](/wiki/Seth_Meyers). He executive produced and regularly appeared as a panelist in [*The Marriage Ref*](/wiki/The_Marriage_Ref_(U.S._TV_series)). In 2010, Seinfeld made a surprise guest appearance on *[The Howard Stern Show](/wiki/The_Howard_Stern_Show)*, ending the feud the two had in the early 1990s.

Seinfeld toured the U.S. in 2011 and made his first stand-up appearance in the [United Kingdom](/wiki/United_Kingdom) in 11 years. That same year, he was a surprise guest on *[The Daily Show](/wiki/The_Daily_Show)*, helping [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart) to suppress his urge to tell "cheap" "[Michele Bachmann](/wiki/Michele_Bachmann)'s husband acts gay" jokes. Seinfeld also launched a personal archives website at JerrySeinfeld.com, and appeared in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) special [*Talking Funny*](/wiki/Talking_Funny_(Television_special)) with fellow comedians [Chris Rock](/wiki/Chris_Rock), [Louis C.K.](/wiki/Louis_C.K.), and [Ricky Gervais](/wiki/Ricky_Gervais) in the same year.

### 2011–present: Talk show and expansion

Main article: [Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee](/wiki/Comedians_in_Cars_Getting_Coffee)

Seinfeld tapping on the Oval Office windows of the [White House](/wiki/White_House) with President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) in 2015

In 2012, Seinfeld started a web series titled *[Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee](/wiki/Comedians_in_Cars_Getting_Coffee)*, in which he would pick up a fellow comedian in a different car every episode and take them out for coffee and conversation. The show originally aired on the [Crackle](/wiki/Crackle_(streaming_service)) streaming service and then was bought by [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix). The initial series consisted of ten episodes lasting from 7 to 25 minutes each. The show has continued to get high-profile guests such as [Alec Baldwin](/wiki/Alec_Baldwin), [Mel Brooks](/wiki/Mel_Brooks), [Bill Burr](/wiki/Bill_Burr), [Dave Chappelle](/wiki/Dave_Chappelle), [Louis C.K.](/wiki/Louis_C.K.), [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David), [Ellen DeGeneres](/wiki/Ellen_DeGeneres), [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey), [David Letterman](/wiki/David_Letterman), [Jerry Lewis](/wiki/Jerry_Lewis), [Steve Martin](/wiki/Steve_Martin), [John Mulaney](/wiki/John_Mulaney), [Eddie Murphy](/wiki/Eddie_Murphy), [Carl Reiner](/wiki/Carl_Reiner), [Don Rickles](/wiki/Don_Rickles), [Chris Rock](/wiki/Chris_Rock), [Howard Stern](/wiki/Howard_Stern), and [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart). The show has also hosted *Seinfeld* alums [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David), [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus), [Jason Alexander](/wiki/Jason_Alexander), and [Michael Richards](/wiki/Michael_Richards). Season seven featured its most high-profile guest, then-President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama). In a farewell tribute video for the Obamas before the President left office, Seinfeld stated, "That knocking on the [Oval Office](/wiki/Oval_Office) window. That probably was the peak of my entire existence."

In 2014, Seinfeld told [David Letterman](/wiki/David_Letterman) he invited [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen) to be on the show but hadn't heard back. That same year he also revealed [Joan Rivers](/wiki/Joan_Rivers) was supposed to be a guest on the show before she died due to a botched medical procedure. Seinfeld signed a deal with [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) in January 2017 that included placing *Seinfeld* and *[Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee](/wiki/Comedians_in_Cars_Getting_Coffee)* on their streaming service as well as two new Seinfeld stand-up specials and the development of scripted and non-scripted comedy programming. As part of the deal, all episodes of *[Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee](/wiki/Comedians_in_Cars_Getting_Coffee)* were made available on the streaming service, in addition to a new 24-episode season. The series was nominated for five [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards) and won three [Webby Awards](/wiki/Webby_Awards).

> It's very important to know what you don't like. A big part of innovation is saying, "You know what I'm really sick of?" For me, that was talk shows where music plays, somebody walks out to a desk, shakes hands with the host, and sits down. "How are you?" "You look great." I'm also sick of people who are really there to sell their show or product. "What am I really sick of?" is where innovation begins.

— Seinfeld, talking about his process of innovation.

In 2013, Seinfeld appeared on rapper [Wale](/wiki/Wale_(rapper))'s album [*The Gifted*](/wiki/The_Gifted_(album)), on the song "Outro About Nothing". Seinfeld received coverage for his speech at the 2014 [Clio Awards](/wiki/Clio_Awards) ceremony, where he received an honorary award, as media reporters said that he "mocked" and "ripped apart" the advertising industry; his statement that "I love advertising because I love lying" received particular attention. In 2014, Seinfeld hosted the special *Don Rickles: One Night Only* at the [Apollo Theatre](/wiki/Apollo_Theatre). The event celebrated [Don Rickles](/wiki/Don_Rickles) and his career, but also served as a roast among friends. Those who participated in the event included [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart), [David Letterman](/wiki/David_Letterman), [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey), [Amy Poehler](/wiki/Amy_Poehler), [Nathan Lane](/wiki/Nathan_Lane), [Regis Philbin](/wiki/Regis_Philbin), [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro), and [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese). In 2015, Seinfeld made a guest appearance on the *[Saturday Night Live 40th Anniversary Special](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live_40th_Anniversary_Special)*, where he hosted the "Questions from the Audience" segment, which included cameos from [Michael Douglas](/wiki/Michael_Douglas), [John Goodman](/wiki/John_Goodman), [James Franco](/wiki/James_Franco), [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David), [Ellen Cleghorne](/wiki/Ellen_Cleghorne), [Dakota Johnson](/wiki/Dakota_Johnson), [Tim Meadows](/wiki/Tim_Meadows), [Bob Odenkirk](/wiki/Bob_Odenkirk), and [Sarah Palin](/wiki/Sarah_Palin) (who Seinfeld initially mistook for [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey)). Seinfeld then made a guest appearance on [David Letterman](/wiki/David_Letterman)'s final [*Late Show*](/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman) episode. Seinfeld joined guests including [Alec Baldwin](/wiki/Alec_Baldwin), [Barbara Walters](/wiki/Barbara_Walters), [Steve Martin](/wiki/Steve_Martin), [Jim Carrey](/wiki/Jim_Carrey), [Chris Rock](/wiki/Chris_Rock), [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus), [Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning), [Tina Fey](/wiki/Tina_Fey), and [Bill Murray](/wiki/Bill_Murray) who all participated in The Top Ten List segment, "Things I've Always Wanted to Say to Dave".

In 2017, Seinfeld went on *[The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon](/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_Starring_Jimmy_Fallon)* and joined [Dave Chappelle](/wiki/Dave_Chappelle) and [Jimmy Fallon](/wiki/Jimmy_Fallon) in honoring outgoing First Lady [Michelle Obama](/wiki/Michelle_Obama), and played a game of *[Catchphrase](/wiki/Catchphrase)*, which Obama and Fallon won to Seinfeld's dismay. On September 19, 2017, Netflix released the stand-up comedy special *[Jerry Before Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Before_Seinfeld)*. It follows Seinfeld as he returns for a stand-up routine at the [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) comedy club, [Comic Strip Live](/wiki/Comic_Strip_Live), which started his career. It is intercut with documentary clips and his stand-up special. It was later released as an LP, CD and download album, and was nominated for a 2018 [Grammy Award for Best Comedy Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Comedy_Album). In 2020, it was announced that Netflix would be releasing Seinfeld's first original stand-up special in 22 years, *[23 Hours to Kill](/wiki/23_Hours_to_Kill)*. The special premiered on May 5. In October 2020, Seinfeld joined [Steve Martin](/wiki/Steve_Martin) in a discussion about comedy at *[The New Yorker Festival](/wiki/The_New_Yorker_Festival)*. They discussed subjects ranging from the creative process, [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix), and [The Oscars](/wiki/The_Oscars), to their comedy backgrounds, and the future of comedy during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).

In 2024 he directed, co-wrote, and produced in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) comedy film *[Unfrosted](/wiki/Unfrosted)*, a satirical spoof about the creation of [Pop-Tarts](/wiki/Pop-Tarts). Seinfeld also starred in the film alongside [Melissa McCarthy](/wiki/Melissa_McCarthy), [Jim Gaffigan](/wiki/Jim_Gaffigan), and [Hugh Grant](/wiki/Hugh_Grant). The film earned mixed reviews with *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* writing the film received a "sharply divided reaction from critics". *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* labeled it a "Critic's Pick" with Anne Nicholson describing it as a "full-fledged, fully ridiculous feature comedy targeted to the audience's sweet-and-salty dopamine receptors". David Ehrlich of *[IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire)* wrote that the "comedy never heats up" and "it's a movie about so many different things at once that it comes to feel like a movie about nothing". Seinfeld appeared as a guest on *[John Mulaney Presents: Everybody's in LA](/wiki/John_Mulaney_Presents:_Everybody%27s_in_LA)* where he joked that it was "the weirdest [talk show](/wiki/Talk_show) I've ever been on in my life". He also embarked on a new tour starting with his first show in Singapore in June 2024 followed by a number of stops in Australia and North America. Seinfeld returned to *[Curb Your Enthusiasm](/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm)* in its final season reuniting with [Larry David](/wiki/Larry_David) where they poked fun at the controversial ending of *[Seinfeld](/wiki/Seinfeld)*. Ben Travers of *[IndieWire](/wiki/IndieWire)* wrote, "If the *Curb* finale is meant to rewrite the *Seinfeld* ending in any way, it's during that first scene between Jerry and Larry. They're playing out the kind of scene they used to write for Jerry and George, and getting that silly, joyful spark between two TV legends – even for a moment – is pure bliss".

## Books

Seinfeld wrote the book *[SeinLanguage](/wiki/SeinLanguage)*, released in 1993. Written as his television show was first rising in popularity, it is primarily an adaptation of his stand-up material. The title comes from an article in *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* listing the numerous catchphrases for which the show was responsible. In 2002, he wrote the [children's book](/wiki/Children%27s_literature) [*Halloween*](/wiki/Halloween_(children%27s_book)). The book was illustrated by James Bennett. Seinfeld wrote the forewords to [Ted L. Nancy](/wiki/Ted_L._Nancy)'s *Letters from a Nut* series of books and [Ed Broth](/wiki/Ed_Broth)'s *Stories from a Moron*. Seinfeld also wrote the foreword to the *[Peanut Butter & Co.](/wiki/Peanut_Butter_%26_Co.) Cookbook*. In October 2020, Seinfeld released his new book [*Is This Anything?*](/wiki/Is_This_Anything%3F_(book)). The book chronicles Seinfeld's 45 years working in comedy and contains many of his best bits that span from various decades. In February 2022, Seinfeld contributed original poetry to *Eating Salad Drunk,* a comedian poetry anthology (edited by author [Gabe Henry](/wiki/Gabe_Henry)) that benefited [Comedy Gives Back](/wiki/Amber_J._Lawson), a nonprofit supporting comedians in financial hardship.

## Influences

Seinfeld has stated, "On the [Mount Rushmore](/wiki/Mount_Rushmore) of stand-up comedy, there are four faces, in my opinion: [Richard Pryor](/wiki/Richard_Pryor), [George Carlin](/wiki/George_Carlin), [Bill Cosby](/wiki/Bill_Cosby), and [Don Rickles](/wiki/Don_Rickles)." Seinfeld has also cited as his influences [Jean Shepherd](/wiki/Jean_Shepherd), *[Mad Magazine](/wiki/Mad_Magazine)*, [Jonathan Winters](/wiki/Jonathan_Winters), [Jerry Lewis](/wiki/Jerry_Lewis), [Robert Klein](/wiki/Robert_Klein), and [Abbott and Costello](/wiki/Abbott_and_Costello). He stated, "[Monty Python](/wiki/Monty_Python) was a gigantic influence on me. They were just about silly, funny things that meant nothing, and that's the stuff I love. There's a wonderful childlike freedom in those kinds of things."

In the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) comedy special, *[Jerry Before Seinfeld](/wiki/Jerry_Before_Seinfeld)*, he displayed his personal comedy albums collection from when he was a teenager. These albums included:

## Personal life

Seinfeld is a fan of the [New York Mets](/wiki/New_York_Mets) and periodically calls [Steve Somers](/wiki/Steve_Somers)' show on [WFAN](/wiki/WFAN_(AM))-AM, a [sports radio](/wiki/Sports_radio) station, as "Jerry from Queens". Seinfeld called four innings of a Mets game on [SNY](/wiki/SNY) on June 23, 2010, reuniting with analyst [Keith Hernandez](/wiki/Keith_Hernandez), who appeared in the *Seinfeld* two-part episode entitled "[The Boyfriend](/wiki/The_Boyfriend_(Seinfeld))". According to Seinfeld, he thinks about baseball "all day" and has said "when I think of retirement, all I would think of is going to a baseball game every day."

Seinfeld is a fan of comic book characters, particularly [Superman](/wiki/Superman). In 2022, he appeared on a variant cover of *Batman/Superman: World's Finest* with the title characters.

Seinfeld is left-handed, and the first joke he wrote professionally was about the topic. In a 2014 interview with [NBC News](/wiki/NBC_News), he made statements suggesting that he believed he was on the [autism spectrum](/wiki/Autism_spectrum). However, following criticism for his alleged [self-diagnosis](/wiki/Self-diagnosis), he later clarified that he is not autistic and had been commenting on a play about the condition that he "related to [...] on some level".

### Relationships and marriage

Years before *Seinfeld* was created, Seinfeld dated [Carol Leifer](/wiki/Carol_Leifer). She was a fellow comedian, and one of the inspirations for the *Seinfeld* character [Elaine Benes](/wiki/Elaine_Benes). On national television with sex therapist and talk show host Dr. [Ruth Westheimer](/wiki/Ruth_Westheimer), he mentioned that he was engaged in 1984 but called it off.

Seinfeld with Lonstein (left) at the [47th Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/47th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) in 1995

In May 1993, then 39-year-old Seinfeld met 17-year-old [Shoshanna Lonstein](/wiki/Shoshanna_Lonstein_Gruss) in [Central Park](/wiki/Central_Park). Seinfeld and Lonstein dated for approximately four years, until 1997. She transferred from [George Washington University](/wiki/George_Washington_University) to [UCLA](/wiki/UCLA), in part to be with him, ultimately citing constant press coverage and missing New York City as reasons for the relationship ending.

Jessica and Jerry Seinfeld in 2010

In August 1998, while at a Reebok Sports Club, Seinfeld met [Jessica Sklar](/wiki/Jessica_Seinfeld), a [public relations](/wiki/Public_relations) executive for [Tommy Hilfiger](/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger) who had just returned from a three-week honeymoon in [Italy](/wiki/Italy) with then-husband Eric Nederlander, a theatrical producer and scion of a [theater-owning family](/wiki/Nederlander_Organization). Unaware of Sklar's marital status, Seinfeld invited her out. When Sklar eventually told Seinfeld about her relationship situation, she said, "I told him I didn't think this was the right time for me to be involved with anybody." Two months later, Sklar filed for divorce and began dating Seinfeld. The pair married on December 25, 1999. Comedian [George Wallace](/wiki/George_Wallace_(American_comedian)) was the best man at the wedding. After the nuptials, Jerry and Jessica Seinfeld bought [Billy Joel](/wiki/Billy_Joel)'s house in [Amagansett, New York](/wiki/Amagansett,_New_York), for US$32 million after news of the couple's interest in the property became public in 2000. The Seinfelds have a daughter and two sons.

### Wealth and charity

In 1999, Seinfeld auctioned a [Breitling](/wiki/Breitling_SA) Chronomat watch as part of the "Famous Faces, Watch Auction For Charity" event in New York City. This watch sold for $11,000. In 2001, Jerry and Jessica Seinfeld created the charitable organization [Good+Foundation](/wiki/Baby_Buggy) after their first child was born. Good+Foundation grants donations of products and services to programs that have demonstrated a capacity to address family poverty in three focus areas: supporting new mothers, investing in early childhood, and engaging fathers. GOOD+ Foundation has donated over $42M worth of items through its partner network across the United States. Seinfeld has also participated in [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart)'s charity event, *[Night of Too Many Stars](/wiki/Night_of_Too_Many_Stars)*.

According to *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* magazine, Seinfeld's cumulative earnings from *Seinfeld* as of 2004 was $267 million, placing him at the top of the celebrity earnings list that year. He turned down $5 million per episode, for 22 episodes, to continue the show for a 10th season. Seinfeld earned $100 million from syndication deals and stand-up performances in 2004, and $60 million in 2006. He also earned $10 million for appearing with [Bill Gates](/wiki/Bill_Gates) in [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft)'s 2008 advertisements for [Windows](/wiki/Windows). Between June 2008 and June 2009, Seinfeld earned $85 million, making him the world's highest-paid comedian during those 12 months. In 2013, *Forbes* documented Seinfeld's annual income as $32 million. In mid-2013, Seinfeld disputed *Forbes*'s claims regarding his income and net worth on *[The Howard Stern Show](/wiki/The_Howard_Stern_Show)*. Seinfeld was ranked by *Forbes* the highest-paid comedian for 2015, the second-highest-paid in 2016, and the highest-paid again in 2017. Seinfeld's income between June 2016 and June 2017 was $69 million.

In 2024, [*Bloomberg*](/wiki/Bloomberg_Businessweek) declared Seinfeld a [billionaire](/wiki/Billionaire), with a net worth standing at more than US$1 [billion](/wiki/Billion), thanks to various syndication deals his sitcom signed, with $465 million coming from those deals, making him one of the [richest celebrities](/wiki/List_of_celebrities_by_net_worth).

### Automobiles

Seinfeld's most common car acquisitions involve [Porsches](/wiki/Porsche).

Seinfeld is an automobile enthusiast and collector, and he owns a collection of about 150 cars, including many [Porsches](/wiki/Porsche). He rented a hangar at [Santa Monica Airport](/wiki/Santa_Monica_Airport) in [Santa Monica, California](/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California), in the 1990s to store cars. In 2002, Seinfeld purchased property on the [Upper West Side](/wiki/Upper_West_Side) of [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan) in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) where he built a $1.4 million two-story garage to store Porsches. One tally has Seinfeld owning 43 Porsches. Paul Bannister has written that Seinfeld's collection includes [Porsche 911s](/wiki/Porsche_911) from various years, 10 [Porsche Boxsters](/wiki/Porsche_Boxster) each painted a different color, and the 1955 [Porsche 550 Spyder](/wiki/Porsche_550_Spyder), the same model and pearl-grey color of the car [James Dean](/wiki/James_Dean) was driving in his fatal crash.

The centerpiece of the Seinfeld collection is [Steve McQueen](/wiki/Steve_McQueen)'s [Porsche 917](/wiki/Porsche_917) Chassis 022, driven extensively in the 1971 film [*Le Mans*](/wiki/Le_Mans_(film)), acquired by Seinfeld in 2002, and restored by Joe Cavaglieri to the 1971-era Gulf Porsche team livery.

The [Discovery Channel](/wiki/Discovery_Channel) television show *[Chasing Classic Cars](/wiki/Chasing_Classic_Cars)* claimed that Seinfeld owns the first and last produced air-cooled Porsche 911s. He has a $700,000 [Porsche 959](/wiki/Porsche_959), one of only 337 built. He was originally not allowed to drive it, because the car was "not [street legal](/wiki/Street-legal_vehicle)." U.S. emissions and crash tests had not been performed for the model because Porsche refused to donate four Porsche 959s for destruction tests. Seinfeld imported the car "[for exhibition purposes](/wiki/Grey_import_vehicles)," on the stipulation that it may never be driven on U.S. roads. The car was made U.S. street legal in 1999 under the "[Show or Display](/wiki/Show_or_Display)" federal law. Seinfeld wrote an article for the February 2004 issue of [*Automobile*](/wiki/Automobile_Magazine), reviewing the [Porsche Carrera GT](/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_GT).

In 2008, Seinfeld was involved in a car accident when the brakes on his 1967 [Fiat 500](/wiki/Fiat_500) failed and, to avoid an intersection, he pulled the emergency brake while turning sharply, ultimately causing the car to flip onto its side. No one was hurt.

### Coffee machines

A coffee aficionado, Seinfeld owns multiple [espresso machines](/wiki/Espresso_machine), including the $17,000 Elektra Belle Epoque and two machines manufactured by Slayer and [Breville](/wiki/Breville). Seinfeld described his single-group Slayer machine, which costs upwards of $8,500, as a "beautiful machine." When [NPR](/wiki/NPR) asked him about the influence of [coffee culture in the U.S.](/wiki/Coffee_culture), Seinfeld responded in 2013:

> I never liked [coffee] and I didn't understand it and I used to do a lot of stuff in my stand-up set in the '80s and '90s about how I don't 'get' coffee. And then something happened about five years ago. I started touring a lot, and we would have these great big, fun breakfasts in the hotel and [coffee] just seemed to go really well [with breakfast]. [Now], I've just started this espresso thing.

In a May 2024 *[GQ](/wiki/GQ)* interview titled "10 Things Jerry Seinfeld Can't Live Without", Seinfeld revealed that a [Bialetti](/wiki/Bialetti) [moka pot](/wiki/Moka_pot) is one of his must-haves. He described the process of making coffee with a moka pot as complex and time-consuming, but a pleasurable way to "waste time".

### Religion and politics

Seinfeld with members of Kibbutz [Be'eri](/wiki/Be%27eri) in the kibbutz dining room, December 19, 2023

Seinfeld is Jewish and has incorporated elements of his [Jewish identity](/wiki/Jewish_identity) in his work. Although he shared that his mother was born into a large family of Syrian Orthodox Jews, he admitted to being non-religious himself. Seinfeld stated that he took a [Scientology](/wiki/Scientology) course when he was in his 20s; he said that he found it interesting but that he did not pursue it any further.

Seinfeld expressed support for [Israel](/wiki/Israel) during the [Gaza war](/wiki/Gaza_war), saying "I will always stand with Israel and the Jewish people." In 2023, Seinfeld visited the headquarters of [Abducted and Missing Families Forum](/wiki/Abducted_and_Missing_Families_Forum) where he met with representatives of the families and with abductees who returned from [Hamas](/wiki/Hamas) captivity, and listened to their stories. On May 12, 2024, Seinfeld gave a [commencement address](/wiki/Commencement_speech) and received an honorary degree at [Duke University](/wiki/Duke_University). During his speech, a number of students booed, waved [Palestinian flags](/wiki/Palestinian_Flag) and walked out in protest. In June 2024, Seinfeld was heckled by protesters during a comedy show in [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney), Australia. Seinfeld responded joking, "You're really influencing everyone here. We're all on your side now, because you've made your point so well, and in the right venue, you've come to the right place for a political conversation". A similar incident occurred at a later show in [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne). In February 2025, Seinfeld was approached outside an event by an influencer who asked for a selfie and then — instead of a photo — held up a peace sign and said "Free Palestine." When asked to repeat the phrase, Seinfeld shook his head and replied: "I don't care about Palestine."

Seinfeld has expressed his distaste for what he calls [political correctness](/wiki/Political_correctness). In 2015, Seinfeld stated that he avoids performing on college campuses because students have become too easily offended by his comedic routines. In a 2024 interview with *[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker)*, Seinfeld claimed political correctness was destroying comedy, saying, "This is the result of the [extreme left](/wiki/Far-left_politics) and PC crap, and people worrying so much about offending other people. When you write a script and it goes into four or five different hands, committees, groups – 'Here's our thought about this joke' – well, that's the end of your comedy." Months later, he walked back those comments on the "Breaking Bread" podcast, stating "I don't think, as I said, 'the extreme left' has done anything to inhibit the art of comedy. I'm taking that back officially." Seinfeld said he regrets his previous comments because changing cultural attitudes are "not [his] business."

On September 9, 2025, speaking at Duke University at an event with [Omer Shem Tov](/wiki/Omer_Shem_Tov), Seinfeld characterized the "Free Palestine" movement as antisemitic and compared it to the [Ku Klux Klan](/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan), saying "Free Palestine is, to me, just — you're free to say you don't like Jews. Just say you don't like Jews." He added, "By saying Free Palestine, you're not admitting what you really think. So it's actually — compared to the Ku Klux Klan, I'm actually thinking the Klan is actually a little better here because they can come right out and say, 'We don't like Blacks, we don't like Jews.' Okay that's honest."

### Transcendental Meditation

In December 2012, Seinfeld said that he had been practicing [Transcendental Meditation](/wiki/Transcendental_Meditation) for 40 years. He promoted the use of the [Transcendental Meditation technique](/wiki/Transcendental_Meditation_technique) in the treatment of [post-traumatic stress disorder](/wiki/Post-traumatic_stress_disorder) with [Bob Roth](/wiki/Bob_Roth) of the [David Lynch Foundation](/wiki/David_Lynch_Foundation) in December 2012 on *[Good Morning America](/wiki/Good_Morning_America)*, and also appeared at a 2009 David Lynch Foundation benefit for TM, at which [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney) and [Ringo Starr](/wiki/Ringo_Starr) appeared. On November 5, 2015, the David Lynch Foundation organized a benefit concert at New York City's [Carnegie Hall](/wiki/Carnegie_Hall) called "Change Begins Within" to promote transcendental meditation for stress control. "It's been the greatest companion technique of living that I've ever come across, and I'm thrilled to be part of this movement that seems to have really been reinvigorated by Bob [Roth] and [David Lynch](/wiki/David_Lynch)," Seinfeld said. "I would do anything that I could to promote it in the world, because I think it's the greatest thing as a life tool, as a work tool and just making things make sense."

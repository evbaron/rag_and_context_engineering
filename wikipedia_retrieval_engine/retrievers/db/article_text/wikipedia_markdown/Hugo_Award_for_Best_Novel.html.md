# Hugo Award for Best Novel

## Infobox

- **Awarded for:** Best science fiction or fantasy story of 40,000 words or more published in the prior calendar year
- **Presented by:** World Science Fiction Society
- **First award:** 1953
- **Most recent winner:** Robert Jackson Bennett ( The Tainted Cup )
- **Website:** thehugoawards.org

The **Hugo Award for Best Novel** is one of the [Hugo Awards](/wiki/Hugo_Award) given each year by the [World Science Fiction Society](/wiki/World_Science_Fiction_Society) for [science fiction](/wiki/Science_fiction) or [fantasy](/wiki/Fantasy) stories published in, or translated to, English during the previous calendar year. The [novel](/wiki/Novel) award is available for works of fiction of 40,000 words or more; awards are also given out in the [short story](/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Short_Story), [novelette](/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Novelette), and [novella](/wiki/Hugo_Award_for_Best_Novella) categories. The Hugo Awards have been described as "a fine showcase for [speculative fiction](/wiki/Speculative_fiction)", and "the best known literary award for science fiction writing".

The Hugo Award for Best Novel has been awarded annually by the World Science Fiction Society since 1953, except in 1954 and 1957. In addition, between 1996 and 2025, Retrospective Hugo Awards or "Retro-Hugos" were available for works published 50, 75, or 100 years prior. Retro-Hugos could only be awarded for years after 1939 in which no awards were originally given. Retro-Hugo awards were given for novels for 1939, 1941, 1943–1946, 1951, and 1954.

Hugo Award nominees and winners are chosen by supporting or attending members of the annual [World Science Fiction Convention](/wiki/Worldcon), or *Worldcon*, and the presentation evening constitutes its central event. The final selection process is defined in the World Science Fiction Society Constitution as [instant-runoff voting](/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting) with six finalists, except in the case of a tie. The novels on the ballot are the six most-nominated by members that year, with no limit on the number of stories that can be nominated. The 1953, 1955, and 1958 awards did not include a recognition of runner-up novels, but since 1959 all final candidates have been recorded. Initial nominations are made by members from January through March, while voting on the ballot of six finalists is performed roughly from April through July, subject to change depending on when that year's Worldcon is held. Prior to 2017, the final ballot was five works; it was changed that year to six, with each initial nominator limited to five nominations. Worldcons are generally held in August or early September, and are held in a different city around the world each year.

During the 80 nomination years, 182 authors have had works as finalists and 55 have won (including co-authors, ties, and Retro-Hugos). Two translators have been noted along with the author of a novel written in a language other than English: [Ken Liu](/wiki/Ken_Liu), in 2015 and 2017, for translations of two works from Chinese; and [Rita Barisse](/wiki/Rita_Barisse), in 2019, who was retroactively noted as the translator of a 1963 French novel. [Robert A. Heinlein](/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein) has won the most Hugos for Best Novel, and also appeared on the most final ballots; he has six wins (four Hugos and two Retro-Hugos) out of twelve finalists. [Lois McMaster Bujold](/wiki/Lois_McMaster_Bujold) has received four Hugos out of ten finalists. Five authors have won three times: [Isaac Asimov](/wiki/Isaac_Asimov) and [Fritz Leiber](/wiki/Fritz_Leiber) (with two Hugos and one Retro-Hugo each), [N. K. Jemisin](/wiki/N._K._Jemisin), [Connie Willis](/wiki/Connie_Willis), and [Vernor Vinge](/wiki/Vernor_Vinge). Ten other authors have won the award twice. The next-most finalists by a winning author are held by [Robert J. Sawyer](/wiki/Robert_J._Sawyer) and [Larry Niven](/wiki/Larry_Niven), who have been finalists nine and eight times, respectively, and have each only won once. With nine finalist appearance, [Robert Silverberg](/wiki/Robert_Silverberg) has the greatest number of finalists without winning any. Three authors have won the award in consecutive years: [Orson Scott Card](/wiki/Orson_Scott_Card) (1986 and 1987), Lois McMaster Bujold (1991 and 1992), and N. K. Jemisin (2016, 2017, and 2018).

### Retro-Hugos

Between the [1996 Worldcon](/wiki/54th_World_Science_Fiction_Convention) and [2025 Worldcon](/wiki/83rd_World_Science_Fiction_Convention), the World Science Fiction Society had the concept of "Retro-Hugos", in which the Hugo award could be retroactively awarded for 50, 75, or 100 years prior. Retro-Hugos could only be awarded for years after 1939 (the year of the first [Worldcon](/wiki/Worldcon)) in which no Hugos were originally awarded. Retro-Hugos were awarded eight times, for 1939, 1941, 1943–46, 1951, and 1954.

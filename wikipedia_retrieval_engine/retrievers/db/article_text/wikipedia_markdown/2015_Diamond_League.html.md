# 2015 Diamond League

## Infobox

- **Edition:** 6th
- **Dates:** 15 May – 11 September
- **Events:** 32
- **Meetings:** 14

The **2015 IAAF Diamond League** was the sixth season of the [annual series](/wiki/Diamond_League) of outdoor [track and field](/wiki/Track_and_field) meetings, organised by the [International Association of Athletics Federations](/wiki/International_Association_of_Athletics_Federations) (IAAF). It consisted of fourteen one-day meetings, starting on 15 May in [Doha](/wiki/Doha), Qatar, and ending on 11 September in [Brussels](/wiki/Brussels), Belgium. Other events were held in [Shanghai](/wiki/Shanghai), [Eugene](/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon), Rome, [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham), [Oslo](/wiki/Oslo), New York City, Paris, [Lausanne](/wiki/Lausanne), [Fontvieille, Monaco](/wiki/Fontvieille,_Monaco), London, [Stockholm](/wiki/Stockholm), and [Zürich](/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich). All previous venues remained on the tour, with the exception that London returned to host the second meeting in the United Kingdom, having been replaced by [Glasgow](/wiki/Glasgow) during the [2014 IAAF Diamond League](/wiki/2014_IAAF_Diamond_League).

## Events

There are 16 men's and 16 women's disciplines in the Diamond League and seven events per discipline in the season. The total prize money for each competition is US$30,000, with a winner's share of $10,000. The season winner of each discipline wins US$40,000.

## Meeting highlights

### Doha

Three Diamond League records were set in [Doha](/wiki/Doha) during the opening competition of the series. [Allyson Felix](/wiki/Allyson_Felix) of the United States won the women's [200 metres](/wiki/200_metres) with a run of 21.98 seconds to equal [Veronica Campbell Brown](/wiki/Veronica_Campbell_Brown)'s time which was set during the inaugural [2010 IAAF Diamond League](/wiki/2010_IAAF_Diamond_League) season. [Jasmin Stowers](/wiki/Jasmin_Stowers), making her debut on the circuit, broke the women's [100 metres hurdles](/wiki/100_metres_hurdles) record with a time of 12.35 seconds – this raised her to seventh on the all-time rankings for the discipline. The third record of the evening came in the men's [triple jump](/wiki/Triple_jump): Cuba's [Pedro Pablo Pichardo](/wiki/Pedro_Pablo_Pichardo) became the first person to go beyond eighteen metres in series history, setting a [national record](/wiki/List_of_Cuban_records_in_athletics) and Diamond League best of 18.06 m (59 ft 3 in). This placed him third on the all-time lists for the sport behind [Jonathan Edwards](/wiki/Jonathan_Edwards_(triple_jumper)) and [Kenny Harrison](/wiki/Kenny_Harrison). The reigning Olympic champion, [Christian Taylor](/wiki/Christian_Taylor_(athlete)), was runner-up and moved himself up to fourth on the all-time lists with a mark of 18.04 m (59 ft 2 in), making the contest the first in history to feature two men going beyond eighteen metres. Among the other results, [Justin Gatlin](/wiki/Justin_Gatlin) of the United States became the fifth fastest man in the history of the [100 metres](/wiki/100_metres) with his winning time of 9.74 seconds (bettering the world record of 9.77 seconds he set in 2006, which was annulled due to a failed doping test).

### Shanghai

[Almaz Ayana](/wiki/Almaz_Ayana) of Ethiopia gave the stand-out performance at the Shanghai Diamond League by running the third fastest women's [5000 metres](/wiki/5000_metres) of all time, finishing in 14:14.32 minutes for a Diamond League record. [Mutaz Essa Barshim](/wiki/Mutaz_Essa_Barshim) defeated [Bohdan Bondarenko](/wiki/Bohdan_Bondarenko) in the men's [high jump](/wiki/High_jump) with a meeting record and world-leading mark of 2.38 m (7 ft 9+1⁄2 in). In addition to these two performances, there were four other world-leading marks set during the competition: [Silas Kiplagat](/wiki/Silas_Kiplagat) (men's 1500 m), [Jairus Kipchoge Birech](/wiki/Jairus_Kipchoge_Birech) (men's steeplechase), [Gong Lijiao](/wiki/Gong_Lijiao) (women's shot put) and [Nikoleta Kyriakopoulou](/wiki/Nikoleta_Kyriakopoulou) (women's pole vault). Kyriakopoulou's vault of 4.75 m (15 ft 7 in) was also a [Greek record](/wiki/List_of_Greek_records_in_athletics). Aside from Gong, [Lü Huihui](/wiki/L%C3%BC_Huihui) was China's only other home nation victor, and her winning mark of 64.08 m (210 ft 2+3⁄4 in) from the women's [javelin throw](/wiki/Javelin_throw) was also a meeting record.

### Eugene

[Renaud Lavillenie](/wiki/Renaud_Lavillenie) continued the streak of Diamond League records by winning the men's [pole vault](/wiki/Pole_vault) with a mark of 6.05 m (19 ft 10 in), raising him to joint second on the all-time outdoor lists. Wind and temperature conditions suited the sprinting events. [Justin Gatlin](/wiki/Justin_Gatlin) won the men's [200 metres](/wiki/200_metres) in a world lead 19.68 seconds, while [Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce](/wiki/Shelly-Ann_Fraser-Pryce) won the women's [100 metres](/wiki/100_metres) in another world lead of 10.81 seconds, sharing that time with [Murielle Ahouré](/wiki/Murielle_Ahour%C3%A9). Six women ran under 10.9 seconds that day, and China's [Su Bingtian](/wiki/Su_Bingtian) became the first man of Asian descent to break the [10-second barrier](/wiki/10-second_barrier) in the 100 m. [Mutaz Essa Barshim](/wiki/Mutaz_Essa_Barshim) continued his high jump streak with a meet record and world lead of 2.41 m (7 ft 10+3⁄4 in), while [Caterine Ibargüen](/wiki/Caterine_Ibarg%C3%BCen) extended her undefeated run to 24 [triple jump](/wiki/Triple_jump) competitions. [Genzebe Dibaba](/wiki/Genzebe_Dibaba) attempted to top Almaz Ayana's [5000 metres](/wiki/5000_metres) run from Shanghai: although she came up short, her winning time of 14:19.76 was the fifth fastest ever run for the distance. A total of thirteen world-leading marks and six meet records were set at the two-day competition.

### Rome

A total of five world leading marks were set at the [Golden Gala](/wiki/Golden_Gala). Ethiopian male distance runners provided two of these, with [Mohamed Aman](/wiki/Mohamed_Aman_(athlete)) winning the 800 m in 1:43.56 minutes and 17-year-old [Yomif Kejelcha](/wiki/Yomif_Kejelcha) taking his first Diamond race victory in the 5000 m with the first sub-13-minute run of the season. [Hyvin Kiyeng Jepkemoi](/wiki/Hyvin_Kiyeng_Jepkemoi), also in her first Diamond race win, took the women's steeplechase in 9:15.08 minutes (a personal best by more than six seconds). The more experienced [Ruth Beitia](/wiki/Ruth_Beitia) topped the women's high jump with a clearance of 2.00 m (6 ft 6+1⁄2 in) – the 36-year-old Spaniard became the oldest woman to jump over that height. American [Jennifer Simpson](/wiki/Jennifer_Simpson) became the first woman under four minutes for the 1500 m that year.

Two meeting records were bettered in Rome. [Justin Gatlin](/wiki/Justin_Gatlin) knocked one hundredth off [Usain Bolt](/wiki/Usain_Bolt)'s 2012 time with 9.75 seconds in the men's 100 m. [Pedro Pablo Pichardo](/wiki/Pedro_Pablo_Pichardo) took down two much longer-standing marks in the men's triple jump with his jump of 17.96 m (58 ft 11 in): [Jonathan Edwards](/wiki/Jonathan_Edwards_(triple_jumper)) 1998 meet record and also [Khristo Markov](/wiki/Khristo_Markov)'s 1987 stadium record, which was the winning mark of the [1987 World Championships in Athletics](/wiki/1987_World_Championships_in_Athletics). Other marks of note were [Thiago Braz da Silva](/wiki/Thiago_Braz_da_Silva)'s [South American record](/wiki/List_of_South_American_records_in_athletics) of 5.86 m (19 ft 2+1⁄2 in) in the men's pole vault and men's javelin throw national records from Kenya's [Julius Yego](/wiki/Julius_Yego) and Olympic champion [Keshorn Walcott](/wiki/Keshorn_Walcott) of Trinidad and Tobago. A strong women's 100 m hurdles field was notable for high-profile failures: Olympic champion [Sally Pearson](/wiki/Sally_Pearson) fell heavily and was injured, world champion [Brianna Rollins](/wiki/Brianna_Rollins) clashed a hurdle and fell, and world leader [Jasmin Stowers](/wiki/Jasmin_Stowers) also hit a hurdle heavily and walked to the finish.

### Birmingham

The best performance at the Birmingham Grand Prix came in the men's javelin with Kenya's [Julius Yego](/wiki/Julius_Yego) greatly improving to 91.39 m (299 ft 10 in) to break the Diamond League record and the [African record](/wiki/List_of_African_records_in_athletics). This mark was the best recorded in the discipline since 2006. There was some discussion over the validity of the throw: officials initially judged the throw to be outside the allowable sector, despite the throw going beyond the area that officials had marked out, and only was the meeting had finished did they accept Yego's effort. Another world lead and African record came in the non-standard 300 m sprint as [Wayde van Niekerk](/wiki/Wayde_van_Niekerk) won in 31.63 seconds.

[Sandra Perković](/wiki/Sandra_Perkovi%C4%87) was the only athlete to set a meeting record, doing so in the women's discus with a mark of 69.23 m (227 ft 1+1⁄2 in), improving on former Olympic champion [Ellina Zvereva](/wiki/Ellina_Zvereva)'s 13-year-old record. Perković win made her the only athlete of the meeting to have won all Diamond races in a discipline after just five meetings, as [Marvin Bracy](/wiki/Marvin_Bracy)'s 100 m win erased Justin Gatlin's streak. Home athlete [Adam Gemili](/wiki/Adam_Gemili) broke the [10-second barrier](/wiki/10-second_barrier) for the first time in that race, becoming the sixth Briton to achieve the feat.

### Oslo

In poor weather conditions, there were few record performances in Oslo. [Genzebe Dibaba](/wiki/Genzebe_Dibaba) fell short in her 5000 m world record attempt, although her winning time of 14:21.29 minutes was still the seventh fastest ever recorded at that point. The sole world-leading performance of the evening was [Kaliese Spencer](/wiki/Kaliese_Spencer)'s 400 m hurdles win in 54.15 seconds. This brought the Jamaican to twelve points in the Diamond race. [Caterine Ibargüen](/wiki/Caterine_Ibarg%C3%BCen) also reached twelve points, extending her three-year unbeaten run. No men were left unbeaten in their Diamond race due to two unexpected victories. A men's high jump billed as a contest between [Mutaz Essa Barshim](/wiki/Mutaz_Essa_Barshim), [Bohdan Bondarenko](/wiki/Bohdan_Bondarenko) and [Ivan Ukhov](/wiki/Ivan_Ukhov) saw all finish outside the top two, as China's [Zhang Guowei](/wiki/Zhang_Guowei_(high_jumper)) had his first Diamond League victory and [Marco Fassinotti](/wiki/Marco_Fassinotti) equalled the [Italian record](/wiki/List_of_Italian_records_in_athletics) of 2.33 m (7 ft 7+1⁄2 in). [Robert Urbanek](/wiki/Robert_Urbanek) ousted his fellow Pole [Piotr Małachowski](/wiki/Piotr_Ma%C5%82achowski) to prevent him from taking three straight wins in the men's discus. In the absence of Diamond race leader [Kirani James](/wiki/Kirani_James), [Steven Gardiner](/wiki/Steven_Gardiner) broke the men's 400 m streak and took his first Diamond League win.

The greatest upset of the night came through [Laura Muir](/wiki/Laura_Muir) in the women's 1500 m. The Briton built up a 40-metre lead to have her first major circuit win ahead of the more favoured [Abeba Aregawi](/wiki/Abeba_Aregawi). [Marharyta Dorozhon](/wiki/Marharyta_Dorozhon) was another first time winner, achieving the feat in the women's javelin in an [Israeli record](/wiki/List_of_Israeli_records_in_athletics) of 64.56 m (211 ft 9+1⁄2 in). The only other national record of the meet was a [Belgian](/wiki/List_of_Belgian_records_in_athletics) one of 3:51.84 minutes in the 1500 m by [Pieter-Jan Hannes](/wiki/Pieter-Jan_Hannes). [Asbel Kiprop](/wiki/Asbel_Kiprop) had his fourth career win at the Bislett [Dream Mile](/wiki/Dream_Mile), which was in its 50th anniversary.

### New York

As in Oslo, poor weather meant only one world-leading mark was recorded in New York: [Francena McCorory](/wiki/Francena_McCorory) improved the women's 400 m time to 49.86 seconds – also a meeting record. The women's field events provided two more meeting records: [Christabel Nettey](/wiki/Christabel_Nettey)'s clearance of 6.92 m (22 ft 8+1⁄4 in) brought her her first long jump win on the circuit, while [Ruth Beitia](/wiki/Ruth_Beitia) and [Blanka Vlašić](/wiki/Blanka_Vla%C5%A1i%C4%87) were both clear at 1.97 m (6 ft 5+1⁄2 in) for a women's high jump record. [Ajeé Wilson](/wiki/Aje%C3%A9_Wilson) brought an end to [Eunice Jepkoech Sum](/wiki/Eunice_Jepkoech_Sum)'s run in the women's 800 m and [Sandra Perković](/wiki/Sandra_Perkovi%C4%87) became the first athlete of the series to gather four straight wins.

In a non-Diamond race, [Usain Bolt](/wiki/Usain_Bolt) extended his unbeaten record in the 200 m, although his winning margin was narrow and his time over a second off his best. [Wayde van Niekerk](/wiki/Wayde_van_Niekerk) took the men's 400 m in a [South African record](/wiki/List_of_South_African_records_in_athletics) time. [Nikoleta Kyriakopoulou](/wiki/Nikoleta_Kyriakopoulou) of Greece also set a national record, but her pole vault clearance of 4.80 m (15 ft 8+3⁄4 in) left her second to [Fabiana Murer](/wiki/Fabiana_Murer) on count-back.

### Paris

Six world leading marks were established in Paris. [Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce](/wiki/Shelly-Ann_Fraser-Pryce)'s 100 m in 10.74 was also a meeting record, as was [Jairus Kipchoge Birech](/wiki/Jairus_Kipchoge_Birech)'s steeplechase victory in 7:58.83. [Nikoleta Kyriakopoulou](/wiki/Nikoleta_Kyriakopoulou)'s women's pole vault world lead also equalled the Diamond League record of 4.83 m (15 ft 10 in) and improved her [Greek record](/wiki/List_of_Greek_records_in_athletics). [Eunice Sum](/wiki/Eunice_Sum) (800 m), [Orlando Ortega](/wiki/Orlando_Ortega) (110 m hurdles) and [Silas Kiplagat](/wiki/Silas_Kiplagat) (1500 m) were the others to set world leads.

[Genzebe Dibaba](/wiki/Genzebe_Dibaba) and [Almaz Ayana](/wiki/Almaz_Ayana) were both short again in a women's 5000 m world record attempt, although Genzebe's winning time of 14:15.21 was a new meet record. Three continental records were set at the meet: [Wayde van Niekerk](/wiki/Wayde_van_Niekerk) of South Africa ran an [African record](/wiki/List_of_African_records_in_athletics) of 43.96 for the men's 400 m, France's [Jimmy Vicaut](/wiki/Jimmy_Vicaut) was runner-up to [Asafa Powell](/wiki/Asafa_Powell) in the men's 100 m but equalled the [European record](/wiki/List_of_European_records_in_athletics) at 9.86, while [Evan Jager](/wiki/Evan_Jager) of the United States set a men's steeplechase [record for the NACAC region](/wiki/List_of_North,_Central_American_and_Caribbean_records_in_athletics). Jager did so despite a fall to the track at the last hurdle – a mistake that cost him the win over Birech and a certain sub-8 performance. A number of [national records in athletics](/wiki/National_records_in_athletics) were also broken that day: [Sergey Shubenkov](/wiki/Sergey_Shubenkov) improved the [Russian record](/wiki/List_of_Russian_records_in_athletics) in the 110 m hurdles, [Konstadinos Filippidis](/wiki/Konstadinos_Filippidis) broke his nation's men's pole vault (a double for Greece in the event), [Konstadinos Douvalidis](/wiki/Konstadinos_Douvalidis) equalled his Greek record in the 110 m hurdles, [Selina Büchel](/wiki/Selina_B%C3%BCchel) improved the women's 800 m [Swiss record](/wiki/List_of_Swiss_records_in_athletics) to 1:57.95, and in the women's 400 m hurdles [Sara Petersen](/wiki/Sara_Petersen_(hurdler)) and [Kemi Adekoya](/wiki/Kemi_Adekoya) broke their respective [Danish](/wiki/List_of_Danish_records_in_athletics) and [Bahraini records](/wiki/List_of_Bahraini_records_in_athletics).

Two long-standing streaks were brought to an end at the competition. [Valerie Adams](/wiki/Valerie_Adams), returning from surgery, lost to [Christina Schwanitz](/wiki/Christina_Schwanitz) in the women's shot put after having had 56 consecutive wins. [Renaud Lavillenie](/wiki/Renaud_Lavillenie) was the favourite for the men's pole vault, but finished in fifth place and recorded his first loss in seven years at the meeting on home soil. [Zuzana Hejnová](/wiki/Zuzana_Hejnov%C3%A1) won the 400 m hurdles to prevent [Kaliese Spencer](/wiki/Kaliese_Spencer) (who was absent) from taking a fourth straight win. One streak that did continue was that of Colombia's [Caterine Ibargüen](/wiki/Caterine_Ibarg%C3%BCen), who joined [Sandra Perković](/wiki/Sandra_Perkovi%C4%87) as the only unbeaten athletes on the Diamond League that year.

### Lausanne

The [Athletissima](/wiki/Athletissima) meeting saw one world leading performance: 2.03 m (6 ft 7+3⁄4 in) by [Anna Chicherova](/wiki/Anna_Chicherova) in the women's high jump. Four meet records were improved. [Christian Taylor](/wiki/Christian_Taylor_(athlete)) defeated [Pedro Pablo Pichardo](/wiki/Pedro_Pablo_Pichardo) with a triple jump of 18.06 m (59 ft 3 in) – the joint fourth best jump ever at that point. [David Storl](/wiki/David_Storl)'s shot put of 22.20 m (72 ft 10 in) was another meet record and the best by a European since 1988. The women's steeplechase record was bettered by [Virginia Nyambura](/wiki/Virginia_Nyambura), while [Keshorn Walcott](/wiki/Keshorn_Walcott) set a [Trinidad and Tobago national record](/wiki/List_of_Trinidad_and_Tobago_records_in_athletics) and meet record of 90.16 m (295 ft 9+1⁄2 in) in the men's javelin.

[Yaime Pérez](/wiki/Yaime_P%C3%A9rez) had her first ever Diamond League win and in doing so brought an end to [Sandra Perković](/wiki/Sandra_Perkovi%C4%87)'s unbeaten streak in the women's discus throw. [Zharnel Hughes](/wiki/Zharnel_Hughes) (men's 200 m) and [Shaunae Miller](/wiki/Shaunae_Miller) (women's 400 m) also had their first ever wins on the Diamond League circuit.

### Monaco

At the [Herculis](/wiki/Herculis) meet [Genzebe Dibaba](/wiki/Genzebe_Dibaba) provided the highlight with her [1500 metres world record](/wiki/1500_metres_world_record) of 3:50.07 minutes, breaking [Qu Yunxia](/wiki/Qu_Yunxia)'s time from the [1993 Chinese Games](/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1993_National_Games_of_China). The veracity of the previous record had been subject to doubt and Genzebe's time—almost two and a half seconds faster than any time set outside the [Chinese Games](/wiki/Athletics_at_the_National_Games_of_China)—attracted questions in addition to plaudits. In response, she said "I want to live my own history. I`m not interested in others or the conditions". Nearly six seconds behind her, [Sifan Hassan](/wiki/Sifan_Hassan) set a [Dutch record](/wiki/List_of_Dutch_records_in_athletics) and [Shannon Rowbury](/wiki/Shannon_Rowbury) ran a [North American record](/wiki/List_of_North_American_records_in_athletics).

One other Diamond League record was improved that evening, by [Joe Kovacs](/wiki/Joe_Kovacs) in the men's shot put whose throw of 22.56 m (74 ft 0 in) moved him to eighth on the all-time lists and was the best throw recorded in over a decade. Though not a Diamond race, the men's 1500 m saw [Asbel Kiprop](/wiki/Asbel_Kiprop) move up to third on the all-time lists with a run of 3:26.69 minutes. A total of six meeting records were broken – [Habiba Ghribi](/wiki/Habiba_Ghribi)'s 9:11.28 in the women's steeplechase, Christian Taylor's 17.75 m (58 ft 2+3⁄4 in) men's triple jump, and [Justin Gatlin](/wiki/Justin_Gatlin)'s 9.78 in the men's 100 m, were the other three marks addition to the above-mentioned performances. A total of eight world-leading performances were recorded.

[Amel Tuka](/wiki/Amel_Tuka) (men's 800 m) and [Candyce McGrone](/wiki/Candyce_McGrone) (women's 200 m) both won their first Diamond League races in personal bests, with Tuka improving over a second and a half in three races in July, breaking his own [Bosnia and Herzegovina record](/wiki/List_of_Bosnian_and_Herzegovinian_records_in_athletics) and becoming the 11th fastest performer of all time. Following defeat at the previous leg, [Sandra Perković](/wiki/Sandra_Perkovi%C4%87) returned for a fifth win in the women's discus.

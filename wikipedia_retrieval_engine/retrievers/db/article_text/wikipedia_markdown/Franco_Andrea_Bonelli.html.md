# Franco Andrea Bonelli

- **Died:** 18 November 1830 (1830-11-18) (aged 46)

**Franco Andrea Bonelli** (10 November 1784 – 18 November 1830) was an Italian [ornithologist](/wiki/Ornithology), [entomologist](/wiki/Entomologist) and collector. He worked at the University of Turin and was involved in organising the collections of the natural history museum. [Bonelli's warbler](/wiki/Bonelli%27s_warbler_(disambiguation)) (now split into two species) and [Bonelli's eagle](/wiki/Bonelli%27s_eagle) are named after him.

## Life

Very little is known about the early life of Bonelli: he was born in [Cuneo](/wiki/Cuneo) in a wealthy family. He went to study in Fossano and Turin. He was interested from an early age in the fauna which surrounded him, making collecting trips, preparing specimens and noting his observations. He corresponded with Michele Spirito Giorna at the University of Turin, the Genoan entomologist Maximilian Spinola, and the French naturalists Latreille and Cuvier.

He became a member of the [Reale Società Agraria di Torino](/wiki/Reale_Societ%C3%A0_Agraria_di_Torino) in 1807 when he presented his first studies relating to the [Coleoptera](/wiki/Coleoptera) of [Piedmont](/wiki/Piedmont). The high quality of these studies attracted the interest of the naturalists of his time.

In April 1810, George Vat was sent to [Turin](/wiki/Turin) by the French government to reorganise the [University of Turin](/wiki/University_of_Turin) and begin its fusion with the [Impériale University](/wiki/University_of_France) founded by [Napoleon](/wiki/Napoleon). The zoology position became vacant with the death of Michele Spirito Giorna. Vat was very impressed by Bonelli's knowledge. Vat encouraged him to further his knowledge by coming to follow courses at the [Natural History Museum](/wiki/Mus%C3%A9um_national_d%27Histoire_naturelle) in [Paris](/wiki/Paris).

[Bonelli's warbler](/wiki/Bonelli%27s_warbler_(disambiguation)) is named after Franco Andrea Bonelli

Bonelli took this advice so as to obtain a professor's chair in the new university. In September 1810, he arrived in Paris.

In 1811, Bonelli was finally named professor of zoology at the [University of Turin](/wiki/University_of_Turin) and keeper of the Natural History Museum of Zoology. During his time at the university, he formed one of the largest ornithological collections in Europe.

In 1811, Bonelli wrote a *Catalogue of the Birds of Piedmont*, in which he described 262 species. In 1815, he discovered the bird [Bonelli's warbler](/wiki/Western_Bonelli%27s_warbler) (*Phylloscopus bonelli*), named by [Louis Vieillot](/wiki/Louis_Vieillot) in 1819. In the same year, he discovered [Bonelli's eagle](/wiki/Bonelli%27s_eagle) (*Hieraaetus fasciatus*), which was likewise named by Vieillot in 1822.

The successor of Bonelli at the Turin Museum was [Carlo Giuseppe Gené](/wiki/Carlo_Giuseppe_Gen%C3%A9). His son was [Cesare Bonelli](/wiki/Cesare_Bonelli) Italian Minister of War from 1878-1880.

## Achievements

Bonelli is most notable for his work on birds and on the beetle family [Carabidae](/wiki/Carabidae). Since he was an early worker on Coleoptera, many of his [genera](/wiki/Genus) later became [Families](/wiki/Family_(biology)), [sub families](/wiki/Subfamily_(biology)) and [tribes](/wiki/Tribe_(biology)). Also, many of his genera survive.

Instances are:

- Omophroninae; Pseudomorphinae; Brachininae; Trechinae; [Harpalinae](/wiki/Harpalinae); Pseudomorphinae; Siagoninae; Pterostichinae and Scaritinae - Subfamilies
- [Dromiidae](/wiki/Dromiidae) 1810 Family
- Pterostichini Tribe

## Works

- *Catalogue des Oiseaux du Piemont* (1811).
- Observations Entomologique. Première partie. *Mém. Acad. Sci. Turin* 18: 21–78, Tabula Synoptica (1810).
- Observations entomologiques. Deuxieme partie. *Mém. Acad. Sci. Turin* 20: 433-484 (1813)

The last two are founding works of entomology, introducing many new [taxa](/wiki/Taxa).

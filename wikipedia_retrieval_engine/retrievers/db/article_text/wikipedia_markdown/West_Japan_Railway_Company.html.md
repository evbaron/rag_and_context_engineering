The **West Japan Railway Company**, also referred to as **JR West** (JR西日本, *Jeiāru Nishi-Nihon*; lit. 'JR West Japan'), is one of the [Japan Railways Group](/wiki/Japan_Railways_Group) (JR Group) companies and operates in western [Honshu](/wiki/Honshu). It has its headquarters in [Kita-ku, Osaka](/wiki/Kita-ku,_Osaka). It is listed in the [Tokyo Stock Exchange](/wiki/Tokyo_Stock_Exchange), is a constituent of the [TOPIX](/wiki/TOPIX) Large70 index, and is also one of only three [Japan Railways Group](/wiki/Japan_Railways_Group) constituents of the [Nikkei 225](/wiki/Nikkei_225) index: the others are [JR East](/wiki/East_Japan_Railway_Company) and [JR Central](/wiki/Central_Japan_Railway_Company). It was also listed in the [Nagoya](/wiki/Nagoya_Stock_Exchange) and [Fukuoka](/wiki/Fukuoka_Stock_Exchange) stock exchanges until late 2020.

JR-West's highest-grossing line is the Sanyo Shinkansen [high-speed rail](/wiki/High-speed_rail) line between [Osaka](/wiki/Osaka) and [Fukuoka](/wiki/Fukuoka,_Fukuoka). The Sanyo Shinkansen alone accounts for about 40% of JR-West's passenger revenues. The company also operates [Hakata Minami Line](/wiki/Hakata_Minami_Line), a short commuter line with Shinkansen trains in [Fukuoka](/wiki/Fukuoka,_Fukuoka).

### Urban Network

The "Urban Network" is JR-West's name for its [commuter rail](/wiki/Commuter_rail) lines in the [Osaka–Kobe–Kyoto metropolitan area](/wiki/Keihanshin). These lines together comprise 610 km of track, have 245 stations and account for about 43% of JR-West's passenger revenues. Urban Network stations are equipped to handle [ICOCA](/wiki/ICOCA) fare cards. Train control on these lines is highly automated, and during peak hours trains run as often as every two minutes.

JR-West's Urban Network competes with a number of private commuter rail operators around Osaka, the "Big 4" being [Hankyu Railway](/wiki/Hankyu_Railway)/[Hanshin Railway](/wiki/Hanshin_Railway) (Hankyu bought Hanshin in April 2005), [Keihan Railway](/wiki/Keihan_Railway), [Kintetsu](/wiki/Kintetsu_Railway), and [Nankai Railway](/wiki/Nankai_Railway). JR-West's market share in the region is roughly equal to that of the Big 4 put together, largely due to its comprehensive network and high-speed commuter trains (Special Rapid Service trains on the Kobe and Kyoto lines operate at up to 130 km/h).

## History

JR-West was incorporated as a business corporation ([kabushiki kaisha](/wiki/Kabushiki_kaisha)) on April 1, 1987, as part of the breakup of the state-owned [Japanese National Railways](/wiki/Japanese_National_Railways) (JNR). Initially, it was a wholly owned subsidiary of the [JNR Settlement Corporation](/wiki/JNR_Settlement_Corporation) (JNRSC), a special company created to hold the assets of the former JNR while they were shuffled among the new JR companies.

For the first four years of its existence, JR-West leased its highest-revenue line, the [Sanyō Shinkansen](/wiki/Sany%C5%8D_Shinkansen), from the separate Shinkansen Holding Corporation. JR-West purchased the line in October 1991 at a cost of 974.1 billion [JPY](/wiki/Japanese_yen) (about US$7.2 billion) in long-term debt.

JNRSC sold 68.3% of JR-West in an [initial public offering](/wiki/Initial_public_offering) on the [Tokyo Stock Exchange](/wiki/Tokyo_Stock_Exchange) in October 1996. After JNRSC was dissolved in October 1998, its shares of JR-West were transferred to the government-owned Japan Railway Construction Public Corporation (JRCC), which merged into the [Japan Railway Construction, Transport and Technology Agency](/wiki/Japan_Railway_Construction,_Transport_and_Technology_Agency) (JRTT) as part of a bureaucratic reform package in October 2003. JRTT offered all of its shares in JR-West to the public in an international IPO in 2004, ending the era of government ownership of JR-West. JR-West is now listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, [Nagoya Stock Exchange](/wiki/Nagoya_Stock_Exchange), [Osaka Securities Exchange](/wiki/Osaka_Securities_Exchange) and [Fukuoka Stock Exchange](/wiki/Fukuoka_Stock_Exchange).

## Accidents and incidents

- [Shigaraki train disaster](/wiki/Shigaraki_train_disaster): A collision between a JR West and a [Shigaraki Kōgen Railway](/wiki/Shigaraki_K%C5%8Dgen_Railway) train in [Shigaraki](/wiki/Shigaraki,_Shiga) (now [Koka](/wiki/Koka,_Shiga)), [Shiga Prefecture](/wiki/Shiga_Prefecture) on 14 May 1991, killed 42 people.
- [Amagasaki derailment](/wiki/Amagasaki_derailment): A train derailment in [Amagasaki](/wiki/Amagasaki), [Hyōgo Prefecture](/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo_Prefecture) on 25 April 2005, killed 107 people.

# Jason Statham

**Jason Statham** ([/ˈsteɪθəm/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*STAY-thəm*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born 26 July 1967) is an English actor. He is known for being [typecast](/wiki/Typecasting) as tough, gritty, or violent characters in [action thriller](/wiki/Action_thriller) films, and has been credited for leading the resurgence of action films during the 2000s and 2010s. By 2017, his films had grossed over [£](/wiki/Pound_sterling)1.1 billion ([$](/wiki/United_States_dollar)1.5 billion), making him one of the industry's most [bankable stars](/wiki/Bankable_star). Films in which he has appeared have grossed over $8.5 billion worldwide.

Statham began practising [Chinese martial arts](/wiki/Chinese_martial_arts), [kickboxing](/wiki/Kickboxing), and [karate](/wiki/Karate) recreationally in his youth while working at local market stalls. An avid [footballer](/wiki/Association_football) and [diver](/wiki/Diving_(sport)), he was a member of [Great Britain's national diving team](/wiki/British_Swimming_(organisation)) and competed for [England](/wiki/England_at_the_Commonwealth_Games) in the [1990 Commonwealth Games](/wiki/Aquatics_at_the_1990_Commonwealth_Games). Shortly after, he was asked to model for [French Connection](/wiki/French_Connection_(clothing)), [Tommy Hilfiger](/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger_(company)), and [Levi's](/wiki/Levi_Strauss_%26_Co.) in various advertising campaigns.

Statham's history of working at market stalls inspired his casting in the [Guy Ritchie](/wiki/Guy_Ritchie) crime films *[Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels](/wiki/Lock,_Stock_and_Two_Smoking_Barrels)* (1998) and *[Snatch](/wiki/Snatch_(film))* (2000). Both films were commercial hits, and they helped catapult Statham to stardom. He went on to play supporting roles in the American action films *[Turn It Up](/wiki/Turn_It_Up_(film))* (2000), *[Ghosts of Mars](/wiki/Ghosts_of_Mars)* and *[The One](/wiki/The_One_(2001_film))* (both 2001). From 2002 to 2008, he played the title role in the first three films in the [*Transporter* film series](/wiki/Transporter_(franchise)) (2002–2008), which solidified his status as an action star. In 2003, he appeared in the ensemble heist action film *[The Italian Job](/wiki/The_Italian_Job_(2003_film))*. He went on to play leading roles in commercially successful films such as *[Crank](/wiki/Crank_(film))* (2006), *[The Bank Job](/wiki/The_Bank_Job)* (2008), *[The Mechanic](/wiki/The_Mechanic_(2011_film))* (2011), *[Homefront](/wiki/Homefront_(2013_film))* (2013), *[Mechanic: Resurrection](/wiki/Mechanic:_Resurrection)* (2016), *[The Meg](/wiki/The_Meg)* (2018), *[Wrath of Man](/wiki/Wrath_of_Man)* (2021), *[Meg 2: The Trench](/wiki/Meg_2:_The_Trench)* (2023), and *[The Beekeeper](/wiki/The_Beekeeper_(2024_film))* (2024), among others.

Statham has also starred as Lee Christmas in the ensemble action film series [*The Expendables*](/wiki/The_Expendables_(franchise)) (2010–2023) and as [Deckard Shaw](/wiki/List_of_Fast_%26_Furious_characters#Deckard_Shaw) in the [*Fast & Furious* franchise](/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious) (2013–2023), including the spin-off *[Hobbs & Shaw](/wiki/Hobbs_%26_Shaw)* (2019), which he co-produced. His voice acting work includes the documentaries *Thai Boxing: A Fighting Chance* (2002), *[Truth in 24](/wiki/Truth_in_24)* (2008) and its [2012 sequel](/wiki/Truth_in_24_II), and the animated film *[Gnomeo & Juliet](/wiki/Gnomeo_%26_Juliet)* (2011).

## Early life and education

Jason Statham was born in [Shirebrook](/wiki/Shirebrook), in the county of [Derbyshire](/wiki/Derbyshire), England, on 26 July 1967, the son of dancer Eileen (née Yates) and street seller Barry Statham. His father also worked odd jobs as a house painter, coal miner, and singer in the [Canary Islands](/wiki/Canary_Islands). Statham moved to [Great Yarmouth](/wiki/Great_Yarmouth), where he initially chose not to follow his father's career working the local market stalls, instead practising [martial arts](/wiki/Martial_arts). He grew up alongside football player [Vinnie Jones](/wiki/Vinnie_Jones), with whom he would later act; Jones introduced him to [football](/wiki/Association_football), and Statham went on to play for the local [grammar school](/wiki/Grammar_school), which he attended from 1978 to 1983. He was also passionate about [diving](/wiki/Diving_(sport)), practising daily to perfect his techniques. He was a member of Great Britain's [National Swimming Squad](/wiki/British_Swimming_(organisation)) for 12 years, and competed for England at the [1990 Commonwealth Games](/wiki/Aquatics_at_the_1990_Commonwealth_Games) in the 10 metre, 3 metre, and 1 metre events, coming tenth, eleventh and eighth respectively. He said in a 2003 interview that his time with the national squad was a "great experience" that taught him "discipline, focus, and certainly [kept him] out of trouble".

Statham was spotted by the sports modelling agency Sports Promotions while training at London's [Crystal Palace National Sports Centre](/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre). He was signed by [Tommy Hilfiger](/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger), Griffin, and [Levi's](/wiki/Levi_Strauss_%26_Co.) for various modelling contracts during their 1996 spring and summer collections. In 1997, he became a model for the clothing brand [French Connection](/wiki/French_Connection_(clothing)). A spokesperson for the high street clothing chain said, "We chose Jason because we wanted our model to look like a normal guy. His look is just right for now: very masculine and not too male-modelly." However, he was still forced to follow in his father's footsteps as a street seller to make ends meet, stating that he sold "fake perfume and jewellery on street corners". He made small appearances in a few music videos, including "Comin' On" by [The Shamen](/wiki/The_Shamen) in 1993, "[Run to the Sun](/wiki/Run_to_the_Sun)" by [Erasure](/wiki/Erasure_(duo)) in 1994, and "[Dream a Little Dream of Me](/wiki/Dream_a_Little_Dream_of_Me)" by [The Beautiful South](/wiki/The_Beautiful_South) in 1995.

## Career

### 2000–2010: Rise to prominence

Statham in 2007

While working as a model for French Connection, Statham was introduced to fledgling filmmaker [Guy Ritchie](/wiki/Guy_Ritchie), who was developing a film and needed to fill the role of a streetwise con artist. After learning about Statham's past as a market stall salesman, Ritchie cast him in the crime comedy thriller *[Lock, Stock and Two Smoking Barrels](/wiki/Lock,_Stock_and_Two_Smoking_Barrels)* (1998). The film was well received by both critics and audiences, and helped put Statham in the public eye; for his role, he was paid [£](/wiki/GBP)5,000. Statham's second collaboration with Ritchie came with *[Snatch](/wiki/Snatch_(film))* (2000), which earned more than $80 million at the box office. For his role in *Snatch*, he was paid £15,000. He was then able to break into Hollywood and appeared in two films in 2001: the science fiction action horror film *[Ghosts of Mars](/wiki/Ghosts_of_Mars)* and the science fiction martial arts action film *[The One](/wiki/The_One_(2001_film))*.

In 2002, he narrated the martial arts documentary *Thai Boxing: A Fighting Chance* (2002), which follows the lives of three individuals who come from very different backgrounds that are all training [Muay Thai](/wiki/Muay_Thai), each with their own unique motivations and stories, as they prepare for their next fight.

Statham was offered more film roles and was cast in the lead role of driver Frank Martin in the action film *[The Transporter](/wiki/The_Transporter)* (2002), written by [Luc Besson](/wiki/Luc_Besson). The film spawned two sequels, *[Transporter 2](/wiki/Transporter_2)* (2005) and *[Transporter 3](/wiki/Transporter_3)* (2008). He also played supporting roles in *[Mean Machine](/wiki/Mean_Machine_(film))* (2002), *[The Italian Job](/wiki/The_Italian_Job_(2003_film))* (2003), and as the lead villain in *[Cellular](/wiki/Cellular_(film))* (2004).

In 2005, Statham was once again cast by Ritchie to star in his new project, *[Revolver](/wiki/Revolver_(2005_film))*, which was a critical and box office failure. He played a dramatic role in the [independent film](/wiki/Independent_film) *[London](/wiki/London_(2005_American_film))* in 2005. In 2006, he played the lead role in the action film *[Crank](/wiki/Crank_(film))*. Statham was asked to promote *Crank* during the 2006 [San Diego Comic-Con Convention](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con). In 2008, Statham starred in the British crime thriller *[The Bank Job](/wiki/The_Bank_Job)* and *[Death Race](/wiki/Death_Race_(2008_film))*, a remake of *[Death Race 2000](/wiki/Death_Race_2000)* (1975). American film critic [Armond White](/wiki/Armond_White) hailed Statham's ascension as an action film star. On the occasion of *[Death Race](/wiki/Death_Race_(2008_film))*, White championed Statham's "best track record of any contemporary movie star." Later in 2008, White praised Statham's *[Transporter 3](/wiki/Transporter_3)* as a great example of kinetic [pop art](/wiki/Pop_art). Chris Hewitt of [*Empire Magazine*](/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine)), noted the film as "a dour, drab affair", but credited the film with "establishing Statham as a new action hero, as at ease with gruff one-liners as he was with Jackie Chan-esque high-kicking".

In 2009, Statham started to develop a new movie written by [David Peoples](/wiki/David_Peoples) and [Janet Peoples](/wiki/Janet_Peoples). Statham stated "We've got a movie we're trying to do, written by David Peoples and Janet Peoples, in the vein of an old film, *The Treasure of the Sierra Madre*. It's not a remake or anything, but it's a little bit like that, about relationships and how greed contaminates the relationships these three people have. The working title is *The Grabbers*." He reprised his role as Chev Chelios in the 2009 sequel *[Crank: High Voltage](/wiki/Crank:_High_Voltage).*

In 2010, Statham appeared alongside fellow action stars [Sylvester Stallone](/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone), [Jet Li](/wiki/Jet_Li), [Dolph Lundgren](/wiki/Dolph_Lundgren) and [Mickey Rourke](/wiki/Mickey_Rourke), among others, in the [ensemble](/wiki/Ensemble_cast) [action film](/wiki/Action_film) *[The Expendables](/wiki/The_Expendables_(2010_film))*. Statham plays [Lee Christmas](/wiki/List_of_characters_from_The_Expendables_film_series#Lee_Christmas), a former SAS soldier and expert at [close quarters combat](/wiki/Close_quarters_combat) using knives. The film was commercially successful, opening at number one at the box office in the United States, the United Kingdom, China and India, and grossed a total of $274 million worldwide.

### 2011–2015: Commercial expansion

Statham in 2012

In his first film of 2011, Statham starred in [the remake](/wiki/The_Mechanic_(2011_film)) of the 1972 [Charles Bronson](/wiki/Charles_Bronson) film *[The Mechanic](/wiki/The_Mechanic_(1972_film))* as Arthur Bishop. A [theatrical trailer](/wiki/Trailer_(promotion)) depicting Statham's character "shooting a man's head off" [was banned from circulation](/wiki/The_Mechanic_(2011_film)#TV_advertisement_ban) by the [Advertising Standards Authority](/wiki/Advertising_Standards_Authority_(United_Kingdom)) for showing excessive violence. His role in *The Mechanic* was positively reviewed by the critics both in the United States and the United Kingdom. *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* praised his performance as possessing a "now-customary efficiency" in attaining "an entertaining hitman thriller". *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* noted Statham as "sleek as a bullet"; and the film "a more powerful recharge" of the original. UK newspaper, *[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)* hailed Statham as "England's best export to action movies in just about forever, a businesslike brute with gentlemanly soul." He returned to British film by starring in the police drama *[Blitz](/wiki/Blitz_(2011_film))* as [Detective Sergeant](/wiki/Sergeant) Tom Brant. The film received mixed reviews with Cath Clake of *The Guardian* reviewing it as "not half bad" and "oddly entertaining". He was then cast in the action film *[Killer Elite](/wiki/Killer_Elite_(film))*. The film was based on real events, which were the subject of [Sir Ranulph Fiennes](/wiki/Ranulph_Fiennes)' novel *[The Feather Men](/wiki/The_Feather_Men)*. Statham played an assassin named Danny who comes out of retirement to save an old friend, played by [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro). The film grossed returned a negative budget, and was panned by the critics.

In August 2011, he began filming *[Parker](/wiki/Parker_(2013_film))* for director [Taylor Hackford](/wiki/Taylor_Hackford); the film was released in January 2013. Statham played [Parker](/wiki/Parker_(fictional_criminal)), the criminal antihero previously played by [Mel Gibson](/wiki/Mel_Gibson) in 1999's *[Payback](/wiki/Payback_(1999_film))* and by [Lee Marvin](/wiki/Lee_Marvin) in 1967's *[Point Blank](/wiki/Point_Blank_(1967_film))* (though their characters were given different surnames). [A. O. Scott](/wiki/A._O._Scott) of *The New York Times* said of the actor in the film: "[Statham], who seems to be made entirely of muscle and scar tissue, is comfortable with his limitations as an actor. His Parker, in any case, is more of an axiom than a fully rounded human being." A 2012 [BBC News](/wiki/BBC_News) report estimated that his ten-year film career to date (2002 to 2012) yielded over one billion dollars in the box office, making him one of the industry's most [bankable](/wiki/Bankable_star) stars. He was signed on to reprise his role as Lee Christmas in *[The Expendables 2](/wiki/The_Expendables_2)* in 2012.

Statham (centre) at the [2014 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/2014_Cannes_Film_Festival)

In 2013, Statham had a cameo appearance at the end of *[Fast & Furious 6](/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious_6)* as [the brother](/wiki/Deckard_Shaw) of the film's antagonist Owen Shaw ([Luke Evans](/wiki/Luke_Evans)). He reprised the character, this time as the main antagonist, in *[Furious 7](/wiki/Furious_7)*, which was released in April 2015. He also starred opposite [James Franco](/wiki/James_Franco) in the thriller *[Homefront](/wiki/Homefront_(2013_film))*, written by [Sylvester Stallone](/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone), and headlined the British thriller *[Hummingbird](/wiki/Hummingbird_(film))*. The latter film was praised by critics for pushing Statham's acting abilities to new heights. His "attempt to develop his 'brand' by trying more adventurous parts" noted by *The Guardian*'s [Mark Kermode](/wiki/Mark_Kermode), "[broadened] his dramatic palette". Statham made a cameo in the 2014 music video *[Summer](/wiki/Summer_(Calvin_Harris_song))* of [Calvin Harris](/wiki/Calvin_Harris) as one of the car racers. In 2014, he returned as Lee Christmas in *[The Expendables 3](/wiki/The_Expendables_3).* Although critically panned, the film would go on to gross $215 million against a $90 million budget.

In 2015, he starred in the action comedy *[Spy](/wiki/Spy_(2015_film))* alongside [Melissa McCarthy](/wiki/Melissa_McCarthy), [Jude Law](/wiki/Jude_Law), and [Rose Byrne](/wiki/Rose_Byrne). The film, a commercial success, was particularly praised for showcasing Statham's comedic side in contrast to his more serious roles. According to an article by *[Empire](/wiki/Empire_(film_magazine))* magazine, a *Spy 2* was development in late 2015, with more screen time dedicated for Statham's character, Rick Ford. He was nominated for the [Critic's Choice Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Movie_Awards) for Best Actor in a Comedy for his role in *Spy*.

Statham was offered a three-film contract to [reboot](/wiki/Reboot_(fiction)) the [*Transporter* series](/wiki/Transporter_(franchise)) in late 2015, but turned it down because he was not given the script before the signing date and unhappy with its compensation package. According to an article by *The Guardian*, Statham expressed interest in playing [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond) in the upcoming *[Spectre](/wiki/Spectre_(2015_film))* film. Its author, Steve Rose noted that "there was no doubt Statham can walk the Bond walk. And talking his talk can hardly be an issue with a character whose accent has fluctuated between [Sean Connery](/wiki/Sean_Connery)'s Scottish brogue and [Timothy Dalton](/wiki/Timothy_Dalton)'s Welsh." After the interview there were multiple calls from critics and the public to instate him as James Bond in a future film.

### 2016–present: Continued success

The sequel to his 2011 film *The Mechanic* was scheduled for production in late 2016 and announced to open as *[Mechanic: Resurrection](/wiki/Mechanic:_Resurrection).* The film received mixed reviews but was highly commercially successful, grossing $109.4 million worldwide on a budget of $40 million. According to *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)*, the film was Statham's "seventh-biggest earner" and most commercially successful solo film venture of his career.

In February 2017, he starred alongside [Gal Gadot](/wiki/Gal_Gadot) in a 30-second [Super Bowl advertisement](/wiki/Super_Bowl_commercials) for [Wix.com](/wiki/Wix.com) during [Super Bowl LI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LI). [CNET](/wiki/CNET) reported that the advertisement reached 22 million user impressions. Statham was asked to re-join the *[Fast & Furious](/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious)* franchise once more in 2016. The ensuing film, *[The Fate of the Furious](/wiki/The_Fate_of_the_Furious)*, was released in April 2017 to commercial success. While the film overall received mixed reviews, Statham was praised for his comedic timing and onscreen chemistry with contemporaries. The film went on to be the [third highest-grossing film of 2017](/wiki/2017_in_film#Highest-grossing_films) and the [12th highest-grossing film of all time](/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_films).

*Spy 2* was confirmed on 15 February 2018. But later in 2018, Feig explained that although a sequel to *Spy* could still happen, "there hasn't been any interest from the studio" in the project.

Statham played the lead, former Naval captain Jonas Taylor, in the 2018 action-horror film *[The Meg](/wiki/The_Meg)*, which was released on 10 August. The film would go on to gross $527.8 million worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing U.S.-Chinese co-production of all time.

In 2019, Statham reprised his role as Deckard Shaw again in *[Fast & Furious Presents: Hobbs & Shaw](/wiki/Hobbs_%26_Shaw)*, a spin-off of the *[Fast & Furious](/wiki/Fast_%26_Furious)* franchise focusing on his and [Dwayne Johnson](/wiki/Dwayne_Johnson)'s characters. The film grossed $758 million worldwide, becoming the [tenth highest-grossing film of 2019](/wiki/2019_in_film#Highest-grossing_films), and received generally positive reviews from critics, with praise for Statham's performance.

In 2022, Statham established his own production company, Punch Palace Productions.

## Public image

An article by Adam Gabbatt in *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* noted that Statham's character contributions to his industry and film niche are "tough [and] uncompromising". Some critics note his presence as a "defining feature" that signals to movie-goers the content of a film. The same exposé commented, "You know what you're getting with a Jason Statham film. He will beat people up. He will crash cars. He will do an unconvincing American accent."
Statham's impact on the action-thriller genre has been seen by Gabbatt as a replacement of the same undertaken by [Arnold Schwarzenegger](/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger), [Sylvester Stallone](/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone), and [Jean-Claude Van Damme](/wiki/Jean-Claude_Van_Damme) during their runs as headliners throughout the 1980s and 1990s. Statham himself cites Stallone, [Bruce Lee](/wiki/Bruce_Lee), [Paul Newman](/wiki/Paul_Newman), [Steve McQueen](/wiki/Steve_McQueen), and [Clint Eastwood](/wiki/Clint_Eastwood) as his inspirations.

An article by *[Times Higher Education](/wiki/Times_Higher_Education)* reported that [Manchester University Press](/wiki/Manchester_University_Press) commissioned an academic study analysing the impact Statham has had on the British and American film industries from his debut in 1998 to 2018. According to the article, professors Steven Gerrard and Robert Shail are looking to show "the changing face of British cinematic masculinity" into "one that embraces cinema across a wide range of projects, but one that also uses cross-textual media in his output".

## In popular media

In 2003, Statham appeared in three British television commercials for the [Kit Kat](/wiki/Kit_Kat) chocolate bar. Described as a "break philosopher", he philosophised about salmon swimming upstream, a Mexican fisherman, and the relative speeds and ages of animals, before ending with a reminder that "taking a break" (alluding to the Kit Kat slogan) is an important part of life.

In the comic book series *[Ultimate Spider-Man](/wiki/Ultimate_Spider-Man)*, that series' version of the villain [Vulture](/wiki/Vulture_(Marvel_Comics)) was rendered by artist [Mark Bagley](/wiki/Mark_Bagley) to resemble actor Statham, per writer [Brian Michael Bendis](/wiki/Brian_Michael_Bendis)' instructions.

In the [Twenty One Pilots](/wiki/Twenty_One_Pilots) song "[Pet Cheetah](/wiki/Trench_(album))" released in 2018, Statham is mentioned in the lyrics.

## Personal life

Statham has been in a relationship with model [Rosie Huntington-Whiteley](/wiki/Rosie_Huntington-Whiteley) since 2010. The couple announced their engagement in January 2016. Their son was born in June 2017. Their daughter was born on 2 February 2022. They lived in [Beverly Hills](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California) for a number of years before moving back to [London](/wiki/London) in 2020.

Statham holds a [black belt](/wiki/Black_belt_(martial_arts)) in [karate](/wiki/Karate).

Statham enjoys [wakeboarding](/wiki/Wakeboarding), [jet skiing](/wiki/Jet_skiing), [windsurfing](/wiki/Windsurfing), and [rock climbing](/wiki/Rock_climbing). He is known for performing many of his own [stunts](/wiki/Stunt). While filming on location in Bulgaria for *[The Expendables 3](/wiki/The_Expendables_3)*, he drove a truck off the road in [Varna](/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria) and crashed into the [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea) due to malfunctioning brakes.

In a 2013 interview with *[Vanity Fair](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine))*, Statham advocated for [stunt performers](/wiki/Stunt_performer) to be given their own category at the [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards): "All of the stunt men, these are the unsung heroes. They really are. Nobody is giving them any credibility. They're risking their necks. And then you've got poncy actors pretending like they're doing [the stunts]."

In 2014, Statham was inducted into the [International Sports Hall of Fame](/wiki/International_Sports_Hall_of_Fame).

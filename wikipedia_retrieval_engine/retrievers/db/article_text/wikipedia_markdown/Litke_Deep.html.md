# Litke Deep

Litke Deep

Location of deepest point in Litke Deep in the Arctic Ocean

Arctic Ocean bathymetry

**Litke Deep** ([Russian](/wiki/Russian_language): Жёлоб Ли́тке) is a [bathymetric](/wiki/Bathymetry) feature in the [Arctic Ocean](/wiki/Arctic_Ocean). The deepest point is 5,449 m (17,877 ft) below [sea level](/wiki/Sea_level). It is the closest point of the upper surface of Earth's [lithosphere](/wiki/Lithosphere) to [Earth's center](/wiki/Earth%27s_center), with [Challenger Deep](/wiki/Challenger_Deep) being 14.7268 km (9.2 mi) further from Earth's centre at a [bathymetric](/wiki/Bathymetry) depth of 6,366.4311 km (3,955.9 mi).

The deepest point in Litke Deep is the closest point on the Earth's surface to the [Earth's center](/wiki/Earth%27s_inner_core) given that it is located along the planet's [polar flattening](/wiki/Flattening). Based on average global sea level (mean sea level), the deepest point in Litke Deep is shallower than [Challenger Deep](/wiki/Challenger_Deep). Litke Deep is the second deepest point in the Arctic Ocean after [Molloy Deep](/wiki/Molloy_Deep).

## Topography

### Location

The Litke Deep is located in the southwestern part of the [Eurasian Basin](/wiki/Eurasian_Basin), which stretches from northeastern part of [Greenland](/wiki/Greenland) past the [Svalbard](/wiki/Svalbard) archipelago, [Franz Josef Land](/wiki/Franz_Josef_Land) and [Severnaya Zemlya](/wiki/Severnaya_Zemlya) to the [Taymyr Peninsula](/wiki/Taymyr_Peninsula). It is situated south of the [underwater ridge](/wiki/Mid-ocean_ridge) [Gakkel Ridge](/wiki/Gakkel_Ridge) roughly 350 kilometers northeast of Svalbard and roughly 220 km north of the island of [Nordaustlandet](/wiki/Nordaustlandet). The deepest part is at 5,449 [metres](/wiki/Metres) (17,881 [feet](/wiki/Foot_(unit_of_measure))) under [sea level](/wiki/Sea_level).

### Closest point to Earth's center

The Challenger Deep is lower below sea level, but the Litke Deep is reported to be the closest point on the surface to Earth's center, with [Molloy Deep](/wiki/Molloy_Deep) a very close second.

The seabed at Litke Deep is the fixed point on Earth that has the least distance from the center – because of the oblate spheroid shape of the planet Earth, which is flatter at [poles](/wiki/Geographical_pole) and thicker at the [equator](/wiki/Equator). Application of the formula at [Earth radius](/wiki/Earth_radius#Radius_at_a_given_geodetic_latitude) shows that the Earth's radius is 14.7268 km (9 mi) lesser at Litke Deep than at Challenger Deep.

Litke Deep is closer to [North Pole](/wiki/North_Pole) at 82°24’ N and the difference between [Earth](/wiki/Earth)'s [diameter at poles and equator](/wiki/Equatorial_bulge) is greater than the depth at Challenger Deep (10,925 m (35,843 ft) below sea level), around 11°22' north, nearer to equator with sea level also having the difference. Despite being 5,475 m (17,963 ft) shallower in depth below sea level, it is 6,351.7043 km (3,946.8 mi) from the Earth's center, 14.7268 km (9 mi) nearer than the Challenger Deep (6,366.4311 km (3,955.9 mi) to the Earth's center). In this ranking, several other Arctic as well as Antarctic depths such as [Molloy Deep](/wiki/Molloy_Deep), seabed at North Pole, Factorian Deep and [Meteor Deep](/wiki/Meteor_Deep) in [Southern Ocean](/wiki/Southern_Ocean) exceed Challenger Deep. However, by depth below sea level, Litke Deep is not the deepest point in the Arctic Ocean.

## Surveys

The Litke Deep was located in 1955 by the Russian icebreaker [*Fyodor Litke*](/wiki/Fyodor_Litke_(1909_icebreaker)) expedition. It is named after Russian explorer [Fyodor Petrovich Litke](/wiki/Fyodor_Petrovich_Litke).

## Descents

No crewed or unmanned descents have been undertaken to Litke Deep as of 2024.

# Niland brothers

The Niland brothers; from left to right: Edward, Preston, Robert, and Frederick

The **Niland brothers** were four American brothers from [Tonawanda, New York](/wiki/Tonawanda,_New_York), who served in the military during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). They were sons of Mr. and Mrs. Michael C. Niland. Two survived the war but, for a time, only one, Frederick "Fritz" Niland, was believed to have survived. After the reported deaths of his three brothers, Fritz was sent back to the United States to complete his service, and only later learned that his brother Edward, missing and presumed dead, was captive in a [Japanese POW camp](/wiki/Allied_prisoners_of_war_of_Japan) in [Burma](/wiki/State_of_Burma).

## Brothers

- Technical Sergeant **Edward Francis Niland** (December 22, 1912 – February 28, 1984), [U.S. Army Air Forces](/wiki/U.S._Army_Air_Forces): Imprisoned in a Japanese POW camp in Burma, he was captured on May 16, 1944, and liberated on May 4, 1945. After Edward's [B-25 Mitchell](/wiki/B-25_Mitchell) was hit, he parachuted and wandered through the Burmese jungle before being taken prisoner. Edward lived in [Tonawanda](/wiki/Tonawanda_(city),_New_York) until his death in 1984 at the age of 71.
- Second Lieutenant **Preston Thomas Niland** (March 6, 1915 – June 7, 1944), 29, [22nd Infantry Regiment](/wiki/U.S._22d_Infantry_Regiment), [4th Infantry Division](/wiki/U.S._4th_Infantry_Division), was killed in action on June 7, 1944, in Normandy, at the [Crisbecq Battery](/wiki/Crisbecq_Battery).
- Technical Sergeant **Robert Joseph "Bob" Niland** (February 2, 1919 – June 6, 1944), 25, D Company, [505th Parachute Infantry Regiment](/wiki/505th_Parachute_Infantry_Regiment), [82nd Airborne Division](/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) was [killed in action](/wiki/Killed_in_action) on June 6, 1944, in Normandy. He volunteered to stay behind with Corporal James Kelly and hold off a German advance while his company retreated from [Neuville-au-Plain](/wiki/Neuville-au-Plain). He was killed while manning his machine gun; Corporal James Kelly survived.
- Sergeant **Frederick William "Fritz" Niland** (April 23, 1920 – December 1, 1983), H Company, [501st Parachute Infantry Regiment](/wiki/501st_Parachute_Infantry_Regiment), [101st Airborne Division](/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division): Fritz was close friends with [Warren Muck](/wiki/Warren_Muck) and [Donald Malarkey](/wiki/Donald_Malarkey), from [E Company, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment](/wiki/E_Company,_506th_Infantry_Regiment_(United_States)), 101st Airborne Division. Fritz fought through the first few days of the Normandy campaign. Nine days following D-Day, Fritz had gone to the [82nd Airborne Division](/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division) to see his brother, Bob. Once he arrived at division, he was informed that Bob had been killed on D-Day. Fritz was shipped back to England, and finally, to the U.S., where he served as an [MP](/wiki/Military_police) in New York until the completion of the war. Fritz was awarded a [Bronze Star](/wiki/Bronze_Star) for his service. This story is evidenced in [Stephen Ambrose](/wiki/Stephen_E._Ambrose)'s book *[Band of Brothers](/wiki/Band_of_Brothers_(book))*, as well as from biographical data on [Francis L. Sampson](/wiki/Francis_L._Sampson). Private James Ryan in the film *[Saving Private Ryan](/wiki/Saving_Private_Ryan)* is loosely based on him. Fritz married Marilyn Hartnett Batt and they had two daughters, Catherine (Cate) and Mary. Fritz died in 1983 in San Francisco at the age of 63.

## Memorials

- Preston T. Niland's grave marker at the [American Cemetery](/wiki/Normandy_American_Cemetery_and_Memorial) near [Colleville-sur-Mer](/wiki/Colleville-sur-Mer), [Normandy](/wiki/Normandy), [France](/wiki/France)
- Robert J. Niland lies next to his brother Preston in the American Cemetery
- Memorial about Robert J. Niland in [Neuville-au-Plain, France](/wiki/Neuville-au-Plain,_France), where he was killed
- SGT Frederick William "Fritz" Niland's grave marker at [Fort Richardson National Cemetery](/wiki/Fort_Richardson_National_Cemetery) in [Anchorage, Alaska](/wiki/Anchorage,_Alaska)

## In popular culture

[Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s 1998 film *[Saving Private Ryan](/wiki/Saving_Private_Ryan)* is loosely based on the brothers' story.

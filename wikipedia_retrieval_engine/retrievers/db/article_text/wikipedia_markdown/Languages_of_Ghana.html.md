[Ghana](/wiki/Ghana) is a [multilingual](/wiki/Multilingual) country in which about 80 languages are spoken. Of these, English, which was inherited from the colonial era, is the [official language](/wiki/Official_language) and [lingua franca](/wiki/Lingua_franca). Of the languages indigenous to Ghana, [Akan](/wiki/Akan_languages) is the most widely spoken in the south. [Dagbani](/wiki/Dagbani_language), Dagare, Sisaala, Waale, and Gonja are among the most widely spoken in the northern part of the country.

[Ghana](/wiki/Ghana) has more than seventy ethnic groups, each with its own distinct language. Languages that belong to the same ethnic group are usually mutually intelligible. The [Dagbanli](/wiki/Dagbani_language), Nanumba and [Mamprusi](/wiki/Mampruli_language) languages of [Northern Region](/wiki/Northern_Region,_Ghana), are almost the same and, are mutually intelligible with the [Frafra](/wiki/Frafra_language) and [Waali](/wiki/Wali_language_(Gur)) languages of the Upper East and [Upper West Regions](/wiki/Upper_West_Region) of [Ghana](/wiki/Ghana). The [Mole–Dagbani](/wiki/Oti%E2%80%93Volta_languages) languages are spoken by more than 20% of the population.

Eleven languages have the status of government-sponsored languages: three [Akan dialects](/wiki/Akan_language) ([Akuapem Twi](/wiki/Akuapem_dialect), [Asante Twi](/wiki/Asante_dialect) and [Fante](/wiki/Fante_dialect)) and two [Mole–Dagbani languages](/wiki/Oti%E2%80%93Volta_languages) ([Dagaare](/wiki/Dagaare) and [Dagbanli](/wiki/Dagomba_language)). The others are [Ewe](/wiki/Ewe_language), [Dangme](/wiki/Adangme_language), [Ga](/wiki/Ga_language), [Nzema](/wiki/Nzema_language), [Gonja](/wiki/Gonja_language), and [Kasem](/wiki/Kasem_language).

In April 2019, the Ghanaian government declared its intention to make French one of Ghana's official languages due to the country being surrounded by Francophone countries ([Burkina Faso](/wiki/Burkina_Faso) to a lesser extent, the [Ivory Coast](/wiki/Ivory_Coast) and [Togo](/wiki/Togo)) and the presence of a French speaking minority in the country.

## Government-sponsored languages

The number of government-sponsored languages is either eleven or nine, depending on whether or not [Akuapem Twi](/wiki/Akuapem_Twi), [Asante Twi](/wiki/Asante_dialect), and [Fante](/wiki/Fante_dialect) are considered a single language. They are supported by the [Bureau of Ghana Languages](/wiki/Bureau_of_Ghana_Languages), which was established in 1951 and publishes materials in the languages; during the periods when Ghanaian languages were used in primary education, these were the languages which were used. All these languages belong to the [Niger–Congo language family](/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Congo_language_family), though to several different branches.

### Akan (Fante, Asante Twi and Akuapem Twi)

A map of Ghana's ethno-linguistic areas

[Akan](/wiki/Akan_languages), part of the [Kwa branch](/wiki/Kwa_languages) of the Niger–Congo family, is a [dialect continuum](/wiki/Dialect_continuum), but with regard to official status, only a few out of the many varieties of Akan are recognised: [Fante](/wiki/Fante_dialect), [Asante Twi](/wiki/Asante_dialect), [Akuapem Twi](/wiki/Akuapem_dialect). Taken as a whole, Akan is the most-widely spoken language in Ghana.

### Ewe

[Ewe](/wiki/Ewe_language) is a [Gbe language](/wiki/Gbe_languages), part of the [Volta–Niger branch](/wiki/Volta%E2%80%93Niger_languages) of the Niger–Congo family. The Ewe Language is spoken in Ghana, Togo and Benin with a trace of the language in West Nigeria. Out of the many dialects of Ewe spoken in Ghana, the major ones are Anlo, Tongu, Vedome, Gbi, and Krepi.

### Dagbani

[Dagbani](/wiki/Dagbani_language) is one of the [Gur languages](/wiki/Gur_languages). It is the most spoken language in Northern Ghana. The number of native speakers numbers more than three million, this number will reach six million if dialects such as Nanumba, Mamprusi and Kamara are added. It belongs to the larger [Mole–Dagbani](/wiki/Oti%E2%80%93Volta_languages) [ethnic group](/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Africa#Overview#Western_Africa) found in [Ghana](/wiki/Ghana) and [makes up about 18.5% of the population](/wiki/Demographics_of_Ghana#Ethnic_groups). It is spoken by [Dagombas](/wiki/Dagomba_people) in the [Northern Region](/wiki/Northern_Region,_Ghana) of [Ghana](/wiki/Ghana).

### Dangme

[Dangme](/wiki/Adangme_language) is one of the [Ga–Dangme languages](/wiki/Ga%E2%80%93Dangme_languages) within the Kwa branch. It is spoken in Greater Accra, in south-east Ghana and Togo. Dangme is a West African Kwa language spoken in Ghana, and it has been gaining popularity among Ghana residents.

### Dagaare

[Dagaare](/wiki/Dagaare_language) is another of the Gur languages. It is spoken in the Upper West Region of Ghana. It is also spoken in [Burkina Faso](/wiki/Burkina_Faso). Waali, spoken by the Wala people, and the Dagaare language are languages that can be understood by each other's speakers.

### Ga

[Ga](/wiki/Ga_language) is the other Ga–Dangme language within the Kwa branch. Ga is spoken in south-eastern Ghana, in and around the capital Accra. It is a Niger-Congo language in the Kwa branch, spoken by around 600,000 people in Ghana. Six separate towns comprised the Ga-speaking peoples: Accra, Osu, Labadi, Teshi, Nungua, and Tema. Each town had a central stool of importance in Ga traditions. Accra, among these towns, rose to prominence and now serves as Ghana's capital.

### Nzema

[Nzema](/wiki/Nzema_language) is one of the [Bia languages](/wiki/Bia_languages), closely related to Akan. It is spoken by the [Nzema people](/wiki/Nzema_people) in the Western Region of Ghana. It is also spoken in the [Ivory Coast](/wiki/C%C3%B4te_d%27Ivoire). Nzema, also known as Appolo, is mainly spoken in Ghana's Jomoro district and Ivory Coast's Comoé district. In 2004, it had around 330,000 speakers. The Nzema language utilizes a Latin-based script and comprises a total of twenty-four alphabetic characters.

### Kasem

[Kasem](/wiki/Kasem_language) is a [Gurunsi language](/wiki/Gurunsi_languages), in the Gur branch. It is spoken in the Upper Eastern Region of Ghana. It is also spoken in [Burkina Faso](/wiki/Burkina_Faso). By 1998, Kasem had around 250,000 speakers, divided between Ghana (130,000) and Burkina Faso (120,000). It's alternatively known as Kasena, Kasim, Kassem, Kasɩm, or Kassena.

### Gonja

[Gonja](/wiki/Gonja_language) is one of the [Guang languages](/wiki/Guang_languages), part of the [Tano languages](/wiki/Tano_languages) within the Kwa branch along with Akan and Bia. It is spoken in the Northern Region of Ghana and Wa. "Gonja" comes from "Kada Goro-Jaa" in Hausa, signifying "land of Red Cola." Ghana has over 285,000 Gonja people.

## Language classification

The language of Ghana belong to the following branches within the [Niger–Congo language family](/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Congo_languages):

- [Kwa languages](/wiki/Kwa_languages) (Akan, Bia, Guang in Tano; Ga and Adangme)
- [Gbe languages](/wiki/Gbe_languages) (Ewe)
- [Gur languages](/wiki/Gur_languages) (Gurunsi, [Dagbani](/wiki/Dagbani_language), Mossi, Dagaare, and Frafra in Oti–Volta)
- [Senufo languages](/wiki/Senufo_languages) ([Nafaanra](/wiki/Nafaanra_language))
- [Kulango languages](/wiki/Kulango_languages)
- [Mande languages](/wiki/Mande_languages) (Wangara, Ligbi)

Older classifications may instead group them as Kwa, Gur, and Mande.

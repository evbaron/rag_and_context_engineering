**Daniel Michael DeVito Jr.** (born November 17, 1944) is an American actor and filmmaker. Known for his short stature, raspy voice, distinct accent, and energetic comedy roles, he gained prominence for his portrayal of the taxi dispatcher [Louie De Palma](/wiki/Louie_De_Palma) in the television series *[Taxi](/wiki/Taxi_(TV_series))* (1978–1983), which won him a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards) and an [Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award). Since 2006, he has played [Frank Reynolds](/wiki/List_of_It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia_characters#Frank_Reynolds) on the [FXX](/wiki/FXX) sitcom *[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia](/wiki/It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia)*.

DeVito is known for his film roles in *[One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest](/wiki/One_Flew_Over_the_Cuckoo%27s_Nest_(film))* (1975), *[Terms of Endearment](/wiki/Terms_of_Endearment)* (1983), *[Romancing the Stone](/wiki/Romancing_the_Stone)* (1984), *[The Jewel of the Nile](/wiki/The_Jewel_of_the_Nile)* (1985), *[Ruthless People](/wiki/Ruthless_People)* (1986), *[Throw Momma from the Train](/wiki/Throw_Momma_from_the_Train)* (1987), *[Twins](/wiki/Twins_(1988_film))* (1988), *[The War of the Roses](/wiki/The_War_of_the_Roses_(film))* (1989), *[Batman Returns](/wiki/Batman_Returns)* (1992), *[Jack the Bear](/wiki/Jack_the_Bear)* (1993), *[Junior](/wiki/Junior_(1994_film))* (1994), *[Matilda](/wiki/Matilda_(1996_film))* (1996), *[L.A. Confidential](/wiki/L.A._Confidential_(film))* (1997), *[The Big Kahuna](/wiki/The_Big_Kahuna_(film))* (1999), *[Big Fish](/wiki/Big_Fish)* (2003), *[Deck the Halls](/wiki/Deck_the_Halls_(2006_film))* (2006), *[When in Rome](/wiki/When_in_Rome_(2010_film))* (2010), *[Wiener-Dog](/wiki/Wiener-Dog_(film))* (2016) and *[Jumanji: The Next Level](/wiki/Jumanji:_The_Next_Level)* (2019). He has voiced roles in such films as *[Hercules](/wiki/Hercules_(1997_film))* (1997), *[The Lorax](/wiki/The_Lorax_(film))* (2012), *[Smallfoot](/wiki/Smallfoot)* (2018) and *[Migration](/wiki/Migration_(2023_film))* (2023).

DeVito and [Michael Shamberg](/wiki/Michael_Shamberg) founded Jersey Films. Soon afterwards, [Stacey Sher](/wiki/Stacey_Sher) became an equal partner. The production company is known for films such as *[Pulp Fiction](/wiki/Pulp_Fiction)* (1994), *[Garden State](/wiki/Garden_State_(film))* (2004) and *[Freedom Writers](/wiki/Freedom_Writers)* (2007). DeVito also owned Jersey Television, which produced the [Comedy Central](/wiki/Comedy_Central) series *[Reno 911!](/wiki/Reno_911!)*. DeVito and wife [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman) starred together in his 1996 film *[Matilda](/wiki/Matilda_(1996_film))*, based on [Roald Dahl](/wiki/Roald_Dahl)'s children's novel. DeVito was also one of the producers nominated for an [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) for *[Erin Brockovich](/wiki/Erin_Brockovich_(film))* (2000).

From 2012 to 2013, DeVito played Willie Clark in the [West End](/wiki/West_End_(theatre)) revival of [Neil Simon](/wiki/Neil_Simon)'s *[The Sunshine Boys](/wiki/The_Sunshine_Boys)*. He made his [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) debut as Gregory Solomon in the revival of [Arthur Miller](/wiki/Arthur_Miller)'s *[The Price](/wiki/The_Price_(play))* (2017), earning a [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Featured_Actor_in_a_Play) nomination for his performance. He returned to Broadway in the [Theresa Rebeck](/wiki/Theresa_Rebeck) play *I Need That* (2023).

## Early life and education

DeVito at Oratory Preparatory School, 1962

DeVito was born at [Raleigh Fitkin-Paul Morgan Memorial Hospital](/wiki/Jersey_Shore_University_Medical_Center) in [Neptune Township, New Jersey](/wiki/Neptune_Township,_New_Jersey), the son of Daniel DeVito Sr., a small business owner and Julia DeVito ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Moccello). He grew up in a family of five, with his parents and two older sisters. He is of [Italo-Albanian](/wiki/Italo-Albanian) descent; his family is originally from [San Fele](/wiki/San_Fele), Basilicata, as well as from the [Arbëresh](/wiki/Arb%C3%ABresh%C3%AB_people) Albanian community of [Calabria](/wiki/Calabria). He was raised in [Asbury Park, New Jersey](/wiki/Asbury_Park,_New_Jersey). He lived a few miles away from the original [Jersey Mike's](/wiki/Jersey_Mike%27s) location and would eat there frequently, which would inspire him to become the sub shop's first celebrity spokesman in a line of commercials that began to air in September 2022.

DeVito was raised as a Catholic. When he was 14, he persuaded his father to send him to boarding school to "keep him out of trouble", and graduated from [Oratory Preparatory School](/wiki/Oratory_Preparatory_School) in [Summit, New Jersey](/wiki/Summit,_New_Jersey), in 1962. While working as a beautician at his sister's salon, his search for a professional makeup instructor led him to the [American Academy of Dramatic Arts](/wiki/American_Academy_of_Dramatic_Arts), where he graduated in 1966. In his early theater days, he performed with the Colonnades Theater Lab at the [Eugene O'Neill Theater Center](/wiki/Eugene_O%27Neill_Theater_Center) in [Waterford, Connecticut](/wiki/Waterford,_Connecticut). Along with his future wife [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman), he appeared in plays produced by the [Westbeth Playwrights Feminist Collective](/wiki/Westbeth_Playwrights_Feminist_Collective).

## Career

### Acting

Before he became an actor, Danny DeVito worked in a [morgue](/wiki/Morgue) as a cosmetologist, styling the hair of the deceased. He began this after a client of his died where he worked as a beautician at his sister's salon in New Jersey, and her family asked him to do her hair for her funeral. DeVito started his career acting [off-Broadway](/wiki/Off-Broadway) in the plays *Shoot Anything With Hair That Moves* and *[The Man with the Flower in His Mouth](/wiki/The_Man_with_the_Flower_in_His_Mouth)* both in 1969. DeVito played Martini in the 1975 film *[One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest](/wiki/One_Flew_Over_the_Cuckoo%27s_Nest_(film))* directed by [Milos Forman](/wiki/Milos_Forman), reprising his role from the 1971 [off-Broadway play](/wiki/One_Flew_Over_the_Cuckoo%27s_Nest_(play)) of the same title. He had his feature film debut in the drama *[Dreams of Glass](/wiki/Dreams_of_Glass)* (1970). Early film roles include *[Lady Liberty](/wiki/Lady_Liberty_(film))* (1971), *[Hurry Up, or I'll Be 30](/wiki/Hurry_Up,_or_I%27ll_Be_30)* (1973) and *[Deadly Hero](/wiki/Deadly_Hero)* (1975).

In 1977, DeVito played the role of John "John John the Apple" DeAppoliso in the *[Starsky & Hutch](/wiki/Starsky_%26_Hutch)* episode "The Collector". DeVito gained fame in 1978 playing Louie De Palma, the short but domineering dispatcher for the fictional Sunshine Cab Company, on the hit TV show *[Taxi](/wiki/Taxi_(TV_series))*. For his performance he received a [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) and [Golden Globe Award for Best Supporting Actor – Series, Miniseries or Television Film](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film). He got the role by astonishing the show's creators during the audition when asking them "Who wrote this shit?" then throwing the script on the table.

After his breakthrough on the sitcom *Taxi*, DeVito devoted more effort to a growing successful film career. He took a supporting role as Vernon Dalhart in the [James L. Brooks](/wiki/James_L._Brooks) directed comedy-drama *[Terms of Endearment](/wiki/Terms_of_Endearment)* (1983) acting alongside [Shirley MacLaine](/wiki/Shirley_MacLaine), [Debra Winger](/wiki/Debra_Winger) and [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson). The film earned critical acclaim as well as the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture).

The following year, he acted in the crime comedy *[Johnny Dangerously](/wiki/Johnny_Dangerously)* (1984) and took the role as the comic rogue Ralph in the romantic adventure *[Romancing the Stone](/wiki/Romancing_the_Stone)* (1984), starring [Michael Douglas](/wiki/Michael_Douglas) and [Kathleen Turner](/wiki/Kathleen_Turner); and its sequel, *[The Jewel of the Nile](/wiki/The_Jewel_of_the_Nile)* (1985). In 1986, DeVito starred in *[Ruthless People](/wiki/Ruthless_People)* with [Bette Midler](/wiki/Bette_Midler) and [Judge Reinhold](/wiki/Judge_Reinhold) and also voiced the character Grundle King in *[My Little Pony: The Movie](/wiki/My_Little_Pony:_The_Movie_(1986_film))*. In 1987, he acted in director [Barry Levinson](/wiki/Barry_Levinson)'s *[Tin Men](/wiki/Tin_Men)*, as a rival salesman to [Richard Dreyfuss](/wiki/Richard_Dreyfuss)' character. DeVito also acted opposite [Arnold Schwarzenegger](/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger) in the comedies *[Twins](/wiki/Twins_(1988_film))* (1988) and *[Junior](/wiki/Junior_(1994_film))* (1994).

In 1990, he and Rhea Perlman played the couple Vic & Paula, commenting on the state of the environment in *[The Earth Day Special](/wiki/The_Earth_Day_Special)*. The following year, he acted in *[Other People's Money](/wiki/Other_People%27s_Money)* (1991) with [Gregory Peck](/wiki/Gregory_Peck). In 1991 and 1992, DeVito voiced [Herb Powell](/wiki/Simpson_family#Herbert_Powell) in *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)* episodes "[Brother, Can You Spare Two Dimes?](/wiki/Brother,_Can_You_Spare_Two_Dimes%3F)" and "[Oh Brother, Where Art Thou?](/wiki/Oh_Brother,_Where_Art_Thou%3F)". In 1992, he portrayed the villain [Oswald Cobblepot / The Penguin](/wiki/Oswald_Cobblepot_(Batman_Returns)) in director [Tim Burton](/wiki/Tim_Burton)'s *[Batman Returns](/wiki/Batman_Returns)* acting opposite [Michael Keaton](/wiki/Michael_Keaton), [Michelle Pfeiffer](/wiki/Michelle_Pfeiffer) and [Christopher Walken](/wiki/Christopher_Walken). That same year he directed and produced the biographical drama film *[Hoffa](/wiki/Hoffa_(film))* starring [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson). He also acted in the film portraying Bobby Ciaro. In 1993, he played a single father in the comedy-drama film *[Jack the Bear](/wiki/Jack_the_Bear)*.

DeVito at [San Diego Comic-Con](/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con) in July 2010

In 1995, DeVito appeared in the gangster comedy *[Get Shorty](/wiki/Get_Shorty_(film))*. In 1996, he took supporting roles as Swackhammer in the [Looney Tunes](/wiki/Looney_Tunes) live-action / animated sports comedy *[Space Jam](/wiki/Space_Jam)* and reunited with [Tim Burton](/wiki/Tim_Burton)'s science fiction comedy *[Mars Attacks!](/wiki/Mars_Attacks!)* starring [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson), [Glenn Close](/wiki/Glenn_Close), [Annette Bening](/wiki/Annette_Bening), [Pierce Brosnan](/wiki/Pierce_Brosnan), [Martin Short](/wiki/Martin_Short) and [Natalie Portman](/wiki/Natalie_Portman).

In 1997, he played Deck Shifflet in the legal thriller *[The Rainmaker](/wiki/The_Rainmaker_(1997_film))* starring [Matt Damon](/wiki/Matt_Damon) and [Claire Danes](/wiki/Claire_Danes) as well as Sid Hudgens, editor of a sleazy [tabloid](/wiki/Tabloid_(newspaper_format)) called *Hush-Hush*, who gets tips ahead of time of celebrity arrests in the [neo-noir](/wiki/Neo-noir) thriller *[L.A. Confidential](/wiki/L.A._Confidential_(film))* with [Russell Crowe](/wiki/Russell_Crowe), [Guy Pearce](/wiki/Guy_Pearce) and [Kevin Spacey](/wiki/Kevin_Spacey), the latter of which was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture). That same year he also voiced [Phil](/wiki/Philoctetes) in the [Walt Disney Animated film](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios) *[Hercules](/wiki/Hercules_(1997_film))* (1997). Leonard Klady of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* praised the voice performances writing, "As in *[Aladdin](/wiki/Aladdin_(1992_Disney_film))*, the melding of character animation with the screen personae of the actors voicing the roles provides forceful and amusing entertainment, particularly in DeVito's turn as a physical trainer and the acid wit [James Woods](/wiki/James_Woods) brings to his villainous role."

He starred in *[Living Out Loud](/wiki/Living_Out_Loud)* (1998) alongside [Helen Hunt](/wiki/Helen_Hunt) and [Queen Latifah](/wiki/Queen_Latifah), reunited with Kevin Spacey in *[The Big Kahuna](/wiki/The_Big_Kahuna_(film))* (1999) and hosted the last *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* episode before the year 2000. In 1999, he produced and co-starred in the biographical drama film *[Man on the Moon](/wiki/Man_on_the_Moon_(film))*, a film about the unusual life of his former *Taxi* co-star [Andy Kaufman](/wiki/Andy_Kaufman), played in the film by [Jim Carrey](/wiki/Jim_Carrey). He also played Dr. Hornicker in the [Sofia Coppola](/wiki/Sofia_Coppola) directed *[The Virgin Suicides](/wiki/The_Virgin_Suicides)* (1999) starring [Kirsten Dunst](/wiki/Kirsten_Dunst). He continued to take roles in comedy films such as *[Drowning Mona](/wiki/Drowning_Mona)* (2000), *[Screwed](/wiki/Screwed_(2000_film))* (2000), *[What's the Worst That Could Happen?](/wiki/What%27s_the_Worst_That_Could_Happen%3F)* (2001), *[Austin Powers in Goldmember](/wiki/Austin_Powers_in_Goldmember)* (2002), *[Anything Else](/wiki/Anything_Else)* (2003) and *[Be Cool](/wiki/Be_Cool)* (2005). He also acted in the drama films *[Heist](/wiki/Heist_(2001_film))* (2001) and *[Big Fish](/wiki/Big_Fish)* (2003). He earned a 2004 [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series) nomination for his role of a stripper in the [NBC](/wiki/NBC) sitcom *[Friends](/wiki/Friends)*,

DeVito with *[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia](/wiki/It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia)* castmates [Kaitlin Olson](/wiki/Kaitlin_Olson) and [Rob McElhenney](/wiki/Rob_McElhenney) at the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) ceremony for DeVito on August 18, 2011

In 2006, he joined the cast of the [FX](/wiki/FX_(TV_channel)) / [FXX](/wiki/FXX) [sitcom](/wiki/Sitcom) *[It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia](/wiki/It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia)* as [Frank Reynolds](/wiki/List_of_It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia_characters#Frank_Reynolds_(It's_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia)). DeVito stars opposite [Glenn Howerton](/wiki/Glenn_Howerton), [Rob McElhenney](/wiki/Rob_McElhenney), [Kaitlin Olson](/wiki/Kaitlin_Olson) and [Charlie Day](/wiki/Charlie_Day). The character of Frank Reynolds is introduced at the beginning of [Season 2](/wiki/It%27s_Always_Sunny_in_Philadelphia_season_2). He received a nomination for the [Satellite Award for Best Actor – Musical or Comedy Series](/wiki/13th_Satellite_Awards). DeVito said of the show "I loved it. It was fucking outrageous just the way they are. I immediately said, 'Yeah, this is an amazing show.'" Also in 2006 he starred opposite [Matthew Broderick](/wiki/Matthew_Broderick) in the Christmas comedy film *[Deck the Halls](/wiki/Deck_the_Halls_(2006_film))*.

He was also interviewed in the documentary *[Revenge of the Electric Car](/wiki/Revenge_of_the_Electric_Car),* discussing his interest in and ownership of [electric vehicles](/wiki/Electric_vehicle). DeVito played a fictional version of himself in the music video of [One Direction](/wiki/One_Direction)'s song "[Steal My Girl](/wiki/Steal_My_Girl)" (2014). He also appeared in the short film *[Curmudgeons](/wiki/Curmudgeons_(film))*, which he also produced and directed. In 2018, he had a guest starring role in the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) comedy series *[The Kominsky Method](/wiki/The_Kominsky_Method)* acting opposite [Michael Douglas](/wiki/Michael_Douglas) and [Alan Arkin](/wiki/Alan_Arkin). In 2021 he played Charlie Goldman the biographical [HBO](/wiki/HBO) drama film *[The Survivor](/wiki/The_Survivor_(2021_film))*. Also in 2023 he returned to Broadway in the [Theresa Rebeck](/wiki/Theresa_Rebeck) play *[I Need That](/wiki/I_Need_That)*.

### Directing

DeVito made his directorial debut in 1984 with *[The Ratings Game](/wiki/The_Ratings_Game)*. In 1986, he directed and starred in the [black comedy](/wiki/Black_comedy) "The Wedding Ring", a season 2 episode of [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s anthology series *[Amazing Stories](/wiki/Amazing_Stories_(1985_TV_series))*, where his character acquires an engagement ring for his wife (played by DeVito's real-life wife, actress [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman)). When the ring is slipped on his wife's finger, she is possessed by the ring's former owner, a murderous black widow. In 1987 he made his feature-directing debut with the [dark comedy](/wiki/Black_comedy) *[Throw Momma from the Train](/wiki/Throw_Momma_from_the_Train)*, in which he starred with [Billy Crystal](/wiki/Billy_Crystal) and [Anne Ramsey](/wiki/Anne_Ramsey). For his performance he earned a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Musical or Comedy](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Musical_or_Comedy) nomination.

DeVito reunited with *[Jewel of the Nile](/wiki/The_Jewel_of_the_Nile)* co-stars [Michael Douglas](/wiki/Michael_Douglas) and [Kathleen Turner](/wiki/Kathleen_Turner) in 1989 when he directed and starred alongside them in *[The War of the Roses](/wiki/The_War_of_the_Roses_(film))*. In 1996 he directed *[Matilda](/wiki/Matilda_(1996_film))* (1996), a film adaptation of [Roald Dahl](/wiki/Roald_Dahl)'s [1988 classic children's novel of the same name](/wiki/Matilda_(novel)). Critic [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) of *[The Chicago Sun-Times](/wiki/The_Chicago_Sun-Times)* wrote, "There is never a moment (except toward the happy ending) that we sense DeVito is anything other than quite serious about this material. He goes with Dahl's macabre vision." He also served as the Narrator and played the villainous used-car dealer father Harry Wormwood opposite his wife [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman). The following year, he was in talks to direct the crime drama *[The Little Things](/wiki/The_Little_Things_(2021_film))* with [Robert De Niro](/wiki/Robert_De_Niro) at that time set to star, and *[The Crowded Room](/wiki/The_Crowded_Room)* with [Leonardo DiCaprio](/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio). Both projects were eventually realized in the 2020s, but without DeVito's involvement.

In 1997, DeVito was in talks for a $13-million deal to both star in and direct *[Mystery Men](/wiki/Mystery_Men)*, but negotiations broke down over who would produce the soundtrack. "It was a big deal for me," DeVito said. "I really wanted it, so I walked away from the project."

In 1998, he committed to star in and direct both the [sci-fi](/wiki/Science_fiction) film *Barthe* for [TriStar](/wiki/TriStar_Pictures), about an alien who falls in love, and a remake of *[The Man Who Came to Dinner](/wiki/The_Man_Who_Came_to_Dinner_(1942_film))* for [DreamWorks](/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures). In 1999, DeVito was attached to star, direct and produce [Jason Keller](/wiki/Jason_Keller_(playwright))'s [spec script](/wiki/Spec_script) *Sugar's Sweet Science of Bruising* for [New Line Cinema](/wiki/New_Line_Cinema).

For the next two years, DeVito was attached to film [Michael Petroni](/wiki/Michael_Petroni)'s *Revelations*, a "supernatural religious thriller" that was to have starred [George Clooney](/wiki/George_Clooney). The project was delayed due to rewrites however. DeVito was also at one time attached to direct *[How to Lose a Guy in 10 Days](/wiki/How_to_Lose_a_Guy_in_10_Days)*. He instead resurfaced with *[Death to Smoochy](/wiki/Death_to_Smoochy)* (2002) and *[Duplex](/wiki/Duplex_(film))* (2003), which were made back-to-back. Before the release of the latter, DeVito agreed to direct a remake of 1942's *[I Married a Witch](/wiki/I_Married_a_Witch)* for [Tom Cruise](/wiki/Tom_Cruise) and [Columbia Pictures](/wiki/Columbia_Pictures), announcing to *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* that he hoped to begin production by the end of the year.

Instead of *I Married a Witch*, he sought to make *Trump vs. Wynn* as his next directorial project after receiving an offer from [HBO](/wiki/HBO). Written by [Rick Cleveland](/wiki/Rick_Cleveland), the script chronicles business tycoons [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump) and [Steve Wynn](/wiki/Steve_Wynn)'s competition to build a casino in [Atlantic City](/wiki/Atlantic_City). DeVito was expected to direct, produce and star in an unspecified role, but he dropped out after meeting with both figures, who protested the film's production.

He directed the [pilot episode](/wiki/Television_pilot) for a proposed 2005 [Imagine TV](/wiki/Imagine_TV) sitcom *Queen B*, which was not picked up by the network for series.

In 2009, it was reported that DeVito would direct and produce the [biopic](/wiki/Biographical_film) *Crazy Eddie*, based on the life of consumer electronics king [Eddie Antar](/wiki/Eddie_Antar), from a script by [Peter Steinfeld](/wiki/Peter_Steinfeld). However the film could not be made due to a [life rights](/wiki/Life_rights) deal that was made with Antar. Instead, he pivoted to making the apocalyptic horror thriller *St. Sebastian*, which completed post-production in 2012 but to this day has not been released. DeVito also worked on the independently financed *Honeymoon with Dad*, a comedy which never went into production.

DeVito has written a film adaptation of and been, since 2008, attached to direct *[The True Confessions of Charlotte Doyle](/wiki/The_True_Confessions_of_Charlotte_Doyle)* with [Morgan Freeman](/wiki/Morgan_Freeman), [Pierce Brosnan](/wiki/Pierce_Brosnan) and [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan) starring. The production was halted when Freeman was seriously injured in a car accident two weeks before filming was scheduled to commence. DeVito returned to the subject in February 2013, saying he was looking for another young actress to star in the title role and scouting movie locations in Ireland. Production was expected to restart in July 2014. The release date is still classified as "TBD" without any new information as to whether it will be shot.

### Producing

DeVito founded Jersey Films in 1991, producing films like *[Pulp Fiction](/wiki/Pulp_Fiction)* (1994), *[Get Shorty](/wiki/Get_Shorty_(film))* (1995), *[Erin Brockovich](/wiki/Erin_Brockovich_(film))* (2000) (for which he received an Academy Award nomination for Best Picture), *[Gattaca](/wiki/Gattaca)* (1997) and *[Garden State](/wiki/Garden_State_(film))* (2004).

DeVito also produced the [Comedy Central](/wiki/Comedy_Central) series *[Reno 911!](/wiki/Reno_911!)*, the film spin-off *[Reno 911!: Miami](/wiki/Reno_911!:_Miami)* and the revival on [Quibi](/wiki/Quibi).

In 2019, his company Jersey Films optioned the screen rights to make a film of *Toms River: A Story of Science and Salvation*, by Dan Fagin.

## Personal life

DeVito stands 5 ft 0 in (1.52 m) tall. His short stature is the result of [multiple epiphyseal dysplasia](/wiki/Multiple_epiphyseal_dysplasia) (Fairbank's disease), a rare genetic disorder that affects bone growth.

DeVito with [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman) in 2006

On January 17, 1971, DeVito met [Rhea Perlman](/wiki/Rhea_Perlman) when she went to see a friend in the single performance of the play *The Shrinking Bride*, which featured DeVito. They moved in together two weeks later and married on January 28, 1982. They have three children: [Lucy](/wiki/Lucy_DeVito), Grace and Jacob.

Perlman and DeVito have acted alongside each other several times, including in the television show *[Taxi](/wiki/Taxi_(TV_series))* and the feature film *[Matilda](/wiki/Matilda_(1996_film))* (where they played Matilda's parents). They [separated](/wiki/Legal_separation) in October 2012, after 30 years of marriage and over 40 years together, then reconciled in March 2013. They separated for a second time in March 2017, but remained on amicable terms and Perlman stated they had no intent of filing for divorce. In 2019, Perlman told interviewer [Andy Cohen](/wiki/Andy_Cohen) that she and DeVito have become closer friends after their separation than they were in their final years as a couple.

DeVito and Perlman had resided in a 14,579-square-foot (1,354 m2) house in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California), that they purchased in 1994; they sold the property for US$24 million in April 2015. They still own a [bungalow](/wiki/Bungalow) near [Rodeo Drive](/wiki/Rodeo_Drive) in Beverly Hills and a multi-residence compound on Broad Beach in [Malibu](/wiki/Malibu,_California). They also frequented a home they owned in [Interlaken, New Jersey](/wiki/Interlaken,_New_Jersey), to get away from Los Angeles.

DeVito has mentioned being a big fan of singer [Mike Patton](/wiki/Mike_Patton)'s various musical projects, being introduced to his work through his son Jacob in 2005. DeVito collaborated with Patton on a music video and has attended several of his concerts, with Perlman contributing an introductory voiceover to an album by [Mr. Bungle](/wiki/Mr._Bungle), one of Patton's bands. In 2016, DeVito also included music from Patton's band [Faith No More](/wiki/Faith_No_More) on *It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia.*

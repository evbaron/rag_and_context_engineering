# Fritz Koenig

## Infobox

- **Born:** ( 1924-06-20 ) 20 June 1924 Würzburg, Bavaria, Germany
- **Died:** 22 February 2017 (2017-02-22) (aged 92) Landshut, Bavaria, Germany
- **Education:** Academy of Fine Arts, Munich
- **Known for:** The Sphere

**Fritz Koenig** (German pronunciation: [[ˈfʁɪts ˈkøːnɪç]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German); 20 June 1924 – 22 February 2017) was a German sculptor, considered one of the most important international German [sculptors](/wiki/Sculpture) of the 20th century.

Koenig's main work and most famous work is *[The Sphere](/wiki/The_Sphere)*. The world's largest bronze sculpture of modern times once stood on the plaza beneath the two [World Trade Center](/wiki/World_Trade_Center_(1973%E2%80%932001)) towers in [Lower Manhattan](/wiki/Lower_Manhattan) until the terrorist attacks on [11 September 2001.](/wiki/September_11_attacks) The artifact, weighing more than 20 tons, was the only remaining work of art to be recovered largely intact from the ruins of the collapsed twin towers after the attacks. With its damage deliberately left unrepaired, the sculpture now stands in Manhattan's [Liberty Park](/wiki/Liberty_Park_(Manhattan)) as a [memorial](/wiki/Memorial) to the victims of the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks).

Koenig's oeuvre includes other works, including other memorials. Numerous works by Koenig and his renowned collections with artifacts from antiquity to the 20th century are located in the Koenigmuseum in [Landshut](/wiki/Landshut), which he designed and established by the Fritz and Maria Koenig Foundation.

## Biography

Koenig was born in [Würzburg](/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg) on 20 June 1924. His family moved to [Landshut](/wiki/Landshut) in 1930, when he was six years old. He entered the *Oberrealschule* (today the *Hans-Leinberger-Gymnasium*) in 1942, and in the same year, he was drafted into the [Wehrmacht](/wiki/Wehrmacht) and sent to the [Eastern Front](/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II)), where he was taken as a [prisoner of war](/wiki/Prisoner_of_war).

In the years after [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), he studied art at the [Academy of Fine Arts](/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts,_Munich) in [Munich](/wiki/Munich) starting in 1946 and graduating in 1952. In 1951 he studied in Paris on a scholarship. In 1957, Koenig was selected to receive a scholarship from the [Villa Massimo](/wiki/Villa_Massimo) in Rome. In 1958, Koenig presented at the XXIX. Biennale in Venice and designed the West German pavilion at the world exhibition [Expo 58](/wiki/Expo_58) in Brussels with his art. In 1959, Koenig was able to exhibit at the [II. documenta](/wiki/II._documenta) in Kassel. In addition, the Günther Franke gallery in Munich presented Koenig's first solo exhibition. Also in the same year, 1959, Koenig married his wife Maria, who was born in Landshut (1921-2010). In 1960, Koenig and his wife bought an agricultural property in the Altdorf district of Ganslberg near Landshut. In 1961, a house, studio and stables were built according to his ideas. Rural life made it possible for the passionate rider and horse lover to set up his own thoroughbred Arabian breed, which achieved worldwide fame and was also of great importance for his artistic work.

Koenig achieved his final international breakthrough in 1961 with a solo exhibition at the Staempfli Gallery in New York. Exhibitions at [documenta III](/wiki/Documenta_III) and XXXII. Bienniale followed in 1964. In the same year he was appointed professor for sculptural design at the [Technical University of Munich](/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), where he participated in the training of architects until 1992. From 1967 to 1971, Koenig created his main work that led him to world fame: At the behest of the World Trade Center architect Minoru Yamasaki and on behalf of the [Port Authority of New York and New Jersey](/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey), Koenig created a fountain system with the bronze sculpture *Große Kugelkaryatide N.Y.* (later known as *[The Sphere](/wiki/The_Sphere)*) for the World Trade Center in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City), which was still under construction.

Over the decades, Fritz Koenig created a diverse work that he was able to keep in representative casts in his spacious country estate in Ganslberg. In addition, the sculptor owned many works of art from a wide variety of cultures and periods from [antiquity](/wiki/Ancient_history) to the 20th century, the quality and diversity of which testify to the lifelong passion for collecting. Koenig's collection focused on a world-renowned collection of African works of art.

Koenig was a board member of the (West) German Association of Artists from 1961 to 1972. Fritz Koenig was also the recipient of numerous awards, including the [Bavarian Maximilian Order for Science and Art](/wiki/Bavarian_Maximilian_Order_for_Science_and_Art) and the [Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany](/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany).

Koenig died on 22 February 2017, at the age of 92 on his country estate in Ganslberg.

## Work

*The Sphere* in 2018, in its current location of [Liberty Park](/wiki/Liberty_Park)

In his work, Koenig was primarily concerned with the elementary "existence" of humans and animals in the area of tension between [religiosity](/wiki/Religion) and [mythology](/wiki/Mythology). The human being in the fragility of his existence, in the field of tension between love, death and impermanence, was another major [leitmotif](/wiki/Leitmotif) of Fritz Koenig's work. The combination of geometric forms like [cuboids](/wiki/Cuboid), [spheres](/wiki/Sphere) and bodies and limbs of [cylinders](/wiki/Cylinder_(geometry)) to create new, organic-looking objects cast in metal made Koenig known in the early 1950s.

### Major works

Koenig's major works include *The Sphere*, now displayed in [Liberty Park](/wiki/Liberty_Park) in New York City, a memorial at [Mauthausen concentration camp](/wiki/Mauthausen-Gusen_concentration_camp) (1983) and a memorial to victims of the [Munich massacre](/wiki/Munich_massacre) during the [1972 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics). Many works are installed in public space, such as at the seat of the President of Germany, [Schloss Bellevue](/wiki/Schloss_Bellevue), at German embassies in Washington, D.C., London, Madrid and Dakar, and the [Würzburg Cathedral](/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg_Cathedral).

## Reception

Koenig is regarded internationally as one of the most important German sculptors of the 20th century. Ludwig Spaenle, minister of culture of Bavaria, wrote that he was a truly great artist, who will be remembered by his works in the world, be it Berlin, Madrid or New York City. The *[Corriere della Sera](/wiki/Corriere_della_Sera)* compared Koenig to [Michelangelo](/wiki/Michelangelo), and a reviewer from the [Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](/wiki/Frankfurter_Allgemeine_Zeitung) saw a connection to [Donatello](/wiki/Donatello).

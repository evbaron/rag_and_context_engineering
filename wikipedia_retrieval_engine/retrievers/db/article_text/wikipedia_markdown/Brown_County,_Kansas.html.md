**Brown County** is a [county](/wiki/County_(United_States)) located in the northeastern portion of the [U.S. state](/wiki/U.S._state) of [Kansas](/wiki/Kansas). Its [county seat](/wiki/County_seat) and most populous city is [Hiawatha](/wiki/Hiawatha,_Kansas). As of the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county population was 9,508. The county was named after [Albert G. Brown](/wiki/Albert_G._Brown), a U.S. Senator from Mississippi and Kansas statehood advocate. The [Kickapoo Indian Reservation of Kansas](/wiki/Kickapoo_Tribe_in_Kansas), the majority of the [Sac and Fox Reservation](/wiki/Sac_and_Fox_Reservation), and the majority of the [Iowa Reservation of Kansas and Nebraska](/wiki/Ioway_Reservation) are located within the county.

## History

### Early history

See also: [History of Kansas](/wiki/History_of_Kansas)

For many [millennia](/wiki/Millennia), the [Great Plains](/wiki/Great_Plains) of [North America](/wiki/North_America) was inhabited by [nomadic](/wiki/Nomadic) [Native Americans](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States). From the 16th century to 18th century, the [Kingdom of France](/wiki/Kingdom_of_France) claimed ownership of large parts of [North America](/wiki/North_America). In 1762, after the [French and Indian War](/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), France secretly ceded [New France](/wiki/New_France) to [Spain](/wiki/Spain), per the [Treaty of Fontainebleau](/wiki/Treaty_of_Fontainebleau_(1762)).

### 19th century

In 1802, Spain returned most of the land to France, but keeping title to about 7,500 square miles. In 1803, most of the land for [modern day Kansas](/wiki/History_of_Kansas) was acquired by the United States from France as part of the 828,000 square mile [Louisiana Purchase](/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) for 2.83 [cents](/wiki/Penny_(United_States_coin)) per [acre](/wiki/Acre).

In 1854, the [Kansas Territory](/wiki/Kansas_Territory) was organized, then in 1861 [Kansas](/wiki/Kansas) became the 34th [U.S. state](/wiki/U.S._state). Brown County was founded in 1855, was named for [Albert G. Brown](/wiki/Albert_G._Brown).

## Geography

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 572 square miles (1,480 km2), of which 571 square miles (1,480 km2) is land and 1.2 square miles (3.1 km2) (0.2%) is water. The [Wolf River](/wiki/Wolf_River_(Kansas)) has its source in the county. [Brown State Fishing Lake](/wiki/Brown_State_Fishing_Lake), formerly known as "Brown County State Park" is in the county, 8 miles (13 km) east of Hiawatha.

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county had a population of 9,508. The median age was 42.6 years, 24.7% of residents were under the age of 18, and 21.2% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 96.9 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 95.4 males age 18 and over. 0.0% of residents lived in urban areas, while 100.0% lived in rural areas.

The racial makeup of the county was 81.7% White, 1.1% [Black or African American](/wiki/African_Americans), 9.2% [American Indian and Alaska Native](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 0.5% [Asian](/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.1% [Native Hawaiian](/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) and [Pacific Islander](/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 1.1% from some other race, and 6.4% from [two or more races](/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). [Hispanic or Latino](/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race comprised 3.7% of the population.

There were 3,841 households in the county, of which 29.0% had children under the age of 18 living with them and 25.1% had a female householder with no spouse or partner present. About 30.3% of all households were made up of individuals and 14.4% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.

There were 4,482 housing units, of which 14.3% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 70.7% were owner-occupied and 29.3% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 2.9% and the rental vacancy rate was 10.3%.

### 2000 census

As of the [2000 census](/wiki/2000_United_States_census), there were 10,724 people, 4,318 households, and 2,949 families residing in the county. The [population density](/wiki/Population_density) was 19 people per square mile (7.3 people/km2). There were 4,815 housing units at an average density of 8 per square mile (3.1/km2). The [racial makeup](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)) of the county was 86.87% [White](/wiki/White_American), 1.56% [Black](/wiki/Black_American) or [African American](/wiki/African_American), 8.82% [Native American](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 0.21% [Asian](/wiki/Asian_American), 0.01% [Pacific Islander](/wiki/Pacific_Islander_American), 0.73% from [other races](/wiki/Race_(United_States_Census)), and 1.81% from [two or more races](/wiki/Multiracial). [Hispanic](/wiki/Hispanic) or [Latino](/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) of any race were 2.32% of the population.

There were 4,318 [households](/wiki/Household), out of which 31.40% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 55.80% were [married couples](/wiki/Marriage) living together, 9.20% had a female householder with no husband present, and 31.70% were non-families. 28.80% of all households were made up of individuals, and 15.70% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.44 and the average family size was 2.99.

In the county, the population was spread out, with 26.40% under the age of 18, 7.40% from 18 to 24, 24.00% from 25 to 44, 22.70% from 45 to 64, and 19.50% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 40 years. For every 100 females there were 93.50 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 89.80 males.

The [median income for a household](/wiki/Median_household_income) in the county was $31,971, and the median income for a family was $39,525. Males had a median income of $29,163 versus $19,829 for females. The [per capita income](/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was $15,163. About 10.60% of families and 12.90% of the population were below the [poverty line](/wiki/Poverty_line), including 16.40% of those under age 18 and 11.80% of those age 65 or over.

Like all of Kansas outside the eastern cities, Brown County is overwhelmingly Republican, although its history of [Yankee](/wiki/Yankee) settlement means it has been thus for longer than certain other parts of the state. Brown was [Alf Landon](/wiki/Alf_Landon)’s strongest county in his home state during his disastrous 1936 presidential campaign. [FDR](/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) was never to win so much as 42 percent of the vote in any of his four Presidential elections; indeed no Democratic presidential nominee has ever won a majority in Brown County, with the highest percentage being 47 percent by [William Jennings Bryan](/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan) in 1896. A mortally divided Republican Party allowed [Woodrow Wilson](/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) to win a plurality in 1912 with under 37 percent of the county's vote – nonetheless since 1968 no Democrat has reached even that percentage.

### Laws

Following amendment to the [Kansas Constitution](/wiki/Kansas_Constitution) in 1986, the county remained a prohibition, or ["dry"](/wiki/Dry_county), county until 2000, when voters approved the sale of alcoholic liquor by the individual drink without a food sales requirement.

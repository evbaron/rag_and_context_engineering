The following is a **list of awards and nominations received by Christopher Plummer**.

**[Christopher Plummer](/wiki/Christopher_Plummer)** was a Canadian actor known for his diverse roles on stage and screen. Plummer is one of the few actors to have earned the [Triple Crown of Acting](/wiki/Triple_Crown_of_Acting), having won the [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards), [Emmy Award](/wiki/Emmy_Awards) and [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Awards). Other awards Plummer has received include a [British Academy Film Award](/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Awards), a [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and a [Screen Actors Guild Award](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards). Plummer was just a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Awards) away from achieving the [EGOT](/wiki/List_of_EGOT_winners) status (Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony), which is considered the "grand slam" of American show business.

Plummer won the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) at the age of 82 for playing an elderly gay man in [Mike Mills](/wiki/Mike_Mills_(director))' comedy drama *[Beginners](/wiki/Beginners)* (2010), becoming the oldest person to win an acting award (a distinction he held until being supplanted by 83-year-old [Anthony Hopkins](/wiki/Anthony_Hopkins) in 2021). He was Oscar-nominated for playing [Leo Tolstoy](/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) in the drama *[The Last Station](/wiki/The_Last_Station)* (2009) and [J. Paul Getty](/wiki/J._Paul_Getty) in the crime thriller *[All the Money in the World](/wiki/All_the_Money_in_the_World)* (2017), the later making him the oldest person to be nominated in an acting category at the age of 88.

For his work on the [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) stage he won two [Tony Awards](/wiki/Tony_Awards), his first for [Best Actor in a Musical](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Musical) for playing [Cyrano de Bergerac](/wiki/Cyrano_de_Bergerac) in the musical *[Cyrano](/wiki/Cyrano_(musical))* (1974) and [Best Actor in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Play) for playing [John Barrymore](/wiki/John_Barrymore) in the play *[Barrymore](/wiki/Barrymore_(play))* (1997). He was Tony-nominated for his performances in the plays *[J.B.](/wiki/J.B._(play))* (1959), *[Othello](/wiki/Othello)* (1982), *[No Man's Land](/wiki/No_Man%27s_Land_(play))* (1994), *[King Lear](/wiki/King_Lear)* (2004), and *[Inherit the Wind](/wiki/Inherit_the_Wind_(play))* (2007).

For his work on television he has also received seven [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nominations winning twice for [Outstanding Lead Actor in a Miniseries or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie) for *Arthur Hailey's the Moneychangers* (1977) and [Outstanding Voice-Over Performance](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Voice-Over_Performance) for *[Madeline](/wiki/Madeline_(TV_series))* in 1994. He has also received a [Grammy Award for Best Children's Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Children%27s_Album) nomination for [Tchaikovsky](/wiki/Tchaikovsky)'s *[The Nutcracker](/wiki/The_Nutcracker)* in 1986.

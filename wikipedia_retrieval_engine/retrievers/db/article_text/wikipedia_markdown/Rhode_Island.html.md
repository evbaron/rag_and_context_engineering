**Rhode Island** ([/ˌroʊd -/](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [ⓘ](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:En-us-Rhode_Island.ogg) [*ROHD*](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [state](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state) in the [New England](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) region of the [Northeastern](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_United_States) [United States](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). It borders [Connecticut](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut) to its west; [Massachusetts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts) to its north and east; and the [Atlantic Ocean](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Ocean) to its south via [Rhode Island Sound](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Sound) and [Block Island Sound](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island_Sound); and shares a small [maritime border](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_border) with [New York](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_(state)), east of [Long Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island). Rhode Island is the [smallest U.S. state by area](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_area) and the [seventh-least populous](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States_by_population), with slightly more than 1.11 million residents as of 2025. The state's population, however, has continually recorded growth in every [decennial census](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_census) since 1790, and it is the [second-most densely populated state](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density) after [New Jersey](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey). The state takes its name from [the eponymous island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquidneck_Island), though most of its land area is on the mainland. [Providence](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island) is its capital and most populous city.

[Native Americans](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_of_the_United_States) lived around [Narragansett Bay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay) before [English](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people) settlers began arriving in the early 17th century. Rhode Island was unique among the [Thirteen British Colonies](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) in having been founded by a refugee, [Roger Williams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Williams), who fled [religious persecution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution) in the [Massachusetts Bay Colony](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) to establish a haven for religious liberty. He founded Providence in 1636 on land purchased from local tribes, creating the first settlement in North America with an explicitly secular government. The [Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Rhode_Island_and_Providence_Plantations) subsequently became a destination for religious and political dissenters and social outcasts, earning it the moniker "Rogue's Island".

Rhode Island was the first colony to call for a [Continental Congress](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress), in 1774, and the first to renounce its allegiance to the British [Crown](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown), on May 4, 1776. After the [American Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), during which it was heavily occupied and contested, Rhode Island became the fourth state to ratify the [Articles of Confederation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation), on February 9, 1778. Because its citizens favored a weaker central government, it boycotted the [1787 convention](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_(United_States)) that had drafted the [United States Constitution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States), which it initially refused to ratify; it finally ratified it on May 29, 1790, the last of the original 13 states to do so.

The state was officially named the **State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations** since the colonial era but came to be commonly known as "Rhode Island". On November 3, 2020, the state's voters [approved an amendment](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Rhode_Island_Question_1) to the [state constitution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Rhode_Island) formally dropping "and Providence Plantations" from its full name. Its official nickname, found on its welcome sign, is the "Ocean State", a reference to its 400 mi (640 km) of coastline and the large bays and inlets that make up about 14% of its area.

## Name

### Origins

Despite its name, most of Rhode Island is on the U.S. mainland. Its official name was *State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations* from its beginning in 1636 until 2020, and it is referred to in that manner in the [United States Constitution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). This name was derived from the merger of Colonial settlements around [Narragansett Bay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay), and outside the jurisdiction of Plymouth colony. The settlements of Rhode Island ([Newport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island) and [Portsmouth](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Rhode_Island)) were on Rhode Island, also known as [Aquidneck Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquidneck_Island). *Providence Plantations* referred to settlements on the mainland of Providence and [Warwick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick,_Rhode_Island).

It is unclear how the island came to be named *Rhode Island*, but two historical events may have been influential:

- Explorer [Giovanni da Verrazzano](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_da_Verrazzano) noted the presence of an island near the mouth of [Narragansett Bay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay) in 1524 which he likened to the island of [Rhodes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes) off the coast of Greece. Subsequent European explorers were unable to precisely identify the island Verrazzano described, but the colonists who settled the area assumed that it was this island.
- [Adriaen Block](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriaen_Block) passed by the island during his expeditions in the 1610s, and described it in a 1625 account of his travels as "an island of reddish appearance", which was "*een rodlich Eylande*" in 17th-century Dutch, meaning a red or reddish island, supposedly evolving into the designation Rhode Island. Historians have theorized that this "reddish appearance" resulted from either red autumn foliage or red clay on portions of the shore.

The earliest documented use of the name "Rhode Island" for Aquidneck was in 1637 by [Roger Williams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Williams). The name was officially applied to the island in 1644 with these words: "Aquethneck shall be henceforth called the Isle of Rodes or Rhode-Island." The name "Isle of Rodes" is used in a legal document as late as 1646. Dutch maps as early as 1659 call the island "Red Island" (*Roodt Eylandt*).

### 2020 change

The first English settlement in Rhode Island was the town of Providence, which the [Narragansett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_people) granted to Roger Williams in 1636. At that time, Williams obtained no permission from the English crown, as he believed the English had no legitimate claim on Narragansett and [Wampanoag](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wampanoag) territory. Williams traveled to London in 1643, during the English Civil War, to obtain legal recognition of the new settlements. A [patent](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent) was granted to "the incorporation of Providence Plantations in Narragansett Bay in New England" by the Parliamentary committee on [Foreign Plantations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_(settlement_or_colony)). After the English Civil war, a Royal Charter was granted in 1663, giving the colony an official name of the "Governor and Company of the English Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations, in New England, in America." Following the [American Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), in 1790 the new state incorporated as the "State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations". However, as matter of convenience, the state came to be commonly known as simply "Rhode Island".

The word *plantation* in the state's name became a contested issue during the 20th century and the increased awareness of slavery and its role in early Rhode Island history. The General Assembly voted in 2009 to hold a referendum in November 2010 on removing "and Providence Plantations" from the official name. Advocates for excising *plantation* argued that the word symbolized a legacy of disenfranchisement for many Rhode Islanders, as well as the proliferation of slavery in the colonies and in the post-colonial United States. Advocates for retaining the name argued that *plantation* was simply an archaic synonym for *colony* and bore no relation to slavery. The people voted overwhelmingly (78% to 22%) to retain the entire original name.

In June 2020, state senator [Harold Metts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Metts) introduced a resolution for another ballot referendum on the subject, saying, "Whatever the meaning of the term 'plantations' in the context of Rhode Island's history, it carries a horrific connotation when considering the tragic and racist history of our nation." Governor [Gina Raimondo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Raimondo) issued an executive order to remove the phrase from a range of official documents and state websites. In July, amidst the [George Floyd protests](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests) and nationwide calls to address systemic racism, the resolution referring the question to the voters was passed by both houses of the [Rhode Island General Assembly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_General_Assembly): 69–1 in the [House of Representatives](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_House_of_Representatives), and 35–0 in the [Senate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Senate). The change was then approved by voters 52.8% to 47.2% as part of the [2020 United States elections](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_elections), taking effect in November 2020 upon certification of the results.

## History

Main article: [History of Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rhode_Island)

### Colonial era: 1636–1770

Main article: [Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Rhode_Island_and_Providence_Plantations)

In 1636, Roger Williams and his followers founded the settlement of Providence Plantations.

At the onset of European colonization what is now Rhode Island was inhabited mainly by five Native American tribes — by far most of the state's territory was inhabited by the [Narragansett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_people), eastern borderlands were occupied by the [Wampanoag](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wampanoag), south-western coast by the [Niantic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niantic_people), western borderlands by the [Pequot](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pequots) and northern borderlands by the [Nipmuc](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipmuc). In 1636, [Roger Williams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Williams) was banished from the [Massachusetts Bay Colony](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) for his religious views, and he settled at the top of Narragansett Bay on land sold or given to him by Narragansett sachem [Canonicus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonicus). He named the site Providence, "having a sense of God's merciful providence unto me in my distress", and it became a place of religious freedom where all were welcome.

In 1936, on the 300th anniversary of the settlement of Rhode Island in 1636, the U.S. Post Office issued a commemorative stamp, depicting Roger Williams

In 1638 (after conferring with Williams), [Anne Hutchinson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hutchinson), [William Coddington](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Coddington), [John Clarke](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clarke_(Baptist_minister)), [Philip Sherman](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sherman_(settler)), and other religious dissenters were allowed to settle on [Aquidneck Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquidneck_Island) (also known as Rhode Island), by the Narragansett Sachems [Canonicus](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonicus) and [Miantonomi](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miantonomi). They were given a few items in reciprocity for their generosity. However, as Roger Williams made clear in a letter to John Winthrop in June 1638: "Sir, concerning the islands Prudence and…Aquedenick…neither of them were sold properly, for a thousand fathom would not have bought either, by strangers. The truth is, not a penny was demanded for either, and what was paid was only gratuity, though I chose, for better assurance and form, to call it sale." This settlement was first called Pocasset and then changed in 1639 to [Portsmouth](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Rhode_Island). The town was governed by the [Portsmouth Compact](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Compact). The island's southern part became the separate settlement of [Newport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island) after disagreements among the founders.

[Samuel Gorton](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Gorton) purchased lands at [Shawomet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick,_Rhode_Island) in 1642 from the Narragansetts, precipitating a dispute with the Massachusetts Bay Colony. In 1644, Providence, Portsmouth, and Newport united for their common independence as the [Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Rhode_Island_and_Providence_Plantations), governed by an elected council and "president". Gorton received a separate charter for his settlement in 1648 which he named [Warwick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick,_Rhode_Island) after his patron.

[Metacomet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacomet) was the [Wampanoag](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wampanoag_people) tribe's war leader, whom the colonists called King Philip. They invaded and burned down several of the towns in the area during [King Philip's War](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Philip%27s_War) (1675–1676), including Providence which was attacked twice. A force of Massachusetts, Connecticut, and Plymouth militia under General [Josiah Winslow](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josiah_Winslow) invaded and destroyed the fortified Narragansett Indian village in the [Great Swamp](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Swamp_Fight) in [South Kingstown, Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kingstown,_Rhode_Island), on December 19, 1675. In one of the final actions of the war, an Indian associated with [Benjamin Church](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Church_(ranger)) killed King Philip in [Bristol, Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol,_Rhode_Island).

In 1680, Newport was the third largest Anglo-American city. It remained a prosperous population center until the 1770s.

The colony was amalgamated into the [Dominion of New England](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_New_England) in 1686, as [King James II](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England) attempted to enforce royal authority over the autonomous colonies in [British North America](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America), but the colony regained its independence under the Royal Charter after the [Glorious Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution) of 1688. Slaves were introduced in Rhode Island at this time, although there is no record of any law legalizing slave-holding. The colony later prospered under the slave trade, distilling rum to sell in Africa as part of a profitable [triangular trade](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_trade) in slaves and sugar with the Caribbean. Rhode Island's legislative body passed an act in 1652 abolishing the holding of slaves (the first British colony to do so), but this edict was never enforced and Rhode Island continued to be heavily involved in the [slave trade](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_trade_in_the_United_States) during the post-revolution era. In 1774, the slave population of Rhode Island was 6.3% of the total (nearly twice the ratio of other [New England](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) colonies).

[Brown University](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) was founded in 1764 as the college in the British Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations. It was one of nine [Colonial colleges](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_colleges) granted charters before the American Revolution and was the first college in America to accept students regardless of religious affiliation.

### Revolutionary to Civil War period: 1770–1860

Providence Revolutionaries burned [HMS *Gaspee*](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspee_Affair) in Warwick in protest of British customs laws.

Rhode Island's tradition of independence and dissent gave it a prominent role in the [American Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). At approximately 2 a.m. on June 10, 1772, a band of Providence residents [attacked](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspee_Affair) the grounded revenue schooner *[HMS Gaspée](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Gasp%C3%A9e_(1773))*, burning it to the waterline for enforcing [unpopular trade regulations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation_Acts) within Narragansett Bay. Rhode Island was the first of the thirteen colonies to renounce its allegiance to the British Crown on May 4, 1776. It was also the last of the thirteen colonies to ratify the [United States Constitution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) on May 29, 1790, and only under threat of heavy trade tariffs from the other former colonies and after assurances were made that a [Bill of Rights](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights) would become part of the Constitution.

A nine-pence banknote issued by Rhode Island in 1786

During the Revolution, the British occupied Newport in December 1776. A combined Franco-American force fought to drive them off Aquidneck Island. Portsmouth was the site of the first African-American military unit, the [1st Rhode Island Regiment](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Rhode_Island_Regiment), to fight for the U.S. in the unsuccessful [Battle of Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rhode_Island) of August 29, 1778. A month earlier, the appearance of a French fleet off Newport caused the British to scuttle some of their own ships in an attempt to block the harbor. The British abandoned Newport in October 1779, concentrating their forces in New York City. An [expedition of 5,500 French troops](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exp%C3%A9dition_Particuli%C3%A8re) under [Count Rochambeau](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Donatien_de_Vimeur,_comte_de_Rochambeau) arrived in Newport by sea on July 10, 1780. The [celebrated march](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington-Rochambeau_Revolutionary_Route) to [Yorktown, Virginia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorktown,_Virginia), in 1781 ended with the defeat of the British at the [Siege of Yorktown](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown) and the [Battle of the Chesapeake](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Chesapeake).

Rhode Island was also heavily involved in the [Industrial Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), which began in America in 1787 when [Thomas Somers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Somers_(investor)) reproduced textile machine plans which he imported from England. He helped to produce the [Beverly Cotton Manufactory](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Cotton_Manufactory), in which [Moses Brown](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Brown_(Providence)) of Providence took an interest. Moses Brown teamed up with [Samuel Slater](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Slater) and helped to create the second cotton mill in America, a water-powered textile mill. The [Industrial Revolution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution#Belgium) moved large numbers of workers into the cities. With the 1663 [colonial charter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Royal_Charter) still in effect, voting was restricted to landowners holding at least $134 in property. At the time of the revolution, 80% of White men in Rhode Island could vote; by 1840, only 40% were still eligible. The charter apportioned legislative seats equally among the state's towns, over-representing rural areas and under-representing the growing industrial centers. Additionally, the charter disallowed landless citizens from filing civil suits without endorsement from a landowner. Bills were periodically introduced in the legislature to expand suffrage, but they were invariably defeated.

In 1841, activists led by [Thomas W. Dorr](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_W._Dorr) organized an extralegal convention to draft a state constitution, arguing the charter government violated the [Guarantee Clause](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guarantee_Clause) in Article Four, Section Four of the [United States Constitution](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). In 1842, the charter government and Dorr's supporters held separate elections, and two rival governments claimed sovereignty over the state. Dorr's supporters led an armed [rebellion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorr_rebellion) against the charter government, and Dorr was arrested and imprisoned for treason against the state.

In response, the legislature drafted a state constitution which replaced property requirements for American-born citizens with a $1 [poll tax](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax_(United_States)), equivalent to $33 in 2025. In a heavily boycotted election in November 1842, voters approved the constitution. Voters also declined to limit the change to "white" men, thus re-enfranchising Black men—Black men meeting the property requirements had been able to vote in Rhode Island until 1822. The constitution also ended slavery. Immigrants remained subject to the property requirement, effectively disenfranchising many Irish-Americans and maintaining urban under-representation. In 1849, in [Luther v. Borden](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_v._Borden), the US Supreme Court declined to rule on the constitutional question raised in Dorr's rebellion, holding that it was a political question outside its jurisdiction.

In the early 19th century, Rhode Island was subject to a tuberculosis outbreak which led to [public hysteria about vampirism](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_vampire_panic).

### Civil War

The [United States Naval Academy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) was moved to [Fort Adams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Adams) in Newport during the Civil War.

Main article: [Rhode Island in the American Civil War](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_in_the_American_Civil_War)

During the [American Civil War](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Rhode Island was the first Union state to send troops in response to [President Lincoln](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln)'s request for help from the states. Rhode Island furnished 23,700 fighting men, of whom 1,685 died. On the home front, Rhode Island and the other northern states used their industrial capacity to supply the Union Army with the materials it needed to win the war. The [United States Naval Academy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) moved to Rhode Island temporarily during the war.

In 1866, Rhode Island abolished racial segregation in the public schools throughout the state.

### Gilded Age

Interior of [The Breakers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breakers), a Newport symbol of the Gilded Age

The 50 years following the Civil War were a time of prosperity and affluence that author William G. McLoughlin calls "Rhode Island's halcyon era". Rhode Island was a center of the [Gilded Age](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilded_Age) and provided a home or summer home to many of the country's most prominent industrialists. This was a time of growth in textile mills and manufacturing and brought an influx of immigrants to fill those jobs, bringing population growth and urbanization. In [Newport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island), New York's wealthiest industrialists created a summer haven to socialize and build [grand mansions](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preservation_Society_of_Newport_County). Thousands of French-Canadian, Italian, Irish, and Portuguese immigrants arrived to fill jobs in the textile and manufacturing mills in Providence, Pawtucket, Central Falls, and Woonsocket.

### World War I

During World War I, Rhode Island furnished 28,817 soldiers, of whom 612 died. After the war, the state was hit hard by the [Spanish Influenza](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Influenza).

In the 1920s and 1930s, rural Rhode Island saw a surge in [Ku Klux Klan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan) membership, largely in reaction to large waves of [immigrants](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrants) moving to the state. The Klan is believed to be responsible for burning the [Watchman Industrial School](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchman_Industrial_School) in [Scituate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scituate,_Rhode_Island), which was a school for African-American children.

### Growth in the modern era: 1929–present

Since the [Great Depression](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), the [Rhode Island Democratic Party](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Democratic_Party) has dominated local politics. Rhode Island has comprehensive health insurance for low-income children and a large [social safety net](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_safety_net). However, many urban areas still have a high rate of child poverty. Due to an influx of residents from [Boston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston), increasing housing costs have resulted in more homelessness in Rhode Island.

Downtown Providence in 2008

The 350th anniversary of the founding of Rhode Island was celebrated with a free concert held on the tarmac of the Quonset State Airport on August 31, 1986. Performers included [Chuck Berry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Berry), [Tommy James](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_James), and headliner [Bob Hope](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope).

## Geography

Further information: [Geology of New England](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_New_England) and [Climate change in Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Rhode_Island)

Topographic map of Rhode Island

Rhode Island covers an area of 1,034 square miles (2,678 km2) within the New England region of the [Northeastern United States](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_United_States) and is bordered on the north and east by Massachusetts, on the west by Connecticut, and on the south by [Rhode Island Sound](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Sound) and the Atlantic Ocean. It shares a narrow maritime border with New York State between Block Island and [Long Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island). The state's mean elevation is 200 feet (61 m). It is only 37 miles (60 km) wide and 48 miles (77 km) long, yet the state has a tidal shoreline on [Narragansett Bay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay) and the Atlantic Ocean of 384 miles (618 km).

Rhode Island is nicknamed the Ocean State and has a number of oceanfront [beaches](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_beaches_in_Rhode_Island). It is mostly flat with no real mountains, and the state's highest natural point is [Jerimoth Hill](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerimoth_Hill), 812 feet (247 m) above sea level. The state has two distinct natural regions. Eastern Rhode Island contains the lowlands of the Narragansett Bay, while Western Rhode Island forms part of the New England upland. Rhode Island's forests are part of the [Northeastern coastal forests](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_coastal_forests) ecoregion.

Narragansett Bay is a major feature of the state's topography. There are more than 30 islands within the bay; the largest is [Aquidneck Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquidneck_Island), which holds the municipalities of Newport, Middletown, and Portsmouth. The second-largest island is [Conanicut](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conanicut_Island), and the third is [Prudence](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudence_Island). [Block Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island) lies about 12 miles (19 km) off the southern coast of the mainland and separates [Block Island Sound](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island_Sound) and the Atlantic Ocean proper.

A rare type of rock called [Cumberlandite](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberlandite) can be found in [Cumberland](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland,_Rhode_Island) (the only place within the United States in which it can be found) and is the state rock. There were initially two known deposits of the mineral, but it is an ore of iron, and one of the deposits was extensively mined for its ferrous content.

### Climate

[Köppen climate types](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Rhode Island, using 1991–2020 [climate normals](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatological_normal)

Rhode Island lies at the broad transition zone from the continental climates to the north and the subtropical climates to the south. The basic climate features warm to hot summers and cool winters with a mix of rain and snow. [Block Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island) has an [oceanic climate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate). The highest temperature recorded in Rhode Island was 104 °F (40 °C), recorded on August 2, 1975, in Providence. The lowest recorded temperature in Rhode Island was −23 °F (−31 °C) on February 5, 1996, in [Greene](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene,_Rhode_Island). Monthly average temperatures range from a high of 83 °F (28 °C) to a low of 20 °F (−7 °C).

Rhode Island is vulnerable to tropical storms and hurricanes due to its location along the East Coast. Hurricanes that have done significant damage in the state include the [1938 New England hurricane](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_New_England_hurricane), [Hurricane Carol](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Carol) (1954), [Hurricane Donna](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Donna) (1960), and [Hurricane Bob](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Bob) (1991).

Due to the relatively flat nature of Rhode Island's seashore, the state is vulnerable to storm surge and coastal erosion, especially as sea levels rise.

## Landmarks

See also: [List of Registered Historic Places in Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Registered_Historic_Places_in_Rhode_Island)

The [Rhode Island State House](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_State_House) in Providence boasts the world's fourth largest self-supported marble dome.

The [state capitol building](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_State_House) is made of white Georgian marble. On top is the world's fourth largest self-supported marble dome. It houses the Rhode Island Charter granted by [King Charles II](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) in 1663, the Brown University charter, and other state treasures.

The [First Baptist Church of Providence](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Baptist_Church_in_America) is the oldest [Baptist church](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists_in_the_United_States) in the Americas, founded by [Roger Williams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Williams) in 1638.

The first fully automated post office in the country is in Providence. There are many historic mansions in the seaside city of Newport, including [The Breakers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Breakers), [Marble House](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_House), and [Belcourt Castle](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belcourt_Castle). Also there is the [Touro Synagogue](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touro_Synagogue), dedicated on December 2, 1763, considered by locals to be the first synagogue within the United States (see below for information on New York City's claim), and still serving. The synagogue showcases the religious freedoms established by Roger Williams, as well as impressive architecture in a mix of the classic colonial and Sephardic style. The [Newport Casino](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Casino) is a [National Historic Landmark](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark) building complex that houses the [International Tennis Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tennis_Hall_of_Fame) and features an active grass-court tennis club.

[The Towers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Towers_(Narragansett,_Rhode_Island)) are a [Narragansett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett,_Rhode_Island) landmark.

[Scenic Route 1A](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_1A) (known locally as Ocean Road) is in [Narragansett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett,_Rhode_Island). "[The Towers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Towers_(Narragansett,_Rhode_Island))" is also in Narragansett featuring a large stone arch. It was once the entrance to a famous Narragansett casino that burned down in 1900. The Towers now serve as an event venue and host the local Chamber of Commerce, which operates a tourist information center.

The [Newport Tower](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Tower_(Rhode_Island)) has been hypothesized to be of [Viking](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) origin, although most experts believe it was a Colonial-era windmill.

## Environment

### Environmental legislation

The [Block Island Wind Farm](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island_Wind_Farm) is the first commercial offshore wind farm in the United States.

On May 29, 2014, Governor Lincoln D. Chafee announced that Rhode Island was one of eight states to release a collaborative Action Plan to put 3.3 million zero-emission vehicles on its roads by 2025. The plan's purpose is to reduce [greenhouse gas](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas) and smog-causing emissions. The plan promotes [zero-emissions vehicles](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-emissions_vehicle) and investments in the infrastructure to support them.

In 2014, Rhode Island received grants of $2,711,685 from the [Environmental Protection Agency](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency) to clean up [Brownfield sites](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownfield_land) in eight locations. The grants provided communities with funding to assess, clean up, and redevelop contaminated properties, boost local economies, and leverage jobs while protecting public health and the environment.

In 2013, the "Lots of Hope" program was established in the City of Providence to focus on increasing the city's green space and local food production, improve urban neighborhoods, promote healthy lifestyles and improve environmental sustainability. Supported by a $100,000 grant, the program will partner with the City of Providence, the Southside Community Land Trust, and the Rhode Island Foundation to convert city-owned vacant lots into productive urban farms.

In 2012, Rhode Island passed bill S2277/H7412, "An act relating to Health and Safety – Environmental Cleanup Objectives for Schools", informally known as the School Siting Bill. Sponsored by Senator [Juan Pichardo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pichardo) and Representative [Scott Slater](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_A._Slater), and signed into law by the governor, it made Rhode Island the first US State to prohibit school construction on Brownfield sites where toxic vapors can potentially affect indoor air quality. It also creates a public participation process whenever a city or town considers building a school on any other kind of contaminated site.

On April 14, 2021, the 2021 Act on Climate was signed. It specified a set of increasingly stringent emissions mandates for the state for the first year of every decade from 2020 until 2050, with the ultimate goal of the state producing [net-zero emissions](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net-zero_emissions) by 2050.

Of the people residing in Rhode Island, 58.7% were born in Rhode Island, 26.6% were born in a different state, 2.0% were born in Puerto Rico, U.S. Island areas or born abroad to American parent(s), and 12.6% were foreign born.

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, as of 2015, Rhode Island had an estimated population of 1,056,298, which is an increase of 1,125, or 0.10%, from the prior year and an increase of 3,731, or 0.35%, since the year 2010. This includes a natural increase since the last census of 15,220 people (that is 66,973 births minus 51,753 deaths) and an increase due to net migration of 14,001 people into the state. [Immigration](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_United_States) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 18,965 people, and migration within the country produced a net decrease of 4,964 people. In 2018, The top countries of origin for Rhode Island's immigrants were the [Dominican Republic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic), [Guatemala](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala), [Portugal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal), [Cape Verde](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde) and [India](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India).

[Hispanics](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanics_in_the_United_States) in the state make up 18.9% of the population, predominantly [Dominican](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Americans) (5.9%), [Puerto Rican](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateside_Puerto_Ricans) (4.4%), and [Guatemalan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemalan_Americans) (3.9%) populations. Rhode Island has the highest share of Dominican Americans in the country according to latest estimates, putting the state at sixth largest Dominican community in the country.

According to the [2000 U.S. census](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census), 84% of the population aged 5 and older spoke only [American English](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English), while 8.07% spoke Spanish at home, 3.80% Portuguese, 1.96% French, 1.39% Italian and 0.78% speak other languages at home accordingly.

The state's most populous ethnic group, non-Hispanic white, has declined from 96.1% in 1970 to 76.5% in 2011. In 2011, 40.3% of Rhode Island's children under the age of one belonged to racial or ethnic minority groups, meaning they had at least one parent who was not non-Hispanic white.

6.1% of Rhode Island's population were reported as under 5, 23.6% under 18, and 14.5% were 65 or older. Females made up approximately 52% of the population.

According to the 2010–2015 [American Community Survey](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), the largest ancestry groups were [Irish](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_American) (18.3%), [Italian](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_American) (18.0%), [English](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_American) (10.5%), [French](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American) (10.4%), and [Portuguese](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_American) (9.3%). Rhode Island has some of the highest percentages of Irish Americans and Italian Americans. Italian Americans make up a plurality in central and southern Providence County and [French-Canadian Americans](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadian_Americans) form a large part of northern Providence County. Irish Americans have a strong presence in Newport and Kent counties. Americans of English ancestry still have a presence in the state as well, especially in [Washington County](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Rhode_Island), and are often referred to as "[Swamp Yankees](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamp_Yankees)".

Rhode Island has a notable Lusophone community, having a higher percentage of Americans of Portuguese ancestry than any other state, including [Portuguese Americans](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_American) and [Cape Verdean Americans](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_American). Additionally, the state also has the highest percentage of [Liberian](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia) immigrants, with more than 15,000 residing in the state. African immigrants, including those from Cape Verde and Liberia, form significant and growing communities in Rhode Island. Rhode Island is one of the few states where Black people of recent foreign origin outnumber Black people of multigenerational American origin (African Americans). Rhode Island also has a sizable Asian community. There is one [federally recognized](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes_by_state) tribe in Rhode Island, the Narragansett Indian Tribe.

Although Rhode Island has the smallest land area of all 50 states, it has the [second highest population density](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density), second to that of New Jersey.

A Pew survey of Rhode Island residents' religious self-identification in 2014 showed the following distribution of affiliations: [Catholic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_United_States) 42%, [Protestant](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) 30%, [Jewish](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jews) 1%, [Jehovah's Witnesses](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah%27s_Witnesses) 2%, [Buddhism](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_United_States) 1%, [Mormonism](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism) 1%, [Hinduism](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_the_United_States) 1%, and [Non-religious](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-religious) 20%. The largest Christian denominations as of 2010 were the [Catholic Church](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) with 456,598 adherents, the [Episcopal Church](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)) with 19,377, the [American Baptist Churches USA](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Baptist_Churches_USA) with 15,220, and the [United Methodist Church](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodist_Church) with 6,901 adherents.

Rhode Island has had the highest proportion of Catholic residents of any state according to a study in 2000, mainly due to large Irish, Italian, and French-Canadian immigration in the past; recently, significant Portuguese and various Hispanic or Latino communities have also been established in the state. Though it has the highest overall Catholic percentage of any state, none of Rhode Island's individual counties ranks among the 10 most Catholic in the United States, as Catholics are evenly spread throughout the state.

According to the [Public Religion Research Institute](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Religion_Research_Institute) (PRRI) in 2020, 67% of the population were Christian, spread among [evangelical](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) and [mainline Protestantism](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant), and Roman Catholicism. In 2022, the Public Religion Research Institute revealed 72% of the population were Christian.

[Touro Synagogue](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touro_Synagogue) in Newport is the [oldest existing synagogue building](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldest_synagogues_in_the_United_States) in the United States.

Rhode Island's Jewish community, centered in the Providence area, emerged during a wave of Jewish immigration predominantly from Eastern Europeans [shtetls](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shtetl) between 1880 and 1920. The presence of the [Touro Synagogue](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touro_Synagogue) in Newport, the oldest existing synagogue in the United States, emphasizes that these second-wave immigrants did not create Rhode Island's first Jewish community; a comparatively smaller wave of [Spanish and Portuguese Jews](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_and_Portuguese_Jews) immigrated to Newport during the colonial era. In 2022, they made up 2% of the state's population.

The religiously unaffiliated since 2014 were 20% of the population, though the separate study by the Public Religion Research Institute determined the irreligious increased to 29% of the adult population. In 2022, the religiously unaffiliated declined to 24% of the population.

### Native American tribes

Today, many inhabitants of Rhode Island identify as being Native American alone (6,058 people in 2010 census and 7,385 in 2020) or Native American in combination with one or more other races (8,336 people in 2010 census and 15,972 in 2020). Many inhabitants of Rhode Island also reported belonging to tribes in 2010 census, the largest of which were the Narragansett (2,820 people), the Cherokee (987), the Wampanoag (559) and the Pequot (424). Other tribes included the Iroquois (278), the [Micmac](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi%27kmaq) (101), the [Abenaki](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abenaki) (100), the Nipmuc (99) and more.

## Economy

See also: [Rhode Island locations by per capita income](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_locations_by_per_capita_income)

The Rhode Island economy had a colonial base in fishing.

[Slater Mill](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slater_Mill_Historic_Site) in Pawtucket is cited as the birthplace of the [Industrial Revolution in the United States](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution_in_the_United_States).

The Blackstone River Valley was a major contributor to the [American](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_the_United_States) Industrial Revolution. It was in [Pawtucket](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket,_Rhode_Island) that [Samuel Slater](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Slater) set up [Slater Mill](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slater_Mill) in 1793, using the waterpower of the [Blackstone River](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_River) to power his [cotton mill](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_mill). For a while, Rhode Island was one of the leaders in textiles. With the [Great Depression](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), most textile factories relocated to [southern U.S. states](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_United_States).
The [textile industry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry) still constitutes a part of the Rhode Island economy but does not have the same power.

Other important industries in Rhode Island's past included toolmaking, [costume jewelry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costume_jewelry), and [silverware](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutlery). An interesting by-product of Rhode Island's industrial history is the number of abandoned factories, many of which are now condominiums, museums, offices, and low-income and elderly housing. Today, much of Rhode Island's economy is based on services, particularly healthcare and education, and still manufacturing to some extent.
The state's nautical history continues in the 21st century in the form of [nuclear submarine](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine) construction.

Per the 2013 [American Community Survey](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), Rhode Island has the highest paid elementary school teachers in the country, with an average salary of $75,028 (adjusted to inflation).

The [headquarters of Textron](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textron_Tower) and [Citizens Financial Group](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Citizens_Plaza) in downtown Providence

The headquarters of [Citizens Financial Group](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Financial_Group), the 14th largest bank in the United States, is in [Providence](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island). The Fortune 500 companies [CVS Caremark](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CVS_Caremark) and [Textron](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textron) are based in [Woonsocket](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woonsocket,_Rhode_Island) and Providence, respectively. [FM Global](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_Global), [GTECH Corporation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTECH_Corporation), [Hasbro](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbro), [American Power Conversion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Power_Conversion), Nortek, and [Amica Mutual Insurance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amica_Mutual_Insurance) are all Fortune 1000 companies based in Rhode Island.

According to the [Bureau of Economic Analysis](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis), Rhode Island's [gross state product](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) in 2024 was [$](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar)82.5 billion, ranking 44th in the nation.

Rhode Island's 2000 total gross state production was $46.18 billion (adjusted to inflation), placing it 45th in the nation. Its 2000 *per capita* personal income was $41,484 (adjusted to inflation), 16th in the nation. Rhode Island has the lowest level of energy consumption per capita of any state. Additionally, Rhode Island was rated as the 5th most energy efficient state in the country. As of August 2025, the state's unemployment rate was 4.6%. The historically highest unemployment rate was recorded April 2020 at 17.9%, and its lowest was 2.6% in June 2023.

Health services are (as of 2007) Rhode Island's largest industry. Second is tourism, supporting 39,000 jobs, with tourism-related sales at $4.56 billion (adjusted to inflation) in the year 2000. The third-largest industry is manufacturing. Its industrial outputs are submarine construction, shipbuilding, costume jewelry, fabricated metal products, electrical equipment, machinery, and boatbuilding. Rhode Island's agricultural outputs are nursery stock, vegetables, dairy products, and eggs. The largest single product is [milk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk), which in 2017 totaled $4,563,000 in sales. In 2025, 98.9% of businesses in Rhode Island were [small businesses](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_business) and employed 51.1% of the state's workforce.

Rhode Island's taxes were appreciably higher than those of neighboring states, because Rhode Island's income tax was based on 25% of the payer's federal income tax payment.
Former [governor Donald Carcieri](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Carcieri) claimed the higher tax rate had an inhibitory effect on business growth in the state and called for reductions to increase the competitiveness of the state's business environment. In 2010, the Rhode Island General Assembly passed a new state income tax structure that Governor Carcieri signed into law on June 9, 2010. The income tax overhaul has made Rhode Island competitive with other New England states by lowering its maximum tax rate to 5.99% and reducing the number of tax brackets to three. The state's first income tax was enacted in 1971.

## Transportation

Further information: [Rhode Island Department of Transportation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Department_of_Transportation)

### Bus

The [Rhode Island Public Transit Authority](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Public_Transit_Authority) operates a statewide system of bus transport.

The [Rhode Island Public Transit Authority](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Public_Transit_Authority) (RIPTA) operates statewide intra- and intercity bus transport from its hubs at [Kennedy Plaza](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Plaza) in Providence, [Pawtucket](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket,_Rhode_Island), and [Newport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island). RIPTA bus routes serve 38 of Rhode Island's 39 cities and towns. ([New Shoreham](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shoreham,_Rhode_Island) on [Block Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island) is not served). RIPTA operates 58 routes, including daytime trolley service (using trolley-style replica buses) in Providence and Newport.

### Ferry

From 2000 through 2008, RIPTA offered seasonal ferry service linking Providence and Newport (already connected by highway) funded by grant money from the [United States Department of Transportation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Transportation). Though the service was popular with residents and tourists, RIPTA was unable to continue after the federal funding ended. Service was discontinued as of 2010. The service resumed in 2016 and has been successful, and as of 2025, is provided by [Seastreak](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seastreak). The privately run Block Island Ferry links [Block Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_Island) with [Newport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island) and [Narragansett](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett,_Rhode_Island) with traditional and fast-ferry service, while the Prudence Island Ferry connects [Bristol](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol,_Rhode_Island) with [Prudence Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudence_Island). Private ferry services also link several Rhode Island communities with ports in [Connecticut](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut), [Massachusetts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts), and New York.

### Rail

A southbound [Northeast Regional](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional) train at [Kingston Station](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_station_(Rhode_Island))

See also: [List of Rhode Island railroads](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Rhode_Island_railroads)

The [MBTA Commuter Rail](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_Commuter_Rail)'s [Providence/Stoughton Line](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence/Stoughton_Line) links Providence and [T. F. Green Airport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._F._Green_Airport) with Boston's [South Station](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Station), with an intermediate stop at [Pawtucket/Central Falls](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket/Central_Falls) and several stations in Massachusetts. The line was later extended southward to [Wickford Junction](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wickford_Junction_(MBTA_station)), with service beginning April 23, 2012. The state has considered proposals for extending the MBTA line to [Kingston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_Railroad_Station_(Rhode_Island)) and [Westerly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerly_(Amtrak_station)), as well as explore the possibility of extending Connecticut's [Shore Line East](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_Line_East) to T.F. Green Airport. Other proposals have contemplated a light-rail tram or rapid bus transit through: Cumberland, Central Falls, Pawtucket, Providence, Cranston, and Warwick as well. The Federal government's Amtrak [Acela Express](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express) stops at [Providence Station](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Station) (the only Acela stop in Rhode Island), linking Providence to other cities in the [Northeast Corridor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor). Amtrak's [Northeast Regional](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional) service makes stops at [Providence Station](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Station), [Kingston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_Railroad_Station_(Rhode_Island)), and [Westerly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerly_(Amtrak_station)).

Rhode Island is one of four states to have high-speed [Northeast Corridor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) tracks capable of 165 mph. This was due to the fact that in the 1990s, Amtrak upgraded the NEC north of New Haven, CT to get it ready for the high-speed *[Acela Express](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express)* trains and to extend electrified [Northeast Corridor](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) services to Boston.

### Aviation

See also: [Aviation in Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_in_Rhode_Island) and [List of airports in Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Rhode_Island)

Rhode Island's primary airport for passenger and cargo transport is [T. F. Green Airport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._F._Green_Airport) in [Warwick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick,_Rhode_Island), though Rhode Islanders who wish to travel internationally on direct flights and those who seek a greater availability of flights and destinations often fly through [Logan International Airport](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_International_Airport) in Boston.

### Limited access highways

The [Jamestown Verrazzano Bridge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Verrazzano_Bridge) (foreground) and [Claiborne Pell Newport Bridge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claiborne_Pell_Newport_Bridge) (background)

[Interstate 95](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95_in_Rhode_Island) (I-95) runs southwest to northeast across the state, linking Rhode Island with other states along the [East Coast](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States). [I-295](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_295_(Rhode_Island-Massachusetts)) functions as a partial [beltway](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road) encircling Providence to the west. [I-195](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_195_(Rhode_Island%E2%80%93Massachusetts)) provides a limited-access highway connection from Providence (and [Connecticut](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut) and New York via I-95) to Cape Cod. Initially built as the easternmost link in the (now cancelled) extension of [I-84](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_84_(east)) from [Hartford, Connecticut](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut), a portion of [U.S. Route 6](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_6_in_Rhode_Island) (US 6) through northern Rhode Island is limited-access and links I-295 with downtown Providence.

Several Rhode Island highways extend the state's limited-access highway network. [Route 4](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_4) is a major north–south freeway linking Providence and [Warwick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick,_Rhode_Island) (via I-95) with suburban and beach communities along [Narragansett Bay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay). [Route 10](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_10) is an urban [connector](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connector_(road)) linking downtown Providence with [Cranston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranston_(RI)) and [Johnston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston,_Rhode_Island). [Route 37](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_37) is an important east–west freeway through Cranston and Warwick and links I-95 with I-295. [Route 99](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_99) links [Woonsocket](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woonsocket,_Rhode_Island) with Providence (via [Route 146](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_146)). Route 146 travels through the [Blackstone Valley](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_Valley), linking Providence and I-95 with [Worcester, Massachusetts](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts) and the [Massachusetts Turnpike](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Turnpike). [Route 403](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Route_403) links Route 4 with [Quonset Point](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quonset_Point).

Several bridges cross [Narragansett Bay](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay) connecting [Aquidneck Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquidneck_Island) and [Conanicut Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conanicut_Island) to the mainland, most notably the [Claiborne Pell Newport Bridge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claiborne_Pell_Newport_Bridge) and the [Jamestown-Verrazano Bridge](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown-Verrazano_Bridge).

### Bicycle paths

The [East Bay Bike Path](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bay_Bike_Path) in [Riverside](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside,_Rhode_Island)

The [East Bay Bike Path](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bay_Bike_Path) stretches from Providence to Bristol along the eastern shore of Narragansett Bay, while the [Blackstone River Bikeway](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_River_Bikeway) will eventually link Providence and Worcester. In 2011, Rhode Island completed work on a marked on-road bicycle path through Pawtucket and Providence, connecting the East Bay Bike Path with the Blackstone River Bikeway, completing a 33.5 miles (54 km) bicycle route through the eastern side of the state. The [William C. O'Neill Bike Path](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_C._O%27Neill_Bike_Path) (commonly known as the South County Bike Path) is an 8 mi (13 km) path through South Kingstown and Narragansett. The 19 mi (31 km) [Washington Secondary Bike Path](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Secondary_Rail_Trail) stretches from Cranston to Coventry, and the 2 mi (3.2 km) Ten Mile River Greenway path runs through East Providence and Pawtucket.

### Future

In late 2019, the [Rhode Island Public Transit Authority](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Public_Transit_Authority) released a draft of the Rhode Island Transit Master Plan, documenting and describing a variety of proposed improvements and additions to be made to the state's public transit network by 2040. Several different proposals were offered and still under consideration as of December 2020, including implementation of a [bus rapid transit](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) system, express bus routes, expansion of Amtrak and MBTA services throughout the state, and construction of a new [light rail](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) network through downtown Providence.

## Culture

### Local accent

Main article: [Eastern New England English § Rhode Island English](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_New_England_English#Rhode_Island_English)

Some Rhode Islanders speak with the distinctive, [non-rhotic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhotic_and_non-rhotic_accents), traditional [Rhode Island accent](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_New_England_English#Rhode_Island_English) linguists describe as a cross between [New York City](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_English) and [Boston accents](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_accent) (e.g., "water" sounds like "watuh" [ˈwɔəɾə]). Many Rhode Islanders distinguish a strong *aw* sound [ɔə] (i.e., resist the [cot–caught merger](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cot%E2%80%93caught_merger) of Boston) much like one might hear in New Jersey or New York City; for example, the word *coffee* is pronounced [ˈkʰɔəfi]. Rhode Islanders sometimes refer to [drinking fountains](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_fountain) as "bubblers", milkshakes as "cabinets", and overstuffed foot-long sandwiches (of whatever kind) as "grinders".

### Food and beverages

Rhode Island, like the rest of New England, has a tradition of [clam chowder](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clam_chowder). Both the white New England and the red Manhattan varieties are popular, but there is also a unique clear-broth chowder known as *Rhode Island Clam Chowder* available in many restaurants. A culinary tradition in Rhode Island is the *[clam cake](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clam_cake)* (also known as a clam fritter outside of Rhode Island), a deep fried ball of buttery dough with chopped bits of clam inside. They are sold by the half-dozen or dozen in most seafood restaurants around the state, and the quintessential summer meal in Rhode Island is chowder and clam cakes.

The [quahog](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_clam) is a large local clam usually used in a chowder. It is also ground and mixed with stuffing or spicy minced sausage, and then baked in its shell to form a *[stuffie](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuffie)*. [Calamari](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calamari) (squid) is sliced into rings and fried as an appetizer in most Italian restaurants, typically served Sicilian-style with sliced banana peppers and marinara sauce on the side. (In 2014, calamari became the official state appetizer.) [Clams Casino](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clams_Casino) originated in Rhode Island, invented by Julius Keller, the [maître d'](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%C3%AEtre_d%27h%C3%B4tel) in the original Casino next to the seaside Towers in Narragansett. Clams Casino resemble the beloved stuffed quahog but are generally made with the smaller littleneck or cherrystone clam and are unique in their use of bacon as a topping.

The official state drink of Rhode Island is *[coffee milk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_milk)*, a beverage created by mixing milk with coffee syrup. This unique syrup was invented in the state and is sold in almost all Rhode Island supermarkets, as well as its bordering states. [Johnnycakes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnnycake) have been a Rhode Island staple since Colonial times, made with corn meal and water then pan-fried much like pancakes.

Submarine sandwiches are called *grinders* throughout Rhode Island, and the Italian grinder, made with cold cuts such as ham, [prosciutto](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosciutto), [capicola](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capicola), [salami](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salami), and [Provolone cheese](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provolone_cheese), is especially popular. [Linguiça](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingui%C3%A7a) or [chouriço](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chouri%C3%A7o) is a spicy Portuguese sausage often served with peppers and eaten with hearty bread.

### In popular culture

[Jacqueline Bouvier](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Bouvier_Kennedy_Onassis) and [John F. Kennedy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) were married at St. Mary's church in Newport. Their reception took place at [Hammersmith Farm](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammersmith_Farm), the Bouvier summer home in Newport.

Cartoonist [Don Bousquet](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bousquet), a state icon, has made a career out of Rhode Island culture, drawing Rhode Island-themed gags in *[The Providence Journal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Providence_Journal)* and *[Yankee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_(magazine))* magazine. ### Notable firsts in Rhode Island

Rhode Island has been the first in a number of initiatives. The [Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Rhode_Island_and_Providence_Plantations) enacted the first law prohibiting slavery in America on May 18, 1652.

The first act of armed rebellion in America against the British Crown was the boarding and burning of the Revenue Schooner *HMS Gaspée* in Narragansett Bay on June 10, 1772. The idea of a [Continental Congress](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress) was first proposed at a town meeting in Providence on May 17, 1774. Rhode Island elected the first delegates ([Stephen Hopkins](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hopkins_(politician)) and [Samuel Ward](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Ward_(Rhode_Island_politician))) to the Continental Congress on June 15, 1774. The Rhode Island General Assembly created the first standing army in the colonies (1,500 men) on April 22, 1775. On June 15, 1775, the first naval engagement took place in the American Revolution between an American sloop commanded by Capt. [Abraham Whipple](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Whipple) and an armed tender of the British Frigate *Rose*. The tender was chased aground and captured. Later in June, the General Assembly created the American Navy when it commissioned the sloops *Katy* and [*Washington*](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Washington_(1776_row_galley)), armed with 24 guns and commanded by Abraham Whipple who was promoted to Commodore. Rhode Island was the first Colony to declare independence from Britain on May 4, 1776.

Slater Mill in Pawtucket was the first commercially successful cotton-spinning mill with a fully mechanized power system in America and was the birthplace of the Industrial Revolution in the US. The oldest Fourth of July parade in the country is still held annually in [Bristol, Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol,_Rhode_Island). The first Baptist church in America was founded in Providence in 1638. [Ann Smith Franklin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Smith_Franklin) of the Newport *Mercury* was the first female newspaper editor in America (August 22, 1762). [Touro Synagogue](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touro_Synagogue) is the oldest synagogue in America, founded in Newport in 1763.

Pelham Street in Newport was the first in America to be illuminated by gaslight in 1806. The first strike in the United States in which women participated occurred in Pawtucket in 1824. [Watch Hill](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watch_Hill,_Rhode_Island) has the nation's oldest flying horses [carousel](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carousel) that has been in continuous operation since 1850. The motion picture machine was patented in Providence on April 23, 1867. The first lunch wagon in America was introduced in Providence in 1872. The first nine-hole golf course in America was completed in Newport in 1890. The first state health laboratory was established in Providence on September 1, 1894. The [Rhode Island State House](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_State_House) was the first building with an all-marble dome to be built in the United States (1895–1901). The first automobile race on a track was held in Cranston on September 7, 1896. The first automobile parade was held in Newport on September 7, 1899, on the grounds of Belcourt Castle.

### Miscellaneous local culture

Rhode Island is nicknamed "The Ocean State", and the nautical nature of Rhode Island's geography pervades its culture. Newport Harbor, in particular, holds many pleasure boats. In the lobby of [T. F. Green](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._F._Green_Airport), the state's main airport, is a large life-sized sailboat, and the state's license plates depict an ocean wave or a sailboat.

Many Rhode Islanders visit [Washington County](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Rhode_Island) for its beaches

The large number of beaches in [Washington County](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Rhode_Island) lures many Rhode Islanders south for summer vacation.

The state constitution protects shore access, including swimming and gathering of seaweed. The 1982 Rhode Island Supreme Court decision in *State v. Ibbison* defines the end of private land as the mean high tide line, which is difficult to determine in day-to-day activities, and has resulted in beach access conflicts. Underfunding of the Rhode Island Coastal Resources Management Council has resulted in lax enforcement against encroachment on public access and building of illegal structures.

The state was notorious for organized crime activity from the 1950s into the 1990s when the [Patriarca crime family](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarca_crime_family) held sway over most of New England from its Providence headquarters.

Rhode Islanders developed a unique style of architecture in the 17th century called the [stone-ender](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone-ender).

Rhode Island is the only state to still celebrate [Victory over Japan Day](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day), which is officially named "Victory Day" but is sometimes referred to as "VJ Day". It is celebrated on the second Monday in August.

Nibbles Woodaway, more commonly referred to as "[the Big Blue Bug](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Blue_Bug)", is a 58-foot-long termite mascot for a Providence extermination business. Since its construction in 1980, it has been featured in several movies and television shows, and has come to be recognized as a cultural landmark by many locals. In more recent times, the Big Blue Bug has been given a mask to remind locals and visitors to mask-up during the [COVID-19 pandemic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Rhode_Island).

### Music

Main article: [Music of Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Rhode_Island)

On September 2, 1977, music icons [the Beach Boys](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beach_Boys) performed a concert at [Narragansett Park](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Park) in [Pawtucket, Rhode Island](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket,_Rhode_Island) attended by 40,000 people, the largest concert audience in Rhode Island history. In 2017, music historians [Al Gomes](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gomes) and Connie Watrous of Big Noise were successful in getting the street where the concert stage stood (510 Narragansett Park Drive in Pawtucket, RI) officially renamed as "Beach Boys Way".

In April 1986, the music charity [Bandwagon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gomes#Bandwagon) was established in Rhode Island. On May 11, 1986, a 150-person celebrity chorus consisting of musicians, journalists (including MTV Networks executive producer Bill Flanagan), TV broadcasters, politicians (including members of the United States Congress), and the heads of human service agencies joined at the recording studio Normandy Sound in Warren, RI to record the charity single "A Piece of Our Hearts". Bandwagon's goal was to raise money for American agencies that aid the hungry and homeless and elevate awareness of the plight of the poor in America. Because of its efforts, the project received national recognition when Bandwagon went on to win the coveted Jefferson Award from the Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Foundation for Outstanding Public Service, joining fellow recipients Oprah Winfrey, [President Jimmy Carter](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Jimmy_Carter), Paul Newman, and [Harry Belafonte](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Belafonte).

### Sports

#### Professional

Two professional minor league teams play in Rhode Island. The [Providence Bruins](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Bruins) ice hockey team—which won the AHL's [Calder Cup](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Cup) during the [1998–99 AHL season](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_AHL_season)—is affiliated with the [Boston Bruins](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins), and play at [Amica Mutual Pavilion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amica_Mutual_Pavilion) in Providence. The [Rhode Island FC](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_FC), an [association football](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) soccer team, began competing in the second tier [USL Championship](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship) in 2024 at [Beirne Stadium](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirne_Stadium) at [Bryant University](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryant_University), and now play at [Centreville Bank Stadium](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centreville_Bank_Stadium) in Pawtucket.

The [Pawtucket Red Sox](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket_Red_Sox) played at [McCoy Stadium](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCoy_Stadium)

The [Pawtucket Red Sox](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket_Red_Sox) baseball team was a [Triple-A](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-A_(baseball)) [International League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_League) affiliate of the nearby [Boston Red Sox](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) from 1973 to 2020. They played at [McCoy Stadium](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCoy_Stadium) in [Pawtucket](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawtucket,_Rhode_Island) and had won four league titles, the [Governors' Cup](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors%27_Cup), in 1973, 1984, 2012, and 2014. McCoy Stadium also has the distinction of being home to the [longest professional baseball game](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_professional_baseball_game) ever played – 33 innings.

The [Providence Reds](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Reds) were a hockey team that played in the [Canadian-American Hockey League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian%E2%80%93American_Hockey_League) (CAHL) from 1926 to 1936, and the [American Hockey League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) (AHL) from 1936 to 1977, the last season of which they played as the Rhode Island Reds. The team won the Calder Cup in 1938, 1940, 1949, and 1956. The Reds played at the Rhode Island Auditorium, on North Main Street in Providence, Rhode Island from 1926 through 1972, when the team affiliated with the New York Rangers and moved into the newly built Providence Civic Center. The team name came from the state bird, a rooster known as the Rhode Island Red. They moved to New York in 1977, then to [Connecticut](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut) in 1997, and are now called the [Hartford Wolf Pack](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Wolf_Pack).

The Reds are the oldest continuously operating minor-league hockey franchise in North America, having fielded a team in one form or another since 1926 in the CAHL. It is also the only AHL franchise to have never missed a season. The AHL returned to Providence in 1992 in the form of the Providence Bruins.

1884 Baseball Champion Providence Grays

Before the great expansion of athletic teams all over the country, Providence and Rhode Island in general played a great role in supporting teams. The [Providence Grays](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Grays) won the first World Championship in baseball history in 1884. The team played their home games at the old Messer Street Field in Providence. The Grays played in the National League from 1878 to 1885. They defeated the New York Metropolitans of the American Association in a best of five-game series at the Polo Grounds in New York. Providence won three straight games to become the first champions in major league baseball history. [Babe Ruth](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_Ruth) played for the minor league Providence Grays of 1914 and hit his only official minor league home run for them before the Grays' parent club, the [Boston Red Stockings](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox), recalled him.

Rhode Island has deep history with the sport of soccer where the sport was played as early as 1886 when the state's first organized league would be founded, known as the Rhode Island Football Association (RIFA). One of their teams, the Pawtucket Free Wanderers, would establish themselves as a regional power and win the [American Cup](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cup) in 1893. The first championship game of the [U.S. Open Cup](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_Cup) was also held in 1914 in Pawtucket's Coates Field to a crowd of 10,000. Later, a team known as Pawtucket Rangers F.C. would win the [1941 edition](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_National_Challenge_Cup) of the U.S. Open Cup (then National Challenge Cup). The [Rhode Island Oceaneers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Oceaneers) would later be founded, and went on to win the [1974 American Soccer League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_American_Soccer_League) championship. Other former semiprofessional soccer teams of the modern era include the [Rhode Island Stingrays](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Stingrays) of the [USL Premier Development League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Premier_Development_League), and the [Rhode Island Reds](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RI_Reds) of the [National Premier Soccer League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Premier_Soccer_League), with both leagues regarded as the fourth tier of American soccer.

The now-defunct professional football team known as the [Providence Steamrollers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Steamrollers_(NFL)) won the 1928 NFL title. They played in a 10,000 person stadium called the Cycledrome. An unrelated basketball team also known as the [Providence Steamrollers](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Steamrollers) played in the [Basketball Association of America](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_Association_of_America), which would become the [National Basketball Association](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association).

Rhode Island's only [rugby league](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) team was the [Rhode Island Rebellion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Rebellion), a semi-professional team that was a founding member of the [USA Rugby League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Rugby_League), which was at the time the top competition in the United States for the sport of rugby league. The Rebellion played their home games at [Classical High School](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_High_School) in Providence.

#### Collegiate and amateur sports

University of Rhode Island's [Meade Stadium](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meade_Stadium) in [Kingston](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Rhode_Island)

There are four [NCAA](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA) Division I schools in Rhode Island. All four schools compete in different conferences. The [Brown University Bears](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) compete in the [Ivy League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League), the [Bryant University Bulldogs](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryant_Bulldogs) compete in the [America East Conference](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_East_Conference), the [Providence College Friars](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Friars) compete in the [Big East Conference](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_East_Conference), and the [University of Rhode Island Rams](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Rams) compete in the [Atlantic 10 Conference](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_10_Conference). Three of the schools' football teams compete in the [Football Championship Subdivision](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Football_Championship_Subdivision), the second-highest level of [college football](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) in the United States. Brown plays FCS football in the [Ivy League](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League), Bryant plays FCS football in the [Big South Conference](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_South_Conference) before that league merges its football operations with those of the [Ohio Valley Conference](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Valley_Conference) in 2023, and Rhode Island plays FCS football in CAA Football, the technically separate football league of the [Colonial Athletic Association](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Athletic_Association). All four Division I schools in the state compete in an intrastate all-sports competition known as the Ocean State Cup, with Bryant winning the most recent cup in 2011–12 academic year.

From 1930 to 1983, [America's Cup](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America%27s_Cup) races were sailed off Newport, and the extreme-sport [X Games](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Games) and [Gravity Games](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_Games) were founded and hosted in the state's capital city.

The [International Tennis Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tennis_Hall_of_Fame) in Newport

The [International Tennis Hall of Fame](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tennis_Hall_of_Fame) is in Newport at the Newport Casino, site of the first U.S. National Championships in 1881. The Hall of Fame and Museum were established in 1954 by [Jimmy Van Alen](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Van_Alen) as "a shrine to the ideals of the game".

Rhode Island is also home to the headquarters of the governing body for youth rugby league in the United States, the American Youth Rugby League Association or AYRLA. The AYRLA has started the first-ever rugby league youth competition in Providence Middle Schools, a program at the RI Training School, in addition to starting the first high school competition in the US in Providence Public High School.

The capital of Rhode Island is Providence. The state's governor is [Daniel McKee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_McKee) (D), and the lieutenant governor is [Sabina Matos](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabina_Matos) (D). [Gina Raimondo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Raimondo) became Rhode Island's first female governor with a plurality of the vote in the November 2014 state elections. Its United States senators are [Jack Reed](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reed_(Rhode_Island_politician)) (D) and [Sheldon Whitehouse](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Whitehouse) (D). Rhode Island's two United States representatives are [Gabe Amo](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabe_Amo) (D-1) and [Seth Magaziner](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Magaziner) (D-2). *See [congressional districts map](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_congressional_districts#Rhode_Island).* Rhode Island is one of a few states that do not have an official governor's residence. *See [List of Rhode Island Governors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Rhode_Island_Governors).*

The state legislature is the [Rhode Island General Assembly](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_General_Assembly), consisting of the 75-member [House of Representatives](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_House_of_Representatives) and the 38-member [Senate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Senate). The Democratic Party dominates both houses of the bicameral body; the Republican Party's presence is minor in the state government, with Republicans holding a handful of seats in both the Senate and House of Representatives.

Rhode Island's population barely crosses the threshold beyond the minimum of three for additional votes in both the federal [House of Representatives](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives) and [Electoral College](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_(United_States)); it is well represented relative to its population, with the [eighth-highest](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population) number of electoral votes and [second-highest](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population) number of House Representatives per resident. Based on its area, Rhode Island has the highest density of electoral votes of any state.

Federally, Rhode Island is a reliably Democratic state during presidential elections, usually supporting the Democratic presidential nominee. The state voted for the Republican presidential candidate until [1908](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_United_States_presidential_election). Since then, it has voted for the Republican nominee for president seven times, and the Democratic nominee 17 times. The last 16 presidential elections in Rhode Island have resulted in the Democratic Party winning the Ocean State's Electoral College votes 12 times. In the [1980 presidential election](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_election), Rhode Island was one of six states to vote against [Republican](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)) [Ronald Reagan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan). Reagan was the last Republican to win any of the state's counties in a Presidential election until Donald Trump won Kent County in 2016. In 1988, [George H. W. Bush](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) won nearly 44% of the state's popular vote, something no Republican did until 2024, when Donald Trump won nearly 42% of the vote.

Rhode Island was the Democrats' leading state in 1960, 1964, 1968, 1988 and 2000, and second-best in 1968, 1972, 1996, and 2004. Rhode Island's most one-sided Presidential election result was in 1964, with over 80% of Rhode Island's votes going for [Lyndon B. Johnson](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson). In 2004, Rhode Island gave [John Kerry](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) more than a 20-percentage-point margin of victory (the third-highest of any state), with 59.4% of its vote. All but three of Rhode Island's 39 cities and towns voted for the Democratic candidate. The exceptions were East Greenwich, West Greenwich, and Scituate. In 2008, Rhode Island gave [Barack Obama](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) a 28-percentage-point margin of victory (the third-highest of any state), with 63% of its vote. All but one of Rhode Island's 39 cities and towns voted for the Democratic candidate (the exception being Scituate).

In a 2020 study, Rhode Island was ranked as the 19th easiest state for citizens to vote in.

## Legislation and taxes

Rhode Island is one of 21 states that have abolished capital punishment; it was second to do so, just after [Michigan](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan), and carried out its last execution in the 1840s. Rhode Island was the second to last state to make prostitution illegal. Until November 2009 Rhode Island law [made prostitution legal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution_in_Rhode_Island) provided it took place indoors. In a 2009 study Rhode Island was listed as the 9th safest state in the country.

In 2011, Rhode Island became the third state in the United States to pass legislation to allow the use of medical marijuana. On May 25, 2022, Rhode Island fully legalized recreational use of marijuana, becoming the nineteenth state to do so. Additionally, the Rhode Island General Assembly passed legislation that allowed civil unions which Governor [Lincoln Chafee](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Chafee) signed into law on July 2, 2011. Rhode Island became the eighth state to fully recognize either same-sex marriage or civil unions. Same-sex marriage became legal on May 2, 2013, and took effect August 1.

Rhode Island has some of the highest taxes in the country, particularly its property taxes, ranking seventh in local and state taxes, and sixth in real estate taxes.

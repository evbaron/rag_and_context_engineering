# Goths

This article is about the Germanic people. For the subculture, see [Goth subculture](/wiki/Goth_subculture). For other uses, see [Goth (disambiguation)](/wiki/Goth_(disambiguation)).

The **Goths** were a [Germanic people](/wiki/Germanic_people) who played a major role in the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire) and the emergence of [medieval Europe](/wiki/Medieval_Europe). They were first mentioned by Greco-Roman authors in the 3rd century AD, living north of the [Danube](/wiki/Danube) in what is now [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine), [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova), and [Romania](/wiki/Romania). From here, they conducted raids into Roman territory, and large numbers of them joined the Roman military. These early Goths lived in the regions where archaeologists find the [Chernyakhov culture](/wiki/Chernyakhov_culture), which flourished throughout this region during the 3rd and 4th centuries.

In the late 4th century, the lands of the Goths in present-day [Ukraine](/wiki/Ukraine) were overwhelmed by a significant westward movement of [Alans](/wiki/Alans) and [Huns](/wiki/Huns) from the east. Large numbers of Goths subsequently began to concentrate near the Roman border at the Lower [Danube](/wiki/Danube), seeking refuge within the Roman Empire. Their entry into the Empire resulted in violence, and Goth-led forces inflicted a devastating defeat upon the Romans at the [Battle of Adrianople](/wiki/Battle_of_Adrianople) in 378. Roman forces regained a level of control but many Goths and other eastern peoples were quickly settled in and near the empire. One of these groups, initially led by their king [Alaric I](/wiki/Alaric_I), [sacked the city of Rome in 410](/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(410)), and were the precursors of the [Visigoths](/wiki/Visigoths). The successors of the Visigoths eventually established a [Visigothic Kingdom](/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom) in Spain at [Toledo](/wiki/Toledo,_Spain). Meanwhile, Goths under Hunnic rule gained their independence in the 5th century, most importantly the [Ostrogoths](/wiki/Ostrogoths). Under their king [Theodoric the Great](/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great), these Goths established the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom) in Italy at [Ravenna](/wiki/Ravenna).

The Ostrogothic Kingdom [was destroyed](/wiki/Gothic_War_(535%E2%80%93554)) by [Byzantium](/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) ([Eastern Roman Empire](/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire)) in the 6th century, while the Visigothic Kingdom was largely [conquered](/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania) by the [Umayyad Caliphate](/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) in the early 8th century, with a [remnant in Asturias](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Asturias) which would go on to initiate the *[Reconquista](/wiki/Reconquista)* under [Pelagius](/wiki/Pelagius_of_Asturias). Remnants of Gothic communities in [Crimea](/wiki/Crimea), known as the [Crimean Goths](/wiki/Crimean_Goths), established a culture that survived for more than a thousand years, although Goths would eventually cease to exist as a distinct people.

[Gothic architecture](/wiki/Gothic_architecture), [Gothic literature](/wiki/Gothic_literature) and the modern-day [Goth subculture](/wiki/Goth_subculture) ultimately derive their names from the ancient Goths, though the Goths themselves did not directly create or influence these art forms.

## Name

Main article: [Name of the Goths](/wiki/Name_of_the_Goths)

In the [Gothic language](/wiki/Gothic_language), the Goths were called the \**[Gut-þiuda](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gut%C3%BEiuda)* ('Gothic people') or \**Gutans* ('Goths'). The [Proto-Germanic](/wiki/Proto-Germanic) form of the Gothic name is reconstructed as \**Gutōz*, but it is proposed that this co-existed with an n-stem variant \**Gutaniz*, attested in *[Gutones](/wiki/Gutones)*, *gutani*, or *gutniskr*. The form \**Gutōz* is etymologically identical to that of the [Gutes](/wiki/Gutes) from Gotland, Sweden, and closely related to that of the [Geats](/wiki/Geats), from mainland Sweden, whose name is reconstructed as \**Gautōz*. Though these names probably all mean the same thing, that meaning is uncertain. They are all thought to be related to the Proto-Germanic verb \**[geuta-](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/geutan%C4%85)*, which means "to pour".

The similarity of these Scandinavian names has long been noted by scholars in connection with the 6th-century book *[Getica](/wiki/Getica)* (c. 551), by the historian [Jordanes](/wiki/Jordanes) who wrote that the Goths originated on [Scandza](/wiki/Scandza) many centuries earlier, and moved to the Vistula delta. However, the accuracy of Jordanes' account for such early Gothic history has been questioned by scholars. A people called the *[Gutones](/wiki/Gutones)* – possibly early Goths – are documented living near the lower [Vistula River](/wiki/Vistula_River) in current [Poland](/wiki/Poland) in the 1st century, where they are associated with the archaeological [Wielbark culture](/wiki/Wielbark_culture).

## Classification

The Goths are classified as a [Germanic people](/wiki/Germanic_people) in modern scholarship. Along with the [Burgundians](/wiki/Burgundians), [Vandals](/wiki/Vandals) and others they belong to the [East Germanic](/wiki/East_Germanic) group. Roman authors of [late antiquity](/wiki/Late_antiquity) did not classify the Goths as *Germani*. In modern scholarship the Goths are sometimes referred to as being *Germani*.

A crucial source on Gothic history is the *[Getica](/wiki/Getica)* of the 6th-century historian [Jordanes](/wiki/Jordanes), who may have been of Gothic descent. Jordanes claims to have based the *Getica* on an earlier lost work by [Cassiodorus](/wiki/Cassiodorus), but also cites material from fifteen other classical sources, including an otherwise unknown writer, [Ablabius](/wiki/Ablabius_(historian)). Many scholars accept that Jordanes' account on Gothic origins is at least partially derived from Gothic tribal tradition and accurate on certain details, and as a result the Goths are often identified as originating from south-central Sweden.

According to Jordanes, the Goths originated on an island called *[Scandza](/wiki/Scandza)* (Scandinavia), from where they emigrated by sea to an area called *[Gothiscandza](/wiki/Gothiscandza)* under their king [Berig](/wiki/Berig). Historians are not in agreement on the authenticity and accuracy of this account. Most scholars agree that Gothic migration from Scandinavia is reflected in the archaeological record, but the evidence is not entirely clear. Rather than a single mass migration of an entire people, scholars open to hypothetical Scandinavian origins envision a process of gradual migration in the 1st centuries BC and AD, which was probably preceded by long-term contacts and perhaps limited to a few elite clans from Scandinavia.

Similarities between the [name of the Goths](/wiki/Name_of_the_Goths), some Swedish [place names](/wiki/Place_name) and the names of the Gutes and Geats have been cited as evidence that the Goths originated in [Gotland](/wiki/Gotland) or [Götaland](/wiki/G%C3%B6taland). The Goths, Geats and Gutes may all have descended from an early community of seafarers active on both sides of the Baltic. Similarities and dissimilarities between the Gothic language and [Scandinavian languages](/wiki/Scandinavian_languages) (particularly [Gutnish](/wiki/Gutnish)) have been cited as evidence both for and against a Scandinavian origin.

Scholars generally locate *Gothiscandza* in the area of the [Wielbark culture](/wiki/Wielbark_culture). This culture emerged in the lower Vistula and along the [Pomeranian](/wiki/Pomerania) coast in the 1st century AD, replacing the preceding [Oksywie culture](/wiki/Oksywie_culture). It is primarily distinguished from the Oksywie by the practice of inhumation, the absence of weapons in graves, and the presence of [stone circles](/wiki/Stone_circle). This area had been intimately connected with Scandinavia since the time of the [Nordic Bronze Age](/wiki/Nordic_Bronze_Age) and the [Lusatian culture](/wiki/Lusatian_culture). Its inhabitants in the Wielbark period are usually thought to have been Germanic peoples, such as the Goths and Rugii. Jordanes writes that the Goths, soon after settling *Gothiscandza*, seized the lands of the [Ulmerugi](/wiki/Ulmerugi) (Rugii).

A [stone circle](/wiki/Stone_circle_(Iron_Age)) in the area of northern [Poland](/wiki/Poland) occupied by the [Wielbark culture](/wiki/Wielbark_culture), which is associated with the Goths

### Early history

Further information: [Gutones](/wiki/Gutones) and [Origin of the Goths](/wiki/Origin_of_the_Goths)

[Oksywie culture](/wiki/Oksywie_culture) and the early [Wielbark culture](/wiki/Wielbark_culture)

Expansion of the Wielbark culture

[Przeworsk culture](/wiki/Przeworsk_culture)

The Goths are generally believed to have been first attested by [Greco-Roman](/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) sources in the 1st century under the name *Gutones*. The equation between Gutones and later Goths is disputed by several historians.

Around 15 AD, [Strabo](/wiki/Strabo) mentions the Butones, [Lugii](/wiki/Lugii), and [Semnones](/wiki/Semnones) as part of a large group of peoples who came under the domination of the [Marcomannic](/wiki/Marcomanni) king [Maroboduus](/wiki/Maroboduus). The "Butones" are generally equated with the Gutones. The Lugii have sometimes been considered the same people as the [Vandals](/wiki/Vandals), with whom they were certainly closely affiliated. The Vandals are associated with the [Przeworsk culture](/wiki/Przeworsk_culture), which was located to the south of the Wielbark culture. Wolfram suggests that the Gutones were clients of the Lugii and Vandals in the 1st century AD.

In 77 AD, [Pliny the Elder](/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) mentions the Gutones as one of the peoples of [Germania](/wiki/Germania). He writes that the Gutones, [Burgundiones](/wiki/Burgundiones), [Varini](/wiki/Varini), and Carini belong to the Vandili. Pliny classifies the Vandili as one of the five principal "German races", along with the coastal [Ingvaeones](/wiki/Ingvaeones), [Istvaeones](/wiki/Istvaeones), [Irminones](/wiki/Irminones), and [Peucini](/wiki/Peucini). In an earlier chapter Pliny writes that the 4th century BC traveler [Pytheas](/wiki/Pytheas) encountered a people called the *Guiones*. Some scholars have equated these *Guiones* with the Gutones, but the authenticity of the Pytheas account is uncertain.

In his work *[Germania](/wiki/Germania_(book))* from around 98 AD, [Tacitus](/wiki/Tacitus) writes that the Gotones (or Gothones) and the neighbouring Rugii and [Lemovii](/wiki/Lemovii) were *Germani* who carried round shields and short swords, and lived near the ocean, beyond the Vandals. He described them as "ruled by kings, a little more strictly than the other German tribes". In another notable work, the *[Annals](/wiki/Annals_(Tacitus))*, Tacitus writes that the Gotones had assisted [Catualda](/wiki/Catualda), a young Marcomannic exile, in overthrowing the rule of Maroboduus. Prior to this, it is probable that both the Gutones and Vandals had been subjects of the Marcomanni.

The [Roman Empire](/wiki/Roman_Empire) under [Hadrian](/wiki/Hadrian), showing the location of the Gothones, then inhabiting the east bank of the [Vistula](/wiki/Vistula) in modern-day Poland

Sometime after settling *Gothiscandza*, Jordanes writes that the Goths defeated the neighbouring Vandals. Wolfram believes the Gutones freed themselves from Vandalic domination at the beginning of the 2nd century AD.

In his *[Geography](/wiki/Geography_(Ptolemy))* from around 150 AD, [Ptolemy](/wiki/Ptolemy) mentions the Gythones (or Gutones) as living east of the Vistula in Sarmatia, between the [Veneti](/wiki/Vistula_Veneti) and the [Fenni](/wiki/Fenni). In an earlier chapter he mentions a people called the Gutae (or Gautae) as living in southern [Scandia](/wiki/Scandia). These Gutae are probably the same as the later [Gauti](/wiki/Geats) mentioned by Procopius. Wolfram suggests that there were close relations between the Gythones and Gutae, and that they might have been of common origin.

### Movement towards the Black Sea

Further information: [Oium](/wiki/Oium)

Beginning in the middle of the 2nd century, the Wielbark culture shifted southeast towards the [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea). During this time the Wielbark culture is believed to have ejected and partially absorbed peoples of the Przeworsk culture. This was part of a wider southward movement of eastern Germanic tribes, which was probably caused by massive population growth. As a result, other tribes were pushed towards the [Roman Empire](/wiki/Roman_Empire), contributing to the beginning of the [Marcomannic Wars](/wiki/Marcomannic_Wars). By 200 AD, Wielbark Goths were probably being recruited into the [Roman army](/wiki/Roman_army).

According to Jordanes, the Goths entered [Oium](/wiki/Oium), part of Scythia, under the king [Filimer](/wiki/Filimer), where they defeated the [Spali](/wiki/Spali). This migration account partly corresponds with the archaeological evidence. The name *Spali* may mean "the giants" in [Slavic](/wiki/Slavic_languages), and the Spali were thus probably not [Slavs](/wiki/Slavs). In the early 3rd century AD, western Scythia was inhabited by the agricultural [Zarubintsy culture](/wiki/Zarubintsy_culture) and the nomadic [Sarmatians](/wiki/Sarmatians). Prior to the Sarmatians, the area had been settled by the [Bastarnae](/wiki/Bastarnae), who are believed to have carried out a migration similar to the Goths in the 3rd century BC. [Peter Heather](/wiki/Peter_Heather) considers the Filimer story to be at least partially derived from Gothic oral tradition. The fact that the expanding Goths appear to have preserved their Gothic language during their migration suggests that their movement involved a fairly large number of people.

By the mid-3rd century AD, the Wielbark culture had contributed to the formation of the [Chernyakhov culture](/wiki/Chernyakhov_culture) in Scythia. This strikingly uniform culture came to stretch from the [Danube](/wiki/Danube) in the west to the [Don](/wiki/Don_(river)) in the east. It is believed to have been dominated by the Goths and other Germanic groups such as the [Heruli](/wiki/Heruli). It nevertheless also included [Iranian](/wiki/Iranian_peoples), [Dacian](/wiki/Dacians), Roman and probably [Slavic](/wiki/Slavs) elements as well.

### 3rd century raids on the Roman Empire

Further information: [Crisis of the Third Century](/wiki/Crisis_of_the_Third_Century), [Battle of Abritus](/wiki/Battle_of_Abritus), and [Battle of Naissus](/wiki/Battle_of_Naissus)

Gothic invasions in the 3rd century

The first incursion into the Roman Empire attributable to Goths involved the sack of [Histria](/wiki/Histria_(Sinoe)) (near the then mouth of the Danube) in 238. The first documentary references to the Goths in the 3rd century call them *Scythians*, as their base, known as Scythia, had historically been occupied by an unrelated people of that name. In the late 3rd century the name *Goths* ([Latin](/wiki/Latin_language): *Gothi*) is first mentioned. Ancient authors do not identify the Goths with the earlier Gutones. [Philologists](/wiki/Philologist) and [linguists](/wiki/Linguist) have no doubt that the names are linked.

On the [Pontic steppe](/wiki/Pontic_steppe) the Goths quickly adopted several nomadic customs from the Sarmatians. They excelled at [horsemanship](/wiki/Horsemanship), [archery](/wiki/Archery) and [falconry](/wiki/Falconry), and also became accomplished [agriculturalists](/wiki/Agriculture) and [seafarers](/wiki/Seamanship). [J. B. Bury](/wiki/J._B._Bury) describes the Gothic period as "the only non-nomadic episode in the history of the steppe". Liebeschuetz sees the Goths of this period as "nomadic or semi-nomadic".
[William H. McNeill](/wiki/William_H._McNeill_(historian)) compares the migration of the Goths to that of the early [Mongols](/wiki/Mongols), who migrated southward from the forests and came to dominate the eastern [Eurasian steppe](/wiki/Eurasian_steppe) around the same time as the Goths predominated in the west. From the 240s at the earliest, Goths were heavily recruited into the [Roman Army](/wiki/Roman_Army) to fight in the [Roman–Persian Wars](/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Persian_Wars), notably participating in the [Battle of Misiche](/wiki/Battle_of_Misiche) in 244. An [inscription at the Ka'ba-ye Zartosht](/wiki/Shapur_I%27s_inscription_at_the_Ka%27ba-ye_Zartosht) in [Parthian](/wiki/Parthian_language), [Persian](/wiki/Middle_Persian) and Greek commemorates the Persian victory over the Romans and the troops drawn from *gwt W g'rmny xštr*, the Gothic and German kingdoms, which is probably a Parthian gloss for the [Danubian (Gothic) *limes*](/wiki/Danubian_Limes) and the [Germanic *limes*](/wiki/Limes_Germanicus).

Meanwhile, Gothic raids on the Roman Empire continued, In 250–51, the Gothic king [Cniva](/wiki/Cniva) [captured the city of Philippopolis](/wiki/Siege_of_Philippopolis_(250)) and inflicted a devastating defeat upon the Romans at the [Battle of Abrittus](/wiki/Battle_of_Abrittus), killing the Roman Emperor [Decius](/wiki/Decius). This was one of the most disastrous defeats in the history of the Roman army.

The first Gothic seaborne raids took place in the 250s. The first two incursions into [Asia Minor](/wiki/Asia_Minor) occurred between 253 and 256; [Zosimus](/wiki/Zosimus_(historian)) attributes them to [Boranoi](/wiki/Borani_(tribe)). This may not be an ethnic term but may just mean "people from the north". It is unknown if Goths were involved in these first raids. [Gregory Thaumaturgus](/wiki/Gregory_Thaumaturgus) attributes a third attack to Goths and Boradoi, and claims that some, "forgetting that they were men of Pontus and Christians", joined the invaders. An unsuccessful attack on [Pityus](/wiki/Pityus) was followed in the second year by another, which sacked Pityus and [Trabzon](/wiki/Trabzon) and ravaged large areas in the [Pontus](/wiki/Pontus_(region)). In the third year, a much larger force devastated large areas of [Bithynia](/wiki/Bithynia) and the [Propontis](/wiki/Propontis), including the cities of [Chalcedon](/wiki/Chalcedon), [Nicomedia](/wiki/Nicomedia), [Nicaea](/wiki/Nicaea), [Apamea Myrlea](/wiki/Apamea_Myrlea), [Cius](/wiki/Cius) and [Bursa](/wiki/Bursa). By the end of the raids, the Goths had seized control over [Crimea](/wiki/Crimea) and the [Bosporus](/wiki/Kerch) and captured several cities on the [Euxine](/wiki/Euxine) coast, including [Olbia](/wiki/Olbia,_Ukraine) and [Tyras](/wiki/Tyras), which enabled them to engage in widespread naval activities.

After a 10-year hiatus, the Goths and the [Heruli](/wiki/Heruli), with a raiding fleet of 500 ships, sacked [Heraclea Pontica](/wiki/Heraclea_Pontica), [Cyzicus](/wiki/Cyzicus) and [Byzantium](/wiki/Byzantium). They were defeated by the [Roman navy](/wiki/Roman_navy) but managed to escape into the [Aegean Sea](/wiki/Aegean_Sea), where they ravaged the islands of [Lemnos](/wiki/Lemnos) and [Scyros](/wiki/Scyros), [broke through Thermopylae](/wiki/Battle_of_Thermopylae_(267)) and sacked several cities of southern Greece ([province of Achaea](/wiki/Achaea_(Roman_province))) including [Athens](/wiki/Athens), [Corinth](/wiki/Ancient_Corinth#Roman_era), [Argos](/wiki/Argos,_Peloponnese), [Olympia](/wiki/Olympia,_Greece) and [Sparta](/wiki/Sparta). Then an Athenian militia, led by the historian [Dexippus](/wiki/Dexippus), pushed the invaders to the north where they were intercepted by the Roman army under [Gallienus](/wiki/Gallienus). He won an important victory near the Nessos ([Nestos](/wiki/Mesta_River)) river, on the boundary between [Macedonia](/wiki/Macedonia_(Roman_province)) and [Thrace](/wiki/Thrace), the Dalmatian cavalry of the Roman army earning a reputation as good fighters. Reported barbarian casualties were 3,000 men. Subsequently, the Heruli leader [Naulobatus](/wiki/Naulobatus) came to terms with the Romans.

After [Gallienus](/wiki/Gallienus) was assassinated outside [Milan](/wiki/Milan) in the summer of 268 in a plot led by high officers in his army, [Claudius](/wiki/Claudius_Gothicus) was proclaimed emperor and headed to Rome to establish his rule. Claudius' immediate concerns were with the [Alamanni](/wiki/Alamanni), who had invaded [Raetia](/wiki/Raetia) and Italy. After he defeated them in the [Battle of Lake Benacus](/wiki/Battle_of_Lake_Benacus), he was finally able to take care of the invasions in the [Balkan](/wiki/Balkan) provinces.

In the meantime, a second and larger sea-borne invasion had started. An enormous coalition consisting of Goths (Greuthungi and Thervingi), Gepids and Peucini, led again by the Heruli, assembled at the mouth of river Tyras (Dniester). The *[Augustan History](/wiki/Augustan_History)* and Zosimus claim a total number of 2,000–6,000 ships and 325,000 men. This is probably a gross exaggeration but remains indicative of the scale of the invasion. After failing to storm some towns on the coasts of the western [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea) and the [Danube](/wiki/Danube) ([Tomi](/wiki/Constan%C5%A3a), [Marcianopolis](/wiki/Marcianopolis)), the invaders attacked [Byzantium](/wiki/Byzantium) and [Chrysopolis](/wiki/%C3%9Csk%C3%BCdar). Part of their fleet was wrecked, either because of the Goth's inexperience in sailing through the violent currents of the [Propontis](/wiki/Propontis) or because they were defeated by the Roman navy. Then they entered the [Aegean Sea](/wiki/Aegean_Sea) and a detachment ravaged the Aegean islands as far as [Crete](/wiki/Crete), [Rhodes](/wiki/Rhodes) and [Cyprus](/wiki/Cyprus). According to the *Augustan History*, the Goths achieved no success on this expedition because they were struck by the [Cyprianic Plague](/wiki/Cyprianic_Plague). The fleet probably also sacked [Troy](/wiki/Troy) and [Ephesus](/wiki/Ephesus), damaging the [Temple of Artemis](/wiki/Temple_of_Artemis), though the temple was repaired and then later torn down by Christians a century later, one of the [Seven Wonders of the Ancient World](/wiki/Seven_Wonders_of_the_Ancient_World). While their main force had constructed siege works and was close to taking the cities of [Thessalonica](/wiki/Thessalonica) and [Cassandreia](/wiki/Cassandreia), it retreated to the Balkan interior at the news that the emperor was advancing.

Europe in AD 300, showing the distribution of the Goths near the [Black Sea](/wiki/Black_Sea)

Learning of the approach of Claudius, the Goths first attempted to directly invade Italy. They were [engaged](/wiki/Battle_of_Naissus) near Naissus by a Roman army led by Claudius advancing from the north. The battle most likely took place in 269, and was fiercely contested. Large numbers on both sides were killed but, at the critical point, the Romans tricked the Goths into an ambush by pretending to retreat. Some 50,000 Goths were allegedly killed or taken captive and their base at [Thessalonika](/wiki/Thessalonika) destroyed. Apparently [Aurelian](/wiki/Aurelian), who was in charge of all Roman cavalry during Claudius' reign, led the decisive attack in the battle. Some survivors were resettled within the empire, while others were incorporated into the Roman army. The battle ensured the survival of the [Roman Empire](/wiki/Roman_Empire) for another two centuries.

In 270, after the death of Claudius, Goths under the leadership of [Cannabaudes](/wiki/Cannabaudes) again launched an invasion of the [Roman Empire](/wiki/Roman_Empire), but were defeated by [Aurelian](/wiki/Aurelian), who, however, did surrender [Dacia](/wiki/Dacia) beyond the [Danube](/wiki/Danube).

Around 275 the Goths launched a last major assault on [Asia Minor](/wiki/Asia_Minor), where piracy by Black Sea Goths was causing great trouble in [Colchis](/wiki/Colchis), Pontus, [Cappadocia](/wiki/Cappadocia), [Galatia](/wiki/Galatia) and even [Cilicia](/wiki/Cilicia). They were defeated sometime in 276 by Emperor [Marcus Claudius Tacitus](/wiki/Marcus_Claudius_Tacitus).

By the late 3rd century, there were at least two groups of Goths, separated by the [Dniester River](/wiki/Dniester_River): the [Thervingi](/wiki/Thervingi) and the [Greuthungi](/wiki/Greuthungi). The [Gepids](/wiki/Gepids), who lived northwest of the Goths, are also attested as this time. Jordanes writes that the Gepids shared common origins with the Goths.

In the late 3rd century, as recorded by Jordanes, the Gepids, under their king [Fastida](/wiki/Fastida), utterly defeated the Burgundians, and then attacked the Goths and their king Ostrogotha. Out of this conflict, Ostrogotha and the Goths emerged victorious. In the last decades of the 3rd century, large numbers of [Carpi](/wiki/Carpi_(people)) are recorded as fleeing from Dacia to the Roman Empire, having probably been driven from the area by Goths.

### Co-existence with the Roman Empire (300–375)

Further information: [Greuthungi](/wiki/Greuthungi), [Thervingi](/wiki/Thervingi), [Oium](/wiki/Oium), [Reidgotland](/wiki/Reidgotland), and [Arheimar](/wiki/Arheimar)

[Ring of Pietroassa](/wiki/Ring_of_Pietroassa), dated AD 250 to AD 400 and found in [Pietroasele](/wiki/Pietroasele), Romania, features a [Gothic language](/wiki/Gothic_language) inscription in the [Elder Futhark](/wiki/Elder_Futhark) [runic alphabet](/wiki/Runic_alphabet).

In 332, [Constantine](/wiki/Constantine_I) helped the Sarmatians to settle on the north banks of the Danube to defend against the Goths' attacks and thereby enforce the Roman border. Around 100,000 Goths were reportedly killed in battle, and [Aoric](/wiki/Aoric), son of the Thervingian king [Ariaric](/wiki/Ariaric), was captured. [Eusebius](/wiki/Eusebius), a historian who wrote in Greek in the early 4th century, wrote that in 334, Constantine evacuated approximately 300,000 [Sarmatians](/wiki/Sarmatians) from the north bank of the Danube after a revolt of the Sarmatians' slaves. From 335 to 336, Constantine, continuing his Danube campaign, defeated many Gothic tribes.

Having been driven from the Danube by the Romans, the Thervingi invaded the territory of the Sarmatians of the [Tisza](/wiki/Tisza). In this conflict, the Thervingi were led by [Vidigoia](/wiki/Vidigoia), "the bravest of the Goths" and were victorious, although Vidigoia was killed. Jordanes states that Aoric was succeeded by [Geberic](/wiki/Geberic), "a man renowned for his valor and noble birth", who waged war on the [Hasdingi](/wiki/Hasdingi) Vandals and their king [Visimar](/wiki/Visimar), forcing them to settle in Pannonia under Roman protection.

Both the Greuthungi and Thervingi became heavily [Romanized](/wiki/Romanization_(cultural)) during the 4th century. This came about through trade with the Romans, as well as through Gothic membership of a military covenant, which was based in Byzantium and involved pledges of military assistance. Reportedly, 40,000 Goths were brought by Constantine to defend [Constantinople](/wiki/Constantinople) in his later reign, and the Palace Guard was thereafter mostly composed of Germanic warriors, as Roman soldiers by this time had largely lost military value. The Goths increasingly became soldiers in the Roman armies in the 4th century leading to a significant [Germanization](/wiki/Germanization) of the Roman Army. Without the recruitment of Germanic warriors in the Roman Army, the Roman Empire would not have survived for as long as it did. Goths who gained prominent positions in the Roman military include [Gainas](/wiki/Gainas), [Tribigild](/wiki/Tribigild), [Fravitta](/wiki/Fravitta) and [Aspar](/wiki/Aspar). [Mardonius](/wiki/Mardonius_(philosopher)), a Gothic eunuch, was the childhood tutor and later adviser of Roman emperor [Julian](/wiki/Julian_(emperor)), on whom he had an immense influence.

The Gothic penchant for wearing [skins](/wiki/Hide_(skin)) became fashionable in Constantinople, a fashion which was loudly denounced by conservatives. The 4th-century Greek bishop [Synesius](/wiki/Synesius) compared the Goths to wolves among sheep, mocked them for wearing skins and questioned their loyalty towards Rome:

> A man in skins leading warriors who wear the [chlamys](/wiki/Chlamys), exchanging his sheepskins for the [toga](/wiki/Toga) to debate with [Roman magistrates](/wiki/Roman_magistrate) and perhaps even sit next to a [Roman consul](/wiki/Roman_consul), while law-abiding men sit behind. Then these same men, once they have gone a little way from the senate house, put on their sheepskins again, and when they have rejoined their fellows they mock the toga, saying that they cannot comfortably draw their swords in it.

*[Athanaric](/wiki/Athanaric) and [Valens](/wiki/Valens) on the Danube*, [Eduard Bendemann](/wiki/Eduard_Bendemann), 1860

In the 4th century, Geberic was succeeded by the Greuthungian king [Ermanaric](/wiki/Ermanaric), who embarked on a large-scale expansion. Jordanes states that Ermanaric conquered a large number of warlike tribes, including the Heruli (who were led by Alaric), the [Aesti](/wiki/Aesti) and the [Vistula Veneti](/wiki/Vistula_Veneti), who, although militarily weak, were very numerous, and put up a strong resistance. Jordanes compares the conquests of Ermanaric to those of [Alexander the Great](/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), and states that he "ruled all the nations of Scythia and Germany by his own prowess alone." Interpreting Jordanes, Herwig Wolfram estimates that Ermanaric dominated a vast area of the Pontic Steppe stretching from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea as far eastwards as the [Ural Mountains](/wiki/Ural_Mountains), encompassing not only the Greuthungi, but also [Baltic Finnic peoples](/wiki/Baltic_Finnic_peoples), Slavs (such as the [Antes](/wiki/Antes_(people))), [Rosomoni](/wiki/Rosomoni) (Roxolani), Alans, [Huns](/wiki/Huns), [Sarmatians](/wiki/Sarmatians) and probably [Aestii](/wiki/Aestii) ([Balts](/wiki/Balts)). According to Wolfram, it is certainly possible that the sphere of influence of the Chernyakhov culture could have extended well beyond its archaeological extent. Chernyakhov archaeological finds have been found far to the north in the [forest steppe](/wiki/Forest_steppe), suggesting Gothic domination of this area. [Peter Heather](/wiki/Peter_Heather) on the other hand, contends that the extent of Ermanaric's power is exaggerated. Ermanaric's possible dominance of the [Volga](/wiki/Volga)-[Don](/wiki/Don_River_(Russia)) trade routes has led historian [Gottfried Schramm](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Schramm_(Historiker)) to consider his realm a forerunner of the [Viking](/wiki/Viking)-founded state of [Kievan Rus'](/wiki/Kievan_Rus%27). In the western part of Gothic territories, dominated by the Thervingi, there were also populations of [Taifali](/wiki/Taifali), Sarmatians and other Iranian peoples, [Dacians](/wiki/Dacians), [Daco-Romans](/wiki/Daco-Romans) and other Romanized populations.

According to [Hervarar saga ok Heiðreks](/wiki/Hervarar_saga_ok_Hei%C3%B0reks) (The Saga of Hervör and Heidrek), a 13th-century [legendary saga](/wiki/Legendary_saga), [Árheimar](/wiki/%C3%81rheimar) was the capital of [Reidgotaland](/wiki/Reidgotaland), the land of the Goths. The saga states that it was located on the Dnieper river. Jordanes refers to the region as Oium.

In the 360s, [Athanaric](/wiki/Athanaric), son of Aoric and leader of the Thervingi, supported the usurper [Procopius](/wiki/Procopius_(usurper)) against the [Eastern Roman Emperor](/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Emperor) [Valens](/wiki/Valens). In retaliation, Valens invaded the territories of Athanaric and [defeated him](/wiki/Battle_of_Noviodunum), but was unable to achieve a decisive victory. Athanaric and Valens thereupon negotiated a peace treaty, favorable to the Thervingi, on a boat in the Danube river, as Athanaric refused to set his feet within the Roman Empire. Soon afterwards, [Fritigern](/wiki/Fritigern), a rival of Athanaric, converted to Arianism, gaining the favor of Valens. Athanaric and Fritigern thereafter fought a civil war in which Athanaric appears to have been victorious. Athanaric thereafter carried out [a crackdown on Christianity](/wiki/Gothic_persecution_of_Christians) in his realm.

### Arrival of the Huns (about 375)

See also: [Migration Period](/wiki/Migration_Period) and [Hlöðskviða](/wiki/Hl%C3%B6%C3%B0skvi%C3%B0a)

*[Gizur](/wiki/Gizur) challenges the Huns* by [Peter Nicolai Arbo](/wiki/Peter_Nicolai_Arbo), 1886

Around 375 the Huns overran the [Alans](/wiki/Alans), an [Iranian](/wiki/Iranian_peoples) people living to the east of the Goths, and then, along with Alans, invaded the territory of the Goths. A source for this period is the Roman historian [Ammianus Marcellinus](/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus), who wrote that Hunnic domination of the Gothic kingdoms in Scythia began in the 370s. It is possible that the Hunnic attack came as a response to the Gothic expansion eastwards.

Upon the suicide of Ermanaric (died 376), the Greuthungi gradually fell under Hunnic domination. [Christopher I. Beckwith](/wiki/Christopher_I._Beckwith) suggests that the Hunnic thrust into Europe and the Roman Empire was an attempt to subdue the independent Goths in the west. The Huns fell upon the Thervingi, and Athanaric sought refuge in the mountains (referred to as [Caucaland](/wiki/Caucaland) in the sagas). [Ambrose](/wiki/Ambrose) makes a passing reference to Athanaric's royal titles before 376 in his *De Spiritu Sancto* (On the Holy Spirit).

Battles between the Goths and the Huns are described in the "[Hlöðskviða](/wiki/Hl%C3%B6%C3%B0skvi%C3%B0a)" (The Battle of the Goths and Huns), a medieval Icelandic saga. The sagas recall that [Gizur](/wiki/Gizur), king of the [Geats](/wiki/Geats), came to the aid of the Goths in an epic conflict with the Huns, although this saga might derive from a later Gothic-Hunnic conflict.

Although the Huns successfully subdued many of the Goths who subsequently joined their ranks, Fritigern approached the [Eastern Roman](/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire) emperor [Valens](/wiki/Valens) in 376 with a portion of his people and asked to be allowed to settle on the south bank of the Danube. Valens permitted this, and even assisted the Goths in their crossing of the river (probably at the fortress of [Durostorum](/wiki/Durostorum)). The Gothic evacuation across the Danube was probably not spontaneous, but rather a carefully planned operation initiated after long debate among leading members of the community. Upon arrival, the Goths were to be disarmed according to their agreement with the Romans, although many of them still managed to keep their arms. The [Moesogoths](/wiki/Moesogoths) settled in Thrace and [Moesia](/wiki/Moesia).

### The Gothic War of 376–382

Main article: [Gothic War (376–382)](/wiki/Gothic_War_(376%E2%80%93382))

Europe in AD 400, showing the distribution of the Goths in the aftermath of the [Hunnic](/wiki/Huns) invasion

Mistreated by corrupt local Roman officials, the Gothic refugees were soon experiencing a famine; some are recorded as having been forced to sell their children to Roman slave traders in return for rotten dog meat. Enraged by this treachery, Fritigern unleashed a widescale rebellion in Thrace, in which he was joined not only by Gothic refugees and slaves, but also by disgruntled Roman workers and peasants, and Gothic deserters from the Roman Army. The ensuing conflict, known as the [Gothic War](/wiki/Gothic_War_(376%E2%80%93382)), lasted for several years. Meanwhile, a group of Greuthungi, led by the chieftains [Alatheus and Saphrax](/wiki/Alatheus_and_Saphrax), who were co-regents with Vithericus, son and heir of the Greuthungi king [Vithimiris](/wiki/Vithimir), crossed the Danube without Roman permission. The Gothic War culminated in the [Battle of Adrianople](/wiki/Battle_of_Adrianople) in 378, in which the Romans were badly defeated and Valens was killed.

Following the decisive Gothic victory at Adrianople, Julius, the [magister militum](/wiki/Magister_militum) of the [Eastern Roman Empire](/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire), organized a wholesale massacre of Goths in [Asia Minor](/wiki/Asia_Minor), [Syria](/wiki/Syria_(Roman_province)) and other parts of the Roman East. Fearing rebellion, Julian lured the Goths into the confines of urban streets from which they could not escape and massacred soldiers and civilians alike. As word spread, the Goths rioted throughout the region, and large numbers were killed. Survivors may have settled in [Phrygia](/wiki/Phrygia).

With the rise of [Theodosius I](/wiki/Theodosius_I) in 379, the Romans launched a renewed offensive to subdue Fritigern and his followers. Around the same time, Athanaric arrived in Constantinople, having fled Caucaland through the scheming of Fritigern. Athanaric received a warm reception by Theodosius, praised the Roman Emperor in return, and was honoured with a magnificent funeral by the emperor following his death shortly after his arrival. In 382, Theodosius decided to enter peace negotiations with the Thervingi, which were concluded on 3 October 382. The Thervingi were subsequently made [foederati](/wiki/Foederati) of the Romans in Thrace and obliged to provide troops to the Roman army.

### Later division and spread of the Goths

In the aftermath of the Hunnic onslaught, two major groups of the Goths would eventually emerge, the [Visigoths](/wiki/Visigoths) and [Ostrogoths](/wiki/Ostrogoths). Visigoths means the "Goths of the west", while Ostrogoths means "Goths of the east". The Visigoths, led by the [Balti dynasty](/wiki/Balti_dynasty), claimed descent from the Thervingi and lived as [foederati](/wiki/Foederati) inside Roman territory, while the Ostrogoths, led by the [Amali dynasty](/wiki/Amali_dynasty), claimed descent from the Greuthungi and were subjects of the Huns. A people closely related to the Goths, the Gepids, were also living under Hunnic domination. A smaller group of Goths were the [Crimean Goths](/wiki/Crimean_Goths), who remained in Crimea and maintained their Gothic identity well into the 18th century.

In his biography of the [West Saxon](/wiki/Wessex) monarch [Alfred the Great](/wiki/Alfred_the_Great), the [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh_people) historian [Asser](/wiki/Asser) states that Alfred's mother [Osburh](/wiki/Osburh) was of partial Goth ancestry through her father Oslac.

#### Visigoths

Main article: [Visigoths](/wiki/Visigoths)

Further information: [Visigothic Kingdom](/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom)

An illustration of [Alaric](/wiki/Alaric_I) entering [Athens](/wiki/Athens) in 395. The depiction, including [Bronze Age](/wiki/Bronze_Age) armour, is anachronistic.

The Visigoths were a new Gothic political unit brought together during the career of their first leader, Alaric I. Following a major settlement of Goths in the Balkans made by Theodosius in 382, Goths received prominent positions in the Roman army. Relations with Roman civilians were sometimes uneasy. In 391, Gothic soldiers, with the blessing of Theodosius I, [massacred](/wiki/Massacre_of_Thessalonica) thousands of Roman spectators at the Hippodrome in [Thessalonica](/wiki/Thessalonica) as vengeance for the lynching of the Gothic general [Butheric](/wiki/Butheric).

Main article: [Revolt of Alaric I](/wiki/Revolt_of_Alaric_I)

The Goths suffered heavy losses while serving Theodosius in the civil war of 394 against [Eugenius](/wiki/Eugenius) and [Arbogast](/wiki/Arbogast_(magister_militum)). In 395, following the death of Theodosius I, Alaric and his Balkan Goths invaded Greece, where they sacked [Piraeus](/wiki/Piraeus) (the port of [Athens](/wiki/Athens)) and destroyed [Corinth](/wiki/Corinth), [Megara](/wiki/Megara), [Argos](/wiki/Argos,_Peloponnese), and [Sparta](/wiki/Sparta). Athens itself was spared by paying a large bribe, and the Eastern emperor [Flavius Arcadius](/wiki/Flavius_Arcadius) subsequently appointed Alaric [magister militum](/wiki/Magister_militum) ("master of the soldiers") in [Illyricum](/wiki/Praetorian_prefecture_of_Illyricum) in 397.

Main article: [Gothic War (401–403)](/wiki/Gothic_War_(401%E2%80%93403))

In 401 and 402, Alaric made two attempts at invading Italy, but was defeated by [Stilicho](/wiki/Stilicho). In 405–406, another Gothic leader, [Radagaisus](/wiki/Radagaisus), also attempted to invade Italy, and was also defeated by Stilicho. In 408, the Western Roman emperor [Flavius Honorius](/wiki/Flavius_Honorius) ordered the execution of Stilicho and his family, then incited the Roman population to massacre tens of thousands of wives and children of Goths serving in the Roman military. Subsequently, around 30,000 Gothic soldiers defected to Alaric. Alaric in turn invaded Italy, seeking to pressure Honorious into granting him permission to settle his people in North Africa. In Italy, Alaric liberated tens of thousands of Gothic slaves, and in 410 he [sacked](/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(410)) the city of Rome. Although the city's riches were plundered, the civilian inhabitants of the city were treated humanely, and only a few buildings were burned. Alaric died soon afterwards, and was buried along with his treasure in an unknown grave under the [Busento](/wiki/Busento) river.

Alaric was succeeded by his brother-in–law [Athaulf](/wiki/Athaulf), husband of Honorius' sister [Galla Placidia](/wiki/Galla_Placidia), who had been seized during Alaric's sack of Rome. Athaulf settled the Visigoths in southern [Gaul](/wiki/Gaul). After failing to gain recognition from the Romans, Athaulf retreated into [Hispania](/wiki/Hispania) in early 415, and was assassinated in [Barcelona](/wiki/Barcelona) shortly afterwards. He was succeeded by [Sigeric](/wiki/Sigeric) and then [Wallia](/wiki/Wallia), who succeeded in having the Visigoths accepted by Honorius as foederati in southern Gaul, with their capital at [Toulouse](/wiki/Toulouse). Wallia subsequently inflicted severe defeats upon the [Silingi](/wiki/Silingi) Vandals and the Alans in Hispania.

Main article: [Gothic War in Spain (416–418)](/wiki/Gothic_War_in_Spain_(416%E2%80%93418))

Wallia was succeeded by [Theodoric I](/wiki/Theodoric_I) who completed the settlement of the Goths in [Aquitania](/wiki/Gallia_Aquitania).
Periodically they marched on [Arles](/wiki/Arles), the seat of the [praetorian prefect](/wiki/Praetorian_prefect) but were always pushed back. In 439 the Visigoths signed a treaty with the Romans which they kept.

Main articles: [Gothic War (436–439)](/wiki/Gothic_War_(436%E2%80%93439)), [Gothic War in Spain (456)](/wiki/Gothic_War_in_Spain_(456)), and [Gothic War (457–458)](/wiki/Gothic_War_(457%E2%80%93458))

The maximum extent of territories ruled by [Theodoric the Great](/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great) in 523

Under [Theodoric II](/wiki/Theodoric_II) the Visigoths allied with the Romans and fought [Attila](/wiki/Attila) to a stalemate in the [Battle of the Catalaunian Fields](/wiki/Battle_of_the_Catalaunian_Fields), although Theodoric was killed in the battle. Under [Euric](/wiki/Euric), the Visigoths established an independent [Visigothic Kingdom](/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom) and succeeded in driving the [Suebi](/wiki/Suebi) out of Hispania proper and back into [Galicia](/wiki/Galicia_(Spain)). Although they controlled Spain, they still formed a tiny minority among a much larger [Hispano-Roman](/wiki/Romanization_of_Hispania) population, approximately 200,000 out of 6,000,000.

In 507, the Visigoths were pushed out of most of Gaul by the [Frankish](/wiki/Franks) king [Clovis I](/wiki/Clovis_I) at the [Battle of Vouillé](/wiki/Battle_of_Vouill%C3%A9). They were able to retain [Narbonensis](/wiki/Narbonensis) and [Provence](/wiki/Provence) after the timely arrival of an Ostrogoth detachment sent by [Theodoric the Great](/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great). The defeat at Vouillé resulted in their penetrating further into Hispania and establishing a new capital at [Toledo](/wiki/Toledo,_Spain).

Under [Liuvigild](/wiki/Liuvigild) in the latter part of the 6th century, the Visigoths succeeded in subduing the Suebi in Galicia and the Byzantines in the south-west, and thus achieved dominance over most of the [Iberian peninsula](/wiki/Iberian_peninsula). Liuvigild also abolished the law that prevented intermarriage between Hispano-Romans and Goths, and he remained an Arian Christian. The conversion of [Reccared I](/wiki/Reccared_I) to [Roman Catholicism](/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) in the late 6th century prompted the assimilation of Goths with the Hispano-Romans.

At the end of the 7th century, the Visigothic Kingdom began to suffer from internal troubles. Their kingdom fell and was progressively [conquered](/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania) by the [Umayyad Caliphate](/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) from 711 after the defeat of their last king [Roderic](/wiki/Roderic) at the [Battle of Guadalete](/wiki/Battle_of_Guadalete). Some Visigothic nobles found refuge in the mountain areas of the [Asturias](/wiki/Asturias), [Pyrenees](/wiki/Pyrenees) and [Cantabria](/wiki/Cantabria). According to Joseph F. O'Callaghan, the remnants of the Hispano-Gothic aristocracy still played an important role in the society of Hispania. At the end of Visigothic rule, the assimilation of Hispano-Romans and Visigoths was occurring at a fast pace. Their nobility had begun to think of themselves as constituting one people, the *gens Gothorum* or the *Hispani*. An unknown number of them fled and took refuge in Asturias or Septimania. In Asturias they supported Pelagius's uprising, and joining with the indigenous leaders, formed a new aristocracy. The population of the mountain region consisted of native [Astures](/wiki/Astures), [Galicians](/wiki/Galicians), [Cantabri](/wiki/Cantabri), [Basques](/wiki/Basques) and other groups unassimilated into Hispano-Gothic society. The Christians began to regain control under the leadership of the nobleman [Pelagius of Asturias](/wiki/Pelagius_of_Asturias), who founded the [Kingdom of Asturias](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Asturias) in 718 and defeated the Muslims at the [Battle of Covadonga](/wiki/Battle_of_Covadonga) in c. 722, in what is taken by historians to be the beginning of the [Reconquista](/wiki/Reconquista). It was from the Asturian kingdom that modern Spain and [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal) evolved.

The Visigoths were never completely [Romanized](/wiki/Romanization); rather, they were 'Hispanicized' as they spread widely over a large territory and population. They progressively adopted a new culture, retaining little of their original culture except for practical military customs, some artistic modalities, family traditions such as heroic songs and folklore, as well as select conventions to include Germanic names still in use in present-day Spain. It is these artifacts of the original Visigothic culture that give ample evidence of its contributing foundation for the present regional culture. Portraying themselves heirs of the Visigoths, the subsequent Christian Spanish monarchs declared their responsibility for the Reconquista of Muslim Spain, which was completed with the [Fall of Granada](/wiki/Fall_of_Granada) in 1492.

#### Ostrogoths

Main article: [Ostrogoths](/wiki/Ostrogoths)

Further information: [Ostrogothic Kingdom](/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom)

The [Mausoleum of Theodoric](/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Theodoric) in [Ravenna](/wiki/Ravenna), Italy. The [frieze](/wiki/Frieze) includes a motif found in Scandinavian metal jewellery.

After the Hunnic invasion, many Goths became subjects of the Huns. A section of these Goths under the leadership of the Amali dynasty came to be known as the [Ostrogoths](/wiki/Ostrogoths). Others sought refuge in the Roman Empire, where many of them were recruited into the Roman army. In the spring of 399, [Tribigild](/wiki/Tribigild), a Gothic leader in charge of troops in [Nakoleia](/wiki/Nakoleia), rose up in rebellion and defeated the first imperial army sent against him, possibly seeking to emulate Alaric's successes in the west. [Gainas](/wiki/Gainas), a Goth who along with Stilicho and [Eutropius](/wiki/Eutropius_(consul_399)) had deposed [Rufinus](/wiki/Rufinus_(consul)) in 395, was sent to suppress Tribigild's rebellion, but instead plotted to use the situation to seize power in the Eastern Roman Empire. This attempt was however thwarted by the pro-Roman Goth [Fravitta](/wiki/Fravitta), and in the aftermath, thousands of Gothic civilians were massacred in Constantinople, many being burned alive in the local Arian church where they had taken shelter. As late as the 6th century Goths were settled as *[foederati](/wiki/Foederati)* in parts of [Asia Minor](/wiki/Asia_Minor). Their descendants, who formed the elite *[Optimatoi](/wiki/Optimatoi)* regiment, still lived there in the early 8th century. While they were largely assimilated, their Gothic origin was still well–known: the chronicler Theophanes the Confessor calls them [Gothograeci](/wiki/Gothograeci).

The Ostrogoths fought together with the Huns at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains in 451. Following the death of Attila and the defeat of the Huns at the [Battle of Nedao](/wiki/Battle_of_Nedao) in 454, the Ostrogoths broke away from Hunnic rule under their king [Valamir](/wiki/Valamir). Mentions of this event were probably preserved in Slavic epic songs. Under his successor, [Theodemir](/wiki/Theodemir_(Ostrogothic_king)), they utterly defeated the Huns at the [Bassianae](/wiki/Battle_of_Bassianae) in 468, and then defeated a coalition of Roman–supported Germanic tribes at the [Battle of Bolia](/wiki/Battle_of_Bolia) in 469, which gained them supremacy in [Pannonia](/wiki/Pannonia).

Theodemir was succeeded by his son [Theodoric](/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great) in 471, who was forced to compete with [Theodoric Strabo](/wiki/Theodoric_Strabo), leader of the [Thracian Goths](/wiki/Thracian_Goths), for the leadership of his people. Fearing the threat posed by Theodoric to Constantinople, the Eastern Roman emperor [Zeno](/wiki/Zeno_(emperor)) ordered Theodoric to invade Italy in 488. By 493, Theodoric had conquered all of Italy from the [Scirian](/wiki/Sciri) [Odoacer](/wiki/Odoacer), whom he killed with his own hands; he subsequently formed the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom). Theodoric settled his entire people in Italy, estimated at 100,000–200,000, mostly in the northern part of the country, and ruled the country very efficiently. The Goths in Italy constituted a small minority of the population in the country. Intermarriage between Goths and Romans were forbidden, and Romans were also forbidden from carrying arms. Nevertheless, the Roman majority was treated fairly.

The Goths were briefly reunited under one crown in the early 6th century under Theodoric, who became regent of the Visigothic kingdom following the death of [Alaric II](/wiki/Alaric_II) at the Battle of Vouillé in 507. Shortly after Theodoric's death, the country was invaded by the Eastern Roman Empire in the [Gothic War](/wiki/Gothic_War_(535%E2%80%93554)), which severely devastated and depopulated the Italian peninsula. The Ostrogoths made a brief resurgence under their king [Totila](/wiki/Totila), who was, however, killed at the [Battle of Taginae](/wiki/Battle_of_Taginae) in 552. After the last stand of the Ostrogothic king [Teia](/wiki/Teia) at the [Battle of Mons Lactarius](/wiki/Battle_of_Mons_Lactarius) in 553, Ostrogothic resistance ended, and the remaining Goths in Italy were assimilated by the [Lombards](/wiki/Lombards), another Germanic tribe, who invaded Italy and founded the [Kingdom of the Lombards](/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards) in 567.

#### Crimean Goths

Main article: [Crimean Goths](/wiki/Crimean_Goths)

Ruins of the citadel of [Doros](/wiki/Doros_(Crimea)), capital of the Crimean Goths

Gothic tribes who remained in the lands around the Black Sea, especially in [Crimea](/wiki/Crimea), were known as the [Crimean Goths](/wiki/Crimean_Goths). During the late 5th and early 6th century, the Crimean Goths had to fend off hordes of Huns who were migrating back eastward after losing control of their European empire. In the 5th century, [Theodoric the Great](/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great) tried to recruit Crimean Goths for his campaigns in Italy, but few showed interest in joining him. They affiliated with the [Eastern Orthodox Church](/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church) through the [Metropolitanate of Gothia](/wiki/Metropolitanate_of_Gothia), and were then closely associated with the [Byzantine Empire](/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).

During the Middle Ages, the Crimean Goths were in perpetual conflict with the [Khazars](/wiki/Khazars). [John of Gothia](/wiki/John_of_Gothia), the [metropolitan bishop](/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) of [Doros](/wiki/Doros_(Crimea)), capital of the Crimean Goths, briefly expelled the Khazars from Crimea in the late 8th century, and was subsequently [canonized](/wiki/Canonized) as an [Eastern Orthodox saint](/wiki/List_of_Eastern_Orthodox_saints).

In the 10th century, the lands of the Crimean Goths were once again raided by the Khazars. As a response, the leaders of the Crimean Goths made an alliance with [Sviatoslav I of Kiev](/wiki/Sviatoslav_I_of_Kiev), who subsequently waged war upon and utterly destroyed the [Khazar Khaganate](/wiki/Khazar_Khaganate). In the late Middle Ages the Crimean Goths were part of the [Principality of Theodoro](/wiki/Principality_of_Theodoro), which was conquered by the [Ottoman Empire](/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) in the late 15th century. As late as the 18th century a small number of people in Crimea may still have spoken [Crimean Gothic](/wiki/Crimean_Gothic).

## Language

Main articles: [Gothic language](/wiki/Gothic_language) and [Gothic alphabet](/wiki/Gothic_alphabet)

The Goths were [Germanic-speaking](/wiki/Germanic_languages). The Gothic language is the [Germanic language](/wiki/Germanic_language) with the earliest attestation (the 4th century), and the only [East Germanic language](/wiki/East_Germanic_languages) documented in more than proper names, [short phrases](/wiki/De_conviviis_barbaris) that survived in historical accounts, and loan-words in other languages, making it a language of great interest in [comparative linguistics](/wiki/Comparative_linguistics). Gothic is known primarily from the [Codex Argenteus](/wiki/Codex_Argenteus), now preserved in [Uppsala](/wiki/Uppsala), Sweden, which contains a partial translation of the Bible credited to [Ulfilas](/wiki/Ulfilas).

The language was in decline by the mid-500s, due to the military victory of the Franks, the elimination of the Goths in Italy, and geographic isolation. In Spain, the language lost its last and probably already declining function as a church language when the Visigoths converted to Catholicism in 589; it survived as a domestic language in the Iberian peninsula (modern Spain and [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal)) as late as the 8th century.

[Frankish](/wiki/Franks) author [Walafrid Strabo](/wiki/Walafrid_Strabo) wrote that Gothic was still spoken in the lower [Danube](/wiki/Danube) area, in what is now Bulgaria, in the early 9th century, and a related dialect known as [Crimean Gothic](/wiki/Crimean_Gothic) was spoken in the Crimea until the 16th century, according to references in the writings of travelers. Most modern scholars believe that Crimean Gothic did not derive from the dialect that was the basis for Ulfilas' translation of the Bible.

## Culture

### Art

#### Early

See also: [Migration Period art](/wiki/Migration_Period_art), [Pietroasele Treasure](/wiki/Pietroasele_Treasure), and [Ring of Pietroassa](/wiki/Ring_of_Pietroassa)

An Ostrogothic eagle-shaped [fibula](/wiki/Fibula_(brooch)), AD 500, Germanisches Nationalmuseum Nuremberg

Before the invasion of the Huns, the Gothic Chernyakhov culture produced jewelry, vessels, and decorative objects in a style much influenced by Greek and Roman craftsmen. They developed a [polychrome](/wiki/Polychrome) style of gold work, using wrought cells or setting to encrust [gemstones](/wiki/Gemstone) into their gold objects.

#### Ostrogoths

The eagle-shaped [fibula](/wiki/Fibula_(brooch)), part of the [Domagnano Treasure](/wiki/Domagnano_Treasure), was used to join clothes c. AD 500; the piece on display in the [Germanisches Nationalmuseum](/wiki/Germanisches_Nationalmuseum) in Nuremberg is well-known.

#### Visigoths

Main article: [Visigothic art and architecture](/wiki/Visigothic_art_and_architecture)

Detail of the [votive crown](/wiki/Votive_crown) of Recceswinth, hanging in Madrid. The hanging letters spell [R]ECCESVINTHVS REX OFFERET [King R. offers this].

Visigothic – Pair of eagle fibulae found at Tierra de Barros (Badajoz, southwest Spain) made of sheet gold with amethysts and coloured glass

In Spain an important collection of Visigothic metalwork was found in the [treasure of Guarrazar](/wiki/Treasure_of_Guarrazar), [Guadamur](/wiki/Guadamur), [Province of Toledo](/wiki/Province_of_Toledo), Castile-La Mancha, an [archeological](/wiki/Archaeology) find composed of twenty-six [votive crowns](/wiki/Votive_crown) and gold [crosses](/wiki/Cross) from the royal workshop in Toledo, with Byzantine influence. The treasure represents the high point of Visigothic goldsmithery, according to [Guerra, Galligaro & Perea (2007)](#CITEREFGuerraGalligaroPerea2007). The two most important votive crowns are those of [Recceswinth](/wiki/Recceswinth) and of [Suintila](/wiki/Suintila), displayed in the National Archaeological Museum of Madrid; both are made of gold, encrusted with sapphires, pearls, and other precious stones. Suintila's crown was stolen in 1921 and never recovered. There are several other small crowns and many votive crosses in the treasure.

These findings, along with others from some neighbouring sites and with the archaeological excavation of the Spanish Ministry of Public Works and the Royal Spanish Academy of History (April 1859), formed a group consisting of:

- [National Archaeological Museum of Spain](/wiki/National_Archaeological_Museum_of_Spain): six crowns, five crosses, a pendant and remnants of foil and channels (almost all of gold).
- [Royal Palace of Madrid](/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Madrid): a crown and a gold cross and a stone engraved with the Annunciation. A crown, and other fragments of a tiller with a crystal ball were stolen from the Royal Palace of Madrid in 1921 and its whereabouts are still unknown.
- [National Museum of the Middle Ages](/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_de_Cluny), Paris: three crowns, two crosses, links and gold pendants.

The aquiliform (eagle-shaped) [fibulae](/wiki/Fibula_(brooch)) that have been discovered in [necropolises](/wiki/Necropolis) such as [Duraton](/wiki/Durat%C3%B3n,_Segovia), [Madrona](/wiki/Madrona_(Segovia)) or Castiltierra (cities of [Segovia](/wiki/Segovia)), are an unmistakable indication of the Visigothic presence in Spain. These fibulae were used individually or in pairs, as clasps or pins in gold, bronze and glass to join clothes, showing the work of the goldsmiths of Visigothic Hispania.

The Visigothic belt buckles, a symbol of rank and status characteristic of Visigothic women's clothing, are also notable as works of goldsmithery. Some pieces contain exceptional [Byzantine-style](/wiki/Byzantine_art) [lapis lazuli](/wiki/Lapis_lazuli) inlays and are generally rectangular in shape, with copper alloy, garnets and glass.

### Society

Further information: [Palace of Omurtag](/wiki/Palace_of_Omurtag)

Archaeological evidence in Visigothic cemeteries shows that social stratification was analogous to that of the village of [Sabbas the Goth](/wiki/Sabbas_the_Goth). The majority of villagers were common [peasants](/wiki/Peasant). Paupers were buried with funeral rites, unlike slaves. In a village of 50 to 100 people, there were four or five elite couples. In Eastern Europe, houses include sunken-floored dwellings, surface dwellings, and stall-houses. The largest known settlement is the [Criuleni District](/wiki/Criuleni_District). Chernyakhov cemeteries feature both [cremation](/wiki/Cremation) and [inhumation](/wiki/Inhumation) burials; among the latter the head aligned to the north. Some graves were left empty. Grave goods often include pottery, bone combs, and iron tools, but hardly ever weapons.

Peter Heather suggests that the freemen constituted the core of Gothic society. These were ranked below the nobility, but above the [freedmen](/wiki/Freedmen) and slaves. It is estimated that around a quarter to a fifth of weapon-bearing Gothic males of the [Ostrogothic Kingdom](/wiki/Ostrogothic_Kingdom) were freemen.

### Religion

Further information: [Gothic paganism](/wiki/Gothic_paganism), [Gothic persecution of Christians](/wiki/Gothic_persecution_of_Christians), and [Gothic Christianity](/wiki/Gothic_Christianity)

*Ulfilas explains the gospel to the Goths*, 1900

Initially practising [Gothic paganism](/wiki/Gothic_paganism), the Goths were gradually converted to [Arianism](/wiki/Arianism) in the course of the 4th century. According to [Basil of Caesarea](/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea), a prisoner named Eutychus taken captive in a raid on Cappadocia in 260 preached the gospel to the Goths and was martyred. It was only in the 4th century, as a result of missionary activity by the Gothic bishop [Ulfilas](/wiki/Ulfilas), whose grandparents were Cappadocians taken captive in the raids of the 250s, that the Goths were gradually converted. Ulfilas devised a [Gothic alphabet](/wiki/Gothic_alphabet) and translated the [Gothic Bible](/wiki/Gothic_Bible).

During the 370s, Goths converting to Christianity were subject to [persecution](/wiki/Gothic_persecution_of_Christians) by the Thervingian king Athanaric, who was a pagan.

The Visigothic Kingdom in Hispania converted to Catholicism in the late 6th century.

The Ostrogoths (and their remnants, the Crimean Goths) were closely connected to the [Patriarchate of Constantinople](/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Constantinople) from the 5th century, and became fully incorporated under the [Metropolitanate of Gothia](/wiki/Metropolitanate_of_Gothia) from the 9th century.

### Warfare

Main article: [Gothic and Vandal warfare](/wiki/Gothic_and_Vandal_warfare)

Further information: [Gothic Wars](/wiki/Gothic_Wars), [Upper Trajan's Wall](/wiki/Upper_Trajan%27s_Wall), [Athanaric's Wall](/wiki/Athanaric%27s_Wall), and [Montes Serrorum](/wiki/Montes_Serrorum)

Germanic spearheads

Gothic arms and armour usually consisted of wooden shield, spear and often swords. 'Rank and file' troops did not wear much protection, while warriors of higher social class were better equipped, as was common for most tribal peoples of the time.

Armour was either a chainmail shirt or lamellar cuirass. Lamellar was popular among horsemen. Shields were either round or oval with a central boss grip. They were decorated with tribe or clan symbols, such as animal drawings. Helmets were often of spangenhelm type, often with cheek and neck plates. Spears were used both for thrusting and throwing, although specialized javelins were also in use. Swords were one handed, double edged and straight, with a very small crossguard and large pommel. It was called the Spatha by the Romans, and it is believed to have first been used by the Celts. Short wooden bows were also used, as well as occasional throwing axes.
Missile weapons were mainly short throwing-axes such as Fransica and short wooden bows. Specialized javelins such as [angon](/wiki/Angon) were more rare but still used.

### Economy

Archaeology shows that the Visigoths, unlike the Ostrogoths, were predominantly farmers. They sowed wheat, barley, rye, and flax. They also raised pigs, poultry, and goats. Horses and donkeys were raised as working animals and fed with hay. Sheep were raised for their wool, which they fashioned into clothing. Archaeology indicates they were skilled potters and blacksmiths. When peace treaties were negotiated with the Romans, the Goths demanded free trade. Imports from Rome included wine and cooking-oil.

Roman writers note that the Goths neither assessed [taxes](/wiki/Taxes) on their own people nor on their subjects. The early 5th-century Christian writer [Salvian](/wiki/Salvian) compared the Goths' and related people's favourable treatment of the poor to the miserable state of peasants in [Roman Gaul](/wiki/Roman_Gaul):

> For in the Gothic country the barbarians are so far from tolerating this sort of oppression that not even Romans who live among them have to bear it. Hence all the Romans in that region have but one desire, that they may never have to return to the Roman jurisdiction. It is the unanimous prayer of the Roman people in that district that they may be permitted to continue to lead their present life among the barbarians.

### Architecture

#### Ostrogoths

The [Mausoleum of Theodoric](/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Theodoric) (Italian: *Mausoleo di Teodorico*) is an ancient monument just outside [Ravenna](/wiki/Ravenna), Italy. It was built in 520 AD by [Theodoric the Great](/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great), an Ostrogoth, as his future tomb.

The current structure of the [mausoleum](/wiki/Mausoleum) is divided into two [decagonal](/wiki/Decagon) orders, one above the other; both are made of [Istria](/wiki/Istria) stone. Its roof is a single 230-tonne [Istrian stone](/wiki/Istrian_stone), 10 meters in diameter. Possibly as a reference to the Goths' tradition of an origin in Scandinavia, the architect decorated the [frieze](/wiki/Frieze) with a pattern found in 5th- and 6th-century Scandinavian metal adornments. A niche leads down to a room that was probably a chapel for funeral [liturgies](/wiki/Liturgy); a stair leads to the upper floor. Located in the centre of the floor is a circular [porphyry](/wiki/Porphyry_(geology)) stone grave, in which Theodoric was buried. His remains were removed during [Byzantine](/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) rule, when the mausoleum was turned into a Christian [oratory](/wiki/Oratory_(worship)). In the late 19th century, silting from a nearby rivulet that had partly submerged the mausoleum was drained and excavated.

The [Palace of Theodoric](/wiki/Palace_of_Theodoric), also in Ravenna, has a symmetrical composition with arches and monolithic marble columns, reused from previous Roman buildings. With capitals of different shapes and sizes. The Ostrogoths restored Roman buildings, some of which have come down to us thanks to them.

#### Visigoths

During their governance of Hispania, the Visigoths built several churches of [basilical](/wiki/Basilica) or [cruciform](/wiki/Cruciform#Cruciform_architectural_plan) floor plan that survive, including the churches of [San Pedro de la Nave](/wiki/San_Pedro_de_la_Nave) in El Campillo, [Santa María de Melque](/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_Melque) in [San Martín de Montalbán](/wiki/San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Montalb%C3%A1n), Santa Lucía del Trampal in Alcuéscar, Santa Comba in Bande, and [Santa María de Lara](/wiki/Hermitage_of_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_Lara) in Quintanilla de las Viñas; the [Visigothic](/wiki/Visigoths) [crypt](/wiki/Crypt) (the Crypt of San Antolín) in the [Palencia Cathedral](/wiki/Palencia_Cathedral) is a [Visigothic](/wiki/Visigoths) chapel from the mid 7th century, built during the reign of [Wamba](/wiki/Wamba_(king)) to preserve the remains of the martyr [Saint Antoninus of Pamiers](/wiki/Antoninus_of_Pamiers), a Visigothic-Gallic nobleman brought from Narbonne to Visigothic Hispania in 672 or 673 by Wamba himself. These are the only remains of the Visigothic cathedral of Palencia.

Visigothic crypt of Saint Antoninus, Palencia Cathedral

[Reccopolis](/wiki/Reccopolis) (Spanish: *Recópolis*), located near the tiny modern village of [Zorita de los Canes](/wiki/Zorita_de_los_Canes) in the [province of Guadalajara](/wiki/Guadalajara_(province)), Castile-La Mancha, Spain, is an archaeological site of one of at least four cities founded in [Hispania](/wiki/Hispania) by the [Visigoths](/wiki/Visigoths). It is the only city in Western Europe to have been founded between the fifth and eighth centuries. According to Lauro Olmo Enciso who is a professor of archaeology at the [University of Alcalá](/wiki/University_of_Alcal%C3%A1), the city was ordered to build by the Visigothic king [Leovigild](/wiki/Liuvigild) to honor his son [Reccared](/wiki/Reccared_II) I and to serve as Reccared's seat as co-king in the Visigothic province of [Celtiberia](/wiki/Celtiberia), to the west of [Carpetania](/wiki/Carpetania), where the main capital, Toledo, lay.

## Physical appearance

In ancient sources, the Goths are always described as tall and athletic, with [light skin](/wiki/Light_skin), blonde hair and [blue eyes](/wiki/Blue_eyes). The 4th-century Greek historian [Eunapius](/wiki/Eunapius) described their characteristic powerful musculature in a pejorative way: "Their bodies provoked contempt in all who saw them, for they were far too big and far too heavy for their feet to carry them, and they were pinched in at the waist – just like those insects [Aristotle](/wiki/Aristotle) writes of." [Procopius](/wiki/Procopius) notes that the Vandals and Gepids looked similar to the Goths, and on this basis, he suggested that they were all of common origin. Of the Goths, he wrote that "they all have white bodies and fair hair, and are tall and handsome to look upon."

## Genetics

Stolarek et al. (2023) and Antonio et al. (2022) both sequenced genomes from the [Wielbark culture](/wiki/Wielbark_culture) Goths. Stolarek et al. includes samples from multiple sites all over the territory of the Wielbark culture, in large numbers. The results are in alignment with archaeological and historical evidence, strongly suggesting that the Wielbark culture formed through migration from Southern Scandinavia. A large majority of the Wielbark culture samples are autosomally Scandinavian-like, and carry predominantly Scandinavian Y-DNA haplogroups. The most common Y-DNA haplogroup among the Wielbark individuals was Y-DNA haplogroup I1-M253, characteristic of the Nordic Bronze Age in Southern Scandinavia, in which it was found at a very high frequency and from where it first expanded. Among the Wielbark Goths, substantial subclade diversity is seen among the I1 carriers, suggesting that the male founders of the culture descended from clans from a rather widespread area in Scandinavia.

Assessing the population movement during late Antiquity, a 2023 study on the Roman frontier on the Danube concludes that "Goths were ethnically diverse confederations". A number of samples obtained from Roman sites close to the limes (such as [Viminacium](/wiki/Viminacium)) dated to the 3rd century or later were shown to carry admixture from Central/North European and Pontic-Kazakh Steppe ancestries in addition to 42%–55% local Balkan Iron Age-related ancestry. 7 out of 9 males among these samples belonged to haplogroups associated with these trans-frontier ancestry sources (I1 and R1b-U106: North European; Z93: Iron Age Steppe). Many of these samples suggest that admixture between Central/North European and Pontic-Kazakh Steppe ancestries likely occurred beyond the frontier prior to the movement into the Roman Empire, "perhaps indicative of, e.g., the formation of diverse confederations under Gothic leadership".

## Legacy

In Spain, the Visigothic nobleman [Pelagius of Asturias](/wiki/Pelagius_of_Asturias) who founded the [Kingdom of Asturias](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Asturias) and began the [Reconquista](/wiki/Reconquista) at the [Battle of Covadonga](/wiki/Battle_of_Covadonga), is a national hero regarded as the country's first monarch.

Further information: [Reconquista](/wiki/Reconquista) and [Gothicism](/wiki/Gothicism)

The Goths' relationship with Sweden became an important part of Swedish nationalism, and until the 19th century, before the Gothic origin had been thoroughly researched by archaeologists, Swedish scholars considered Swedes to be the direct descendants of the Goths. Today, scholars identify this as a [cultural movement](/wiki/Cultural_movement) called [Gothicismus](/wiki/Gothicismus), which included an enthusiasm for things [Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse).

In [medieval](/wiki/Spain_in_the_Middle_Ages) and modern Spain, the Visigoths were believed to be the progenitors of the [Spanish nobility](/wiki/Spanish_nobility) (compare [Gobineau](/wiki/Gobineau) for a similar French idea). By the early 7th century, the ethnic distinction between Visigoths and Hispano-Romans had all but disappeared, but recognition of a Gothic origin, e.g. on gravestones, still survived among the nobility. The 7th century Visigothic aristocracy saw itself as bearers of a particular Gothic consciousness and as guardians of old traditions such as Germanic namegiving; probably these traditions were on the whole restricted to the family sphere (Hispano-Roman nobles were doing service for the Visigothic Royal Court in Toulouse already in the 5th century and the two branches of Spanish aristocracy had fully adopted similar customs two centuries later).

Beginning in 1278, when King [Magnus Ladulås](/wiki/Magnus_Ladul%C3%A5s) ascended to the throne, [a reference to Gothic origins](/wiki/King_of_the_Goths) was included in the title of the king of Sweden: "We N.N. by the Grace of God King of the Swedes, the Goths and the Vends". In 1973, with the accession of King [Carl XVI Gustaf](/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf), the title was changed to simply "King of Sweden".

> In all history there is nothing more romantically marvellous than the swift rise of this people to the height of greatness, or than the suddenness and the tragic completeness of their ruin.

— [Henry Bradley](/wiki/Henry_Bradley), The Story of the Goths (1888)

The Spanish and Swedish claims of Gothic origins led to a clash at the [Council of Basel](/wiki/Council_of_Basel) in 1434. Before the assembled [cardinals](/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)) and delegations could engage in theological discussion, they had to decide how to sit during the proceedings. The delegations from the more prominent nations argued that they should sit closest to the Pope, and there were also disputes over who were to have the finest chairs and who were to have their chairs on mats. In some cases, they compromised so that some would have half a chair leg on the rim of a mat. In this conflict, [Nicolaus Ragvaldi](/wiki/Nicolaus_Ragvaldi), bishop of the [Diocese of Växjö](/wiki/Diocese_of_V%C3%A4xj%C3%B6), claimed that the Swedes were the descendants of the great Goths, and that the people of Västergötland (*Westrogothia* in Latin) were the Visigoths and the people of Östergötland (*Ostrogothia* in Latin) were the Ostrogoths. The Spanish delegation retorted that it was only the "lazy" and "unenterprising" Goths who had remained in Sweden, whereas the "heroic" Goths had left Sweden, invaded the Roman empire and settled in Spain.

In Spain, a man acting with arrogance would be said to be "*haciéndose los godos*" ("making himself to act like the Goths"). In Chile, [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina), and the [Canary Islands](/wiki/Canary_Islands), *godo* was an [ethnic slur](/wiki/Ethnic_slur) used against European Spaniards, who in the early colonial period often felt superior to the people born locally (*[criollos](/wiki/Spanish_Criollo_peoples)*). In Colombia, it remains as slang for a person with [conservative](/wiki/Colombian_Conservative_Party) views.

A large amount of literature has been produced on the Goths, with [Henry Bradley](/wiki/Henry_Bradley)'s *The Goths* (1888) being the standard English-language text for many decades. More recently, [Peter Heather](/wiki/Peter_Heather) has established himself as the leading authority on the Goths in the [English-speaking world](/wiki/English-speaking_world). The leading authority on the Goths in the [German-speaking world](/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_where_German_is_an_official_language) is [Herwig Wolfram](/wiki/Herwig_Wolfram).

**Michael Benjamin Bay** (born February 17, 1965) is an American film director and producer. He is best known for making big-budget [high-concept](/wiki/High-concept) action films with [fast cutting](/wiki/Fast_cutting), stylistic [cinematography](/wiki/Cinematography) and visuals, and extensive use of [special effects](/wiki/Special_effect), including frequent depictions of explosions. The films he has directed include *[Bad Boys](/wiki/Bad_Boys_(1995_film))* (1995) and its sequel *[Bad Boys II](/wiki/Bad_Boys_II)* (2003), *[The Rock](/wiki/The_Rock_(film))* (1996), *[Armageddon](/wiki/Armageddon_(1998_film))* (1998), *[Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Pearl_Harbor_(film))* (2001), the first five films in the *[Transformers](/wiki/Transformers_(film_series))* film series, *[Pain & Gain](/wiki/Pain_%26_Gain)* (2013), *[13 Hours: The Secret Soldiers of Benghazi](/wiki/13_Hours:_The_Secret_Soldiers_of_Benghazi)* (2016), and *[Ambulance](/wiki/Ambulance_(2022_film))* (2022). His films have grossed over US$6.6 billion worldwide, making him the [fifth-most commercially successful director in history](/wiki/List_of_highest-grossing_directors).

He is co-founder of the production house the Institute. He co-owns [Platinum Dunes](/wiki/Platinum_Dunes), a production house which has remade [horror films](/wiki/Horror_film), including *[The Texas Chainsaw Massacre](/wiki/The_Texas_Chainsaw_Massacre_(2003_film))* (2003), *[The Amityville Horror](/wiki/The_Amityville_Horror_(2005_film))* (2005), *[The Hitcher](/wiki/The_Hitcher_(2007_film))* (2007), *[Friday the 13th](/wiki/Friday_the_13th_(2009_film))* (2009) and *[A Nightmare on Elm Street](/wiki/A_Nightmare_on_Elm_Street_(2010_film))* (2010).

## Early life and education

Bay was born in Los Angeles. He was raised by his adoptive parents Harriet, a bookstore owner and [child psychiatrist](/wiki/Child_psychiatrist), and Jim, a [Certified Public Accountant](/wiki/Certified_Public_Accountant). He was raised [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish). His grandfather was from [Russia](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia). His cousin, [Susan Bay](/wiki/Susan_Bay), is the widow of *[Star Trek](/wiki/Star_Trek)* actor [Leonard Nimoy](/wiki/Leonard_Nimoy) (whom he eventually cast as the voice actor for Sentinel Prime in *[Transformers: Dark of the Moon](/wiki/Transformers:_Dark_of_the_Moon)*). He attended the exclusive [Crossroads School](/wiki/Crossroads_School_(Santa_Monica)) in [Santa Monica, California](/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California).

Bay often traces his interest in action films back to an incident during his childhood. As a boy, he attached some firecrackers to a toy train and filmed the ensuing fiery disaster with his mother's [8 millimeter](/wiki/8_mm_film) camera. The fire department was called and he was grounded.

Bay got his start in the film industry interning with [George Lucas](/wiki/George_Lucas) when he was 15, filing the [storyboards](/wiki/Storyboards) for *[Raiders of the Lost Ark](/wiki/Raiders_of_the_Lost_Ark)*, which Bay believed was going to be terrible. His opinion changed after seeing it in the theater and he was so impressed by the experience that he decided to become a film director. He graduated from [Wesleyan University](/wiki/Wesleyan_University) in 1986, majoring in both English and film. He was a member of the [Psi Upsilon](/wiki/Psi_Upsilon) fraternity and a favorite student of film historian [Jeanine Basinger](/wiki/Jeanine_Basinger). For his graduate work, he attended [Art Center College of Design](/wiki/Art_Center_College_of_Design) in [Pasadena](/wiki/Pasadena,_California), where he also studied film. His classmates included future Hollywood film directors [Tarsem Singh](/wiki/Tarsem_Singh) and [Zack Snyder](/wiki/Zack_Snyder). Singh also appeared in one of Bay's student films as a camel salesman.

## Career

### 1992–2005: Breakthrough and stardom

Bay began working at [Propaganda Films](/wiki/Propaganda_Films), directing commercials and music videos, two weeks after finishing his postgraduate degree. His 90-second [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II)–inspired [Coca-Cola](/wiki/Coca-Cola) advertisement was picked up by [Capitol Records](/wiki/Capitol_Records). His first national commercial was for the [Red Cross](/wiki/Red_Cross), which won a [Clio Award](/wiki/Clio_Awards) in 1992. He directed [Goodby, Silverstein & Partners'](/wiki/Goodby,_Silverstein_%26_Partners) "[Aaron Burr](/wiki/Aaron_Burr_(advertisement))" commercial as part of the "Got Milk?" ad campaign for the California Milk Processors Board in 1993, which also won a Grand Prix Clio Award for Commercial of the Year.

Bay's success in music videos gained the attention of producers [Jerry Bruckheimer](/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer) and [Don Simpson](/wiki/Don_Simpson), who selected him to direct his first feature-length film, *[Bad Boys](/wiki/Bad_Boys_(1995_film))*. It was shot in [Miami](/wiki/Miami) in 1994 and starred [Will Smith](/wiki/Will_Smith) and [Martin Lawrence](/wiki/Martin_Lawrence). The action film was a breakout role for Smith, who was making a transition from television at the time. Shooting in Miami was a good experience for Bay, who would later own a home in the city and spend a great deal of time there. The film was completed for $19 million and grossed a remarkable $141 million in the summer of 1995. Bay's success led to a strong partnership and friendship with [Jerry Bruckheimer](/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer).

His follow-up film, *[The Rock](/wiki/The_Rock_(film))* (1996), an action movie set on [Alcatraz Island](/wiki/Alcatraz_Island) and in the [San Francisco Bay area](/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_area), starred [Sean Connery](/wiki/Sean_Connery), [Nicolas Cage](/wiki/Nicolas_Cage) and [Ed Harris](/wiki/Ed_Harris). It was produced by [Jerry Bruckheimer](/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer) and the late [Don Simpson](/wiki/Don_Simpson), who died five months before its release. The film is dedicated to him. Connery and Cage won "Best On-Screen Duo" at the [MTV Movie Awards](/wiki/MTV_Movie_Awards) in 1997, and the film was nominated for an Academy Award in the Best Achievement in Sound category for the work of Greg P. Russell, [Kevin O'Connell](/wiki/Kevin_O%27Connell_(sound_re-recording_mixer)) and Keith A. Wester. After the success of *The Rock*, Bay established his production company Bay Films, with a two-picture deal with Disney.

Bay (left) and [Jerry Bruckheimer](/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer) during the filming of 1998's *[Armageddon](/wiki/Armageddon_(1998_film))*

In 1998, Bay again collaborated with Jerry Bruckheimer, this time as a co-producer, as well as directing the action-adventure film *[Armageddon](/wiki/Armageddon_(1998_film))*. The film, about a group of tough oil drillers who are sent by [NASA](/wiki/NASA) to deflect an [asteroid](/wiki/Asteroid) from a collision course with [Earth](/wiki/Earth), starred [Bruce Willis](/wiki/Bruce_Willis), [Billy Bob Thornton](/wiki/Billy_Bob_Thornton), [Ben Affleck](/wiki/Ben_Affleck) and [Liv Tyler](/wiki/Liv_Tyler). It was nominated for four [Oscars](/wiki/Academy_Awards) at the [71st Academy Awards](/wiki/71st_Academy_Awards), including [Best Sound](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Sound_Mixing), [Best Visual Effects](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Visual_Effects), [Best Sound Editing](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Sound_Editing) and [Best Original Song](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song). It earned $9.6 million on its opening day and $36.5 million through the first weekend. Its budget of $140 million was one of the highest of the summer of 1998. It went on to gross over $553 million worldwide, the highest-grossing film of that year.

In 2001, Bay directed *[Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Pearl_Harbor_(film)),* starring [Ben Affleck](/wiki/Ben_Affleck), [Josh Hartnett](/wiki/Josh_Hartnett), [Kate Beckinsale](/wiki/Kate_Beckinsale) and [Cuba Gooding, Jr.](/wiki/Cuba_Gooding,_Jr.) It was released on [Memorial Day](/wiki/Memorial_Day) weekend in 2001, again produced by Bay with Jerry Bruckheimer. It received four Academy Award nominations, including Best Sound, Best Visual Effects, Best Sound Editing and Best Song. Kevin O'Connell received another nomination for Best Sound, but did not win. *Pearl Harbor* won in the category for Sound Editing, making it Bay's first (and, to date, only) film to win an Oscar. Bay also directed the music video for the nominated track "There You'll Be" by vocal artist [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill).

Bay reteamed with Will Smith and Martin Lawrence for *[Bad Boys II](/wiki/Bad_Boys_II)*, a sequel that was Bay's fifth collaboration with [Jerry Bruckheimer](/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer). It grossed $138 million domestically, enough to cover the production budget, and $273 million worldwide, almost twice as much as the first movie. In 2005, Bay directed *[The Island](/wiki/The_Island_(2005_film))*, a science fiction film starring [Ewan McGregor](/wiki/Ewan_McGregor) and [Scarlett Johansson](/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson). It was the first film Bay made without Jerry Bruckheimer as a producer. It cost $126 million to produce and earned $36 million domestically and $127 million overseas, for a total of $163 million. Bay said that he was not comfortable with the domestic marketing campaign, as it confused the audience about the film's true subject.

### 2007–2016

Bay on the set of *[Transformers](/wiki/Transformers_(film))*, New Mexico, May 2006

In 2007, he teamed up with executive producer [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg) to direct *[Transformers](/wiki/Transformers_(film))*, a live action film based on the [*Transformers* franchise](/wiki/Transformers). Released in July 2007, by November of that year it had made over $709 million worldwide.

Bay returned as director and executive producer for *[Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen](/wiki/Transformers:_Revenge_of_the_Fallen)*, which was released on June 24, 2009 and grossed over $832 million worldwide. Although it received mostly negative reviews by critics, including aggressively critical reviews by American film critics such as [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert), [Michael Phillips](/wiki/Michael_Phillips_(critic)) and [David Denby](/wiki/David_Denby_(film_critic)) (who referred to Bay as "stunningly, almost viciously, untalented"), the film was well received by its intended audience and one of the highest-grossing of 2009. In 2010, it earned seven [Golden Raspberry Award](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award) nominations, and won three: [Worst Picture](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Picture), [Worst Director](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Director) and [Worst Screenplay](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Screenplay). It was also one of the best-selling DVD and Blu-ray Discs of 2009, second only to *[Twilight](/wiki/Twilight_(2008_film))* in DVD format, and the #1 of all time in [Blu-ray](/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc) format until it was surpassed by Blu-ray sales of [James Cameron](/wiki/James_Cameron)'s *[Avatar](/wiki/Avatar_(2009_film))* in April 2010.

Bay directed *[Transformers: Dark of the Moon](/wiki/Transformers:_Dark_of_the_Moon)*, released on June 29, 2011, which grossed $1.123 billion globally. His next film was a comparatively small one he had been developing for years, called *[Pain & Gain](/wiki/Pain_%26_Gain)*. The [true crime](/wiki/True_crime) story, based on events described in a *[Miami New Times](/wiki/Miami_New_Times)* article by Pete Collins, concerns a group of bumbling bodybuilders working together to commit a robbery. It starred [Mark Wahlberg](/wiki/Mark_Wahlberg), [Dwayne Johnson](/wiki/Dwayne_Johnson), [Anthony Mackie](/wiki/Anthony_Mackie), [Tony Shalhoub](/wiki/Tony_Shalhoub) and [Ed Harris](/wiki/Ed_Harris).

Bay filming *[Transformers: Age of Extinction](/wiki/Transformers:_Age_of_Extinction)*; actresses [Abigail Klein](/wiki/Abigail_Klein), Melanie Specht and [Victoria Summer](/wiki/Victoria_Summer) are walking in a corridor.

Bay produced [DreamWorks](/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures)' *[I Am Number Four](/wiki/I_Am_Number_Four_(film))*, based on a series of novels by [Pittacus Lore](/wiki/Pittacus_Lore) published by [HarperCollins](/wiki/HarperCollins) Children's Books. [D. J. Caruso](/wiki/D._J._Caruso) (*[Eagle Eye](/wiki/Eagle_Eye)*, *[Disturbia](/wiki/Disturbia_(film))*) directed.

A fourth Bay-directed Transformers movie, *[Transformers: Age of Extinction](/wiki/Transformers:_Age_of_Extinction)*, was released in June 2014. Starring [Mark Wahlberg](/wiki/Mark_Wahlberg), it earned $1.1 billion at the global box office. On January 12, 2016, [Paramount Pictures](/wiki/Paramount_Pictures) released *[13 Hours](/wiki/13_Hours:_The_Secret_Soldiers_of_Benghazi)*, which Bay produced and directed, based on the [2012 Benghazi attack](/wiki/2012_Benghazi_attack). While the lowest-grossing film of Bay's career at the box office, it went on to massive DVD sales on its digital release in May 2016, earning over $40 million in home video revenue.

### 2017–present

On May 23, 2017, Bay was honored with his own hand-and-footprint ceremony at The [TCL Chinese Theatre](/wiki/Grauman%27s_Chinese_Theatre). His English mastiff, Rebel, put her paw in the cement with him.

Bay's fifth *Transformers* film as director, *[Transformers: The Last Knight](/wiki/Transformers:_The_Last_Knight)*, was released on June 21, 2017. It grossed $68.5 million in its five-day North American opening weekend, the franchise's lowest opening, and $605 million worldwide. In a 2016 *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* interview, Bay said it would be his final *Transformers* film as director.

In 2018, it was announced that Bay would direct the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) action thriller film *[6 Underground](/wiki/6_Underground_(film))*, starring [Ryan Reynolds](/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds), [Mélanie Laurent](/wiki/M%C3%A9lanie_Laurent), [Manuel Garcia-Rulfo](/wiki/Manuel_Garcia-Rulfo), [Adria Arjona](/wiki/Adria_Arjona), [Corey Hawkins](/wiki/Corey_Hawkins), [Ben Hardy](/wiki/Ben_Hardy_(actor)) and [Dave Franco](/wiki/Dave_Franco). It was released on December 13, 2019. Bay was set to begin production on the action film *Black Five* in 2020, but the project was put on hold because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), and Bay subsequently moved on to direct *[Ambulance](/wiki/Ambulance_(2022_film))*.

Bay produced the pandemic-themed thriller *[Songbird](/wiki/Songbird_(2020_film))* starting [Demi Moore](/wiki/Demi_Moore), [Craig Robinson](/wiki/Craig_Robinson_(actor)), [Paul Walter Hauser](/wiki/Paul_Walter_Hauser) and [Peter Stormare](/wiki/Peter_Stormare).

In 2021, it was reported that Bay requested financial compensation from film studio [Paramount Pictures](/wiki/Paramount_Pictures) for indirectly limiting his income as a result of Paramount cutting the theatrical-only run of *[A Quiet Place Part II](/wiki/A_Quiet_Place_Part_II)* from 90 to 45 days due to the effects of the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). Bay, and other producers as well as actors for the film, as is typically the case, receive payment in part based on box-office performance, and a reduction in the theatrical run's exclusivity affected the pay they received.

Bay's next film was 2022's *[Ambulance](/wiki/Ambulance_(2022_film))*, starring [Jake Gyllenhaal](/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal), [Yahya Abdul-Mateen II](/wiki/Yahya_Abdul-Mateen_II), and [Eiza González](/wiki/Eiza_Gonz%C3%A1lez). It follows two bank robbers who hijack an ambulance and take two hostages. Shot during the COVID-19 pandemic in Los Angeles, it was released in the US on April 8, 2022, by Universal Pictures.

On September 2, 2024, Bay's first [documentary series](/wiki/Documentary_series), *Born Evil: The Serial Killer and the Savior*, premiered on [Investigation Discovery](/wiki/Investigation_Discovery). Bay directed and executive produced the five-episode series about serial killer [Hadden Clark](/wiki/Hadden_Clark).

On July 24, 2024, it was reported that Bay was developing a franchise based on Alexey Gerasimov's *[Skibidi Toilet](/wiki/Skibidi_Toilet)* web series. Bay later denied any involvement in such a project.

On April 21, 2025, it was reported that Bay is working with [Universal](/wiki/Universal_Studios) to develop a film based on the 1980s arcade game *[OutRun](/wiki/OutRun)*, with [Sydney Sweeney](/wiki/Sydney_Sweeney) onboard to produce.

On August 7, 2025, it was reported that Bay is working with Paramount to develop a new *Transformers* film.

## Production and effects companies

### Platinum Dunes

Main article: [Platinum Dunes](/wiki/Platinum_Dunes)

Bay founded production house [Platinum Dunes](/wiki/Platinum_Dunes) with fellow producers [Brad Fuller](/wiki/Brad_Fuller_(producer)) and [Andrew Form](/wiki/Andrew_Form) in 2001.

### Digital Domain

Main article: [Digital Domain](/wiki/Digital_Domain)

Bay and Wyndcrest Holdings, a Florida-based investment firm, acquired the [digital effects](/wiki/Visual_effects) company [Digital Domain](/wiki/Digital_Domain) from [James Cameron](/wiki/James_Cameron) and [Stan Winston](/wiki/Stan_Winston) in 2006, infusing the struggling business with a $50 million investment. Digital Domain considered an [initial public offering](/wiki/Initial_public_offering) in 2009 but withdrew the offer due to lack of interest. It was sold to the production group [Beijing Galloping Horse](/wiki/Beijing_Galloping_Horse_Group) in 2012.

### The Institute

After leaving [Propaganda Films](/wiki/Propaganda_Films), Bay and producer Scott Gardenhour, also formerly at Propaganda, formed the Institute for the Development of Enhanced Perceptual Awareness (now known as the Institute), to produce commercials and other projects. Through the Institute, Bay has directed and produced spots for [Victoria's Secret](/wiki/Victoria%27s_Secret), [Lexus](/wiki/Lexus), [Budweiser](/wiki/Budweiser_(Anheuser-Busch)), [Reebok](/wiki/Reebok), [Mercedes-Benz](/wiki/Mercedes-Benz), and [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.). One of his Victoria's Secret ads was for the 2009 "A Thousand Fantasies" holiday campaign.

### 451 Media Group

Bay co-founded 451 Media Group with Doug Nunes (who is CEO), and with John and Anthony Gentile, who previously marketed brands such as [Micronauts](/wiki/Micronauts), *[Visionaries](/wiki/Visionaries:_Knights_of_the_Magical_Light)*, [Sky Dancers](/wiki/Sky_Dancers) and the [Power Glove](/wiki/Power_Glove). In 2015, the company announced an interactive publishing division to offer "[augmented reality](/wiki/Augmented_reality)" content from printed graphic novels with digital video. The graphic novels employ Touchcode technology from T+ink (previously used in the Power Glove), in which ink used in the printing process unlocks access to exclusive content that is housed on the [Machinima Network](/wiki/Machinima_Network), which is transferred to users' touch-screen-enabled mobile devices when the printed books are touched to those devices. The company's premiere slate of graphic novels was unveiled at the October 2015 [New York Comic Con](/wiki/New_York_Comic_Con). The creators involved included [Scott Rosenberg](/wiki/Scott_Rosenberg), [Skip Woods](/wiki/Skip_Woods), [George Pelecanos](/wiki/George_Pelecanos), Mark Mallouk, Clay McLeod Chapman and Peter and Paul Williams.

### Rogue Initiative

In June 2016, Bay joined the Rogue Initiative, a production studio and technology company, as a strategic advisor and stakeholder. The studio merges Hollywood production with interactive talent to generate story-driven content for games, mobile, virtual reality, mixed reality, television and feature film. As part of the partnership, Bay will develop and direct a multiplatform action-adventure game and cinematic VR experiences, based on an original IP conceived by him.

## Personal life

Bay lives in [Miami](/wiki/Miami) with his three [English mastiffs](/wiki/English_mastiff), named for characters in his films. As a boy, he donated his [Bar Mitzvah](/wiki/Bar_Mitzvah) money to an [animal shelter](/wiki/Animal_shelter) and he now often includes his dogs in his films. Bonecrusher appeared as Mikaela's dog "Bones" in *[Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen](/wiki/Transformers:_Revenge_of_the_Fallen)*. Mason, his first English mastiff, was named for John Patrick Mason, played by [Sean Connery](/wiki/Sean_Connery) in *[The Rock](/wiki/The_Rock_(film))*. Mason appeared as Marcus's dog in *[Bad Boys II](/wiki/Bad_Boys_II)* and as Miles' dog in *[Transformers](/wiki/Transformers_(film))*; he died during production of the latter film in March 2007.

Bay is not married and has no children. He previously dated sportscaster [Lisa Dergan](/wiki/Lisa_Dergan).

In a 2016 *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* interview, Bay said that his net worth was around $500 million. He owned a $50 million [Gulfstream G550](/wiki/Gulfstream_G550) jet, as well as a [Bentley](/wiki/Bentley), a [Range Rover](/wiki/Range_Rover), an [Escalade](/wiki/Cadillac_Escalade), a [Ferrari](/wiki/Ferrari), a [Lamborghini](/wiki/Lamborghini), and two [Camaros](/wiki/Camaro) from the [*Transformers* franchise](/wiki/Transformers_(film_series)).

## Critical reception

> His movies look like a more compacted, cartoonish messy version of films by British commercial directors like [Adrian Lyne](/wiki/Adrian_Lyne) and [Ridley](/wiki/Ridley_Scott) and [Tony Scott](/wiki/Tony_Scott). He packs each frame with baroque detail and floods the screen with light and smog; he prefers extreme wide angles or punishingly tight telephoto close-ups, which make each shot dense enough to burst.

— [Matt Zoller Seitz](/wiki/Matt_Zoller_Seitz) writing in *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, 1996.

Bay's work is divisive and has often been poorly received by film critics, and his name has been used [pejoratively](/wiki/Pejorative) in [art-house](/wiki/Art_film) circles. Bay has responded to his critics, saying: "I make movies for teenage boys. Oh, dear, what a crime." Besides accusing him of making films that pander to a young demographic, critics and audiences have been critical of elements of Bay's filmmaking style such as the overuse of [Dutch angles](/wiki/Dutch_angle), extreme patriotism, overly broad and [sophomoric humor](/wiki/Sophomoric_humor), excessive product placement, oversaturated orange and teal [color grading](/wiki/Color_grading), reusing footage from his previous films, and his preference of action and spectacle over story and characters, with his films' excessive use of [explosions](/wiki/Explosion) often being mentioned or parodied. Another point of contention with Bay's films is his portrayal and use of offensive racial stereotypes as comedic relief; one alleged example being the characters Skids and Mudflap in *[Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen](/wiki/Transformers:_Revenge_of_the_Fallen)*.

Bay has also been accused of heavily [objectifying](/wiki/Objectification) women in his films, with critics describing Bay's manner of filming actresses as "lascivious" and "pornographic". He has faced criticism for routinely making sexist remarks and showing female characters in a stereotypical light. He came under scrutiny for firing [Megan Fox](/wiki/Megan_Fox) in [retaliation](/wiki/Organizational_retaliatory_behavior) after she made comments about him mistreating her on the set of the *Transformers* films and compared him to [Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) and [Napoleon](/wiki/Napoleon). Bay published an open letter written by three anonymous members of the crew of *Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen* that referred to Fox as, among other things, "Ms. Sourpants", "porn star", "unfriendly bitch", and "dumb-as-a-rock".

In 2009, it was reported that Fox, at the age of 15, was made to wash Bay's car while auditioning for [*Transformers*](/wiki/Transformers_(film)). In 2020, Fox revisited the incident and denied that she was underaged (for *Transformers*) or "made to 'wash' or work on someone's cars in a way that was extraneous from the materials in the actual script." Fox was 15 when she first appeared as a bikini-clad extra in the Bay film *Bad Boys II*.

Actress [Kate Beckinsale](/wiki/Kate_Beckinsale) also spoke out publicly by reporting that she was body-shamed and regularly criticized for her appearance by Bay during the making of *[Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Pearl_Harbor_(film))*.

Six of Bay's films have been nominated for the [Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Picture](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Picture) and [Golden Raspberry Award for Worst Director](/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Director) (*Armageddon*, *Pearl Harbor*, *Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen*, *Transformers: Dark of the Moon*, *Transformers: Age of Extinction* and *Transformers: The Last Knight*), with *Revenge of the Fallen* and *Age of Extinction* winning the award for "Worst Director". *Revenge of the Fallen* also became the first film by Bay and also the highest-grossing film to be awarded "Worst Picture".

Conversely, some critics and actors have praised Bay's films and style. Film historian [Jeanine Basinger](/wiki/Jeanine_Basinger) has described him as "the most cinematic and fluid and unafraid director", while Scott Foundas of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* has lauded his "grand, epic vision" and positively compared him to [William Wyler](/wiki/William_Wyler). Actress [Scarlett Johansson](/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson) wrote that Bay is "a truly ambitious storyteller who celebrates characters, actors, and leading men and women alike."

# Maria von Trapp

## Infobox

- **Born:** Maria Augusta Kuczera ( 1905-01-26 ) 26 January 1905 Vienna, Austria-Hungary
- **Died:** 28 March 1987 (1987-03-28) (aged 82) Morrisville, Vermont, U.S.
- **Resting place:** Trapp Family Cemetery, Trapp Family Lodge, Stowe, Vermont, U.S.

This article is about the matriarch of the Trapp Family Singers. For her stepdaughter, see [Maria Franziska von Trapp](/wiki/Maria_Franziska_von_Trapp).

**Maria Augusta von Trapp** [DHS](/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre_(Catholic)) ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Kutschera**; 26 January 1905 – 28 March 1987), often styled as "**Baroness**", was the stepmother and matriarch of the [Trapp Family](/wiki/Trapp_Family). She wrote the [memoir](/wiki/Memoir) *[The Story of the Trapp Family Singers](/wiki/The_Story_of_the_Trapp_Family_Singers)*, which was published in 1949 and was the inspiration for the 1956 West German film *[The Trapp Family](/wiki/The_Trapp_Family)*, which in turn inspired the 1959 Broadway musical *[The Sound of Music](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music)* and its [1965 film version](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music_(film)).

## Biography

### Early life

Maria was purportedly born on 26 January 1905 to Karl and Augusta (*née* Rainer) Kuczera. She says she was delivered on a train on the night of the 25th, during her mother's return from her homeland of [Tyrol](/wiki/Tyrol_(state)) to their family residence in [Vienna](/wiki/Vienna), Austria. She was baptized into the [Catholic Church](/wiki/Catholic_Church) on the 29th within the [Alservorstadt](/wiki/Alservorstadt) parish and maternity hospital.

Her father was a [hotel commissionaire](/wiki/Commissionaire), born in Vienna, the son of Josef Kučera from a [Moravian](/wiki/Moravians) village, [Vídeň](/wiki/V%C3%ADde%C5%88_(%C5%BD%C4%8F%C3%A1r_nad_S%C3%A1zavou_District)). Karl was first married in [Graz](/wiki/Graz) to Klara Rainer in 1887. The couple had a son Karl in 1888 before Klara's death a few months later. Maria's father remained a widower until he remarried to Klara's younger sister, Augusta, in 1903. Augusta died of [pulmonary tuberculosis](/wiki/Pulmonary_tuberculosis) when Maria was nearly 10 months old. Maria's grief-stricken father left her with his cousin in [Kagran](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagran), who also fostered Maria's half-brother Karl after his mother Klara had died. Maria's father then traveled the world, although Maria would visit him upon occasion at his apartment in Vienna. He changed the spelling of their surname to *Kutschera* in 1914, dying at home later that year. Maria's foster mother's son-in-law, Uncle Franz, then became her guardian.

Uncle Franz mistreated Maria and punished her for things she did not do; he was later found to be mentally ill. This changed Maria from a shy child into the teenage "class cut-up", figuring she may as well have fun if she were going to get in trouble either way. Despite this change, Maria continued to get good grades.

After graduating from high school at 15, Maria ran away to stay with a friend, with the intent to become a tutor for children staying at nearby hotels. Because she looked so young, no one took her seriously. Finally, a hotel manager asked her to be umpire for a tennis tournament. Although she did not know what an umpire was and had never played tennis, she took the job.

From this job, she saved enough money to enter the State Teachers College for Progressive Education in Vienna, where she also received a scholarship. She graduated at age 18 in 1923.

In 1924, she entered [Nonnberg Abbey](/wiki/Nonnberg_Abbey), a [Benedictine](/wiki/Benedictines) monastery in [Salzburg](/wiki/Salzburg), as a [postulant](/wiki/Postulant), intending to become a nun.

### Marriage

[Georg von Trapp](/wiki/Georg_von_Trapp) on the bridge of submarine [*U-5*](/wiki/SM_U-5_(Austria-Hungary)) of the [Austro-Hungarian Navy](/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Navy) (1915)

While still teaching at the Abbey in 1926, Maria was asked to teach [Maria Franziska von Trapp](/wiki/Maria_Franziska_von_Trapp), one of seven children born to widowed naval commander [Georg von Trapp](/wiki/Georg_von_Trapp). His first wife, the Anglo-Austrian heiress [Agathe Whitehead](/wiki/Agathe_Whitehead), had earlier died in 1922 from [scarlet fever](/wiki/Scarlet_fever). Eventually, Maria began to look after the other children: [Rupert](/wiki/Rupert_von_Trapp), [Agathe](/wiki/Agathe_von_Trapp), [Werner](/wiki/Werner_von_Trapp), [Hedwig](/wiki/Hedwig_von_Trapp), [Johanna](/wiki/Johanna_von_Trapp), and [Martina](/wiki/Martina_von_Trapp).

Captain von Trapp saw how much she cared about his children and asked her to marry him, although he was 25 years her senior. Frightened, she fled back to Nonnberg Abbey to seek guidance from the [mother abbess](/wiki/Abbess), [Virgilia Lütz](/wiki/Virgilia_L%C3%BCtz), who advised her it was God's will that she should marry him. She then returned to the family and accepted his proposal. She wrote in her autobiography that she was very angry on her wedding day, both at God and at her new husband, because she really wanted to be a nun. "I really and truly was not in love. I liked him but didn't love him. However, I loved the children, so in a way I really married the children. I learned to love him more than I have ever loved before or after." They married at [Nonnberg Abbey](/wiki/Nonnberg_Abbey) on 26 November 1927 and had three children together: Rosmarie (1929–2022), Eleonore ("Lorli") (1931–2021) and [Johannes](/wiki/Johannes_von_Trapp) (born 1939).

### Medical problems

The von Trapps enjoyed hiking. On one outing, they stayed overnight at a farmer's house. The next morning, they were informed that Maria and two of Georg's daughters, Johanna and Martina, had [scarlet fever](/wiki/Scarlet_fever). Johanna and Martina recovered, but the older Maria developed [kidney stones](/wiki/Kidney_stone_disease) due to [dehydration](/wiki/Dehydration). Her stepdaughter, Maria Franziska, accompanied her to Vienna for a successful surgery, but Maria experienced lifelong kidney problems.

### Financial problems

The family met with financial ruin in 1935. Georg had transferred his savings from a bank in [London](/wiki/London) to an Austrian bank run by a friend named Auguste Caroline Lammer. Austria was experiencing economic difficulties during a [worldwide depression](/wiki/The_Great_Depression) because of the [Crash of 1929](/wiki/Crash_of_1929), and Lammer's bank failed. To survive, the Trapps dismissed most of their servants, moved into the top floor of their house, and rented out the other rooms. The [Archbishop of Salzburg](/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Salzburg), Sigismund Waitz, sent Father [Franz Wasner](/wiki/Franz_Wasner) to stay with them as their [chaplain](/wiki/Chaplain) and this began their singing career.

### Early musical career and departure from Austria

Soprano [Lotte Lehmann](/wiki/Lotte_Lehmann) heard the family sing, and she suggested they perform at concerts. When the [Austrian Chancellor](/wiki/Chancellor_of_Austria) [Kurt Schuschnigg](/wiki/Kurt_Schuschnigg) heard them over the radio, he invited them to perform in Vienna.

After performing at a festival in 1935, they became a popular touring act. They experienced life under the [Nazis](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) after the [Anschluss](/wiki/Anschluss) (the annexation of Austria by Germany) in March 1938. Life became increasingly difficult as they witnessed hostility toward Jewish children by their classmates, the use of children against their parents, and finally by the extension of an offer for [Georg](/wiki/Georg_von_Trapp) to join the [German Navy](/wiki/Kriegsmarine). In September, the family fled Austria for [Italy](/wiki/Italy) via train, then to [England](/wiki/England) and finally the [United States](/wiki/United_States). The Nazis made use of their abandoned home as [Heinrich Himmler](/wiki/Heinrich_Himmler)'s headquarters.

Initially calling themselves the "Trapp Family Choir", the von Trapps began to perform in the United States and [Canada](/wiki/Canada). They performed in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) at [The Town Hall](/wiki/The_Town_Hall_(New_York_City)) on 10 December 1938. The *[New York Times](/wiki/New_York_Times)* wrote:

> There was something unusually lovable and appealing about the modest, serious singers of this little family aggregation as they formed a close semicircle about their self-effacing director for their initial offering, the handsome Mme. von Trapp in simple black, and the youthful sisters garbed in black and white Austrian folk costumes enlivened with red ribbons. It was only natural to expect work of exceeding refinement from them, and one was not disappointed in this.

Trapp Family Singers preparing for a concert in Boston in 1941. Maria is the third from left, in a dark suit.

[Charles Wagner](/wiki/Charles_Wagner) was their first booking agent; Then they signed on with [Frederick Christian Schang](/wiki/Frederick_Christian_Schang). Thinking the name "Trapp Family Choir" too "churchy", Schang Americanized their repertoire and, following his suggestion, the group changed its name to the "Trapp Family Singers". The family, which by then included all ten children, was soon touring the world giving concert performances. [Alix Williamson](/wiki/Alix_Williamson) served as the group's publicist for over two decades. After the war, they founded the [Trapp Family Austrian Relief](/wiki/Trapp_Family_Austrian_Relief) fund, which sent food and clothing to the impoverished in Austria.

### Move to the United States

Maria von Trapp's certificate of arrival at [Niagara Falls, New York](/wiki/Niagara_Falls,_New_York), on 30 December 1942

In the 1940s, the family moved to [Stowe, Vermont](/wiki/Stowe,_Vermont), where they ran a [music camp](/wiki/Music_camp) when they were not touring. In 1944, Maria Augusta, Maria Franziska, Johanna, Martina, Hedwig and Agathe applied for U.S. citizenship, whereas Georg never applied to become a citizen. At application, the preposition 'von' was dropped from their family name. Rupert and Werner became citizens by serving during World War II, while Rosmarie and Eleonore became citizens by virtue of their mother's citizenship. Johannes was born in the United States in [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia) on 17 January 1939 during a concert tour. Georg von Trapp died in 1947 in Vermont after suffering lung cancer.

The family made a series of 78-rpm records for [RCA Victor](/wiki/RCA_Victor) in the 1950s, some of which were later issued on [RCA Camden](/wiki/RCA_Camden) LPs. There were also a few later recordings released on LPs, including some stereo sessions. In 1957, the Trapp Family Singers disbanded and went their separate ways. Maria and three of her children became missionaries in [Papua New Guinea](/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea). In 1965, Maria moved back to Vermont to manage the [Trapp Family Lodge](/wiki/Trapp_Family_Lodge), which had been named *Cor Unum*. She began turning over management of the lodge to her son Johannes, although she was initially reluctant to do so. Hedwig returned to Austria and worked as a teacher in [Umhausen](/wiki/Umhausen).

## Death

Maria von Trapp died of heart failure on 28 March 1987, aged 82, in [Morrisville, Vermont](/wiki/Morrisville,_Vermont), three days following surgery. She is interred in the family cemetery at the lodge, along with her husband and five of her step-children.

## Decorations and awards

The family has won the following awards:

- 1949 – [Benemerenti Medal](/wiki/Benemerenti_Medal) ([Pope Pius XII](/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII)), in recognition of the benefits of the [Trapp Family Austrian Relief](/wiki/Trapp_Family_Austrian_Relief) for needy Austrians
- 1952 – Dame of the [Order of the Holy Sepulchre](/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre_(Catholic)) (Vatican-Pope Pius XII)
- 1956 – Catholic Mother of the Year in the United States. Women receive this honorary title, to recognise exemplary behavior
- 1957 – [Decoration of Honour in Gold for Services to the Republic of Austria](/wiki/Decoration_of_Honour_in_Gold_for_Services_to_the_Republic_of_Austria)
- 1962 – Siena Medal – an award given by [Theta Phi Alpha](/wiki/Theta_Phi_Alpha) women's fraternity to "an outstanding woman to recognize her for her endurance and great accomplishment." The medal is the highest honor the organization bestows upon a non-member and is named after Saint Catherine of Siena.
- 1967 – [Austrian Cross of Honour for Science and Art, 1st class](/wiki/Austrian_Cross_of_Honour_for_Science_and_Art,_1st_class)
- 2007 – The von Trapp Family received the Egon Ranshofen Wertheimer Prize in [Braunau am Inn](/wiki/Braunau_am_Inn)
- 2012 – Naming of Maria Trapp-Platz in [Donaustadt](/wiki/Donaustadt) (22nd District of Vienna)

## Adaptations of the autobiography

Main article: [The Sound of Music](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music)

Maria von Trapp's book, *[The Story of the Trapp Family Singers](/wiki/The_Story_of_the_Trapp_Family_Singers)*, published in 1949, was a best-seller. It was made into two successful German / Austrian films:

- *[The Trapp Family](/wiki/The_Trapp_Family)* (1956)
- *[The Trapp Family in America](/wiki/The_Trapp_Family_in_America)* (1958)

The book was then adapted into *[The Sound of Music](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music)*, a 1959 [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) musical by [Rodgers and Hammerstein](/wiki/Rodgers_and_Hammerstein), starring [Mary Martin](/wiki/Mary_Martin) and [Theodore Bikel](/wiki/Theodore_Bikel). It was a success, running for more than three years. The musical was adapted in 1965 as a motion picture [of the same name](/wiki/The_Sound_of_Music_(film)), starring [Julie Andrews](/wiki/Julie_Andrews). The film version set US box office records, and Maria von Trapp received about $500,000 ($5.58 million today) in royalties.

Maria von Trapp made a [cameo appearance](/wiki/Cameo_appearance) in the movie version of *The Sound of Music* (1965). For an instant, she, her daughter Rosmarie, and Werner's daughter Barbara can be seen walking past an archway during the song, "I Have Confidence", at the line, "I must stop these doubts, all these worries / If I don't, I just know I'll turn back."

Maria von Trapp sang "[Edelweiss](/wiki/Edelweiss_(song))" with Andrews on *[The Julie Andrews Hour](/wiki/The_Julie_Andrews_Hour)* in 1973. In 1991, a 40 episode [anime](/wiki/Anime) series, titled *[Trapp Family Story](/wiki/Trapp_Family_Story)* aired in Japan, her character referred to by her maiden name (Maria Kutschera), voiced by [Masako Katsuki](/wiki/Masako_Katsuki). She was portrayed in the 2015 film *[The von Trapp Family: A Life of Music](/wiki/The_von_Trapp_Family:_A_Life_of_Music)* by [Yvonne Catterfeld](/wiki/Yvonne_Catterfeld).

[General](/wiki/General) **William Rose Mansfield, 1st Baron Sandhurst**, [GCB](/wiki/Knight_Grand_Cross_of_the_Order_of_the_Bath), [GCSI](/wiki/Knight_Grand_Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_Star_of_India), [PC (Ire)](/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Ireland) (21 June 1819 – 23 June 1876) was a British military commander who served as [Commander-in-Chief](/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_India) of [India](/wiki/British_India) from 1865 to 1870.

In Bombay now Mumbai, there is local train station named after Baron Sandhurst. Sanhurst Road station in Central Line.

## Background and early life

*[Vanity Fair](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(British_magazine))* caricature, 1874

Mansfield was born on 21 June 1819, in [Ruxley](/wiki/Ruxley), the fifth of the seven sons of John Mansfield of Diggeswell House in [Hertfordshire](/wiki/Hertfordshire), and his wife, Mary Buchanan Smith, daughter of General [Samuel Smith](/wiki/Samuel_Smith_(Maryland_politician)) of [Baltimore](/wiki/Baltimore) in the [United States](/wiki/United_States). His grandfather was the prominent lawyer [Sir James Mansfield](/wiki/James_Mansfield), [Solicitor General](/wiki/Solicitor_General_for_England_and_Wales) from 1780 to 1782 and in 1783 and [Chief Justice of the Common Pleas](/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Common_Pleas) from 1804 to 1814.

In 1854, he married [Margaret Fellowes](/wiki/Margaret_Sandhurst), who became a noted suffragist and spiritualist after his death. They had several children, among whom the eldest son, [William](/wiki/William_Mansfield,_1st_Viscount_Sandhurst), was a [Liberal](/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK)) politician who served as [Governor of Bombay](/wiki/Governor_of_Bombay) and [Lord Chamberlain of the Household](/wiki/Lord_Chamberlain_of_the_Household) of [George V](/wiki/George_V), before being [ennobled](/wiki/Ennobled).

## Military career

Mansfield was educated at [Royal Military College, Sandhurst](/wiki/Royal_Military_College_Sandhurst), and was commissioned into the [53rd Foot](/wiki/53rd_Foot) as an ensign in 1835. He was promoted to lieutenant on 31 August 1838 and to captain on 10 February 1843. He was mostly active in [India](/wiki/India) and served in the [Sutlej](/wiki/Sutlej) campaign of 1845 to 1846. Promoted to major on 3 December 1847, he commanded the 53rd Regiment in the [Punjab](/wiki/Punjab_(British_India)) from 1848 to 1849 and was employed in the [Peshawar](/wiki/Peshawar) operations in 1851 and 1852, receiving promotion to lieutenant-colonel on 9 May 1851 and to colonel on 6 October 1854.

In 1855, during the [Crimean War](/wiki/Crimean_War), Mansfield was appointed military adviser to the Ambassador at Constantinople [Lord Stratford de Redcliff](/wiki/Stratford_Canning,_1st_Viscount_Stratford_de_Redcliffe), and accompanied him to the Crimea. He then returned to [India](/wiki/India) and served as Chief-of-Staff during the [Indian Mutiny campaign](/wiki/Indian_rebellion_of_1857) from 1857 to 1859, initially with the local rank of major-general. His role during the [Siege of Lucknow](/wiki/Siege_of_Lucknow) in November 1857 saw his appointment [Knight Commander of the Order of the Bath](/wiki/Order_of_the_Bath) (KCB) in March 1858. Promoted to major-general on 18 May 1858, he served as Commander-in-Chief of the [Bombay Army](/wiki/Bombay_Army) from 1860 to 1865 and as [Commander-in-Chief, India](/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_India) from 1865 to 1870. During this time, he was made Knight Grand Commander of the [Order of the Star of India](/wiki/Order_of_the_Star_of_India) (GCSI) and subsequently Knight Grand Cross of the Order of the Bath (GCB). Mansfield was then [Commander-in-Chief, Ireland](/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_Ireland) from 1870 to 1875. He was promoted to full general on 23 May 1872.

In 1871, he was admitted to the [Irish Privy Council](/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Ireland) and raised to the peerage as [Baron Sandhurst](/wiki/Baron_Sandhurst), of Sandhurst in the County of Berkshire.

Sandhurst died in [London](/wiki/London) on 23 June 1876, aged 57, and was buried at [Digswell](/wiki/Digswell) church, [Hertfordshire](/wiki/Hertfordshire). He was succeeded in the barony by his eldest son [William](/wiki/William_Mansfield,_1st_Viscount_Sandhurst), who was created Viscount Sandhurst in 1917. Upon the latter's death, the viscountcy became extinct, while the barony passed to Sandhurt's younger son John.

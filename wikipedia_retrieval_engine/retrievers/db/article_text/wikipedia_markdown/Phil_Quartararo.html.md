# Phil Quartararo

## Infobox

- **Born:** ( 1956-01-07 ) January 7, 1956 Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
- **Died:** November 22, 2023 (2023-11-22) (aged 67) Los Angeles, California, U.S.
- **Other name:** Phil Q
- **Occupation:** Music industry executive

**Philip Michael Quartararo** (January 7, 1956 – November 22, 2023) was an American music industry executive. He was the president and chairman of The Hello Group, and held positions as CEO at [Virgin Records](/wiki/Virgin_Records), [Warner Bros. Records](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records), and [EMI](/wiki/EMI), and was involved in the careers of recording artists such as [Linkin Park](/wiki/Linkin_Park), [Josh Groban](/wiki/Josh_Groban), [Spice Girls](/wiki/Spice_Girls), [U2](/wiki/U2), and [Yoshiki](/wiki/Yoshiki_(musician)). Quartararo was known as a defender of artist rights. He spoke out on the fight against music piracy and restoring the value of music. Quartararo was regarded as one of the most promotion-minded executives in the music business and was regarded as a thought leader on the subject of partnership between brands and artists.

## Biography

### Early life

Philip Michael Quartararo was born in [Brooklyn, New York](/wiki/Brooklyn), on January 7, 1956. His work in the music business began at an early age, booking music acts out of his bedroom while he was still in high school. He attended [Chaminade High School](/wiki/Chaminade_High_School) in [Mineola, New York](/wiki/Mineola,_New_York), where he booked the sockhop bands. He attended [Syracuse University](/wiki/Syracuse_University) where his college roommates were [John Sykes](/wiki/John_Sykes_(American_businessman)) and Rob Light. Sykes went on to become co-founder of [MTV](/wiki/MTV) and [VH1](/wiki/VH1) cable networks, as well as hold senior positions at record labels and [radio broadcast networks](/wiki/Clear_Channel_Communications). Light went on to become managing partner and head of music for [CAA](/wiki/Creative_Artists_Agency). Quartararo graduated from the [S.I. Newhouse School of Public Communications](/wiki/S.I._Newhouse_School_of_Public_Communications) in 1977.

Quartararo began his career in the record industry as a local radio promotion manager for [A&M Records](/wiki/A%26M_Records) from 1977 to 1981. He moved on to become a regional promotion manager for [RCA Records](/wiki/RCA_Records) from 1981 to 1983. His executive breakthrough came when he was hired as senior vice-president of promotion and marketing for [Island Records](/wiki/Island_Records) from 1982 to 1986, where he was instrumental in introducing [U2](/wiki/U2) in America.

### Virgin Records America

In 1986, Quartararo was recruited by [Richard Branson](/wiki/Richard_Branson) as part of the team to launch [Virgin Records America](/wiki/Virgin_Records_America), along with executives Jordan Harris and [Jeff Ayeroff](/wiki/Jeff_Ayeroff). In 1992, he became president and CEO of Virgin Records America. At Virgin he was influential in the recording careers of [Smashing Pumpkins](/wiki/Smashing_Pumpkins), [Lenny Kravitz](/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz), [Paula Abdul](/wiki/Paula_Abdul), [After 7](/wiki/After_7), [Rolling Stones](/wiki/Rolling_Stones), [Janet Jackson](/wiki/Janet_Jackson), and [Ben Harper](/wiki/Ben_Harper). He is credited with the marketing blitz that launched the [Spice Girls](/wiki/Spice_Girls) in the U.S., the success of which brought him the attention of Warner Bros. Records.

### Warner Bros. Records

In 1997, Quartararo was recruited as president of Warner Bros. Records, which was [ranked #1](/wiki/Record_label) out of world's largest record companies. At Warner Bros., he worked with artists including [Linkin Park](/wiki/Linkin_Park), [Josh Groban](/wiki/Josh_Groban), [Madonna](/wiki/Madonna_(entertainer)), [Stevie Nicks](/wiki/Stevie_Nicks), [Cher](/wiki/Cher), [Wilco](/wiki/Wilco), [Eric Clapton](/wiki/Eric_Clapton), [Red Hot Chili Peppers](/wiki/Red_Hot_Chili_Peppers), [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill), [Goo Goo Dolls](/wiki/Goo_Goo_Dolls), and [Green Day](/wiki/Green_Day).

### EMI Music

In 2002, Quartararo was hired to become executive vice-president of [EMI Music](/wiki/EMI_Music) and president of EMI marketing. Quartararo reorganized EMI, shifting its focus from sales and distribution to marketing. He directed the marketing and launch of recordings of [Coldplay](/wiki/Coldplay), [Norah Jones](/wiki/Norah_Jones), [Keith Urban](/wiki/Keith_Urban), [Blake Shelton](/wiki/Blake_Shelton), [Trace Adkins](/wiki/Trace_Adkins), [RBD](/wiki/RBD), and [Beach Boys](/wiki/Beach_Boys), as well as marketing the estate catalogs of [Frank Sinatra](/wiki/Frank_Sinatra), [Beatles](/wiki/Beatles), [Les Paul](/wiki/Les_Paul), and [Dean Martin](/wiki/Dean_Martin).

### Other endeavors

In late 2007, Quartararo left EMI to work with start-up companies focused on digital music distribution. In 2009, he started his own firm, Tripod Partners, which has worked with various music industry companies such as Beta Records TV, global online talent search [Avon Voices](/wiki/Avon_Voices), the [Shazam](/wiki/Shazam_(music_app)) music discovery app, and the [Guvera](/wiki/Guvera) music streaming service. He was associated with the talent management firm The Collective, and was a principal at the consultancy QP2 Group. Quartararo also managed [Yoshiki](/wiki/Yoshiki_(musician)), singer-songwriter [Brandon Howard](/wiki/Brandon_Howard), and heavy metal group [XJapan](/wiki/X_Japan). Quartararo was executive producer of the eight-hour [PBS](/wiki/PBS) miniseries *The Soundtrack of Our Lives*. He also served as executive producer of the double [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award)-winning album, *[Les Paul And Friends: A Tribute To A Legend](/wiki/Les_Paul)*. At the 2013 [Midem](/wiki/Midem) conference, Quartararo was referenced by industry media for his observation, "The old record business didn't get killed. It committed suicide. The industry didn't listen to its consumers."

## Philanthropy

Quartararo sat on the boards of the [Grammy Foundation](/wiki/Grammy_Foundation), [City of Hope](/wiki/City_of_Hope_National_Medical_Center), [Cedar Sinai](/wiki/Cedars_Sinai), [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA), [T J Martell Foundation](/wiki/Tony_Martell#T.J._Martell_Foundation), [Pacific Science Center](/wiki/Pacific_Science_Center), State of California Board for Engineers and Surveyors, and Syracuse University's [S.I. Newhouse School of Public Communications](/wiki/S.I._Newhouse_School_of_Public_Communications).

Quartararo was recognized for his philanthropic contributions with awards that included City of Hope's "Spirit of Life" Award, [Russell Simmons](/wiki/Russell_Simmons) Award for Diversity and Equality, the Vincent Testaverde Award for Spinal Injury, [Sons of Italy](/wiki/Sons_of_Italy) Award, and [Syracuse University's](/wiki/Syracuse_University) 40 at 40 Award.

## Illness and death

Phil Quartararo died from pancreatic cancer in [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) on November 22, 2023, at the age of 67.

## Music industry honors

Quartararo was named by *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* magazine as Music Executive of the Year (2001), and received the [NARAS](/wiki/Grammy_Awards) Governors Award.

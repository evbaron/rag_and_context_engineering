***Bridgerton*** is an American [alternative history](/wiki/Alternative_history), [Regency romance](/wiki/Regency_romance) television series created by [Chris Van Dusen](/wiki/Chris_Van_Dusen) for [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix). Based on the [book series of the same name](/wiki/Bridgerton_(novel_series)) by [Julia Quinn](/wiki/Julia_Quinn), it is [Shondaland](/wiki/Shondaland)'s first scripted show for Netflix. The series stars an [ensemble cast](/wiki/Ensemble_cast), led by [Adjoa Andoh](/wiki/Adjoa_Andoh), [Nicola Coughlan](/wiki/Nicola_Coughlan), [Phoebe Dynevor](/wiki/Phoebe_Dynevor), [Ruth Gemmell](/wiki/Ruth_Gemmell), [Claudia Jessie](/wiki/Claudia_Jessie), [Luke Newton](/wiki/Luke_Newton), [Rege-Jean Page](/wiki/Rege-Jean_Page), [Golda Rosheuvel](/wiki/Golda_Rosheuvel), [Luke Thompson](/wiki/Luke_Thompson_(actor)), and [Polly Walker](/wiki/Polly_Walker).

Narrated by [Julie Andrews](/wiki/Julie_Andrews), it follows the close-knit siblings of the noble and influential Bridgerton family as they navigate the highly competitive [social season](/wiki/Social_season), where young marriageable members of the [ton](/wiki/Ton_(society)), consisting of [nobility](/wiki/Nobility) and [gentry](/wiki/Landed_gentry), are [introduced into society](/wiki/Debutante). The series is set during the early 19th century, in an alternative London [Regency era](/wiki/Regency_era), in which [George III](/wiki/George_III) established racial equality and granted [aristocratic titles](/wiki/Title#Aristocratic_titles) to people of color due to [Queen Charlotte](/wiki/Queen_Charlotte)'s fictionalized African heritage within the series.

The first season debuted on December 25, 2020. The [second season](/wiki/Bridgerton_season_2) premiered on March 25, 2022. The third season was released in two parts, which premiered on May 16 and June 13, 2024, respectively. The fourth season was again released in two parts, premiering on January 29 and February 26, 2026, respectively. The series has been renewed for a fifth season. *[Queen Charlotte: A Bridgerton Story](/wiki/Queen_Charlotte:_A_Bridgerton_Story)*, a spin-off series focused on Queen Charlotte, was released in May 2023.

*Bridgerton* has been positively received for its direction, actors' performances, production and set design, winning five [Primetime Creative Arts Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Creative_Arts_Emmy_Awards), a [Make-Up Artists And Hair Stylists Guild Awards](/wiki/Make-Up_Artists_And_Hair_Stylists_Guild_Awards), and nominations at the [Primetime Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards), [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards), [Satellite Awards](/wiki/Satellite_Awards) and [NAACP Image Awards](/wiki/NAACP_Image_Awards). The music score by [Kris Bowers](/wiki/Kris_Bowers) earned a [Grammy Award nomination for Best Score Soundtrack for Visual Media](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Score_Soundtrack_for_Visual_Media).

## Premise

Set against the backdrop of the [Regency era](/wiki/Regency_era), the eight close-knit siblings of the [noble](/wiki/British_nobility) and powerful Bridgerton family – Anthony, Benedict, Colin, Daphne, Eloise, Francesca, Gregory, and Hyacinth – navigate [London high society](/wiki/Ton_(society)) in search of love, surrounded by friends and rivals.

## Production

### Development

*Bridgerton* executive producer [Shonda Rhimes](/wiki/Shonda_Rhimes)

On July 20, 2018, Netflix announced that [Shonda Rhimes](/wiki/Shonda_Rhimes), through her production company [Shondaland](/wiki/Shondaland), would produce the screen adaptation of the bestselling novels of the [*Bridgerton* series](/wiki/Bridgerton_(novel_series)) by [Julia Quinn](/wiki/Julia_Quinn), while [Chris Van Dusen](/wiki/Chris_Van_Dusen) would serve as showrunner. Quinn explained on *[The Tamron Hall Show](/wiki/The_Tamron_Hall_Show)* that when she heard from her agent that Rhimes was interested in adapting her novels, she "almost fell off of [her] stool," and quickly agreed to the offer. Season 1 of the show adapted *[The Duke and I](/wiki/The_Duke_and_I)*, the first book of Quinn's series. Van Dusen said in a Shondaland article: "I think [period shows] are considered a bit traditional and conservative. With *Bridgerton,* I wanted to take everything I loved about a period show and turn it into something fresh, topical, and relatable."

Before the first season aired, the show was already in [pre-production](/wiki/Casting_(performing_arts)) for a second season which was officially announced in January 2021. It is focused on Anthony and based on the book *[The Viscount Who Loved Me](/wiki/The_Viscount_Who_Loved_Me)*. On April 13, 2021, creator Van Dusen revealed on Twitter that the series had additionally been renewed for a third and fourth season.

Unlike the first two seasons, which followed the order of the book series, the third season focuses on Colin and Penelope, based on Quinn's fourth novel *[Romancing Mister Bridgerton](/wiki/Romancing_Mister_Bridgerton)*, rather than Benedict. Jess Brownell replaced Van Dusen as writer and showrunner for the third and fourth seasons. In July 2024, it was confirmed that Benedict Bridgerton would be the lead for Season 4. In May 2025, Netflix officially announced a dual season renewal of seasons 5 and 6. On 24 March 2026, it was confirmed that Francesca Bridgerton would be the lead for Season 5.

### Casting

Unlike the series of novels, *Bridgerton* is set in an [alternate history](/wiki/Alternate_history) with a [racially integrated](/wiki/Racially_integrated) London where people of color are members of the *[ton](/wiki/Ton_(le_bon_ton))*, some with titles granted by the [sovereign](/wiki/Sovereign). Creator [Chris Van Dusen](/wiki/Chris_Van_Dusen) was inspired by historical debate over the [1940s African ancestry claims](/wiki/Margarita_de_Castro_e_Souza) of Queen Charlotte "...to base the show in an alternative history in which Queen Charlotte's [mixed race](/wiki/Mixed_race) heritage was not only well-established but was transformative for Black people and other people of color in England." Van Dusen says the series is not "[color-blind](/wiki/Color-blind_casting)" because "that would imply that color and race were never considered, when color and race are part of the show."

On June 19, 2019, [Julie Andrews](/wiki/Julie_Andrews) was cast as Lady Whistledown, whose [voice-overs](/wiki/Voice-overs) explain much of the series' action. In addition, [Phoebe Dynevor](/wiki/Phoebe_Dynevor) and [Regé-Jean Page](/wiki/Reg%C3%A9-Jean_Page) were cast to play the leads with [Jonathan Bailey](/wiki/Jonathan_Bailey), [Golda Rosheuvel](/wiki/Golda_Rosheuvel), [Luke Newton](/wiki/Luke_Newton), [Claudia Jessie](/wiki/Claudia_Jessie), [Nicola Coughlan](/wiki/Nicola_Coughlan), [Ruby Barker](/wiki/Ruby_Barker), [Sabrina Bartlett](/wiki/Sabrina_Bartlett), [Ruth Gemmell](/wiki/Ruth_Gemmell), [Adjoa Andoh](/wiki/Adjoa_Andoh) and [Polly Walker](/wiki/Polly_Walker) co-starring. [Luke Thompson](/wiki/Luke_Thompson_(actor)), [Will Tilston](/wiki/Will_Tilston), [Florence Hunt](/wiki/Florence_Hunt), and [Ruby Stokes](/wiki/Ruby_Stokes) were cast as the remaining four Bridgerton siblings at the end of July. Rounding out the cast were [Ben Miller](/wiki/Ben_Miller), [Bessie Carter](/wiki/Bessie_Carter), [Harriet Cains](/wiki/Harriet_Cains), [Martins Imhangbe](/wiki/Martins_Imhangbe), and [Lorraine Ashbourne](/wiki/Lorraine_Ashbourne).

On January 21, 2021, it was announced that [Jonathan Bailey](/wiki/Jonathan_Bailey) would be reprising his role as Anthony and would be the center of the show's sophomore season. The following month, [Simone Ashley](/wiki/Simone_Ashley) had been cast as Kate. On April 5, 2021, [Charithra Chandran](/wiki/Charithra_Chandran) joined the cast as Edwina; [Rupert Young](/wiki/Rupert_Young) joined to play a new character; [Shelley Conn](/wiki/Shelley_Conn) was cast as Kate's stepmother and Edwina's mother, Mary; and [Calam Lynch](/wiki/Calam_Lynch) was cast as Theo Sharpe. On May 28, 2021, [Rupert Evans](/wiki/Rupert_Evans) joined the cast as Edmund Bridgerton, the late patriarch of the Bridgerton family.

### Filming

[Principal photography](/wiki/Principal_photography) commenced in July 2019 and [wrapped](/wiki/Wrap_(filmmaking)) in late February 2020. *Bridgerton* was filmed in London and [Bath](/wiki/Bath,_Somerset), as well as at various estates and parks around England. Although the series takes place in London, most street scenes were filmed in Bath, [York](/wiki/York), and [Chatham](/wiki/Chatham,_Kent). The grounds of [Wilton House](/wiki/Wilton_House) were used for [Hyde Park](/wiki/Hyde_Park,_London) and the grounds of [Somerley](/wiki/Somerley) were used for [Hampstead Heath](/wiki/Hampstead_Heath). Garden scenes were filmed at [Painshill](/wiki/Painshill) near [Cobham](/wiki/Cobham,_Surrey) and the Commissioner's House in Chatham.

[Ranger's House](/wiki/Ranger%27s_House) is used as the exterior of the Bridgerton family's house in London

Filming locations included [Ranger's House](/wiki/Ranger%27s_House) in [Greenwich](/wiki/Greenwich) (standing in for the Bridgerton House in London exterior); [Halton House](/wiki/Halton_House) at [RAF Halton](/wiki/RAF_Halton) (Bridgerton House interior, Featheringtons' interior); Wilton House (Simon's Hastings House, Clyvedon estate interior, St James's Palace throne room); [Syon House](/wiki/Syon_House) and [Badminton House](/wiki/Badminton_House) (Hastings House); [Castle Howard](/wiki/Castle_Howard) (Clyvedon estate); [Coneysthorpe](/wiki/Coneysthorpe) (Clyvedon village); [Hampton Court Palace](/wiki/Hampton_Court_Palace) and [Lancaster House](/wiki/Lancaster_House) ([St. James's Palace](/wiki/St._James%27s_Palace)); [Holburne Museum](/wiki/Holburne_Museum) (Lady Danbury's estate); [Hatfield House](/wiki/Hatfield_House) (Featheringtons' interior); [No. 1 Royal Crescent](/wiki/No._1_Royal_Crescent) ([Grosvenor Square](/wiki/Grosvenor_Square)); [Queen's House](/wiki/Queen%27s_House) and Somerley ([Somerset House](/wiki/Somerset_House)); and [Dorney Court](/wiki/Dorney_Court) (coaching inn).

[Vauxhall Pleasure Gardens](/wiki/Vauxhall_Pleasure_Gardens) no longer exists in its entirety. The production team recreated it for Lady Danbury's ball by combining the remaining parts with [Castle Howard](/wiki/Castle_Howard) and [Stowe Park](/wiki/Stowe_Park). The banqueting room at the [Guildhall, Bath](/wiki/Guildhall,_Bath) was used for another ball as well as the Great Hall at [Leigh Court](/wiki/Leigh_Court) in Somerset.

Anthony Bridgerton and Simon Basset meet in the real-life [Reform Club](/wiki/Reform_Club) on [Pall Mall](/wiki/Pall_Mall,_London) in [central London](/wiki/Central_London). The scene in which Lady Featherington takes Marina to the slums was filmed at [Chatham Dockyard](/wiki/Chatham_Dockyard) in Kent. [Boxing](/wiki/Boxing) scenes were also filmed here in addition to [Normansfield Theatre](/wiki/Normansfield_Theatre) in [Teddington](/wiki/Teddington). Theatre scenes were filmed at the [Hackney Empire](/wiki/Hackney_Empire). A café in Bath, Pickled Greens, was used as the site of the [Modiste shop](/wiki/Dressmaker) and the Bathrooms at No.5 store on [Trim Street](/wiki/Trim_Street,_Bath) became the site of [Gunter's Tea Shop](/wiki/Gunter%27s_Tea_Shop).

The costuming was led by [Ellen Mirojnick](/wiki/Ellen_Mirojnick) and involved over two hundred people and five months of preparation to create 5,000 costumes.

Production on the second season began in March 2021. In May 2021, it was reported that the [Royal Borough of Windsor and Maidenhead](/wiki/Royal_Borough_of_Windsor_and_Maidenhead) refused permission to build a film set for the second season in [Sunninghill Park](/wiki/Sunninghill_Park) near [Windsor](/wiki/Windsor,_Berkshire), despite royal approval. ### Music

Further information: [Music of Bridgerton](/wiki/Music_of_Bridgerton)

American composer and pianist [Kris Bowers](/wiki/Kris_Bowers) composed and arranged the score for the series. Bowers wrote and composed the first season's soundtrack, featuring nineteen songs. Musicians recorded the score remotely from their home studios during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).

The first season featured orchestral covers of contemporary popular music, which director and executive producer [Julie Anne Robinson](/wiki/Julie_Anne_Robinson) said was inspired by the use of classic rock songs in the 2001 film *[A Knight's Tale](/wiki/A_Knight%27s_Tale)*. Songs featured included [Ariana Grande](/wiki/Ariana_Grande)'s "[Thank U, Next](/wiki/Thank_U,_Next_(song))", [Maroon 5](/wiki/Maroon_5)'s "[Girls Like You](/wiki/Girls_Like_You)", [Shawn Mendes](/wiki/Shawn_Mendes)'s "[In My Blood](/wiki/In_My_Blood_(Shawn_Mendes_song))" and [Billie Eilish](/wiki/Billie_Eilish)'s "[Bad Guy](/wiki/Bad_Guy_(Billie_Eilish_song))", all four of which are performed by [Vitamin String Quartet](/wiki/Vitamin_String_Quartet). Also included are [Celeste](/wiki/Celeste_(singer))'s "[Strange](/wiki/Strange_(Celeste_song))" performed by Bowers, and [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift)'s "[Wildest Dreams](/wiki/Wildest_Dreams)" performed by Duomo. Bowers also included modern interpretations of classical music, such as [Bach](/wiki/Bach)'s [Cello Suite No. 6 in D major](/wiki/BWV_1012) from [Peter Gregson](/wiki/Peter_Gregson_(cellist))'s *[Recomposed by Peter Gregson: Bach – The Cello Suites](/wiki/Recomposed_by_Peter_Gregson:_Bach_%E2%80%93_The_Cello_Suites)* and [Vivaldi](/wiki/Vivaldi)'s *[The Four Seasons](/wiki/The_Four_Seasons_(Vivaldi))* from [Max Richter](/wiki/Max_Richter)'s *[Recomposed by Max Richter: Vivaldi – The Four Seasons](/wiki/Recomposed_by_Max_Richter:_Vivaldi_%E2%80%93_The_Four_Seasons)*. The first season also included [JPOLND](/wiki/JPOLND)'s "The End" which has a "swingy melody but intense lyrics."

In the second season, covers include [Nirvana](/wiki/Nirvana_(band))'s "[Stay Away](/wiki/Stay_Away_(Nirvana_song))" and [Robyn](/wiki/Robyn)'s "[Dancing On My Own](/wiki/Dancing_On_My_Own)" by Vitamin String Quartet, [Madonna](/wiki/Madonna)'s "[Material Girl](/wiki/Material_Girl)", "Kabhi Khushi Kabhie Gham" from the soundtrack of the film of the [same name](/wiki/Kabhi_Khushi_Kabhie_Gham) and [Calvin Harris](/wiki/Calvin_Harris)' "[How Deep Is Your Love](/wiki/How_Deep_Is_Your_Love_(Calvin_Harris_and_Disciples_song))" by Bowers. Also included are [Alanis Morissette](/wiki/Alanis_Morissette)'s "[You Oughta Know](/wiki/You_Oughta_Know)" and [Pink](/wiki/Pink_(singer))'s "[What About Us](/wiki/What_About_Us_(Pink_song))" by Duomo, [Harry Styles](/wiki/Harry_Styles)' "[Sign of the Times](/wiki/Sign_of_the_Times_(Harry_Styles_song))" by Steve Horner, [Rihanna](/wiki/Rihanna)'s "[Diamonds](/wiki/Diamonds_(Rihanna_song))" by Hannah V and Joe Rodwell and [Miley Cyrus](/wiki/Miley_Cyrus)' "[Wrecking Ball](/wiki/Wrecking_Ball_(Miley_Cyrus_song))" by Midnight String Quartet. To promote the show, Morisette appeared in a video performing "You Oughta Know" with Duomo. When discussing the music of season two, music supervisor Justin Kramps explained that "even for these songs that are just huge songs that everyone knows, [an instrumental version] still breathes new life and brings them to a new audience, and in a different way. Pop is where we start, because it fits the style of the show, and it's often using these super-recognizable songs that just add a lot of joy, which is what pop does in general."

In the third season, covers include [Gayle](/wiki/Gayle_(singer))'s "[ABCDEFU](/wiki/ABCDEFU)" by Vitula, [BTS](/wiki/BTS)' "[Dynamite](/wiki/Dynamite_(BTS_song))", [Sia](/wiki/Sia)'s "[Cheap Thrills](/wiki/Cheap_Thrills_(song))", [Billie Eilish](/wiki/Billie_Eilish)'s "[Happier Than Ever](/wiki/Happier_Than_Ever_(song))", and [Coldplay](/wiki/Coldplay)'s "[Yellow](/wiki/Yellow_(Coldplay_song))" by [Vitamin String Quartet](/wiki/Vitamin_String_Quartet), [Nick Jonas](/wiki/Nick_Jonas)' "[Jealous](/wiki/Jealous_(Nick_Jonas_song))", by Shimmer, [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift)'s "[Snow on the Beach](/wiki/Snow_on_the_Beach)" by Atwood Quartet, [Pitbull](/wiki/Pitbull_(rapper))'s "[Give Me Everything](/wiki/Give_Me_Everything)", [Demi Lovato](/wiki/Demi_Lovato)'s "[Confident](/wiki/Confident_(album))", and [Ellie Goulding](/wiki/Ellie_Goulding)'s "Lights (Stripped Down)" by Archer Marsh, [Ariana Grande](/wiki/Ariana_Grande)'s "[POV](/wiki/POV_(song))" by Strings from Paris, [Imagine Dragons](/wiki/Imagine_Dragons)' "[Thunder](/wiki/Thunder_(Imagine_Dragons_song))" by Thomas Mercier, and [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift)'s "[You Belong with Me](/wiki/You_Belong_with_Me)" by Duomo.

#### Season 1

The first season received positive reviews. On the [review aggregator](/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) 87% 100 reviews are positive, with an average rating of 7.7/10. The website's critics consensus reads, "Sumptuous design, soapy drama, and a sterling cast make *Bridgerton* a delightful treat." [Metacritic](/wiki/Metacritic) gave the series a weighted average score of 75 out of 100 based on 34 reviews, indicating "generally favorable" reviews.

Kristen Baldwin of *[Entertainment Weekly](/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* gave the series a B+ and wrote, "*Bridgerton*, it seems, is a wonderful diversion for those who love *[Pride & Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_%26_Prejudice_(2005_film))* but wish it had more stairway sex." [Richard Roeper](/wiki/Richard_Roeper) of *[Chicago Sun-Times](/wiki/Chicago_Sun-Times)* gave the series four out of four stars and called it "A show that will give you that unmistakable binge twinge and have you activating that 'Next Episode" time and again, until there are no more 'Next Episodes'." British *[GQ](/wiki/GQ)* described *Bridgerton* as a cross between *[Downton Abbey](/wiki/Downton_Abbey)* and *[Gossip Girl](/wiki/Gossip_Girl)*, but noted that it "may just end up being another disappointment" and "it could have been brilliant". [Salamishah Tillet](/wiki/Salamishah_Tillet) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* said "Bridgerton provides a blueprint for British period shows in which Black characters can thrive within the melodramatic story lines, extravagant costumes and bucolic beauty [...] without having to be servants or enslaved." Erum Salam of *[Cosmopolitan](/wiki/Cosmopolitan_(magazine))* wrote "I kept wanting more. I wanted more explanations of how race factored into this society..." Carolyn Hinds from *[Observer](/wiki/Observer.com)* stated "*Bridgerton* has been praised as a racially diverse show set in the Regency era, because having Black people in a big budget production period show—or film—about England before the 1900s feels like a foreign concept for white directors and writers, even though Black, South Asian, Asian and other people of color had been living there for hundreds of years as a result of mercantilism, and the monarchy’s colonization of over half the world." *[Vanity Fair](/wiki/Vanity_Fair_(magazine))*'s Caroline Framke describes the sex in the series "isn't altogether shocking material for Shondaland to mine for its first drama series absent broadcast restraints."

The first season's 6th episode drew criticism with regard to the [non-consensual](/wiki/Sexual_assault) nature of a sex scene between Simon and Daphne, which amounted to [marital rape](/wiki/Marital_rape). The episode depicts Daphne, while having sex with Simon, changing her position to be on top, preventing Simon from [pulling out](/wiki/Pulling_out) of her when he climaxes despite his objections, to prove he could [impregnate her](/wiki/Sperm_theft) but chooses not to. Described as one of the toxic plot points of their relationship, it further drew scrutiny due to the fact that the deceit was never addressed as sexual assault in the series. Critics pointed out that it failed to acknowledge the difficulties of [male victims of rape](/wiki/Rape_of_males) and the further [fetishization of black men](/wiki/Big_Black_Cock) in media.

### Audience viewership

#### Season 1

On January 27, 2021, Netflix announced that 82 million households had watched at least two minutes of the first season, amounting to 625 million hours viewed. It was the most-watched original series launch on the service at the time of its premiere, prior to being surpassed by *[Squid Game](/wiki/Squid_Game)* in October 2021. Caroline Framke of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* attributed this huge viewership to "the perfect storm of a perfectly timed premiere (Christmas Day of 2020), providing silly and sexy entertainment after the first terrible year of COVID".

#### Season 2

Season 2 premiered on March 25, 2022, and debuted number one in 92 countries on the platform. It was also the most viewed show on United States [television screens](/wiki/Television) for three weeks per [Nielsen Media Research](/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research). Season 2 amassed 193 million viewing hours in its opening weekend, the highest opening for any English-language Netflix series at the time. It also broke the record for most viewed English-language series in a single week at that time, with 251.74 million viewing hours from March 28 to April 3. The first season also re-entered Netflix's top ten in second place. By April 19, *Bridgerton*'s second season had overtaken its predecessor as the [most watched English-language television series on Netflix](/wiki/List_of_most-watched_Netflix_original_programming) at the time with 627.11 million hours viewed since its March 25, 2022, launch. This viewing numbers went up to 656.16 million by the 28-day mark.

[Nielsen Media Research](/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), analyzing the 2.55 billion minutes viewed on United States television screens in the first three days of season 2's availability (double all others across streamers for the week of March 21), characterized *Bridgerton*'s audience as "diverse and broad". The firm found one third of viewers to be Hispanic or African American and an even split in popularity across the 18–34, 35–49, and 50–64 age groups at 25% each. The only exception was gender parity, with 76% of the audience reported to be female. It added 3.2 billion minutes viewed in its second week on top of the chart. It topped the chart for a third week with 1.6 billion minutes viewed.

#### Season 3

In 2023, between seasons 2 and 3, Netflix rolled out a new, different method of counting viewership, which is to divide the number of hours watched by a show's runtime. The platform also expanded the measurement window from 28 days to 91 days. Under this new method of measurement, season 3 part 1, which had 220 minutes of runtime, received 45.05 million views in its first week, and then 25.3 million views in its second week, 11.6 million in its third, and 6.9 million in its fourth. After season 3 part 2 debuted (based on 480 minutes of runtime), the overall season received 28 million views that debut week, 16.5 million views the following week, 6.6 million views the third week, and 4 million the fourth.

Season 3 part 1 recorded the show's biggest opening weekend to date with 165.2 million views (on 220 minutes of runtime). At the time, Season 3 also recorded the highest single weekly view count for any Netflix series, regardless of language, since Netflix started the list in June 2023. On July 2, 2024, Season 3 became Netflix's tenth biggest English-language series ever with 91.9 million views so far. As of January 1, 2025, Season 3 was sixth in Netflix's top-10 all-time list, for any language, with 846.5 million hours viewed/106 million views.

Season 3 became the second most watched series on Netflix during the first half of 2024, with 92 million views. Season 3 has the second-highest viewing figures for streaming in 2024, with 11 billion minutes of watch time. According to Nielsen, *Bridgerton* was the most-streamed original series of 2024 with 21.42 billion minutes viewed, with Season 3 accounting for 56% of those views.

### Cultural impact

Fashion and interior design trends influenced or made popular by the series have been dubbed "[Regencycore](/wiki/Regencycore)" or "the *Bridgerton* effect". *Lyst* reported an increase in searches for items such as [corsets](/wiki/Corset), headpieces, and elbow-length gloves after the series' premiere. The series' first two female leads, Daphne and Kate, were also linked to a popularity in the colors sky blue and lilac respectively. In 2021, [Abigail Barlow](/wiki/Abigail_Barlow) and [Emily Bear](/wiki/Emily_Bear) wrote a concept album based on characters and situations in season 1 of the series titled *[The Unofficial Bridgerton Musical](/wiki/The_Unofficial_Bridgerton_Musical),* which won the 2022 [Grammy Award for Best Musical Theater Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Musical_Theater_Album). After the two presented the songs from the album live in concert at the [Kennedy Center](/wiki/Kennedy_Center) in July 2022, Netflix sued them for copyright infringement.

To ring in the second season, an official "Queen's Ball" was held in [Washington DC](/wiki/Washington_DC), Chicago, [Montréal](/wiki/Montr%C3%A9al), and Los Angeles, with similar events and experiences taking place in London and [Johannesburg](/wiki/Johannesburg). [Bloomingdale's](/wiki/Bloomingdale%27s) put together a *Bridgerton*-themed pop-up collection and tea bar, displaying real costumes from the series in the U.S. for the first time in its 59th Street windows.

After season two featured the main characters playing [pall-mall](/wiki/Pall-mall), a lawn game considered to be the precursor to [croquet](/wiki/Croquet), retailer [John Lewis](/wiki/John_Lewis_%26_Partners) reported a 90% rise in sales for croquet sets. There was also a notable increase in internet searches and purchases of [tiaras](/wiki/Tiara) and corsets after season two's premiere.

Stately homes around England saw an uptick in interest and visitors. Regarding Ranger's House, Chris Small of [English Heritage](/wiki/English_Heritage) said, "Since the launch of *Bridgerton* in 2020, we have seen many people who were previously unaware of the site inspired to visit." [Walking tours](/wiki/Walking_tour) of the filming locations of the series have also been created, including an official one by Netflix. [Castle Howard](/wiki/Castle_Howard) opened an exhibition titled *Castle Howard on Screen: From Brideshead to Bridgerton* in May 2022.

The [finale episode](/wiki/Poorhouse_Rock) of the [33rd season](/wiki/The_Simpsons_(season_33)) of *[The Simpsons](/wiki/The_Simpsons)* on May 22, 2022, featured [Marge](/wiki/Marge_Simpson) and her friends watching a period drama called "Tunnelton" with a narrator who sounds like Lady Whistledown, and a character emerging drenched from a lake, like Anthony in the fifth episode of the second season. The creators behind "[Rogue](/wiki/Rogue_(Doctor_Who))", the sixth episode of the [2024 season](/wiki/Doctor_Who_series_14) of *[Doctor Who](/wiki/Doctor_Who)*, took great inspiration from *Bridgerton* as the episode revolves around intrigue and romance at an English country party in 1813, with the show being directly referenced by main character [Ruby Sunday](/wiki/Ruby_Sunday).

After season three part one, "The Carriage Scene" became a standalone viral moment for the series, and was individually nominated for a [BAFTA P&O Cruises Memorable Moment Award](/wiki/British_Academy_Television_Award_for_P%26O_Cruises_Memorable_Moment) and an Edinburgh TV Award for TV Moment of the Year, winning the latter award on August 21, 2025, at the [Edinburgh International Television Festival](/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Television_Festival). The romantic moment between season three leads Colin and Penelope would go on to be directly referenced in episode 3 of *[Emily in Paris](/wiki/Emily_in_Paris)* season four when character Mindy Chen jokes “Oh! Someone's living out their horny Bridgerton fantasies" when she sees a carriage rocking outside of a ball. *Bridgerton*'s carriage scene's social impact also inspired comparisons between the friends to lovers storylines of *Bridgerton*'s "Polin" and *[The Gilded Age](/wiki/The_Gilded_Age_(TV_series))*'s "Larian" when a carriage scene was used in advertisements for *The Gilded Age* season three in summer of 2025. In October 2024, Netflix would use "The Carriage Scene" with the tagline "What a Ride" and the timestamp of the scene as the advertisement to launch their "Moments" feature to the Netflix mobile app. The orchestral cover of "[Give Me Everything](/wiki/Give_Me_Everything)" arranged by Archer Marsh that was used to score The Carriage Scene also contributed to the scene's virality on the social media app [TikTok](/wiki/TikTok). The original artist [Pitbull](/wiki/Pitbull_(rapper)) later took to Instagram to post the scene with the caption "This again shows the world how music is the international language that transcends over boundaries more so how a hit song can remain timeless."

Netflix tripled its brand partnerships for season 3, and the Queen's Ball expanded to a dozen cities in addition to an afternoon tea event at [the Lanesborough](/wiki/The_Lanesborough) in London. *[The Wrap](/wiki/The_Wrap)* called *Bridgerton* fans a "ripe testing group" for this international merchandising and live-experience strategy from Netflix, comparing it to the likes of [Disney](/wiki/Disney) and [Universal](/wiki/Universal_Studios,_Inc.) franchises.

## Historical inaccuracies

A portrait of [Queen Charlotte](/wiki/Queen_Charlotte), painted by [Thomas Gainsborough](/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough)

[Chris Van Dusen](/wiki/Chris_Van_Dusen) has said that although the show takes place during the Regency era, it "is a reimagined world, we're not a history lesson, it's not a documentary. What we're really doing with the show is marrying history and fantasy in what I think is a very exciting way. One approach that we took to that is our approach to race." The Bridgerton family, Lady Whistledown, and most of the other characters in the show are fictional.

The theory that [Queen Charlotte](/wiki/Queen_Charlotte) may have had African ancestry has been called an unhistorical assertion by most scholars. In an interview with *[Insider](/wiki/Insider.com)* magazine, American historian Marlene Koenig said the show's representation of [Regency-era London](/wiki/Regency_era) was more diverse than it was in reality, adding that "diversity as we know what the word means did not exist" in [Britain](/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) during that period. Some classical music pieces used in the first season were composed later than 1813, the year of the first season. Examples include [Dmitri Shostakovich](/wiki/Dmitri_Shostakovich)'s [Suite for Variety Orchestra](/wiki/Suite_for_Variety_Orchestra), which was written sometime after 1956, and "[Belle nuit, ô nuit d'amour](/wiki/Belle_nuit,_%C3%B4_nuit_d%27amour)" from [Jacques Offenbach](/wiki/Jacques_Offenbach)'s 1881 opera *[The Tales of Hoffmann](/wiki/The_Tales_of_Hoffmann)*.

Historians have pointed out inaccuracies in clothing, such as the show using [corsets](/wiki/Corset) to represent the oppression placed on women in society instead of supportive undergarments they were considered as in the [Regency era](/wiki/Regency_era). In a scene from season 1, Daphne is seen with bruises on her back from the corset; however, at the time it was universal to wear a [chemise](/wiki/Chemise) under it to prevent this. Fabrics and patterns appearing in the show are too modern compared to the ones used in the historical period in which the events are supposed to take place. Other historical inaccuracies include the lack of bonnets and the contemporary styles of the fabrics and colors. However, a majority of the show's historical inaccuracies regarding clothing are intentional. The show does not reflect an accurate portrayal of the Regency era but rather a world that "borders on historical fantasy". The show depicts a dramatization of the Regency era, where the show is "rooted in Regency times but the volume is turned up".

## Themes

### Gender

A recurring topic throughout the show is gender, especially the role of women in British high society. Chris Van Dusen aimed for the show to have a "running, modern commentary about how, over the last 200 years, everything has changed and nothing has changed, for both women and men."

With the focus on each year's social season, Daphne, Kate, Penelope and the other women struggle against the societal expectations held for them. Daphne Bridgerton, the Queen's Diamond of her season, fights to maintain her high reputation in society and avoid ruin while still marrying for love. Kate Sharma, considered "too old" for marrying, attempts to regain her family's place in high society as she tries to set her sister, Edwina, up to marry well. Eloise Bridgerton, uninterested in marrying and wanting to pursue a more meaningful life, finds herself more politically involved.

The men, on the other hand, face significantly lower stakes to their reputations. After overhearing Anthony say that he desires a more agreeable wife, Kate confronts him on his viewpoints toward women and argues that Anthony's high standards for women do not match the standards he has for himself.

### Race

Queen Charlotte in the series as portrayed by [Golda Rosheuvel](/wiki/Golda_Rosheuvel)

A notable, modernized twist on the show is the diverse cast of characters. Straying from the source novels, Van Dusen "used Queen Charlotte as a centerpiece to weave racial equality in the show." With Queen Charlotte cast as a Black woman, Van Dusen explains, "Could she have used her power to elevate other people of color in society? Could she have given them titles and lands and dukedoms?"

When Simon Basset rejects the idea of pursuing Daphne Bridgerton, Lady Danbury explains that love conquers all, using Queen Charlotte and King George as an example of how their marriage had changed society. However, Simon also counters that, even though Queen Charlotte is currently in power, "Black progress is fragile and dependent on the whims of whichever white king is in charge."

### Literature

*Bridgerton* also explores the "power of the written word", with authors such as Lady Whistledown having immense influence over the social season with her Society Papers. Similar to how media can strongly sway public opinion, Van Dusen makes the statement that "social media is kind of like a modern-day corset".

Throughout the show, Lady Whistledown releases Society Papers that comment on notable figures during the social season, whether it be praising them or airing out their secrets. The pamphlets' influence is enough to sway even Queen Charlotte herself, who seeks to find the author of the Society Papers in order to shut them down.

## Prequel miniseries

Main article: [*Queen Charlotte: A Bridgerton Story*](/wiki/Queen_Charlotte:_A_Bridgerton_Story)

In May 2021, Netflix ordered a limited prequel series from [Shondaland](/wiki/Shondaland) to focus on young Queen Charlotte, a character who does not appear in the *Bridgerton* novels. Rhimes wrote the spin-off and served as executive producer alongside [Betsy Beers](/wiki/Betsy_Beers) and [Tom Verica](/wiki/Tom_Verica). Rosheuvel, Andoh, Gemmell, and Fleet reprise their roles, while [India Amarteifio](/wiki/India_Amarteifio), [Michelle Fairley](/wiki/Michelle_Fairley), [Corey Mylchreest](/wiki/Corey_Mylchreest), and [Arsema Thomas](/wiki/Arsema_Thomas) were cast, together with [Connie Jenkins-Greig](/wiki/Connie_Jenkins-Greig), who plays young Violet. In September 2022, Netflix announced that *Queen Charlotte: A Bridgerton Story* would be the title for the series and released the first look. The series was released on [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) on May 4, 2023, consisting of six episodes.

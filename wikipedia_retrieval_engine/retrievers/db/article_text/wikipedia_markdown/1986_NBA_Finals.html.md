The **1986 NBA Finals** was the [championship series](/wiki/NBA_Finals) of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association)'s (NBA) [1985–86 season](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_NBA_season), and the culmination of the [season's playoffs](/wiki/1986_NBA_Playoffs). It pitted the [Eastern Conference](/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Boston Celtics](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Boston_Celtics_season) against the [Western Conference](/wiki/Western_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Houston Rockets](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Houston_Rockets_season), in a rematch of the [1981 NBA Finals](/wiki/1981_NBA_Finals) (though only [Allen Leavell](/wiki/Allen_Leavell) and [Robert Reid](/wiki/Robert_Reid_(basketball)) remained from the Rockets' 1981 team). It was the second and last NBA Championship Series of the 1980s not to feature the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers), who were eliminated by the Rockets on both occasions. The heavily favored Celtics defeated the Rockets four games to two to win their then-record 16th NBA championship. The championship would be the Celtics' last until [2008](/wiki/2008_NBA_Finals). [Larry Bird](/wiki/Larry_Bird) was named the [Finals MVP](/wiki/Bill_Russell_NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award).

On another note, this series marked the first time the "NBA Finals" branding was officially used, as they dropped the "NBA World Championship Series" branding which had been in use since the beginning of the league, though it had been unofficially called the "NBA Finals" for years.

Until the [2011](/wiki/2011_NBA_Finals) series, this was the last time the NBA Finals had started before June. Since game three, all NBA Finals games have been played in June. Starting with [the following year](/wiki/1987_NBA_Finals), the NBA Finals would be held exclusively in the month of June. It was also the last NBA Finals series to schedule a game on a Monday until [1999](/wiki/1999_NBA_Finals) and also the last NBA Finals game to be played on [Memorial Day](/wiki/Memorial_Day). Until the [2018](/wiki/2018_NBA_Finals) series, it was the last to conclude before June 10.

[CBS Sports](/wiki/NBA_on_CBS) used [Dick Stockton](/wiki/Dick_Stockton) and [Tom Heinsohn](/wiki/Tom_Heinsohn) as the play-by-play man and color commentator respectively. Meanwhile, [Brent Musburger](/wiki/Brent_Musburger) was the host and [Pat O'Brien](/wiki/Pat_O%27Brien_(television)) (the [Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets)' sideline) and [Lesley Visser](/wiki/Lesley_Visser) (the [Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics)' sideline) were the [sideline reporters](/wiki/Sideline_reporter).

## Background

See also: [1986 NBA playoffs](/wiki/1986_NBA_playoffs)

### Boston Celtics

Main article: [1985–86 Boston Celtics season](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Boston_Celtics_season)

The Celtics made the [1985 NBA Finals](/wiki/1985_NBA_Finals), but lost in six games to the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season). The series exposed some of Boston's weaknesses, such as the lack of bench scoring, which was exploited after [Kevin McHale](/wiki/Kevin_McHale_(basketball)) moved to the starting lineup with [Cedric Maxwell](/wiki/Cedric_Maxwell) bothered by knee injuries. In addition, [Larry Bird](/wiki/Larry_Bird) played through an elbow injury, which severely affected his shooting. In the offseason, president [Red Auerbach](/wiki/Red_Auerbach) decided to tweak the roster, trading Maxwell to the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Los_Angeles_Clippers_season) for oft-injured center [Bill Walton](/wiki/Bill_Walton). He also made a trade with the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Indiana_Pacers_season), acquiring [Jerry Sichting](/wiki/Jerry_Sichting) for [Quinn Buckner](/wiki/Quinn_Buckner). These moves would pave the way for the Celtics' greatest season yet.

Entering the 1985–86 season, the Celtics surged to a league-best 67–15 record, powered by an NBA record 40 victories at home. Their incomparable home record alone (since tied by the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_San_Antonio_Spurs_season) in the [2015–2016](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NBA_season) regular season) put the Celtics in the conversation among the NBA's greatest teams in a single season.

In the playoffs, Boston needed just three games to defeat the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Chicago_Bulls_season) in the first round, despite a playoff record 63 points by [Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan) in Game 2. When asked about Jordan's performance in Boston's 135-131 2OT victory, Boston's coach [K. C. Jones](/wiki/K._C._Jones) said, "I don't have a word for today." In retrospect, this game is considered to be a classic clash of the NBA's (arguably) greatest player, Jordan, and the NBA's (arguably) greatest team, Bird's 1985–86 Celtics. In the second round, Boston eliminated the [Atlanta Hawks](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Atlanta_Hawks_season) in five games, with the clinching Game 5 a no-doubter as Boston outscored Atlanta 36–6 in the third quarter in route to a 132–99 victory. Then in the conference finals, Boston swept the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Milwaukee_Bucks_season) in four games, a direct reversal of their second round meeting in the [1983 NBA playoffs](/wiki/1983_NBA_playoffs).

### Houston Rockets

Main article: [1985–86 Houston Rockets season](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Houston_Rockets_season)

Following their previous Finals appearance in [1981](/wiki/1981_NBA_Finals), the Rockets entered a brief rebuilding period. Long-time Rockets [Calvin Murphy](/wiki/Calvin_Murphy) and [Rudy Tomjanovich](/wiki/Rudy_Tomjanovich) retired, while [Mike Dunleavy, Sr.](/wiki/Mike_Dunleavy,_Sr.), [Bill Willoughby](/wiki/Bill_Willoughby), [Tom Henderson](/wiki/Tom_Henderson_(basketball)) and [Billy Paultz](/wiki/Billy_Paultz) moved on to different teams. But the biggest move came during the 1982 offseason, when the Rockets traded [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone) to the [1983](/wiki/1983_NBA_Finals) champions [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Philadelphia_76ers_season). The loss of Malone sent the Rockets to a league-worst 14–68 record in the [1982–83 season](/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_Houston_Rockets_season), after which the Rockets were awarded the top pick of the [1983 NBA draft](/wiki/1983_NBA_draft) and selected [Ralph Sampson](/wiki/Ralph_Sampson).

After a 29-win season in [1984](/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_Houston_Rockets_season), the Rockets were once again rewarded with the top pick in the [1984 NBA draft](/wiki/1984_NBA_draft). They selected another center in [Akeem Olajuwon](/wiki/Hakeem_Olajuwon), and paired alongside Sampson, they were dubbed as the "Twin Towers". Houston also added some valuable role players to complement the duo and holdovers [Allen Leavell](/wiki/Allen_Leavell) and [Robert Reid](/wiki/Robert_Reid_(basketball)), acquiring [Rodney McCray](/wiki/Rodney_McCray_(basketball)), [Lewis Lloyd](/wiki/Lewis_Lloyd), [Craig Ehlo](/wiki/Craig_Ehlo), [Mitchell Wiggins](/wiki/Mitchell_Wiggins) and [Jim Petersen](/wiki/Jim_Petersen).

Under third-year head coach [Bill Fitch](/wiki/Bill_Fitch) (the head coach of the [1980–81 Celtics](/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_Boston_Celtics_season) championship team), the Rockets posted a 51–31 record and won the [Midwest Division](/wiki/Midwest_Division_(NBA)) title. In the first round, they swept the [Sacramento Kings](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Sacramento_Kings_season), then eliminated the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Denver_Nuggets_season) in six games during the second round. In the conference finals, they were matched up against the defending champion [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season), and after losing Game 1, the Rockets stunned the Lakers by winning the final four games, highlighted by a series-clinching buzzer beater by Sampson in Game 5.

## The Finals

The [Larry Bird](/wiki/Larry_Bird)–led Celtics defeated the Rockets again 4 games to 2 in the 1986 NBA Finals. The Celtics won the first two games at the [Boston Garden](/wiki/Boston_Garden), where they had gone 40–1 during the [regular season](/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_NBA_season). The Rockets had been almost as good at home during the regular season, and they defeated the Celtics 106–104 in game three. Game 4 was a tense battle at the [Summit](/wiki/Compaq_Center_(Houston)), which the Celtics won 106–103, with [Bill Walton](/wiki/Bill_Walton) coming off the bench for a tired [Robert Parish](/wiki/Robert_Parish) to score a crucial basket. The infamous fifth game featured the signature moment of the series, when 7'4" [Ralph Sampson](/wiki/Ralph_Sampson) ignited a brawl with [Jerry Sichting](/wiki/Jerry_Sichting), a player 15 inches (380 mm) shorter than Sampson, leading to his ejection. While [Jim Petersen](/wiki/Jim_Petersen) led the Rockets to a decisive victory, Sampson's actions motivated the Celtics to end the series in six. Bird dismantled the young Rockets in game 6; the raucous Garden crowd booed every time Sampson touched the ball. The Celtics eliminated the Rockets 114–97 in a game that wasn't as close as the score would indicate.

With backup forward [Scott Wedman](/wiki/Scott_Wedman) sidelined due to a wrist injury, Bird got very little rest during the six-game series, logging 269 out of a possible 288 minutes of floor time. Bird was named the Finals' MVP for that year, averaging 24 points, 9.7 rebounds, 9.7 assists, and 2.7 steals per game for the series. The Celtics' victory capped off a 29-year period that had seen them win 16 championships and it was their last championship until [2008](/wiki/2008_NBA_Finals).

The game was highlighted by Ralph Sampson's ejection early in the second quarter. With 9:40 remaining in the second, Sampson threw punches at the Celtics' reserve guard [Jerry Sichting](/wiki/Jerry_Sichting), 16 inches shorter than Sampson, leading to his ejection while the benches were cleared. The Rockets were leading 34–33 at the time of the brawl, and would lead by as many as 25 points in the second half to score a lopsided victory. Akeem Olajuwon scored 32 points while blocking 8 shots. Though Kevin McHale scored 33, Larry Bird was held to only 17 points, ultimately leading to one of the worst losses suffered by the Celtics that season.

As of 2023, this remained the last NBA Finals game to be played by the Celtics in a state other than [Massachusetts](/wiki/Massachusetts) or [California](/wiki/California). The next time the Celtics played an NBA Finals game outside those two states also occurred in Texas, during [Game Three of the 2024 NBA Finals against the Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/2024_NBA_Finals).

Larry Bird recorded a triple-double of 29 points, 11 rebounds and 12 assists to pace a lopsided Boston win that clinched their 16th NBA championship. Kevin McHale added 29 points, 10 rebounds and four blocks. Olajuwon paced the Rockets with 21 points and 10 rebounds, but Ralph Sampson was held to only eight points on 4-for-12 shooting, visibly distracted by an angry Boston Garden crowd in the aftermath of Game 5. The Celtics led by as much as 30 in the fourth to put away the Rockets.

Following the conclusion of the 1986 NBA Finals, a video documentary of the 1985–86 NBA season, known as "Sweet Sixteen", was released. David Perry was the narrator after [Dick Stockton](/wiki/Dick_Stockton) narrated the last three NBA season documentaries.

This would be the city of Boston's last professional sports championship until 2002 when the [New England Patriots](/wiki/2001_New_England_Patriots_season) won [Super Bowl XXXVI](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVI).

## Aftermath

The [1986–87](/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Boston_Celtics_season) version of the Celtics were still a great team, but the death of #2 overall pick [Len Bias](/wiki/Len_Bias) less than two days after the [1986 NBA draft](/wiki/1986_NBA_draft), and nagging injuries loomed heavily over their season. [Kevin McHale](/wiki/Kevin_McHale_(basketball)) broke his foot in March and the team was never the same. McHale kept playing on the foot despite being told not to by doctors. The battered and fatigued Celtics – with not just McHale, but also [Parish](/wiki/Robert_Parish) (two sprained ankles), [Ainge](/wiki/Danny_Ainge) (leg injury) and [Walton](/wiki/Bill_Walton) (broken foot) playing hurt – lost to the [Lakers](/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) in six games in the [1987 NBA Finals](/wiki/1987_NBA_Finals). Afterwards, the Celtics entered a rare and unusual slump for the next two decades, and would not win the championship again until [2008](/wiki/2008_NBA_Finals), in which the Celtics defeated the Lakers in six games.

Years afterward, *[The Boston Globe](/wiki/The_Boston_Globe)* sportswriter [Bob Ryan](/wiki/Bob_Ryan) would always consider this a win by proxy for the Celtics in their [rivalry](/wiki/Celtics%E2%80%93Lakers_rivalry) in the 1980s with the Lakers, as the Rockets had eliminated the Lakers in the Western Conference Finals in a convincing five games. Ryan would also say, "I believe the 1987 Lakers and the 1986 Celtics was the greatest series that never was".

As of 2026, this is the last time the Rockets lost in the NBA Finals. With a revamped roster around [Akeem Olajuwon](/wiki/Hakeem_Olajuwon), they would return to the Finals in [1994](/wiki/1994_NBA_Finals) and defeated the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks) in seven games for their first championship. Then, the Rockets would repeat as champions [the following season](/wiki/1995_NBA_Finals), sweeping the [Shaquille O'Neal](/wiki/Shaquille_O%27Neal)-led [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic).

The 1986 NBA Finals was the second of three "[Big Four](/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada)" championship rounds involving Boston-based sports teams to take place in the 1986 calendar year. In January, the [New England Patriots](/wiki/1985_New_England_Patriots_season) lost to the [Chicago Bears](/wiki/1985_Chicago_Bears_season) 46–10 in [Super Bowl XX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XX). Then in October, the [Boston Red Sox](/wiki/1986_Boston_Red_Sox_season) lost in seven games to the [New York Mets](/wiki/1986_New_York_Mets_season) in the [World Series](/wiki/1986_World_Series). The Mets' prior victory in the [National League Championship Series](/wiki/1986_National_League_Championship_Series) against the [Houston Astros](/wiki/1986_Houston_Astros_season) also prevented a second championship series in a calendar year between teams from Boston and Houston.

Kevin McHale would later coach the Rockets from 2011 to 2015.

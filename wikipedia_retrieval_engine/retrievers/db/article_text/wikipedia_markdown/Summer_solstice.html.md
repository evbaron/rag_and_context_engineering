The **summer solstice** or **estival solstice** occurs when one of [Earth](/wiki/Earth)'s [poles](/wiki/Geographical_pole) has its maximum tilt toward the [Sun](/wiki/Sun). It happens twice yearly, once in each [hemisphere](/wiki/Hemispheres_of_Earth) ([Northern](/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere) and [Southern](/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere)). The summer solstice is the day with the longest [period of daylight](/wiki/Daytime) and shortest night of the year in that hemisphere, when the sun is at its [highest position](/wiki/Culmination) in the sky. At either [pole](/wiki/Geographical_pole) there is [continuous daylight](/wiki/Midnight_sun) at the time of its summer solstice. The opposite event is the [winter solstice](/wiki/Winter_solstice).

The summer solstice occurs during the hemisphere's [summer](/wiki/Summer). In the [Northern Hemisphere](/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), this is the [June solstice](/wiki/June_solstice) (20 or 21 June) and in the [Southern Hemisphere](/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere), this is the [December solstice](/wiki/December_solstice) (21 or 22 December). Since prehistory, the summer solstice has been a significant time of year in many cultures, and has been marked by festivals and rituals. Traditionally, in [temperate](/wiki/Temperate_climate) regions (especially Europe), the summer solstice is seen as the middle of summer and referred to as "[midsummer](/wiki/Midsummer)", although today in some countries and calendars it is seen as summer's beginning.

On the summer solstice, Earth's maximum [axial tilt](/wiki/Axial_tilt) toward the Sun is 23.44°. Likewise, the Sun's [declination](/wiki/Declination) from the [celestial equator](/wiki/Celestial_equator) is 23.44°. In areas outside the [tropics](/wiki/Tropics), the sun reaches its highest [elevation angle](/wiki/Solar_elevation_angle) at [solar noon](/wiki/Solar_noon) on the summer solstice.

Diagram of Earth's seasons as seen from the north. Far left: summer [solstice](/wiki/Solstice) for the Northern Hemisphere. Front right: summer solstice for the Southern Hemisphere.

Although the summer solstice is the longest day of the year for that hemisphere, the dates of earliest sunrise and latest sunset vary by a few days. This is because Earth orbits the Sun in an ellipse, and its orbital speed varies slightly during the year.

## Culture

Main article: [Midsummer](/wiki/Midsummer)

The solstice being celebrated at [Stonehenge](/wiki/Stonehenge) in England

There is evidence that the summer solstice has been culturally important since the [Neolithic](/wiki/Neolithic) era. [Many ancient monuments](/wiki/List_of_archaeoastronomical_sites_by_country) in Europe especially, as well as parts of the Middle East, Asia and the Americas, are aligned with the sunrise or sunset on the summer solstice (see [archaeoastronomy](/wiki/Archaeoastronomy)). The significance of the summer solstice has varied among cultures, but most recognize the event in some way with [holidays](/wiki/Holiday), [festivals](/wiki/Festival), and [rituals](/wiki/Ritual) around that time with themes of fertility.

In the [Julian calendar](/wiki/Julian_calendar) of the [Roman Empire](/wiki/Roman_Empire), the date of the summer solstice was 24 June, and [Marcus Terentius Varro](/wiki/Marcus_Terentius_Varro) wrote in the 1st century BC that the Romans saw this as the middle of summer. The Julian calendar had a flaw in that the solstices and equinoxes gradually fell on earlier dates. At the [First Council of Nicaea](/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea) (325), the Christian Church set the date of the spring equinox to 21 March, for the purpose of [calculating Easter](/wiki/Date_of_Easter). This also brought the date of the summer solstice forward to 20 June.

The summer solstice was seen as the middle of summer in [Anglo-Saxon England](/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England) and called '[midsummer](/wiki/Midsummer)'. Some [Anglo-Saxon calendars](/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_calendar) placed midsummer on the older date of 24 June while others place it on 20 June.

Elsewhere in northern Europe, traditionally the summer solstice is also seen as the middle of summer and it was reckoned as the night of 23–24 June. The summer solstice continues to be seen as the middle of summer in many European cultures, but in some cultures or calendars it is seen as summer's beginning. In [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden), midsummer is one of the year's major holidays when the country closes down as much as during Christmas.

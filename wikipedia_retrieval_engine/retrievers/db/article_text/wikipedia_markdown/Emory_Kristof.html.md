**Emory Kristof** (November 19, 1942 – February 6, 2023) was an American photographer. He was on the expedition that discovered the [Titanic](/wiki/Titanic). His work has been published in *[National Geographic Magazine](/wiki/National_Geographic_Magazine)* and elsewhere.

## Life and career

Kristof was born in [Laurel, Maryland](/wiki/Laurel,_Maryland), on November 19, 1942.

Kristof participated in multiple undersea expeditions with Canadian explorers [Joseph MacInnis](/wiki/Joseph_B._MacInnis) and [Phil Nuytten](/wiki/Phil_Nuytten), including the exploration of the [*Breadalbane*](/wiki/Breadalbane_(ship)), the world's northernmost known shipwreck, and the 1995 expedition to recover the bell from the wreck of the [SS *Edmund Fitzgerald*](/wiki/SS_Edmund_Fitzgerald). Kristof also accompanied MacInnis and Russian explorer [Anatoly Sagalevich](/wiki/Anatoly_Sagalevich) on a descent 16,400 feet into [Kings Trough](/wiki/Kings_Trough) in the eastern North Atlantic aboard the submersible [*Mir 1*](/wiki/MIR_(submersible)), and on the expedition which made the [IMAX](/wiki/IMAX) film *[Titanica](/wiki/Titanica)*.

Kristof served as Supervising Producer of 2003 [IMAX](/wiki/IMAX) documentary, [Volcanoes of the Deep Sea](/wiki/Volcanoes_of_the_Deep_Sea), about the ecosystems surrounding [hydrothermal vents](/wiki/Hydrothermal_vent).

Kristof died [Northfield, Massachusetts](/wiki/Northfield,_Massachusetts), on February 6, 2023, at the age of 80.

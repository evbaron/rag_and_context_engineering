# List of MLS Cup finals

[Sporting Kansas City](/wiki/Sporting_Kansas_City), winners of [MLS Cup 2013](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2013), are hosted by President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) at the [White House](/wiki/White_House).

The [MLS Cup](/wiki/MLS_Cup) is the annual championship match of [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) (MLS), the top-level men's [soccer](/wiki/Association_football) league for the United States and Canada. The match marks the conclusion of the [MLS Cup playoffs](/wiki/MLS_Cup_playoffs), a five-round knockout competition contested by the top nine teams from each of the league's two conferences. The playoffs tournament is organized by the league at the end of the regular season in a format which is similar to other [major professional sports leagues](/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) in the United States and Canada, but unlike most soccer leagues. The league also awards the [Supporters' Shield](/wiki/Supporters%27_Shield) to teams that have the most [points](/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win) during the regular season. Both the MLS Cup champion and Supporters' Shield winner qualify for the [CONCACAF Champions Cup](/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup), contested by the champions of [CONCACAF](/wiki/CONCACAF) leagues in North America, Central America, and the Caribbean. The MLS Cup champion also qualifies for the [Campeones Cup](/wiki/Campeones_Cup), a friendly held since 2018 against the winners of the Mexican [Campeón de Campeones](/wiki/Campe%C3%B3n_de_Campeones) from [Liga MX](/wiki/Liga_MX).

First contested in 1996, the MLS Cup was originally hosted by a predetermined [neutral site](/wiki/Neutral_venue) selected by the league before the regular season. Since the [2012 edition](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2012), the match has been hosted by the remaining team with the highest regular season standing. The final, originally contested in October, was moved to November and later December as the length of the regular season and playoffs were extended by the league. The playoffs originally allowed for lower-ranked seeds, known as [wild cards](/wiki/Wild_card_(sports)), to be placed into different sides of the bracket regardless of their actual conference. As a result, several MLS Cups have featured two teams from the same conference.

[Inter Miami CF](/wiki/Inter_Miami_CF) are the reigning cup-holders, having defeated [Vancouver Whitecaps FC](/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC) in the [2025 final](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2025) for their first title. The [LA Galaxy](/wiki/LA_Galaxy) hold the record for most MLS Cup titles, having won six times in ten appearances. The championship has been won by the same team in two or more consecutive years on three occasions, and the match has featured consecutive sets of finalists on three occasions. Five finals have featured two teams participating as finalists for the first time. Nine teams have also won "[the double](/wiki/Double_(association_football))", claiming the MLS Cup and either the Supporters' Shield, the [U.S. Open Cup](/wiki/U.S._Open_Cup), or the [Canadian Championship](/wiki/Canadian_Championship) during the same season; only Toronto FC has won a [treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football)), having achieved it in 2017. [Landon Donovan](/wiki/Landon_Donovan) has played in seven MLS Cup finals and totaled 726 minutes—both competition records.

The highest recorded attendance for the MLS Cup was set in the [2018 final](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2018), with 73,019 spectators at [Mercedes-Benz Stadium](/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_Stadium) in [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta), Georgia. From 1996 to 2008, the English broadcast of the MLS Cup was carried in the United States on terrestrial network [ABC](/wiki/ESPN_on_ABC); it was moved to sister channel [ESPN](/wiki/ESPN) for the following seven editions. From 2015 to 2022, ESPN and [Fox](/wiki/Fox_Sports_(United_States)) held rights to alternating editions of the cup; the 2019 cup, originally slated to be broadcast on ESPN, was moved to ABC. The Spanish language rights for the MLS Cup in the U.S. were awarded to [Univision](/wiki/Univision) in 2007 and the match was aired on their various networks until 2023. The U.S. linear television rights beginning in 2023 are held by Fox in English and [Fox Deportes](/wiki/Fox_Deportes) in Spanish; the MLS Cup final will air on those channels as well as [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B)'s [MLS Season Pass](/wiki/MLS_Season_Pass) streaming service worldwide. In Canada, the MLS Cup has been broadcast in English by [TSN](/wiki/The_Sports_Network) since 2011 and in French by [TVA Sports](/wiki/TVA_Sports) since 2017. The largest television audience for an MLS Cup broadcast was the [2016 final](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2016), which drew 3.5 million viewers in the United States and Canada.

## Results by team

As of 2025, 33 teams have played in the league, 21 of whom have appeared in an MLS Cup final, including 16 that have won a championship. The [LA Galaxy](/wiki/LA_Galaxy) has appeared at and won the MLS Cup the most times, with six championships in ten appearances. The [New England Revolution](/wiki/New_England_Revolution) has appeared five times as a finalist, but has not won an MLS Cup. The [Chicago Fire](/wiki/Chicago_Fire_FC) won the MLS Cup in their inaugural season in [1998](/wiki/1998_Major_League_Soccer_season); the only previous professional American soccer team to win a league championship in their inaugural season was the [Philadelphia Atoms](/wiki/Philadelphia_Atoms) in the 1973 [NASL](/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_(1968%E2%80%931984)) season.

## Stadiums

The [Dignity Health Sports Park](/wiki/Dignity_Health_Sports_Park), home of the [LA Galaxy](/wiki/LA_Galaxy), has hosted seven editions of the MLS Cup

From 1996 to 2011, the MLS Cup was hosted by a neutral site selected before the start of the season in a manner similar to the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League)'s [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl) championship. Three teams advanced to the final after being named as hosts: [D.C. United](/wiki/D.C._United) in 1997, the [New England Revolution](/wiki/New_England_Revolution) in 2002, and the [LA Galaxy](/wiki/LA_Galaxy) in 2011. Since the [2012 edition](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2012), the match has been hosted by the finalist with the highest regular season standing. Several teams with smaller or inadequate stadiums have also considered using larger [American football](/wiki/American_football) stadiums to host the MLS Cup, but all post-2012 editions have been played at regular MLS venues. The move towards a non-neutral venue was deemed a risk due to the cold November and December weather in some northern cities, as well as the lack of adequate stadiums for some teams.

As of 2023, the MLS Cup has been hosted in 14 stadiums across 10 metropolitan areas in the United States and Canada. [Dignity Health Sports Park](/wiki/Dignity_Health_Sports_Park), previously named the Home Depot Center and StubHub Center, in [Carson, California](/wiki/Carson,_California), has hosted the MLS Cup the most times of any venue, with eight editions between 2003 and 2024. The [Los Angeles metropolitan area](/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area) has hosted the MLS Cup nine times at three venues: the [Rose Bowl](/wiki/Rose_Bowl_(stadium)), Dignity Health Sports Park, and [Banc of California Stadium](/wiki/Banc_of_California_Stadium). The largest attendance for an MLS Cup final was the [2018 edition](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2018) at [Mercedes-Benz Stadium](/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_Stadium) in [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta), Georgia, with 73,019 spectators; the smallest was in [2020](/wiki/MLS_Cup_2020) at [Mapfre Stadium](/wiki/Mapfre_Stadium) in [Columbus, Ohio](/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio), with only 1,500 spectators allowed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). Three editions have been hosted outside the United States, all at [BMO Field](/wiki/BMO_Field) in [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto), Canada. As of 2024, 11 of the 14 editions under the non-neutral venue format have been won by the host team.

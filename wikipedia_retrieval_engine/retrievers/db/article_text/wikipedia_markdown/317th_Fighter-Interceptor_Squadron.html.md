The **317th Fighter Interceptor Squadron** is an inactive [United States Air Force](/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) unit, last assigned to [Aerospace Defense Command](/wiki/Aerospace_Defense_Command), at [Elmendorf Air Force Base](/wiki/Elmendorf_Air_Force_Base), Alaska, where it was inactivated on 31 December 1969.

The [squadron](/wiki/Squadron_(aviation)) was first activated as the **317th Fighter Squadron** during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). After training in the United States, it deployed to North Africa. In combat operations in the [Mediterranean Theater of Operations](/wiki/Mediterranean_Theater_of_Operations). It was withdrawn from combat from September to December 1943 while it equipped with different aircraft and moved from Africa to Italy. It earned two [Distinguished Unit Citations](/wiki/Distinguished_Unit_Citation) before returning to the United States for inactivation.

The squadron returned to service as an [air defense](/wiki/Air_defense) unit in 1947, serving in that role until inactivated in 1969.

## History

### World War II

The [squadron](/wiki/Squadron_(aviation)) was first organized at [Mitchel Field](/wiki/Mitchel_Field), New York on 3 August 1942 as one of the three original squadrons of the [325th Fighter Group](/wiki/325th_Fighter_Group), but moved the same day to [Hillsgrove Army Air Field](/wiki/Hillsgrove_Army_Air_Field), Rhode Island. It equipped with [Curtiss P-40 Warhawks](/wiki/Curtiss_P-40_Warhawk) and trained for combat at Hillsgrove until late January 1943, when it began to deploy overseas.

P-40s at Tafaraoui Airfield

The squadron arrived in the [Mediterranean Theater of Operations](/wiki/Mediterranean_Theater_of_Operations) in February and was established at its first combat station, [Tafaraoui Airfield](/wiki/Tafaraoui_Airfield), Algeria by 28 February 1943. It flew its first combat mission on 17 April. It escorted [medium bombers](/wiki/Medium_bomber). It flew [strafing](/wiki/Strafing) missions and flew sweeps over the Mediterranean Sea from bases in Algeria and Tunisia. The squadron participated in the defeat of [Axis](/wiki/Axis_powers) forces in Tunisia. It participated in the reduction of [Pantelleria](/wiki/Pantelleria) and in [Operation Husky](/wiki/Operation_Husky), the invasion and conquest of Sicily. On 30 July, the 325th Group used diversionary tactics to lure a superior number of enemy planes into the air over Sardinia, destroying more than half of them. The squadron was awarded its first [Distinguished Unit Citation](/wiki/Distinguished_Unit_Citation) for this action. In late September 1943, the squadron was withdrawn from combat to convert to [Republic P-47 Thunderbolts](/wiki/Republic_P-47_Thunderbolt) and prepare to move to the Italian peninsula.

Squadron P-47

By early December 1943, the squadron began to operate its Thunderbolts from [Foggia Airfield](/wiki/Foggia_Airfield), Italy, flying its first mission with the new plane on 14 December. However, it only operated the P-47 for a short period, converting to [North American P-51 Mustangs](/wiki/North_American_P-51_Mustang) in March 1944, and moving to [Lesina Airfield](/wiki/Lesina_Airfield), Italy on the 29th of the month. However, on 30 January it flew its "T-Bolts" more than 300 miles at very low altitude to make a surprise attack on German [interceptors](/wiki/Interceptor_aircraft) defending airdromes near [Villorba](/wiki/Villorba). The severe losses it inflicted on the defending forces enabled [heavy bombers](/wiki/Heavy_bomber) to attack vital targets in the area without encountering serious opposition. This action resulted in the second award of the Distinguished Unit Citation to the squadron.

Squadron P-51D Mustangs taxi before a combat mission

It escorted the heavy bombers of [Fifteenth Air Force](/wiki/Fifteenth_Air_Force) on long range missions against the [Daimler Benz](/wiki/Daimler_Benz) factory in [Berlin](/wiki/Berlin), the [Messerschmitt](/wiki/Messerschmitt) factory in [Regensburg](/wiki/Regensburg) and [oil refineries](/wiki/Oil_refineries) near [Vienna](/wiki/Vienna). It also flew escort for attacks on other targets, such as [airfields](/wiki/Airfield) and [marshalling yards](/wiki/Marshalling_yard) and [lines of communication](/wiki/Lines_of_communication) in Italy, France, Germany, Czechoslovakia, Austria, Hungary, Yugoslavia and Romania. It also protected [reconnaissance aircraft](/wiki/Aerial_reconnaissance) and strafed trains, vehicles and airfields. The squadron continued operations until May 1945. The 317th was credited with the destruction of 209 enemy aircraft in air to air combat. After [V-E Day](/wiki/V-E_Day), it moved to [Vincenzo Airfield](/wiki/Vincenzo_Airfield), Italy, remaining there until October, when it returned to the United States and was inactivated at [Camp Kilmer](/wiki/Camp_Kilmer), New Jersey on 28 October.

### Air Defense Command

317th Fighter-All Weather Squadron P-82F Twin Mustang

In May 1947, the squadron was reactivated at [Mitchel Field](/wiki/Mitchel_Field), New York and equipped with [Northrop P-61 Black Widows](/wiki/Northrop_P-61_Black_Widow). it moved to [Moses Lake Air Force Base](/wiki/Moses_Lake_Air_Force_Base), Washington, the squadron was re-equipped with the new [North American F-82 Twin Mustang](/wiki/North_American_F-82_Twin_Mustang) in October 1948. The squadron was assigned for the defense of the [Hanford Nuclear Reservation](/wiki/Hanford_Nuclear_Reservation) in Eastern Washington. One month later it was reassigned to [Hamilton Air Force Base](/wiki/Hamilton_Air_Force_Base), California, and in April 1950, returned to [McChord Air Force Base](/wiki/McChord_Air_Force_Base), Washington.

317th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron F-94 Starfire

In 1951. the squadron was redesignated as the **317th Fighter Interceptor Squadron**, a component of the [25th Air Division](/wiki/25th_Air_Division), [Air Defense Command](/wiki/Air_Defense_Command), During the period between 1947 and 1956. the 317th flew the F-82. the [Lockheed F-94 Starfire](/wiki/Lockheed_F-94_Starfire), and the [North American F-86D Sabre](/wiki/North_American_F-86D_Sabre) interceptor. In 1955 and 1956, at [Vincent Air Force Base](/wiki/Vincent_Air_Force_Base), Yuma, Arizona, flying the F-86D, the 317th engaged in airborne rocket competition, breaking all records previously set. In August 1956 the squadron was selected as the best air defense unit in the Air Force and was awarded the Hughes Trophy, In addition, the squadron received its first Outstanding Unit Award for high combat readiness for the period of 1953 through 1956. In November 1956, the 317th began transitioning into the [Convair F-102 Delta Dagger](/wiki/Convair_F-102_Delta_Dagger).

### Alaskan Air Command

F-102 Delta Dagger from the 317th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron.

In November 1956, the 317th began transitioning into the F-102A Delta Dagger, becoming the second squadron to receive the new delta-winged supersonic aircraft. In August 1957, the 317th was reassigned in force, to [Elmendorf Air Force Base](/wiki/Elmendorf_Air_Force_Base), Alaska, where it assumed air defense mission of the southern portion of [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska) and the northwestern approaches to the United States.

In the summer of 1960. the [449th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron](/wiki/449th_Fighter-Interceptor_Squadron) was inactivated and [Ladd Air Force Base](/wiki/Ladd_Air_Force_Base) was closed. leaving the entire Alaska air defense mission to the 317th. To carry out this mission, the squadron was authorized 40 aircraft and 60 pilots. The squadron received its second [Air Force Outstanding Unit Award](/wiki/Air_Force_Outstanding_Unit_Award) for maintaining full combat readiness during and after the [1964 Alaska earthquake](/wiki/1964_Alaska_earthquake) on 27 March 1964.

In the summer of 1965, the 317th was reduced to 29 aircraft and the manning reduced accordingly, but the air defense mission remained the same. In October 1965, the squadron was awarded the Hughes Trophy for the second time, and a third Air Force Outstanding Unit Award for combat readiness.

In December 1969, due to budget reductions, the 317th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron was inactivated.

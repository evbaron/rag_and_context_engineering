The **Geibi Line** (芸備線, *Geibi-sen*) is a railway line operated by [West Japan Railway Company](/wiki/West_Japan_Railway_Company) (JR West) in the mountainous area of the [Chūgoku region](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABgoku_region) in [Japan](/wiki/Japan). It begins at [Bitchū-Kōjiro Station](/wiki/Bitch%C5%AB-K%C5%8Djiro_Station) on the west side of [Niimi](/wiki/Niimi,_Okayama), [Okayama Prefecture](/wiki/Okayama_Prefecture), connecting through [Miyoshi Station](/wiki/Miyoshi_Station_(Hiroshima)) in [Miyoshi](/wiki/Miyoshi,_Hiroshima), [Hiroshima Prefecture](/wiki/Hiroshima_Prefecture), and terminating at [Hiroshima Station](/wiki/Hiroshima_Station) in [Hiroshima](/wiki/Hiroshima). In addition to the [Chūgoku Expressway](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABgoku_Expressway), the Geibi Line is considered the main commuter and local rail line covering the route between northern Hiroshima Prefecture and the city of Hiroshima. The name of the line refers to the ancient [provinces](/wiki/Provinces_of_Japan) of [Aki](/wiki/Aki_Province) (安**芸**) (in Hiroshima Prefecture) and [Bitchū](/wiki/Bitch%C5%AB_Province) (**備**中) (in Okayama Prefecture), which the line connects.

Since 2007, the [ICOCA](/wiki/ICOCA) card can be used in all stations between Hiroshima Station and Karuga Station (stations in the [Hiroshima City Network](/wiki/Hiroshima_City_Network)).

The majority of the line was out of service after a bridge was destroyed in the [2018 Japan floods](/wiki/2018_Japan_floods). The entire line reopened in October 2019.

The line is one of the least used and least profitable in the JR West network, with average daily ridership of just 13 people (slightly more than two per train) on its least trafficked segment. The segment between Bitchu-Kojiro and Bingo-Yawata runs at an annual loss of JPY 700 million, while the segment between Tojo and Bingo-Shobara is both circuitous and speed-restricted, making it less attractive than bus service. However, local authorities along the route have refused to discuss alternative transportation options with JR West.

## History

The Geibi Line consists of the section opened by the Geibi Railway, which connected Hiroshima Station and Bingo-Shōbara, the Shōbara Line between Bingo-Shōbara and Bingo-Ochiai which was partly built by the Geibi Railway and then nationalised and extended by the Japanese Government Railways (JGR) and the Sanshin Line built by the JGR between Onuka and Bitchū-Kōjiro. In 1936, the line between Hiroshima and Bitchū-Kōjiro was completed, and the Geibi Railway was nationalised the following year, bringing the entire line under the control of the JGR. Opening dates for individual sections are given below.

### Geibi Railway

- 18 April 1915: The Geibi Railway opened between Higashi Hiroshima (different from the current [Higashi-Hiroshima Station](/wiki/Higashi-Hiroshima_Station)) and [Shiwachi](/wiki/Shiwachi_Station).
- 1 June 1915: The line is extended from Shiwachi to Miyoshi (the current [Nishi Miyoshi Station](/wiki/Nishi_Miyoshi_Station)).
- 15 April 1916: [Hesaka Station](/wiki/Hesaka_Station) opens.
- 15 July 1920: The line between [Hiroshima](/wiki/Hiroshima_Station) and Higashi Hiroshima opens. [JNR](/wiki/Japanese_National_Railways) Hiroshima Station opens.
- 7 June 1922: The line between Miyoshi (the current Nishi Miyoshi Station) and Shiomachi (the current [Kamisugi Station](/wiki/Kamisugi_Station_(Hiroshima))) opens.
- 8 December 1923: The line between Shiomachi (the current Kamisugi Station) and [Bingo-Shōbara](/wiki/Bingo-Sh%C5%8Dbara_Station) opens.
- 20 September 1924: [Nakafukawa Station](/wiki/Nakafukawa_Station) and [Yamanouchi Station](/wiki/Yamanouchi_Station_(Hiroshima)) open.
- 1 February 1925: Wadamura Station becomes [Shimowachi Station](/wiki/Shimowachi_Station).
- 20 March 1929: [Yaga Station](/wiki/Yaga_Station_(Hiroshima)) and [Kamifukawa Station](/wiki/Kamifukawa_Station) open.
- 1 January 1930: Shirakiyamaguchi Station, Mita Yoshinaga Station, and Tōkaichi Station open.
- 22 April 1930: Takō Station (the current [Shiomachi Station](/wiki/Shiomachi_Station)) opens.
- 25 April 1930: Mikkaichi Station opens.

### Shōbara Line

- 1 June 1933: Tōkaichi Station is renamed Bingo-Tōkaichi Station and Mikkaichi Station is renamed Bingo-Mikkaichi Station. The Geibi Railway line between Bingo-Tōkaichi Station (the current [Miyoshi Station](/wiki/Miyoshi_Station_(Hiroshima))) and Bingo-Shōbara Station is nationalized and renamed the Shōbara Line.
- 1 January 1934: Shiomachi Station is renamed [Kamisugi Station](/wiki/Kamisugi_Station_(Hiroshima)), and Takō Station is renamed [Shiomachi Station](/wiki/Shiomachi_Station).
- 15 March 1934: The line between Bingo-Shōbara Station and [Bingo-Saijō Station](/wiki/Bingo-Saij%C5%8D_Station) opens.
- 20 December 1935: The line between Bingo-Saijō Station and [Bingo-Ochiai Station](/wiki/Bingo-Ochiai_Station) opened.

### Sanshin Line

- 10 February 1930: The Sanshin Line opens between [Bitchū-Kōjiro Station](/wiki/Bitch%C5%AB-K%C5%8Djiro_Station) and [Yagami Station](/wiki/Yagami_Station).
- 25 November 1930: The line opens between Yagami Station and [Tōjō Station](/wiki/T%C5%8Dj%C5%8D_Station_(Hiroshima)).
- 15 June 1935: The line between Tōjō Station and [Onuka Station](/wiki/Onuka_Station) opens.
- 10 October 1936: The line between Onuka Station and Bingo-Ochia Station opens. The Shōbara Line is absorbed into the Sanshin Line, which now includes everything between Bitchū-Kōjiro Station and Bingo-Tōkaichi Station.
- 21 November 1936: [Dōgoyama Station](/wiki/D%C5%8Dgoyama_Station) opens.

### Geibi Line

- 1 July 1937: The Geibi Railway line between [Hiroshima Station](/wiki/Hiroshima_Station) and Bingo-Tōkaichi Station is nationalized, and the Sanshin Line is absorbed into the Geibi Line. Kawatachi Station is renamed [Kamikawatachi Station](/wiki/Kamikawatachi_Station), Mita Yoshinaga Station is renamed [Kamimita Station](/wiki/Kamimita_Station), Shirakiyamaguchi Station is renamed [Shirakiyama Station](/wiki/Shirakiyama_Station), and Yaguchi Station is renamed [Akiyaguchi Station](/wiki/Akiyaguchi_Station).
- 10 August 1941: [Kamifukawa Station](/wiki/Kamifukawa_Station) and [Yaga Station](/wiki/Yaga_Station_(Hiroshima)) close.
- 28 October 1942: Yaga Station closes, and a [signal box](/wiki/Signal_box) is installed.
- 2 April 1943: Yaga Station reopens.
- 10 August 1948: Kamifukawa Station reopens.
- 1 February 1952: [Hirako Station](/wiki/Hirako_Station) opens.
- 1 October 1953: [Ichioka Station](/wiki/Ichioka_Station) opens.
- 10 November 1954: The former Miyoshi Station is renamed [Nishi Miyoshi Station](/wiki/Nishi_Miyoshi_Station).
- 10 December 1954: Bingo-Tōkaichi Station is renamed [Miyoshi Station](/wiki/Miyoshi_Station_(Hiroshima)).
- 20 July 1955: [Uchina Station](/wiki/Uchina_Station) opens.
- 11 November 1955: The *Chidori* service begins.
- 20 December 1956: Bingo-Kumano Station is renamed [Hibayama Station](/wiki/Hibayama_Station).
- 13 April 1959: The *Chidori* is upgraded to a local express train.
- 15 March 1962: The *Taishaku* service begins.
- 5 March 1968: The *Taishaku* and *Chidori* services are upgraded to regular express trains.
- 1 March 1983: The line between Miyoshi Station and Hiroshima Station uses [CTC](/wiki/Centralized_traffic_control).
- 31 October 1983: The line between Bitchū-Kōjiro and Miyoshi uses CTC.
- 15 March 1985: The *Miyoshi* express service begins.
- 1 November 1986: Freight service is discontinued on the Geibi Line.
- 1 April 1987: The Geibi Line becomes part of [West Japan Railway Company](/wiki/West_Japan_Railway_Company) following privatization of [Japanese National Railways](/wiki/Japanese_National_Railways).
- 1 April 1991: *Wanman* [driver-only operation](/wiki/Driver-only_operation) commences on the line between [Niimi](/wiki/Niimi_Station) and [Miyoshi](/wiki/Miyoshi_Station_(Hiroshima)).
- 1 November 1991: The line between Miyoshi and Hiroshima is converted to *wanman* [driver-only operation](/wiki/Driver-only_operation).
- 22 March 2002: The *Chidori* and *Taishaku* express services are absorbed into the *Miyoshi* express service.
- 1 October 2003: The *Miyoshi Liner* and *Tsūkin Liner* services begin.
- 23 April 2006: The Geibi Line is moved between Kamikawatachi and Kōtachi following widening of Hiroshima Prefectural Route 37 between Hiroshima and Miyoshi.
- 19 July 2006: Services between Bingo-Ochiai and Bingo-Saijō are suspended due to storm damage of the Geibi Line. An interim bus service begins the following day.
- 1 April 2007: Train service is resumed between Bingo-Ochiai and Bingo-Saijō.
- 1 July 2007: *Miyoshi* express services are discontinued, and *Tsūkin Liner* rapid services are integrated into *Miyoshi Liner* rapid services.
- 7 July 2018: The [2018 Japan floods](/wiki/2018_Japan_floods) result in damage to the line in several places, most significantly destroying the bridge over the Misasagawa River between Shirakiyama and Karuga stations, resulting in the closure of the entire line.
- 23 July 2018: The Hiroshima – Shimofukawa section of the line is reopened. JR West advises replacement of the Misasagawa Bridge is expected to take up to a year.
- 25 August 2018: The Karuga – Shimofukawa section of the line is reopened.
- 27 August 2018: The Bitchū-Kōjiro – Tōjō section of the line is reopened.
- 31 August 2018: The Tōjō – Bingo-Ochiai section of the line is reopened.
- 4 October 2018: The Bingo-Shōbara – Miyoshi section of the line is reopened.
- 20 December 2018: The Bingo-Ochiai – Bingo-Shōbara section of the line is reopened.
- 4 April 2019: The Miyoshi – Nakamita section of the line is reopened. However, trains only operate in the morning and evening hours (Operations were suspended from 25 July to 31 August).
- 23 October 2019: The Nakamita – Karuga section of the line is reopened. The entire line reopened after 1 year and 3 months from the 2018 Japan floods.
- 9 March 2020: A KiHa 120 car derails near Bingo-Yawata station, causing a temporary closure of the line between Tōjō and Bingo-Ochiai.
- 8 June 2021: JR West initiated a work group with local communities along the [Bingo-Shōbara](/wiki/Bingo-Sh%C5%8Dbara_Station) – [Niimi](/wiki/Niimi_Station) section to assess the future of the line. According to JR West data, on average only 81 passengers use the line between [Bitchū-Kōjiro](/wiki/Bitch%C5%AB-K%C5%8Djiro_Station) and [Tōjō](/wiki/T%C5%8Dj%C5%8D_Station_(Hiroshima)), 11 between Tōjō and [Bingo-Ochiai](/wiki/Bingo-Ochiai_Station) and 215 between Bingo-Ochiai and [Miyoshi](/wiki/Miyoshi_Station_(Hiroshima)). Due to the lack of patronage, JR West is considering to abolish the aforementioned section and replace it with bus services.

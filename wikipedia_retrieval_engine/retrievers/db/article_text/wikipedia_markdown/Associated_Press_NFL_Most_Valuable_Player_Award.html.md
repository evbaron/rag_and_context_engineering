# AP NFL Most Valuable Player

## Infobox

- **Awarded for:** Most valuable player in the NFL
- **Presented by:** Associated Press
- **First award:** 1957
- **Most wins:** Peyton Manning (5)
- **Most recent:** Matthew Stafford (1)

[Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning) won the award a record five times.

The **AP NFL Most Valuable Player (MVP)** is an annual award presented by the [Associated Press](/wiki/Associated_Press) (AP) to a player in the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League) (NFL) adjudged to have been the [most valuable](/wiki/Most_valuable_player) in that year's [regular season](/wiki/NFL_regular_season). While there have been many selectors of [NFL MVPs](/wiki/NFL_MVP) in the past, today the MVP award presented by the AP is considered the *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* official NFL MVP award and the most prestigious. Since 2012, the NFL has held the annual [NFL Honors](/wiki/NFL_Honors) ceremony to recognize the winner of each year's AP MVP award, along with other AP awards, such as the [AP NFL Offensive Player of the Year](/wiki/AP_NFL_Offensive_Player_of_the_Year) and [AP NFL Defensive Player of the Year](/wiki/AP_NFL_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year). The most recent winner is quarterback [Matthew Stafford](/wiki/Matthew_Stafford) of the [Los Angeles Rams](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams).

The AP has presented an award recognizing the NFL's top player since 1957. The award is voted upon by a panel of 50 sportswriters at the end of the [regular season](/wiki/Regular_season), before the [playoffs](/wiki/Playoffs), though the results are not announced to the public until the day before the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl). The sportswriters chosen regularly follow the NFL, and remain mostly consistent from year to year. They are chosen based on expertise and are independent of the league itself. Voters for the award have included [Troy Aikman](/wiki/Troy_Aikman) of [Fox Sports](/wiki/Fox_Sports); [Cris Collinsworth](/wiki/Cris_Collinsworth) and [Tony Dungy](/wiki/Tony_Dungy) of [NBC Sports](/wiki/NBC_Sports); and [Herm Edwards](/wiki/Herm_Edwards) of [ESPN](/wiki/ESPN). Only two players in the history of the award have won it unanimously: [New England Patriots](/wiki/New_England_Patriots) quarterback [Tom Brady](/wiki/Tom_Brady) in [2010](/wiki/2010_NFL_season) and [Baltimore Ravens](/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens) quarterback [Lamar Jackson](/wiki/Lamar_Jackson) in [2019](/wiki/2019_NFL_season).

Due to voters' tendency to favor offensive positions (mostly the quarterback and sometimes the running back), the award has been overwhelmingly dominated by offensive players; of the 54 undisputed winners, 54 played an offensive position: 50 quarterbacks and 4 [running backs](/wiki/Running_back). Two defensive players have won the award: [Alan Page](/wiki/Alan_Page) in 1971 as a [defensive tackle](/wiki/Defensive_tackle), and [Lawrence Taylor](/wiki/Lawrence_Taylor) as a [linebacker](/wiki/Linebacker) in 1986. The sole [special teams](/wiki/Special_teams) player to be named AP NFL MVP was [Mark Moseley](/wiki/Mark_Moseley), who won as a [placekicker](/wiki/Placekicker) in 1982.

Thirteen awardees also won the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl) (or [NFL Championship Game](/wiki/NFL_Championship_Game) prior to 1966) in the same season. However, this did not occur from 1997 to 2021. During that span, nine AP NFL MVPs have led their team to the Super Bowl and were defeated each time. This has led to tongue-in-cheek claims in recent years that there is a "curse" preventing the awardee's team from winning the Super Bowl.

Six NFL franchises have not produced an MVP, the [New York Jets](/wiki/New_York_Jets), [Houston Texans](/wiki/Houston_Texans), [Jacksonville Jaguars](/wiki/Jacksonville_Jaguars), [Chicago/St. Louis/Phoenix/Arizona Cardinals](/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals), [New Orleans Saints](/wiki/New_Orleans_Saints), and the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers).

The [Green Bay Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) have the most overall winners with ten; if including disputed awards (see below), the Colts would be tied with ten. The Green Bay Packers also have the most unique winners with five players winning the award.

## Discrepancies

The AP has presented an award recognizing the NFL's top player since the 1957 season, although the pre-1961 awardees are recognized in the *Official NFL Record and Fact Book* as winning the AP's "NFL Most Outstanding Player" award, and the 1962 winner was recognized as the AP's "Player of the Year". The AP considers 1961 to be the first year in which it presented a "Most Valuable Player" award. Thus there are numerous inconsistencies among sources regarding each of the first four awards, and whether or not the winners are included in the overall list of AP MVP winners at all. The discrepancies include 1958's winner being either [Jim Brown](/wiki/Jim_Brown) or [Gino Marchetti](/wiki/Gino_Marchetti); the 1959 winner as [Johnny Unitas](/wiki/Johnny_Unitas) or [Charlie Conerly](/wiki/Charlie_Conerly); and whether or not [Norm Van Brocklin](/wiki/Norm_Van_Brocklin) shared the award in 1960 with [Joe Schmidt](/wiki/Joe_Schmidt_(American_football)).

## MVP Super Bowl curse

In recent years, if a player that won the MVP makes it to the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl), the MVP often loses the Super Bowl in the year they won the MVP. That includes, [Kurt Warner](/wiki/Kurt_Warner) in [2001](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVI), [Rich Gannon](/wiki/Rich_Gannon) in [2002](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVII), [Shaun Alexander](/wiki/Shaun_Alexander) in [2005](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XL), [Tom Brady](/wiki/Tom_Brady) in [2007](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLII), [Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning) in [2009](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIV) and [2013](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLVIII), [Cam Newton](/wiki/Cam_Newton) in [2015](/wiki/Super_Bowl_50), [Matt Ryan](/wiki/Matt_Ryan_(American_football)) in [2016](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LI), and [Tom Brady](/wiki/Tom_Brady) in [2017](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LII).

Eleven players have won the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl) and MVP in the same season: [Bart Starr](/wiki/Bart_Starr) in [1966](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I), [Terry Bradshaw](/wiki/Terry_Bradshaw) in [1978](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIII), [Mark Moseley](/wiki/Mark_Moseley) in [1982](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XVII), [Lawrence Taylor](/wiki/Lawrence_Taylor) in [1986](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXI), [Joe Montana](/wiki/Joe_Montana) in [1989](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIV), [Emmitt Smith](/wiki/Emmitt_Smith) in [1993](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII), [Steve Young](/wiki/Steve_Young) in [1994](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXIX), [Brett Favre](/wiki/Brett_Favre) in [1996](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXI), [Terrell Davis](/wiki/Terrell_Davis) in [1998](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIII), [Kurt Warner](/wiki/Kurt_Warner) in [1999](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXIV), and [Patrick Mahomes](/wiki/Patrick_Mahomes) in [2022](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVII). In these eleven cases, all but four regular season MVP winners were also the Super Bowl MVP for their respective games - with Moseley, Taylor, Favre and Davis not completing the duplicate MVP year. From Warner's MVP and Super Bowl win in the same year in 1999, no NFL MVP would become a Super Bowl champion in the same year for 23 years until 2022, when [Patrick Mahomes](/wiki/Patrick_Mahomes) broke the MVP Super Bowl curse at [Super Bowl LVII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVII).

## Winners

[Johnny Unitas](/wiki/Johnny_Unitas) won three AP NFL MVP awards as quarterback of the [Baltimore Colts](/wiki/Baltimore_Colts).

Quarterback [Bart Starr](/wiki/Bart_Starr) was awarded in 1966 after passing for 2,257 yards and 14 touchdowns and leading the [Green Bay Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) to [Super Bowl I](/wiki/Super_Bowl_I).

Running back [O. J. Simpson](/wiki/O._J._Simpson) became the first player to [rush for 2,000 yards](/wiki/2,000-yard_club) in a season en route to winning the 1973 award with the [Buffalo Bills](/wiki/Buffalo_Bills).

Running back [Marcus Allen](/wiki/Marcus_Allen) set an NFL record with 2,314 [yards from scrimmage](/wiki/Yards_from_scrimmage) in 1985 for the [Los Angeles Raiders](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Raiders).

Quarterback [Brett Favre](/wiki/Brett_Favre) won three straight awards from 1995 to 1997 with the [Green Bay Packers](/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers).

Quarterback [Peyton Manning](/wiki/Peyton_Manning) won four awards with the [Indianapolis Colts](/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts) and one with the [Denver Broncos](/wiki/Denver_Broncos). He is the only player to win an MVP award with two teams.

**Luigi Ferraris** (18 November 1887 – 23 August 1915) was an Italian footballer, engineer and soldier who died during [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I).

## Biography

Ferraris was born [Florence](/wiki/Florence), while his family hailed from [Saluzzo](/wiki/Saluzzo), [Piedmont](/wiki/Piedmont). He joined [Genoa](/wiki/Genoa_C.F.C.) in 1902, and played there his entire career, where he won the reserve championship ([it](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seconda_Categoria_(campionato_riserve))) 4–0 against [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus_F.C._Youth_Sector) in 1904.

He studied engineering at the [Polytechnic University of Milan](/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Milan) from 1906 to 1911. Afterwards, he worked at the Officine Elettriche Genovesi (OEG) in San Fruttuoso, then at [Pirelli](/wiki/Pirelli) in [Milan](/wiki/Milan).

During the [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I), Ferraris served as a volunteer then reached the rank of lieutenant. On 23 August 1915, he died due to a 152mm shrapnel artillery shell which killed him instantly, during a mission in Val Posina, a minor valley of the Val d'Astico at the municipality of [Posina](/wiki/Posina), and was buried by his comrades in arms at Monte Maggio.

In 1933, the stadium, [Stadio Luigi Ferraris](/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris), was named after him. His [Silver Medal of Military Valor](/wiki/Silver_Medal_of_Military_Valor) was then buried under the "Gradinata Nord" of the stadium, home of the Genoa ultras.

# Hydrochoerus

The genus ***Hydrochoerus*** contains two living (the [lesser](/wiki/Lesser_capybara) and [greater capybaras](/wiki/Capybara)) and three extinct species of [rodents](/wiki/Rodent) from [South America](/wiki/South_America), the Caribbean island of [Grenada](/wiki/Grenada), [California](/wiki/California) and [Panama](/wiki/Panama). Capybaras are the largest living rodents. The name of the genus is derived from [Ancient Greek](/wiki/Ancient_Greek) [ὕδωρ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BD%95%CE%B4%CF%89%CF%81) (*húdor*), meaning "water", and [χοῖρος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CF%87%CE%BF%E1%BF%96%CF%81%CE%BF%CF%82) (*khoîros*), meaning "pig".

## Characteristics

Capybaras are [semiaquatic](/wiki/Semiaquatic), found in and near lakes, rivers, swamps, and flooded savanna. Their diets are dominated by grasses. Adults weigh up to 65 kg (143 lb). The [gestation period](/wiki/Gestation_period) is 130–150 days, with two to eight (most commonly four) young born to females.

## Behavior

Capybaras are highly social, living in groups of up to 100 and communicating through a variety of [vocalizations](/wiki/Animal_communication#Auditory). Breeding is [polygynous](/wiki/Polygyny_in_animals), with males forming [harems](/wiki/Harem_(zoology)).

## Phylogeny and taxonomy

[Molecular](/wiki/Molecular_phylogeny) results have consistently suggested *Hydrochoerus* is most closely related to *[Kerodon](/wiki/Kerodon)* (the rock cavies), and the two evolved from within the [Caviidae](/wiki/Caviidae). This led Woods and Kilpatrick to unite the two into the subfamily [Hydrochoerinae](/wiki/Hydrochoerinae) within the Caviidae. Based on use of a [molecular clock](/wiki/Molecular_clock) approach, *Hydrochoerus* appears to have diverged from *Kerodon* in the late Middle [Miocene](/wiki/Miocene) (about 12 million years ago).

The extinct North American species formerly recognized as *[Hydrochoerus holmesi](/wiki/Hydrochoerus_holmesi)* is now assigned to *[Neochoerus](/wiki/Neochoerus)*.

## Distribution

Presently, capybaras live in northern South America and adjacent southern Central America (lesser capybara) and in the tropical to subtropical regions of South America (capybara). The fossil species inhabited [Buenos Aires Province](/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province) in Argentina (*H. ballesterensis*) and the Caribbean island of [Grenada](/wiki/Grenada) (*H. gaylordi*). One species, *H. hesperotiganites* even ranged as far north as California. Fossils of unspecified *Hydrochoerus* have been found in [Late Pleistocene](/wiki/Pleistocene) to Holocene sediments of [Curití](/wiki/Curit%C3%AD), [Santander](/wiki/Santander_Department), at an altitude of 1,500 m (4,900 ft) in the [Eastern Ranges](/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental_(Colombia)) of the Colombian [Andes](/wiki/Andes). Fauna found at the same site included the [South American tapir](/wiki/South_American_tapir) (*Tapirus terrestris*), *[Cryptotis](/wiki/Cryptotis)* sp., [collared peccary](/wiki/Collared_peccary) (*Tayassu tajacu*), [white-lipped peccary](/wiki/White-lipped_peccary) (*Tayassu pecari*), and *[Mazama](/wiki/Brocket_deer)* sp.

# Small-mouth salamander

The **small-mouth salamander** (***Ambystoma texanum***) is a [species](/wiki/Species) of [mole salamander](/wiki/Mole_salamander) found in the central [United States](/wiki/United_States), from the [Great Lakes](/wiki/Great_Lakes) region in [Michigan](/wiki/Michigan) to [Nebraska](/wiki/Nebraska), south to [Texas](/wiki/Texas), and east to [Tennessee](/wiki/Tennessee), with a population in [Canada](/wiki/Canada), in [Pelee, Ontario](/wiki/Pelee,_Ontario). It is sometimes referred to as the **Texas salamander**, **porphyry salamander**, or the **narrow-mouthed salamander**. The Kelley's Island salamander (*Ambystoma nothagenes*) was synonymized with *A. texanum* in 1995.

## Description

The small-mouth salamander grows from 4.5 to 7.0 in. It is typically black or dark brown in color with light-grey or silvery-colored flecking, or grey blotching. It has a fairly small head, relative to its body, and a long tail. Males are typically smaller than females. Their bellies are black, often with tiny flecks, and have 14 to 15 costal grooves.

## Behavior

Small-mouth salamanders are [nocturnal](/wiki/Nocturnal_animal), often subterranean, preferring moist habitats near permanent bodies of water. Breeding occurs in the spring, with groups of salamanders congregating near the water. Females can lay up to 700 eggs, which they attach in small clumps of up to 30 eggs at a time, to rocks or vegetation under the water. Their [diets](/wiki/Diet_(nutrition)) include [insects](/wiki/Insect), [slugs](/wiki/Slug), and [earthworms](/wiki/Earthworm). Larvae hatch at 0.5 in (13 mm); they metamorphose in May to June at about 1.6 in (40 mm). When disturbed, the small-mouth salamander raises its tail and waves it back and forth. Being shy and sensitive, it shares breeding pools with larger [spotted salamanders](/wiki/Spotted_salamander) and [marbled salamanders](/wiki/Marbled_salamander).

## Habitat and range

Small-mouth salamanders live in moist [pine](/wiki/Pine) [woodlands](/wiki/Woodland) and [deciduous forest](/wiki/Deciduous_forest) [bottomlands](/wiki/Bottomlands), [tallgrass prairies](/wiki/Tallgrass_prairie), farming areas, near temporary ponds, and along streams. Their range is from western [West Virginia](/wiki/West_Virginia) south to the Gulf of Mexico, west to [Kansas](/wiki/Kansas), [Oklahoma](/wiki/Oklahoma), and [Texas](/wiki/Texas).

## Conservation

The only known Canadian population is on [Pelee Island](/wiki/Pelee,_Ontario) in [Lake Erie](/wiki/Lake_Erie). It is listed as endangered by the [Committee on the Status of Endangered Wildlife in Canada](/wiki/Committee_on_the_Status_of_Endangered_Wildlife_in_Canada).

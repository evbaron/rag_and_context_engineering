The **1954 Masters Tournament** was the 18th [Masters Tournament](/wiki/Masters_Tournament), held April 8–12 at [Augusta National Golf Club](/wiki/Augusta_National_Golf_Club) in [Augusta, Georgia](/wiki/Augusta,_Georgia). [Sam Snead](/wiki/Sam_Snead) defeated defending champion [Ben Hogan](/wiki/Ben_Hogan) by one stroke in an 18-hole Monday playoff to win his third Masters tournament. It was Snead's seventh and final [major](/wiki/Men%27s_major_golf_championships) victory.

Both Snead and Hogan were age 41, and they had won the previous three Masters; Snead in [1952](/wiki/1952_Masters_Tournament) and Hogan in [1951](/wiki/1951_Masters_Tournament) and [1953](/wiki/1953_Masters_Tournament). Hogan was also the reigning champion of the [U.S. Open](/wiki/1953_U.S._Open_(golf)) and [British Open](/wiki/1953_Open_Championship); he did not win another major, but often contended until his final appearances in 1967 at the [Masters](/wiki/1967_Masters_Tournament) and [U.S. Open](/wiki/1967_U.S._Open_(golf)).

Snead's 289 (+1), along with [Jack Burke Jr.](/wiki/Jack_Burke_Jr.) in [1956](/wiki/1956_Masters_Tournament) and [Zach Johnson](/wiki/Zach_Johnson) in [2007](/wiki/2007_Masters_Tournament), remains the highest winning total in Masters history. Amateur [Billy Joe Patton](/wiki/Billy_Joe_Patton), 31, led after the first and second rounds and during the fourth, but a seven at the 13th hole and a six at the 15th ended his title hopes, and he finished one stroke back.

Snead remained the oldest winner of the Masters for nearly a quarter century, until [Gary Player](/wiki/Gary_Player) won his third [green jacket](/wiki/Augusta_National_Golf_Club#Green_jacket) at age 42 in [1978](/wiki/1978_Masters_Tournament).

**Robert Fripp** (born 16 May 1946) is an English musician, composer, record producer, and author, best known as the guitarist, founder and only constant member of the [progressive rock](/wiki/Progressive_rock) band [King Crimson](/wiki/King_Crimson). He has worked extensively as a session musician and collaborator, notably with [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie), [Blondie](/wiki/Blondie_(band)), [Brian Eno](/wiki/Brian_Eno), [Peter Gabriel](/wiki/Peter_Gabriel), [Peter Hammill](/wiki/Peter_Hammill), [Daryl Hall](/wiki/Daryl_Hall), [the Roches](/wiki/The_Roches), [Talking Heads](/wiki/Talking_Heads), and [David Sylvian](/wiki/David_Sylvian). He also composed the startup sound of [Windows Vista](/wiki/Windows_Vista), in collaboration with [Tucker Martine](/wiki/Tucker_Martine) and Steve Ball. [His discography](/wiki/Robert_Fripp_discography) includes contributions to more than 700 official releases.

His compositions often feature unusual [asymmetric rhythms](/wiki/Asymmetric_rhythm), influenced by classical and folk traditions. His innovations include a [tape delay](/wiki/Tape_loop) system known as "[Frippertronics](/wiki/Frippertronics)" (superseded in the 1990s by a more sophisticated digital system called "Soundscapes") and [New Standard Tuning](/wiki/New_standard_tuning).

Fripp is married to English singer and actress [Toyah Willcox](/wiki/Toyah_Willcox).

## Early life

Robert Fripp was born in [Wimborne Minster](/wiki/Wimborne_Minster), a town in [Dorset](/wiki/Dorset), [England](/wiki/England), the second child of a working-class family. His mother Edith (*[née](/wiki/Birth_name)* Greene; 1914–1993) was from a [Welsh](/wiki/Welsh_people) mining family; Fripp considers himself to be half Welsh. Her earnings from working at the Bournemouth Records Office allowed his father, Arthur Henry Fripp (1910–1985) to start a business as an [estate agent](/wiki/Estate_agent). In 1957, at age eleven, Fripp received a guitar for Christmas from his parents and recalled, "Almost immediately I knew that this guitar was going to be my life". He then took guitar lessons from Kathleen Gartell and Don Strike; [Elvis Presley](/wiki/Elvis_Presley)'s guitarist [Scotty Moore](/wiki/Scotty_Moore) inspired Fripp to play rock and roll, moving on to traditional jazz at thirteen and modern jazz at fifteen. Fripp has cited jazz musicians [Charlie Parker](/wiki/Charlie_Parker) and [Charles Mingus](/wiki/Charles_Mingus) as musical influences during this time.

In 1961, the fifteen-year-old Fripp joined his first band, the Ravens, which also included [Gordon Haskell](/wiki/Gordon_Haskell) on bass. After they split in the following year, Fripp concentrated on his [O-level studies](/wiki/GCE_Ordinary_Level_(United_Kingdom)) and joined his father's firm as a junior negotiator. At this point, he intended to study [estate management](/wiki/Property_management) and, eventually, take over his father's business. However, at seventeen, Fripp decided to become a professional musician. He became the guitarist in the jazz outfit The Douglas Ward Trio, playing in the [Chewton Glen](/wiki/Chewton_Glen) hotel in [New Milton](/wiki/New_Milton), followed by a stint in the rock and roll band The League of Gentlemen, which included two former Ravens members. For a time, he gave guitar lessons to a local Wimborne friend, [Al Stewart](/wiki/Al_Stewart), who went on to achieve fame as a singer-songwriter. Much later, when Fripp was asked if any of his guitar students had found success, he replied, "Only one of them ever made it, and that was Al Stewart, and he did it by ignoring everything I ever tried to teach him.”

In 1965, Fripp left the group to attend [Bournemouth College](/wiki/Bournemouth_and_Poole_College), where he studied economics, economic history, and political history for his A-levels. In February 1965, Fripp went to see the [Duke Ellington](/wiki/Duke_Ellington) Orchestra, an experience which moved him deeply. He subsequently spent three further years playing light jazz in the Majestic Dance Orchestra at Bournemouth's Majestic Hotel (replacing [Andy Summers](/wiki/Andy_Summers), who had left for London with [Zoot Money](/wiki/Zoot_Money)). During this time, Fripp met musicians that he would collaborate with in his career, including [John Wetton](/wiki/John_Wetton), [Richard Palmer-James](/wiki/Richard_Palmer-James), and [Greg Lake](/wiki/Greg_Lake). At age 21, going back home from college late at night, Fripp tuned on to [Radio Luxembourg](/wiki/Radio_Luxembourg), where he heard the last moments of [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles)' "[A Day in the Life](/wiki/A_Day_in_the_Life)". "Galvanized" by the experience, he went on to listen to the Beatles' 1967 album *[Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band](/wiki/Sgt._Pepper%27s_Lonely_Hearts_Club_Band)*, [Béla Bartók](/wiki/B%C3%A9la_Bart%C3%B3k)'s [string quartets](/wiki/List_of_string_quartets_by_B%C3%A9la_Bart%C3%B3k), [Antonín Dvořák](/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k)'s *[New World Symphony](/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k))*, [Jimi Hendrix](/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix)'s *[Are You Experienced](/wiki/Are_You_Experienced)* and [John Mayall & the Bluesbreakers](/wiki/John_Mayall_%26_the_Bluesbreakers). Many years later, Fripp would recall that "although all the dialects are different, the voice was the same... I knew I couldn't say no". As a band leader, Fripp pointed out that [Miles Davis](/wiki/Miles_Davis) and Duke Ellington inspired him to seek "constant change".

## Career

### 1967–1974: Giles, Giles and Fripp and King Crimson

In 1967, Fripp responded to an advertisement placed by Bournemouth-born brothers [Peter](/wiki/Peter_Giles_(musician)) and [Michael Giles](/wiki/Michael_Giles), who wanted to work with a singing organist. Though Fripp was not what they sought, his audition with them was a success and the trio relocated to London and became [Giles, Giles and Fripp](/wiki/Giles,_Giles_and_Fripp). Their only studio album, *[The Cheerful Insanity of Giles, Giles and Fripp](/wiki/The_Cheerful_Insanity_of_Giles,_Giles_and_Fripp)*, was released in 1968. Despite the recruitment of two further members – singer [Judy Dyble](/wiki/Judy_Dyble) (formerly with [Fairport Convention](/wiki/Fairport_Convention) and later of [Trader Horne](/wiki/Trader_Horne_(band))) and multi-instrumentalist [Ian McDonald](/wiki/Ian_McDonald_(musician)) – Fripp felt that he was outgrowing the eccentric pop approach favoured by Peter Giles, preferring the more ambitious compositions being written by McDonald, and the band broke up in 1968.

Fripp, on tour with King Crimson, in 1974.

Almost immediately, Fripp, McDonald and Michael Giles formed the first lineup of King Crimson in mid-1968, recruiting Fripp's old Bournemouth College friend [Greg Lake](/wiki/Greg_Lake) as lead singer and bassist and McDonald's writing partner [Peter Sinfield](/wiki/Peter_Sinfield) as lyricist, light show designer and general creative consultant. King Crimson's debut album, *[In the Court of the Crimson King](/wiki/In_the_Court_of_the_Crimson_King)*, was released in late 1969 to great success: drawing on rock, jazz and European folk/classical music ideas, it is now regarded as one of the most influential albums in the history of [progressive rock](/wiki/Progressive_rock). The band was tipped for stardom, but, due to growing musical differences between Fripp on one side and Giles and McDonald on the other, broke up after its first American tour in 1970. A despondent Fripp offered to leave if it would allow King Crimson to survive; however, Giles and McDonald had independently decided that the band's music was "more Fripp's than theirs" and that it would be better if they were the ones to leave.

During the recording of the band's second album *[In the Wake of Poseidon](/wiki/In_the_Wake_of_Poseidon)*, Greg Lake departed to form [Emerson, Lake and Palmer](/wiki/Emerson,_Lake_and_Palmer) with [Keith Emerson](/wiki/Keith_Emerson) of [the Nice](/wiki/The_Nice) and [Carl Palmer](/wiki/Carl_Palmer) of [Atomic Rooster](/wiki/Atomic_Rooster). King Crimson issued two more albums, *[Lizard](/wiki/Lizard_(album))* and *[Islands](/wiki/Islands_(King_Crimson_album)),* with Fripp and Sinfield the only constants in a regularly changing lineup variously including Gordon Haskell, woodwind player [Mel Collins](/wiki/Mel_Collins), drummers [Andy McCulloch](/wiki/Andrew_McCulloch_(drummer)) and [Ian Wallace](/wiki/Ian_Wallace_(drummer)) and future [Bad Company](/wiki/Bad_Company) bassist [Boz Burrell](/wiki/Boz_Burrell), in addition to a palette of guest players. Fripp was listed as the sole composer of the band's music during this time, which built on the first album's blueprint but progressed further into [jazz-rock](/wiki/Jazz_fusion) and [free jazz](/wiki/Free_jazz) while also taking form from Sinfield's esoteric lyrical and mythological concepts.

In 1971, Fripp ousted Sinfield and took over *de facto* leadership of King Crimson (although he has always formally rejected the label, preferring to describe his role as "quality control" or "a kind of glue"). From this point onwards, Fripp would be the only constant member of the band, which in turn would be defined primarily by his compositional and conceptual ideas. With avant-garde percussionist [Jamie Muir](/wiki/Jamie_Muir), violinist [David Cross](/wiki/David_Cross_(musician)), former [Family](/wiki/Family_(band)) bassist and singer [John Wetton](/wiki/John_Wetton) and former [Yes](/wiki/Yes_(band)) drummer [Bill Bruford](/wiki/Bill_Bruford) now in the ranks, King Crimson produced three more albums of innovative and increasingly [experimental rock](/wiki/Experimental_rock), shedding members as they progressed: beginning with *[Larks' Tongues in Aspic](/wiki/Larks%27_Tongues_in_Aspic)*, progressing with *[Starless and Bible Black](/wiki/Starless_and_Bible_Black)* after Muir's departure and culminating in *[Red](/wiki/Red_(King_Crimson_album))* after Cross was fired. Fripp formally disbanded the group in 1974, in what eventually turned out to be merely the first in a regular series of long hiatuses and further transformations.

### 1971–1985: Collaborations, side projects, and solo career

Fripp pursued side projects during King Crimson's less active periods. He worked with [Keith Tippett](/wiki/Keith_Tippett) (and others who appeared on King Crimson records) on projects far from rock music, playing with and producing [Centipede](/wiki/Centipede_(band))'s *[Septober Energy](/wiki/Septober_Energy)* in 1971 and *Ovary Lodge* in 1973. During this period he also worked with [Van der Graaf Generator](/wiki/Van_der_Graaf_Generator), playing on their albums *[H to He, Who Am the Only One](/wiki/H_to_He,_Who_Am_the_Only_One)* and *[Pawn Hearts](/wiki/Pawn_Hearts)*. He produced [Matching Mole](/wiki/Matching_Mole)'s *[Matching Mole's Little Red Record](/wiki/Matching_Mole%27s_Little_Red_Record)* in 1972. Prior to forming the *Larks*-era KC, he collaborated on a spoken-word album with a woman he described as "a witch", but the resulting *Robert Fripp & Walli Elmlark: The Cosmic Children of Rock* was never officially released.

With [Brian Eno](/wiki/Brian_Eno), Fripp recorded *[(No Pussyfooting)](/wiki/(No_Pussyfooting))* in 1972, and *[Evening Star](/wiki/Evening_Star_(Fripp_%26_Eno_album))* in 1974. These experimented with several avant-garde musical techniques that were new to rock. On "The Heavenly Music Corporation" from *(No Pussyfooting)*, Fripp used a [delay](/wiki/Delay_(audio_effect)) system using two modified [Revox](/wiki/Revox) A77 reel-to-reel tape machines. The technique went on to play a central role in Fripp's later work, and became known as "Frippertronics".

In 1973, Fripp performed the guitar solo on "[Baby's on Fire](/wiki/Baby%27s_on_Fire)" from Eno's solo album *[Here Come the Warm Jets](/wiki/Here_Come_the_Warm_Jets)*. In 1975, Fripp and Eno played live shows in Europe, and Fripp also contributed guitar solos to Eno's 1975 album *[Another Green World](/wiki/Another_Green_World)*.

Fripp started what was intended as a permanent sabbatical from his musical career in 1975, during which he studied at [J. G. Bennett](/wiki/John_G._Bennett)'s International Academy for Continuous Education, becoming interested in the mystical and philosophical ideas of Bennett's teacher [George Gurdjieff](/wiki/George_Gurdjieff). He returned to musical work the following year as a session guitarist on [Peter Gabriel](/wiki/Peter_Gabriel)'s [debut solo album](/wiki/Peter_Gabriel_(1977_album)), released in 1977. Fripp toured with Gabriel to support the album, but used the pseudonym "Dusty Rhodes" and concealed himself on stage.

Fripp also produced and played on Gabriel's [second album](/wiki/Peter_Gabriel_(1978_album)) in 1978. "Robert is particularly skilful at keeping things fresh, and I like that a lot," Gabriel enthused. "I was very interested in Robert's experimental side; that corresponded exactly to what I wanted to do on this second record… There are two *(Fripp)* solos: one on 'On the Air' and the other on 'White Shadow'. And then he plays on 'Exposure'. He gives the colour to this piece, being fifty per cent responsible for its construction. And he also plays classical guitar here and there. He's a musician I admire a lot, because he's one of the only ones to mix discipline and madness with so much talent."

In 1977, Fripp played on [David Bowie](/wiki/David_Bowie)'s album *["Heroes"](/wiki/%22Heroes%22_(David_Bowie_album))* at Eno's invitation. Fripp soon collaborated with [Daryl Hall](/wiki/Daryl_Hall) on *[Sacred Songs](/wiki/Sacred_Songs)*.

During this period, Fripp began working on solo material, with contributions from poet/lyricist Joanna Walton and several other musicians, including Eno, Gabriel, and Hall (including the latter's partner, John Oates), as well as [Peter Hammill](/wiki/Peter_Hammill), [Jerry Marotta](/wiki/Jerry_Marotta), [Phil Collins](/wiki/Phil_Collins), [Tony Levin](/wiki/Tony_Levin) and [Terre Roche](/wiki/The_Roches). This material eventually became his first solo album, *[Exposure](/wiki/Exposure_(Robert_Fripp_album))*, released in 1979, followed by the *Frippertronics* tour in the same year.

While living in New York, Fripp contributed to albums and live performances by [Blondie](/wiki/Blondie_(band)) (*[Parallel Lines](/wiki/Parallel_Lines)*) and [Talking Heads](/wiki/Talking_Heads) (*[Fear of Music](/wiki/Fear_of_Music)*), and produced [The Roches](/wiki/The_Roches)' first and third albums, which featured several of Fripp's characteristic guitar solos. A second set of sessions with Bowie produced *[Scary Monsters (and Super Creeps)](/wiki/Scary_Monsters_(and_Super_Creeps))*, and he collaborated with Gabriel again on [his third solo album](/wiki/Peter_Gabriel_(1980_album)). With Blondie, Fripp appeared live on stage at the Hammersmith Odeon on 12 January 1980, participating in a cover version of Bowie's "'Heroes'".

In 1980, Fripp would release *[God Save the Queen/Under Heavy Manners](/wiki/God_Save_the_Queen/Under_Heavy_Manners),* a project that saw two different musical approaches to Frippertronics on one LP. The "A" side of the record, titled "God Save the Queen" attempted what Fripp referred to as "pure Frippertronics" which is "where Frippertronics is used alone." The "B" side of the record, titled "Under Heavy Manners" featured a collaboration with bassist [Busta Jones](/wiki/Busta_Jones), drummer Paul Duskin, and [David Byrne](/wiki/David_Byrne_(musician)) of Talking Heads (as Absalm el Habib). The sounds of this side of the record featured what Fripp called "Discotronics" which was defined as "that musical experience resulting at the interstice of Frippertronics and disco."

Concurrent to this, Fripp would assemble what he called a "second-division touring new wave instrumental dance band" under the name [League of Gentlemen](/wiki/League_of_Gentlemen_(band)), with bassist [Sara Lee](/wiki/Sara_Lee_(musician)), keyboardist [Barry Andrews](/wiki/Barry_Andrews_(musician)) and drummer [Johnny Elichaoff](/wiki/Johnny_Elichaoff) (credited as "Johnny Toobad"). Elichaoff was later replaced by Kevin Wilkinson. The LOG toured for the duration of 1980.

In 1985 he produced the album *Journey to Inaccessible Places* by classical pianist [Elan Sicroff](/wiki/Elan_Sicroff), released on the [Editions E.G.](/wiki/Editions_E.G.) label.

### 1981–1984: Reforming King Crimson

Later versions of *Discipline* featured this design by Steve Ball.

1981 saw the formation of a new King Crimson lineup, reuniting Fripp with Bruford and opening a new partnership with two American musicians: bassist/[Chapman Stick](/wiki/Chapman_Stick) player [Tony Levin](/wiki/Tony_Levin) (who had played with Fripp on *Exposure* and in the first Peter Gabriel touring band) and [Adrian Belew](/wiki/Adrian_Belew), a singer and guitarist who had previously played with Bowie, Talking Heads and [Frank Zappa](/wiki/Frank_Zappa). Although the band had been conceptualised under the name Discipline, it came to Fripp's attention that the other members thought the name King Crimson was more appropriate: for Fripp, King Crimson had always been "a way of doing things" rather than a particular group of musicians. With the more pop-inspired Belew as main songwriter (complementing Fripp as main instrumental composer) the band took on a new style incorporating influences from Indonesian [gamelan](/wiki/Gamelan), [new wave](/wiki/New_wave_music), and [classical minimalism](/wiki/Minimal_music), with both guitarists experimenting extensively with guitar synthesizers. After releasing three albums (*[Discipline](/wiki/Discipline_(King_Crimson_album))*, *[Beat](/wiki/Beat_(King_Crimson_album))* and *[Three of a Perfect Pair](/wiki/Three_of_a_Perfect_Pair)*), Fripp dissolved the band in 1984.

During this period Fripp made two albums with [Andy Summers](/wiki/Andy_Summers) of [The Police](/wiki/The_Police). On *[I Advance Masked](/wiki/I_Advance_Masked)*, Fripp and Summers played all the instruments. *[Bewitched](/wiki/Bewitched_(Andy_Summers_and_Robert_Fripp_album))* was dominated more by Summers, who produced the record and collaborated with other musicians in addition to Fripp.

In 1982 Fripp produced and played guitar on *[Keep On Doing](/wiki/Keep_On_Doing)* by the Roches. *[Village Voice](/wiki/Village_Voice)* rock critic [Robert Christgau](/wiki/Robert_Christgau) wrote that the album "sounds so good I'm beginning to believe Robert Fripp was put on earth to produce the Roches."

### Guitar Craft

The first Guitar Craft course began 25 March 1985 at the [Claymont Court](/wiki/Claymont_Court) site in [Charles Town, West Virginia](/wiki/Charles_Town,_West_Virginia).

Main article: [Guitar Craft](/wiki/Guitar_Craft)

Fripp was offered a teaching position at the American Society for Continuous Education (ASCE) at Claymont Court in [Charles Town, West Virginia](/wiki/Charles_Town,_West_Virginia), in 1984. He had been involved with the ASCE since 1978, eventually serving on its board of directors, and had long been considering the idea of teaching guitar through ideas derived from Bennett and Gurdjieff. His course, Guitar Craft, was begun in 1985, an offshoot of which was a performance group, "the League of Crafty Guitarists", which has released several albums. In 1986, he released the first of two collaborations with his wife, [Toyah Willcox](/wiki/Toyah_Willcox). The members of the [California Guitar Trio](/wiki/California_Guitar_Trio) are former members of The League of Crafty Guitarists and have also toured with King Crimson. Fripp is the patron of the [Guitar Circle of Europe](/wiki/Guitar_Craft#Community), which was founded in 2007, and of the [Seattle Circle Guitar School](/wiki/Guitar_Craft#Community), which was founded in 2010.

In February 2009, Fripp recommended that Guitar Craft cease to exist on its 25th anniversary in 2010.

On 1 September 2022 Fripp published *The Guitar Circle*, a book of writings concerning Guitar Craft.

### Soundscapes

Fripp returned to recording solo in 1994, using an updated version of the Frippertronics technique that creates loops employing digital technology instead of analogue tapes. Fripp has released a number of records that he called "soundscapes", including *1999*, *Radiophonics*, *A Blessing of Tears*, *That Which Passes*, *November Suite*, *The Gates of Paradise*, *Love Cannot Bear* and *At the End of Time*, as well as numerous download-only live recordings. (The sampler *Pie Jesu* consists of material compiled from *A Blessing of Tears* and *The Gates of Paradise*.)

### 1990s collaborations with David Sylvian and others

Fripp's collaborations with [David Sylvian](/wiki/David_Sylvian) feature some of his most exuberant guitar playing. Fripp contributed to Sylvian's twenty-minute track "Steel Cathedrals" from his *[Alchemy: An Index of Possibilities](/wiki/Alchemy:_An_Index_of_Possibilities)* album of 1985. Then Fripp performed on several tracks from Sylvian's 1986 release, *[Gone to Earth](/wiki/Gone_to_Earth_(David_Sylvian_album))*.

In late 1991, Fripp had asked Sylvian to join a re-forming King Crimson as a vocalist. Sylvian declined the invitation, but proposed a possible collaboration between the two that would eventually become a tour of Japan and Italy in the spring of 1992.

Also in 1991, Fripp released an album with the project Sunday All Over The World, also featuring his wife Toyah Willcox, former League of Crafty Guitarists member [Trey Gunn](/wiki/Trey_Gunn) on Chapman Stick, and drummer Paul Beavis. The prior name of this band was Fripp Fripp, and they toured as such in 1988. They renamed to SAOTW, and toured again as SAOTW, in 1989.

In July 1993, Sylvian and Fripp released the collaborative effort *[The First Day](/wiki/The_First_Day_(David_Sylvian_and_Robert_Fripp_album))*. Other contributors were soon-to-be King Crimson member [Trey Gunn](/wiki/Trey_Gunn) on [Chapman Stick](/wiki/Chapman_Stick) and Jerry Marotta (who, like Sylvian, almost became a member of King Crimson) on drums. When the group toured to promote the CD, future King Crimson member [Pat Mastelotto](/wiki/Pat_Mastelotto) took over the drumming spot. The live document *Damage* was released in 1994, as was the joint venture, *Redemption – Approaching Silence*, which featured Sylvian's ambient sound sculptures (Approaching Silence) accompanying Fripp reading his own text (Redemption).

During the early and mid-1990s Fripp contributed guitar/soundscapes to *[Lifeforms](/wiki/Lifeforms_(The_Future_Sound_of_London_album))* (1994) by [the Future Sound of London](/wiki/The_Future_Sound_of_London) and *[Cydonia](/wiki/Cydonia_(album))* (released 2001) by [the Orb](/wiki/The_Orb), as well as *[FFWD](/wiki/FFWD)*, a collaborative effort with the latter's members. In addition, Fripp worked with Brian Eno co-writing and supplying guitar to two tracks for a CD-ROM project released in 1994 entitled *[Headcandy](/wiki/Headcandy)* created by Chris Juul and Doug Jipson. Eno thought the visual aspects of the disc (video feedback effects) were very disappointing upon completion, and regretted participation. During this period, Fripp also contributed to albums by [No-Man](/wiki/No-Man) and [the Beloved](/wiki/The_Beloved_(band)) (1994's *[Flowermouth](/wiki/Flowermouth)* and 1996's *X*, respectively). He also contributed soundscapes and guitar to two albums by the UK band [Iona](/wiki/Iona_(band)): 1993's *[Beyond These Shores](/wiki/Beyond_These_Shores)* and 1996's *[Journey into the Morn](/wiki/Journey_into_the_Morn)*.

### 1994–2010: King Crimson redux

Trey Gunn, Adrian Belew, and Fripp from 2003 King Crimson

In late 1994, Fripp re-formed King Crimson for its fifth incarnation, adding Trey Gunn and Pat Mastelotto to the 1980s quartet in a configuration known as a "double trio". This line-up released the *[VROOOM](/wiki/Vrooom)* EP in 1994 and the *[THRAK](/wiki/Thrak)* album the following year.

Though musically and relatively commercially successful, the double-trio King Crimson proved difficult to sustain in the long-term. From 1997 to 1999, the band "fraKctalised" into five experimental instrumental sub-groups known as [ProjeKcts](/wiki/ProjeKcts). By 1998 Bruford had quit the band altogether: in 2000, Fripp, Belew, Gunn and Mastelotto reunited as a four-piece King Crimson. This lineup produced two [industrial metal](/wiki/Industrial_metal)-influenced studio albums, *[the construKction of light](/wiki/The_Construkction_of_Light)* in 2000 and *[The Power to Believe](/wiki/The_Power_to_Believe)* in 2003.

Gunn departed at the end of 2003. Although Levin immediately returned to replace him, another hiatus followed until King Crimson reappeared in 2007 with the addition of [Porcupine Tree](/wiki/Porcupine_Tree) drummer [Gavin Harrison](/wiki/Gavin_Harrison). This version of the band toured the eastern United States in 2008, reassessing the 1981-2003 back catalogue and introducing lengthy duets between the two drummers. No new original material was recorded by this line-up, and in 2010, Fripp announced that King Crimson were on another indefinite hiatus.

### Work with G3, Porcupine Tree, Slow Music, Theo Travis, the Humans and others

In 2004, Fripp toured with [Joe Satriani](/wiki/Joe_Satriani) and [Steve Vai](/wiki/Steve_Vai) as part of their [G3](/wiki/G3_(tour)#2004) series. He also worked at [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft)'s studios to record the startup sound for [Windows Vista](/wiki/Windows_Vista). Fripp designed the soundscape and composed the melody, while [Tucker Martine](/wiki/Tucker_Martine) created the rhythm and Microsoft's Steve Ball added the harmonies and created the final arrangement.

> this interesting factoid: in addition to 200 million Vista users with the 4 note splash, an extract from the Soundscapes' Vista sessions is estimated to strike up 91% of 32 trillion times on the new MS Mail programme this year. So, one of the planet's least popular music forms will also be the planet's most sounded in 2008. This has to be some kind of a record.

— Fripp's online diary at dgmlive.com

In late 2005 and early 2006, Fripp joined [R.E.M.](/wiki/R.E.M.)/[Nine Inch Nails](/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails) drummer [Bill Rieflin](/wiki/Bill_Rieflin)'s improvisational Slow Music project, along with R.E.M. guitarist [Peter Buck](/wiki/Peter_Buck), bassist [Fred Chalenor](/wiki/Fred_Chalenor), session drummer [Matt Chamberlain](/wiki/Matt_Chamberlain) and [Hector Zazou](/wiki/Hector_Zazou) on electronics. This collective of musicians toured the west coast of America in May 2006.

In 2006 Fripp contributed his composition "At The End Of Time" to the Artists for Charity album *[Guitarists 4 the Kids](/wiki/Guitarists_4_the_Kids)*, produced by Slang Productions, to assist [World Vision Canada](/wiki/World_Vision_International) in helping underprivileged children. Throughout 2006, Fripp performed many solo concerts of soundscapes in intimate settings in churches around England and Estonia. In October 2006, ProjeKct Six (Fripp and Adrian Belew) played at select venues on the east coast of the U.S., opening for Porcupine Tree. In the same year, Fripp contributed to two songs from [Porcupine Tree](/wiki/Porcupine_Tree)'s *[Fear of a Blank Planet](/wiki/Fear_of_a_Blank_Planet)* ("[Way Out of Here](/wiki/Way_Out_of_Here)" and "[Nil Recurring](/wiki/Nil_Recurring)"). Fripp also sporadically performed as an opening act for Porcupine Tree on various tours from 2006 through 2009.

Fripp in 2007

In 2008, Fripp collaborated with [Theo Travis](/wiki/Theo_Travis) on an album of guitar and flute-or-saxophone duets called 'Thread', and the duo played a brief English tour in 2009 (repeating the collaboration with the *Follow* album in 2012). Also in 2009, Fripp played a concert with the band [the Humans](/wiki/The_Humans_(British_band)) (which consists of his wife [Toyah Willcox](/wiki/Toyah_Willcox), [Bill Rieflin](/wiki/Bill_Rieflin) and Chris Wong), appeared on [Judy Dyble](/wiki/Judy_Dyble)'s *[Talking With Strangers](/wiki/Talking_With_Strangers)* (along with Pat Mastelotto and others) and played on two tracks on [Jakko Jakszyk](/wiki/Jakko_Jakszyk)'s album *The Bruised Romantic Glee Club*. In 2010, Fripp contributed a guitar solo to an extended version of the song '[Heathen Child](/wiki/Heathen_Child)' by [Grinderman](/wiki/Grinderman), released as a B-side on the 'Super Heathen Child' single.

In 2021, the ambient/electronica album Leviathan was released. Fripp produced it and played guitar, in collaboration with British [EDM](/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) Duo [The Grid](/wiki/The_Grid).

### *A Scarcity of Miracles*, musical 'retirement' and new lineup of King Crimson

In May 2011, Jakko Jakszyk, Robert Fripp and Mel Collins released *[A Scarcity of Miracles: A King Crimson ProjeKct](/wiki/A_Scarcity_of_Miracles)* on the Panegyric label. The album also featured contributions by Tony Levin and Gavin Harrison, leading to speculation that the project was a dry run for a new King Crimson lineup.

In an interview published 3 August 2012, Fripp stated that he had retired from working as a professional musician, citing long-standing differences with [Universal Music Group](/wiki/Universal_Music_Group) and stating that working within the music industry had become "a joyless exercise in futility". This retirement proved to be short-lived, only lasting as long as it took to come to a settlement with UMG.

In his online diary entry for 6 September 2013, Fripp announced the return of King Crimson as a seven-piece unit with "four Englishmen and three Americans". The new lineup was Fripp, Levin, both Mastelotto and Harrison on drums, returning 1970s band member Mel Collins and two new members: [Jakko Jakszyk](/wiki/Jakko_Jakszyk) as singer and second guitarist, and Bill Rieflin as a third drummer. This version of the band went on tour in 2014 and 2015 with a setlist reworking and reconfiguring the band's 1960s and 1970s material (plus songs from *A Scarcity of Miracles* and new compositions). In early 2016, it was announced that former [Lemon Trees](/wiki/The_Lemon_Trees)/[Noel Gallagher](/wiki/Noel_Gallagher%27s_High_Flying_Birds) drummer Jeremy Stacey would substitute for Rieflin on that year's tour while the latter was on sabbatical. King Crimson continued touring as a seven- or eight-piece unit with Stacey as a permanent member on drums and keyboards, plus Rieflin (when available) on keyboards and "fairy dusting" until 2021. Rieflin last played with Crimson in 2018; he died 24 March 2020.

## Equipment

During the early years of King Crimson (1968–74), Fripp used two [Gibson Les Paul](/wiki/Gibson_Les_Paul) guitars from 1957 and 1959. The '57 guitar featured three humbucker pick-ups (with one volume control on the pickguard controlling the middle pick-up). In the band's 1980s era, he favoured Roland GR-303 & GR-808 guitars for both straight guitar and synth control. In subsequent years, Fripp has used customized Les Paul-style guitars by [Tokai](/wiki/T%C5%8Dkai_Gakki), 48th St Custom, and [Fernandes](/wiki/Fernandes_Guitars).

A signature model named for the guitarist (Crimson Guitars Robert Fripp Signature) features [Fernandes Sustainer](/wiki/Fernandes_Sustainer) and MIDI pickups with a Les Paul-style body. A significant difference from the Gibson Les Paul is that the signature model is built using a [deep set neck tenon](/wiki/Set-through_neck) rather than a traditional set neck.

Fripp recommended that Guitar Craft students adopt the [Ovation](/wiki/Ovation_Guitar_Company) 1867 Legend [steel-string acoustic guitar](/wiki/Steel-string_acoustic_guitar). "Fripp liked the way the Ovation 1867 fitted against his body, which made it possible for him to assume the right-arm picking position he had developed using electric guitars over the years; on deeper-bodied guitars, the Frippian arm position is impossible without uncomfortable contortions", according to Tamm. While the 1867 Legend is no longer manufactured, it influenced the design of the Guitar Craft Pro Model of Guitar Craft Guitars, which has been endorsed by Fripp.

## Guitar technique

The knotwork symbol of Guitar Craft

See also: [New standard tuning](/wiki/New_standard_tuning) and [Guitar Craft](/wiki/Guitar_Craft)

Fripp began playing guitar at the age of eleven. When he started, he was [tone deaf](/wiki/Amusia) and had no rhythmic sense, weaknesses which led him later to comment that "Music so wishes to be heard that it sometimes calls on unlikely characters to give it voice." He was also naturally left-handed but opted to play the guitar right-handed.

While being taught guitar basics by his teacher Don Strike, Fripp began to develop the technique of [crosspicking](/wiki/Crosspicking), which became one of his specialities. Fripp teaches crosspicking to his students in Guitar Craft.

In 1985, Fripp began using a tuning he called "[New Standard Tuning](/wiki/New_standard_tuning)" (C2-G2-D3-A3-E4-G4), which would also become popularised in Guitar Craft.

Fripp's guitar technique, unlike most rock guitarists of his era, is not blues-based but rather influenced by [avant-garde jazz](/wiki/Avant-garde_jazz) and [European classical music](/wiki/European_classical_music). He combines rapid [alternate picking](/wiki/Alternate_picking) and crosspicking with motifs employing [whole-tone](/wiki/Whole_tone_scale) or [diminished](/wiki/Diminished_seventh_chord) pitch structures and extended [sixteenth-note](/wiki/Sixteenth_note) patterns in *[moto perpetuo](/wiki/Perpetuum_mobile)*.

Rather than stand when performing, he seats himself on a stool (unusual for a performer in rock music), and by doing so was called in a May 1974 issue of *Guitar Player* "the guitarist who sits on stage".

## Personal life

Fripp married singer and actress [Toyah Willcox](/wiki/Toyah_Willcox) on 16 May 1986 in [Poole](/wiki/Poole). From December 1987 until July 1999 they lived at and renovated [Reddish House](/wiki/Reddish_House), the former home of [Cecil Beaton](/wiki/Cecil_Beaton), in the village of [Broad Chalke](/wiki/Broad_Chalke) in Wiltshire.

Fripp previously lived at Thornhill Cottage, Holt, Dorset (1971–1980) and Fernhill House, [Witchampton](/wiki/Witchampton) (1980–1987). After Reddish House, the couple lived at [Evershot](/wiki/Evershot) Old Mansion (1999–2001). They then moved to their present home in [Pershore](/wiki/Pershore), Worcestershire. The couple have no children and have arranged their will so as to leave their assets to the establishment of a musical educational trust for children.

Fripp is the patron of the Seattle Circle Guitar School in the United States and the Shallal Dance Theatre in [Penzance](/wiki/Penzance). He also has had engagements as a [motivational speaker](/wiki/Motivational_speaker), often at events with his sister Patricia, who is a [keynote speaker](/wiki/Keynote_speaker) and speech coach.

Fripp is a [pescetarian](/wiki/Pescetarian). [Nevill Drury](/wiki/Nevill_Drury) details in *Music for Inner Space: Techniques for Meditation & Visualisation* that Fripp was interested in the [Hermetic Qabalah](/wiki/Hermetic_Qabalah), [Wicca](/wiki/Wicca), [German Renaissance](/wiki/German_Renaissance) philosopher [Paracelsus](/wiki/Paracelsus), and [George Gurdjieff](/wiki/George_Gurdjieff) via [J. G. Bennett](/wiki/John_G._Bennett).

During the [COVID-19 lockdowns](/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns), Fripp and Willcox uploaded many short, humorous videos to [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube), usually covers of well-known songs, and mostly titled *Toyah and Robert's Sunday Lunch*. According to rock and metal news website [MetalSucks](/wiki/MetalSucks), their stories about these covers were extremely popular; their cover of [Metallica](/wiki/Metallica)'s "[Enter Sandman](/wiki/Enter_Sandman)" was the site's sixth-most popular story that year. The duo toured the UK in 2023, performing the *Sunday Lunch* songs in concert.

[Alfie Fripp](/wiki/Alfie_Fripp), the last of the "39ers", shot down by the [Luftwaffe](/wiki/Luftwaffe) and then held in 12 different POW camps during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), was his uncle.

Asteroid [81947 Fripp](/wiki/81947_Fripp), discovered by [Marc Buie](/wiki/Marc_Buie) at [Cerro Tololo](/wiki/Cerro_Tololo_Inter-American_Observatory) in 2000, was named in his honour. The official [naming citation](/wiki/Meanings_of_minor-planet_names:_81001%E2%80%9382000#m947) was published by the [Minor Planet Center](/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center) on 18 May 2019 ([M.P.C.](/wiki/Minor_Planet_Circulars) 114955).

Tied with [Andrés Segovia](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Segovia), he is ranked 47th on [Gibson's](/wiki/List_of_Gibson_players) Top 50 guitarists of all time.

## Discipline Global Mobile

Main article: [Discipline Global Mobile](/wiki/Discipline_Global_Mobile)

In 1992, Fripp and producer/online content developer [David Singleton](/wiki/David_Singleton) co-founded [Discipline Global Mobile](/wiki/Discipline_Global_Mobile) (DGM) as an [independent music label](/wiki/Independent_record_label). DGM releases music by Fripp, KC, related acts, and other artists in CDs and in downloadable files. A 1998 *Billboard* profile stated that DGM had ten staff-members in [Salisbury](/wiki/Salisbury) (England) and Los Angeles (USA). DGM has an aim "to be a model of ethical business in an industry founded on exploitation, oiled by deceit, riven with theft and fueled by greed." DGM insists that its artists retain all copyrights; consequently, even DGM's "knotwork" corporate-logo *(pictured above)* is owned by its designer, Steve Ball; the "knotwork" logo appeared earlier on the cover of later versions of the *Discipline* album. DGM's aims were called "exemplary" by Bill [Martin (1997)](#CITEREFMartin1997), who wrote that "Fripp has done something very important for the possibilities of experimental music" in creating DGM, which "has played a major role in creating favorable conditions for" King Crimson.

DGM publishes an on-line diary by Fripp, who often comments on performances and on relations with fans. A moderated forum allows fans to ask questions or to leave comments. Together, Fripp's diary and the fan forum display delayed dialogs in which Fripp and fans discuss diary-entries and forum-postings.

### Copyright infringement complaints

In 2009, Fripp released a statement claiming that [EMI](/wiki/EMI) and Sanctuary Universal had uploaded music to various music stores without his consent, stating "NONE of these downloads were licensed, authorised or legitimised. that is, every single download of any KC track represents copyright violation. or, to use one syllable instead of seven, theft."

In 2011, Fripp complained that the streaming service [Grooveshark](/wiki/Grooveshark) continued to stream his music despite his having delivered repeated [takedown notices](/wiki/Notice_and_take_down). Fripp and Grooveshark's correspondence was published by *[Digital Music News](/wiki/Digital_Music_News)* and in his diaries, which appear on the website of Discipline Global Mobile.

Fripp's published exchange was included in a suit against Grooveshark by [Universal Music Group](/wiki/Universal_Music_Group), which was filed in November 2011. UMG cited internal documents revealing that Grooveshark employees uploaded thousands of illegal copies of UMG-owned recordings. Fripp had previous experience protecting his music in litigation with music companies.

Fripp has stated that he believes "Unauthorised streaming or MP3 giveaways – it amounts to the same thing – copyright theft."

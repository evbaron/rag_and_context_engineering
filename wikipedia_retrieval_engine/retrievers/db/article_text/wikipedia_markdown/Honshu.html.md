**Honshu** ([Japanese](/wiki/Japanese_language): 本州, [Hepburn](/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Honshū*; pronounced [[hoꜜɰ̃.ɕɯː]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:Ja-Honshu.ogg); [lit.](/wiki/Literal_translation) 'main province'), historically known as **Akitsushima** (秋津島; lit. 'dragonfly island') or **Hondo** (lit. 'mainland') , is the largest of [Japan's four main islands](/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Japan#Main_islands). It lies between the [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean) (east) and the [Sea of Japan](/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) (west). It is the [seventh-largest island](/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) in the world, and the [second-most populous](/wiki/List_of_islands_by_population) after the [Indonesian](/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia) island of [Java](/wiki/Java).

Honshu had a population of 104 million as of 2017, constituting 81.3% of the entire population of Japan, and mostly concentrated in the coastal areas and plains. Approximately 30% of the total population resides in the [Greater Tokyo Area](/wiki/Greater_Tokyo_Area) on the [Kantō Plain](/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_Plain). As the historical center of Japanese cultural and political power, the island includes several past Japanese capitals, including [Kyoto](/wiki/Kyoto), [Nara](/wiki/Nara_(city)), and [Kamakura](/wiki/Kamakura). Much of the island's southern shore forms part of the [Taiheiyō Belt](/wiki/Taiheiy%C5%8D_Belt), a [megalopolis](/wiki/Megalopolis) that spans several of the Japanese islands. Honshu also contains Japan's highest mountain, [Mount Fuji](/wiki/Mount_Fuji), and its largest lake, [Lake Biwa](/wiki/Lake_Biwa).

Most of Japan's industry is located in a belt running along Honshu's southern coast, from [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo) to [Nagoya](/wiki/Nagoya), [Kyoto](/wiki/Kyoto), [Osaka](/wiki/Osaka), [Kobe](/wiki/Kobe), and [Hiroshima](/wiki/Hiroshima). The island is linked to the other three major Japanese islands by a number of bridges and tunnels. The island primarily shares two climates, with Northern Honshu having four seasons with largely varying temperatures while the south experiences long, hot summers and cool to mild winters.

## Etymology

The name of the island, Honshū (本州), stems from [Middle Chinese](/wiki/Middle_Chinese). It directly translates to "main province" or "home land" in English.

## History

See also: [History of Japan](/wiki/History_of_Japan)

### Early history

Humans first arrived in Honshu at least approximately 37,000 years ago. The first humans to arrive in Honshu were [Stone Age](/wiki/Stone_Age) hunter-gatherers from Northeast Asia, likely following the migration of [ice age](/wiki/Ice_age) [megafauna](/wiki/Megafauna). Surviving artifacts from this period include finely crafted stone blades, similar to those found in [Siberia](/wiki/Siberia).
After the initial arrival of hunter-gatherers, the island saw the emergence of the [Jōmon period](/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) (c. 14,000–300 BCE), one of the earliest known eras of prehistoric Japanese culture. The Jōmon people were known for their distinctive cord-marked pottery and dogū clay figurines, many of which have been excavated at archaeological sites across Honshu. These artifacts reflect a complex spiritual life and early forms of sedentary communities, particularly along the coasts and river valleys.

### Meiji Restoration

The Meiji Restoration, in Japanese history, is the political revolution in 1868 that brought the final demise of the Tokugawa [shogunate](/wiki/Shogunate) (which was a military government). It ended the [Edo](/wiki/Edo) (Tokugawa) Period (1603–1867) and at least nominally returned control of the country to direct imperial rule under Mutsuhito (the [Emperor Meiji](/wiki/Emperor_Meiji)). One of the main leaders of the restoration (who were mostly young [samurai](/wiki/Samurai)) was Chōshū in far western Honshu, which was one of the feudal areas, hostile to Tokugawa authority domains.

## Geography

Japan as seen from a satellite. Honshu is the largest, middle island.

The island is roughly 1,300 km (810 mi) long and ranges from 50 to 230 km (31 to 143 mi) wide, and its total area is 227,960 km2 (88,020 sq mi). It is slightly larger than [Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain). Its land area has been increasing with [land reclamation](/wiki/Land_reclamation) and coastal uplift in the north due to [plate tectonics](/wiki/Plate_tectonics) with a [convergent boundary](/wiki/Convergent_boundary). Honshu has 10,084 kilometres (6,266 mi) of coastline.

Mountainous and volcanic, Honshu experiences frequent earthquakes (such as the [Great Kantō earthquake](/wiki/1923_Great_Kant%C5%8D_earthquake), which heavily damaged Tokyo in September 1923; and the [earthquake of March 2011](/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami), which moved the northeastern part of the island by varying amounts of as much as 5.3 m (17 ft) while causing devastating tsunamis). The highest peak is the active volcano [Mount Fuji](/wiki/Mount_Fuji) at 3,776 m (12,388 ft), which makes Honshu the world's [7th highest island](/wiki/List_of_islands_by_highest_point). There are many rivers, including the [Shinano River](/wiki/Shinano_River), Japan's longest. The [Japanese Alps](/wiki/Japanese_Alps) span the width of Honshu, from the 'Sea of Japan' coast to the Pacific shore. Western Japan experiences a temperate climate with hot summers and cool to mild winters.
In addition to the general climate patterns, central Honshu, particularly the regions surrounding the Japanese Alps, experiences heavy snowfall in winter. Areas such as Niigata, Toyama, and Nagano prefectures are renowned for their snow accumulation, making them popular destinations for skiing and winter sports. These regions are among the snowiest inhabited places in the world, due to the humid air masses from the Sea of Japan colliding with the mountainous terrain.

### Population

Honshu has a total population of 104 million people, according to a 2017 estimate, 81.3% of the entire population of Japan. The largest city is [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo) (population: 13,988,129), the capital of Japan and part of the [Greater Tokyo Area](/wiki/Greater_Tokyo_Area), the most populous [metropolitan area](/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the world.

### Extreme points

#### Bridges and tunnels

Honshu is connected to the islands of [Hokkaido](/wiki/Hokkaido), [Kyushu](/wiki/Kyushu) and [Shikoku](/wiki/Shikoku) by tunnels and bridges. Three bridge systems have been built across the islands of the Inland Sea between Honshu and Shikoku ([Akashi Kaikyo Bridge](/wiki/Akashi_Kaikyo_Bridge) and the [Ōnaruto Bridge](/wiki/%C5%8Cnaruto_Bridge); Shin-Onomichi Bridge, [Innoshima Bridge](/wiki/Innoshima_Bridge), Ikuchi Bridge, [Tatara Bridge](/wiki/Tatara_Bridge), Ōmishima Bridge, [Hakata–Ōshima Bridge](/wiki/Hakata%E2%80%93%C5%8Cshima_Bridge), and the [Kurushima Kaikyō Bridge](/wiki/Kurushima_Kaiky%C5%8D_Bridge); [Shimotsui-Seto Bridge](/wiki/Shimotsui-Seto_Bridge), [Hitsuishijima Bridge](/wiki/Hitsuishijima_Bridge), [Iwakurojima Bridge](/wiki/Iwakurojima_Bridge), [Yoshima Bridge](/wiki/Yoshima_Bridge), [Kita Bisan-Seto Bridge](/wiki/Kita_Bisan-Seto_Bridge), and the [Minami Bisan-Seto Bridge](/wiki/Minami_Bisan-Seto_Bridge)), the [Seikan Tunnel](/wiki/Seikan_Tunnel) connects Honshu with Hokkaido, and the [Kanmonkyo Bridge](/wiki/Kanmonkyo_Bridge) and [Kanmon Tunnel](/wiki/Kanmon_Railway_Tunnel) connect Honshu with Toyo.

### Geologic activity

See also: [Category:Volcanoes of Honshu](/wiki/Category:Volcanoes_of_Honshu)

Being on the [Ring of Fire](/wiki/Ring_of_Fire), the island of Honshu is seismically active, and is home to 40 active volcanoes.

In 2011, an [earthquake of magnitude 9.0–9.1](/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami) occurred off the coast of Honshu, generating tsunami waves up to 40.5 meters (133 ft) high and killing 19,747. It was the most powerful [earthquake ever recorded in Japan](/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Japan), and the [fourth most powerful earthquake in the world](/wiki/Largest_earthquakes_by_magnitude) since modern record-keeping began in 1900. The tsunami subsequently led to the meltdown of 3 nuclear reactors at the [Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant](/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_Nuclear_Power_Plant), leading to the [Fukushima nuclear disaster](/wiki/Fukushima_nuclear_disaster).

## Economy

Honshu island generates around [US$](/wiki/US$)3.5 trillion or more than 80% of Japan's [GDP](/wiki/GDP).

### Agriculture

Fruit, vegetables, grains, rice and cotton make up the main produce grown in Honshu. The [Tōhoku region](/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_region), spanning the north-eastern part of the island, is notable for its rice production, with 65% of cultivated land being rice paddy fields – almost a quarter of all paddy fields in Japan. [Chiba Prefecture](/wiki/Chiba_Prefecture) is famous for its peanuts, also being the largest producer in Japan. Rare species of the lichen genus [Menegazzia](/wiki/Menegazzia_caviisidia) are found only in Honshu.

### Industry

Most of Japan's tea and silk is from Honshu. Japan's three largest industrial regions are all located on Honshu: the [Keihin region](/wiki/Keihin_region), the [Hanshin Industrial Region](/wiki/Hanshin_Industrial_Region), and the [Chūkyō Industrial Area](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABky%C5%8D_Industrial_Area).

### Minerals and fuels

Honshu is home to a large portion of Japan's minimal mineral reserves, including small oil and coal deposits. Several coal deposits are located in the northern part of the island, concentrated in [Fukushima Prefecture](/wiki/Fukushima_Prefecture) and [Niigata Prefecture](/wiki/Niigata_Prefecture), though Honshu's coal production is negligible in comparison to [Hokkaido](/wiki/Hokkaido) and [Kyushu](/wiki/Kyushu). Most of Japan's oil reserves are also located in northern Honshu, along the west coast, spanning Niigata, [Yamagata](/wiki/Yamagata_Prefecture), and [Akita](/wiki/Akita_Prefecture) Prefectures.

Most of Japan's [copper](/wiki/Copper), [lead](/wiki/Lead), [zinc](/wiki/Zinc) and [chromite](/wiki/Chromite) is located on Honshu, along with smaller, scattered deposits of [gold](/wiki/Gold), [silver](/wiki/Silver), [arsenic](/wiki/Arsenic), [sulfur](/wiki/Sulfur) and [pyrite](/wiki/Pyrite).

## Transportation

Mt. Fuji and the [Tokaido Shinkansen](/wiki/Tokaido_Shinkansen)

The [Tokaido Shinkansen](/wiki/Tokaido_Shinkansen), opened in 1964 between [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo_Station) and [Shin-Ōsaka](/wiki/Shin-%C5%8Csaka_Station), is Japan's first high-speed rail line. It is the world's oldest high-speed rail line and one of the most heavily used. The [San'yō Shinkansen](/wiki/San%27y%C5%8D_Shinkansen) connects stations in the two largest cities in western Japan, [Shin-Osaka](/wiki/Shin-%C5%8Csaka_Station) in [Osaka](/wiki/Osaka) with [Hakata Station](/wiki/Hakata_Station) in [Fukuoka](/wiki/Fukuoka). Both the Tokaido Shinkansen and the Sanyo Shinkansen help form a continuous high-speed railway through the [Taiheiyō Belt](/wiki/Taiheiy%C5%8D_Belt) megalopolis.

The island is served by Japan's 4 major airports which are:

- [Haneda Airport](/wiki/Haneda_Airport) (serving [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo))
- [Narita International Airport](/wiki/Narita_International_Airport) (serving Tokyo and [Chiba Prefecture](/wiki/Chiba_Prefecture))
- [Chubu Centrair International Airport](/wiki/Chubu_Centrair_International_Airport) (serving the [Chūkyō](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABky%C5%8D_metropolitan_area) region)
- [Kansai International Airport](/wiki/Kansai_International_Airport) (serving the [Keihanshin](/wiki/Keihanshin) region)

## Administrative regions and prefectures

The island is divided into five nominal regions and contains 34 prefectures, including metropolitan Tokyo. Administratively, some smaller islands are included within these prefectures, notably including the [Ogasawara Islands](/wiki/Ogasawara_Islands), [Sado Island](/wiki/Sado_Island), [Izu Ōshima](/wiki/Izu_%C5%8Cshima), and [Awaji Island](/wiki/Awaji_Island).

# Andrew Fluegelman

## Infobox

- **Born:** Andrew Cardozo Fluegelman ( 1943-11-27 ) November 27, 1943
- **Disappeared:** c. July 6, 1985 (1985-07-06) (aged 41)
- **Occupation:** Publisher

**Andrew Cardozo Fluegelman** (November 27, 1943 – c.  July 6, 1985) was an American publisher, photographer, [programmer](/wiki/Programmer) and attorney best known as a pioneer of what is now known as the [shareware](/wiki/Shareware) business model for software marketing. He was also the founding editor of both *[PC World](/wiki/PC_World_(magazine))* and *[Macworld](/wiki/Macworld)* and the leader of the 1970s *[New Games](/wiki/New_Games_Book)* movement, which advocated the development of [noncompetitive games](/wiki/Competition).

## Early life

Fluegelman was raised in [White Plains, New York](/wiki/White_Plains,_New_York). He graduated from [Yale University](/wiki/Yale_University) in 1969.

## Career

### Attorney

Following graduation, Fluegelman worked in [Midtown Manhattan](/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan) before moving to California and working for a law firm in [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco). He was admitted to the [State Bar of California](/wiki/State_Bar_of_California) in January 1971. He resigned in 1972 without any particular plan about his future.

### Writing

The following year, Fluegelman started working for the [Whole Earth Catalog](/wiki/Whole_Earth_Catalog), a job that lasted for about a year. During that time, he separated from his wife and starting living in [Sausalito, California](/wiki/Sausalito,_California). At one point, he fasted for 49 days, drinking only water.

He started writing and publishing books, such as *San Francisco Free and Easy* and *[The New Games Book](/wiki/The_New_Games_Book)*. By 1981 he had published about 15 books, and began writing computer software reviews, the first being for [EasyWriter](/wiki/EasyWriter).

### Publisher

In 1981, Fluegelman was the owner and sole employee of The Headlands Press, a small book publisher in [Tiburon, California](/wiki/Tiburon,_California). He had attended an early [computer expo](/wiki/Computer_expo) in San Francisco in the late 1970s, and after agreeing to publish and coauthor *Writing in the Computer Age* decided to purchase his first computer. In October, Fluegelman bought one of the first [IBM PCs](/wiki/IBM_PC) sold in San Francisco, and in two weeks began to write his own accounting program in [IBM BASIC](/wiki/IBM_BASIC), despite never having used a computer before that year. He recalled "I spent the first month [programming], and I *loved it*".

### Shareware

In 1982 Fluegelman developed [PC-Talk](/wiki/PC-Talk), a very popular and successful communications program. He marketed it under a system he called "Freeware" and became known as [shareware](/wiki/Shareware), which he characterized as "an experiment in economics more than [altruism](/wiki/Altruism)". PC-Talk was licensed under terms that encouraged users to make voluntary payments for the software, and it allowed users to copy and redistribute the software freely as long as the license terms and text were not altered. He collaborated with [PC-File](/wiki/PC-File) developer [Jim Knopf](/wiki/Jim_Knopf) to adopt similar names (PC-File was originally "Easy-File"), and prices, for their initial shareware offerings; they also agreed to mention each other's products in their program's documentation.

### Magazine editor

Fluegelman edited [*PC World*](/wiki/PC_World_(magazine)) magazine from its introduction in 1982 until 1985, and *[Macworld](/wiki/Macworld)* magazine from its introduction in 1984 until 1985.

## Disappearance

Fluegelman suffered from [ulcerative colitis](/wiki/Ulcerative_colitis). In July 1985, he was prescribed [prednisone](/wiki/Prednisone) to treat the condition. Within a few days of beginning to take the medication, he became depressed and agitated. He started profusely apologizing to his colleague for perceived failures. In order to reduce his stress, his employer rearranged his work schedule; Fluegelman's behavior did not improve.

On the afternoon of July 6, 1985, he left his office in [Tiburon, California](/wiki/Tiburon,_California). A week later, his abandoned car was found at the north end of the [Golden Gate Bridge](/wiki/Golden_Gate_Bridge) near San Francisco. His family held a memorial service for Fluegelman, and he is [presumed dead](/wiki/Declared_death_in_absentia), though his body has never been found. Kevin Strehlo, *[InfoWorld](/wiki/InfoWorld)* columnist, submitted a memorial column which mentioned that "friends say a [suicide note](/wiki/Suicide_note) was found inside" his car. *InfoWorld* rejected this column, but an online news service published it.

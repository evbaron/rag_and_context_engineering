# Rosales

For other uses, see [Rosales (disambiguation)](/wiki/Rosales_(disambiguation)).

**Rosales** ([/roʊˈzeɪliːz/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English), [*roh-ZAY-leez*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) are an [order](/wiki/Order_(biology)) of [flowering plants](/wiki/Flowering_plant). Well-known members of Rosales include: [roses](/wiki/Rose), [strawberries](/wiki/Strawberry), [blackberries](/wiki/Blackberry) and [raspberries](/wiki/Raspberry), [apples](/wiki/Apple), [cherries](/wiki/Cherry), [pears](/wiki/Pear), [plums](/wiki/Plum), [peaches](/wiki/Peach) and [apricots](/wiki/Apricot), [almonds](/wiki/Almond), [rowan](/wiki/Rowan) and [hawthorn](/wiki/Crataegus), [jujube](/wiki/Jujube), [elms](/wiki/Elm), [banyans](/wiki/Banyan), [figs](/wiki/Ficus), [mulberries](/wiki/Morus_(plant)), [breadfruit](/wiki/Breadfruit), [nettles](/wiki/Urtica), [hops](/wiki/Humulus_lupulus), and [cannabis](/wiki/Cannabis).

Rosales contain about 7,700 [species](/wiki/Species), distributed into nine [families](/wiki/Family_(biology)) and about 260 [genera](/wiki/Genera). Their [type](/wiki/Type_(biology)) family is the rose family, [Rosaceae](/wiki/Rosaceae). The largest families are [Rosaceae](/wiki/Rosaceae) (91/4828) and [Urticaceae](/wiki/Urticaceae) (53/2625).

## Taxonomy

The order Rosales is strongly [supported](/wiki/Resampling_(statistics)) as [monophyletic](/wiki/Monophyletic) in [phylogenetic](/wiki/Phylogenetic) analyses of [DNA sequences](/wiki/DNA_sequence), such as those carried out by members of the [Angiosperm Phylogeny Group](/wiki/Angiosperm_Phylogeny_Group). In their [APG III system](/wiki/APG_III_system) of [plant classification](/wiki/History_of_plant_systematics), they [defined](/wiki/Circumscription_(taxonomy)) Rosales as consisting of the nine families:

- [Cannabaceae](/wiki/Cannabaceae) ([hemp](/wiki/Hemp) family)
- [Dirachmaceae](/wiki/Dirachmaceae)
- [Elaeagnaceae](/wiki/Elaeagnaceae) ([oleaster](/wiki/Oleaster)/[Russian olive](/wiki/Russian_olive) family)
- [Moraceae](/wiki/Moraceae) ([mulberry](/wiki/Mulberry) family)
- [Rhamnaceae](/wiki/Rhamnaceae) ([buckthorn](/wiki/Buckthorn) family)
- [Rosaceae](/wiki/Rosaceae) ([rose](/wiki/Rose) family)
- [Ulmaceae](/wiki/Ulmaceae) ([elm](/wiki/Elm) family)
- [Urticaceae](/wiki/Urticaceae) ([nettle](/wiki/Urtica) family)

In the older classification system of [Dahlgren](/wiki/Rolf_Dahlgren) the Rosales were in the [superorder](/wiki/Superorder) Rosiflorae (also called Rosanae). In the obsolete [Cronquist system](/wiki/Cronquist_system), the order Rosales was many times [polyphyletic](/wiki/Polyphyletic). It consisted of the family Rosaceae and 23 other families that are now placed in various other orders. These families and their placement in the [APG III system](/wiki/APG_III_system) are:

The order Rosales is divided into three [clades](/wiki/Clade) that have never been assigned a [taxonomic rank](/wiki/Taxonomic_rank). The [basal](/wiki/Basal_(phylogenetics)) clade consists of the family Rosaceae; another clade consists of four families, including [Rhamnaceae](/wiki/Rhamnaceae); and the third clade consists of the four [urticalean](/wiki/Urticales) families.

The order is a [sister](/wiki/Sister_taxon) to a [clade](/wiki/Clade) consisting of [Fagales](/wiki/Fagales) and [Cucurbitales](/wiki/Cucurbitales).

## Distribution

Different plants that fall under the order Rosales grow in many different parts of the world. They can be found in [the mountains](/wiki/Mountain), [the tropics](/wiki/Tropics) and [the arctic.](/wiki/Arctic) Even though you can find a member of the order Rosales nearly anywhere, the specific families grow in different specific [geographical locations](/wiki/Geography). [Wind-pollination](/wiki/Anemophily) is the way that the majority of the families that fall under the order Rosales (including [Moraceae](/wiki/Moraceae), [Ulmaceae](/wiki/Ulmaceae), and [Urticaceae](/wiki/Urticaceae) etc.) pollinate.

## Importance

Within the order Rosales is the family [Rosaceae](/wiki/Rosaceae), which includes numerous species that are cultivated for their fruit, making this one of the most economically important families of plants. Fruit produced by members of this family include apples, pears, plums, peaches, cherries, almonds, strawberries, blackberries and raspberries. Many ornamental species of plant are also in the family Rosaceae, including the rose after which the family and order were named. The [rose](/wiki/Rose), considered a symbol of love in many cultures, is featured prominently in poetry and [literature](/wiki/Literature). Modern garden varieties of roses such as hybrid teas, floribunda, and grandifora, originated from complex hybrids of several separate wild species native to different regions of Eurasia.

The [Moraceae](/wiki/Moraceae) also produce important fruits, such as [mulberries](/wiki/Mulberries), [figs](/wiki/Figs), [jackfruits](/wiki/Jackfruit), and [breadfruits](/wiki/Breadfruits), and the leaves of the mulberry provide food for the silkworms used in commercial silk production.

The wood from the black cherry (*[Prunus serotina](/wiki/Prunus_serotina)*) and sweet cherry (*[P. avium](/wiki/Prunus_avium)*) is used to make high quality furniture due to its color and ability to be bent. The *[Cannabis](/wiki/Cannabis)* plant has been highly prized for millennia for its [hemp](/wiki/Hemp), which has numerous uses. Other varieties of *Cannabis* are [grown as a drug](/wiki/Cannabis_(drug)).

Plants in the order Rosales were used in the traditional [medicines](/wiki/Medication) of many cultures. [Medical cannabis](/wiki/Medical_cannabis) has been recognized for its pharmaceutical use. The latex of some species of fig trees contains the enzyme [ficin](/wiki/Ficin), which is effective in killing roundworms that infect the intestinal tracts of animals.

**Andrew Stephen Roddick** (born August 30, 1982) is an American former professional [tennis](/wiki/Tennis) player. He was ranked as the [world No. 1](/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players) in men's singles by the [Association of Tennis Professionals](/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals) (ATP) for 13 weeks, including as the [year-end No. 1](/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players#Year-end_No._1_players) in [2003](/wiki/2003_ATP_Tour). Roddick won 32 [ATP Tour](/wiki/ATP_Tour)-level singles titles, including a [major](/wiki/Grand_Slam_(tennis)#Tournaments) at the [2003 US Open](/wiki/2003_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) and five [Masters](/wiki/ATP_Masters_1000_tournaments) events, and led the [United States](/wiki/United_States_Davis_Cup_team) to the [2007 Davis Cup](/wiki/2007_Davis_Cup) title. He was the runner-up at four other majors ([Wimbledon](/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon) in [2004](/wiki/2004_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), [2005](/wiki/2005_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), and [2009](/wiki/2009_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), and the US Open in [2006](/wiki/2006_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles)), losing to [rival](/wiki/Federer%E2%80%93Roddick_rivalry) [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) each time.

Roddick was ranked in the year-end top 10 for nine consecutive years (2002–2010), first reaching the No. 1 spot in 2003. Roddick retired from the sport following the [2012 US Open](/wiki/2012_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) to focus on his work at the [Andy Roddick Foundation](/wiki/Andy_Roddick_Foundation). In retirement, Roddick played for the [Austin Aces](/wiki/Austin_Aces) in [World Team Tennis](/wiki/World_Team_Tennis) in 2015. He was also the 2015 and 2017 champion of the [QQQ Champions Series](/wiki/Champions_Series_(senior_men%27s_tennis_tour)). Roddick was inducted into the [International Tennis Hall of Fame](/wiki/International_Tennis_Hall_of_Fame) in 2017. He is married to swimwear model and actress [Brooklyn Decker](/wiki/Brooklyn_Decker).

## Early life

Roddick was born on August 30, 1982, in [Omaha, Nebraska](/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska), the youngest son of Blanche (née Corell), a [school teacher](/wiki/School_teacher), and Jerry Roddick, a businessman. Roddick has two older brothers, Lawrence and [John](/wiki/John_Roddick), who were both promising tennis players at a young age.

Roddick lived in [Austin, Texas](/wiki/Austin,_Texas), from ages 4 to 11, and then moved to [Boca Raton, Florida](/wiki/Boca_Raton,_Florida), in the interest of his brother's tennis career, attending SEK Boca Prep International School, and graduating in 2000. Roddick also took high school classes online through the [University of Nebraska High School](/wiki/University_of_Nebraska_High_School).

## Career

### 1997–2000: Juniors

Roddick considered quitting competitive tennis at age 17 when he had a losing streak in the juniors. His coach [Tarik Benhabiles](/wiki/Tarik_Benhabiles) talked him into giving tennis four more months of undivided attention. Roddick finished as the No. 6 junior in the U.S. in 1999, and as the No. 1 junior in the world in 2000. He won six world junior singles titles and seven world junior doubles titles, and won the [US Open](/wiki/US_Open_(tennis)) and Australian Open junior singles titles in 2000.

### 2000–2002: Breakthrough

In March in Miami, in the first round, Roddick had his first ATP level victory as he beat No. 41 [Fernando Vicente](/wiki/Fernando_Vicente) of Spain. In August in Washington, D.C., he beat No. 30 [Fabrice Santoro](/wiki/Fabrice_Santoro) of France. Roddick played the *Banana Bowl* in the city of [São Paulo](/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo) and won, beating [Joachim Johansson](/wiki/Joachim_Johansson) in the final. Roddick also won the Australian Junior Open, defeating [Mario Ančić](/wiki/Mario_An%C4%8Di%C4%87) in the final.

Entering the pros in 2001 at the age of 18, Roddick quickly showed his promise when he defeated 7-time Wimbledon champion and world No. 4 [Pete Sampras](/wiki/Pete_Sampras) in the third round of the [Miami Masters](/wiki/Miami_Masters). Later that year, he dispatched then World No. 1 [Gustavo Kuerten](/wiki/Gustavo_Kuerten) of Brazil in August. Earlier, at the [2001 French Open](/wiki/2001_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick defeated a French Open champion, [Michael Chang](/wiki/Michael_Chang), in a five set battle in the second round. During the ensuing [Wimbledon](/wiki/2001_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), he further showed potential by taking a set from eventual winner [Goran Ivanišević](/wiki/Goran_Ivani%C5%A1evi%C4%87).

### 2003: US Open title and world No. 1

Roddick's breakthrough year was 2003, in which he defeated [Younes El Aynaoui](/wiki/Younes_El_Aynaoui) in the quarterfinals of the [2003 Australian Open](/wiki/2003_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles). Roddick and the Moroccan battled for five hours, with the fifth set (21–19 in favor of Roddick) at the time the longest fifth set in a Grand Slam tournament during the [open era](/wiki/Tennis_open_era), at 2 hours and 23 minutes. Despite a lackluster [French Open](/wiki/2003_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick enjoyed success in the United Kingdom by winning [Queen's Club](/wiki/Queen%27s_Club_Championships), beating No. 2 [Andre Agassi](/wiki/Andre_Agassi) on a final set tie break along the way, and reaching the Wimbledon semifinals, where he lost to eventual champion [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) in straight sets. He avenged that loss in August, beating then No. 3 Federer in [Montreal](/wiki/Rogers_Cup_(tennis)) in a third set tie break. It is one of three times that Roddick defeated Federer in an official ATP tournament.

Roddick's hard-court record in 2003 included his first [Masters Series](/wiki/ATP_Masters_Series) titles—coming at [Canada](/wiki/Canada_Masters) and [Cincinnati](/wiki/Cincinnati_Masters)—and his only Grand Slam title. At the [2003 US Open](/wiki/2003_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick rallied from two sets down and a match point in the semifinals to beat [David Nalbandian](/wiki/David_Nalbandian) of Argentina in five sets. He then defeated No. 3 [Juan Carlos Ferrero](/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero) in the final in straight sets. At the [Tennis Masters Cup](/wiki/Tennis_Masters_Cup) in Houston, he defeated No. 7 [Carlos Moyá](/wiki/Carlos_Moy%C3%A1) of Spain, and No. 4 [Guillermo Coria](/wiki/Guillermo_Coria) of Argentina, before losing to [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) in the semifinals. By the end of the year, at age 21, he was ranked No. 1, the first American to finish a year at No. 1 since [Andre Agassi](/wiki/Andre_Agassi) in 1999.

### 2004: First Wimbledon final

Roddick's reign at No. 1 ended the following February, when [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) ascended to the top position, after winning his first Australian Open; the [2004 Australian Open](/wiki/2004_Australian_Open) would be the only time in Roddick's career that he was the No. 1 seed in a Grand Slam. In April, Roddick again beat No. 6 Moyá. In June, Roddick advanced to his first Wimbledon final after taking the first set from defending champion Federer, losing in four sets. Roddick was knocked out during the [2004 US Open](/wiki/2004_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) in a five-set quarterfinal against another big server, [Joachim Johansson](/wiki/Joachim_Johansson). In September, he beat No. 9 [Marat Safin](/wiki/Marat_Safin) of Russia in Bangkok. At the [2004 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics), Roddick lost to Chilean [Fernando González](/wiki/Fernando_Gonz%C3%A1lez), the eventual [bronze medal](/wiki/Bronze_medal) winner, in the third round. In November he beat No. 7 [Tim Henman](/wiki/Tim_Henman) of Great Britain, No. 4 Safin, and No. 6 [Guillermo Coria](/wiki/Guillermo_Coria). Later that year, Roddick teamed up with [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish) and [Bob and Mike Bryan](/wiki/Bob_and_Mike_Bryan) on the U.S. [Davis Cup](/wiki/Davis_Cup) team that lost to Spain in the final in [Seville](/wiki/Seville). Roddick lost his singles match against [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), who would in the following year win the French Open. Towards the end of 2004, Roddick fired his coach of 18 months, [Brad Gilbert](/wiki/Brad_Gilbert), and hired assistant Davis Cup coach [Dean Goldfine](/wiki/Dean_Goldfine). Roddick finished 2004 ranked as the world No. 2, U.S. No. 1, and player with the most [aces](/wiki/Ace_(tennis)) (2,017). In 2004, Roddick saved fellow tennis player [Sjeng Schalken](/wiki/Sjeng_Schalken) and other guests (including close friends Ben Campezi and Dean Monroe) from a hotel fire.

### 2005: Second Wimbledon final

Roddick on his way to the first tournament win of 2005

Roddick's first 2005 tournament victory was the [SAP Open](/wiki/SAP_Open) in [San Jose, California](/wiki/San_Jose,_California), where he became the first to win the event in consecutive years since [Mark Philippoussis](/wiki/Mark_Philippoussis) in 1999 and 2000. The top-seeded Roddick defeated [Cyril Saulnier](/wiki/Cyril_Saulnier) in 50 minutes, the event's first championship shutout set since [Arthur Ashe](/wiki/Arthur_Ashe) beat [Guillermo Vilas](/wiki/Guillermo_Vilas) in 1975. In March, he defeated No. 7 [Carlos Moyá](/wiki/Carlos_Moy%C3%A1). In April, Roddick won the U.S. Men's Claycourt Championships, reclaiming the title he won in 2001 and 2002. (He lost in 2003 to Agassi, and in 2004 to [Tommy Haas](/wiki/Tommy_Haas).) At the [Rome Masters](/wiki/Italian_Open_(tennis)) in May, Roddick had match point in the round of 16 against Spain's [Fernando Verdasco](/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco). Verdasco was attempting to save the [match point](/wiki/Match_point_(tennis)) on his second serve, when the [linesman](/wiki/Official_(tennis)) erroneously called the serve out. If this call had held, Roddick would have won the match. Roddick motioned to the umpire, pointing to the clear ball mark on the clay indicating that the ball was in, and the call was consequently changed. Verdasco went on to win the match. At the [2005 French Open](/wiki/2005_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick lost to unseeded Argentine [José Acasuso](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Acasuso) in the second round, and at Wimbledon, Roddick lost to Federer in the final for the second consecutive year. In August, he defeated No. 3 [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt) at the [Masters Series tournament in Cincinnati](/wiki/Western_%26_Southern_Financial_Group_Masters). At the [US Open](/wiki/2005_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick was defeated by No. 70 [Gilles Müller](/wiki/Gilles_M%C3%BCller) in the first round. Roddick's most recent US Open first-round loss had been in 2000. At the [Grand Prix de Tennis de Lyon](/wiki/Grand_Prix_de_Tennis_de_Lyon), Roddick defeated [Gaël Monfils](/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils) to wrap up a tournament without losing a set or getting his serve broken.

### 2006: US Open final

Roddick's first ATP event of the year was the [Australian Open](/wiki/2006_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles). There, he reached the fourth round, before being upset by unseeded and eventual finalist, [Marcos Baghdatis](/wiki/Marcos_Baghdatis). At the [French Open](/wiki/2006_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick retired in the first round, after sustaining a foot injury during the match. Two weeks later at [Wimbledon](/wiki/2006_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick was upset in the third round by British hopeful [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray). This loss caused Roddick to fall below the top 10 for the first time since 2002. After Wimbledon, Roddick began working with a new coach, tennis legend [Jimmy Connors](/wiki/Jimmy_Connors). In his first event with his new coach, Roddick reached the final of Indianapolis, before losing to good friend and fellow American, [James Blake](/wiki/James_Blake_(tennis)). His resurgence finally came at the Cincinnati Masters, where he won the event by defeating [Juan Carlos Ferrero](/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero) in the final, making this the first masters event he won since 2004. At the [US Open](/wiki/2006_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick easily won his first two matches against [Florent Serra](/wiki/Florent_Serra) and [Kristian Pless](/wiki/Kristian_Pless). He then won a five-set match against [Fernando Verdasco](/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco). Next, he beat [Benjamin Becker](/wiki/Benjamin_Becker), who was coming off a huge win against recently retired [Andre Agassi](/wiki/Andre_Agassi). In the quarterfinals, Roddick beat [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt), avenging his loss in 2001, in straight sets. Now in the semifinals for the first time since he won in 2003, Roddick beat [Mikhail Youzhny](/wiki/Mikhail_Youzhny) in four sets. In the finals of a Grand Slam for the first time since Wimbledon a year prior, Roddick lost to No. 1 Federer in four sets. He then qualified for the year-ending Tennis Masters Cup, where he defeated No. 4 [Ivan Ljubičić](/wiki/Ivan_Ljubi%C4%8Di%C4%87) of Croatia, but lost in the [round robin](/wiki/Round-robin_tournament) to No. 1 Federer in a tough three-set battle, despite holding three match points in the second-set tiebreaker.

### 2007: Davis Cup victory

At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2007_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) in his first-round match, he lost a marathon first-set tiebreak 20–18, but eventually won the match in four sets against [wild card](/wiki/Wild_card_(sports)) [Jo-Wilfried Tsonga](/wiki/Jo-Wilfried_Tsonga). Roddick defeated Marat Safin in the third round, [Mario Ančić](/wiki/Mario_An%C4%8Di%C4%87) in a five-set fourth-round match and [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish) in the quarterfinals. His run was ended in the semifinals by No. 1 Federer, who defeated him in straight sets, making his head-to-head record against Federer 1–13. In first-round Davis Cup action, Roddick helped the US defeat the [Czech Republic](/wiki/Czech_Republic), winning his singles matches against [Ivo Minář](/wiki/Ivo_Min%C3%A1%C5%99) and [Tomáš Berdych](/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Berdych). Roddick bowed out to [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray) in the semifinals of the [SAP Open](/wiki/SAP_Open) in [San Jose, California](/wiki/San_Jose,_California) and then defeated Murray in the semifinals of the [Regions Morgan Keegan Championships](/wiki/Regions_Morgan_Keegan_Championships) and the [Cellular South Cup](/wiki/Cellular_South_Cup) in [Memphis, Tennessee](/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee), before losing in the final to defending champion [Tommy Haas](/wiki/Tommy_Haas). Reaching the final, however, enabled Roddick to overtake [Nikolay Davydenko](/wiki/Nikolay_Davydenko) for the No. 3 position, his first week inside the top three since March 6, 2006. At [Pacific Life Open](/wiki/Pacific_Life_Open) in [Indian Wells, California](/wiki/Indian_Wells,_California) Roddick beat No. 8 [Ivan Ljubičić](/wiki/Ivan_Ljubi%C4%8Di%C4%87), but lost to No. 2 [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in the semifinals.

Roddick then retired in the quarters of [Miami Masters](/wiki/Miami_Masters) against [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray) due to a left [hamstring](/wiki/Hamstring) injury. Roddick helped the U.S. defeat Spain and advance to the Davis Cup semifinals, winning his lone singles match against [Fernando Verdasco](/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco). However, Roddick re-aggravated his hamstring injury during the Davis Cup tie, and was subsequently forced to pull out of the [U.S. Men's Clay Court Championships](/wiki/U.S._Men%27s_Clay_Court_Championships) in [Houston, Texas](/wiki/Houston,_Texas). His next tournament was the [Internazionali d'Italia](/wiki/Rome_Masters), where lost to [Juan Ignacio Chela](/wiki/Juan_Ignacio_Chela) in the third round. At the French Open he was eliminated in the first round by [Igor Andreev](/wiki/Igor_Andreev) in four sets. Roddick was victorious at the [Stella Artois Championships](/wiki/Stella_Artois_Championships) for the fourth time, when he defeated [Nicolas Mahut](/wiki/Nicolas_Mahut) in the final. At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2007_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick lost in five close sets to [Richard Gasquet](/wiki/Richard_Gasquet) in the quarter finals.

Roddick in Washington, 2007

During the summer hard-court season, Roddick played four tournaments in four weeks. Roddick lost in the semifinals of the [Indianapolis Tennis Championships](/wiki/Indianapolis_Tennis_Championships) to [Frank Dancevic](/wiki/Frank_Dancevic). Roddick claimed his second ATP title of the year by winning the [Legg Mason Tennis Classic](/wiki/Legg_Mason_Tennis_Classic) in Washington, D.C. for the third time, when he beat [John Isner](/wiki/John_Isner). He then lost in the quarterfinals of the [Rogers Cup](/wiki/Canada_Masters) in [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal) to [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic), and in the third round of the [Western & Southern Financial Group Masters](/wiki/Cincinnati_Masters) tournament in [Cincinnati](/wiki/Cincinnati) to [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer). At the [US Open](/wiki/2007_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick defeated Gimelstob in the first round. He won his next three matches, one in straight sets and the other two when his opponent retired. In the quarterfinals, Roddick once again lost to Federer, bringing his head-to-head record with Federer 1–14.

At [Lyon, France](/wiki/Lyon,_France), Roddick lost in the first round to [Fabrice Santoro](/wiki/Fabrice_Santoro). Roddick then withdrew from the [Paris Masters](/wiki/Paris_Masters), incurring a $22,600 fine for not fulfilling his media obligations at the tournament. At the season-ending [Tennis Masters Cup](/wiki/Tennis_Masters_Cup) in Shanghai, Roddick defeated No. 4 [Nikolay Davydenko](/wiki/Nikolay_Davydenko) in his first round-robin match, and then defeated No. 7 [Fernando González](/wiki/Fernando_Gonz%C3%A1lez) to become the first player to qualify for the semifinals of the tournament. In his final round-robin match, Roddick lost once again to Federer for the 15th time in 16 career matches. In the semifinals, Roddick lost to sixth seed [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer). This was Roddick's third semifinal finish in five years at the Tennis Masters Cup (he reached the semifinals in 2003 and 2004, withdrew in 2005, and failed to advance to the semifinals in 2006 after a 1–2 round-robin record). Roddick finished the year by helping the U.S. defeat Russia and win the [2007 Davis Cup](/wiki/2007_Davis_Cup), its 32nd Davis Cup victory, but first since 1995. Roddick won his rubber against [Dmitry Tursunov](/wiki/Dmitry_Tursunov).

### 2008: Series of injuries

Roddick beat [Ivan Ljubičić](/wiki/Ivan_Ljubi%C4%8Di%C4%87), Safin and [Marcos Baghdatis](/wiki/Marcos_Baghdatis) to win the [AAMI Kooyong Classic](/wiki/AAMI_Kooyong_Classic) for the third consecutive year. Roddick At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2008_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) he beat [Lukáš Dlouhý](/wiki/Luk%C3%A1%C5%A1_Dlouh%C3%BD) and [Michael Berrer](/wiki/Michael_Berrer) and lost to the 29th seed [Philipp Kohlschreiber](/wiki/Philipp_Kohlschreiber) in the third round in five sets. Despite losing, Roddick served a career-high of 42 aces in the match. Roddick won his 24th career title and his third title of the year at the [SAP Open](/wiki/SAP_Open) in San Jose, California, defeating [Radek Štěpánek](/wiki/Radek_%C5%A0t%C4%9Bp%C3%A1nek) in straight sets. At the [Dubai Tennis Championships](/wiki/Dubai_Tennis_Championships) he made it to the semifinals beating No. 2 [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), his first victory over Nadal since the second round of the 2004 US Open. The win also marked Roddick's first victory over a player ranked in the top two since June 2003. He beat No. 3 and 2008 Australian Open singles champion [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic) in the semifinal. In the final he defeated [Feliciano López](/wiki/Feliciano_L%C3%B3pez) to win his 25th career title. He never lost his serve during the entire tournament.

Following Roddick's quarterfinal match in Dubai, he announced that he had split with his coach of two years, [Jimmy Connors](/wiki/Jimmy_Connors). Connors had resigned a week earlier, saying he wanted to spend more time with his family. Roddick would continue to be coached by his brother, John Roddick. He then fell to No. 2 [Tommy Haas](/wiki/Tommy_Haas) at the [Pacific Life Open](/wiki/Pacific_Life_Open) in the second round. At the [2008 Sony Ericsson Open](/wiki/2008_Sony_Ericsson_Open), Roddick advanced to the semifinals after defeating No. 1 [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) an hour after proposing to [Brooklyn Decker](/wiki/Brooklyn_Decker), bringing his head-to-head record against Federer to 2–15. Roddick improved to 3–0 against top-3 players in 2008. Roddick lost in the semifinals to [Nikolay Davydenko](/wiki/Nikolay_Davydenko). Roddick reached the semifinals at Rome, where he retired against [Stanislas Wawrinka](/wiki/Stanislas_Wawrinka) in the pair's first encounter, due to a back injury.

Roddick was forced to pull out of the [French Open](/wiki/2008_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) due to a shoulder injury (later diagnosed as an inflammation of the [rotator cuff](/wiki/Rotator_cuff)). His first tournament after the shoulder injury was the [Artois Championship](/wiki/Queen%27s_Club_Championships), where he was the defending champion. Roddick defeated [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish) and [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray), before losing to eventual champion [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in the semifinals. At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2008_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick was beaten by [Janko Tipsarević](/wiki/Janko_Tipsarevi%C4%87) in the second round. This was his earliest exit at Wimbledon. Roddick was beaten at the [Rogers Cup](/wiki/Rogers_Cup_(tennis)) in the third round by [Marin Čilić](/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87). He was then forced to pull out of the [Cincinnati Masters](/wiki/Cincinnati_Masters) following a neck injury, which he said may have been caused by a poor sleeping posture. At Los Angeles, Roddick lost to [Juan Martín del Potro](/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro) in the final. At the [US Open](/wiki/2008_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick defeated [Fabrice Santoro](/wiki/Fabrice_Santoro), [Ernests Gulbis](/wiki/Ernests_Gulbis), [Andreas Seppi](/wiki/Andreas_Seppi), and [Fernando González](/wiki/Fernando_Gonz%C3%A1lez). In the quarterfinals, Roddick lost to No. 3 [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic), bringing his head-to-head record with Djokovic to 1–2.

Capturing the 26th title of his career in China, 2008

Roddick captured his 26th ATP title in Beijing at the China Open in September, beating [Dudi Sela](/wiki/Dudi_Sela) in the final. In the third round of the [Madrid Masters](/wiki/2008_Madrid_Masters), he lost to [Gaël Monfils](/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils) in three sets. Two weeks later, Roddick reached the quarterfinals of the [Paris Masters](/wiki/Paris_Masters) by defeating [Gilles Simon](/wiki/Gilles_Simon), before losing to [Jo-Wilfried Tsonga](/wiki/Jo-Wilfried_Tsonga). Due to his performance in the tournament, Roddick qualified for the season-ending [Tennis Masters Cup](/wiki/2008_Tennis_Masters_Cup). At the Masters Cup in Shanghai, he played [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray) in his first round-robin match and lost. He was then scheduled to play [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer), but retired due to an ankle injury and was replaced by [Radek Štěpánek](/wiki/Radek_%C5%A0t%C4%9Bp%C3%A1nek).

### 2009: Longest Wimbledon final

Roddick hired [Larry Stefanki](/wiki/Larry_Stefanki) as his new coach and started working with him on December 1, 2008. Stefanki had previously trained [John McEnroe](/wiki/John_McEnroe), [Marcelo Ríos](/wiki/Marcelo_R%C3%ADos), [Yevgeny Kafelnikov](/wiki/Yevgeny_Kafelnikov), [Fernando González](/wiki/Fernando_Gonz%C3%A1lez), and [Tim Henman](/wiki/Tim_Henman). Roddick began official tournament competition at the [Qatar ExxonMobil Open](/wiki/2009_Qatar_ExxonMobil_Open). He defeated [Gaël Monfils](/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils) in the semifinals, before losing to [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray) in the final. At the [2009 Australian Open](/wiki/2009_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Djokovic retired in the fourth set in the quarters while trailing, which allowed Roddick to reach the fourth Australian Open semifinal of his career. Roddick was defeated there by eventual runner-up [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) in straight sets.

Roddick reached the fourth round of the French Open for the first time.

At [SAP Open](/wiki/2009_SAP_Open), he beat [Tommy Haas](/wiki/Tommy_Haas) in the quarterfinals and lost in semifinals to [Radek Štěpánek](/wiki/Radek_%C5%A0t%C4%9Bp%C3%A1nek). At the [Regions Morgan Keegan Championships](/wiki/2009_Regions_Morgan_Keegan_Championships), Roddick beat [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt) in the semifinals and Štěpánek in the final. Roddick chose not to defend his [Dubai](/wiki/Dubai_Tennis_Championships) title, with prize money of $2 million, to protest the refusal of the [United Arab Emirates](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates) to grant [Israeli](/wiki/Israel) [Shahar Pe'er](/wiki/Shahar_Pe%27er) a [visa](/wiki/Visa_(document)) for the [Women's Tennis Association](/wiki/Women%27s_Tennis_Association) event. "I really didn't agree with what went on over there", Roddick said. At [BNP Paribas Open](/wiki/2009_BNP_Paribas_Open) in Indian Wells he defeated defending champion [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic) in the quarterfinals before losing to world No. 1 [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) in the semifinals. However, he won the doubles title with partner [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish). It was his fourth doubles title overall and his second partnering Fish. At the [Miami Masters](/wiki/2009_Sony_Ericsson_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick beat Gaël Monfils in the fourth round but lost to Roger Federer in the quarterfinals.

After a break from tournament tennis to get married, Roddick returned to action at the ATP World Tour Masters 1000 clay-court event in Madrid. In his first match, Roddick survived two match points in the second-set tiebreaker to defeat Tommy Haas. In the quarterfinals, Roddick again lost to Federer. Roddick had his career-best result at the [French Open](/wiki/2009_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), when he defeated [Marc Gicquel](/wiki/Marc_Gicquel) in the third round. He lost in the fourth round to Monfils.

A [twisted ankle](/wiki/Twisted_ankle) forced Roddick to retire from his semifinal against [James Blake](/wiki/James_Blake_(tennis)) at 4–4 during the [Aegon Championships](/wiki/Aegon_Championships). At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2009_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick defeated Lleyton Hewitt in the quarterfinals, serving a career-high of 43 aces, and third-seeded Andy Murray in the semifinals. He then lost to Federer for the third time in a Wimbledon final with a fifth set reaching 14–16, and he was praised for his performance. Even though Roddick lost this match, he set a record for number of games won in a Wimbledon final at 39. This was his fourth meeting with Federer in a Grand Slam final, all won by Federer. The match set records for the longest men's Grand Slam final in history at 77 games and fifth set in a men's Grand Slam final. Following the match, when asked to elaborate on his marathon performance, Roddick replied, "I lost." On the strength of his Wimbledon performance, Roddick returned to the top five on July 13, 2009.

Roddick reached the final of Wimbledon but had a disappointing US Open.

At the [Legg Mason Tennis Classic](/wiki/2009_Legg_Mason_Tennis_Classic) he lost in the final to defending champion [Juan Martín del Potro](/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro), despite saving three match points. At [Rogers Cup](/wiki/2009_Rogers_Cup_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), he defeated No. 4 [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic) in the quarter finals, improving his career record against Djokovic to 4–2 (3–0 in 2009). He then lost to [Juan Martín del Potro](/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro) in the semifinals, despite having a match point. The loss dropped his career record against del Potro to 0–3. At [Cincinnati](/wiki/2009_Western_%26_Southern_Financial_Group_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles), he lost to [Sam Querrey](/wiki/Sam_Querrey) in his first match. Roddick entered the [US Open](/wiki/2009_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) as the fifth seed. He defeated [Björn Phau](/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Phau) and [Marc Gicquel](/wiki/Marc_Gicquel) before losing to [John Isner](/wiki/John_Isner) in the third round. He lost his serve only once during the match, as was the case in the Wimbledon final.

At [China Open](/wiki/2009_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) in Beijing, he was defeated in the first round by qualifier and No. 143 [Łukasz Kubot](/wiki/%C5%81ukasz_Kubot). He and [Mark Knowles](/wiki/Mark_Knowles) reached the doubles final, losing to [Bob and Mike Bryan](/wiki/Bob_and_Mike_Bryan). Roddick retired from his first-round match at the [2009 Shanghai Masters](/wiki/2009_Shanghai_Masters) against [Stanislas Wawrinka](/wiki/Stanislas_Wawrinka) while leading 4–3. It was later announced that Roddick would return to the United States to seek medical advice on a left-knee injury. He qualified for the Year-End Masters in London, securing the sixth spot. However, Roddick withdrew from the [2009 Valencia Open 500](/wiki/2009_Valencia_Open_500), the [2009 BNP Paribas Masters](/wiki/2009_BNP_Paribas_Masters), and the [2009 Barclays ATP World Tour Finals](/wiki/2009_Barclays_ATP_World_Tour_Finals) due to the injury he suffered at the Shanghai Masters. He finished 2009 as the No. 7 in the world.

### 2010: Fifth Masters title

Roddick playing at the 2010 Australian Open

At the [Brisbane](/wiki/2010_Brisbane_International) Roddick beat [Radek Štěpánek](/wiki/Radek_%C5%A0t%C4%9Bp%C3%A1nek) for his first ATP Tour title since February 2009, and making 2010 his tenth consecutive season with at least one ATP singles title. Roddick teamed with [James Blake](/wiki/James_Blake_(tennis)) in the men's doubles and lost in the semis to [Jérémy Chardy](/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my_Chardy) and [Marc Gicquel](/wiki/Marc_Gicquel). At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2010_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) he lost in the quarterfinals to [Marin Čilić](/wiki/Marin_%C4%8Cili%C4%87), despite coming back from two sets down while battling an apparent shoulder injury.

In [SAP Open](/wiki/2010_SAP_Open), he beat [Sam Querrey](/wiki/Sam_Querrey) in the semifinals before losing the final to [Fernando Verdasco](/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco). At [Regions Morgan Keegan Championships](/wiki/2010_Regions_Morgan_Keegan_Championships), he lost in the quarterfinals in a rematch of the San Jose semifinals to [Sam Querrey](/wiki/Sam_Querrey). At [Indian Wells](/wiki/2010_BNP_Paribas_Open) Roddick beat [Robin Söderling](/wiki/Robin_S%C3%B6derling), before losing to [Ivan Ljubičić](/wiki/Ivan_Ljubi%C4%8Di%C4%87) in the final. This was Roddick's first Masters Series final since the 2006 Cincinnati Masters. In the [Sony Ericsson Open](/wiki/2010_Sony_Ericsson_Open), Roddick defeated [Igor Andreev](/wiki/Igor_Andreev), [Sergiy Stakhovsky](/wiki/Sergiy_Stakhovsky), [Benjamin Becker](/wiki/Benjamin_Becker), [Nicolás Almagro](/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Almagro) and [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) to reach his fourth final of the year. In the final, Roddick won his second Sony Ericsson Open title, after defeating [Tomáš Berdych](/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Berdych) in straight sets. This was Roddick's 29th title in 49 finals, fifth ATP Masters 1000 title, and first Masters 1000 title since 2006.

Roddick did not fare well during the clay-court season, withdrawing from Rome due for personal reasons and from Madrid due to a stomach virus. He then lost in the third round of the [French Open](/wiki/2010_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) to [Teymuraz Gabashvili](/wiki/Teymuraz_Gabashvili) in straight sets. Roddick suffered his earliest ever exit in the [2010 Aegon Championships](/wiki/2010_Aegon_Championships), a grass-court Wimbledon tune-up event. He was beaten by [Dudi Sela](/wiki/Dudi_Sela) in the third round. At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2010_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick was seeded fifth, two spots higher than his ATP ranking of seven. He was defeated in the fourth round by No. 82 [Lu Yen-hsun](/wiki/Lu_Yen-hsun) of Taiwan in five sets. Like his final match with [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) the previous year, his serve was broken only once during the match, in the fifth set.

Andy Roddick playing at US Open 2010

At [Atlanta](/wiki/2010_Atlanta_Tennis_Championships), he was eliminated in the semifinals by eventual champion [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish). At [Legg Mason Tennis Classic](/wiki/2010_Legg_Mason_Tennis_Classic), he lost in the round of 16 to [Gilles Simon](/wiki/Gilles_Simon). He dropped out of the top 10 and for the first time since the inception of the ATP world rankings, there was no American man in the top 10. On August 14, 2010, Roddick revealed that he had been diagnosed with [mononucleosis](/wiki/Mononucleosis), although he said his doctor believed it was in its later stages and he would make a complete recovery soon. At the [Cincinnati Masters](/wiki/2010_Western_%26_Southern_Financial_Group_Masters_%E2%80%93_Singles), he beat No. 5 [Robin Söderling](/wiki/Robin_S%C3%B6derling) to reach the quarterfinals, where he defeated second seed [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic). The win was Roddick's fourth consecutive over Djokovic, raising his career head-to-head record against Djokovic to 5–2 and ensuring Roddick's return to the top 10. In the semifinals, Roddick lost to [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish), failing to serve out the match at 5–3 in the second set. In the second round of the [US Open](/wiki/2010_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick was beaten by [Janko Tipsarević](/wiki/Janko_Tipsarevi%C4%87) in four sets.

At the [Japan Open](/wiki/2010_Rakuten_Japan_Open_Tennis_Championships), Roddick lost in the semifinals to [Gaël Monfils](/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Monfils). At [Shanghai Masters](/wiki/2010_Shanghai_Rolex_Masters_1000), Roddick beat [Philipp Kohlschreiber](/wiki/Philipp_Kohlschreiber) after Kohlschreiber retired in the second set and then Roddick himself retired in the second set against [Guillermo García-López](/wiki/Guillermo_Garc%C3%ADa-L%C3%B3pez), after suffering a groin injury. At the [Basel](/wiki/2010_Davidoff_Swiss_Indoors), he beat [Sam Querrey](/wiki/Sam_Querrey), [Andrey Golubev](/wiki/Andrey_Golubev), and [David Nalbandian](/wiki/David_Nalbandian), before losing to [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) in straight sets.

At the [BNP Paribas Masters](/wiki/2010_BNP_Paribas_Masters), Roddick beat [Jarkko Nieminen](/wiki/Jarkko_Nieminen) and [Ernests Gulbis](/wiki/Ernests_Gulbis) before losing to [Robin Söderling](/wiki/Robin_S%C3%B6derling) in the quarterfinals. With [Fernando Verdasco](/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco) failing to reach the final, Roddick automatically qualified for the [Barclays ATP World Tour Finals](/wiki/Barclays_ATP_World_Tour_Finals) for the eighth consecutive year. Though he had dropped out of the top 10 in the ATP rankings after his early exit from the US Open, his victory over Gulbis in Paris assured his return to the top 10 at year end, making him and Federer the only players to maintain year-end top-10 ATP rankings from 2002 through 2010. Roddick played his final tournament of the year at the [Barclays ATP World Tour Finals](/wiki/2010_ATP_World_Tour_Finals). Roddick lost all his round-robin matches to [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic), and [Tomáš Berdych](/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Berdych). Roddick ended the year No. 8, his ninth consecutive season finishing in the top 10.

### 2011: Drop out of top 10

At [Brisbane](/wiki/2011_Brisbane_International), Roddick was defeated by [Robin Söderling](/wiki/Robin_S%C3%B6derling) in the final. At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2011_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick lost to [Stanislas Wawrinka](/wiki/Stanislas_Wawrinka) in round four in straight sets. He then won the [2011 Regions Morgan Keegan Championships](/wiki/2011_Regions_Morgan_Keegan_Championships), defeating [Ričardas Berankis](/wiki/Ri%C4%8Dardas_Berankis), [Janko Tipsarević](/wiki/Janko_Tipsarevi%C4%87), [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt), [Juan Martín del Potro](/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro) and [Milos Raonic](/wiki/Milos_Raonic). Roddick began his [Davis Cup](/wiki/2011_Davis_Cup) campaign for the United States against Chile. He beat [Nicolás Massú](/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Mass%C3%BA) in four sets and beat [Paul Capdeville](/wiki/Paul_Capdeville) in four sets to clinch the victory for the U.S. Roddick improved his record to 12–0 in Davis Cup clinchers.

At the [BNP Paribas Open](/wiki/2011_BNP_Paribas_Open), he was beaten by [Richard Gasquet](/wiki/Richard_Gasquet) in the fourth round. In the [2011 Sony Ericsson Open](/wiki/2011_Sony_Ericsson_Open), as the defending champion, Roddick was upset by [Pablo Cuevas](/wiki/Pablo_Cuevas) in the second round. This loss dropped Roddick to No. 12 in the rankings and the second-ranked American behind compatriot [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish). Roddick then began his clay-court season at the [Mutua Madrid Open](/wiki/2011_Mutua_Madrid_Open), but he was upset in the first round by qualifier [Flavio Cipolla](/wiki/Flavio_Cipolla) in three sets. At [Internazionali BNL d'Italia](/wiki/2011_Internazionali_BNL_d%27Italia) he lost in the first round for the second straight tournament to [Gilles Simon](/wiki/Gilles_Simon). He teamed with [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish) to play doubles in Rome, and they went to the final before Roddick had to withdraw because of a shoulder injury. Roddick also withdrew from the Nice Open in France and pulled out of the [2011 French Open](/wiki/2011_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), after failing to recover.

Andy Roddick at the Queen's Championships

At the [Aegon Championships](/wiki/2011_Aegon_Championships), Roddick lost to [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray) in the semi finals, their first meeting since the Wimbledon 2009 semifinals. Roddick was seeded eighth for [Wimbledon](/wiki/2011_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), and lost in the third round in straight sets to [Feliciano López](/wiki/Feliciano_L%C3%B3pez). Roddick had failed to reach the quarterfinals of any Grand Slam since the 2010 Australian open. This was the longest Roddick had ever gone in his career without reaching the quarterfinals of a Grand Slam. In the Davis Cup quarterfinals match against Spain, Roddick drew [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer) first, but lost in three tight sets (Spain won the tie).

Roddick withdrew from the [Legg Masson Tennis Classic](/wiki/2011_Legg_Mason_Tennis_Classic), and [Rogers Cup](/wiki/2011_Rogers_Cup), after partially tearing his oblique muscle while practicing. At the [Western & Southern Open](/wiki/2011_Western_%26_Southern_Open), he lost in the first round to [Philipp Kohlschreiber](/wiki/Philipp_Kohlschreiber). This loss made him drop out of the top 20 for the first time since August 2001. After suffering an early exit at Cincinnati, Roddick played at the [Winston-Salem Open](/wiki/2011_Winston-Salem_Open) in North Carolina, where he lost in the semifinals to [John Isner](/wiki/John_Isner). Roddick was seeded 21st at the [U.S. Open](/wiki/2011_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles). He beat [Michael Russell](/wiki/Michael_Russell_(tennis)), [Jack Sock](/wiki/Jack_Sock), [Julien Benneteau](/wiki/Julien_Benneteau) and [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer) to advance to his first Grand Slam quarterfinal since the 2010 Australian Open, where he lost to No. 2 [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal).

At the [China Open](/wiki/2011_China_Open_(tennis)), he lost to [Kevin Anderson](/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis)). At [Shanghai Rolex Masters](/wiki/2011_Shanghai_Rolex_Masters), he lost to [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer) in the quarterfinals. After this, he played at the [Swiss Indoors Basel](/wiki/2011_Swiss_Indoors_Basel), where he lost to [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) in the quarterfinals. At [Paris](/wiki/2011_BNP_Paribas_Masters), he had a third-round loss to [Andy Murray](/wiki/Andy_Murray). This loss ended Roddick's 2011 season, which left him out of the top 10, after being there for nine consecutive years.

### 2012: Retirement

At the [Australian Open](/wiki/2012_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick beat [Robin Haase](/wiki/Robin_Haase) in straight sets, but he retired against [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt) while trailing, due to a hamstring injury. At [San Jose](/wiki/2012_SAP_Open) he beat [Denis Kudla](/wiki/Denis_Kudla) in the round of 16, but lost in the quarterfinals to [Denis Istomin](/wiki/Denis_Istomin). At [Regions Morgan Keegan Championships](/wiki/2012_Regions_Morgan_Keegan_Championships) in Memphis, his title defense was short-lived, as he lost his first-round match to [Xavier Malisse](/wiki/Xavier_Malisse). At [Delray Beach International Tennis Championships](/wiki/2012_Delray_Beach_International_Tennis_Championships), he defeated [Philipp Petzschner](/wiki/Philipp_Petzschner) and Istomin in the first and second rounds, respectively, before falling to [Kevin Anderson](/wiki/Kevin_Anderson_(tennis)).

Andy Roddick at the Queen's Championships 2012 with Hewitt

In March, at [Indian Wells](/wiki/2012_BNP_Paribas_Open), Roddick beat [Łukasz Kubot](/wiki/%C5%81ukasz_Kubot) in the second round, but then lost in the next round to [Tomáš Berdych](/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Berdych). Later in the month, at [Sony Ericsson Open](/wiki/2012_Sony_Ericsson_Open), Roddick faced No. 3 Federer, this being the first time they played each other before the quarterfinals of any tournament. Roddick defeated Federer for only the third time in his career to reach the fourth round, but then lost to [Juan Mónaco](/wiki/Juan_M%C3%B3naco). Roddick then elected not to play in the final three clay-court Masters events leading up to the French Open. At [French Open](/wiki/2012_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), he fell to [Nicolas Mahut](/wiki/Nicolas_Mahut) in the first round in four sets. After this, Roddick played at [Aegon Championships](/wiki/2012_Aegon_Championships), where he lost in the second round to [Édouard Roger-Vasselin](/wiki/%C3%89douard_Roger-Vasselin), despite having a match point in the third set.

At the [Aegon International](/wiki/2012_Aegon_International), he received a wild card into the main draw as the sixth seed. He beat [Sam Querrey](/wiki/Sam_Querrey), [Jérémy Chardy](/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my_Chardy), [Fabio Fognini](/wiki/Fabio_Fognini), [Steve Darcis](/wiki/Steve_Darcis) and defending champion [Andreas Seppi](/wiki/Andreas_Seppi) in the final for his 31st career title and first of 2012. He thus became the only male tennis player besides [Roger Federer](/wiki/Roger_Federer) to have won at least one title every year in the past 12 years. At [Wimbledon](/wiki/2012_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) he beat [Jamie Baker](/wiki/Jamie_Baker_(tennis)) and [Björn Phau](/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Phau), but lost to [David Ferrer](/wiki/David_Ferrer) in the third round in four sets.

At the [BB&T Atlanta Open](/wiki/2012_BB%26T_Atlanta_Open) he beat [Nicolas Mahut](/wiki/Nicolas_Mahut), [Michael Russell](/wiki/Michael_Russell_(tennis)) in the quarterfinals, [John Isner](/wiki/John_Isner) in the semifinals and [Gilles Müller](/wiki/Gilles_M%C3%BCller) in the final. Roddick's London Olympics campaign began with a victory over [Martin Kližan](/wiki/Martin_Kli%C5%BEan). In the second round, Roddick lost to No. 2 [Novak Djokovic](/wiki/Novak_Djokovic). After the Olympics, Roddick decided not to play in Toronto and went straight to Cincinnati, where he lost in the first round to Frenchman [Jérémy Chardy](/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%A9my_Chardy).

In the [US Open](/wiki/2012_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), Roddick began his campaign with a victory over his countryman [Rhyne Williams](/wiki/Rhyne_Williams). On August 30, 2012, which was his thirtieth birthday, Roddick announced that he would retire after the tournament. After announcing his retirement, Roddick defeated [Bernard Tomic](/wiki/Bernard_Tomic) and [Fabio Fognini](/wiki/Fabio_Fognini) before his final match on September 5, 2012, where he lost to [Juan Martín del Potro](/wiki/Juan_Mart%C3%ADn_del_Potro) in four sets in the fourth round. Four days after his loss, Roddick was honored in a special ceremony in Arthur Ashe Stadium on his retirement, in which [Andre Agassi](/wiki/Andre_Agassi) participated. Due to his retirement, he ended the year at No. 39, the lowest he had been since 2000, the year he turned professional, when he only played five events.

### 2015

Roddick played one tournament on tour, the [2015 BB&T Atlanta Open](/wiki/2015_BB%26T_Atlanta_Open), where he competed in the men's doubles event with [Mardy Fish](/wiki/Mardy_Fish). They lost in the quarterfinals.

### 2017: Hall of Fame induction

Roddick was inducted into the [International Tennis Hall of Fame](/wiki/International_Tennis_Hall_of_Fame) on July 22, 2017, alongside [Kim Clijsters](/wiki/Kim_Clijsters) of Belgium.

## Nicknames and on-court behavior

Roddick at 2011 US Open

Roddick is often called "A-Rod", a play on the nickname of New York Yankees baseball star [Alex Rodriguez](/wiki/Alex_Rodriguez), referring to his first initial and the first syllable of his last name.

Roddick made frequent outbursts against umpires and linesmen on the court. At the [2001 US Open](/wiki/2001_US_Open_(tennis)), Roddick angrily disputed a ball that landed wide at a crucial point in the fifth set of his quarter-final match against [Lleyton Hewitt](/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt) (he was serving at \*4–5), calling umpire Jorge Dias "a moron" and copping a code violation for audible obscenity. During his third-round match at the Australian Open in 2008, he abused umpire Emmanuel Joseph saying, "You're an idiot! Stay in school, kids or you'll end up being an umpire."

Roddick lost his temper again at the [2010 Australian Open](/wiki/2010_Australian_Open) after a ball that he did not play was called out on match point, but ruled to be in after a video review. He continued arguing with the umpire after the conclusion of the match but later said that he was wrong. Later again that year, he launched into a tirade at an official over a foot-fault call at the [2010 US Open](/wiki/2010_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles), a match he eventually lost to Serbia's [Janko Tipsarević](/wiki/Janko_Tipsarevi%C4%87). In 2011, Roddick snapped at the chair umpire at [Indian Wells](/wiki/2011_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles) on his way to lose to France's [Richard Gasquet](/wiki/Richard_Gasquet).

In a second-round match against Philipp Kohlschreiber at the 2011 Cincinnati Masters, Roddick was given a penalty point, which resulted in a critical break of serve in favor of his opponent. The penalty point was given due to ball-abuse, when Roddick smashed a ball into the stands in frustration after he had already been warned earlier in the match by umpire Carlos Bernardes for an episode of racquet abuse. This triggered another series of altercations with the umpire, with Roddick expressing his displeasure at the umpire's call. Roddick lost the match.

## Endorsements

From 2000 to 2005, Roddick wore [Reebok](/wiki/Reebok) apparel. After Reebok announced it would not renew its contract with Roddick, Roddick signed a five-year, $25 million endorsement deal with [Lacoste](/wiki/Lacoste) in April 2005. In November, he signed an agreement to endorse Lacoste eyewear for four years worth between $750,000 and $1 million. In 2011, Lacoste introduced the Andy Roddick collection which included polos, jackets, tennis shorts and track pants. In June 2013, Roddick became an investor and brand ambassador to [TravisMathew](/wiki/Callaway_Golf_Company) Apparel.

In 2005, Roddick signed a multiyear worldwide endorsement deal with carmaker [Lexus](/wiki/Lexus). His other endorsement deals include [American Express](/wiki/American_Express), [Rolex](/wiki/Rolex), [Powerade](/wiki/Powerade) in 2002, Parlux Fragrances, [Arizona Beverage Company](/wiki/Arizona_Beverage_Company) in 2009, [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft) [Xbox](/wiki/Xbox_(console)) and [Sega](/wiki/Sega).

## Playing style

Roddick serving in 2004

Roddick's serve was known for its power, usually travelling at 130–150 mph (209~242 km/h) and often unreturnable. He once held the record for fastest serve at 155 mph (249 km/h). Roddick's favorite shot is his off-forehand, which he used in combination with his kicker out wide. Roddick used to play his off-forehand frequently but later adjusted and used it to create points. He usually targeted the two corners to win aces. As for his second serve, he usually employed a heavy [kick serve](/wiki/Kick_serve), then used a variety of spins, slices, and angles in the rally to throw off his opponent. He was noted for using heavy topspin on both his serves and his twist serve was particularly high kicking.

Roddick also occasionally used the [serve-and-volley](/wiki/Serve-and-volley) tactic on both first and second services to surprise his opponent, though he generally remained near the [baseline](/wiki/Tennis_strategy#Baseliner_strategies) after a serve. He later developed a more all-court playing style compared to the aggressive baseline style he played with for most of his early career. Although Roddick's backhand was a weakness throughout his career, it improved somewhat in 2009 under Stefanki's guidance.

## Media appearances

On April 5, 2002, Roddick guest-starred on the television show *[Sabrina the Teenage Witch](/wiki/Sabrina,_the_Teenage_Witch_(1996_TV_series))* as himself. In the episode, Sabrina summoned him so he could give her tennis lessons.

Roddick appeared on *[The Late Late Show with Craig Kilborn](/wiki/The_Late_Late_Show_(CBS_TV_series))* in 2002 and 2003, *[Late Show with David Letterman](/wiki/Late_Show_with_David_Letterman)* in 2003 and 2009, *[Late Night with Conan O'Brien](/wiki/Late_Night_with_Conan_O%27Brien)*, and *[Live with Regis and Kelly](/wiki/Live_with_Regis_and_Kelly)* in 2003, *[Jimmy Kimmel Live!](/wiki/Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)* in 2004 and 2005, *[The Tonight Show with Jay Leno](/wiki/The_Tonight_Show_with_Jay_Leno)* in 2005 and 2007, and *[The Ellen DeGeneres Show](/wiki/The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show)* in 2006. Roddick also appeared on *[Friday Night with Jonathan Ross](/wiki/Friday_Night_with_Jonathan_Ross)* in 2007 and 2010.

Roddick hosted *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* on November 8, 2003, becoming the second professional tennis player to host ([Chris Evert](/wiki/Chris_Evert) being the first during the show's 15th season) and the first (and, as of September 2023, only) male tennis player to host.

Pre-Wimbledon, Roddick appeared on an episode of the British version of *[The Weakest Link](/wiki/The_Weakest_Link)*, which was broadcast on 18 June 2004.

Roddick was in an advertisement for *[This is SportsCenter](/wiki/This_is_SportsCenter)*, with [Stuart Scott](/wiki/Stuart_Scott), in which he confronts the Sports Center anchor about the anchors not calling him "A-Rod", and asks him "Did [Alex Rodriguez](/wiki/Alex_Rodriguez) put you up to this?" Scott replies "Who?" Roddick says "A-Rod!" Scott gets a sneaky look on his face, and Roddick leaves disgusted.

The June/July issue of *[Men's Fitness](/wiki/Men%27s_Fitness)* magazine carried an article on Roddick. The cover shot featured the tennis ace in a T-shirt, straining to contain massive, pumped-up biceps and hulking shoulder and chest muscles. The image set off widespread online speculation that the magazine had altered Roddick's likeness, a suspicion echoed by Roddick himself. Roddick has quipped that he saw the photo, and that "Nadal wanted his arms back."

In March 2009, Roddick appeared in the "Speed Feels Better" music video for singer-songwriter [Michael Tolcher](/wiki/Michael_Tolcher). Other athletes in the video included [Amanda Beard](/wiki/Amanda_Beard), [Barry Sanders](/wiki/Barry_Sanders), [Kimmie Meissner](/wiki/Kimmie_Meissner), and [Rick Ankiel](/wiki/Rick_Ankiel).

Roddick played tennis using a frying pan instead of a racquet for the book *Andy Roddick Beat Me With a Frying Pan* by [Todd Gallagher](/wiki/Todd_Gallagher).

In 2011, Roddick made a cameo at the end of the film *[Just Go With It](/wiki/Just_Go_With_It)*, as the new lover of the film's jilted bride, played by Decker.

In 2011 Roddick co-hosted a radio show for one day on *[Fox Sports Radio](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Radio)* with his friend Bobby Bones on the latter's eponymous [program](/wiki/Bobby_Bones_Show). Due to the success of that one-time show, Fox Sports Radio offered Roddick and Bones a nationally syndicated sports radio show. The show debuted on January 7, 2012, and could be heard nationally on Saturdays from 12 to 3 PM CST. It was a mix of sports, pop culture and entertainment. On February 16, 2012, Roddick interviewed his wife, Brooklyn, on the radio show and during that interview he first revealed his plans on retiring and turning the radio show into a daily show and into his new career.

At the 2013 AT&T Pebble Beach Pro-Am, Roddick played his first professional golf tournament (as an amateur) where he teamed up with professional golfer, John Mallinger. Although Roddick's team missed the cut to get the final round, he and Mallinger ended with a combined score of 16 under par (with Roddick individually hitting at a 6 handicap).

In 2013, Roddick was hired by [Fox Sports 1](/wiki/Fox_Sports_1) as co-host for the network's flagship program [Fox Sports Live](/wiki/Fox_Sports_Live).

In 2015, Roddick joined the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) as pundit and commentator for the [2015 Wimbledon Championships](/wiki/2015_Wimbledon_Championships).

In 2017, Roddick's wife revealed that he discarded most of his tennis trophies except the US Open trophy.

In 2019, Roddick appeared on *[Celebrity Family Feud](/wiki/Celebrity_Family_Feud)* with his wife and the rest of the Decker family, competing against [Bobby Bones](/wiki/Bobby_Bones) and his family.

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), Roddick tweeted [Tennis Channel](/wiki/Tennis_Channel) requesting a "job." This led him to become a temporary contributor to the channel.

On April 2, 2023, Roddick participated with Michael Chang, Andre Agassi and John McEnroe in the first live airing of Pickleball on ESPN in the Million dollar Pickleball Slam at the Hard Rock Casino in Hollywood, Fla.

In 2024, Roddick began a weekly podcast series for Tennis Channel named "Served with Andy Roddick".

## Personal life

From 2003 to 2004, Roddick dated actress and singer [Mandy Moore](/wiki/Mandy_Moore).

It was while Roddick was watching a show on the CNN/[Sports Illustrated](/wiki/Sports_Illustrated) website called *She Says [Z](/wiki/Paul_Zimmerman_(sportswriter)) Says* that he first noticed [Brooklyn Decker](/wiki/Brooklyn_Decker), to whom he is now married. According to Decker in an interview with [David Letterman](/wiki/David_Letterman), Roddick had his agent contact her agent to arrange an initial meeting. The two began dating in 2007, and on March 31, 2008, Roddick announced on his website that they had become engaged. The couple were married on April 17, 2009, in Austin, Texas. Decker gave birth to the couple's first child on September 30, 2015. She gave birth to their second child, a daughter, on November 27, 2017.

## Awards and records

In 2004, Roddick produced what was then the fastest [serve](/wiki/Serve_(tennis)) in professional tennis: 249.4 km/h (155.0 mph) during a [Davis Cup](/wiki/Davis_Cup) semifinal match with [Vladimir Voltchkov](/wiki/Vladimir_Voltchkov) on hard court in Charleston, South Carolina. Roddick's record serve has since been superseded by [Ivo Karlović](/wiki/Ivo_Karlovi%C4%87), who served at 251 km/h (156 mph) playing at the Davis Cup in March 2011. Roddick also had the fastest serve in U.S. Open history: 244 km/h (152 mph) against American [Scoville Jenkins](/wiki/Scoville_Jenkins), and against future No. 1 [Rafael Nadal](/wiki/Rafael_Nadal). Roddick also won the 2004 [ESPY Award](/wiki/ESPY_Awards) for Best Male Tennis Player.

That same year he won the [Arthur Ashe](/wiki/Arthur_Ashe) Humanitarian Award of the Year because of his charity efforts, which included: raising money for the survivors of the [tsunami](/wiki/Tsunami) following [2004 Indian Ocean earthquake](/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake) through Serving for Tsunami Relief and other efforts; auctioning off several rackets and autographs to raise money for [UNICEF](/wiki/UNICEF); and creating the [Andy Roddick Foundation](/wiki/Andy_Roddick_Foundation) to help at-risk youth.

In 2007, Roddick and the Andy Roddick Foundation were awarded by the Arthur Ashe Institute for Urban Health. Roddick was the first male tennis player ever to receive the award.

Serve records since 1991:

- Fastest serve in Australian Open: 148 mph.
- Fastest serve in Dubai: 150 mph.
- Fastest average in first serve: 134 mph.
- Fastest serve in Beijing: 148 mph.
- Fastest serve in San Jose: 150 mph.
- Fastest serve in Madrid: 151 mph.
- Fastest serve in Washington: 151 mph.
- Fastest serve in Queens: 153 mph.
- Fastest serve in Lyon: 142 mph.
- Fastest serve in US Open: 152 mph.
- Fastest serve at Roland Garros: 144 mph (2006–2010).
- Fastest serve at Queens: 151 mph.
- Fastest serve at Wimbledon: 143 mph (2011).
- Fastest serve on record (Davis Cup): 155 mph (2004–2011).

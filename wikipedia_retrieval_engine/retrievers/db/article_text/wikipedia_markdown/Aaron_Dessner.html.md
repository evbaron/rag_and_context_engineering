# Aaron Dessner

## Infobox

- **Born:** Aaron Brooking Dessner ( 1976-04-23 ) April 23, 1976 (age 50) Cincinnati, Ohio, U.S.
- **Occupations:** Record producer songwriter instrumentalist
- **Instruments:** Guitar keyboards bass harmonica mandola drum machine vocals
- **Years active:** 1998–present
- **Member of:** The National Big Red Machine
- **Spouse:** Stine Wengler

**Aaron Brooking Dessner** (born April 23, 1976) is an American musician. He is best known as a founding member of the rock band [the National](/wiki/The_National_(band)), with whom he has recorded ten studio albums; a co-founder of the [indie rock](/wiki/Indie_rock) duo [Big Red Machine](/wiki/Big_Red_Machine_(band)), teaming with [Bon Iver](/wiki/Bon_Iver)'s [Justin Vernon](/wiki/Justin_Vernon); and a collaborator on [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift)'s critically acclaimed studio albums *[Folklore](/wiki/Folklore_(Taylor_Swift_album))* and *[Evermore](/wiki/Evermore)*, both of which contended for the [Grammy Award for Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year) in [2021](/wiki/63rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards) and [2022](/wiki/64th_Annual_Grammy_Awards), respectively, with the former winning the accolade; as well as *[The Tortured Poets Department](/wiki/The_Tortured_Poets_Department)* (2024).

Dessner has co-written, produced or co-produced songs by [Noah Kahan](/wiki/Noah_Kahan), [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift), [Ed Sheeran](/wiki/Ed_Sheeran), [Michael Stipe](/wiki/Michael_Stipe), [Gracie Abrams](/wiki/Gracie_Abrams), [Frightened Rabbit](/wiki/Frightened_Rabbit), [Ben Howard](/wiki/Ben_Howard), [Sharon Van Etten](/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten), [Local Natives](/wiki/Local_Natives), [This Is the Kit](/wiki/This_Is_the_Kit), [Adia Victoria](/wiki/Adia_Victoria), [Lisa Hannigan](/wiki/Lisa_Hannigan), [Fred Again](/wiki/Fred_Again), [Girl in Red](/wiki/Girl_in_Red), [Mustafa the Poet](/wiki/Mustafa_the_Poet), [Brian Eno](/wiki/Brian_Eno), [Laufey](/wiki/Laufey_(singer)), [Mumford & Sons](/wiki/Mumford_%26_Sons), [Florence and The Machine](/wiki/Florence_and_the_Machine), [Brandi Carlile](/wiki/Brandi_Carlile), and [Lone Bellow](/wiki/Lone_Bellow), among others. Dessner co-founded and curates three music festivals: [Eaux Claires](/wiki/Eaux_Claires) in [Eau Claire, Wisconsin](/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin), alongside his Big Red Machine partner Vernon; HAVEN in [Copenhagen](/wiki/Copenhagen) with his brother Bryce, and [Boston Calling Music Festival](/wiki/Boston_Calling_Music_Festival).

On May 19, 2022, alongside [Questlove](/wiki/Questlove), Dessner was awarded a [Doctor of Fine Arts honoris causa](/wiki/Honorary_Doctor_of_Fine_Arts) from [The University of the Arts](/wiki/The_University_of_the_Arts), for "his eagerness to seek out opportunities, to really be a thoughtful collaborator, and someone who would encourage every single student at UArts to explore with their peers across disciplines, new opportunities, and new ideas."

Dessner, along with his twin brother [Bryce](/wiki/Bryce_Dessner), was named the 243rd greatest guitarist of all time by *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* in 2023.

## Early life

Dessner grew up in [Cincinnati, Ohio](/wiki/Cincinnati,_Ohio) with his twin brother, [Bryce Dessner](/wiki/Bryce_Dessner). Dessner was raised as [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish) and has [Polish Jewish](/wiki/Polish_Jewish) and [Russian Jewish](/wiki/Russian_Jewish) ancestry. Dessner has said that his Jewish background influences his music: "My brother and I have always been fascinated by liturgical religious melodies in Judaism ... I don't like [major chords](/wiki/Major_chord). I like the meditative patterns in certain prayers, and I like music that repeats itself." Dessner attended high school at [Cincinnati Country Day School](/wiki/Cincinnati_Country_Day_School) and graduated in 1994. Dessner went on to study Modern European History at [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University).

## Career

### The National

Main article: [The National (band)](/wiki/The_National_(band))

Aaron Dessner and his twin brother [Bryce](/wiki/Bryce_Dessner) were childhood friends with [Bryan Devendorf](/wiki/Bryan_Devendorf). In 1998, after Dessner's earlier band, Project Nim, broke up, Devendorf invited the brothers to join his band [the National](/wiki/The_National_(band)). With Devendorf and the Dessner brothers were [Scott Devendorf](/wiki/Scott_Devendorf), Bryan's older brother, and [Matt Berninger](/wiki/Matt_Berninger). The band's self-titled first album was released in 2001 on Brassland Records, a label Dessner co-founded along with his brother Bryce and Alec Hanley Bemis. The band's second album, *[Sad Songs for Dirty Lovers](/wiki/Sad_Songs_for_Dirty_Lovers)*, was released in 2003, also on Brassland. After some critical success with *Sad Songs*, the band signed with [Beggars Banquet Records](/wiki/Beggars_Banquet_Records). With the new label, they released their third studio album, *[Alligator](/wiki/Alligator_(The_National_album))*, in 2005. The album received a positive response from critics, which exposed the band to a larger audience.

In 2007, the band released their fourth album, *[Boxer](/wiki/Boxer_(The_National_album))*, also through Beggars Banquet. Upon release, the album received widespread critical acclaim, and was named to "album of the year" lists. Several songs from the album appeared in TV shows and movies. In 2008, then-Senator Barack Obama used an instrumental version of the song "Fake Empire" in a presidential campaign video.

Following the success of *Boxer*, the band began collaborating with other artists on various songs and compilation albums. In 2009, the band participated in *[Dark Was the Night](/wiki/Dark_Was_the_Night_(album))*, a charity album to benefit the [Red Hot Organization](/wiki/Red_Hot_Organization). That same year, they recorded a song for the album *[Ciao My Shining Star: The Songs of Mark Mulcahy](/wiki/Ciao_My_Shining_Star:_The_Songs_of_Mark_Mulcahy)*, to help support [Polaris](/wiki/Polaris_(American_band)) frontman [Mark Mulcahy](/wiki/Mark_Mulcahy) who had recently lost his wife. In 2010, the band released *[High Violet](/wiki/High_Violet)*, their fifth full-length album. The album saw both critical and commercial success, debuting at No. 3 on US charts, and going on to sell over 850,000 copies worldwide. The band followed up *High Violet* by releasing their sixth album, *[Trouble Will Find Me](/wiki/Trouble_Will_Find_Me)*, on May 21, 2013, via 4AD. Like their previous two efforts, *Trouble* was named to several year-end best-of lists. The album was nominated for [Best Alternative Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album) at the [56th Grammy Awards](/wiki/56th_Grammy_Awards). Later that year, Dessner and the rest of the band were featured in the documentary *[Mistaken for Strangers](/wiki/Mistaken_for_Strangers_(film))*, which followed the band's tour for *High Violet* and early recording of *Trouble*.

In 2017, the band released *[Sleep Well Beast](/wiki/Sleep_Well_Beast)*, their seventh full-length album. The lead single from the album, "[The System Only Dreams in Total Darkness](/wiki/The_System_Only_Dreams_in_Total_Darkness)", became the band's first song to reach No. 1 on a *Billboards* chart after topping the [Adult Alternative Songs](/wiki/Adult_Alternative_Songs) list in August that same year. The band released their eighth studio album *[I Am Easy to Find](/wiki/I_Am_Easy_to_Find_(album))* in 2019 alongside a [short film](/wiki/I_Am_Easy_to_Find_(film)) by [Mike Mills](/wiki/Mike_Mills_(director)). In 2023, the band released two studio albums, *[First Two Pages of Frankenstein](/wiki/First_Two_Pages_of_Frankenstein)* and *[Laugh Track](/wiki/Laugh_Track)*, with the latter being a [surprise album](/wiki/Surprise_album).

Dessner has production credits on all of the band's albums since [*Alligator*](/wiki/Alligator_(The_National_album)). The majority of both *[High Violet](/wiki/High_Violet)* and *[Trouble Will Find Me](/wiki/Trouble_Will_Find_Me)* were recorded in his garage studio behind his house in Brooklyn, and *Alligator* and *Boxer* were partially recorded in his attic and in his sister's attic, which is on the same street in Brooklyn.

### Big Red Machine

Main article: [Big Red Machine (band)](/wiki/Big_Red_Machine_(band))

In 2008, Dessner sent [Justin Vernon](/wiki/Justin_Vernon) an instrumental sketch of a song called "Big Red Machine" for the compilation album *Dark Was the Night*. Vernon wrote a song to it, interpreting the Big Red Machine title as a heart. After 10 years, the pair formed a band called [Big Red Machine](/wiki/Big_Red_Machine_(band)), and on August 31, 2018, released a [self-titled album](/wiki/Big_Red_Machine_(album)). The record was produced by Vernon and Dessner with longtime collaborator Brad Cook and engineered by Jonathan Low primarily at Dessner's studio Long Pond in Upper Hudson Valley, New York.

The duo released a second studio album *[How Long Do You Think It's Gonna Last?](/wiki/How_Long_Do_You_Think_It%27s_Gonna_Last%3F)* on August 27, 2021. The album was produced by Dessner and featured many guests and many of his previous collaborators, including [Ben Howard](/wiki/Ben_Howard), [Sharon Van Etten](/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten), and [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift).

### Other production work

Dessner worked with singer [Sharon Van Etten](/wiki/Sharon_Van_Etten) on her album, [*Tramp*](/wiki/Tramp_(album)) (2012). Van Etten and Dessner spent over fourteen months working in his studio, finally releasing the album to widespread critical success. Dessner produced [Local Natives](/wiki/Local_Natives)' second full-length album [*Hummingbird*](/wiki/Hummingbird_(Local_Natives_album)), in 2013. Dessner was first introduced to the band while on tour with the National. *Hummingbird* received positive reviews, and *[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website))* praised Dessner's production work especially, saying that he "knows how to make things sound *good*, and there's plenty of richness and depth to these songs." Dessner produced [the Lone Bellow](/wiki/The_Lone_Bellow)'s *Then Came the Morning,* which was recorded in Dessner's garage as well as in a 19th-century church in upstate New York. The album was released in January 2015 on Descendent Records. That same year, he worked with [This Is the Kit](/wiki/This_Is_the_Kit) for their album *Bashed Out*, which was released through Dessner's Brassland Records.

In 2016, Dessner produced [Lisa Hannigan](/wiki/Lisa_Hannigan)'s album *[At Swim](/wiki/At_Swim)*. The album reached No. 1 in Ireland, No. 7 on the US [Top Heatseekers Albums](/wiki/Top_Heatseekers_Albums) chart and No. 24 in the UK, and received widespread critical acclaim. *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)*, which awarded it four out of five stars, commented on the album's "stunningly pretty songs with quietly powerful undertones" and the *[Evening Standard](/wiki/Evening_Standard)*, which also awarded it four out of five stars, praised the "new-found accessibility" of the album. Additionally, Dessner produced *[Day of the Dead](/wiki/Day_of_the_Dead_(2016_album))*, which was released in 2016, and *[Dark Was the Night](/wiki/Dark_Was_the_Night_(album))*, both of which are AIDS charity compilations for the Red Hot Organization; and [Doveman](/wiki/Doveman)'s 2009 release *The Conformist*. [Frightened Rabbit](/wiki/Frightened_Rabbit)'s fifth album *[Painting of a Panic Attack](/wiki/Painting_of_a_Panic_Attack)*, released in 2016 via [Atlantic Records](/wiki/Atlantic_Records), was also produced by Dessner. Dessner produced the Lone Bellow's 2020 album *[Half Moon Light](/wiki/Half_Moon_Light)* and co-wrote several tracks on it. He also co-wrote and produced the acclaimed debut of UK songwriter Eve Owen, *Don't Let the Ink Dry*, released in 2020.

In April 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), Dessner was approached by American singer-songwriter [Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift) to collaborate on music, and he subsequently produced and co-wrote her eighth studio album, *[Folklore](/wiki/Folklore_(Taylor_Swift_album))*. It was a surprise release that debuted to critical acclaim on July 24, 2020, and won [Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year) at the [63rd Annual Grammy Awards](/wiki/63rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards) in 2021. Dessner continued his collaboration with Swift on her subsequent albums *[Evermore](/wiki/Evermore)* (2020), *[Midnights](/wiki/Midnights)* (2022), and *[The Tortured Poets Department](/wiki/The_Tortured_Poets_Department)* (2024), as well as her [re-recorded](/wiki/Re-recording_(music)) albums *[Fearless (Taylor's Version)](/wiki/Fearless_(Taylor%27s_Version))* (2021), *[Red (Taylor's Version)](/wiki/Red_(Taylor%27s_Version))* (2021), and *[Speak Now (Taylor's Version)](/wiki/Speak_Now_(Taylor%27s_Version))* (2023). After the release of *Folklore*, the documentary film *[Folklore: The Long Pond Studio Sessions](/wiki/Folklore:_The_Long_Pond_Studio_Sessions)* and *Evermore*, artists such as [Maya Hawke](/wiki/Maya_Hawke), [Gracie Abrams](/wiki/Gracie_Abrams), [Ed Sheeran](/wiki/Ed_Sheeran), [King Princess](/wiki/King_Princess), and [Girl in Red](/wiki/Girl_in_Red) expressed interest in collaborating with Dessner and recording songs at his Long Pond Studio. Dessner stated, "After Taylor, it was a bit crazy how many people reached out. And getting to meet and write songs with people you wouldn't have had access to... I'm so grateful for it."

Dessner also co-produced British singer-songwriter [Ben Howard](/wiki/Ben_Howard)'s fourth studio album, *[Collections from the Whiteout](/wiki/Collections_from_the_Whiteout)*, alongside Howard himself, which was released on March 26, 2021. On October 7, 2022, [Indigo Sparke](/wiki/Indigo_Sparke)'s sophomore album was released with Dessner credited as a producer, co-writer, and musician. On Instagram, Dessner congratulated Sparke on her upcoming single, saying that together they "made one of [his] favorite records [he's] ever been a part of".

### Film scores

Dessner and his brother Bryce co-composed the score for *[Transpecos](/wiki/Transpecos_(film))*, which won the Audience Award at the 2016 [South by Southwest](/wiki/South_by_Southwest). They also worked together on the score for 2013 film *[Big Sur](/wiki/Big_Sur_(film))*, an adaptation of the 1962 novel of the same name by [Jack Kerouac](/wiki/Jack_Kerouac). The film debuted on January 23, 2013, at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival), where it received positive reviews. In 2021, together with his brother, Dessner scored two films – [Mike Mills](/wiki/Mike_Mills_(director))' *[C'mon C'mon](/wiki/C%27mon_C%27mon_(film))* and *[Cyrano](/wiki/Cyrano_(film))*.

## Collaborations

### Day of the Dead

On March 17, 2016, Aaron and [Bryce Dessner](/wiki/Bryce_Dessner) announced *Day of the Dead*, a charity tribute album to the [Grateful Dead](/wiki/Grateful_Dead) released by 4AD on May 20, 2016. *Day of the Dead* was created, curated and produced by both Dessner brothers. The compilation is a wide-ranging tribute to the songwriting and experimentalism of the Dead which took four years to record, features over 60 artists from varied musical backgrounds, 59 tracks and is almost 6 hours long. All profits will help fight for AIDS/ HIV and related health issues around the world through the Red Hot Organization. *Day of the Dead* is the follow-up to 2009's *Dark Was the Night* (4AD), a 32-track, multi-artist compilation also produced by the Dessner brothers for Red Hot.

Of the 59 tracks on the compilation, many feature a house band made up of Aaron, Bryce, fellow the National bandmates and brothers Scott and [Bryan Devendorf](/wiki/Bryan_Devendorf), Josh Kaufman (who co-produced the project), and Conrad Doucette along with Sam Cohen and Walter Martin. The National have a couple of tracks on the album, including "Peggy-O", "Morning Dew" and "I Know You Rider".

A *Day of the Dead* live performance took place in August 2016 at the second annual Eaux Claires Festival (August 12–13) featuring Jenny Lewis, Matthew Houck, [Lucius](/wiki/Lucius), [Will Oldham](/wiki/Will_Oldham), [Sam Amidon](/wiki/Sam_Amidon), [Richard Reed Parry](/wiki/Richard_Reed_Parry), Justin Vernon, [Bruce Hornsby](/wiki/Bruce_Hornsby), Ruban Nielson and The National.

### Forever Love

Forever Love is a collaboration between Aaron and Bryce Dessner, renowned Icelandic performance artist [Ragnar Kjartansson](/wiki/Ragnar_Kjartansson_(performance_artist)) and Gyða and Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir, formerly of the Icelandic band [múm](/wiki/M%C3%BAm). The project is a blending of visual and performance art with live music, all centered around a song cycle written and performed by Aaron and Bryce alongside Gyða and Kristín Anna Valtýsdóttir. It was commissioned by Eaux Claires Festival and made its world premiere in 2015 where it served as the official starting point of the festival on both Friday and Saturday.

Forever Love marked a live performance reunion for the Dessner twins and Kjartansson, as both artists had previously collaborated on a six-hour video work, A Lot of Sorrow, which documents the National performing their three-song "Sorrow" for six hours in front of a live audience at [MoMA PS1](/wiki/MoMA_PS1).

### The Long Count

The Long Count was a large commission for the BAM Next Wave Festival in 2009. Dessner and his brother, Bryce, worked alongside visual artist [Matthew Ritchie](/wiki/Matthew_Ritchie), creating a work loosely based on the Mayan creation story [Popol Vuh](/wiki/Popol_Vuh). The work included a 12-piece orchestra and a number of guest singers, including Kim and Kelley Deal ([the Breeders](/wiki/The_Breeders), [the Pixies](/wiki/The_Pixies)), [Matt Berninger](/wiki/Matt_Berninger) (the National), [Shara Worden](/wiki/Shara_Worden) ([My Brightest Diamond](/wiki/My_Brightest_Diamond)), and [Tunde Adebimpe](/wiki/Tunde_Adebimpe) ([TV on the Radio](/wiki/TV_on_the_Radio)). The work had its world premiere at the [Krannert Center for the Performing Arts](/wiki/Krannert_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) in September 2009 as part of the Ellnora Festival.

### Dark Was the Night

Main article: [Dark Was the Night (album)](/wiki/Dark_Was_the_Night_(album))

In 2009, Aaron and Bryce Dessner produced an extensive AIDS charity compilation, *Dark Was the Night*, for the Red Hot Organization. *Dark Was the Night* has raised over 2 million dollars for AIDS charities as of January 2012. In 2009, Dessner contributed a track to the Dark Was the Night compilation. On May 3, 2009, 4AD and Red Hot produced *Dark Was the Night* – Live, a concert celebrating the newest Red Hot album. The show took place at [Radio City Music Hall](/wiki/Radio_City_Music_Hall) and featured several of the artists that contributed to the compilation.

### Other collaborations and contributions

Dessner is a frequent collaborator with a wide range of musicians, including his brother and bandmate [Bryce Dessner](/wiki/Bryce_Dessner). In August 2008, Aaron and Bryce performed a collaborative concert with David Cossin, and Luca Tarantino as a part of Soundres, an international residency program for contemporary music and art in Salento, Italy and at the Guitare Au Palais Festival Perpignan France. They also performed at Matthew Ritchie's Ghost Operator opening at the White Cube Gallery in London. Aaron and Bryce have also collaborated with many world-renowned orchestras. Most recently the brothers played with the Copenhagen Philharmonic in a concert billed as "Sixty Minutes of the Dessners".

The program included "St. Carolyn by the Sea", "Lachrimae" and "Raphael", all of which were composed by Bryce Dessner. Bryce and Aaron have also performed "St. Carolyn by the Sea" and "Raphael" with the Amsterdam Sinfonietta during the [Holland Festival](/wiki/Holland_Festival). These performances took place at [Muziekgebouw aan 't IJ](/wiki/Muziekgebouw_aan_%27t_IJ) in Amsterdam and Muziekgebouw Frits Philips in Eindhoven. In October 2011, Dessner joined his brother to perform "St. Carolyn by the Sea" with the [American Composers Orchestra](/wiki/American_Composers_Orchestra) at The World Financial Center in New York City.

Marshall Curry's award-winning documentary Racing Dreams includes music contributed by both Dessner and the National. "Win Win" directed by Thomas McCarthy closes with "Think You Can Wait", a track written by Dessner and Matt Berninger, recorded by the National with additional vocals from Sharon Van Etten. Dessner also played an important role in the National's contribution to the HBO show [Game of Thrones](/wiki/Game_of_Thrones).

In March 2012, Dessner, Scott Devendorf and Bryan Devendorf teamed up with Grateful Dead guitarist Bob Weir for a live webcast, which featured two musical sets and a political discussion. The event was produced by Head Count, a non-partisan organization that uses live music events to promote voter registration and awareness. In May 2012, the National's performance of "Rains of Castamere" played during the closing credits of season two episode nine. In December 2012, Dessner curated a day of Other Voices, an Irish music festival that aired live on RTÉ Two in Ireland. The performances took place in the Church of St James in Dingle, County Kerry.

## Curator

### Eaux Claires Music & Arts Festival

Eaux Claires is a music festival founded and curated by Dessner and Justin Vernon. The inaugural festival took place in July 2015 in Vernon's hometown of [Eau Claire, Wisconsin](/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin). Speaking about the festival, Dessner and Vernon said they were driven by the idea that this festival would "encourage music-genre-walls to melt away".

### PEOPLE Festival

PEOPLE Festival is a not-for-profit gathering of artists in Berlin for a weekend-long festival of music curated and produced by Aaron Dessner along with his brother Bryce Dessner, [Bon Iver](/wiki/Bon_Iver), Berlin's Michelberger Hotel and others. The first festival took place October 1–2, 2016, under the name "Michelberger Music" in the Funkhaus, the historical radio recording studios of the former GDR.

### Boston Calling Music Festival

Boston Calling is a music festival co-curated by Dessner. Debuting in May 2013, Boston Calling previously took place twice a year, May and September, at [City Hall Plaza](/wiki/Boston_City_Hall_Plaza). Boston Calling moved to the [Harvard Athletic Complex](/wiki/Harvard_Stadium) in [Allston](/wiki/Allston) in May 2017 and transitioned to one festival per year.

## Record labels

Dessner co-founded the record label 37d03d (read: PEOPLE upside down) with [Justin Vernon](/wiki/Justin_Vernon) and [Bryce Dessner](/wiki/Bryce_Dessner).

Alongside Alec Hanley Bemis and Bryce Dessner, Dessner founded [Brassland Records](/wiki/Brassland_Records), a label that has released albums from the National, [This Is the Kit](/wiki/This_Is_the_Kit), [Clogs](/wiki/Clogs_(band)), Doveman and [Nico Muhly](/wiki/Nico_Muhly).

## Musical equipment

Dessner often plays a [1965 Gibson Firebird](/wiki/Gibson_Firebird) purchased on [eBay](/wiki/EBay) and refurbished. During the *Sleep Well Beast* recording sessions, he used the Firebird as well as a 1972 [Fender Telecaster](/wiki/Fender_Telecaster). During live shows, Dessner usually plays the Firebird and a 1963 [Fender Jazzmaster](/wiki/Fender_Jazzmaster).

For the *Sleep Well Beast* sessions, amplifiers used include a 1959 [Fender Champ](/wiki/Fender_Champ), a 1960s [Fender Princeton](/wiki/Fender_Princeton), a 1970s [Music Man](/wiki/Music_Man_(company)), an [Ampeg](/wiki/Ampeg) Gemini, and a [Fender Bassman](/wiki/Fender_Bassman).

## Personal life

Dessner married Stine Wengler around 2016. They have three children, one of whom is named Robin. Taylor Swift's [song of the same name](/wiki/Robin_(Taylor_Swift_song)) is speculated to be about him. Dessner is a keen [soccer](/wiki/Association_football) fan and an avid supporter of [Liverpool F.C.](/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.)

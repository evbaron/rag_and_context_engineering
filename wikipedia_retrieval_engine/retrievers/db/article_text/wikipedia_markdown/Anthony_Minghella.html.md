**Anthony Minghella** (6 January 1954 – 18 March 2008) was a British playwright and filmmaker. He was chairman of the board of Governors at the [British Film Institute](/wiki/British_Film_Institute) between 2003 and 2007. He directed *[Truly, Madly, Deeply](/wiki/Truly,_Madly,_Deeply_(film))* (1990), *[The English Patient](/wiki/The_English_Patient_(film))* (1996), *[The Talented Mr. Ripley](/wiki/The_Talented_Mr._Ripley_(film))* (1999), and *[Cold Mountain](/wiki/Cold_Mountain_(film))* (2003), and produced *[Iris](/wiki/Iris_(2001_film))* (2001).

He received the [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) for *The English Patient*. In addition, he received three more [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations; he was nominated for [Best Adapted Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Adapted_Screenplay) for both *The English Patient* and *The Talented Mr. Ripley*, and was posthumously nominated for [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) for *[The Reader](/wiki/The_Reader_(2008_film))* (2008), as a producer.

## Early life and education

Minghella was born in [Ryde](/wiki/Ryde) on the [Isle of Wight](/wiki/Isle_of_Wight). His family are well known on the island, where they ran a café in Ryde until the 1980s and have run an eponymous business making and selling [Italian-style](/wiki/Gelato) [ice cream](/wiki/Ice_cream) since the 1950s. His parents were Edoardo Minghella (an Italian immigrant) and Leeds-born Gloria Alberta (née Arcari). His mother's ancestors originally came from [Valvori](/wiki/Valvori), a small village in southern [Lazio](/wiki/Lazio), Italy. He was one of five children, his sisters Gioia Minghella-Giddens, Edana Minghella and [Loretta Minghella](/wiki/Loretta_Minghella), and a brother [Dominic Minghella](/wiki/Dominic_Minghella) who also became a screenwriter and producer.

Minghella attended St. Mary's Catholic Primary School, Ryde, Sandown Grammar School, and [St John's College, Portsmouth](/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Portsmouth). Early interests suggested a possible career as a musician, with Minghella playing keyboards with local bands Earthlight and Dancer. The latter recorded an album titled *Tales of the Riverbank* in 1972, although it was not released until 2001.

He attended the [University of Hull](/wiki/University_of_Hull), studying drama. As an undergraduate he had arrived at university with an EMI contract for the band, in which he sang and played keyboards; while at university he wrote words and music for an adaptation of [Gabriel Josipovici](/wiki/Gabriel_Josipovici)'s *Mobius the Stripper* (1975).

Minghella graduated after three years and stayed on to pursue a PhD. He also taught at the university for several years, on [Samuel Beckett](/wiki/Samuel_Beckett) and on the [medieval theatre](/wiki/Medieval_theatre). Ultimately, he abandoned his pursuit of a PhD to work for the [BBC](/wiki/BBC).

## Career

Minghella's debut work was a stage adaptation of Gabriel Josipovici's *Mobius the Stripper* (1975) and it was his *Whale Music* (1985) that brought him notice. His double bill of Samuel Beckett's *[Play](/wiki/Play_(play))* and *[Happy Days](/wiki/Happy_Days_(play))* was his directorial debut and debut feature film as a director was *[A Little Like Drowning](/wiki/A_Little_Like_Drowning)* (1978). During the 1980s, he worked in television, starting as a [runner](/wiki/Television_crew#runner) on *[Magpie](/wiki/Magpie_(TV_series))* before moving into [script editing](/wiki/Script_editor) the children's drama series *[Grange Hill](/wiki/Grange_Hill)* for the [BBC](/wiki/BBC) and later writing *[The Storyteller](/wiki/The_Storyteller_(TV_series))* series for [Jim Henson](/wiki/Jim_Henson). He wrote three episodes of the [ITV](/wiki/ITV_(TV_network)) detective drama *[Inspector Morse](/wiki/Inspector_Morse_(TV_series))* and an episode of long-running ITV drama *[Boon](/wiki/Boon_(TV_series))*. *[Made in Bangkok](/wiki/Made_in_Bangkok)* (1986) found mainstream success in the [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre).

Radio success followed with a [Giles Cooper Award](/wiki/Giles_Cooper_Awards) for the radio drama *Cigarettes and Chocolate* first broadcast on [BBC Radio 4](/wiki/BBC_Radio_4) in 1988. It was revived on 3 May 2008 as a tribute to its author director following his death. His production starred [Juliet Stevenson](/wiki/Juliet_Stevenson), [Bill Nighy](/wiki/Bill_Nighy) and [Jenny Howe](/wiki/Jenny_Howe). His first radio play *Hang Up*, starring [Anton Lesser](/wiki/Anton_Lesser) and Juliet Stevenson, was revived on 10 May 2008 as part of the BBC Radio 4 Minghella season.

*[Truly, Madly, Deeply](/wiki/Truly,_Madly,_Deeply_(film))* (1990), a feature drama written and directed for the BBC's *[Screen Two](/wiki/Screen_Two)* anthology strand, bypassed TV broadcast and instead had a cinema release. He turned down an offer to direct another Inspector Morse to do the project, even though he believed that the Morse episode would have been a much higher-profile assignment. *[The English Patient](/wiki/The_English_Patient_(film))* (1996) brought him two [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations, Best Director (which he won) and [Adapted Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Writing_(Adapted_Screenplay)). He also received an Adapted Screenplay nomination for *[The Talented Mr. Ripley](/wiki/The_Talented_Mr._Ripley_(film))* (1999).

His adaptation of *[The No. 1 Ladies' Detective Agency](/wiki/The_No._1_Ladies%27_Detective_Agency_(TV_series))*, which he co-wrote and directed, was broadcast posthumously on [BBC One](/wiki/BBC_One) (23 March 2008) as a TV movie; watched by 6.3 million viewers. He vocally supported *I Know I'm Not Alone*, a film of musician [Michael Franti](/wiki/Michael_Franti)'s peacemaking excursions into [Iraq](/wiki/Iraq), [Palestine](/wiki/Palestinian_territories) and [Israel](/wiki/Israel). He directed a [party election broadcast](/wiki/Party_political_broadcast) for the [Labour Party](/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK)) in 2005. The short film depicted [Tony Blair](/wiki/Tony_Blair) and [Gordon Brown](/wiki/Gordon_Brown) working together and was criticised for being insincere: "The Anthony Minghella party political broadcast last year was full of body language fibs", said Peter Collett, a psychologist at the [University of Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford). "When you are talking to me, I'll give you my full attention only if I think you are very high status or if I love you. On that party political broadcast, they are staring at each other like lovers. It is completely false."

With Samuel Beckett's 100th birthday celebrations, he returned to radio on [BBC Radio 3](/wiki/BBC_Radio_3) with *Eyes Down Looking* (2006), with: [Jude Law](/wiki/Jude_Law), Juliet Stevenson and [David Threlfall](/wiki/David_Threlfall). An operatic directorial debut came with [Puccini](/wiki/Giacomo_Puccini)'s *[Madama Butterfly](/wiki/Madama_Butterfly)*. Premiered at the [English National Opera](/wiki/English_National_Opera) (London, 2005), then at the [Lithuanian National Opera and Ballet Theatre](/wiki/Lithuanian_National_Opera_and_Ballet_Theatre) (Vilnius, March 2006) and at the [Metropolitan Opera](/wiki/Metropolitan_Opera) (New York City, September 2006). The latter was transmitted live into cinemas worldwide (7 March 2009) as part of the [Met's HD series](/wiki/Metropolitan_Opera_Live_in_HD) and is now available on DVD. The ENO work was to have led to other operatic projects, directing again at English National Opera and collaborating with [Osvaldo Golijov](/wiki/Osvaldo_Golijov) on a new opera for the Met and ENO, writing the libretto and directing the production.

He was honoured with the naming of The Anthony Minghella Theatre at the [Quay Arts Centre](/wiki/The_Quay_Arts) ([Isle of Wight](/wiki/Isle_of_Wight)). He made an appearance in the 2007 film *[Atonement](/wiki/Atonement_(2007_film))* as a television host interviewing the novelist central to the story.

His last work was the screenplay of [the film adaptation of](/wiki/Nine_(2009_live-action_film)) the Tony Award-winning musical *[Nine](/wiki/Nine_(musical))* (1982); [Arthur Kopit](/wiki/Arthur_Kopit) (book) and [Maury Yeston](/wiki/Maury_Yeston) (score). It is based on the film *[8½](/wiki/8%C2%BD)*. He shared credit with [Michael Tolkin](/wiki/Michael_Tolkin) on the screenplay.

[The department of Film, Theatre & Television](https://www.reading.ac.uk/film-theatre-television/) at the [University of Reading](/wiki/University_of_Reading), opened in 2012, was named in his honour.

## Personal life and death

Minghella met his first wife, Yvonne Millar, when they were students. They had one daughter, and later divorced. In 1985 Minghella married Hong Kong–born choreographer and dancer Carolyn Jane Choa. They had one son, [Max](/wiki/Max_Minghella).

Minghella's younger brother, [Dominic Minghella](/wiki/Dominic_Minghella), is the creator of the television series *[Robin Hood](/wiki/Robin_Hood_(2006_TV_series))* and *[Doc Martin](/wiki/Doc_Martin)*. His sister, [Loretta Minghella](/wiki/Loretta_Minghella), is Master of [Clare College, Cambridge](/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge).

Minghella was a fan of [Portsmouth F.C.](/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.), and appeared in the [Channel 4](/wiki/Channel_4) documentary, *Hallowed Be Thy Game*. His home had two double bedrooms dedicated to the display of Portsmouth memorabilia dating back to the club's founding in 1898.

Minghella died of a [haemorrhage](/wiki/Bleeding) on 18 March 2008 in [Charing Cross Hospital](/wiki/Charing_Cross_Hospital), Hammersmith, following an operation the previous week to remove cancer of the [tonsils and neck](/wiki/Head_and_neck_cancer).He was 54 years old.

## Memorial plaques

A [memorial plaque](/wiki/Memorial_plaque) to Minghella was unveiled on 2 March 2016 by [Jude Law](/wiki/Jude_Law), at Western Gardens, Ryde, Isle of Wight.
He is commemorated with a [green plaque](/wiki/Green_plaque) on [The Avenues, Kingston upon Hull](/wiki/The_Avenues,_Kingston_upon_Hull). The 2009 film *[Nine](/wiki/Nine_(2009_live-action_film))* is dedicated in his memory.

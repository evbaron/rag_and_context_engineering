**Stanley Tucci Jr.** ([/ˈtuːtʃi/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*TOO-chee*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key) Italian pronunciation: [[ˈtuttʃi]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian); born November 11, 1960) is an American actor. Known as a [character actor](/wiki/Character_actor), he has played a [wide variety of roles](/wiki/Stanley_Tucci_on_screen_and_stage), earning [numerous accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Stanley_Tucci) for his work.

Tucci made his film debut in [John Huston](/wiki/John_Huston)'s *[Prizzi's Honor](/wiki/Prizzi%27s_Honor)* (1985), before taking supporting roles in films such as *[Deconstructing Harry](/wiki/Deconstructing_Harry)* (1997), *[Road to Perdition](/wiki/Road_to_Perdition)* (2002), and *[The Terminal](/wiki/The_Terminal)* (2004). He was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) for playing a sinister neighbor in *[The Lovely Bones](/wiki/The_Lovely_Bones_(film))* (2009). He also acted in *[The Devil Wears Prada](/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(film))* (2006), *[Julie & Julia](/wiki/Julie_%26_Julia)* (2009), [*Burlesque*](/wiki/Burlesque_(2010_American_film)) (2010), *[Easy A](/wiki/Easy_A)* (2010), *[Captain America: The First Avenger](/wiki/Captain_America:_The_First_Avenger)* (2011), *[Margin Call](/wiki/Margin_Call)* (2011), [*The Hunger Games* film series](/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_(franchise)#Films) (2012–2015), [*Transformers* films](/wiki/Transformers_(film_series)) (2014–2017), *[Spotlight](/wiki/Spotlight_(film))* (2015), *[Supernova](/wiki/Supernova_(2020_film))* (2020), *[Worth](/wiki/Worth_(film))* (2021), [*Conclave*](/wiki/Conclave_(film)) (2024), and *[The Devil Wears Prada 2](/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_2)* (2026). He made his directorial debut with the comedy *[Big Night](/wiki/Big_Night)* (1996), which he also co-wrote and starred.

He has starred in numerous television series such as the legal drama [*Murder One*](/wiki/Murder_One_(TV_series)) (1995–1997), the medical drama *[3 lbs](/wiki/3_lbs)* (2006), [Ryan Murphy](/wiki/Ryan_Murphy_(producer))'s limited series [*Feud: Bette & Joan*](/wiki/Feud_(American_TV_series)) (2017), and the drama [*Limetown*](/wiki/Limetown_(TV_series)) (2018). He played [Stanley Kubrick](/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick) in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) film *[The Life and Death of Peter Sellers](/wiki/The_Life_and_Death_of_Peter_Sellers)* (2004). For his portrayal of [Walter Winchell](/wiki/Walter_Winchell) in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) film *[Winchell](/wiki/Winchell_(film))* (1998) he received the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Miniseries or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Limited_or_Anthology_Series_or_Movie). From 2020 to 2022, Tucci voiced Bitsy Brandenham in the [Apple TV+](/wiki/Apple_TV%2B) animated series *[Central Park](/wiki/Central_Park_(TV_series))*.

From 2021 to 2022, he hosted the [CNN](/wiki/CNN) food and travel documentary series *[Stanley Tucci: Searching for Italy](/wiki/Stanley_Tucci:_Searching_for_Italy)* for which he won two consecutive [Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Hosted Nonfiction Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Hosted_Nonfiction_Series_or_Special). He was nominated for a [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Play) for his role in *[Frankie and Johnny in the Clair de Lune](/wiki/Frankie_and_Johnny_in_the_Clair_de_Lune)* (2003), and a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for narrating the audiobook *The One and Only Shrek!* (2008).

## Early life and education

Tucci was born on November 11, 1960, in [Peekskill, New York](/wiki/Peekskill,_New_York), and grew up in nearby [Katonah, New York](/wiki/Katonah,_New_York). His parents, Joan (née Tropiano), a secretary and writer, and Stanley Tucci Sr. an art teacher at [Horace Greeley High School](/wiki/Horace_Greeley_High_School) in [Chappaqua, New York](/wiki/Chappaqua,_New_York), both of Italian descent, have distant roots in the town of [Marzi](/wiki/Marzi,_Calabria) in [Calabria](/wiki/Calabria), Southern Italy. Tucci is the oldest of three children, including his sister, actress [Christine Tucci](/wiki/Christine_Tucci). Screenwriter Joseph Tropiano is a cousin. During the early 1970s, he spent a year living in [Florence](/wiki/Florence), Italy.

He attended [John Jay High School](/wiki/John_Jay_High_School_(Cross_River,_New_York)), in [Lewisboro, New York](/wiki/Lewisboro,_New_York), where he played on the soccer and baseball teams, although his main interest lay in the school's drama club, where he and fellow actor and high school friend [Campbell Scott](/wiki/Campbell_Scott), son of actors [George C. Scott](/wiki/George_C._Scott) and [Colleen Dewhurst](/wiki/Colleen_Dewhurst), gave well-received performances at many of John Jay's drama club productions. Tucci then attended [State University of New York at Purchase](/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_at_Purchase), where he majored in acting and graduated in 1982. Among his classmates at SUNY Purchase was fellow acting student [Ving Rhames](/wiki/Ving_Rhames). It was Tucci who gave Rhames, born Irving, the nickname "Ving".

## Career

### 1982–1995: early roles and Broadway debut

In 1982, Tucci earned his [Actors' Equity](/wiki/Actors%27_Equity) card when actress [Colleen Dewhurst](/wiki/Colleen_Dewhurst), the mother of Tucci's high-school friend, actor [Campbell Scott](/wiki/Campbell_Scott), arranged for the two young men to have parts as soldiers in a Broadway play in which she was co-starring, *The Queen and the Rebels* which premiered on September 30, 1982. Around this time, Tucci also worked as a model, with his most notable work being a television commercial for [Levi's](/wiki/Levi%27s) 501 jeans. In 1985, Tucci made his film debut as a soldier in [John Huston](/wiki/John_Huston)'s black comedy crime film *[Prizzi's Honor](/wiki/Prizzi%27s_Honor)* starring [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson) and [Kathleen Turner](/wiki/Kathleen_Turner). During this period, Tucci portrayed minor and supporting roles in a number of films, including the psychological horror film [*Monkey Shines*](/wiki/Monkey_Shines_(film)) (1988), the comedy-drama *[Slaves of New York](/wiki/Slaves_of_New_York)* (1989) and the comedy *[Fear, Anxiety & Depression](/wiki/Fear,_Anxiety_%26_Depression)* (1989).

In 1991, he acted in the biographical drama film [*Billy Bathgate*](/wiki/Billy_Bathgate_(film)) starring [Dustin Hoffman](/wiki/Dustin_Hoffman) and [Nicole Kidman](/wiki/Nicole_Kidman). Here, Tucci portrayed mobster [Lucky Luciano](/wiki/Lucky_Luciano). That same year he performed the role of [Scapino](/wiki/Scapino) at the [Yale Repertory Theatre](/wiki/Yale_Repertory_Theatre) in [Molière](/wiki/Moli%C3%A8re)'s *[Scapin](/wiki/Scapin_the_Schemer).* In 1992, Tucci acted in the family comedy [*Beethoven*](/wiki/Beethoven_(film)) and the romantic fantasy [*Prelude to a Kiss*](/wiki/Prelude_to_a_Kiss_(film)). The former spawned a [franchise of the same name](/wiki/Beethoven_(franchise)) due to the success of the 1992 film. Tucci continued to take roles in films such as the legal thriller *[The Pelican Brief](/wiki/The_Pelican_Brief_(film))* (1993) starring [Denzel Washington](/wiki/Denzel_Washington) and [Julia Roberts](/wiki/Julia_Roberts), and the romantic comedy *[It Could Happen to You](/wiki/It_Could_Happen_to_You_(1994_film))* (1994) with [Nicolas Cage](/wiki/Nicolas_Cage) and [Bridget Fonda](/wiki/Bridget_Fonda). From 1995 to 1996, Tucci starred in the television series [*Murder One*](/wiki/Murder_One_(TV_series)) as the mysterious Richard Cross. Tucci received his first [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nomination for his performance in the series, specifically for [Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Drama Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series). Following disappointing ratings, ABC decided to revamp the series resulting in Tucci being dismissed from the series.

### 1996–2005: directorial debut and other roles

In 1996, Tucci co-wrote and co-directed the comedy-drama film *[Big Night](/wiki/Big_Night)*. Tucci co-wrote the screenplay with his cousin Joseph Tropiano, and directed the film with his friend Campbell Scott. Tucci starred with [Tony Shalhoub](/wiki/Tony_Shalhoub) as two brothers trying to make their authentic Italian restaurant a success at a place and time where spaghetti and meatballs are the height of gastronomic sophistication.

The film premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival) where it was nominated for the "Grand Jury Prize". Tucci and Tropiano won the [Independent Spirit Award for Best First Screenplay](/wiki/Independent_Spirit_Award_for_Best_First_Screenplay) for writing the screenplay of the film. The film also featured his sister [Christine](/wiki/Christine_Tucci) and their mother, who wrote a cookbook for the film. The film also starred [Minnie Driver](/wiki/Minnie_Driver), [Isabella Rossellini](/wiki/Isabella_Rossellini), [Ian Holm](/wiki/Ian_Holm), and [Allison Janney](/wiki/Allison_Janney). Critic [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) praised the film writing, "To some degree this film must represent a break for [Tucci]: He has been in good movies before, but not enough of them...Now here is their labor of love. Their perfect risotto. They include just what is needed and nothing else."

That same year, Tucci also acted in the independent drama *[The Daytrippers](/wiki/The_Daytrippers)* which was written and directed by [Greg Mottola](/wiki/Greg_Mottola). The film also starred [Hope Davis](/wiki/Hope_Davis), [Liev Schreiber](/wiki/Liev_Schreiber), and [Parker Posey](/wiki/Parker_Posey). The following year, he appeared in [Woody Allen](/wiki/Woody_Allen)'s comedy *[Deconstructing Harry](/wiki/Deconstructing_Harry)* which received an [Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay) nomination. In 1998, Tucci wrote, directed, co-produced and starred in the comedy film *[The Impostors](/wiki/The_Impostors)*. The film starred Tucci and [Oliver Platt](/wiki/Oliver_Platt) who play [Laurel and Hardy](/wiki/Laurel_and_Hardy) like comedians who are struggling in the 1930s. The film premiered at the [1998 Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/1998_Cannes_Film_Festival) where it received positive reviews. Shortly thereafter, Tucci starred in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) biographical television film [*Winchell*](/wiki/Winchell_(film)) (1998) in which Tucci portrayed columnist [Walter Winchell](/wiki/Walter_Winchell). For his portrayal of Winchell, Tucci won the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Limited Series or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie) and the [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Miniseries or Television Film](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Miniseries_or_Television_Film). Tucci also received a [Screen Actors Guild Awards](/wiki/Screen_Actors_Guild_Awards) nomination for his performance in the film.

In 1999, he played [Robin Goodfellow / Puck](/wiki/Robin_Goodfellow_(Shakespeare)) in the [Michael Hoffman](/wiki/Michael_Hoffman_(director)) [adaptation](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream_(1999_film)) of *[A Midsummer Night's Dream](/wiki/A_Midsummer_Night%27s_Dream)*. In 2000, Tucci directed, produced and starred in the drama film [*Joe Gould's Secret*](/wiki/Joe_Gould%27s_Secret_(film)), based on [a 1964 biographical essay about Gould](/wiki/Joe_Gould%27s_Secret) by *[The New Yorker](/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* reporter [Joseph Mitchell](/wiki/Joseph_Mitchell_(writer)). In 2001, Tucci starred in the HBO television war film [*Conspiracy*](/wiki/Conspiracy_(2001_film)) as [Adolf Eichmann](/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann). The project also starred [Kenneth Branagh](/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh) and [Colin Firth](/wiki/Colin_Firth). For his portrayal of Eichmann, Tucci won another Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Miniseries or Television Film. The film was critically acclaimed and won a [Peabody Award](/wiki/Peabody_Award).

In 2002, Tucci returned to the stage by starring in the revival of [Terrence McNally](/wiki/Terrence_McNally)'s *[Frankie and Johnny in the Clair de Lune](/wiki/Frankie_and_Johnny_in_the_Clair_de_Lune).* Tucci received a [Tony Award for Best Actor in a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Actor_in_a_Play) nomination for his performance in the play. Also in 2002, he starred in [Sam Mendes](/wiki/Sam_Mendes)' *[Road to Perdition](/wiki/Road_to_Perdition)* opposite [Tom Hanks](/wiki/Tom_Hanks). The film went on to receive $181 million at the box office and received six [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) nominations. He reunited with Hanks in [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s *[The Terminal](/wiki/The_Terminal)* (2004). That same year, Tucci appeared in *[Shall We Dance](/wiki/Shall_We_Dance_(2004_film))* (2004). Tucci also portrayed [Stanley Kubrick](/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick) in the HBO television film, *[The Life and Death of Peter Sellers](/wiki/The_Life_and_Death_of_Peter_Sellers)* (2004). He also was a guest caller in an episode of *[Frasier](/wiki/Frasier)*. Also that year, [Caedmon Audio](/wiki/Caedmon_Audio) released an audiobook of Tucci reading [Kurt Vonnegut](/wiki/Kurt_Vonnegut)'s 1973 novel *[Breakfast of Champions](/wiki/Breakfast_of_Champions)*. In 2005, Tucci had his first voice role in the animated film [*Robots*](/wiki/Robots_(2005_film)), which features other notable actors' voices such as [Ewan McGregor](/wiki/Ewan_McGregor), [Halle Berry](/wiki/Halle_Berry), and [Robin Williams](/wiki/Robin_Williams).

### 2006–2010: work with Meryl Streep and acclaim

Tucci acted opposite [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep) in *[The Devil Wears Prada](/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(film))* (2006) and *[Julie & Julia](/wiki/Julie_%26_Julia)* (2009)

In 2006, Tucci had a major role in the comedy film *[The Devil Wears Prada](/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(film))*, opposite [Meryl Streep](/wiki/Meryl_Streep), [Anne Hathaway](/wiki/Anne_Hathaway), and [Emily Blunt](/wiki/Emily_Blunt). Tucci portrays Nigel Kipling, a loyal confidant of Miranda Priestly (Streep). The film was a breakout role for him, with [A.O. Scott](/wiki/A.O._Scott) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* declaring, "Mr. Tucci ... has never been better". The film was Tucci's highest-grossing film until *[Captain America: The First Avenger](/wiki/Captain_America:_The_First_Avenger)* in 2011. Also in 2006, Tucci made an appearance on the television series *[Monk](/wiki/Monk_(TV_series))*, for which he won the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series). Tucci returned to broadcast television by starring in the drama series *[3 lbs](/wiki/3_lbs)*, which premiered on November 14, 2006. However, due to low ratings CBS cancelled the series. The following year, Tucci went on to recur in the medical drama series *[ER](/wiki/ER_(TV_series))*. For his performance in *ER,* Tucci was nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Drama Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Guest_Actor_in_a_Drama_Series). He was nominated for a [Grammy Award](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for narrating the audiobook *The One and Only Shrek!* (2008).

In 2009, Tucci portrayed George Harvey, a serial killer of young girls, in *[The Lovely Bones](/wiki/The_Lovely_Bones_(film))* starring [Saoirse Ronan](/wiki/Saoirse_Ronan). The film, [Peter Jackson](/wiki/Peter_Jackson)'s adaptation of [Alice Sebold](/wiki/Alice_Sebold)'s [novel](/wiki/The_Lovely_Bones), earned Tucci [Academy](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor) and [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture) award nominations for Best Supporting Actor. To prepare for the role, he consulted with retired FBI [profiler](/wiki/Offender_profiling) [John Douglas](/wiki/John_E._Douglas). Also in 2009, Tucci reunited with Meryl Streep in [Nora Ephron](/wiki/Nora_Ephron)'s biographical comedy *[Julie & Julia](/wiki/Julie_%26_Julia)*. In the film Tucci portrayed [Paul Child](/wiki/Paul_Cushing_Child), the husband of [Julia Child](/wiki/Julia_Child) (Streep). They were praised for their on-screen chemistry with [Peter Travers](/wiki/Peter_Travers) of *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* declaring, "Tucci and Streep are magical together, creating a portrait of an unconventional marriage that deserves its own movie."

The following year, Tucci directed a revival of the [Ken Ludwig](/wiki/Ken_Ludwig) play *[Lend Me a Tenor](/wiki/Lend_Me_a_Tenor)* on Broadway, starring [Tony Shalhoub](/wiki/Tony_Shalhoub). Furthermore, Tucci had a supporting role in the teen coming-of-age romantic comedy film *[Easy A](/wiki/Easy_A)* (2010) starring [Emma Stone](/wiki/Emma_Stone). Tucci and [Patricia Clarkson](/wiki/Patricia_Clarkson) portrayed her easygoing and funny parents in the film. That same year, Tucci starred alongside [Cher](/wiki/Cher) and [Christina Aguilera](/wiki/Christina_Aguilera) in *[Burlesque](/wiki/Burlesque_(2010_American_film))*. The following year, Tucci played Dr. [Abraham Erskine](/wiki/Abraham_Erskine_(Marvel_Cinematic_Universe)) in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) film *[Captain America: The First Avenger](/wiki/Captain_America:_The_First_Avenger)* (2011) starring [Chris Evans](/wiki/Chris_Evans_(actor)).

### 2011–2019: franchise roles and other work

Tucci with [Hayley Atwell](/wiki/Hayley_Atwell) in [London](/wiki/London), 2017

In 2011, Tucci took the role of Eric Dale in the [J.C. Chandor](/wiki/J.C._Chandor) directed drama film *[Margin Call](/wiki/Margin_Call)* starring [Kevin Spacey](/wiki/Kevin_Spacey), [Jeremy Irons](/wiki/Jeremy_Irons), [Demi Moore](/wiki/Demi_Moore), and [Zachary Quinto](/wiki/Zachary_Quinto). The film premiered at the [Sundance Film Festival](/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival), where it earned critical acclaim and later a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay). In the fall of 2012, Tucci released his first cookbook titled *The Tucci Cookbook.* Tucci was also a co-owner of the Finch Tavern restaurant in Croton Falls, New York. He played Henry Warren Chang in the *[30 Rock](/wiki/30_Rock)* episode "[Alexis Goodlooking and the Case of the Missing Whisky](/wiki/Alexis_Goodlooking_and_the_Case_of_the_Missing_Whisky)" in 2012.

From 2012 to 2015, Tucci portrayed Caesar Flickerman in *[The Hunger Games](/wiki/The_Hunger_Games_(film))* (2012) and its sequels *[The Hunger Games: Catching Fire](/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Catching_Fire)* (2013), *[The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 1](/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Mockingjay_%E2%80%93_Part_1)* (2014) and *[The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 2](/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Mockingjay_%E2%80%93_Part_2)* (2015). In 2013, Tucci played the role of the Ancient Greek God [Dionysus](/wiki/Dionysus) in *[Percy Jackson: Sea of Monsters](/wiki/Percy_Jackson:_Sea_of_Monsters)*. Also, in 2013, he lent his voice to an episode of the animated series *[American Dad!](/wiki/American_Dad!).* During this time, Tucci portrayed CEO [Joshua Joyce](/wiki/List_of_Transformers_film_series_cast_and_characters#Joshua_Joyce) in *[Transformers: Age of Extinction](/wiki/Transformers:_Age_of_Extinction)* (2014). The same year, he voiced [Leonardo da Vinci](/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) in the animated film *[Mr. Peabody & Sherman](/wiki/Mr._Peabody_%26_Sherman)* and had a cameo in *[Muppets Most Wanted](/wiki/Muppets_Most_Wanted)*.

From 2014 to 2020, he had a recurring role voicing character [Herb Kazzaz](/wiki/Herb_Kazzaz) in the animated series *[BoJack Horseman](/wiki/BoJack_Horseman)*. Tucci portrayed [Philippe I, Duke of Orléans](/wiki/Philippe_I,_Duke_of_Orl%C3%A9ans) in [Alan Rickman](/wiki/Alan_Rickman)'s biographical costume romance drama *[A Little Chaos](/wiki/A_Little_Chaos)* starring [Kate Winslet](/wiki/Kate_Winslet). The following year, Tucci portrayed [Mitchell Garabedian](/wiki/Mitchell_Garabedian), a lawyer representing victims of sexual abuse in the biographical drama film [*Spotlight*](/wiki/Spotlight_(film)). The film was directed by [Tom McCarthy](/wiki/Tom_McCarthy_(director)) and starred [Michael Keaton](/wiki/Michael_Keaton), [Mark Ruffalo](/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo), [Rachel McAdams](/wiki/Rachel_McAdams), [John Slattery](/wiki/John_Slattery), and [Liev Schreiber](/wiki/Liev_Schreiber). The film received rave reviews with Geoffrey Mcnab of *[The Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)* writing, "Spotlight is an old-fashioned film that tells its story in a painstaking and thoroughly absorbing fashion. It's the kind of movie that you could imagine Henry Fonda or James Stewart starring in as decent, upstanding journalist heroes who refuse to give up on their story in the face of considerable difficulty and intimidation." The film won the [Academy Award for Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) as well as [Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay). Also in 2015, Tucci starred in the British series [*Fortitude*](/wiki/Fortitude_(TV_series)) as DCI Eugene Morton.

In 2017, Tucci wrote and directed the drama film *[Final Portrait](/wiki/Final_Portrait)*. The same year, Tucci played the role of the composer [Maestro Cadenza](/wiki/Maestro_Cadenza) in the live-action adaptation of [Disney's](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Pictures) *[Beauty and the Beast](/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_(2017_film))*, co-starring with [Emma Watson](/wiki/Emma_Watson) and [Dan Stevens](/wiki/Dan_Stevens). Tucci also returned to the [*Transformers* film series](/wiki/Transformers_(film_series)) by portraying [Merlin](/wiki/Merlin) in *[Transformers: The Last Knight](/wiki/Transformers:_The_Last_Knight)*. Furthermore, Tucci played the husband of Dame Fiona Maye, a British High Court judge, opposite [Emma Thompson](/wiki/Emma_Thompson) in *[The Children Act](/wiki/The_Children_Act_(film))* (2017), based on the [book of the same name](/wiki/The_Children_Act_(novel)) by [Ian McEwan](/wiki/Ian_McEwan). Also, in 2017, Tucci starred in the miniseries [*Feud*](/wiki/Feud_(American_TV_series)) as [Warner Bros.](/wiki/Warner_Bros.) studio head [Jack L. Warner](/wiki/Jack_L._Warner). *Feud* received critical acclaim and Tucci received a nomination for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actor in a Limited Series or Movie](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Limited_Series_or_Movie). In 2018, Tucci starred in the independent films [*Patient Zero*](/wiki/Patient_Zero_(film)), *[A Private War](/wiki/A_Private_War)* and [*Night Hunter*](/wiki/Night_Hunter_(2018_film)). In 2019, Tucci starred in the drama series [*Limetown*](/wiki/Limetown_(TV_series)), [based on the podcast of the same name](/wiki/Limetown). Facebook cancelled the series after one season had aired. The same year, Tucci starred in the horror film [*The Silence*](/wiki/The_Silence_(2019_film)).

### 2020–present

Tucci alongside [Ralph Fiennes](/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes) and [Isabella Rossellini](/wiki/Isabella_Rossellini) at the premiere of *[Conclave](/wiki/Conclave_(film))* in 2024

In 2020, Tucci began voicing the character Bitsy Brandenham in the animated series [*Central Park*](/wiki/Central_Park_(TV_series)). The series received a two-season order from [Apple Inc.](/wiki/Apple_Inc.), with each season set to consist of 13 episodes each. The series premiered on May 29, 2020. In the same year, Tucci narrated the series *The California Century*, on notable people in the history of California told from the point of view of a screenwriter. In 2020, Tucci starred in the British drama film [*Supernova*](/wiki/Supernova_(2020_film)) opposite Colin Firth. The film explores the relationship between a couple played by Tucci and Firth as one of them succumbs to [early onset dementia](/wiki/Early_onset_dementia). The film premiered at the [San Sebastián International Film Festival](/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_International_Film_Festival) and since has received critical acclaim. Guy Lodge, critic for *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* wrote of their chemistry:

> Firth and Tucci are such reliable stalwarts that we tend not to regard their presence too closely in films these days: Almost invariably, they fulfill our expectations of their refined gravitas. But there's something lovely and surprising in what they bring out of each other here, as they complement and reflect each other's curtness, evasiveness and occasional spillages of tenderness in the way that long-term couples do.

In 2021, Tucci hosted the culinary travel series, *[Stanley Tucci: Searching for Italy](/wiki/Stanley_Tucci:_Searching_for_Italy)*, a six-part original [CNN](/wiki/CNN) series following the actor on a food tour of Italy. The series received two Primetime Emmy Award nominations, including one for [Outstanding Hosted Nonfiction Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Hosted_Nonfiction_Series_or_Special), and was renewed for a second season set to air in 2022. Caroline Framke of *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* praised the series writing, "It’s exactly the picturesque scenario you might expect and want from a travel series hosted by Tucci, an extraordinarily charming presence who knows and loves Italian food".

Tucci acted in the title role in the [BBC One](/wiki/BBC_One) thriller series *[Inside Man](/wiki/Inside_Man_(2022_TV_series))* (2022). The series was created by [Steven Moffat](/wiki/Steven_Moffat) and also starred [David Tennant](/wiki/David_Tennant). In September, Tucci was cast as Grammy-winning producer [Clive Davis](/wiki/Clive_Davis) in *[Whitney Houston: I Wanna Dance with Somebody](/wiki/Whitney_Houston:_I_Wanna_Dance_with_Somebody)* (2022), a [biopic](/wiki/Biopic) on [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston). In 2023, Tucci acted in the [Amazon Prime Video](/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video) series *[Citadel](/wiki/Citadel_(TV_series))* starring [Priyanka Chopra](/wiki/Priyanka_Chopra) and [Richard Madden](/wiki/Richard_Madden). In 2024, Tucci played a major role in the [Edward Berger](/wiki/Edward_Berger) film *[Conclave](/wiki/Conclave_(film))* alongside [Ralph Fiennes](/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes), which premiered at the [51st Telluride Film Festival](/wiki/51st_Telluride_Film_Festival). Tucci's 2024 book *What I Ate in One Year* won the "Non-Fiction: Lifestyle and Illustrated" category in the 2025 [British Book Awards](/wiki/British_Book_Awards).

Tucci reprised the role of Nigel Kipling in *[The Devil Wears Prada 2](/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_2)*, which was released in May 2026.

## Personal life

### Marriages and family

Tucci in 2017

Tucci married Kathryn Spath (born 1962, died 2009) in 1995. She was a [social worker](/wiki/Social_worker), former wife of actor and [stage manager](/wiki/Stage_manager) Alexander R. Scott, and former sister-in-law of Tucci's high school friend [Campbell Scott](/wiki/Campbell_Scott). They had three children together. The couple also raised Kathryn's two children from her previous marriage.
Tucci had an [affair](/wiki/Affair) in 2002 with actress [Edie Falco](/wiki/Edie_Falco), with whom he was appearing on Broadway in [Terrence McNally](/wiki/Terrence_McNally)'s *[Frankie and Johnny in the Clair de Lune](/wiki/Frankie_and_Johnny_in_the_Clair_de_Lune)*, but the affair ended, and he returned to his wife and children. Spath died of [breast cancer](/wiki/Breast_cancer) in 2009, aged 47.

In 2006, Tucci first met Felicity Blunt (born 1981), a British [literary agent](/wiki/Literary_agent) of the [Curtis Brown Group](/wiki/Curtis_Brown_(agency)), at *[The Devil Wears Prada](/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_(film))* (2006) premiere. They later reconnected in 2010 at the wedding of actor [John Krasinski](/wiki/John_Krasinski) to Felicity's sister, British actress [Emily Blunt](/wiki/Emily_Blunt) (who co-starred with Tucci in *The Devil Wears Prada*). Tucci and Felicity became engaged in 2011, and married in a [civil ceremony](/wiki/Civil_ceremony) in the summer of 2012, followed by a larger observance at [Middle Temple](/wiki/Middle_Temple) Hall in London on September 29, 2012. They have a son and a daughter.

### Charity work

On September 12, 2016, Tucci, alongside other celebrities, appeared in a video from the [United Nations'](/wiki/United_Nations_System) refugee agency [UNHCR](/wiki/UNHCR) to help raise awareness of the global [refugee crisis](/wiki/Refugee_crisis). The video, titled "What They Took With Them", has the actors reading a poem, written by Jenifer Toksvig and inspired by primary accounts of refugees, and is part of UNHCR's #WithRefugees campaign, which also includes a petition to governments to expand asylum to provide further shelter, integrating job opportunities, and education.

On May 21, 2021, Tucci received a [doctorate](/wiki/Doctorate) [*honoris causa*](/wiki/Honorary_degree) in Humane Letters from the [American University of Rome](/wiki/American_University_of_Rome), in Rome, Italy, for his lifelong contribution to the arts and humanities.

### Health issues

In September 2021, Tucci revealed that he had been diagnosed with [oropharyngeal cancer](/wiki/Oropharyngeal_cancer) three years earlier. He had received treatment ([chemotherapy](/wiki/Chemotherapy) and [radiation](/wiki/Radiation_therapy)) after a [tumor](/wiki/Tumor) was found at the base of his [tongue](/wiki/Tongue), and said it was unlikely that the tumor would return. In November 2022, he said there are still some foods he cannot eat, as a result of his cancer.

In October 2021, his memoir *Taste: My Life Through Food* was published, which describes his encounter with cancer and his love of food. The memoir was on [*The New York Times* Best Seller List](/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_List) for 16 weeks, until February 13, 2022.

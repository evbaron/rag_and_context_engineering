# List of capitals in the United States

For a list of capitol buildings, see [List of state and territorial capitols in the United States](/wiki/List_of_state_and_territorial_capitols_in_the_United_States).

U.S. states with their capitals

This is a list of [capital cities](/wiki/Capital_city) of the [United States](/wiki/United_States), including places that serve or have served as federal, state, insular area, territorial, colonial and Native American capitals.

[Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) has been the [federal capital](/wiki/Federal_capital) of the United States since 1800. Each [U.S. state](/wiki/U.S._state) has its own capital city, as do many of its [insular areas](/wiki/Insular_area). Most states have not changed their capital city since becoming a state, but the capital cities of their respective preceding colonies, territories, kingdoms, and republics typically changed multiple times. There have also been other governments within the current borders of the United States with their own capitals, such as the [Republic of Texas](/wiki/Republic_of_Texas), Native American nations, and other unrecognized governments.

## National capitals

See also: [§ Colonies of British America](#Colonies_of_British_America)

The buildings in cities identified in the chart below served either as official capitals of the United States under the [United States Constitution](/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States), or, prior to its ratification, sites where the [Second Continental Congress](/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) or [Congress of the Confederation](/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation) met. The United States did not have a permanent capital under the [Articles of Confederation](/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation).

The [U.S. Constitution](/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States) was ratified in 1787, and gave the Congress the power to exercise "exclusive legislation" over a district that "may, by Cession of particular States, and the acceptance of [Congress](/wiki/United_States_Congress), become the Seat of the Government of the United States." The [1st Congress](/wiki/1st_United_States_Congress) met at [Federal Hall](/wiki/Federal_Hall) in [New York](/wiki/New_York_City). In 1790, it passed the [Residence Act](/wiki/Residence_Act), which established the national capital at a site along the [Potomac River](/wiki/Potomac_River) that would become [Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) For the next ten years, [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia) served as the temporary capital. There, Congress met at [Congress Hall](/wiki/Congress_Hall). On November 17, 1800, the [6th United States Congress](/wiki/6th_United_States_Congress) formally convened in Washington, D.C. Congress has met outside of Washington only twice since: on July 16, 1987, at Independence Hall in Philadelphia, to commemorate the 200th anniversary of ratification of the Constitution; and at Federal Hall National Memorial in New York on September 6, 2002, to mark the first anniversary of the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks). Both meetings were ceremonial.

## Former national capitals

Four of the 50 U.S. states, Hawaii, Texas, California, and Vermont, were once *[de jure](/wiki/De_jure)* [sovereign states](/wiki/Sovereign_state) with [diplomatic recognition](/wiki/Diplomatic_recognition) from the [international community](/wiki/International_community). California did not have a capital during its time as a sovereign nation.

### Hawaii

During its history as a sovereign nation ([Kingdom of Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaiian_Kingdom), 1795–1893; [Republic of Hawaii](/wiki/Republic_of_Hawaii), 1894–1898), five sites served as the capital of Hawaii:

[Honolulu](/wiki/Honolulu) twice served as the national capital of Hawaii and is now the state capital.

- [Waikīkī](/wiki/Waikiki), 1795–1796
- [Hilo](/wiki/Hilo,_Hawaii), 1796–1803
- [Honolulu](/wiki/Honolulu), 1803–1812
- [Kailua-Kona](/wiki/Kailua-Kona,_Hawaii), 1812–1820
- [Lahaina](/wiki/Lahaina,_Hawaii), 1820–1845
- Honolulu, 1845–1898

[Annexed by the United States](/wiki/Newlands_Resolution) in 1898, Honolulu remained the capital, first of the [Territory](/wiki/Territory_of_Hawaii) of Hawaii ([1900](/wiki/Hawaiian_Organic_Act)–1959), and then of the state ([since 1959](/wiki/Hawaii_Admission_Act)).

### Texas

During its history as a sovereign nation ([Republic of Texas](/wiki/Republic_of_Texas), 1836–1845), seven sites served as the capital of Texas:

- [Washington](/wiki/Washington-on-the-Brazos,_Texas) (now Washington-on-the-Brazos), 1836
- [Harrisburg](/wiki/Harrisburg,_Houston) (now part of Houston), 1836
- [Galveston](/wiki/Galveston,_Texas), 1836
- [Velasco](/wiki/Velasco,_Texas), 1836
- [West Columbia](/wiki/West_Columbia,_Texas), 1836
- [Houston](/wiki/Houston), 1837–1839
- [Austin](/wiki/Austin,_Texas), 1839–1845

[Annexed by the United States](/wiki/Texas_annexation) in 1845, Austin remains the capital of the state of Texas.

### Vermont

Three sites have served as the capital of the [Vermont Republic](/wiki/Vermont_Republic):

- [Westminster](/wiki/Westminster,_Vermont), 1777
- [Windsor](/wiki/Windsor,_Vermont), 1777–1791
- [Castleton](/wiki/Castleton,_Vermont), 1791

When the state was admitted to the Union in 1791, [Burlington](/wiki/Burlington,_Vermont) was chosen to be the capital and it remained as such until 1805, when it was changed to [Montpelier](/wiki/Montpelier,_Vermont), where it remains today.

## Native American capitals

The [Navajo Nation Council Chamber](/wiki/Navajo_Nation_Council_Chamber) in [Window Rock, Arizona](/wiki/Window_Rock,_Arizona) is the center of government for the [Navajo Nation](/wiki/Navajo_Nation)

Some Native American tribes, in particular the [Five Civilized Tribes](/wiki/Five_Civilized_Tribes), organized their states with constitutions and capitals in Western style. Others, like the Iroquois, had long-standing, pre-Columbian traditions of a 'capitol' longhouse where wampum and council fires were maintained with special status. Since they did business with the U.S. Federal Government, these capitals can be seen as officially recognized in some sense.

### Cherokee Nation

- [New Echota](/wiki/New_Echota) 1825–1832

New Echota, now near [Calhoun](/wiki/Calhoun,_Georgia), [Georgia](/wiki/Georgia_(U.S._state)), was founded in 1825, realizing the dream and plans of Cherokee Chief [Major Ridge](/wiki/Major_Ridge). Major Ridge chose the site because of its centrality in [the historic Cherokee Nation](/wiki/Cherokee_Nation_(1794%E2%80%931907)) which spanned parts of Georgia, North Carolina, Tennessee and Alabama, and because it was near the confluence of the [Conasauga](/wiki/Conasauga_River) and [Coosawattee](/wiki/Coosawattee_River) rivers. The town's layout was partly inspired by Ridge's many visits to Washington D.C. and to Baltimore, but also invoked traditional themes of the [Southeastern ceremonial complex](/wiki/Southeastern_Ceremonial_Complex). Complete with the Council House, Supreme Court, [Cherokee syllabary](/wiki/Cherokee_syllabary) printing press, and the houses of several of the Nation's constitutional officers, New Echota served as the capital until 1832 when the state of Georgia outlawed Native American assembly in an attempt to undermine the Nation. Thousands of Cherokee would gather in New Echota for the annual National Councils, camping along the nearby rivers and holding long [stomp dances](/wiki/Stomp_dance) in the park-like woods that were typical of many Southeastern Native American settlements.

The [New Echota](/wiki/New_Echota) Council House (since reconstructed)

- [Red Clay](/wiki/Red_Clay_State_Historic_Park) 1832–1838

The Cherokee National council grounds were moved to Red Clay, Tennessee, on the Georgia state line, in order to evade the Georgia state militia. The log cabins, limestone springs, and park-like woods of Red Clay served as the capital until the Cherokee Nation was removed to [Indian Territory](/wiki/Indian_Territory) ([Oklahoma](/wiki/Oklahoma)) on the [Trail of Tears](/wiki/Trail_of_Tears).

- [Tahlequah](/wiki/Tahlequah,_Oklahoma) 1839–1907, 1938–present

Tahlequah, in present-day Oklahoma, served as the capital of [the original Cherokee Nation](/wiki/Cherokee_Nation_(1794%E2%80%931907)) after [Removal](/wiki/Indian_removal). After the Civil War, a turbulent period for the Nation which was involved in its own civil war resulting from pervasive anger and disagreements over removal from Georgia, the Cherokee Nation built a new [National Capitol](/wiki/Cherokee_National_Capitol) in Tahlequah out of brick. The building served as the capitol until 1907, when the [Dawes Act](/wiki/Dawes_Act) finally dissolved the Cherokee Nation and Tahlequah became the county seat of [Cherokee County, Oklahoma](/wiki/Cherokee_County,_Oklahoma). The Cherokee National government was re-established in 1938 and Tahlequah remains the capital of the modern [Cherokee Nation](/wiki/Cherokee_Nation); it is also the capital of the [United Keetoowah Band of Cherokee Indians](/wiki/United_Keetoowah_Band_of_Cherokee_Indians).

- [Cherokee](/wiki/Cherokee,_North_Carolina) 20th century–present ([Eastern Band of Cherokee](/wiki/Eastern_Band_of_Cherokee_Indians))

Approximately four to eight hundred Cherokees escaped removal because they lived on a separated tract, purchased later with the help of Confederate Colonel [William Holland Thomas](/wiki/William_Holland_Thomas), along the [Oconaluftee River](/wiki/Oconaluftee_River) deep in the Smoky Mountains of North Carolina. Some Cherokees fleeing the Federal Army, sent for the "round up", fled to the remote settlements separated from the rest of the Cherokee Territory in Georgia and North Carolina, in order to remain in their homeland. In the 20th century, their descendants organized as the [Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians](/wiki/Eastern_Band_of_Cherokee_Indians); its capital is at [Cherokee, North Carolina](/wiki/Cherokee,_North_Carolina), in the tribally-controlled [Qualla Boundary](/wiki/Qualla_Boundary).

### Muscogee Creek Nation

- [Hot Springs, Arkansas](/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas) c. 1837–1866

After Removal from their Alabama-Georgia homeland, the Creek national government met near Hot Springs which was then part of their new territory as prescribed in the Treaty of Cusseta. Because some Creeks fought with the [Confederacy](/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) in the [American Civil War](/wiki/American_Civil_War), the Union forced the [Creeks](/wiki/Muscogee_(Creek)_Nation) to cede over 3,000,000-acre (1,200,000 ha) - half of their land in what is now Arkansas.

- [Okmulgee](/wiki/Okmulgee,_Oklahoma) 1867–1906

Served as the National capital after the American Civil War. It was probably named after [Ocmulgee](/wiki/Ocmulgee_Mounds_National_Historical_Park), on the [Ocmulgee river](/wiki/Ocmulgee_River) in [Macon](/wiki/Macon,_Georgia), a principle [Coosa](/wiki/Coosa_chiefdom) and later [Creek](/wiki/Muscogee) town built with mounds and functioning as part of the [Southeastern ceremonial complex](/wiki/Southeastern_Ceremonial_Complex). However, there were other traditional Creek "mother-towns" before removal. The Ocmulgee mounds were ceded illegally in 1821 with the [Treaty of Indian Springs](/wiki/Treaty_of_Indian_Springs_(1821)).

### Iroquois Confederacy

- [Onondaga](/wiki/Onondaga_Reservation) (Onondaga privilege c. 1450–present)

The [Iroquois Confederacy](/wiki/Iroquois) or **Haudenosaunee**, which means "People of the Longhouse", was an alliance between the Five and later Six-Nations of Iroquoian language and culture of upstate [New York](/wiki/New_York_(state)). These include the [Seneca](/wiki/Seneca_people), [Cayuga](/wiki/Cayuga_people), [Onondaga](/wiki/Onondaga_people), [Oneida](/wiki/Oneida_people), [Mohawk](/wiki/Mohawk_people), and, after 1722, the [Tuscarora](/wiki/Tuscarora_people) Nations. Since the Confederacy's formation around 1450, the Onondaga Nation has held privilege of hosting the Iroquois Grand Council and the status of Keepers of the Fire and the Wampum —which they still do at the official Longhouse on the Onondaga Reservation. Now spread over reservations in New York and [Ontario](/wiki/Ontario), the Six Nations of the Haudenosaunee preserve this arrangement to this day in what they claim to be the "world's oldest representative democracy."

#### Seneca Nation of Indians

- [Jimerson Town](/wiki/Jimerson_Town,_New_York) ([Allegany Reservation](/wiki/Allegany_Indian_Reservation))
- [Irving](/wiki/Irving,_New_York) ([Cattaraugus Reservation](/wiki/Cattaraugus_Reservation))

The [Seneca Nation](/wiki/Seneca_Nation_of_New_York) republic was founded in 1848 and has two capitals that rotate responsibilities every two years. Jimerson Town was founded in the 1960s following the formation of the [Allegheny Reservoir](/wiki/Allegheny_Reservoir). The Senecas also have an administrative longhouse in [Steamburg](/wiki/Steamburg,_New_York) but do not consider that location to be a capital.

### Navajo Nation

- [Window Rock](/wiki/Window_Rock,_Arizona)

[Window Rock](/wiki/Window_Rock,_Arizona) ([Navajo](/wiki/Navajo_language): *Tségháhoodzání*), Arizona, is a small city that serves as the seat of government and capital of the [Navajo Nation](/wiki/Navajo_Nation) (1936–present), the largest territory of a sovereign Native American nation in North America. It lies within the boundaries of the St. Michaels Chapter, adjacent to the Arizona and New Mexico state line. Window Rock hosts the Navajo Nation governmental campus which contains the Navajo Nation Council, Navajo Nation Supreme Court, the offices of the Navajo Nation President and Vice President, and many Navajo government buildings.

## Unrecognized national capitals

There have been a handful of self-declared or undeclared nations within the current borders of the United States which were never officially recognized as legally independent sovereign entities; however, these nations did have *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* control over their respective regions during their existence.

### State of Franklin

The [State of Franklin](/wiki/State_of_Franklin) was an autonomous, secessionist United States territory created not long after the end of the American Revolution from territory that later was ceded by North Carolina to the federal government. Franklin's territory later became part of the state of Tennessee. Franklin was never officially admitted into the Union of the United States and existed for only four years.

- [Jonesborough, Tennessee](/wiki/Jonesborough,_Tennessee), 1784–1785
- [Greeneville, Tennessee](/wiki/Greeneville,_Tennessee), 1785–1788

### State of Muskogee

The [State of Muskogee](/wiki/State_of_Muskogee) was a [Native American](/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) state in [Spanish Florida](/wiki/Spanish_Florida) created by the Englishman [William Augustus Bowles](/wiki/William_Augustus_Bowles), who was its "Director General", author of its Constitution, and designer of its flag. It consisted of several tribes of [Creeks](/wiki/Muscogee) and [Seminoles](/wiki/Seminole). It existed from 1799 to 1803. It had one capital:

- [Miccosukee](/wiki/Miccosukee,_Florida), 1799–1803

### Republic of West Florida

The [Republic of West Florida](/wiki/Republic_of_West_Florida) was a short-lived nation that broke away from the territory of [Spanish West Florida](/wiki/Spanish_West_Florida) in 1810. It comprised the [Florida Parishes](/wiki/Florida_Parishes) of the modern state of [Louisiana](/wiki/Louisiana) and the [Mobile District](/wiki/Mobile_District) of the modern states of [Mississippi](/wiki/Mississippi) and [Alabama](/wiki/Alabama). (The Republic of West Florida did not include any part of the modern state of [Florida](/wiki/Florida).) Ownership of the area had been in dispute between Spain and the United States, which claimed that it had been included in the [Louisiana Purchase](/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) of 1803. Within two months of the settlers' rebellion and the declaration of an independent nation, President [James Madison](/wiki/James_Madison) sent American forces to peaceably occupy the new republic. It was formally annexed by the United States in 1812 over the objections of Spain and the land was divided between the [Territory of Orleans](/wiki/Territory_of_Orleans) and [Territory of Mississippi](/wiki/Mississippi_Territory). During its brief existence, the capital of the Republic of West Florida was:

- [St. Francisville, Louisiana](/wiki/St._Francisville,_Louisiana), 1810

### Republic of Indian Stream

The [Republic of Indian Stream](/wiki/Republic_of_Indian_Stream) was an unrecognized independent nation within the present state of [New Hampshire](/wiki/New_Hampshire).

- The area that would become [Pittsburg, New Hampshire](/wiki/Pittsburg,_New_Hampshire), 1832–1835

### California Republic

Before being annexed by the United States in 1848 (following the [Mexican–American War](/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War)), a small portion of north-central California declared itself the [California Republic](/wiki/California_Republic), in an act of independence from Mexico, in 1846 (see [Bear Flag Revolt](/wiki/California_Republic)). The republic only existed a month before it disbanded itself to join the advancing American army; its claimed territory later became part of the United States as a result of the [Mexican Cession](/wiki/Mexican_Cession).

The original of Todd's [Bear Flag](/wiki/Bear_Flag), photographed in 1890

Modern flag of the State of California

The very short-lived California Republic was never recognized by the United States, Mexico or any other nation. The flag, featuring a silhouette of a [California grizzly bear](/wiki/California_grizzly_bear), a star, and the words "California Republic", became known as the [Bear Flag](/wiki/Bear_Flag) and was later the basis for the official state flag of California.

There was one *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* capital of the California Republic:

- [Sonoma](/wiki/Sonoma,_California), 1846

### Confederate States

[Richmond](/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia) served as the second capital of the [Confederate States of America](/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America). The city has been Virginia's capital since 1780.

The [Confederate States of America](/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) (C.S.A.) had three capitals during its existence. The first capital was established February 4, 1861, in [Montgomery](/wiki/History_of_Montgomery,_Alabama#Montgomery_in_the_Civil_War), Alabama, and remained there until it was moved to [Richmond](/wiki/Richmond_in_the_American_Civil_War), Virginia, on May 29, 1861, after Virginia seceded on May 23.

The individual state capitals remained the same in the Confederacy as they had been in the [Union](/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War)) (U.S.A.), although as the advancing [Union Army](/wiki/Union_Army) used those cities for military districts, some of the Confederate governments were relocated or moved out of state, traveling along with [secessionist armies](/wiki/Confederate_States_Army).

- [Montgomery, Alabama](/wiki/History_of_Montgomery,_Alabama#Montgomery_in_the_Civil_War), February 4, 1861 – May 29, 1861
- [Richmond, Virginia](/wiki/Richmond_in_the_American_Civil_War), May 29, 1861 – April 3, 1865
- [Danville, Virginia](/wiki/Danville,_Virginia), April 2, 1865- May 10, 1865

#### Free State of Jones

In 1863 and 1864, [Jones County, Mississippi](/wiki/Jones_County,_Mississippi) revolted against Confederate rule and became practically independent under the name [Free State of Jones](/wiki/Free_State_of_Jones). The Free State fought a number of skirmishes with Confederate troops. By the spring of 1864 the Jones County rebels had taken effective control of the county from the Confederate government, raised an American flag over the courthouse in Ellisville, and sent a letter to Union General William T. Sherman declaring Jones County's independence from the Confederacy.

Scholars have disputed whether the county truly seceded, with some concluding it did not fully secede. Lack of documentation makes the situation difficult to assess. The rebellion in Jones County has been variously characterized as consisting of local skirmishes to being a full-fledged war of independence.

- [Ellisville, Mississippi](/wiki/Ellisville,_Mississippi)

## Historical state, colonial, and territorial capitals

Most of the original [Thirteen Colonies](/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) had their capitals occupied or attacked by the British during the [American Revolutionary War](/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). State governments operated where and as they could. The [City of New York](/wiki/New_York_City) was occupied by [British troops](/wiki/British_Armed_Forces) from 1776 to 1783. A similar situation occurred during the [War of 1812](/wiki/War_of_1812), during the [American Civil War](/wiki/American_Civil_War) in many [Confederate states](/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America), and during the [Pueblo Revolt](/wiki/Pueblo_Revolt) of 1680–1692 in [New Mexico](/wiki/New_Mexico).

[St. Augustine](/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida) served as Florida's capital from 1565 until the 1820s.

Twenty-two state capitals have been a capital longer than their state has been a state, since they served as the capital of a predecessor territory, colony, or republic. [Boston](/wiki/Boston), [Massachusetts](/wiki/Massachusetts), has been a capital city since 1630; it is the oldest continuously running capital in the United States. [Santa Fe, New Mexico](/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico), is the oldest capital city, having become capital in 1610 and interrupted only by the aforementioned [Pueblo Revolt](/wiki/Pueblo_Revolt). An even older [Spanish](/wiki/New_Spain) city, [St. Augustine, Florida](/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida), served as a colonial capital from 1565 until about 1820, more than 250 years.

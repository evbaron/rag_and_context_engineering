[Van Halen](/wiki/Van_Halen) was an American [hard rock](/wiki/Hard_rock) band formed in [Pasadena, California](/wiki/Pasadena,_California) in 1972 by the Dutch-born American brothers [Eddie Van Halen](/wiki/Eddie_Van_Halen) (guitar) and [Alex Van Halen](/wiki/Alex_Van_Halen) (drums), plus singer [David Lee Roth](/wiki/David_Lee_Roth) and bassist [Michael Anthony](/wiki/Michael_Anthony_(musician)). The band's discography consists of 12 studio albums, three [live albums](/wiki/Live_albums), four [compilation albums](/wiki/Compilation_album), and 56 [singles](/wiki/Single_(music)).

The band signed a contract with [Warner Bros. Records](/wiki/Warner_Bros._Records) in 1977, and in the following year released the album *[Van Halen](/wiki/Van_Halen_(album))*. Over the next few years, the band alternated album releases, one per year between 1979 and 1982, and [touring](/wiki/Van_Halen_concert_tours) to increasing commercial and critical acclaim, becoming one of the world's most successful and influential rock bands. In 1984, Van Halen released *[1984](/wiki/1984_(Van_Halen_album))*, which peaked at No. 2 on the [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200) and had the band's sole No. 1 hit on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100), "[Jump](/wiki/Jump_(Van_Halen_song))". After the [tour promoting that album](/wiki/1984_Tour), Roth left the band due to artistic and personal tensions with Eddie.

To replace Roth, Eddie picked [Sammy Hagar](/wiki/Sammy_Hagar), formerly of [Montrose](/wiki/Montrose_(band)) and at that time a very successful solo artist. Van Halen's first album with Hagar, 1986's *[5150](/wiki/5150_(album))*, was the band's first No. 1 on the *Billboard* 200. The three studio albums that followed, *[OU812](/wiki/OU812)*, *[For Unlawful Carnal Knowledge](/wiki/For_Unlawful_Carnal_Knowledge)* and *[Balance](/wiki/Balance_(Van_Halen_album))*, also topped the charts. In 1996, Hagar left Van Halen amidst similar tension with the Van Halen brothers.

Roth rejoined briefly and recorded two songs with the band for the 1996 compilation *[Best Of – Volume I](/wiki/Best_Of_%E2%80%93_Volume_I_(Van_Halen_album))*, but Van Halen eventually settled on [Gary Cherone](/wiki/Gary_Cherone), frontman of the then defunct [Boston](/wiki/Boston)-based band [Extreme](/wiki/Extreme_(band)). Cherone's sole release with the band was *[Van Halen III](/wiki/Van_Halen_III)*, released in 1998 to mixed reviews and diminishing sales, being the only Van Halen studio album not to get a platinum [RIAA certification](/wiki/RIAA_certification). Van Halen's last release for Warner was the 2004 compilation *[Best of Both Worlds](/wiki/The_Best_of_Both_Worlds_(Van_Halen_album))*, which featured three new songs with Hagar. In 2011, the band left the label and signed with [Interscope Records](/wiki/Interscope_Records).

In February 2012, Van Halen released its twelfth and final studio album, *[A Different Kind of Truth](/wiki/A_Different_Kind_of_Truth)*, featuring Roth on vocals and Eddie's son [Wolfgang](/wiki/Wolfgang_Van_Halen) on bass guitar. The album debuted at number two on the *Billboard* 200, and became the band's highest-charting album on the [UK Albums Chart](/wiki/UK_Albums_Chart) with a sixth spot.

As of 2007, Van Halen has sold 75 million albums worldwide  and have thirteen No. 1 hits on the [*Billboard*](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine)) [Mainstream Rock](/wiki/Mainstream_Rock_(chart)) chart. During the 1980s, they, along with the band Heart, had more [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) hits, fifteen, than any other hard rock or heavy metal band. According to the [RIAA](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America), Van Halen is the 19th best-selling music group/artist of all time with sales of over 56 million albums in the US, and is one of five rock bands that have had two albums (*Van Halen* and *1984*) sell more than ten million copies in the US.

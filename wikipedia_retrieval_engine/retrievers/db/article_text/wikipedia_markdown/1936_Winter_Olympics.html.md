# 1936 Winter Olympics

## Infobox

- **Location:** Garmisch-Partenkirchen, Germany
- **Nations:** 28
- **Athletes:** 672 (592 men, 80 women)
- **Events:** 17 in 4 sports (8 disciplines)
- **Opening:** 6 February 1936
- **Closing:** 16 February 1936
- **Opened by:** Chancellor Adolf Hitler
- **Stadium:** Große Olympiaschanze

The **1936 Winter Olympics**, officially known as the **IV Olympic Winter Games** ([German](/wiki/German_language): *IV. Olympische Winterspiele*) and commonly known as **Garmisch-Partenkirchen 1936**, were a winter [multi-sport event](/wiki/Multi-sport_event) held from 6 to 16 February 1936 in the [market town](/wiki/Market_town) of [Garmisch-Partenkirchen](/wiki/Garmisch-Partenkirchen), [Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany). Later that year, the country also hosted the [1936 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics), which were held in [Berlin](/wiki/Berlin). It was the last year in which the Summer and Winter Games both took place in the same country (the cancelled 1940 Olympics would have been held in [Japan](/wiki/Japan), with [Tokyo](/wiki/Tokyo) hosting the [Summer Games](/wiki/1940_Summer_Olympics) and [Sapporo](/wiki/Sapporo) hosting the [Winter Games](/wiki/1940_Winter_Olympics)).

The 1936 Winter Games were organized on behalf of the [German League of the Reich for Physical Exercise](/wiki/National_Socialist_League_of_the_Reich_for_Physical_Exercise) (DRL) by [Karl Ritter von Halt](/wiki/Karl_Ritter_von_Halt), who had been named president of the committee for the organization of the Fourth Winter Olympics in Garmisch-Partenkirchen by *Reichssportführer* [Hans von Tschammer und Osten](/wiki/Hans_von_Tschammer_und_Osten).

## Organization and politics

While the [1936 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) held in [Berlin](/wiki/Berlin) months later have attracted extensive examination for the [Nazi Party](/wiki/Nazi_Party)'s spectacles and the accompanying racial controversies, including the exclusion of most [Jewish](/wiki/Jews) athletes and [Jesse Owens](/wiki/Jesse_Owens)'s achievements, the Winter Games took place five months earlier and saw some of the same efforts by [Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)'s propaganda machine.

Winter sports were only accepted by the Nazi Party because the Italian dictator [Mussolini](/wiki/Mussolini) promoted skiing. The [middle class](/wiki/Middle_class) was encouraged to visit [ski resorts](/wiki/Ski_resort). In 1936 more than 550,000 winter sports tourists visited Italy. Stylish skiing with Aryan looks was graphic designed by the propaganda artist [Ludwig Hohlwein](/wiki/Ludwig_Hohlwein). Globally, there had been efforts to initiate boycotts from different countries, and a number of Jewish athletes faced pressure not to participate in an event held in a nation ruled by a blatantly [antisemitic](/wiki/Antisemitism) regime.

The Nazis took steps to soften the appearance of their harsher policies before visitors from other nations arrived, such as removing antisemitic signage that was common in Germany, and – under pressure from a potential American boycott and Olympic officials – allowing the Jewish athlete [Rudi Ball](/wiki/Rudi_Ball) to play on Germany's ice hockey team. German troops moved back into the demilitarized [Rhineland](/wiki/Rhineland) for the Winter Games. A few weeks before the Games began, [William L. Shirer](/wiki/William_L._Shirer), the Berlin correspondent for the Universal wire service, wrote a series of articles describing preparations for the competition. He concluded, "that the Nazis at Garmisch had pulled down all the signs saying that Jews are unwanted (they're all over Germany) and that the Olympic visitors would thus be spared any signs of the kind of treatment meted out to Jews in this country."

[Opening Ceremony](/wiki/Olympic_Games_ceremony) with [Rudolf Hess](/wiki/Rudolf_Hess), [IOC president](/wiki/President_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee) [Henri de Baillet-Latour](/wiki/Henri_de_Baillet-Latour), and [Adolf Hitler](/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)

None of the member nations boycotted the Winter Games, and 49 in all participated, the greatest number at that time. The Games were completed with a minimum of political controversy, although the Canadian [skiing](/wiki/Skiing) team raised their arms in what appeared to be a Nazi salute as they entered the opening ceremonies. The German crowd erupted in applause at the salute, which was later explained as the "[Olympic Salute](/wiki/Roman_salute)" that was identical to the [Nazi version](/wiki/Nazi_salute) but with the arm extended laterally instead of forward.

However, even Shirer was impressed by the effectiveness of the Nazis' efforts, writing:

> This has been a more pleasant interlude than I expected. ... On the whole the Nazis have done a wonderful propaganda job. They've greatly impressed most of the visiting foreigners with the lavish but smooth way in which they've run the games and with their kind manners, which to us who came from Berlin of course seemed staged. I was so alarmed at this that I gave a luncheon for some of our businessmen and invited Douglas Miller, our commercial attaché in Berlin, and the best-informed man on Germany we have in our embassy, to enlighten them a little. But they told *him* what things were like, and Doug scarcely got a word in. ... Back to Berlin tomorrow to the grind of covering Nazi politics."

Twelve days after the Games closed, Hitler sent German troops to [remilitarize the Rhineland](/wiki/Remilitarization_of_the_Rhineland), his first territorial violation of the [Treaty of Versailles](/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) and a critical test of European resolve to resist Germany's military expansion.

## Highlights

- German skier [Willy Bogner](/wiki/Willy_Bogner,_Sr.) took the [Olympic oath](/wiki/Olympic_oath) during the opening ceremonies.
- [Alpine skiing](/wiki/Alpine_skiing) made its first appearance in the Winter Olympics as the [combined](/wiki/Alpine_skiing_combined), which added a skier's results in both the [downhill](/wiki/Downhill_(ski_competition)) and [slalom](/wiki/Slalom_skiing). German athletes [Franz Pfnür](/wiki/Franz_Pfn%C3%BCr) and [Christl Cranz](/wiki/Christl_Cranz) won the men's and women's alpine events, respectively.
- [Ivar Ballangrud](/wiki/Ivar_Ballangrud) won three out of the four [speed skating](/wiki/Speed_skating) races.
- [Sonja Henie](/wiki/Sonja_Henie) won her third consecutive gold medal in woman's [figure skating](/wiki/Figure_skating).
- [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland) won the four-man bobsled competition with a time of 5:19.85.
- [Great Britain](/wiki/Great_Britain_men%27s_national_ice_hockey_team) upset 1932 gold medalists [Canada](/wiki/Canada_men%27s_national_ice_hockey_team) in [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) when [Edgar Brenchley](/wiki/Edgar_Brenchley) scored the winning goal within the last 90 seconds.
- [Norway](/wiki/Norway) won the overall games with a total of seven gold medals, five silver medals and three bronze medals.
- The 1936 Winter Olympics presented the largest and heaviest medals ever awarded to athletes: 100 mm (3.9 in) diameter, 4 mm (0.16 in) thick and weighing 324 g (11.4 oz).

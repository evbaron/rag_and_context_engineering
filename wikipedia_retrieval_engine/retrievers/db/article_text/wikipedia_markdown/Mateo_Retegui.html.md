**Mateo Retegui** (born 29 April 1999) is a professional [footballer](/wiki/Association_football) who plays as a [striker](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Striker) for [Saudi Pro League](/wiki/Saudi_Pro_League) club [Al-Qadsiah](/wiki/Al_Qadsiah_FC). Born in Argentina, he plays for the [Italy national team](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team).

## Early life

Retegui was born and raised in Argentina. His maternal grandfather, Angelo Dimarco, emigrated to Argentina from [Canicattì](/wiki/Canicatt%C3%AC), [Sicily](/wiki/Sicily). His father's grandparents on one side of the family were also Italian, specifically from [Genoa](/wiki/Genoa), while the paternal ancestors were originally Basque (the surname Retegui being from [Gipuzkoa](/wiki/Gipuzkoa), Spain).

He is the son of former [field hockey](/wiki/Field_hockey) player [Carlos Retegui](/wiki/Carlos_Retegui), who represented Argentina as a player and coach at various [Pan American Games](/wiki/Pan_American_Games) and [Olympic Games](/wiki/Olympic_Games). His sister [Micaela](/wiki/Micaela_Retegui) is also an Olympian field hockey player.

## Club career

### Early career in Argentina

Retegui began his senior career with [Boca Juniors](/wiki/Boca_Juniors), who signed him years prior from [River Plate](/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate). He was first promoted into the club's first-team by manager [Guillermo Barros Schelotto](/wiki/Guillermo_Barros_Schelotto) during the [2017–18 Argentine Primera División](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Argentine_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n) season, appearing as an unused substitute on occasions against [Arsenal de Sarandí](/wiki/Arsenal_de_Sarand%C3%AD) and [Estudiantes](/wiki/Estudiantes_de_La_Plata) in December 2017. His professional debut arrived on 17 November 2018, with the forward being substituted on for the final moments of a 1–0 home win over [Patronato](/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_Patronato).

January 2019 saw Retegui loaned to Estudiantes for eighteen months. He appeared eight times in [2018–19](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Argentine_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n), before scoring five goals in twenty-one appearances in [2019–20](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Argentine_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n).

In February 2022, Retegui joined [Tigre](/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_Tigre) on a loan deal until the end of 2023.

### Genoa

On 26 July 2023, Retegui joined [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A) club [Genoa](/wiki/Genoa_CFC) on a five-year deal, for a reported fee of €15 million. On 11 August 2023, he scored his first two goals for the club in a [Coppa Italia](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Coppa_Italia) match against [Serie B](/wiki/Serie_B) side [Modena](/wiki/Modena_FC_2018).

### Atalanta

Retegui moved to fellow Serie A club [Atalanta](/wiki/Atalanta_BC) in a deal worth a reported €28 million on 8 August 2024, following an [anterior cruciate ligament injury](/wiki/Anterior_cruciate_ligament_injury) to Atalanta forward and Italy teammate [Gianluca Scamacca](/wiki/Gianluca_Scamacca). Later that year, on 26 November, he scored his first [Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League) goals by netting a brace in a 6–1 away victory over [Young Boys](/wiki/BSC_Young_Boys). He scored four goals in a 5–0 demolition of [Hellas Verona](/wiki/Hellas_Verona_FC) on 8 February 2025, in which he became the first player to score a first-half hat-trick for Atalanta in Serie A. A month later, on 9 March, he scored from the penalty spot in a 4–0 away win over [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus_FC), handing them their worst home defeat since 1967. He concluded the [2024–25 season](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Serie_A) as the *[Capocannoniere](/wiki/Capocannoniere)*, netting 25 goals and becoming only the second Atalanta player to earn the title after [Filippo Inzaghi](/wiki/Filippo_Inzaghi) in the 1996–97 campaign.

### Al-Qadsiah

On 21 July 2025, Retegui joined [Saudi Pro League](/wiki/Saudi_Pro_League) club [Al-Qadsiah](/wiki/Al_Qadsiah_FC) for a reported transfer fee of €65 million. A month later, on 30 August, he marked his league debut with a brace in a 3–1 win over [Al-Najma](/wiki/Al-Najma_SC_(Saudi_Arabia)).

## International career

Retegui represented Argentina at U19 and [U20](/wiki/Argentina_national_under-20_football_team) level, including for the latter at the [2018 South American Games](/wiki/Football_at_the_2018_South_American_Games).

Being eligible to play for [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) through descent, in February 2023 it was reported Retegui was pre-selected by [Roberto Mancini](/wiki/Roberto_Mancini) to join the *Azzurri* team for the first [UEFA Euro 2024](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) qualification games. Retegui received his first official call up to the Italy national team on 17 March 2023, and proceeded to score in his international debut on 23 March against [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) in a [Euro 2024 qualifying match](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying) lost 2–1 in [Naples](/wiki/Naples). He would tally again in his subsequent appearance on 26 March, netting the winner in a 2–0 win over [Malta](/wiki/Malta_national_football_team) in a Euro 2024 qualifier in [Ta' Qali](/wiki/National_Stadium,_Ta%27_Qali), to become the first player to score in his first two competitive matches for the national side since [Pierino Prati](/wiki/Pierino_Prati) accomplished the feat in 1968.

On 21 March 2024, Retegui scored twice in a 2–1 victory over [Venezuela](/wiki/Venezuela_national_football_team). He was the first Genoa player to score a brace for Italy since [Virgilio Levratto](/wiki/Virgilio_Levratto) at the [1928 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics). In June 2024, Retegui was included by manager [Luciano Spalletti](/wiki/Luciano_Spalletti) in Italy's squad for [UEFA Euro 2024](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). Later that year, on 6 September, he was named player of the match, in which he also provided an assist, in a 3–1 away win over [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) during the [UEFA Nations League](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League_A), helping his country to secure their first win against their opponent in Paris since April 1954.

On 14 October 2025, Retegui scored twice in a 3–0 win against [Israel](/wiki/Israel_national_football_team) during the [2026 World Cup qualifiers](/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_I).

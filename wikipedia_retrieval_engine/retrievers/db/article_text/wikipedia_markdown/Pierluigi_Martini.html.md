**Pierluigi Martini** (Italian pronunciation: [[ˌpjɛrluˈiːdʒi marˈtiːni]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian); born 23 April 1961) is an Italian former [racing driver](/wiki/Racing_driver), who competed in [Formula One](/wiki/Formula_One) from [1984](/wiki/1984_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [1985](/wiki/1985_Formula_One_World_Championship) and from [1988](/wiki/1988_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [1995](/wiki/1995_Formula_One_World_Championship). In [endurance racing](/wiki/Endurance_racing_(motorsport)), Martini won the [24 Hours of Le Mans](/wiki/24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) in [1999](/wiki/1999_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) with [BMW](/wiki/BMW_in_motorsport).

Born in [Lugo, Emilia-Romagna](/wiki/Lugo,_Emilia-Romagna), Martini is the nephew of [Formula Two](/wiki/Formula_Two) driver [Giancarlo Martini](/wiki/Giancarlo_Martini) and the older brother of [Oliver](/wiki/Oliver_Martini). He finished runner-up to [Ivan Capelli](/wiki/Ivan_Capelli) in the [1986 International Formula 3000 Championship](/wiki/1986_International_Formula_3000_Championship). Martini contested 124 [Formula One Grands Prix](/wiki/Formula_One_Grands_Prix) between [1984](/wiki/1984_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [1995](/wiki/1995_Formula_One_World_Championship) for [Toleman](/wiki/Toleman), [Minardi](/wiki/Minardi), and [Scuderia Italia](/wiki/Scuderia_Italia), achieving 18 championship points.

## Early life

Martini's uncle, [Giancarlo Martini](/wiki/Giancarlo_Martini), raced during the 1970s, including some non-championship races in a [Ferrari](/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari) [312T](/wiki/Ferrari_312T) entered by Scuderia Everest, a team owned by [Giancarlo Minardi](/wiki/Giancarlo_Minardi). Pierluigi's younger brother, [Oliver](/wiki/Oliver_Martini), is also a racing driver. In 1983 Martini competed in [Formula 3](/wiki/Formula_3) with Luigi Pavesi racing. He won four races and secured both the [Italian Formula 3](/wiki/Italian_Formula_3) and [European Formula 3](/wiki/FIA_European_Formula_3_Championship_(1975%E2%80%931984)) titles the same year.

## Formula One

Martini driving for [Minardi](/wiki/Minardi) at the [1985 European Grand Prix](/wiki/1985_European_Grand_Prix)

Martini participated in 124 [Formula One](/wiki/Formula_One) Grands Prix, debuting on 9 September 1984, driving for [Toleman](/wiki/Toleman) in place of suspended [Ayrton Senna](/wiki/Ayrton_Senna) at the [1984 Italian Grand Prix](/wiki/1984_Italian_Grand_Prix). He scored a total of 18 championship points and was synonymous with the [Minardi](/wiki/Minardi) team (run by the same [Giancarlo Minardi](/wiki/Giancarlo_Minardi) who had previously owned Scuderia Everest).

Martini driving a [Minardi M194](/wiki/Minardi_M194) at the [1994 British Grand Prix](/wiki/1994_British_Grand_Prix)

Indeed, aside from a single outing with Toleman and a one-season dalliance with [Scuderia Italia](/wiki/Scuderia_Italia) in [1992](/wiki/1992_Formula_One_World_Championship), Martini's entire Formula One career was spent with the Italian outfit. He raced with the minnow team in three different stints, drove for them on their debut in 1985, scored their first point in the [1988 Detroit Grand Prix](/wiki/1988_Detroit_Grand_Prix), and their only front-row start at the [1990 United States Grand Prix](/wiki/1990_United_States_Grand_Prix) (where unexpected rain on Saturday meant that the grid was decided entirely by times from Friday's session. Pirelli's soft qualifying tyres caught Goodyear off guard, and the Italian manufacturer put five of its teams in the top ten positions). Both Martini and Minardi led a race for a single lap at the [1989 Portuguese Grand Prix](/wiki/1989_Portuguese_Grand_Prix), and their joint-best finish was fourth at the [1991 San Marino Grand Prix](/wiki/1991_San_Marino_Grand_Prix) and [1991 Portuguese Grand Prix](/wiki/1991_Portuguese_Grand_Prix), the latter being Martini's single finish on the lead lap. Initially out of a drive for [1993](/wiki/1993_Formula_One_World_Championship), he was recalled back to the little Italian team midway through the season in place of [Fabrizio Barbazza](/wiki/Fabrizio_Barbazza), Martini impressed by outpacing his young teammate [Christian Fittipaldi](/wiki/Christian_Fittipaldi).

Martini was also one of the drivers with a reputation for ignoring [blue flags](/wiki/Racing_flags#Blue_flag). Examples given are the [1991 Monaco Grand Prix](/wiki/1991_Monaco_Grand_Prix) when he held up [Emanuele Pirro](/wiki/Emanuele_Pirro) in the [Dallara](/wiki/Dallara), [Stefano Modena](/wiki/Stefano_Modena) in the [Tyrrell](/wiki/Tyrrell_Racing), and [Riccardo Patrese](/wiki/Riccardo_Patrese) in the [Williams](/wiki/Williams_Grand_Prix_Engineering) for several laps despite running towards the back of the field, and the [1995 Canadian Grand Prix](/wiki/1995_Canadian_Grand_Prix) where he blocked [Gerhard Berger](/wiki/Gerhard_Berger) in the Ferrari when the Austrian tried to lap him. On both occasions, Martini was called in for a ten-second stop-and-go penalty for ignoring blue flags.

Martini driving a [Minardi M195](/wiki/Minardi_M195) at the [1995 British Grand Prix](/wiki/1995_British_Grand_Prix)

Quizzed about this attitude on the occasion of the [1995 San Marino Grand Prix](/wiki/1995_San_Marino_Grand_Prix), where he held up winner [Damon Hill](/wiki/Damon_Hill), Martini replied: 'What should I exactly apologise for? My trajectories are always clean. Should I just park the car on the grass? I'm here to do my race like anybody else. I've always been correct. Those who complain about my conduct should explain why they cannot overtake me when their car has at least 150 hp more than mine'.

## Sportscars

Prior to commencing his Formula One career, Martini drove a [Lancia LC2](/wiki/Lancia_LC2) in the [1984 24 Hours of Le Mans](/wiki/1984_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans). After leaving Formula One, he began a successful career in [sportscar racing](/wiki/Sportscar_racing). He contested the [1996 24 Hours of Le Mans](/wiki/1996_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) in a Porsche run by [Joest Racing](/wiki/Joest_Racing). 1997 brought a fourth-place finish in a [Porsche 911 GT1](/wiki/Porsche_911_GT1) which he also raced in the [FIA GT Championship](/wiki/FIA_GT_Championship) that year. In 1998, he joined the brand-new Le Mans program of BMW Motorsports.

Martini and a [Minardi M189](/wiki/Minardi_M189) at the 2016 Adelaide Motorsport Festival

In 1999, Martini, [Yannick Dalmas](/wiki/Yannick_Dalmas) and [Joachim Winkelhock](/wiki/Joachim_Winkelhock) won the [Le Mans 24 Hours](/wiki/Le_Mans_24_Hours).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierluigi_Martini#endnote_grandprix) The trio drove for BMW. The team had to fight both Toyota and Mercedes works cars and won the race by a lap over the runner-up Toyota.

Martini returned to motorsports in 2006, competing in the [Grand Prix Masters](/wiki/Grand_Prix_Masters) series for retired Formula One drivers.

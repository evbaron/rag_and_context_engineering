# General of the branch

A **general of the branch**, **general of the branch of service** or **general of the ...** (where instead of the ellipsis an appropriate name of the military branch is being put) is a [three](/wiki/Three-star_rank) or [four-star](/wiki/Four-star_rank) general officer rank in some armies. Several nations divide—or used to divide—their senior [general officer](/wiki/General_officer) ranks by the branch of troops they are qualified to command, or simply as an honorific title.

## Austria-Hungary

Collar insignia of an Austro-Hungarian "general of the branch"

In the [Austro-Hungarian Army](/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) there were three *general of the branch* ranks:

- [General der Infanterie](/wiki/General_der_Infanterie) (en: General of the Infantry)
- *[General der Kavallerie](/wiki/General_of_the_Cavalry_(Austria))* (en: General of the Cavalry)
- [Feldzeugmeister](/wiki/Feldzeugmeister) (en: General of the Artillery)

The rank of General der Infanterie was introduced in 1908, prior to this both infantrymen and gunners were appointed as Feldzeugmeisters.

Historically, the rank of general of artillery ([German](/wiki/German_language): *Feldzeugmeister*; literally "battlefield ordnance master"; "gun master"; in Hungarian *Táborszernagy*) was equivalent to [lieutenant general](/wiki/Lieutenant_general). In [French](/wiki/French_language), the equivalent expression was *grand maitre d'artillerie*, used since the time of [Philip VI of France](/wiki/Philip_VI_of_France). The English position of [Master-General of the Ordnance](/wiki/Master-General_of_the_Ordnance) was similarly derived.

See also: [Army ranks and insignia of the Austro-Hungarian Army § Generals](/wiki/Army_ranks_and_insignia_of_the_Austro-Hungarian_Army#Generals)

## Bulgaria

The [Third Bulgarian State](/wiki/History_of_Bulgaria_(1878%E2%80%931946)) from its inception in 1878 had a highest military rank of "general" (Bulgarian: генерал), but in 1897 this rank was split into three grades: general of infantry (генерал от пехотата), of cavalry (генерал от кавалерията) and of artillery (генерал от артилерията). The rank was replaced after World War II, when Bulgaria fell into the Soviet sphere of influence, with the all-encompassing rank of general.

## Finland

Generic General's collar insignia without service branch colour

Full generals (4 star; NATO OF-9) in the [Finnish military](/wiki/Finnish_Defence_Forces) were classified as generals of the branch. These were the generals of the infantry (*Jalkaväenkenraali*), of the cavalry (*Ratsuväenkenraali*), of the jaegers (*Jääkärikenraali*) and of the artillery (*Tykistönkenraali*). The title is now merely honorific, and only one 4-star general is active at any one time in the modern Finnish military.

In the German *[Wehrmacht](/wiki/Wehrmacht)* a General of a branch ([German](/wiki/German_language): *General der Waffengattung*) was linked to service arms of the *[Heer](/wiki/German_Army_(Wehrmacht))* (army) and *[Luftwaffe](/wiki/Luftwaffe)* (air force), depending on where the officer served and what troops he (nominally) commanded. It was equivalent to the [three-star ranks](/wiki/Three-star_rank) of [admiral](/wiki/Admiral_(Germany)) in the Nazi *[Kriegsmarine](/wiki/Kriegsmarine)*, and *[SS-Obergruppenführer und General der Waffen-SS](/wiki/Obergruppenf%C3%BChrer)* in the *Waffen-SS*. A [commander-in-chief](/wiki/Commander-in-chief) (*Kommandierender General* or *Befehlshaber*) of a German [army corps](/wiki/Army_corps) was usually of this rank. In our time this rank might be comparable to NATO [OF-8](/wiki/OF-8).

### Bundeswehr

When the contemporary German Army, the [Bundeswehr](/wiki/Bundeswehr), was founded (on November the 12th, 1955) some of the [names for general ranks](/wiki/General_(Germany)#World_War_II) were replaced with the current ones.

The denomination *General der Panzertruppen*, *General der Infanterie*, *General der Artillerie* and *General der Fernmeldetruppe* are still around, but they are not longer *ranks* but *positions*. These positions seem to roughly correspond to the pre-Bundeswehr *Inspekteur der ...*. For example [Heinz Guderian](/wiki/Heinz_Guderian) had the position of *Inspekteur der Panzertruppen* for a while.

## Poland

Sleeve insignia of a Polish *generał broni*

In the Polish armed forces the rank equivalent to lieutenant general is *[generał](/wiki/Genera%C5%82) broni* ("general of a branch").

## Russian Empire

"General of the branch", Imperial Russian Army

See also: [Ranks and rank insignia of the Imperial Russian Army until 1917](/wiki/Ranks_and_rank_insignia_of_the_Imperial_Russian_Army_until_1917)

General of the Branch is known in [Russian](/wiki/Russian_language) as *General roda Voysk*. [Peter the Great](/wiki/Peter_the_Great) created the ranks of [general of infantry](/wiki/General_of_the_Infantry_(Imperial_Russia)) and [general of cavalry](/wiki/General_of_the_Cavalry) in the [Imperial Russian Army](/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) in early 1700s, though for much of the 18th century a single rank of *[general-en-chef](/wiki/General-in-chief)* was used instead. It was Class 2 in the [Table of Ranks](/wiki/Table_of_Ranks).

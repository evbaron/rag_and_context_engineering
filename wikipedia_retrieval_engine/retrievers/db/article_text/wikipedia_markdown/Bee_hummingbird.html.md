# Bee hummingbird

Not to be confused with [bumblebee hummingbird](/wiki/Bumblebee_hummingbird).

The **bee hummingbird**, **zunzuncito** or **Helena hummingbird** (***Mellisuga helenae***) is a [species](/wiki/Species) of [hummingbird](/wiki/Hummingbird), native to the island of [Cuba](/wiki/Cuba) in the [Caribbean](/wiki/Caribbean). It is the smallest known [bird](/wiki/Bird). The bee hummingbird feeds on nectar of flowers and bugs found in Cuba.

## Description

The bee hummingbird is the [smallest living bird](/wiki/Smallest_organisms). Females weigh 2.6 g (0.092 oz) and are 6.1 cm (2+3⁄8 in) long, and are slightly larger than males, which have an average weight of 1.95 g (0.069 oz) and length of 5.5 cm (2+1⁄8 in). Like all hummingbirds, it is a swift, strong flier.

The male has a green [pileum](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pileum) and bright red throat, an iridescent [gorget](/wiki/Gorget_(bird)) with elongated lateral [plumes](/wiki/Plumage), bluish upper parts, and the rest of the underparts are mostly greyish white. Compared to other small hummingbirds, which often have a slender appearance, the bee hummingbird looks rounded and plump.

Female bee hummingbirds are bluish green with a pale gray underside. The tips of their tail feathers have white spots. During the mating season, males have a reddish to pink head, chin, and throat. The female lays only two eggs at a time, each about the size of a [coffee bean](/wiki/Coffee_bean).

The bee hummingbird's [feathers](/wiki/Feather) have [iridescent](/wiki/Iridescent) colors, which are not always noticeable, but depend on the viewing angle. The bird's slender, pointed bill is adapted for probing deep into [flowers](/wiki/Flower). The bee hummingbird feeds mainly on [nectar](/wiki/Nectar), by moving its tongue rapidly in and out of its mouth. In the process of feeding, the bird picks up [pollen](/wiki/Pollen) on its [bill](/wiki/Beak) and head. When it flies from flower to flower, it transfers the pollen. In this way, it plays an important role in [plant reproduction](/wiki/Plant_reproduction). In one day, the bee hummingbird may visit 1,500 flowers. It is a diurnal bird that can fly at 40–48 km/h (22–26 kn; 11–13 m/s), and it beats its wings 80–200 times per second, which allows it to remain stationary in the air to feed on flowers. The bee hummingbird lives up to seven years in the wild, and 10 years in captivity.

The bee hummingbird has also been described as the [smallest known dinosaur](/wiki/Dinosaur_size). This characterization is based upon the recognition that birds are a living form of [theropod](/wiki/Theropod) dinosaurs (or, strictly speaking, [avian dinosaurs](/wiki/Origin_of_birds)), and no smaller bird or non-avian dinosaur has been found in the fossil record.

The call is described as a "high pitched, jumbled twitter". Within their territory a male will often sing atop the highest tree.

## Taxonomy

The closest evolutionary relative of the bee hummingbird is the [vervain hummingbird](/wiki/Vervain_hummingbird) (*Mellisuga minima)*, the only other member of its genus. The habitats of the vervain hummingbird are in Cuba's neighboring islands, [Hispaniola](/wiki/Hispaniola) and [Jamaica](/wiki/Jamaica).

## Habitat and distribution

The bee hummingbird is [endemic](/wiki/Endemism) to the entire Cuban [archipelago](/wiki/Archipelago), including the main island of Cuba and the [Isla de la Juventud](/wiki/Isla_de_la_Juventud) in the [West Indies](/wiki/West_Indies). In these regions bee hummingbirds generally live in areas of thick growth that contain lianas and epiphytes. Its population is fragmented; it is found in Cuba's [mogote](/wiki/Mogote) areas in [Pinar del Río Province](/wiki/Pinar_del_R%C3%ADo_Province) and more commonly in [Zapata Swamp](/wiki/Zapata_Swamp) ([Matanzas Province](/wiki/Matanzas_Province)) and in eastern Cuba, with reference localities in Alexander Humboldt National Park and Baitiquirí Ecological Reserve ([Guantánamo Province](/wiki/Guant%C3%A1namo_Province)) and Gibara and Sierra Cristal ([Holguín Province](/wiki/Holgu%C3%ADn_Province)).

## Breeding

Side view of the nest

Bee hummingbirds reach sexual maturity at one year of age. The bee hummingbird's breeding season is March–June, with the female laying one or two eggs.

Using strands of [cobwebs](/wiki/Spider_web), [bark](/wiki/Bark_(botany)), and [lichen](/wiki/Lichen), female bee hummingbirds build a cup-shaped nest about 2.5 cm (1 in) in diameter and 3–5 m (9.8–16.4 ft) off the ground. The nest is lined with a layer of soft plant wool. Branches in mature, leafy jucaro (*[Terminalia buceras](/wiki/Terminalia_buceras)*) and juvenile ocuje (*[Calophyllum antillanum](/wiki/Calophyllum_antillanum)*) trees are commonly used for nest building. After completion of the nest, the eggs are [incubated](/wiki/Egg_incubation) for 21 days by only the female, followed by 2 days of hatching, and 18 days of care by the mother. During days of care the mother will hunt for small insects while chicks are left alone in the nest. Over the final 4–5 days of [care](/wiki/Bird#Parental_care_and_fledging), juvenile bee hummingbirds practice their flight capabilities. The nests are used only once.

## Coevolution with flowers

The bee hummingbird's interaction with the flowers that supply nectar is a notable example of bird–plant [coevolution](/wiki/Coevolution) with its primary food source (flowers for [nectar](/wiki/Nectar)). Flowers that bee hummingbirds often feed from are odorless, have long narrow tubular corolla that are brightly colored, and have dilute nectar.

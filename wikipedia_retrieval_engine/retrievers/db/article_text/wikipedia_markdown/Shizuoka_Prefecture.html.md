**Shizuoka Prefecture** (静岡県, *Shizuoka-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [[ɕi.(d)zɯꜜ.o.ka, ɕi.(d)zɯ.o.kaꜜ.keɴ]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Japanese)) is a [prefecture](/wiki/Prefectures_of_Japan) of [Japan](/wiki/Japan) located in the [Chūbu region](/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu_region) of [Honshu](/wiki/Honshu). As of September 2023, Shizuoka Prefecture has a population of 3,555,818 and has a geographic area of 7,777.42 km2 (3,002.88 sq mi). Shizuoka Prefecture borders [Kanagawa Prefecture](/wiki/Kanagawa_Prefecture) to the east, [Yamanashi Prefecture](/wiki/Yamanashi_Prefecture) to the northeast, [Nagano Prefecture](/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to the north, and [Aichi Prefecture](/wiki/Aichi_Prefecture) to the west.

[Shizuoka](/wiki/Shizuoka_(city)) is the capital and [Hamamatsu](/wiki/Hamamatsu) is the largest city in Shizuoka Prefecture, with other major cities including [Fuji](/wiki/Fuji,_Shizuoka), [Numazu](/wiki/Numazu), and [Iwata](/wiki/Iwata,_Shizuoka). Shizuoka Prefecture is located on Japan's [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean) coast and features [Suruga Bay](/wiki/Suruga_Bay) formed by the [Izu Peninsula](/wiki/Izu_Peninsula), and [Lake Hamana](/wiki/Lake_Hamana) which is considered to be one of Japan's largest lakes. [Mount Fuji](/wiki/Mount_Fuji), the tallest [volcano](/wiki/Volcano) in Japan and [cultural icon](/wiki/Cultural_icon) of the country, is partially located in Shizuoka Prefecture on the border with Yamanashi Prefecture. Shizuoka Prefecture has a significant [motoring](/wiki/Motor_vehicle) heritage as the founding location of [Honda](/wiki/Honda), [Suzuki](/wiki/Suzuki_Motor_Corporation), and [Yamaha](/wiki/Yamaha_Corporation), and is home to the [Fuji International Speedway](/wiki/Fuji_International_Speedway).

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Shizuoka Prefecture](/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Shizuoka_Prefecture)

Shizuoka Prefecture was established from the former [Tōtōmi](/wiki/T%C5%8Dt%C5%8Dmi_Province), [Suruga](/wiki/Suruga_Province) and [Izu](/wiki/Izu_Province) provinces.

The area was the home of the first [Tokugawa shōgun](/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate). [Tokugawa Ieyasu](/wiki/Tokugawa_Ieyasu) held the region until he conquered the lands of the [Hōjō clan](/wiki/H%C5%8Dj%C5%8D_clan) in the [Kantō region](/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) and placed land under the stewardship of [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi). After becoming *shōgun*, Tokugawa took the land back for his family and put the area around modern-day Shizuoka City under the direct supervision of the shogunate. With the creation of the Shizuoka han from the [Sunpu Domain](/wiki/Sunpu_Domain) in 1868, it once again became the residence of the Tokugawa family.

## Geography

Shizuoka Prefecture is an elongated region following the coast of the [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean) at the [Suruga Bay](/wiki/Suruga_Bay). In the west, the prefecture extends deep into the [Japan Alps](/wiki/Japan_Alps). In the east, it becomes a narrower coast bounded in the north by [Mount Fuji](/wiki/Mount_Fuji), until it comes to the [Izu Peninsula](/wiki/Izu_Peninsula), a popular resort area pointing south into the Pacific.

As of April 2012, 11% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [natural parks](/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely the [Fuji-Hakone-Izu](/wiki/Fuji-Hakone-Izu_National_Park) and [Minami Alps](/wiki/Minami_Alps_National_Park) National Parks; [Tenryū-Okumikawa Quasi-National Park](/wiki/Tenry%C5%AB-Okumikawa_Quasi-National_Park); and four Prefectural Natural Parks.

### Climate

In Shizuoka prefecture, the [temperature](/wiki/Temperature), over the course of the year, typically varies from 1.1 °C (34 °F) to 30.5 °C (87 °F) and is rarely below -2.2 °C (28 °F) or above 33.8 °C (93 °F.) The summers in Shizuoka are warm, oppressive, and mostly cloudy; the winters are very cold, windy, and mostly clear.

### Disaster

On 15 March 2011, Shizuoka Prefecture was hit with [a magnitude 6.2 earthquake](/wiki/2011_Shizuoka_earthquake) approximately 42 km (26 mi) NNE of [Shizuoka City](/wiki/Shizuoka,_Shizuoka). It is said, that throughout history, Shizuoka area has experienced a large earthquake every 100 to 150 years.

### Mergers

Main article: [List of mergers in Shizuoka Prefecture](/wiki/List_of_mergers_in_Shizuoka_Prefecture)

After the introduction of modern municipalities in 1889, Shizuoka consisted of 337 municipalities: 1 (by definition: district-independent) city and 23 districts with 31 towns and 305 [villages](/wiki/Villages_of_Japan). The Great Shōwa mergers of the 1950s reduced the total from 281 to 97 between 1953 and 1960, including 18 cities by then. The Great Heisei mergers of the 2000s combined the 74 remaining municipalities in the year 2000 into the current 35 by 2010.

## Industry

Home to a shogun, cherry shrimp, and Japan's green tea heartland. Situated along Suruga Bay between Tokyo and Nagoya on the historic Tokaido route, the Pacific coast city of Shizuoka is famed for supplying most of Japan's tea and maguro tuna.

### Motorcycles

Shizuoka-based companies are world leaders in several major industrial sectors. **[Honda](/wiki/Honda)**, **[Yamaha](/wiki/Yamaha_Motor_Company)**, and **[Suzuki](/wiki/Suzuki)** all have their roots in Shizuoka prefecture and are still manufacturing here. Thanks to this, Shizuoka prefecture accounts for 28% of Japanese [motorcycle](/wiki/Motorcycle) [exports](/wiki/Export).

### Musical instruments

**Yamaha** and **[Kawai](/wiki/Kawai_Musical_Instruments)** are both global [piano](/wiki/Piano) brands. Yamaha has the largest share in the global piano market. Kawai has the second largest share. They both got their start in Shizuoka prefecture in the early twentieth century.

**Yamaha** and **[Roland](/wiki/Roland_Corporation)** are major brand for [electronic musical instruments](/wiki/Electronic_musical_instrument). In the [electronic piano](/wiki/Electronic_piano) world market, Yamaha has the world's largest share. Roland and Kawai have the second and third place share. Roland and Yamaha also manufacture high-quality [synthesizers](/wiki/Synthesizer) and [drum machines](/wiki/Drum_machine) for professional musicians.

In addition, various instruments such as [wind instruments](/wiki/Wind_instrument) and [guitars](/wiki/Guitar) are manufactured in this prefecture. There are about 200 companies that manufacture musical instruments, in this prefecture.

Most of these musical instruments are especially produced in [Hamamatsu](/wiki/Hamamatsu) City.

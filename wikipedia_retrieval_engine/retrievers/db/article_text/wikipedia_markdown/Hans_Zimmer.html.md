**Hans Florian Zimmer** (German pronunciation: [[ˈhans ˈfloːʁi̯aːn ˈtsɪmɐ]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:De-Hans_Florian_Zimmer.flac); born 12 September 1957) is a German film composer and music producer. He has received [numerous accolades](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Hans_Zimmer), including two [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards), a [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award), three [Golden Globe Awards](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Awards), and five [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards) as well as nominations for seven [Emmy Awards](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Awards). Zimmer was also named one of the Top 100 Living Geniuses by *[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)* in 2007.

His works are notable for integrating [electronic music](/wiki/Electronic_music) sounds with traditional orchestral arrangements. Since the 1980s, Zimmer has composed music for over 150 films. He has won two [Academy Awards for Best Original Score](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Score) for *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)* (1994), and for *[Dune](/wiki/Dune_(2021_film))* (2021). His works include *[Gladiator](/wiki/Gladiator_(2000_film))* (2000), *[Black Hawk Down](/wiki/Black_Hawk_Down_(film))* (2001), *[The Last Samurai](/wiki/The_Last_Samurai)* (2003), the *[Pirates of the Caribbean](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean_(film_series))* series (2006–2011), [*The Dark Knight* trilogy](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Trilogy) (2005–2012), *[Inception](/wiki/Inception)* (2010), *[Man of Steel](/wiki/Man_of_Steel_(film))* (2013), *[Interstellar](/wiki/Interstellar_(film))* (2014), *[Dunkirk](/wiki/Dunkirk_(2017_film))* (2017), *[No Time to Die](/wiki/No_Time_to_Die)* (2021), the *[Dune](/wiki/Dune_(2021_film)#Sequels_and_spin-offs)* series (2021–), and *[F1](/wiki/F1_(film))* (2025).

Zimmer spent the early part of his career in the United Kingdom before moving to the United States. He is the head of the film music division at [DreamWorks Pictures](/wiki/DreamWorks_Pictures) and [DreamWorks Animation](/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation) studios and works with other composers through the company that he founded, [Remote Control Productions](/wiki/Remote_Control_Productions_(American_company)), formerly known as Media Ventures. His studio in [Santa Monica, California](/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California), has an extensive range of computer equipment and keyboards, allowing demo versions of film scores to be created quickly.

Zimmer has collaborated with [Christopher Nolan](/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) on six films starting with *[Batman Begins](/wiki/Batman_Begins)* (2005). He has also worked on multiple projects with directors including [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott), [Ron Howard](/wiki/Ron_Howard), [Gore Verbinski](/wiki/Gore_Verbinski), [Michael Bay](/wiki/Michael_Bay), [Guy Ritchie](/wiki/Guy_Ritchie), [Denis Villeneuve](/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve), and [Tony Scott](/wiki/Tony_Scott).

## Early life and education

Zimmer was born in [Frankfurt](/wiki/Frankfurt), West Germany. As a young child, he lived in [Königstein-Falkenstein](/wiki/K%C3%B6nigstein_im_Taunus), where he played the piano at home but had piano lessons only briefly, as he disliked the discipline of formal lessons, jokingly stating in a 2002 interview that "either I was going to kill the teacher, or he was going to kill me". Zimmer attended the [Ecole d'Humanité](/wiki/Ecole_d%27Humanit%C3%A9), an international boarding school in the [Canton of Bern](/wiki/Canton_of_Bern), Switzerland. He moved to [London](/wiki/London) as a teenager and attended [Hurtwood House](/wiki/Hurtwood_House) school. During his childhood, he was strongly influenced by the film scores of [Ennio Morricone](/wiki/Ennio_Morricone) and has cited *[Once Upon a Time in the West](/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_the_West#Music)* as the score that inspired him to become a film composer.

In a speech at the [1999 Berlin Film Festival](/wiki/49th_Berlin_International_Film_Festival), Zimmer stated that he is [Jewish](/wiki/Jews), and talked about his mother surviving [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II) thanks to her escape from Germany to the UK in 1939. In an interview in May 2014, Zimmer revealed that it was difficult growing up in [post-War](/wiki/History_of_Germany_(1945%E2%80%931990)) Germany being Jewish and said, "I think my parents were always wary of me telling the neighbors."

In an interview with [Mashable](/wiki/Mashable) in February 2013, he said of his parents: "My mother was very musical, basically a musician and my father was an engineer and an inventor. So I grew up modifying the piano, shall we say, which made my mother gasp in horror, and my father would think it was fantastic when I would attach chainsaws and stuff like that to the piano because he thought it was an evolution in technology." In an interview with the German television station [ZDF](/wiki/ZDF) in 2006, he said: "My father died when I was just a child, and I escaped somehow into the music and music has been my best friend."

## Career

See also: [Hans Zimmer discography](/wiki/Hans_Zimmer_discography)

### 1970s and 1980s

Zimmer began his career playing keyboards and synthesizers in the 1970s, with the band Krakatoa. He worked with [the Buggles](/wiki/The_Buggles), a [new wave](/wiki/New_wave_music) band formed in London in 1977 with [Trevor Horn](/wiki/Trevor_Horn), [Geoff Downes](/wiki/Geoff_Downes) and [Bruce Woolley](/wiki/Bruce_Woolley). Zimmer can be seen briefly in the Buggles' music video for the 1979 song "[Video Killed the Radio Star](/wiki/Video_Killed_the_Radio_Star)". After working with the Buggles, he started to work for the Italian group [Krisma](/wiki/Krisma), a [new wave](/wiki/New_wave_music) band formed in 1976 with [Maurizio Arcieri](/wiki/Maurizio_Arcieri) and Christina Moser. He was a featured synthesist for Krisma's third album, *Cathode Mamma*. He has also worked with the band [Helden](/wiki/Helden_(band)) (with [Warren Cann](/wiki/Warren_Cann) from [Ultravox](/wiki/Ultravox)).

In 1984, both Zimmer (on keyboards) and Cann (on drums), were invited to be part of the Spanish group [Mecano](/wiki/Mecano) for a live performance in Segovia (Spain). Two songs from this concert were included in the *[Mecano: En Concierto](/wiki/Mecano:_En_Concierto)* album released in 1985 only in Spain. In 1985, he contributed to the [Shriekback](/wiki/Shriekback) album *[Oil and Gold](/wiki/Oil_and_Gold)*. In 1980, Zimmer co-produced a single, "[History of the World, Part 1](/wiki/The_History_of_the_World_(Part_1))", with, and for, UK punk band [The Damned](/wiki/The_Damned_(band)), which was also included on their 1980 LP release, *[The Black Album](/wiki/The_Black_Album_(The_Damned_album))*, and carried the description of his efforts as "Over-Produced by Hans Zimmer."

While living in London, Zimmer wrote advertising jingles for Air-Edel Associates. In the 1980s, Zimmer partnered with [Stanley Myers](/wiki/Stanley_Myers), a prolific film composer who wrote the scores for over sixty films. Zimmer and Myers co–founded the London–based Lillie Yard recording studio. Together, Myers and Zimmer worked on fusing the traditional orchestral sound with electronic instruments. Some of the films on which Zimmer and Myers worked are *[Moonlighting](/wiki/Moonlighting_(film))* (1982), *[Success Is the Best Revenge](/wiki/Success_Is_the_Best_Revenge)* (1984), *[Insignificance](/wiki/Insignificance_(film))* (1985), and *[My Beautiful Laundrette](/wiki/My_Beautiful_Laundrette)* (1985). Zimmer's first solo score was *[Terminal Exposure](/wiki/Terminal_Exposure)* for director [Nico Mastorakis](/wiki/Nico_Mastorakis) in 1987, for which he also wrote the songs. Zimmer acted as score producer for the 1987 film *[The Last Emperor](/wiki/The_Last_Emperor)*, which won the [Academy Award for Best Original Score](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Music_(Scoring)).

One of Zimmer's most durable works from his time in the United Kingdom was the theme song for the television game show *[Going for Gold](/wiki/Going_for_Gold)*, which he composed with Sandy McClelland in 1987. In an interview with the [BBC](/wiki/BBC), Zimmer said: "*Going for Gold* was a lot of fun. It's the sort of stuff you do when you don't have a career yet. God, I just felt so lucky because this thing paid my rent for the longest time."

A turning point in Zimmer's career occurred with the 1988 film *[Rain Man](/wiki/Rain_Man)*. [Hollywood](/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles,_California) director [Barry Levinson](/wiki/Barry_Levinson) was looking for someone to score *Rain Man*, and his wife heard the soundtrack CD of the [anti-apartheid](/wiki/Internal_resistance_to_apartheid) drama *[A World Apart](/wiki/A_World_Apart_(1988_film))*, for which Zimmer had composed the music. Levinson was impressed by Zimmer's work and hired him to score *Rain Man*. In the score, Zimmer uses synthesizers, mostly a [Fairlight CMI](/wiki/Fairlight_CMI), mixed with [steel drums](/wiki/Steelpan). Zimmer explained that "It was a [road movie](/wiki/Road_movie), and road movies usually have jangly guitars or a bunch of strings. I kept thinking don't be bigger than the characters. Try to keep it contained. The Raymond character doesn't actually know where he is. The world is so different to him. He might as well be on Mars. So, why don't we just invent our own world music for a world that doesn't really exist?" Zimmer's score for *Rain Man* was nominated for an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) in 1989, and the film won four Academy Awards including [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture).

A year after *Rain Man*, Zimmer was asked to compose the score for [Bruce Beresford](/wiki/Bruce_Beresford)'s *[Driving Miss Daisy](/wiki/Driving_Miss_Daisy)* which, like *Rain Man*, won an Academy Award for Best Picture. *Driving Miss Daisy'*s instrumentation consisted entirely of synthesizers and [samplers](/wiki/Sampler_(musical_instrument)), played by Zimmer. According to an interview with *[Sound on Sound](/wiki/Sound_on_Sound)* magazine in 2002, the piano sounds heard within the score come from the [Roland](/wiki/Roland_Corporation) MKS–20, a rackmount synthesizer. Zimmer joked: "It didn't sound anything like a piano, but it behaved like a piano."

### 1990s and 2000s

> "I listen to [Zimmer's] music and I don't even have to shut my eyes. I can see the pictures. And that's why, in many respects, I know I can talk pictures with Hans. He responds to pictures."

— [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott), director and producer and frequent collaborator with Zimmer.

The soundtrack to [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott)'s 1991 film *[Thelma & Louise](/wiki/Thelma_%26_Louise)* by Zimmer featured the trademark [slide guitar](/wiki/Slide_guitar) performance by [Pete Haycock](/wiki/Pete_Haycock) on the "Thunderbird" theme in the film. As a teenager, Zimmer was a fan of Haycock, and their collaboration on film scores includes *[K2](/wiki/K2_(film))* and *[Drop Zone](/wiki/Drop_Zone_(film))*. Zimmer wrote the theme for [Tony Scott](/wiki/Tony_Scott)'s 1993 film *[True Romance](/wiki/True_Romance)*, which he based on [Carl Orff](/wiki/Carl_Orff)'s *[Gassenhauer](/wiki/Gassenhauer)*. *Gassenhauer* had previously been used in the 1973 film *[Badlands](/wiki/Badlands_(film))*, which had a similar story of a young man and a girl on the run following a violent crime. The catchy theme, played on nine [marimbas](/wiki/Marimba), contrasts starkly with the violence of the film.

For the 1992 film *[The Power of One](/wiki/The_Power_of_One_(film))*, Zimmer travelled to Africa in order to use African choirs and drums in the recording of the score. On the strength of this work, [Walt Disney Animation Studios](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios) approached Zimmer to compose the score for the 1994 film *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)*. This was to be his first score for an animated film. Zimmer said that he had wanted to go to South Africa to record parts of the soundtrack, but was unable to visit the country as he had a police record there "for doing 'subversive' movies" after his work on *The Power of One*. Disney studio bosses expressed fears that Zimmer would be killed if he went to South Africa, so the recording of the choirs was organised during a visit by [Lebo M](/wiki/Lebo_M). Zimmer won numerous awards for his work on *The Lion King*, including an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) for [Best Original Score](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Score), a [Golden Globe](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award), and two [Grammys](/wiki/Grammy). In 1997, the score was adapted into a [Broadway musical version](/wiki/The_Lion_King_(musical)) which won the [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) for Best Musical in 1998. As of April 2012, the musical version of *The Lion King* is the highest grossing Broadway show of all time, having grossed $853.8 million.

Zimmer's score for [Tony Scott](/wiki/Tony_Scott)'s 1995 film *[Crimson Tide](/wiki/Crimson_Tide_(film))* won a Grammy Award for the main theme, which makes heavy use of synthesizers in place of traditional orchestral instruments. For *[The Thin Red Line](/wiki/The_Thin_Red_Line_(1998_film))* (1998), Zimmer said that the director [Terrence Malick](/wiki/Terrence_Malick) wanted the music before he started filming, so he recorded six and a half hours of music. Zimmer's next project was *[The Prince of Egypt](/wiki/The_Prince_of_Egypt)* (1998), which was produced by [DreamWorks Animation](/wiki/DreamWorks_Animation). He introduced [Ofra Haza](/wiki/Ofra_Haza), an Israeli Yemenite singer, to the directors, and they thought she was so beautiful that they designed one of the characters in the film to look like her. Zimmer's [score for the](/wiki/The_Thin_Red_Line_(soundtrack)) 1998 film *[The Thin Red Line](/wiki/The_Thin_Red_Line_(1998_film))* is regarded as one of his most important works. The nine-minute cue at the climax of the film, "The Journey to the Line", uses a recurring theme based on four chords, combined with a "ticking clock" [motif](/wiki/Motif_(music)) that has been featured in a range of subsequent scores composed by Zimmer. The piece has been used in numerous trailers and video games, and has earned the nickname "the forbidden cue" due to the tendency of film makers to use it as a [temp track](/wiki/Temp_track) for dramatic scenes.

Zimmer at *[The Dark Knight](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight)* premiere in 2008

In the 2000s, Zimmer composed scores for Hollywood blockbuster films including three Ridley Scott films, *[Gladiator](/wiki/Gladiator_(2000_film))* (2000), *[Black Hawk Down](/wiki/Black_Hawk_Down_(film))* and *[Hannibal](/wiki/Hannibal_(2001_film))* (2001), followed by *[The Last Samurai](/wiki/The_Last_Samurai)* (2003), *[Madagascar](/wiki/Madagascar_(2005_film))* (2005), *[The Da Vinci Code](/wiki/The_Da_Vinci_Code_(film))* (2006), *[The Simpsons Movie](/wiki/The_Simpsons_Movie)* (2007), *[Kung Fu Panda](/wiki/Kung_Fu_Panda_(film))* (2008), which he co-composed with [John Powell](/wiki/John_Powell_(film_composer)), *[Angels & Demons](/wiki/Angels_%26_Demons_(film))* (2009), and *[Sherlock Holmes](/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_(2009_film))* (2009). Other work in the 2000s included the Spanish language film *Casi Divas*, and *[The Burning Plain](/wiki/The_Burning_Plain)* (2009). He composed the theme for the television boxing series *[The Contender](/wiki/The_Contender_(TV_series))* and worked with [Lorne Balfe](/wiki/Lorne_Balfe) on the music for *[Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 2](/wiki/Call_of_Duty:_Modern_Warfare_2)*, which was his first video game project. Zimmer also collaborated with composers [Borislav Slavov](/wiki/Borislav_Slavov) and Tilman Sillescu to create the music score for the video game *[Crysis 2](/wiki/Crysis_2)*.

In October 2000, Zimmer performed live in concert for the first time with an orchestra and choir at the 27th Annual Flanders International Film Festival in [Ghent](/wiki/Ghent). While writing the score for *The Last Samurai*, Zimmer felt that his knowledge of [Japanese music](/wiki/Music_of_Japan) was extremely limited. He began doing extensive research, but the more he studied, the less he felt he knew. Zimmer took what he had written to Japan for feedback and was shocked when he was asked how he knew so much about Japanese music.

During the scoring of *The Last Samurai* in early 2003, Zimmer was approached by the producer [Jerry Bruckheimer](/wiki/Jerry_Bruckheimer), with whom he had worked previously on *[Crimson Tide](/wiki/Crimson_Tide_(film))*, *[Days of Thunder](/wiki/Days_of_Thunder)*, *[The Rock](/wiki/The_Rock_(film)),* and *[Pearl Harbor](/wiki/Pearl_Harbor_(film))*. Bruckheimer had finished shooting *[Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean:_The_Curse_of_the_Black_Pearl)* but was unhappy with the music composed for the film by [Alan Silvestri](/wiki/Alan_Silvestri) and wanted a replacement score. Bruckheimer wanted Zimmer to rescore the film, but due to his commitments on *The Last Samurai*, the task of composing and supervising music for *Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl* was given to [Klaus Badelt](/wiki/Klaus_Badelt), one of Zimmer's colleagues at [Media Ventures](/wiki/Media_Ventures). Zimmer provided some themes that were used in the film, although he is not credited on screen. Zimmer was hired as the composer for the three subsequent films in the series, *[Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man's Chest](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean:_Dead_Man%27s_Chest)* (2006), *[Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean:_At_World%27s_End)* (2007), and *[Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides](/wiki/Pirates_of_the_Caribbean:_On_Stranger_Tides)* (2011), collaborating with [Rodrigo y Gabriela](/wiki/Rodrigo_y_Gabriela) for the last.

Zimmer is noted for his work on the scores of [Christopher Nolan](/wiki/Christopher_Nolan)'s *[Batman Begins](/wiki/Batman_Begins)* (2005) and *[The Dark Knight](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight)* (2008), which he co-composed with [James Newton Howard](/wiki/James_Newton_Howard). For the [soundtrack](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_(soundtrack)) of *The Dark Knight*, Zimmer decided to represent the villain of [the Joker](/wiki/Joker_(The_Dark_Knight)) by a single note played on the cello by his long-time colleague [Martin Tillman](/wiki/Martin_Tillman). Zimmer said: "I wanted to write something people would truly hate." The scores for these films were disqualified from receiving [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) nominations for Best Original Score due to too many composers being listed on the cue sheet. Zimmer succeeded in reversing the decision not to nominate *The Dark Knight* in December 2008, arguing that the process of creating a modern film score was collaborative and that it was important to credit a range of people who had played a part in its production. Zimmer explained his approach to scoring with other musicians in an interview with *[Soundtrack.net](/wiki/SoundtrackNet)* in 2006:

> Originally I had this idea that it should be possible to create some kind of community around this kind of work, and I think by muddying the titles – not having "you are the composer, you are the arranger, you are the orchestrator" – it just sort of helped us to work more collaboratively. It wasn't that important to me that I had "score by Hans Zimmer" and took sole credit on these things. It's like *Gladiator*: I gave [Lisa Gerrard](/wiki/Lisa_Gerrard) the co-credit because, even though she didn't write the main theme, her presence and contributions were very influential. She was more than just a soloist, and this is why I have such a problem with specific credits.

For the 2009 film *[Sherlock Holmes](/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes_(2009_film))*,  *[Daily Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))* reported that Zimmer purchased an out-of-tune piano for 200 dollars and used it throughout the scoring process because of its "quirkiness". For the 2011 sequel, *[Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows](/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes:_A_Game_of_Shadows)*, Zimmer and director [Guy Ritchie](/wiki/Guy_Ritchie) incorporated authentic [Romani](/wiki/Romani_people) music, which they researched by visiting Slovakia, Italy, and France. The music in the film is played by Romani musicians.

### 2010s

For the 2010 film *[Inception](/wiki/Inception)*, Zimmer used electronic manipulation of the song "[Non, je ne regrette rien](/wiki/Non,_je_ne_regrette_rien)". The horn sound in the score, described by Zimmer as "like huge foghorns over a city" became a popular feature in [film trailers](/wiki/Trailer_(promotion)). "It's funny how that sort of thing becomes part of the [zeitgeist](/wiki/Zeitgeist)", he said. "But I suppose that's exactly what trailers are looking for: something iconic, lasts less than a second, and shakes the seats in the theater."

In 2012, Zimmer composed and produced the music for the [84th Academy Awards](/wiki/84th_Academy_Awards) with [Pharrell Williams](/wiki/Pharrell_Williams) of [The Neptunes](/wiki/The_Neptunes). He also composed a new version of the theme music for *[ABC World News](/wiki/ABC_World_News)*. Zimmer composed the score for *[The Dark Knight Rises](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises)*, the final instalment of Christopher Nolan's [*The Dark Knight* Trilogy](/wiki/Batman_in_film). The film was released in July 2012. Zimmer described himself as "devastated" in the aftermath of the [2012 Aurora, Colorado shooting](/wiki/2012_Aurora,_Colorado_shooting), which occurred at a screening of *The Dark Knight Rises:* "I just feel so incredibly sad for these people." He recorded a track entitled "Aurora", a choral arrangement of a theme from the [*Dark Knight Rises* soundtrack](/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises_(soundtrack)), to raise money for the victims of the shooting.

Zimmer co-composed the music for the television series *[The Bible](/wiki/The_Bible_(TV_series))*, which was broadcast in March 2013, with [Lorne Balfe](/wiki/Lorne_Balfe) and [Lisa Gerrard](/wiki/Lisa_Gerrard), and the score for *[12 Years a Slave](/wiki/12_Years_a_Slave_(film))*, which won the [Academy Award](/wiki/86th_Academy_Awards) for Best Picture in March 2014. Zimmer composed the *Tomorrowland Hymn* for the [Tomorrowland](/wiki/Tomorrowland_(festival)) festival to celebrate its tenth anniversary in July 2014. Zimmer composed the music for the 2014 film *[The Amazing Spider-Man 2](/wiki/The_Amazing_Spider-Man_2)* alongside "The Magnificent Six", which consisted of [Pharrell Williams](/wiki/Pharrell_Williams), [Johnny Marr](/wiki/Johnny_Marr), [Michael Einziger](/wiki/Michael_Einziger), [Junkie XL](/wiki/Tom_Holkenborg), Andrew Kawczynski, and Steve Mazzaro.
Zimmer composed the music for Christopher Nolan's 2014 film *[Interstellar](/wiki/Interstellar_(film))*, which earned him another Academy Award nomination for Best Original Score. He collaborated with Junkie XL to compose the music for the 2016 film *[Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice](/wiki/Batman_v_Superman:_Dawn_of_Justice)*. In an interview with BBC News in March 2016, Zimmer said that he was retiring from composing the music for [superhero films](/wiki/Superhero_films), saying of *Batman v Superman*: "This one was very hard for me to do, to try to find new language".

Zimmer composed the main theme for the 2016 BBC nature documentary *[Planet Earth II](/wiki/Planet_Earth_II)*, presented by [David Attenborough](/wiki/David_Attenborough). He later composed the score for the 2017 BBC nature documentary *[Blue Planet II](/wiki/Blue_Planet_II)* alongside Jacob Shea and David Fleming, also presented by David Attenborough. In an interview with [Kirsty Wark](/wiki/Kirsty_Wark) on BBC Radio, Zimmer said in October 2022: "I think the only work that I've done that's worth anything is working for Sir David Attenborough on those programmes, because it's the only thing that actually means something to this world and ultimately might have an impact on how we approach this world." Zimmer composed the main theme for the 2016 [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) production *[The Crown](/wiki/The_Crown_(TV_series))*. Also in 2016 Zimmer released an online course teaching the basics of film scoring. He next composed the score for Christopher Nolan's 2017 film *[Dunkirk](/wiki/Dunkirk_(2017_film))*, basing part of the score on a recording of a ticking watch that he had been given by Nolan. Zimmer also worked on the score for [Denis Villeneuve](/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve)'s *[Blade Runner 2049](/wiki/Blade_Runner_2049)*. Hans Zimmer and co-composer [Benjamin Wallfisch](/wiki/Benjamin_Wallfisch) took over scoring duties after [Jóhann Jóhannsson](/wiki/J%C3%B3hann_J%C3%B3hannsson) left the project.

In 2018, Zimmer composed the score for the new [FIFA Anthem](/wiki/FIFA_Anthem), called "Living Football" in reference to the new motto of FIFA, "Living Football" was also used as television intro theme for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup) in Russia. Also in 2018, Zimmer remixed the [UEFA Champions League Anthem](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League_Anthem) with rapper [Vince Staples](/wiki/Vince_Staples) for EA Sports' [*FIFA*](/wiki/FIFA_(video_game_series)) video game *[FIFA 19](/wiki/FIFA_19)*, with it also featuring in the game's reveal trailer. Zimmer composed the score for *[Dark Phoenix](/wiki/Dark_Phoenix_(film))*, directed by [Simon Kinberg](/wiki/Simon_Kinberg), contrary to his 2016 statements of not scoring another superhero film following his experience working on *Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice*. In 2019, he scored the [photorealistic animated remake](/wiki/The_Lion_King_(2019_film)) of Disney's *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)*, directed by [Jon Favreau](/wiki/Jon_Favreau). In August 2018, Zimmer was announced as the composer for *[Wonder Woman 1984](/wiki/Wonder_Woman_1984)*. In March 2019, it was announced that Zimmer would be scoring [Denis Villeneuve](/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve)'s *[Dune](/wiki/Dune_(2021_film))*. In June 2019, Zimmer was hired to create sounds for [BMW](/wiki/BMW_M)'s concept vehicle, the [Vision M Next](/wiki/BMW_M1#Vision_M_Next_concept). He produced and composed [warning sounds](/wiki/Electric_vehicle_warning_sounds) for the electric car line. These sounds are both for those on the interior and on the exterior, and could help warn pedestrians that an electric car is approaching.

### 2020s

Zimmer and the [Royal Philharmonic Orchestra](/wiki/Royal_Philharmonic_Orchestra) at the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) in London in October 2022 during *The Sound of 007 in Concert*

On 6 January 2020, Zimmer took over as composer for the *[James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond)* film *[No Time to Die](/wiki/No_Time_to_Die)* after previous composer [Dan Romer](/wiki/Dan_Romer) left the project. Speaking on scoring for *No Time to Die*, Zimmer told *[The Hindu](/wiki/The_Hindu)*, "Having done the three *Batman* movies—which are three movies to you but are 12 years of my life to me—I really understand where [Daniel [Craig]](/wiki/Daniel_Craig) was with this; he dedicated 16 years of his life to Bond... I wanted to not only do a good score but dig a little deeper and honour the man, the work and all the people who've been working on this for so long". On 26 February 2020, [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) released an anthem for its [25th season](/wiki/2020_Major_League_Soccer_season), which was composed by Zimmer. In March 2020, Zimmer composed the score for *[Hillbilly Elegy](/wiki/Hillbilly_Elegy_(film))*.

In 2022, he collaborated with [Camila Cabello](/wiki/Camila_Cabello) on the song "Take Me Back Home" for the documentary series *[Frozen Planet II](/wiki/Frozen_Planet_II)*. [Aurora](/wiki/Aurora_(singer)) also worked with Zimmer, contributing vocals to the series' soundtrack and performing "Take Me Back Home" alongside Zimmer at the [Royal Albert Hall](/wiki/Royal_Albert_Hall) during the BBC's Earth Proms. Zimmer worked on the 2022 film *[Top Gun: Maverick](/wiki/Top_Gun:_Maverick)*, contributing to the main title credits and other tracks.

Zimmer composed the score for the film *[Dune](/wiki/Dune_(2021_film))* (2021), which earned him his second [Academy Award for Best Original Score](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Score). The [film's music](/wiki/Music_of_Dune_(2021_film)) was released in several different volumes. He composed the score for the [sequel](/wiki/Dune:_Part_Two), released in 2024. Both films heavily featured the use of the [Armenian duduk](/wiki/Duduk). As of 2024, Zimmer has begun writing music for a [third *Dune* film](/wiki/Dune:_Part_Three) while Villeneuve has started on the film's script. In 2025, Zimmer scored *[F1](/wiki/F1_(film))*, a sports film starring [Brad Pitt](/wiki/Brad_Pitt) and directed by [Joseph Kosinski](/wiki/Joseph_Kosinski).

In 2025, Zimmer was announced as one of three composers for [Zack Snyder](/wiki/Zack_Snyder)'s upcoming film *[The Last Photograph](/wiki/The_Last_Photograph_(upcoming_film))*, collaborating with Omer Benyamin and Steven Doar on the project's score. Zimmer said that he had turned down multiple offers to compose for films in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe), explaining that he was "looking for other things" and that scheduling played a role in his decision.

## Personal life

Zimmer's first wife was model Vicki Carolin, with whom he has a daughter. The couple divorced in 1992. On 3 April 2020, Zimmer filed for divorce from his second wife, Suzanne Zimmer, whom he married in 1995 and with whom he had three children. On 15 June 2023, Zimmer proposed to his partner Dina De Luca on stage at London's [O2 Arena](/wiki/The_O2_Arena) during one of his shows.

## Awards and honours

Main article: [List of awards and nominations received by Hans Zimmer](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Hans_Zimmer)

Zimmer's Star on the "[Boulevard der Stars](/wiki/Boulevard_der_Stars)" in [Berlin](/wiki/Berlin)

Zimmer has received a range of honours and awards, including the [Max Steiner Film Music Achievement Award](/wiki/Hollywood_in_Vienna#Max_Steiner_Film_Music_Achievement_Award) at [Hollywood in Vienna](/wiki/Hollywood_in_Vienna) in 2018, the Career Achievement Award "for excellence in film music composition" from the [National Board of Review](/wiki/National_Board_of_Review) in 2003, the Frederick Loewe Award for Film Composing at the [Palm Springs International Film Festival](/wiki/Palm_Springs_International_Film_Festival) in 2003, the [ASCAP](/wiki/American_Society_of_Composers,_Authors_and_Publishers) Henry Mancini Award in 2003, and the Richard Kirk Career Achievement Award from the [BMI Film Music Awards](/wiki/BMI_Film_%26_TV_Awards) in 1996.

In December 2010, Zimmer received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame). He dedicated the award to his publicist and long-term friend [Ronni Chasen](/wiki/Ronni_Chasen), who had been shot and killed in [Beverly Hills](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California) the previous month.

In June 2016, Zimmer was one of the inaugural winners of the [Stephen Hawking Medal for Science Communication](/wiki/Stephen_Hawking_Medal_for_Science_Communication).

In November 2017, a [main-belt asteroid](/wiki/Asteroid_belt)—[(495253) 2013 OC8](/wiki/List_of_minor_planets:_495001%E2%80%93496000#253)—discovered by Polish astronomers [Michał Kusiak](/wiki/List_of_minor_planet_discoverers#M._Kusiak) and [Michał Żołnowski](/wiki/List_of_minor_planet_discoverers#M._Zolnowski), was named "Hanszimmer".

As of 2022, Zimmer has received twelve [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Awards) nominations for his work, with two wins; the first being at the [67th Academy Awards](/wiki/67th_Academy_Awards) for the 1994 film *[The Lion King](/wiki/The_Lion_King)* and the second being for the 2021 film *[Dune](/wiki/Dune_(2021_film))* at the [94th Academy Awards](/wiki/94th_Academy_Awards).

On 2 October 2018, Zimmer received the [Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany](/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany).

In 2019, Zimmer was inducted as a [Disney Legend](/wiki/Disney_Legends).

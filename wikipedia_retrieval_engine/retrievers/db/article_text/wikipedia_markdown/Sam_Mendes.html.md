**Sir Samuel Alexander Mendes** [CBE](/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (born 1 August 1965) is an English filmmaker and stage director. In 2000, Mendes was appointed a CBE for his services to drama, and he was [knighted](/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in the [2020 New Years Honours List](/wiki/2020_New_Year_Honours). In 2000, Mendes was awarded the [Shakespeare Prize](/wiki/Shakespeare_Prize) by the [Alfred Toepfer Foundation](/wiki/Alfred_Toepfer_Stiftung_F.V.S.) in [Hamburg](/wiki/Hamburg), [Germany](/wiki/Germany). In 2005, he received a lifetime achievement award from the [Directors Guild of Great Britain](/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_Great_Britain). In 2008, *[The Daily Telegraph](/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)* ranked him number 15 in their list of the "100 most powerful people in [British culture](/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom)".

Born in [Reading](/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire) to a [Trinidadian](/wiki/Trinidadians_and_Tobagonians) Catholic father and an [English Jewish](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_England) mother, Mendes grew up in North London. He read English at [Peterhouse](/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge) at [Cambridge University](/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and began directing plays there before joining [Donmar Warehouse](/wiki/Donmar_Warehouse), which became a centre of 1990s [London theatre](/wiki/Theatre_of_the_United_Kingdom) culture. In theatre, he is known for his dark re-inventions of the stage musicals *[Cabaret](/wiki/Cabaret_(musical))* (1993), *[Oliver!](/wiki/Oliver!)* (1994), *[Company](/wiki/Company_(musical))* (1995), and *[Gypsy](/wiki/Gypsy_(musical))* (2003).

He directed an original [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) stage musical for the first time with *[Charlie and the Chocolate Factory](/wiki/Charlie_and_the_Chocolate_Factory_(musical))* (2013). For his work on the London stage, Mendes has received five [Laurence Olivier Awards](/wiki/Laurence_Olivier_Awards) for *Company*, *Twelfth Night* and *The Ferryman* and for his work on [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) he has earned two [Tony Awards](/wiki/Tony_Awards) for [Best Direction of a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Direction_of_a_Play) for his work on *[The Ferryman](/wiki/The_Ferryman_(play))* in 2019, and *[The Lehman Trilogy](/wiki/The_Lehman_Trilogy)* in 2022.

In film, he made his directorial debut with the drama *[American Beauty](/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film))* (1999), which earned him the [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) and [Golden Globe Award for Best Director](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director). He has since directed the films *[Road to Perdition](/wiki/Road_to_Perdition)* (2002), *[Jarhead](/wiki/Jarhead_(film))* (2005), *[Revolutionary Road](/wiki/Revolutionary_Road_(film))* (2008), and the [James Bond](/wiki/Production_of_the_James_Bond_films) films *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)* (2012) and *[Spectre](/wiki/Spectre_(2015_film))* (2015). For the war film *[1917](/wiki/1917_(2019_film))* (2019), he received the [BAFTA Award](/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Direction) and Golden Globe Award for Best Director, as well as his second Academy Award nominations for Best Director, [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture) and [Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Screenplay). Most recently, Mendes produced Academy Award nominated film *[Hamnet](/wiki/Hamnet_(film))*.

Mendes is currently developing a set of four individual but interconnected films entitled *[The Beatles – A Four-Film Cinematic Event](/wiki/The_Beatles_%E2%80%93_A_Four-Film_Cinematic_Event)*, based on the lives of each of the members of [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles).

## Early life and education

Mendes was born on 1 August 1965 in [Reading, Berkshire](/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire). He is the son of [Valerie Mendes](/wiki/Valerie_Mendes) (born Barnett), a publisher and author, and Jameson Peter Mendes, a university professor. His father is a [Roman Catholic](/wiki/Catholic_Church) of [Portuguese](/wiki/Portuguese_Trinidadian_and_Tobagonian) descent from [Trinidad and Tobago](/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago), and his mother is an [English Jew](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_England). His grandfather was the [Trinidadian writer](/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_literature) [Alfred Hubert Mendes](/wiki/Alfred_Mendes).

Mendes's parents divorced when he was three years old, after which Mendes and his mother settled in [Primrose Hill](/wiki/Primrose_Hill) in North London. He attended Primrose Hill Primary School and was in the same class as future [Foreign Secretary](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs) [David Miliband](/wiki/David_Miliband) and author [Zoë Heller](/wiki/Zo%C3%AB_Heller). In 1976, the family relocated to [Woodstock](/wiki/Woodstock,_Oxfordshire) near [Oxford](/wiki/Oxford), where Mendes's mother found work as a senior editor at [Oxford University Press](/wiki/Oxford_University_Press). Mendes was educated at [Magdalen College School](/wiki/Magdalen_College_School,_Oxford) where he met future theatre designer [Tom Piper](/wiki/Tom_Piper), who went on to work with Mendes on a [National Theatre](/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre) revival of [Harold Pinter](/wiki/Harold_Pinter)'s *[The Birthday Party](/wiki/The_Birthday_Party_(play))*.

Mendes had an early interest in cinema and applied to the [University of Warwick](/wiki/University_of_Warwick) (then the only university in the UK that offered an undergraduate film course), but was turned down. He was then accepted at the [University of Cambridge](/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), graduating at [Peterhouse](/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge) with [first-class honours](/wiki/First-class_honours) in English. Having developed a passion for theatre only in his late teens, Mendes became a member of the [Marlowe Society](/wiki/The_Marlowe_Society) at Cambridge and directed several plays. His first play was [David Halliwell](/wiki/David_Halliwell)'s *[Little Malcolm and His Struggle Against the Eunuchs](/wiki/Little_Malcolm_and_His_Struggle_Against_the_Eunuchs)*, and one of his later productions was *[Cyrano de Bergerac](/wiki/Cyrano_de_Bergerac_(play))* with [Tom Hollander](/wiki/Tom_Hollander) and [Jonathan Cake](/wiki/Jonathan_Cake) among the cast members. During his time at Cambridge, Mendes also became enthusiastic about cinema in earnest. He cited *[Paris, Texas](/wiki/Paris,_Texas_(film))*, *[Repo Man](/wiki/Repo_Man_(film))* and *[True Stories](/wiki/True_Stories_(film))* as three "seminal film moments" that influenced his stage and film career.

Mendes was noted as a "brilliant schoolboy cricketer" by *[Wisden Cricketers' Almanack](/wiki/Wisden_Cricketers%27_Almanack)*, scoring 1,153 runs at 46 and taking 83 wickets at under 16 for Magdalen College School in 1983 and 1984. He also played cricket for [Cambridge University Cricket Club](/wiki/Cambridge_University_Cricket_Club), and in 1997 played for [Shipton-under-Wychwood](/wiki/Shipton-under-Wychwood) in the final of the Village Cricket Cup, the only winner of the [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) to have played at [Lord's](/wiki/Lord%27s).

## Career

### Stage career

#### Early work

After graduating from Cambridge in 1987, Mendes was hired as assistant director at the [Chichester Festival Theatre](/wiki/Chichester_Festival_Theatre). In September 1987, Mendes made his professional directing debut with a double bill of two [Anton Chekhov](/wiki/Anton_Chekhov) plays, *[The Bear](/wiki/The_Bear_(play))* and *[The Proposal](/wiki/A_Marriage_Proposal)*. In 1989, he was appointed the inaugural director of the [Minerva Theatre](/wiki/Minerva_Theatre,_Chichester).

In 1989, following the abrupt departure of director [Robin Phillips](/wiki/Robin_Phillips), Mendes took over a production of [Dion Boucicault](/wiki/Dion_Boucicault)'s *[London Assurance](/wiki/London_Assurance)* at Chichester. Later that year, Mendes made his West End debut at the [Aldwych](/wiki/Aldwych_Theatre) with a production of Chekhov's *[The Cherry Orchard](/wiki/The_Cherry_Orchard)*, starring [Judi Dench](/wiki/Judi_Dench). *London Assurance* then transferred to the West End following a six-month run at Chichester, opening at the [Theatre Royal Haymarket](/wiki/Theatre_Royal_Haymarket). The successes of the plays established Mendes as a theatre director of national renown.

#### Donmar Warehouse (1990–2002)

In 1990, Mendes was appointed artistic director of the [Donmar Warehouse](/wiki/Donmar_Warehouse), a [Covent Garden](/wiki/Covent_Garden) studio space previously used by the [Royal Shakespeare Company](/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company). He spent two years overseeing the redesign of the theatre, which formally opened in 1992 with the British premiere of [Stephen Sondheim](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim)'s *[Assassins](/wiki/Assassins_(musical))*. Mendes's tenure at the Donmar saw its transformation into one of the most successful and fashionable playhouses in London.

In 1993, Mendes staged an acclaimed revival of [John Kander](/wiki/John_Kander) and [Fred Ebb](/wiki/Fred_Ebb)'s *[Cabaret](/wiki/Cabaret_(musical))* starring [Jane Horrocks](/wiki/Jane_Horrocks) as [Sally Bowles](/wiki/Sally_Bowles) and [Alan Cumming](/wiki/Alan_Cumming) as Emcee. The production was approached with a fresh concept, differing greatly from both the original 1966 production directed by [Harold Prince](/wiki/Harold_Prince) and the famed film version, directed by [Bob Fosse](/wiki/Bob_Fosse). This production opened at the Donmar and received four [Olivier Award](/wiki/Olivier_Award) nominations including Best Musical Revival, before transferring promptly to Broadway where it played for several years at the Kit Kat Club (i.e. the [Stephen Sondheim Theater](/wiki/Stephen_Sondheim_Theater)). The Broadway cast included Cumming once again as Emcee, with [Natasha Richardson](/wiki/Natasha_Richardson) as Sally, [Mary Louise Wilson](/wiki/Mary_Louise_Wilson) as Fraulein Schneider, [John Benjamin Hickey](/wiki/John_Benjamin_Hickey) as Cliff, and [Ron Rifkin](/wiki/Ron_Rifkin) as Herr Schultz. Cumming, Richardson, and Rifkin all won [Tony Awards](/wiki/Tony_Award) for their performances.

1994 saw Mendes stage a new production of [Lionel Bart](/wiki/Lionel_Bart)'s *[Oliver!](/wiki/Oliver!)*, produced by [Cameron Mackintosh](/wiki/Cameron_Mackintosh). Mendes, a longtime fan of the work, worked in close collaboration with Bart and other production team members, William David Brohn, Martin Koch and Anthony Ward, to create a fresh staging of the well-known classic. Bart added new musical material and Mendes updated the book slightly, while the orchestrations were radically rewritten to suit the show's cinematic feel. The cast included [Jonathan Pryce](/wiki/Jonathan_Pryce) (after much persuasion) as Fagin, [Sally Dexter](/wiki/Sally_Dexter) as Nancy, and [Miles Anderson](/wiki/Miles_Anderson) as Bill Sikes. Mendes, Pryce and Dexter received [Olivier Award](/wiki/Olivier_Award) nominations for their work on *Oliver!*.

Mendes also directed productions of [David Hare](/wiki/David_Hare_(playwright))'s *[The Blue Room](/wiki/The_Blue_Room_(play))* in 1998, starring [Nicole Kidman](/wiki/Nicole_Kidman); [Richard Greenberg](/wiki/Richard_Greenberg)'s *[Three Days of Rain](/wiki/Three_Days_of_Rain)* in 1999, with [Colin Firth](/wiki/Colin_Firth), [David Morrissey](/wiki/David_Morrissey) and [Elizabeth McGovern](/wiki/Elizabeth_McGovern); as well as his farewell duo in 2002, Chekhov's *[Uncle Vanya](/wiki/Uncle_Vanya)* and *[Twelfth Night](/wiki/Twelfth_Night)*, both headed by [Simon Russell Beale](/wiki/Simon_Russell_Beale), [Helen McCrory](/wiki/Helen_McCrory), [Emily Watson](/wiki/Emily_Watson) and [Mark Strong](/wiki/Mark_Strong). He stepped down as artistic director of the Donmar in December 2002 and was succeeded by [Michael Grandage](/wiki/Michael_Grandage).

#### After the Donmar (2002–present)

In 2003, Mendes directed a revival of the musical *[Gypsy](/wiki/Gypsy_(musical))*. Originally, he planned to stage this production in London's [West End](/wiki/West_End_theatre) with an eventual Broadway transfer, but when negotiations fell through, he brought it to New York. The cast included [Bernadette Peters](/wiki/Bernadette_Peters) as Rose, [Tammy Blanchard](/wiki/Tammy_Blanchard) as Louise and [John Dossett](/wiki/John_Dossett) as Herbie.

Mendes also directed the 2013 Olivier Award-nominated [stage adaptation](/wiki/Charlie_and_the_Chocolate_Factory_(musical)) of [Roald Dahl](/wiki/Roald_Dahl)'s *[Charlie and the Chocolate Factory](/wiki/Charlie_and_the_Chocolate_Factory)* which ran in London's West End until January 2017. It starred [Douglas Hodge](/wiki/Douglas_Hodge) as [Willy Wonka](/wiki/Willy_Wonka), followed by [Alex Jennings](/wiki/Alex_Jennings) and [Jonathan Slinger](/wiki/Jonathan_Slinger) who later took over the role.

In 2014, Mendes directed [Simon Russell Beale](/wiki/Simon_Russell_Beale) in *[King Lear](/wiki/King_Lear)* by [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare) at the [National Theatre, London](/wiki/Royal_National_Theatre). Mendes directed [Jez Butterworth](/wiki/Jez_Butterworth)'s *[The Ferryman](/wiki/The_Ferryman_(play))* for the [Royal Court Theatre](/wiki/Royal_Court_Theatre) in London in 2017, before transferring to the West End later that year and Broadway in 2018, for which he won an Olivier Award and Tony Award for Best Director.

In 2018, Mendes directed *[The Lehman Trilogy](/wiki/The_Lehman_Trilogy)* by Stefano Massini in an English adaptation by Ben Power for the National Theatre, London starring [Simon Russell Beale](/wiki/Simon_Russell_Beale), [Adam Godley](/wiki/Adam_Godley) and [Ben Miles](/wiki/Ben_Miles). In 2019 the play played a season at the [Park Avenue Armory](/wiki/Park_Avenue_Armory) in New York before returning for another London season in the West End. The play made its [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_(theatre)) transfer in 2020 briefly but was stalled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). The play resumed performances in 2021 and went on to receive eight [Tony Award](/wiki/Tony_Award) nominations winning five awards including [Best Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Play) and [Best Director of a Play](/wiki/Tony_Award_for_Best_Direction_of_a_Play).

### Film career

#### *American Beauty* to *Away We Go* (1999–2009)

In 1999, Mendes made his film directorial debut with *[American Beauty](/wiki/American_Beauty_(1999_film))*, starring [Kevin Spacey](/wiki/Kevin_Spacey). He had been approached by [Steven Spielberg](/wiki/Steven_Spielberg), who was impressed by his productions of *Oliver!* and *Cabaret*. The film grossed [$](/wiki/United_States_dollar)356.3 million worldwide. The film won the Golden Globe Award, the BAFTA Award and the Academy Award for Best Picture. Mendes won the [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director), [Directors Guild of America Award](/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_America_Award_for_Outstanding_Directing_%E2%80%93_Feature_Film), and the [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director), becoming the sixth director to earn the Academy Award for his feature film debut.

Mendes's second film, in 2002, was *[Road to Perdition](/wiki/Road_to_Perdition)*, which grossed US$181 million. As of October 2023, the aggregate review score on [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) is currently 81%; critics praised [Paul Newman](/wiki/Paul_Newman) for his performance. The film was nominated for six Academy Awards, including Best Supporting Actor for Newman; it won for Best Cinematography.

In 2003, Mendes established [Neal Street Productions](/wiki/Neal_Street_Productions), a film, television and theatre production company he would use to finance much of his later work. In 2005, Mendes directed the war film *[Jarhead](/wiki/Jarhead_(film))*, in association with his production company Neal Street Productions. The film received mixed reviews, with a Rotten Tomatoes rating of 61%, and a gross revenue of US$96.9 million worldwide. The film focused on the boredom and other psychological challenges of wartime.

In 2008, Mendes directed *[Revolutionary Road](/wiki/Revolutionary_Road_(film))*, starring his then-wife, [Kate Winslet](/wiki/Kate_Winslet), along with [Leonardo DiCaprio](/wiki/Leonardo_DiCaprio) and [Kathy Bates](/wiki/Kathy_Bates). In a January 2009 interview, Mendes commented, about directing his wife for the first time: "I would open my eyes in the morning and there Kate would be, going, 'Great! You're awake! Now let's talk about the second scene.'" Mendes's comedy-drama *[Away We Go](/wiki/Away_We_Go)* opened the 2009 [Edinburgh International Film Festival](/wiki/Edinburgh_International_Film_Festival). The film follows a couple ([John Krasinski](/wiki/John_Krasinski), [Maya Rudolph](/wiki/Maya_Rudolph)) searching North America for the perfect community in which to settle down and start a family. The film was well received by critics but performed poorly at the box office.

Mendes (right) collaborated with [Javier Bardem](/wiki/Javier_Bardem) for *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)*, November 2012

In 2010, Mendes co-produced a critically acclaimed documentary film *[Out of the Ashes](/wiki/Out_of_the_Ashes_(2010_film))* that deals with cricket in Afghanistan. On 5 January 2010, news broke that Mendes was employed to direct the 23rd [Eon Productions](/wiki/Eon_Productions) instalment of the [James Bond](/wiki/James_Bond_in_film) franchise. The film, *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)*, was subsequently released on 26 October 2012, to coincide with the 50th anniversary of the Bond films. Mendes had been employed as a consultant on the film when it was in pre-production, and had remained attached to the project during the financial troubles of MGM. The film was a major critical and commercial success, becoming the 14th film to gross over $1 billion worldwide.

In 2012, Mendes's Neal Street Productions produced the first series of the [BBC One](/wiki/BBC_One) drama series, *[Call the Midwife](/wiki/Call_the_Midwife)*, following it with a second season which began transmission in early 2013.

#### *Skyfall*, *Empire of Light*, and The Beatles biopic film series (2013–present)

After the success of *[Skyfall](/wiki/Skyfall)*, Mendes was asked if he was returning to direct the next Bond film. He responded, "I felt I put everything I possibly could into this film and it was the Bond film I wanted to make. And if I felt I could do the same again, then absolutely I would consider doing another one. But it is a big task and I wouldn't do it unless I knew I could." It was reported that one reason Mendes was reluctant to commit was that one proposal involved making two films back-to-back, based on an idea by *Skyfall* writer [John Logan](/wiki/John_Logan_(writer)), which would have resulted in Mendes and other creative personnel being tied up with filming for around four years. It was reported in February 2013 that this idea had since been shelved and that the next two films would be stand-alone. Mendes said in an interview with film magazine *[Empire](/wiki/Empire_(magazine))* in March 2013 that "it has been a very difficult decision not to accept Michael and Barbara's very generous offer to direct the next Bond movie." He cited, amongst other reasons, his commitments to the stage version of *[Charlie and the Chocolate Factory](/wiki/Charlie_and_the_Chocolate_Factory_(musical))* and *[King Lear](/wiki/King_Lear)*.

However, on 29 May 2013, it was reported that Mendes was back in negotiations with producers [Michael G. Wilson](/wiki/Michael_G._Wilson) and [Barbara Broccoli](/wiki/Barbara_Broccoli) to direct the next Bond film, going back on his previous comments. Wilson and Broccoli were willing to postpone production of the film to ensure Mendes's participation. On 11 July 2013, it was announced that Mendes would direct the 24th James Bond film, *[Spectre](/wiki/Spectre_(2015_film))*; it was released in October 2015. This made him the first filmmaker since [John Glen](/wiki/John_Glen_(director)) to direct two Bond films consecutively. In April 2016, Mendes was named as the president of the jury for the [73rd Venice International Film Festival](/wiki/73rd_Venice_International_Film_Festival).

Mendes's next film, war epic *[1917](/wiki/1917_(2019_film))*, was released by [Universal Pictures](/wiki/Universal_Pictures) on 25 December 2019 in the US and on 10 January 2020 in the UK. Based in part on an account told to Mendes by his paternal grandfather, [Alfred Mendes](/wiki/Alfred_Mendes), it chronicles the story of two young British soldiers in the spring of 1917 at a [critical point](/wiki/Nivelle_offensive) during [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I). Mendes went on to win the [Golden Globe Award for Best Director](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Director) for his achievement in directing; in his acceptance speech, he saluted his grandfather, as well as acknowledging the contribution to cinema of fellow nominee [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese), who was nominated for *[The Irishman](/wiki/The_Irishman)*.

On 25 January 2020, he won the [Directors Guild of America Award for Outstanding Directing – Feature Film](/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_America_Award_for_Outstanding_Directing_%E2%80%93_Feature_Film), following which he was installed by the press as the favourite to win the [Academy Award for Best Director](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Director) at the then approaching [92nd Academy Awards](/wiki/92nd_Academy_Awards). However that plaudit went instead to [Bong Joon-ho](/wiki/Bong_Joon-ho) for the [South Korean](/wiki/South_Korea) film *[Parasite](/wiki/Parasite_(2019_film))*. The two directors had shared the honours for directing at the [25th Critics' Choice Awards](/wiki/25th_Critics%27_Choice_Awards) several weeks prior.

In 2022, his next feature was the romantic drama *[Empire of Light](/wiki/Empire_of_Light)*, starring [Olivia Colman](/wiki/Olivia_Colman) and [Micheal Ward](/wiki/Micheal_Ward).

In February 2024, it was reported that Mendes would produce and direct four separate feature films about each member of [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles), to be released in April 2028. Collectively titled, *[The Beatles – A Four-Film Cinematic Event](/wiki/The_Beatles_%E2%80%93_A_Four-Film_Cinematic_Event)*, these will be the first Beatles biopics to have full cooperation from [Paul McCartney](/wiki/Paul_McCartney), [Ringo Starr](/wiki/Ringo_Starr), and the families of [John Lennon](/wiki/John_Lennon) and [George Harrison](/wiki/George_Harrison). The films will star [Paul Mescal](/wiki/Paul_Mescal) (McCartney), [Barry Keoghan](/wiki/Barry_Keoghan) (Starr), [Joseph Quinn](/wiki/Joseph_Quinn) (Harrison), and [Harris Dickinson](/wiki/Harris_Dickinson) (Lennon).

In April 2025, Mendes's first television [documentary film](/wiki/Documentary_film) *[What They Found](/wiki/What_They_Found)* was aired on [BBC Two](/wiki/BBC_Two) on the 80th anniversary of the liberation of the [Bergen-Belsen concentration camp](/wiki/Bergen-Belsen_concentration_camp). It was made in collaboration with the [Imperial War Museum](/wiki/Imperial_War_Museum), and depicts the discovery of the camp using original footage and testimonies of two British Army cameramen. Mendes said the documentary aimed to give "a unique perspective on the discovery of the horrors of Belsen and the reality of the [Holocaust](/wiki/Holocaust)." The producers said the film was intended as a historical record at a time when the awareness of the Holocaust had been declining among younger generations.

## Filmmaking style and techniques

### Influences

Mendes has listed [Stanley Kubrick](/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick), [the Coen brothers](/wiki/The_Coen_brothers), [Alfred Hitchcock](/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock), [Martin Scorsese](/wiki/Martin_Scorsese), [Wim Wenders](/wiki/Wim_Wenders), [Howard Davies](/wiki/Howard_Davies_(director)), [David Lynch](/wiki/David_Lynch), [Peter Brook](/wiki/Peter_Brook), [Ingmar Bergman](/wiki/Ingmar_Bergman), [Orson Welles](/wiki/Orson_Welles), and [Francis Ford Coppola](/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola) as amongst his cinematic and theatrical influences. He cited *[Paris, Texas](/wiki/Paris,_Texas_(film))*, *[Repo Man](/wiki/Repo_Man_(film))* and *[True Stories](/wiki/True_Stories_(film))* as three "seminal film moments" that influenced his stage and film career, and is also heavily influenced by British comedy acts such as [Monty Python](/wiki/Monty_Python), [The Goons](/wiki/The_Goons_(The_Goon_Show)), [Tommy Cooper](/wiki/Tommy_Cooper), and [Morecambe and Wise](/wiki/Morecambe_and_Wise).

### Style and themes

Much of his film directing techniques were informed by his background in theatre, which consisted of meticulous attention to detail, slow pacing, [pictorialist](/wiki/Pictorialism) composition, close collaborations with actors, use of tranquil atmosphere, periods of wordless [visual storytelling](/wiki/Visual_narrative), and long takes. Even though he was widely known for his use of long takes in *Spectre* and *1917*, he has used them since *American Beauty*. His first two films established a reputation for him of utilising a visual style that was considered formalist and classical, preferring to shoot and stage scenes with theatrical-style [mise-en-scene](/wiki/Mise-en-sc%C3%A8ne) and use of [chiaroscuro](/wiki/Chiaroscuro). His third film, *Jarhead*, which would mark the first of a long-time collaboration between him and cinematographer Roger Deakins, served as a stylistic departure from the former two films as it relied heavily on a grittier feel with improvised dialogue and looser handheld close-ups. Despite this, it shared a similar attention to detail and flawed characters.

Although he has tackled a variety of genres over the course of his career, Mendes has frequently explored themes of family and isolation in his work. The protagonists in his films are realistically flawed and struggle to fit in a world that is hostile towards them, a theme that was initially established in *American Beauty* and would be further explored in his subsequent films, including *Skyfall* and *Spectre*, and *1917*. The exploration of such themes are owed to his early childhood experiences, particularly with his parents, with the most direct being his grandfather and his mother serving as inspirations for the characters of Schofield and Hilary Small in *1917* and *Empire of Light*, respectively.

In an interview in 2014, Mendes explained his reasoning for exploring such themes: "If you are doing a play or a film, you have to have a secret way in if you are directing it. Sometimes it’s big things. *American Beauty*, for me, was about my adolescence. *Road to Perdition* was about my childhood. *Skyfall* was about middle-age and mortality."

## Personal life

Mendes has been married to British Classical musician [Alison Balsom](/wiki/Alison_Balsom) since 5 January 2017. Their daughter was born in September 2017. Mendes has a son, actor [Joe Anders](/wiki/Joe_Anders), born on 22 December 2003, from his marriage to actress [Kate Winslet](/wiki/Kate_Winslet), and a stepson born in March 2010 from Balsom's previous relationship with conductor [Edward Gardner](/wiki/Edward_Gardner_(conductor)). Mendes had a stepdaughter, [Mia Threapleton](/wiki/Mia_Threapleton), from Winslet's first marriage to filmmaker [Jim Threapleton](/wiki/Jim_Threapleton).

Mendes was appointed a [Knight Bachelor](/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in the [2020 New Years Honours List](/wiki/2020_New_Year_Honours) for services to drama.

In 2009, Mendes signed a petition in support of film director [Roman Polanski](/wiki/Roman_Polanski), calling for his release after Polanski was arrested in Switzerland in relation to his [1977 charge](/wiki/Roman_Polanski_sexual_abuse_case) for drugging and raping a 13-year-old girl.

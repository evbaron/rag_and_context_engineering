**Elon Reeve Musk** ([/ˈiːlɒn/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*EE-lon*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born June 28, 1971) is a businessman and entrepreneur known for [his leadership](/wiki/Business_career_of_Elon_Musk) of [Tesla](/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.), [SpaceX](/wiki/SpaceX), [X](/wiki/X_(social_network)), and [xAI](/wiki/XAI_(company)). Musk has been the [wealthiest person in the world](/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires) since 2025; as of May 2026, *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)* estimates [his net worth](/wiki/Wealth_of_Elon_Musk) to be US$788 billion.

Born into the wealthy [Musk family](/wiki/Musk_family) in [Pretoria](/wiki/Pretoria), South Africa, Musk emigrated in 1989 to Canada; he has Canadian citizenship since his mother was born there. He received bachelor's degrees in 1997 from the [University of Pennsylvania](/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) before moving to [California](/wiki/California) to pursue business ventures. In 1995, Musk co-founded the software company [Zip2](/wiki/Zip2). Following its sale in 1999, he co-founded [X.com](/wiki/X.com_(bank)), an online payment company that later merged to form [PayPal](/wiki/PayPal), which was acquired by [eBay](/wiki/EBay) in 2002. Musk also became an American citizen in 2002.

In 2002, Musk founded the [space technology](/wiki/Space_technology) company SpaceX, becoming its CEO and [chief engineer](/wiki/Chief_technology_officer); the company has since led innovations in [reusable rockets](/wiki/Reusable_rocket) and [commercial spaceflight](/wiki/Commercial_spaceflight). Musk joined the automaker Tesla as an early investor in 2004 and became its CEO and product architect in 2008; it has since become a leader in [electric vehicles](/wiki/Electric_vehicle). In 2015, he co-founded [OpenAI](/wiki/OpenAI) to advance [artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) (AI) research, but later left; growing discontent with the organization's direction and leadership in the [AI boom](/wiki/AI_boom) in the 2020s led him to establish xAI, which became a subsidiary of SpaceX in 2026. In 2022, [he acquired](/wiki/Acquisition_of_Twitter_by_Elon_Musk) the social network Twitter, [implementing significant changes](/wiki/Twitter_under_Elon_Musk), and rebranding it as X in 2023. His other businesses include the [neurotechnology](/wiki/Neurotechnology) company [Neuralink](/wiki/Neuralink), which he co-founded in 2016, and the tunneling company [the Boring Company](/wiki/The_Boring_Company), which he founded in 2017. In November 2025, a Tesla pay package worth $1 trillion for Musk was approved, which he is to receive over 10 years if he meets specific goals.

Musk is a supporter of global [far-right](/wiki/Far-right) politics, figures, and political parties. He was the largest donor in the [2024 U.S. presidential election](/wiki/2024_U.S._presidential_election), where he supported [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump). After Trump was inaugurated as president in January 2025, Musk served as [Senior Advisor to the President](/wiki/Senior_Advisor_to_the_President) and as the *[de facto](/wiki/De_facto)* head of the [Department of Government Efficiency](/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency) (DOGE). Shortly before [a public feud with Trump](/wiki/Trump%E2%80%93Musk_feud), Musk left the Trump administration in May 2025 and returned to managing his companies.

[Musk's political activities](/wiki/Political_activities_of_Elon_Musk), statements and [views](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk) have made him [a polarizing figure](/wiki/Public_image_of_Elon_Musk). He has been criticized for making unscientific and misleading statements, including spreading [COVID-19 misinformation](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk#COVID-19_pandemic), [promoting conspiracy theories](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk#Conspiracy_theories), and [affirming antisemitic](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk#Race_and_white_nationalism), racist, and [transphobic comments](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk#Transgender_issues). His acquisition of Twitter was controversial due to [a subsequent increase in hate speech](/wiki/Twitter_under_Elon_Musk#Increase_in_hate_speech) and the [spread of misinformation on the service](/wiki/Twitter_under_Elon_Musk#Handling_of_misinformation_and_disinformation), following his pledge to decrease [censorship](/wiki/Censorship). His role in the [second Trump administration](/wiki/Second_Trump_administration) attracted [public backlash](/wiki/Protests_against_Elon_Musk), particularly in [response to DOGE](/wiki/Response_to_the_Department_of_Government_Efficiency). The emails Musk sent to [Jeffrey Epstein](/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein) are included in the [Epstein files](/wiki/Epstein_files), which were published in 2025 and 2026 and became a topic of worldwide debate.

## Early life

See also: [Musk family](/wiki/Musk_family)

Elon Reeve Musk was born on June 28, 1971, in [Pretoria](/wiki/Pretoria), South Africa's administrative capital. He is of British and [Pennsylvania Dutch](/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch) ancestry. His mother, [Maye](/wiki/Maye_Musk) ([née](/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Haldeman), is a model and dietitian born in [Saskatchewan](/wiki/Saskatchewan), Canada, and raised in South Africa. Musk therefore holds both South African and Canadian citizenship from birth. His father, [Errol Musk](/wiki/Errol_Musk), is a South African [electromechanical engineer](/wiki/Electromechanics), pilot, sailor, consultant, [emerald](/wiki/Emerald) dealer, and property developer, who partly owned a rental lodge at [Timbavati Private Nature Reserve](/wiki/Timbavati_Private_Nature_Reserve).

His maternal grandfather, [Joshua N. Haldeman](/wiki/Joshua_N._Haldeman), who died in a plane crash when Elon was a toddler, was an American-born Canadian chiropractor, aviator and political activist in the [Technocracy movement](/wiki/Technocracy_movement) who moved to South Africa in 1950. Haldeman's anti-government, anti-democratic and conspiracist views, which included the promotion of far-right antisemitic conspiracy theories, "fanatical" support of [apartheid](/wiki/Apartheid), and according to Errol Musk, support of [Nazism](/wiki/Nazism), have been suggested as an [influence on Elon](/wiki/Joshua_N._Haldeman#Influence_on_Elon_Musk). During his childhood, Elon was told stories by his grandmother of Haldeman's travels and exploits, and Elon has suggested that all of Haldeman's descendants have his "desire for adventure, exploration – doing crazy things".

Elon has a younger brother, [Kimbal](/wiki/Kimbal_Musk), a younger sister, [Tosca](/wiki/Tosca_Musk), and four paternal half-siblings. Musk was [baptized](/wiki/Baptism) as a child in the [Anglican Church of Southern Africa](/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Southern_Africa). The Musk family was wealthy during Elon's youth. Despite both Elon and Errol previously stating that Errol was a part owner of a Zambian emerald mine, in 2023, Errol recounted that the deal he made was to receive "a portion of the emeralds produced at three small mines". Errol was elected to the Pretoria City Council as a representative of the anti-apartheid [Progressive Party](/wiki/Progressive_Party_(South_Africa)) and has said that his children shared their father's dislike of [apartheid](/wiki/Apartheid).

After his parents divorced in 1979, Elon, aged around 9, chose to live with his father because he had an *[Encyclopædia Britannica](/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica)* set and a computer. Elon later regretted his decision and became estranged from his father. Elon has recounted trips to a wilderness school that he described as a "paramilitary *[Lord of the Flies](/wiki/Lord_of_the_Flies)*" where "bullying was a virtue" and children were encouraged to fight over rations. In one incident, after an altercation with a fellow pupil, Elon was thrown down concrete steps and beaten severely, leading to him being hospitalized for his injuries. Elon described his father berating him after he was discharged from the hospital. Errol denied berating Elon and claimed, "The [other] boy had just lost his father to suicide, and Elon had called him stupid. Elon had a tendency to call people stupid. How could I possibly blame that child?"

Elon was an enthusiastic reader of books, and had attributed his success in part to having read *[The Lord of the Rings](/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings)*, the [*Foundation* series](/wiki/Foundation_(book_series)), and [*The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*](/wiki/The_Hitchhiker%27s_Guide_to_the_Galaxy_(novel)). At age ten, he developed an interest in computing and video games, teaching himself how to program from the [VIC-20](/wiki/VIC-20) user manual. At age twelve, Elon sold his [BASIC](/wiki/BASIC)-based game *Blastar* to *PC and Office Technology* magazine for approximately $500 (equivalent to $1,600 in 2025).

### Education

Musk graduated from [Pretoria Boys High School](/wiki/Pretoria_Boys_High_School) in South Africa.

Musk attended [Waterkloof House Preparatory School](/wiki/Waterkloof_House_Preparatory_School), [Bryanston High School](/wiki/Bryanston_High_School), and then [Pretoria Boys High School](/wiki/Pretoria_Boys_High_School), where he graduated. Musk was a decent but unexceptional student, earning a 61/100 in [Afrikaans](/wiki/Afrikaans) and a B on his senior math certification. Musk applied for a Canadian passport [through](/wiki/Jus_sanguinis) his Canadian-born mother to avoid South Africa's [mandatory military service](/wiki/Conscription_in_South_Africa), which would have forced him to participate in the apartheid regime, as well as to ease his path to immigration to the United States. While waiting for his application to be processed, he attended the [University of Pretoria](/wiki/University_of_Pretoria) for five months.

Musk arrived in Canada in June 1989, connected with a second cousin in [Saskatchewan](/wiki/Saskatchewan), and worked odd jobs, including at a farm and a lumber mill. In 1990, he entered [Queen's University](/wiki/Queen%27s_University_at_Kingston) in [Kingston, Ontario](/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario). Two years later, he transferred to the [University of Pennsylvania](/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he studied until 1995. Although Musk has said that he earned his degrees in 1995, the University of Pennsylvania did not award them until 1997 – a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in physics and a [Bachelor of Science](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in economics from the university's [Wharton School](/wiki/Wharton_School). He reportedly hosted large, ticketed house parties to help pay for tuition, and wrote a business plan for an electronic book-scanning service similar to [Google Books](/wiki/Google_Books).

In 1994, Musk held two internships in [Silicon Valley](/wiki/Silicon_Valley): one at energy storage startup Pinnacle Research Institute, which investigated electrolytic [supercapacitors](/wiki/Supercapacitor) for energy storage, and another at [Palo Alto](/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California)–based startup [Rocket Science Games](/wiki/Rocket_Science_Games). In 1995, he was accepted to a graduate program in [materials science](/wiki/Materials_science) at [Stanford University](/wiki/Stanford_University), but did not enroll. Musk decided to join the [Internet boom](/wiki/Dot-com_bubble) of the 1990s, applying for a job at [Netscape](/wiki/Netscape), to which he reportedly never received a response. *[The Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* reported that Musk lacked legal authorization to remain and work in the United States after failing to enroll at Stanford. In response, Musk said he was allowed to work at that time and that his student visa transitioned to an [H1-B](/wiki/H-1B_visa). According to numerous former business associates and shareholders, Musk said he was on a student visa at the time.

### Zip2

Main article: [Zip2](/wiki/Zip2)

In 1995, Musk, his brother [Kimbal](/wiki/Kimbal), and Greg Kouri founded the web software company [Zip2](/wiki/Zip2) with funding from a group of angel investors. They housed the venture at a small rented office in Palo Alto. Replying to *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*, Musk denounced the notion that they started their company with funds borrowed from Elon's father Errol Musk, but in a tweet, he recognized that his father contributed 10% of a later funding round. The company developed and marketed an Internet city guide for the newspaper publishing industry, with maps, directions, and yellow pages.

According to Musk, "The website was up during the day and I was coding it at night, seven days a week, all the time." To impress investors, Musk built a large plastic structure around a standard computer to create the impression that Zip2 was powered by a small supercomputer. The Musk brothers obtained contracts with *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* and the *[Chicago Tribune](/wiki/Chicago_Tribune),* and persuaded the board of directors to abandon plans for a merger with [CitySearch](/wiki/CitySearch). Musk's attempts to become CEO were thwarted by the board. [Compaq](/wiki/Compaq) acquired Zip2 for $307 million in cash in February 1999 (equivalent to $590,000,000 in 2025), and Musk received $22 million (equivalent to $43,000,000 in 2025) for his 7-percent share.

### X.com and PayPal

Main articles: [X.com (bank)](/wiki/X.com_(bank)), [PayPal](/wiki/PayPal), and [PayPal Mafia](/wiki/PayPal_Mafia)

In 1999, Musk co-founded [X.com](/wiki/X.com_(bank)), an online financial services and e-mail payment company. The startup was one of the first [federally insured](/wiki/Federal_Deposit_Insurance_Corporation) online banks, and, in its initial months of operation, over 200,000 customers joined the service. The company's investors regarded Musk as inexperienced and replaced him with [Intuit](/wiki/Intuit) CEO Bill Harris by the end of the year. The following year, X.com merged with online bank [Confinity](/wiki/Confinity) to avoid competition. Founded by [Max Levchin](/wiki/Max_Levchin) and [Peter Thiel](/wiki/Peter_Thiel), Confinity had its own money-transfer service, [PayPal](/wiki/PayPal), which was more popular than X.com's service.

Within the merged company, Musk returned as CEO. Musk's preference for [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft) software over [Unix](/wiki/Unix) created a rift in the company and caused Thiel to resign. Due to resulting technological issues and lack of a cohesive business model, the board ousted Musk and replaced him with Thiel in 2000. Under Thiel, the company focused on the PayPal service and was renamed PayPal in 2001. In 2002, PayPal was acquired by [eBay](/wiki/EBay) for $1.5 billion (equivalent to $2,700,000,000 in 2025) in stock, of which Musk—the largest shareholder with 11.72% of shares—received $175.8 million (equivalent to $320,000,000 in 2025). In 2017, Musk purchased the domain X.com from PayPal for an undisclosed amount, stating that it had sentimental value.

### SpaceX

Main article: [SpaceX](/wiki/SpaceX)

Musk explains [Starship](/wiki/SpaceX_Starship) capabilities to leaders of [North American Aerospace Defense Command](/wiki/NORAD), U.S. Northern Command, and [Air Force Space Command](/wiki/Space_Operations_Command) in 2019.

In 2001, Musk became involved with the nonprofit [Mars Society](/wiki/Mars_Society) and discussed funding plans to place a [growth-chamber for plants](/wiki/Space_farming) on [Mars](/wiki/Mars). Seeking a way to launch the greenhouse payloads into space, Musk made two unsuccessful trips to Moscow to purchase [intercontinental ballistic missiles](/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missiles) (ICBMs) from Russian companies [NPO Lavochkin](/wiki/NPO_Lavochkin) and [Kosmotras](/wiki/Kosmotras). Musk instead decided to start a company to build affordable rockets. With $100 million of his early fortune, (equivalent to $180,000,000 in 2025) Musk founded SpaceX in May 2002 and became the company's CEO and Chief Engineer.

SpaceX attempted its first launch of the [Falcon 1](/wiki/Falcon_1) rocket in 2006. Although the rocket failed to reach [Earth orbit](/wiki/Geocentric_orbit), it was awarded a [Commercial Orbital Transportation Services](/wiki/Commercial_Orbital_Transportation_Services) program contract from [NASA](/wiki/NASA), then led by [Mike Griffin](/wiki/Michael_D._Griffin). After two more failed attempts that nearly caused Musk to go bankrupt, SpaceX succeeded in launching the Falcon 1 into orbit in 2008. Later that year, SpaceX received a $1.6 billion NASA contract (equivalent to $2,400,000,000 in 2025) for [Falcon 9](/wiki/Falcon_9)-launched [Dragon spacecraft](/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon) flights to the [International Space Station](/wiki/International_Space_Station) (ISS), replacing the [Space Shuttle](/wiki/Space_Shuttle) after its 2011 [retirement](/wiki/Space_Shuttle_retirement). In 2012, the Dragon vehicle docked [with the ISS](/wiki/SpaceX_COTS_Demo_Flight_2), a first for a commercial spacecraft.

Working towards its goal of [reusable rockets](/wiki/SpaceX_reusable_launch_system_development_program), in 2015 SpaceX successfully landed the [first stage](/wiki/Multistage_rocket) of a Falcon 9 on a land platform. Later landings were achieved on [autonomous spaceport drone ships](/wiki/Autonomous_spaceport_drone_ship), an ocean-based recovery platform. In 2018, SpaceX launched the [Falcon Heavy](/wiki/Falcon_Heavy); the [inaugural mission](/wiki/Falcon_Heavy_test_flight) carried [Musk's personal Tesla Roadster](/wiki/Elon_Musk%27s_Tesla_Roadster) as a [dummy payload](/wiki/Boilerplate_(spaceflight)). Since 2019, SpaceX has been developing [Starship](/wiki/SpaceX_Starship), a reusable, [super heavy-lift launch vehicle](/wiki/Super_heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) intended to replace the Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy. In 2020, SpaceX launched its first crewed flight, the [Demo-2](/wiki/Crew_Dragon_Demo-2), becoming the first private company to place astronauts into orbit and dock a crewed spacecraft with the ISS. In 2024, NASA awarded SpaceX an $843 million (equivalent to $865,000,000 in 2025) contract to build a spacecraft that NASA will use to [deorbit](/wiki/Atmospheric_entry) the ISS at the end of its lifespan.

#### Starlink

Main article: [Starlink](/wiki/Starlink)

See also: [Starlink in the Russo-Ukrainian War](/wiki/Starlink_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War)

50 Starlink satellites shortly before deployment to low Earth orbit, 2019

In 2015, SpaceX began development of the [Starlink](/wiki/Starlink) constellation of [low Earth orbit](/wiki/Low_Earth_orbit) satellites to provide [satellite Internet access](/wiki/Satellite_Internet_access). After the launch of prototype satellites in 2018, the first large constellation was deployed in May 2019. As of May 2025, over 7,600 Starlink satellites are operational, comprising 65% of all operational Earth satellites. The total cost of the decade-long project to design, build, and deploy the constellation was estimated by SpaceX in 2020 to be $10 billion (equivalent to $12,000,000,000 in 2025).

During the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Musk provided free [Starlink service to Ukraine](/wiki/Starlink_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War), permitting Internet access and communication at a yearly cost to SpaceX of $400 million (equivalent to $440,000,000 in 2025). However, Musk refused to block [Russian state media](/wiki/Mass_media_in_Russia) on Starlink. In 2023, Musk denied Ukraine's request to activate Starlink over [Crimea](/wiki/Crimea) to aid an attack against the Russian navy, citing fears of a nuclear response.

### Tesla

Main article: [Tesla, Inc.](/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.)

Musk next to a [Tesla Model S](/wiki/Tesla_Model_S), 2011

[Tesla, Inc.](/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.), originally Tesla Motors, was incorporated in July 2003 by [Martin Eberhard](/wiki/Martin_Eberhard) and [Marc Tarpenning](/wiki/Marc_Tarpenning). Both men played active roles in the company's early development prior to Musk's involvement. Musk led the [Series A round](/wiki/Series_A_round) of investment in February 2004; he invested $6.35 million (equivalent to $11,000,000 in 2025), became the majority shareholder, and joined Tesla's board of directors as chairman. Musk took an active role within the company and oversaw [Roadster](/wiki/Tesla_Roadster_(first_generation)) product design, but was not deeply involved in day-to-day business operations. Following a series of escalating conflicts in 2007 and the [2008 financial crisis](/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), Eberhard was ousted from the firm. Musk assumed leadership of the company as CEO and product architect in 2008. A 2009 lawsuit settlement with Eberhard designated Musk as a Tesla co-founder, along with Tarpenning and two others.

Tesla began delivery of the Roadster, an electric sports car, in 2008. With sales of about 2,500 vehicles, it was the first [mass production](/wiki/Mass_production) all-electric car to use [lithium-ion battery](/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) cells. Under Musk, Tesla has since launched several well-selling electric vehicles, including the four-door sedan [Model S](/wiki/Tesla_Model_S) (2012), the [crossover](/wiki/Crossover_(automobile)) [Model X](/wiki/Tesla_Model_X) (2015), the mass-market sedan [Model 3](/wiki/Tesla_Model_3) (2017), the crossover [Model Y](/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y) (2020), and the pickup truck [Cybertruck](/wiki/Tesla_Cybertruck) (2023). The company has also constructed multiple lithium-ion battery and electric vehicle factories, called [Gigafactories](/wiki/Gigafactory). In 2026, [Jon McNeill](/wiki/Jon_McNeill), who served as president of Tesla from 2015 to 2018 and oversaw a period of revenue growth from $2 billion to $20 billion, published *The Algorithm*, a book detailing the five-step operational framework used at Tesla and SpaceX.

In May 2020, Musk resigned as chairman of the board as part of the settlement of a [lawsuit from the SEC](#Legal_affairs) over him tweeting that funding had been "secured" for potentially taking Tesla private.

Since its [initial public offering](/wiki/Initial_public_offering) in 2010, Tesla stock has risen significantly; it became the most valuable carmaker in summer 2020, and it entered the [S&P 500](/wiki/S%26P_500) later that year. In October 2021, it reached a market capitalization of $1 trillion (equivalent to $1,200,000,000,000 in 2025), the sixth company in U.S. history to do so.

#### SolarCity and Tesla Energy

Main articles: [SolarCity](/wiki/SolarCity) and [Tesla Energy](/wiki/Tesla_Energy)

Musk provided the initial concept and financial capital for [SolarCity](/wiki/SolarCity), which his cousins [Lyndon](/wiki/Lyndon_Rive) and Peter Rive founded in 2006. By 2013, SolarCity was the second largest provider of solar power systems in the United States. In 2014, Musk promoted the idea of SolarCity building an advanced production facility in [Buffalo](/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), New York, triple the size of the largest solar plant in the United States. Construction of [the factory](/wiki/Gigafactory_New_York) started in 2014 and was completed in 2017. It operated as a joint venture with [Panasonic](/wiki/Panasonic) until early 2020.

Tesla acquired SolarCity for $2 billion in 2016 (equivalent to $2,700,000,000 in 2025) and merged it with its battery unit to create [Tesla Energy](/wiki/Tesla_Energy). The deal's announcement resulted in a more than 10% drop in Tesla's stock price; at the time, SolarCity was facing liquidity issues. Multiple shareholder groups filed a lawsuit against Musk and Tesla's directors, stating that the purchase of SolarCity was done solely to benefit Musk and came at the expense of Tesla and its shareholders. Tesla directors settled the lawsuit in January 2020, leaving Musk the sole remaining defendant. Two years later, the court ruled in Musk's favor.

### Neuralink

Main article: [Neuralink](/wiki/Neuralink)

Musk discussing a [Neuralink](/wiki/Neuralink) device during a live demonstration in 2020

In 2016, Musk co-founded [Neuralink](/wiki/Neuralink), a [neurotechnology](/wiki/Neurotechnology) startup, with an investment of $100 million. Neuralink aims to integrate the human brain with [artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) (AI) by creating devices that are embedded in the brain. Such technology could enhance memory or allow the devices to communicate with software. The company also hopes to develop devices to treat neurological conditions like [spinal cord injuries](/wiki/Spinal_cord_injury). In 2022, Neuralink announced that clinical trials would begin by the end of the year. In September 2023, the [Food and Drug Administration](/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration) approved Neuralink to initiate six-year human trials.

Neuralink has conducted [animal testing](/wiki/Animal_testing) on [macaques](/wiki/Macaque) at the [University of California, Davis](/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis). In 2021, the company released a video in which a macaque played the video game *[Pong](/wiki/Pong)* via a Neuralink implant. The company's animal trials—which have caused the deaths of some monkeys—have led to claims of [animal cruelty](/wiki/Cruelty_to_animals). The [Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine](/wiki/Physicians_Committee_for_Responsible_Medicine) has alleged that Neuralink violated the [Animal Welfare Act](/wiki/Animal_Welfare_Act_of_1966). Employees have complained that pressure from Musk to accelerate development has led to botched experiments and unnecessary animal deaths. In 2022, a federal probe was launched into possible animal welfare violations by Neuralink.

### The Boring Company

Main article: [The Boring Company](/wiki/The_Boring_Company)

Musk during the 2018 inauguration of the [Boring test tunnel](/wiki/Boring_test_tunnel) in [Hawthorne, California](/wiki/Hawthorne,_California)

In 2017, Musk founded the Boring Company to construct tunnels; he also revealed plans for specialized, underground, high-occupancy vehicles that could travel up to 150 miles per hour (240 km/h) and thus circumvent above-ground traffic in major cities. Early in 2017, the company began discussions with regulatory bodies and initiated construction of a 30-foot (9.1 m) wide, 50-foot (15 m) long, and 15-foot (4.6 m) deep "test trench" on the premises of SpaceX's offices, as that required no permits.

The [Los Angeles tunnel](/wiki/Boring_test_tunnel), less than two miles (3.2 km) in length, debuted to journalists in 2018. It used Tesla Model Xs and was reported to be a rough ride while traveling at suboptimal speeds. Two tunnel projects announced in 2018, in Chicago and West Los Angeles, have been canceled. A tunnel beneath the [Las Vegas Convention Center](/wiki/Las_Vegas_Convention_Center) was completed in early 2021. Local officials have approved further expansions of the tunnel system.

Having backtracked on his initial decision, Musk bought the company on October 27, 2022.

Immediately after [the acquisition](/wiki/Acquisition_of_Twitter_by_Elon_Musk), Musk fired several top Twitter executives including CEO [Parag Agrawal](/wiki/Parag_Agrawal); Musk became the CEO instead. [Under Elon Musk](/wiki/Twitter_under_Elon_Musk), Twitter instituted monthly subscriptions for a "[blue check](/wiki/Twitter_verification)", and laid off a significant portion of the company's staff. Musk lessened content moderation and hate speech also increased on the platform after his takeover. In late 2022, Musk released [internal documents](/wiki/Twitter_Files) relating to Twitter's moderation of [Hunter Biden's laptop controversy](/wiki/Hunter_Biden_laptop_controversy) in the lead-up to the [2020 presidential election](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election). Musk also promised to step down as CEO after a Twitter poll, and five months later, Musk stepped down as CEO and transitioned his role to [executive chairman](/wiki/Chair_(officer)) and [chief technology officer](/wiki/Chief_technology_officer) (CTO).

Despite Musk stepping down as CEO, X continues to struggle with challenges such as viral [misinformation](/wiki/Misinformation), [hate speech](/wiki/Hate_speech), and [antisemitism](/wiki/Antisemitism) controversies. Musk has been accused of [trying to silence some of his critics](/wiki/Twitter_under_Elon_Musk#Lawsuits) such as [Twitch](/wiki/Twitch_(service)) streamer [Asmongold](/wiki/Asmongold), who criticized him during one of his streams. Musk has been accused of removing their accounts' [blue checkmarks](/wiki/Twitter_verification), which hinders visibility and is considered a form of [shadow banning](/wiki/Shadow_banning), or suspending their accounts without justification.

### OpenAI and xAI

Main articles: [OpenAI](/wiki/OpenAI) and [xAI (company)](/wiki/XAI_(company))

In December 2015, Musk co-founded [OpenAI](/wiki/OpenAI), a [not-for-profit](/wiki/Nonprofit_corporation) [artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) (AI) research company aiming to develop [artificial general intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence), intended to be safe and beneficial to humanity. Musk pledged $1 billion of funding to the company, but only donated $50 million. In 2018, Musk left the OpenAI board. Since 2018, OpenAI has made significant advances in machine learning. In July 2023, Musk launched the artificial intelligence company [xAI](/wiki/XAI_(company)), which aims to develop a [generative AI](/wiki/Generative_artificial_intelligence) program that competes with existing offerings like OpenAI's [ChatGPT](/wiki/ChatGPT). Musk obtained funding from investors in SpaceX and Tesla, and xAI hired engineers from Google and OpenAI.

### Grokipedia

Main article: [Grokipedia](/wiki/Grokipedia)

In October 2025, Musk's company xAI launched Grokipedia, an AI-generated online encyclopedia that he promoted as an alternative to Wikipedia. Articles on Grokipedia are generated and reviewed by xAI's [Grok](/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)) chatbot.

Media coverage and academic analysis described Grokipedia as frequently reusing Wikipedia content but framing contested political and social topics in line with Musk's own views and right-wing narratives. A study by Cornell University researchers and NBC News stated that Grokipedia cites sources that are blacklisted or considered "generally unreliable" on Wikipedia, for example, the conspiracy site [Infowars](/wiki/Infowars) and the neo-Nazi forum [Stormfront](/wiki/Stormfront_(website)). *[Wired](/wiki/Wired_(magazine))*, *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* and *[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* criticized Grokipedia for factual errors and for presenting Musk himself in unusually positive terms while downplaying controversies.

## Politics

Main article: [Political activities of Elon Musk](/wiki/Political_activities_of_Elon_Musk)

See also: [Protests against Elon Musk](/wiki/Protests_against_Elon_Musk)

Musk with president [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump) in May 2025

Musk is an outlier among business leaders who typically avoid partisan political advocacy. Musk was a registered [independent voter](/wiki/Independent_voter) when he lived in California. Historically, he has donated to both [Democrats](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) and [Republicans](/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)), many of whom serve in states in which he has a vested interest. Musk supported Democrat [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) in [2008](/wiki/2008_US_presidential_election) and [2012](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election), Democrat [Hillary Clinton](/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in [2016](/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), and Democrat [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden) in [2020](/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election). In the [2020 Democratic Party presidential primaries](/wiki/2020_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries), Musk endorsed candidate [Andrew Yang](/wiki/Andrew_Yang) and expressed support for Yang's proposed [universal basic income](/wiki/Universal_basic_income), and endorsed [Kanye West](/wiki/Kanye_West)'s [2020 presidential campaign](/wiki/Kanye_West_2020_presidential_campaign).

On August 5, 2021, Biden signed an [Executive Order](/wiki/Executive_order) targeting that half of cars sold in 2030 should be electric. [General Motors](/wiki/General_Motors) CEO [Mary Barra](/wiki/Mary_Barra) was in attendance. Musk commented, "seems odd that Tesla wasn't invited." Barra said in 2024 Musk never forgave not being invited. In 2025, former vice president [Kamala Harris](/wiki/Kamala_Harris) said that she believed that not inviting Musk was a "big mistake." In September 2021, Musk criticized Biden as "not the friendliest administration." In December 2021, Musk publicly expressed opposition to the [Build Back Better Act](/wiki/Build_Back_Better_Act).

Since 2022, his political contributions have mostly supported Republicans, with his first vote for a Republican going to [Mayra Flores](/wiki/Mayra_Flores) in the [2022 Texas's 34th congressional district special election](/wiki/2022_Texas%27s_34th_congressional_district_special_election). In 2022, Musk gave over $50 million to [Citizens for Sanity](/wiki/Citizens_for_Sanity), a conservative [political action committee](/wiki/Political_action_committee). In 2023, he supported Republican [Ron DeSantis](/wiki/Ron_DeSantis) for the [2024 U.S. presidential election](/wiki/2024_US_presidential_election), giving $10 million to his campaign, and hosted DeSantis's campaign announcement on a [Twitter Spaces](/wiki/Twitter_Spaces) event. From June 2023 to January 2024, Musk hosted a bipartisan set of X Spaces with Republican and Democratic candidates, including [Robert F. Kennedy Jr.](/wiki/Robert_F._Kennedy_Jr.), [Vivek Ramaswamy](/wiki/Vivek_Ramaswamy), and [Dean Phillips](/wiki/Dean_Phillips).

In 2024, he started supporting international [far-right](/wiki/Far-right) political parties, activists, and causes, and has shared far-right misinformation and numerous [conspiracy theories](/wiki/Conspiracy_theories). Since 2024, [his views](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk) have been generally described as right-wing.

### Salute at Trump's second inauguration

Main article: [Elon Musk salute controversy](/wiki/Elon_Musk_salute_controversy)

Musk giving a gesture at the [second inauguration of Donald Trump](/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) before saying "My heart goes out to you. It is thanks to you that the future of civilization is assured."

During his speech after the [second inauguration of Donald Trump](/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump), Musk twice made a gesture interpreted by many as a [Nazi](/wiki/Nazi_salute) or a fascist [Roman salute](/wiki/Roman_salute). He thumped his right hand over his heart, fingers spread wide, and then extended his right arm out, emphatically, at an upward angle, palm down and fingers together. He then repeated the gesture to the crowd behind him. As he finished the gestures, he said to the crowd, "My heart goes out to you. It is thanks to you that the future of civilization is assured."

It was widely condemned as an intentional Nazi salute in Germany, where [making such gestures is illegal](/wiki/Strafgesetzbuch_section_86a). The [Anti-Defamation League](/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League) said it was not a Nazi salute, but other Jewish organizations disagreed and condemned the salute. American public opinion was divided on partisan lines as to whether it was a fascist salute. Musk dismissed the accusations of [Nazi](/wiki/Nazi) sympathies, deriding them as "dirty tricks" and a "tired" attack. [Neo-Nazi](/wiki/Neo-Nazi) and [white supremacist](/wiki/White_supremacist) groups celebrated it as a Nazi salute. Multiple European political parties demanded that Musk be banned from entering their countries.

### Department of Government Efficiency

Main article: [Department of Government Efficiency](/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency)

Elon Musk wielding a chainsaw at the [Conservative Political Action Conference](/wiki/Conservative_Political_Action_Conference) (CPAC) in 2025, imitating a publicity stunt used by [Javier Milei](/wiki/Javier_Milei) symbolic of efficiency, [federal mass layoffs](/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_mass_layoffs) and [tax cutting](/wiki/Tax_cut)

The concept of DOGE emerged in a discussion between Musk and [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump), and in August 2024, Trump committed to giving Musk an advisory role, with Musk accepting the offer. In November and December 2024, Musk suggested that the organization could help to cut the [U.S. federal budget](/wiki/United_States_federal_budget), consolidate the number of federal agencies, and eliminate the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/wiki/Consumer_Financial_Protection_Bureau), and that its final stage would be "deleting itself".

In January 2025, the organization was created by [executive order](/wiki/Executive_order), and Musk was designated a "[special government employee](/wiki/Special_government_employee)". Musk led the organization and was a [senior advisor to the president](/wiki/Senior_Advisor_to_the_President_of_the_United_States), although his [official role](/wiki/Political_activities_of_Elon_Musk) is not clear. In sworn statement during a lawsuit, the director of the White House [Office of Administration](/wiki/Office_of_Administration) stated that Musk "is not an employee of the U.S. DOGE Service or U.S. DOGE Service Temporary Organization", "is not the U.S. DOGE Service administrator", and has "no actual or formal authority to make government decisions himself". Trump said two days later that he had put Musk in charge of DOGE. A federal judge has ruled that Musk acted as the *de facto* leader of DOGE.

Musk's role in the [second Trump administration](/wiki/Second_Trump_administration), particularly in [response to DOGE](/wiki/Response_to_the_Department_of_Government_Efficiency), has [attracted public backlash](/wiki/Protests_against_Elon_Musk). He was criticized for his treatment of federal government employees, including his influence over the [mass layoffs of the federal workforce](/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_mass_layoffs). He has prioritized secrecy within the organization and has accused others of violating privacy laws. A Senate report alleged that Musk could avoid up to $2 billion in legal liability as a result of DOGE's actions. In May 2025, [Bill Gates](/wiki/Bill_Gates) accused Musk of "killing the world's poorest children" through his cuts to [USAID](/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development). Modeling by [Boston University](/wiki/Boston_University) estimated the cuts resulted in 400,000 children and 200,000 adults deaths by November. Musk departed on May 28, 2025, when the special government employee's 130 day deadline expired.

## Legal affairs

Main article: [Legal affairs of Elon Musk](/wiki/Legal_affairs_of_Elon_Musk)

Further information: [List of lawsuits involving Tesla, Inc.](/wiki/List_of_lawsuits_involving_Tesla,_Inc.) and [Criticism of Tesla, Inc.](/wiki/Criticism_of_Tesla,_Inc.)

In 2018, Musk was [sued](/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.#Lawsuits_and_controversies) by the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) (SEC) for a [tweet](/wiki/Tweet_(social_media)) stating that funding had been secured for potentially taking Tesla private. The securities fraud lawsuit characterized the tweet as false, misleading, and damaging to investors, and sought to bar Musk from serving as CEO of [publicly traded companies](/wiki/Public_company). Two days later, Musk settled with the SEC, without admitting or denying the SEC's allegations. As a result, Musk and Tesla were fined $20 million each, and Musk was forced to step down for three years as Tesla chairman but was able to remain as CEO. Shareholders filed a lawsuit over the tweet, and in February 2023, a jury found Musk and Tesla not liable. Musk has stated in interviews that he does not regret posting the tweet that triggered the SEC investigation.

In 2019, Musk stated in a tweet that Tesla would build half a million cars that year. The SEC reacted by asking a court to hold him [in contempt](/wiki/Contempt_of_court) for violating the terms of the 2018 settlement agreement. A joint agreement between Musk and the SEC eventually clarified the previous agreement details, including a list of topics about which Musk needed preclearance. In 2020, a judge blocked a lawsuit that claimed a tweet by Musk regarding Tesla stock price ("too high [imo](/wiki/List_of_acronyms:_I)") violated the agreement. [Freedom of Information Act](/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_(United_States)) (FOIA)-released records showed that the SEC concluded Musk had subsequently violated the agreement twice by tweeting regarding "[Tesla's solar roof](/wiki/Tesla_Energy) production volumes and its stock price".

In October 2023, the SEC sued Musk over his refusal to testify a third time in an investigation into whether he violated federal law by purchasing Twitter stock in 2022. In February 2024, Judge Laurel Beeler ruled that Musk must testify again. In January 2025, the SEC filed a lawsuit against Musk for securities violations related to his purchase of Twitter.

In January 2024, Delaware judge [Kathaleen McCormick](/wiki/Kathaleen_McCormick) ruled in a 2018 lawsuit that Musk's $55 billion pay package from Tesla be rescinded. McCormick called the compensation granted by the company's board "an unfathomable sum" that was unfair to shareholders. The [Delaware Supreme Court](/wiki/Delaware_Supreme_Court) overturned McCormick's decision in December 2025, restoring Musk's compensation package and awarding $1 in [nominal damages](/wiki/Nominal_damages).

On March 20, 2026, a jury in [San Francisco, California](/wiki/San_Francisco,_California), found Musk liable for misleading investors during his [acquisition of Twitter](/wiki/Acquisition_of_Twitter_by_Elon_Musk), though they absolved him of some fraud allegations, finding he did not "scheme" to defraud investors. Specifically, Musk was found to have misled shareholders in two tweets, but not in a podcast, as the views he expressed in that were deemed by the jury to be an opinion. Damages have yet to be calculated, but plaintiffs' lawyers estimated them to be $2.5 billion.

[Jury selection](/wiki/Jury_selection) for Musk's [lawsuit](/wiki/Musk_v._Altman) against OpenAI and its current executives, including [Sam Altman](/wiki/Sam_Altman) and [Greg Brockman](/wiki/Greg_Brockman), began on April 27, 2026. On April 28, 2026, trial testimony was by now underway, with Elon Musk beginning his testimony against Altman and OpenAI. On April 30, 2026 Musk would enter his third day of testimony.

## Personal life

Official [Royal Society](/wiki/Royal_Society) portrait, 2018

Musk became a U.S. citizen in 2002. From the early 2000s until late 2020, Musk resided in California, where both Tesla and SpaceX were founded. He then relocated to [Cameron County, Texas](/wiki/Cameron_County,_Texas), saying that California had become "complacent" about its economic success.

While hosting *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* in 2021, Musk stated that he has [Asperger syndrome](/wiki/Asperger_syndrome) (an outdated term for a mild strain of [autism spectrum disorder](/wiki/Autism)), although he has never been formally diagnosed. When asked about his experience growing up with Asperger's syndrome in a [TED conference](/wiki/TED_(conference)) in Vancouver in 2022, Musk stated that "the social cues were not intuitive ... I would just tend to take things very literally ... but then that turned out to be wrong — [people were not] simply saying exactly what they mean, there's all sorts of other things that are meant, and [it] took me a while to figure that out." Musk suffers from back pain and has undergone several spine-related surgeries, including a [disc](/wiki/Intervertebral_disc) replacement. In 2000, he contracted a severe case of [malaria](/wiki/Malaria) while on vacation in South Africa.

Musk has stated he uses doctor-prescribed [ketamine](/wiki/Ketamine) for occasional depression and that he doses "a small amount once every other week or something like that"; since January 2024, some media outlets have reported that he takes ketamine, [marijuana](/wiki/Marijuana), [LSD](/wiki/LSD), [ecstasy](/wiki/Ecstasy_(drug)), [mushrooms](/wiki/Hallucinogenic_mushrooms), [cocaine](/wiki/Cocaine) and other drugs. Musk at first refused to comment on his alleged drug use, before responding that he had not [tested](/wiki/Drug_test) positive for drugs, and that if drugs somehow improved his productivity, "I would definitely take them!". *[The New York Times'](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* investigations revealed Musk's overuse of ketamine and numerous other drugs, as well as strained family relationships and concerns from close associates who have become troubled by his public behavior as he became more involved in political activities and government work. According to *The Washington Post*, President Trump described Musk as "a big-time drug addict".

Through his own label Emo G Records, Musk released a [rap](/wiki/Rap) track, "RIP [Harambe](/wiki/Harambe)", on [SoundCloud](/wiki/SoundCloud) in March 2019. The following year, he released an [EDM](/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) track, "Don't Doubt Ur Vibe", featuring his own lyrics and vocals.

Musk plays video games, which he stated has a "'restoring effect' that helps his 'mental calibration'". Some games he plays include [*Quake*](/wiki/Quake_(series)), *[Diablo IV](/wiki/Diablo_IV)*, *[Elden Ring](/wiki/Elden_Ring)*, and [*Polytopia*](/wiki/The_Battle_of_Polytopia). Musk once claimed to be one of the world's top video game players but has since admitted to "[account boosting](/wiki/Boosting_(video_games))", or cheating by hiring outside services to achieve top player rankings. Musk has justified the boosting by claiming that all top accounts do it so he has to as well to remain competitive. In 2024 and 2025, Musk criticized the video game *[Assassin's Creed Shadows](/wiki/Assassin%27s_Creed_Shadows)* and its creator [Ubisoft](/wiki/Ubisoft) for "woke" content. Musk posted to X that "DEI kills art" and specified the inclusion of the historical figure [Yasuke](/wiki/Yasuke) in the *Assassin's Creed* game as offensive; he also called the game "terrible". Ubisoft responded by saying that Musk's comments were "just feeding hatred" and that they were focused on producing a game not pushing politics.

### Relationships and children

Further information: [Musk family](/wiki/Musk_family)

Musk with his son, X Æ A-Xii, in the [Oval Office](/wiki/Oval_Office) on February 11, 2025

Musk has fathered at least 14 children, one of whom died as an infant. *The Wall Street Journal* reported in 2025 that sources close to Musk suggest that the "true number of Musk's children is much higher than publicly known". He had six children with his first wife, Canadian author [Justine Wilson](/wiki/Justine_Wilson), whom he met while attending Queen's University in Ontario, Canada; they married in 2000. In 2002, their first child, Nevada Alexander Musk, died of [sudden infant death syndrome](/wiki/Sudden_infant_death_syndrome) at the age of 10 days. After his death, the couple used [in vitro fertilization](/wiki/In_vitro_fertilisation) (IVF) to continue their family; they had twins in 2004, followed by triplets in 2006. The couple divorced in 2008 and have [shared custody](/wiki/Joint_custody_(United_States)) of their children. The elder twin he had with Wilson [came out](/wiki/Gender_identity) as a [trans woman](/wiki/Trans_woman) and, in 2022, officially [changed her name](/wiki/Name_change) to [Vivian Jenna Wilson](/wiki/Vivian_Wilson), adopting her mother's surname because she no longer wished to be associated with Musk.

Musk began dating English actress [Talulah Riley](/wiki/Talulah_Riley) in 2008. They married two years later at [Dornoch Cathedral](/wiki/Dornoch_Cathedral) in Scotland. In 2012, the couple divorced, then remarried the following year. After briefly filing for divorce in 2014, Musk finalized a second divorce from Riley in 2016. Musk then dated the American actress [Amber Heard](/wiki/Amber_Heard) for several months in 2017; he had reportedly been "pursuing" her since 2012.

In 2018, Musk and Canadian musician [Grimes](/wiki/Grimes) confirmed they were dating. Grimes and Musk have three children, born in 2020, 2021, and 2022. Musk and Grimes originally gave their eldest child the name "X [Æ](/wiki/%C3%86) A-12", which would have violated California regulations as it contained characters that are not in the modern English alphabet; the names registered on the birth certificate are "X" as a first name, "Æ A-[Xii](/wiki/Roman_numerals)" as a middle name, and "Musk" as a last name. They received criticism for choosing a name perceived to be impractical and difficult to pronounce; Musk has said the intended pronunciation is "X Ash A Twelve". Their second child was born via [surrogacy](/wiki/Surrogacy). Despite the pregnancy, Musk confirmed reports that the couple were "semi-separated" in September 2021; in an interview with [*Time*](/wiki/Time_(magazine)) in December 2021, he said he was single. In October 2023, Grimes sued Musk over parental rights and custody of X Æ A-Xii. Elon Musk has taken X Æ A-Xii to multiple official events in Washington, D.C. during [Trump's second term in office](/wiki/Second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump).

Also in July 2022, *The Wall Street Journal* reported that Musk allegedly had an affair with [Nicole Shanahan](/wiki/Nicole_Shanahan), the wife of Google co-founder [Sergey Brin](/wiki/Sergey_Brin), in 2021, leading to their divorce the following year. Musk denied the report. Musk also had a relationship with Australian actress [Natasha Bassett](/wiki/Natasha_Bassett), who has been described as "an occasional girlfriend". In October 2024, *The New York Times* reported Musk bought a Texas compound for his children and their mothers, though Musk denied having done so.

Musk also has four children with [Shivon Zilis](/wiki/Shivon_Zilis), director of operations and special projects at [Neuralink](/wiki/Neuralink): twins born via [IVF](/wiki/In_vitro_fertilisation) in 2021, a child born in 2024 via surrogacy and a child born in 2025.

On February 14, 2025, [Ashley St. Clair](/wiki/Ashley_St._Clair), an influencer and author, posted on X claiming to have given birth to Musk's son Romulus five months earlier, which media outlets reported as Musk's supposed thirteenth child. On February 22, 2025, it was reported that St Clair had filed for sole custody of her five-month-old son and for Musk to be recognised as the child's father. On March 31, 2025, Musk wrote that, while he was unsure if he was the father of St. Clair's child, he had paid St. Clair $2.5 million and would continue paying her $500,000 per year. Later reporting from the *Wall Street Journal* indicated that $1 million of these payments to St. Clair were structured as a loan.

### Involvement with Jeffrey Epstein

See also: [List of people named in the Epstein files](/wiki/List_of_people_named_in_the_Epstein_files)

In 2014, Musk and [Ghislaine Maxwell](/wiki/Ghislaine_Maxwell) appeared together in a photograph taken at an Academy Awards after-party, which Musk later described as a "[photobomb](/wiki/Photobombing)". The January 2026 [Epstein files](/wiki/Epstein_files) contain emails between Musk and Epstein from 2012 to 2013, after [Epstein's first conviction](/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein#First_criminal_case_(2005–2011)).

Emails released on January 30, 2026, indicated that Epstein invited Musk to visit his private island on multiple occasions. The correspondence showed that while Epstein repeatedly encouraged Musk to attend, Musk did not visit the island. In one instance, Musk discussed the possibility of attending a party with his then-wife [Talulah Riley](/wiki/Talulah_Riley) and asked which day would be the "wildest party"; according to the emails, the visit did not take place after Epstein later cancelled the plans.

On [Christmas day](/wiki/Christmas_day) in 2012, Musk emailed Epstein asking "Do you have any parties planned? I've been working to the edge of sanity this year and so, once my kids head home after Christmas, I really want to hit the party scene in [St Barts](/wiki/St._Barts) or elsewhere and let loose. The invitation is much appreciated, but a peaceful island experience is the opposite of what I'm looking for". Epstein replied that the "ratio on my island" might make Musk's wife uncomfortable to which Musk responded, "Ratio is not a problem for Talulah".

On September 11, 2013, Epstein sent an email asking Musk if he had any plans for coming to New York for the opening of the [United Nations General Assembly](/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly), saying that many "interesting people" would be coming to Epstein's house. Musk responded that "Flying to NY to see UN diplomats do nothing would be an unwise use of time". Epstein responded by stating "Do you think i am retarded. Just kidding, there is no one over 25 and all very cute."

Musk has denied any close relationship with Epstein and described him as a "creep" who attempted to ingratiate himself with influential people. When Musk was asked in 2019 if he introduced Epstein to [Mark Zuckerberg](/wiki/Mark_Zuckerberg), Musk responded: "I don't recall introducing Epstein to anyone, as I don't know the guy well enough to do so." The released emails nonetheless showed cordial exchanges on a range of topics, including Musk's inquiry about parties on the island. The correspondence also indicated that Musk suggested hosting Epstein at SpaceX, while Epstein separately discussed plans to tour SpaceX and bring "the girls", though there is no evidence that such a visit occurred. Musk has described the release of the files a "distraction", later accusing the [second Trump administration](/wiki/Second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump) of suppressing them to protect powerful individuals, including Trump himself.

## Wealth

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Wealth of Elon Musk](/wiki/Wealth_of_Elon_Musk).[(https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Wealth_of_Elon_Musk&action=edit)]

Elon Musk is the wealthiest person in the world, with an estimated [net worth](/wiki/Net_worth) of US$659 billion as of May 2026, according to the *[Bloomberg Billionaires Index](/wiki/Bloomberg_Billionaires_Index)*, and $788 billion according to *[Forbes](/wiki/Forbes)*, primarily from his ownership stakes in [SpaceX](/wiki/SpaceX) and [Tesla](/wiki/Tesla,_Inc.).

Having been first listed on the [*Forbes Billionaires List*](/wiki/The_World%27s_Billionaires) in 2012, around 75% of Musk's wealth was derived from Tesla stock in November 2020, although he describes himself as "[cash poor](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cash_poor)". According to *Forbes*, he became the first person in the world to achieve a net worth of $300 billion in 2021; $400 billion in December 2024; $500 billion in October 2025; $600 billion in mid-December 2025; $700 billion later that month; and $800 billion in February 2026.

In November 2025, a Tesla pay package worth potentially $1 trillion for Musk was approved, which he is to receive over 10 years if he meets specific goals.

### Musk Foundation

Main article: [Musk Foundation](/wiki/Musk_Foundation)

The Musk Foundation is a United States–based charitable [foundation](/wiki/Foundation_(nonprofit)) funded and directed primarily by Musk. The foundation is dedicated to promoting [renewable energy](/wiki/Renewable_energy), [crewed space exploration](/wiki/Space_exploration), [pediatrics](/wiki/Pediatrics), science and engineering education, and the "development of safe [artificial intelligence](/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) for the benefit of humanity".

At the end of 2024, the Musk Foundation had over $14 billion in assets. Since 2020, donations have largely been sent to organizations affiliated with Musk and his businesses.

## Public image

Main article: [Public image of Elon Musk](/wiki/Public_image_of_Elon_Musk)

Although his ventures have been highly influential within their separate industries starting in the 2000s, Musk only became a public figure in the early 2010s. He has been described as an eccentric who makes spontaneous and impactful decisions, while also often making controversial statements, contrary to other billionaires who prefer reclusiveness to protect their businesses. Musk's actions and [his expressed views](/wiki/Views_of_Elon_Musk) have made him a polarizing figure. Biographer [Ashlee Vance](/wiki/Ashlee_Vance) described people's opinions of Musk as polarized due to his "part philosopher, part [troll](/wiki/Troll_(slang))" persona on Twitter. He has drawn denouncement for using his platform to mock the self-selection of [personal pronouns](/wiki/Preferred_gender_pronoun), while also receiving praise for bringing international attention to matters like [British](/wiki/British_people) survivors of [grooming gangs](/wiki/Grooming_gangs_scandal).

Musk has been described as an American [oligarch](/wiki/Oligarchy) due to his extensive influence over public discourse, social media, industry, politics, and government policy. After Trump's re-election, Musk's [influence and actions](/wiki/Political_activities_of_Elon_Musk#United_States) during the transition period and the [second presidency of Donald Trump](/wiki/Second_presidency_of_Donald_Trump) led some to call him "President Musk", the "actual president-elect", "shadow president" or "co-president".

### Accolades

Main article: [List of awards and honors received by Elon Musk](/wiki/List_of_awards_and_honors_received_by_Elon_Musk)

Musk receiving the [Order of Defence Merit](/wiki/Order_of_Defence_Merit) from the [Brazilian Armed Forces](/wiki/Brazilian_Armed_Forces) in 2022

Awards for his contributions to the development of the [Falcon rockets](/wiki/Falcon_rocket) include the [American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics](/wiki/American_Institute_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics) George Low Transportation Award in 2008, the [Fédération Aéronautique Internationale](/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_A%C3%A9ronautique_Internationale) Gold Space Medal in 2010, and the [Royal Aeronautical Society](/wiki/Royal_Aeronautical_Society) Gold Medal in 2012. In 2015, he received an honorary doctorate in engineering and technology from [Yale University](/wiki/Yale_University) and an [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers Honorary Membership](/wiki/IEEE_Honorary_Membership). Musk was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2018. In 2022, Musk was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering).

*[Time](/wiki/Time_(magazine))* has listed Musk as [one of the most influential people in the world](/wiki/Time_100) in 2010, 2013, 2018, and 2021. Musk was selected as *Time*'s "[Person of the Year](/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year)" for 2021. Then *Time* editor-in-chief [Edward Felsenthal](/wiki/Edward_Felsenthal) wrote that, "Person of the Year is a marker of influence, and few individuals have had more influence than Musk on life on Earth, and potentially life off Earth too."

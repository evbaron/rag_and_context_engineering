**Stan Roto Walker** (born 23 October 1990) is an Australian-New Zealand singer, actor, and television personality. In 2009, Walker was the winner of the [seventh season](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Idol_(season_7)) of *[Australian Idol](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Idol)*. He subsequently signed a recording contract with [Sony Music Australia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Music_Australia). It was in December 2009, Walker released his debut studio album, *[Introducing Stan Walker](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introducing_Stan_Walker)*, which included the hit single, "[Black Box](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Box_(song))". The album debuted at number three on the Australian [ARIA Albums Chart](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Charts) and was certified platinum by the [Australian Recording Industry Association](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association) (ARIA). It also appeared on the [New Zealand Albums Chart](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_New_Zealand) at number two and was certified triple platinum by the Recording Industry Association of New Zealand (RIANZ).

In 2010, Walker released his second studio album, *[From the Inside Out](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_Inside_Out)*, which spawned the hit singles, "[Unbroken](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbroken_(Stan_Walker_song))" and "[Choose You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choose_You)". The album debuted at number one on the New Zealand Albums Chart and number two on the ARIA Albums Chart. In November 2011, Walker released his third studio album, *[Let the Music Play](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_the_Music_Play_(Stan_Walker_album))*, which debuted at number 18 on the ARIA Albums Chart and number 12 in New Zealand, and spawned the hit single "[Loud](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loud_(Stan_Walker_song))". During his career, Walker has won five [New Zealand Music Awards](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Music_Awards) and has received five [ARIA Music Award](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Music_Awards) nominations. Walker was a judge on the [first](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X_Factor_(New_Zealand_series_1)) and [second season](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X_Factor_(New_Zealand_series_2)) of *[The X Factor NZ](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X_Factor_NZ)* in 2013 and 2015. He made his acting debut in the New Zealand film, *[Mt Zion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt_Zion_(film))* (2013), and played the role of Benjy in the 2015 New Zealand film, *Born to Dance*. Walker's sixth studio album, *[Te Arohanui](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Arohanui)* was released on 17 September 2021 and was his first in [te reo Māori](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_reo_M%C4%81ori).

## Life and career

### Early life (1990–2009)

Stan Walker's parents are Ross and April Walker; he is the nephew of [Tūhoe](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%ABhoe) artist and activist [Tāme Iti](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81me_Iti), and grand-nephew of singer [Whirimako Black](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirimako_Black). He is [Māori](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people), of [Tūhoe](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81i_T%C5%ABhoe), [Ngāi Te Rangi](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81i_Te_Rangi) and [Ngāti Porou](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81ti_Porou) descent. Walker grew up on a [marae](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marae#Marae_in_New_Zealand) in [Mount Maunganui](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Maunganui), New Zealand. There he attended [Fairfield Intermediate](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_Intermediate) and [Hamilton Boys' High School](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Boys%27_High_School) in [Hamilton](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_New_Zealand), and boarded at [New Plymouth Boys' High School](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Plymouth_Boys%27_High_School).

Walker has compared his early life to that portrayed in the 1994 New Zealand film, *[Once Were Warriors](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Were_Warriors_(film))*, which tells the story of an urban Māori family and their problems with poverty, alcoholism and domestic violence. His father was repeatedly jailed for beating his wife, April, and five children, including him. This led to Walker smoking [marijuana](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana) and [thieving](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theft). He was also sexually abused by a relative over a nine-month period. His parents were drug dealers and both have spent time in jail for drug offences.

Walker in 2013

At the age of 15, Walker started going to church, after hearing a testimony of a girl who had a similar family background to his. Walker says his passion for music and church made him a "good boy" again. After several violent episodes, Walker and his family moved back to Australia and settled in [Coolangatta, Queensland](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolangatta,_Queensland). In 2009, Walker lost a daughter when his then-girlfriend suffered a [miscarriage](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage). He had planned to call his baby, Ataahua, which is Māori for "beautiful", and wears her name as a tattoo on his neck. Before Walker had entered *Australian Idol*, he was a shop assistant at a menswear shop in Coolangatta.

Walker says that moving to Australia helped him pursue a career in entertainment.

### 2009: *Australian Idol* and *Introducing Stan Walker*

Main article: [Australian Idol (season 7)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Idol_(season_7))

In 2009, Walker auditioned for the seventh season of *[Australian Idol](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Idol)*, singing [John Legend](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Legend)'s "[Ordinary People](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_People_(John_Legend_song))" at his [Brisbane](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) audition. He received praise from the judges and progressed through to the theatre rounds receiving more recognition from the judges, with [Kyle Sandilands](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Sandilands) saying, "You are the only person after the audition that I ever told anyone about family, friends other people that I run into. I only told them about you." After all three theatre rounds were over, it was announced that Walker had made it into the top twenty four semi-finals. Walker was a part of the first group to perform in the semi-finals. The following evening, it was announced that the first two contestants to be put through to the final top twelve according to viewer votes, were Walker and fellow contestant [Kate Cook](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Cook_(singer)).

Throughout the season, Walker was praised continually for his voice and for the daring song choices he took with his performances. One of his most highly praised performances on the show was of the [Prince](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_(musician)) song, "[Purple Rain](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Rain_(song))", which received a [standing ovation](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_ovation) from the judging panel and the audience. The grand final was held on 22 November 2009 at the [Sydney Opera House](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Opera_House). After the viewer votes had been tallied, it was announced that the winner was Walker. He is the first person of New Zealand descent to win *Australian Idol*. After coming out of *Australian Idol* as the winner, Walker signed to [Sony Music Australia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Music_Australia) and received an artist's development fund worth [$](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_dollar)200,000. During this time, he established his own company called, Stan Walker Music Pty Ltd, which is run by his mother, April. The company was set up in order to negotiate his contract with Sony Music and not end up like past *Australian Idol* contestant [Damien Leith](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damien_Leith), who claimed he did not make any money from his *Winners Journey* release.

Walker's debut single, "[Black Box](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Box_(song))", was released [digitally](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_download) following his win on *Australian Idol* on 22 November. The song peaked at number two on the [ARIA Singles Chart](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARIA_Charts) and was certified double platinum by the [Australian Recording Industry Association](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association) (ARIA), for selling 140,000 copies. It also peaked at number one on the [New Zealand Singles Chart](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_New_Zealand) and was certified double platinum by the Recording Industry Association of New Zealand (RIANZ), for selling 30,000 copies. Walker's debut studio album, *[Introducing Stan Walker](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introducing_Stan_Walker)*, was released on 8 December 2009, three days ahead of its original release date. The album featured the selected songs Walker had performed as part of the top twelve on *Australian Idol*, as well the two original songs, "Black Box" and "Think of Me", which was produced by the first *Australian Idol* winner, [Guy Sebastian](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Sebastian). *Introducing Stan Walker* debuted at number three on the ARIA Albums Chart and was certified platinum by ARIA. It also made its debut on the New Zealand Albums Chart at number two and was certified triple platinum by the RIANZ, for selling 45,000 copies.

### 2010–2012: *From the Inside Out* and *Let the Music Play*

In April 2010, Walker flew to [Haiti](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti) to help [Compassion Australia](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compassion_Australia)'s relief effort of the [2010 Haiti earthquake](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake) as an ambassador. Walker's second studio album, *[From the Inside Out](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_the_Inside_Out)*, was released on 20 August 2010. The album debuted at number two on the ARIA Albums Chart. In New Zealand, it debuted at number one on the New Zealand Albums Chart, and became Walker's first number-one album in the country. Eventually, *From the Inside Out* was certified platinum by the RIANZ. "[Unbroken](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbroken_(Stan_Walker_song))" was released as the album's [lead single](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_single) on 12 April 2010. On the New Zealand Singles Chart, the song peaked at number nine and was certified gold. In Australia, "Unbroken" peaked at number 23 on the ARIA Singles Chart and was certified gold. In June 2010, Walker featured on New Zealand rapper [Young Sid](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Sid)'s single, "[Stuck in a Box](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuck_in_a_Box)". The album's second single, "[Choose You](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choose_You)", was released on 20 July 2010. The song peaked at number three on the New Zealand Singles Chart and was certified platinum. It also appeared on the ARIA Singles Chart at number 16 and was certified platinum. "[Homesick](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homesick_(Stan_Walker_song))", featuring rapper Kayo, was released as the album's third and final single on 29 October. It peaked at number 21 on the New Zealand Singles Chart and was certified gold. This single did not chart in Australia.

Walker and [Jessica Mauboy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Mauboy) performing during the Galaxy Tour in January 2012.

In November 2010, Walker served as a support act for the [Summerbeatz](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerbeatz) tour in Australia, touring alongside [Flo Rida](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flo_Rida), [Jay Sean](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Sean), [Akon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akon) and [Ciara](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciara), among other artists. He performed at the [Parachute music festival](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute_music_festival) in January 2011. In February 2011, Walker held his first headlining tour in New Zealand.

Walker's third studio album, *[Let the Music Play](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_the_Music_Play_(Stan_Walker_album))*, was released on 18 November 2011. It debuted at number 18 on the ARIA Album Chart and number 12 on the New Zealand Albums Chart. Its lead single, "[Loud](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loud_(Stan_Walker_song))", was released on 2 May 2011. The song peaked at number nine on the ARIA Singles Chart and was certified platinum. On the New Zealand Singles Chart, "Loud" peaked at number eight and was certified gold. The album's second single, "[Light It Up](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_It_Up_(Stan_Walker_song))", featuring [Static Revenger](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_Revenger), was released on 16 September 2011. The song peaked at number 23 on the New Zealand Singles Chart and has reached number 45 on the ARIA Singles Chart. In October 2011, Walker was featured on [Jessica Mauboy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Mauboy)'s single, "[Galaxy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_(Jessica_Mauboy_song))", which peaked at number 13 on the ARIA Singles Chart. Walker and Mauboy embarked on their [Galaxy Tour](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_%27Em_Girls#Galaxy_Tour) across Australia in January 2012, to celebrate the release of the song. The tour ended in February 2012.

In March 2012, "[Music Won't Break Your Heart](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_Won%27t_Break_Your_Heart)" was released as the third single from *Let the Music Play* and it peaked at number 25 in Australia and number 32 in New Zealand. In May 2012, Walker became a supporting act for [Nicki Minaj](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicki_Minaj) on the Australian leg of her [Pink Friday Tour](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Friday_Tour_(2012)).

### 2013–2016: *The X Factor*, acting debut, *Inventing Myself* and *Truth & Soul*

On 6 November 2012, it was announced that Walker would be a judge on the first season of the New Zealand version of [*The X Factor*](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X_Factor_(New_Zealand)) in 2013. Walker made his acting debut playing the lead role of Turei in the New Zealand film, *[Mt Zion](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Zion_(film))*, about a young Māori musician who seeks to open for [Bob Marley](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Marley)'s 1979 Auckland show, but his dream clashes with his family's ideals. When speaking of his role in the film, Walker told *[Herald Sun](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_Sun)*, "They approached me ... the script is so good, it's basically who I was six years ago ... It's definitely the main reason I wanted to get involved.". The film was released on 6 February 2013. Walker recorded "[Take It Easy](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_It_Easy_(Stan_Walker_song))" for the film's soundtrack; the song was released as a single in December 2012 and was also included in Walker's [greatest hits album](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_hits_album), *The Complete Collection*.

Walker opened for [Beyoncé](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9)'s [The Mrs. Carter Show World Tour](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mrs._Carter_Show_World_Tour) in October 2013 for the Auckland and Perth concerts and three of the four Sydney concerts, following [Iggy Azalea](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iggy_Azalea) pulling out of these dates. Walker's fourth studio album, *[Inventing Myself](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventing_Myself)*, was released on 25 October 2013. In August 2014, Walker featured with other New Zealand artists on the [charity single](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_single) "Song for Everyone". His fifth studio album *[Truth & Soul](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_%26_Soul)*, featuring a collection of soul covers, was released in Australia on 17 April 2015.

Walker returned to *The X Factor NZ* in 2015 alongside returning judge [Melanie Blatt](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Blatt) and new judges [Natalia Kills](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Kills) and [Willy Moon](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Moon). Following the first night of live shows both Kills and Moon were fired from the panel following rude comments made to a contestant and were replaced by [Natalie Bassingthwaite](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Bassingthwaite) and Shelton Woolwright. Walker placed runner-up with his contestant Nyssa Collins.

In 2015, he appeared with rugby player [Israel Dagg](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Dagg) and actor [Rip Torn](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rip_Torn) in a *[Men In Black](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_in_Black_(film_series))* themed safety ad for [Air New Zealand](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_New_Zealand).

### 2017–2019: Stomach cancer, *Stan* & *Faith Hope Love*

In January 2018, fans of Walker expressed concern for his health after gaunt photos appeared on social media. In March, it was announced Walker had his stomach removed in September 2017 after discovering he inherited a rare cancer-causing gene mutation called CDH1 that has been responsible for the deaths of more than 25 of his family members. Walker said "The past nine months have been a life changing journey full of ups & downs to say the least, [but] all I'm gonna say is that I’m blessed to be alive and well." A feature-length documentary titled *Stan* was released in March 2018.

On 26 March 2018, Walker released a 6-track [self-titled EP](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_(EP)). Upon its release, Walker said “This EP has songs I've written 11 years ago, 5 years ago, 3 years ago & now. All these songs were written in break even times in my life.” On 7 December 2018, Walker released "Gimme Your Love" alongside the video which features a fly on-the-wall insight into Stan's National 2018 tour.

In May 2019, Walker released *[Faith Hope Love](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Hope_Love_(EP))*, an EP dedicated to the victims of the [Christchurch mosque shootings](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch_mosque_shootings).

### 2020-present: *Impossible: My Story*, *Te Arohanui* & *All In*

In 2020, Walker began releasing new singles weekly, commencing with "Mexico" on 21 February 2020.

In October 2020, Walker released an autobiography titled *Impossible: My Story*. A compilation album was released to coincide with the album, titled *[Impossible (Music by the Book)](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impossible_(Music_by_the_Book))*. The album peaked at number 22 on the New Zealand chart.

In November 2020 he was named one of the best dressed men in show business on [David Hartnell MNZM's](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hartnell) Best Dressed List.

In August 2021, Walker released "Don't Worry Baby" with Australian singer Celina Sharma, the lead single from his then-forthcoming seventh studio album *All In*. Walker said "This whole album, if I can say anything, is all soul and R&B. The seasons of my life over the last four years; love, heartbreak, time of change, racism, mental health, everything."

On 7 September 2021, Walker announced the release of a [Māori language](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language) studio album *[Te Arohanui](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Arohanui)* for release on 17 September.

In August 2022, Walker released his seventh studio album, *[All In](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_In_(Stan_Walker_album))*.

In November 2022, Walker released the Christmas single "Favourite Part About Christmas".

In November 2023, Walker released the single "[I Am](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_(Stan_Walker_song))", from the soundtrack for the film *[Origin](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_(film))*.

On 22 February 2024, Walker premiered the single "Māori ki te Ao", sung in [te reo Māori](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_reo_M%C4%81ori).

## Musical style and inspiration

Walker cites [Beyoncé](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9) as a main inspiration; "She's musically why I do what I do." According to Jason Birchmeier of [Allmusic](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allmusic), Walker sings predominantly [pop music](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) "with an R&B style". Walker's second album, *From the Inside Out*, drew from pop, R&B and [hip hop](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop_music), with elements of [electro](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro_music), [glam metal](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_metal), [pop-rap](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-rap) and [funk](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_music) also present. He describes his third album, the more pop and [dance](//en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_music)-oriented *Let the Music Play*, as thematically his most personal. "These are my songs, my stories, my tunes, my melodies, my words, me."

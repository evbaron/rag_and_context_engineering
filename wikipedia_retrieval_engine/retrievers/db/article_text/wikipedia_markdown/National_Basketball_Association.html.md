The **National Basketball Association** (**NBA**) is a professional [basketball](/wiki/Basketball) league in [North America](/wiki/North_America) composed of 30 teams (29 in the [United States](/wiki/United_States) and 1 in [Canada](/wiki/Canada)). The NBA is one of the [major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada](/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) and is considered the premier professional basketball league in the world. The league is headquartered in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) and [Secaucus, New Jersey](/wiki/Secaucus,_New_Jersey).

The NBA was created on August 3, 1949, with the merger of the [Basketball Association of America](/wiki/Basketball_Association_of_America) (BAA) and the [National Basketball League](/wiki/National_Basketball_League_(United_States)) (NBL). The league later adopted the BAA's history and considers its founding on June 6, 1946, as its own. In 1976, the NBA and the [American Basketball Association](/wiki/American_Basketball_Association) (ABA) [merged](/wiki/ABA%E2%80%93NBA_merger), adding four franchises to the NBA. The NBA's regular season runs from October to April, with each team playing 82 games. The [league's playoff tournament](/wiki/NBA_playoffs) extends into June, culminating with the [NBA Finals](/wiki/NBA_Finals) championship series.

The NBA is a member of [USA Basketball](/wiki/USA_Basketball) (USAB), which is recognized by the [International Basketball Federation](/wiki/FIBA) (FIBA) as the [governing body](/wiki/Sports_governing_body) for basketball in the United States. The NBA is the second-wealthiest [professional sports](/wiki/Professional_sports) league in the world [by revenue](/wiki/List_of_professional_sports_leagues_by_revenue) after the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League) (NFL). As of 2020, NBA players are the world's highest paid athletes by average annual salary per player. In 1996, the NBA created a women's league, the [Women's National Basketball Association](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) (WNBA). The NBA also operates the [NBA G League](/wiki/NBA_G_League), [NBA 2K League](/wiki/NBA_2K_League), and the [Basketball Africa League](/wiki/Basketball_Africa_League).

The [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) have the most [NBA championships](/wiki/List_of_NBA_champions) with 18, winning most recently in 2024. The [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) are the reigning league champions, having defeated the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) in the [2025 NBA Finals](/wiki/2025_NBA_Finals).

## History

### Creation and BAA–NBL merger (1946–1956)

Main article: [Basketball Association of America](/wiki/Basketball_Association_of_America)

[Maple Leaf Gardens](/wiki/Maple_Leaf_Gardens) in [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto), site of the first-ever NBA game on November 1, 1946

The NBA began as the [Basketball Association of America](/wiki/Basketball_Association_of_America) (BAA), which was founded in 1946 by owners of the major [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) arenas in the [Northeastern](/wiki/Northeastern_United_States) and [Midwestern United States](/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) and Canada. On November 1, 1946, in [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto), Ontario, Canada, the [Toronto Huskies](/wiki/Toronto_Huskies) hosted the [New York Knickerbockers](/wiki/New_York_Knicks) at [Maple Leaf Gardens](/wiki/Maple_Leaf_Gardens), in a game the league now calls the first game played in NBA history. The first basket was made by [Ossie Schectman](/wiki/Ossie_Schectman) of the Knickerbockers.

Although there had been earlier attempts at professional basketball leagues, including the [American Basketball League](/wiki/American_Basketball_League_(1925%E2%80%9355)) (ABL) and the [National Basketball League](/wiki/National_Basketball_League_(United_States)) (NBL), the BAA was the first league to attempt to play primarily in large arenas in major cities. During its early years, the quality of play in the BAA was not significantly better than in competing leagues or among leading independent clubs such as the [Harlem Globetrotters](/wiki/Harlem_Globetrotters). For instance, the 1947 ABL finalist [Baltimore Bullets](/wiki/Baltimore_Bullets_(1944%E2%80%9354)) moved to the BAA and won that league's 1948 title, and the 1948 NBL champion [Minneapolis Lakers](/wiki/Minneapolis_Lakers) won the 1949 BAA title.

Before the 1948–49 season, the BAA lured away several NBL teams—the [Fort Wayne Pistons](/wiki/Fort_Wayne_Pistons), [Indianapolis Kautskys](/wiki/Indianapolis_Kautskys), [Minneapolis Lakers](/wiki/Minneapolis_Lakers), and [Rochester Royals](/wiki/Rochester_Royals)—with the prospect of playing in major venues such as [Boston Garden](/wiki/Boston_Garden) and [Madison Square Garden](/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden). The NBL hit back by outbidding the BAA for the services of several players, including [Al Cervi](/wiki/Al_Cervi), rookie [Dolph Schayes](/wiki/Dolph_Schayes) and five stars from the [University of Kentucky](/wiki/University_of_Kentucky), while also gaining the upper hand in Indianapolis with the creation of the [Indianapolis Olympians](/wiki/Indianapolis_Olympians) while the Kautskys folded. With several teams facing financial difficulties, the BAA and the NBL agreed to merge on August 3, 1949, to create the National Basketball Association. BAA president [Maurice Podoloff](/wiki/Maurice_Podoloff) became the president of the NBA while NBL chairman [Ike Duffey](/wiki/Ike_Duffey) became the chairman. The NBA later adopted the BAA's history and statistics as its own but did not do the same for NBL records and statistics.

Asian American point guard [Wat Misaka](/wiki/Wat_Misaka) broke BAA/NBA's [color barrier](/wiki/Racial_segregation) as the first non-white player to play in the BAA in 1947.

The new league had 17 franchises located in a mix of large and small cities, as well as large arenas and smaller gymnasiums and [armories](/wiki/Arsenal). In 1950, the NBA consolidated to 11 franchises, then in 1954–55 shrank to eight: the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks), [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics), [Philadelphia Warriors](/wiki/Philadelphia_Warriors), [Minneapolis Lakers](/wiki/Minneapolis_Lakers), [Rochester Royals](/wiki/Rochester_Royals), [Fort Wayne Pistons](/wiki/Fort_Wayne_Pistons), [Milwaukee Hawks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Hawks), and [Syracuse Nationals](/wiki/Syracuse_Nationals). All of these teams remain in the league today, albeit the latter six in new cities. During the contraction, the league's smaller-city franchises moved to larger cities. The Hawks shifted from the [Tri-Cities](/wiki/Quad_Cities) to [Milwaukee](/wiki/Milwaukee) in 1951, then to [St. Louis](/wiki/St._Louis) in 1955. In 1957, the Rochester Royals moved from [Rochester, New York](/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), to [Cincinnati](/wiki/Cincinnati) and the Pistons moved from [Fort Wayne, Indiana](/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana), to Detroit.

[Japanese-American](/wiki/Japanese-American) [Wataru Misaka](/wiki/Wataru_Misaka) is considered to have broken the [NBA color barrier](/wiki/NBA_color_barrier) in the [1947–48 season](/wiki/1947%E2%80%9348_NBA_season), when he played for the [New York Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks) in the BAA. He remained the league's only non-white player until the first African-American, [Harold Hunter](/wiki/Harold_Hunter_(basketball)), signed with the [Washington Capitols](/wiki/Washington_Capitols) in 1950. Hunter was cut from the team during training camp, but several other African Americans played in the league later that year, including [Chuck Cooper](/wiki/Chuck_Cooper_(basketball)) with the Celtics, [Nathaniel "Sweetwater" Clifton](/wiki/Nathaniel_%22Sweetwater%22_Clifton) with the Knicks, and [Earl Lloyd](/wiki/Earl_Lloyd) with the [Washington Capitols](/wiki/Washington_Capitols). During this period, the Minneapolis Lakers won five [NBA championships](/wiki/NBA_Finals) and established themselves as the league's first [dynasty](/wiki/Dynasty_(sports)); their squad was led by center [George Mikan](/wiki/George_Mikan), the NBA's first [superstar](/wiki/Superstar). In 1954, the league introduced the 24-second [shot clock](/wiki/Shot_clock) to encourage shooting and discourage stalling.

### Celtics' dominance, league expansion and competition (1956–1979)

In 1957, rookie center [Bill Russell](/wiki/Bill_Russell) joined the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics), which already had guard [Bob Cousy](/wiki/Bob_Cousy) and coach [Red Auerbach](/wiki/Red_Auerbach), and went on to lead the franchise to 11 NBA titles in thirteen seasons. Center [Wilt Chamberlain](/wiki/Wilt_Chamberlain) entered the league with the Warriors in 1959 and became a star of the 1960s, setting single-game records in scoring ([100](/wiki/Wilt_Chamberlain%27s_100-point_game)) and rebounding (55). [Russell's rivalry with Chamberlain](/wiki/Russell%E2%80%93Chamberlain_rivalry) became one of the greatest rivalries in the history of American team sports.

[Bill Russell](/wiki/Bill_Russell) defending against [Wilt Chamberlain](/wiki/Wilt_Chamberlain) in 1966

The 1960s were dominated by the Celtics. Led by Russell, Cousy, and Auerbach, Boston won eight straight championships from 1959 to 1966—the longest championship streak in American professional sports. They did not win the title in [1966–67](/wiki/1966%E2%80%9367_NBA_season), but regained it in the [1967–68 season](/wiki/1967%E2%80%9368_NBA_season) and repeated in [1969](/wiki/1968%E2%80%9369_NBA_season), earning nine of the decade's championship banners.

Through this period, the NBA continued to evolve with the shift of the [Minneapolis Lakers](/wiki/Minneapolis_Lakers) to Los Angeles, the [Philadelphia Warriors](/wiki/Philadelphia_Warriors) to San Francisco, the [Syracuse Nationals](/wiki/Syracuse_Nationals) to [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia) to become the [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers), and the [St. Louis Hawks](/wiki/St._Louis_Hawks) moving to [Atlanta](/wiki/Atlanta), as well as the addition of its first expansion franchises. The Chicago Packers (now [Washington Wizards](/wiki/Washington_Wizards)) became the ninth NBA team in 1961. From 1966 to 1968, the league expanded from 9 to 14 teams, introducing the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/Chicago_Bulls), [Seattle SuperSonics](/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics) (now [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder)), [San Diego Rockets](/wiki/San_Diego_Rockets) (who moved to [Houston](/wiki/Houston) four years later), [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks), and [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/Phoenix_Suns).

In 1967, the league faced a new external threat with the formation of the [American Basketball Association](/wiki/American_Basketball_Association) (ABA). The leagues engaged in a bidding war. The NBA landed the most important college star of the era, [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) (then known as Lew Alcindor), who went on to become the league's best player of the 1970s. However, the NBA's leading scorer, [Rick Barry](/wiki/Rick_Barry), jumped to the ABA, as did four veteran referees—[Norm Drucker](/wiki/Norm_Drucker), [Earl Strom](/wiki/Earl_Strom), John Vanak, and Joe Gushue.

[Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar), shown shooting his signature "[skyhook](/wiki/Hook_shot#Skyhook)" shot, was a record six-time [MVP](/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player) winner.

In 1969, [Alan Siegel](/wiki/Alan_Siegel), who oversaw the design of Jerry Dior's [Major League Baseball logo](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_logo) the previous year, created the modern NBA logo inspired by the MLB's. It incorporates the silhouette of [Jerry West](/wiki/Jerry_West), based on a photo by Wen Roberts. The NBA would not confirm that a particular player was used because, according to Siegel, "They want to institutionalize it rather than individualize it. It's become such a ubiquitous, classic symbol and focal point of their identity and their licensing program that they don't necessarily want to identify it with one player." The logo debuted in 1971 (with a small change to the typeface on the NBA wordmark in 2017) and would remain a fixture of the NBA brand.

The ABA signed a number of major stars in the 1970s, including [Julius Erving](/wiki/Julius_Erving) of the [Virginia Squires](/wiki/Virginia_Squires), in part because it allowed teams to sign college undergraduates. From 1966 to 1974, the NBA grew from nine franchises to 18. In 1970, the [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers), [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers), and [Buffalo Braves](/wiki/Buffalo_Braves) (now the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers)) expanded the league to 17. The New Orleans Jazz (now in [Utah](/wiki/Utah_Jazz)) came aboard in 1974. After the 1976 season, [the leagues reached a settlement](/wiki/ABA%E2%80%93NBA_merger) that would add four ABA franchises to the NBA—the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs), [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets), [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers), and [New York Nets](/wiki/New_York_Nets) (now the Brooklyn Nets)—bringing the number of NBA teams to 22. Some of the biggest stars of this era were Abdul-Jabbar, Barry, [Dave Cowens](/wiki/Dave_Cowens), Erving, [Elvin Hayes](/wiki/Elvin_Hayes), [Walt Frazier](/wiki/Walt_Frazier), [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone), [Artis Gilmore](/wiki/Artis_Gilmore), [George Gervin](/wiki/George_Gervin), [Dan Issel](/wiki/Dan_Issel), and [Pete Maravich](/wiki/Pete_Maravich). The end of the decade saw declining television ratings, low attendance, and drug-related player issues – both perceived and real – that threatened to derail the league.

### Surging popularity and Bulls' dynasty (1979–1998)

Both [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) and [Larry Bird](/wiki/Larry_Bird) became key stars for the NBA during the 1980s.

The league added the ABA's [three-point field goal](/wiki/Three-point_field_goal) in 1979. That same year, rookies [Larry Bird](/wiki/Larry_Bird) and [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) joined the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) and [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) respectively, helping to draw fans to the league. The two had faced each other in the [1979 NCAA Division I Basketball Championship Game](/wiki/1979_NCAA_Division_I_Basketball_Championship_Game), and they later played against each other in three NBA Finals (1984, 1985, and 1987). In the 10 seasons of the 1980s, Johnson led the Lakers to five titles while Bird led the Celtics to three titles. Also in the early 1980s, the NBA added another expansion franchise, the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks), bringing the league to 23 teams. Later, Bird won the first three three-point shooting contests. On February 1, 1984 [David Stern](/wiki/David_Stern) became commissioner of the NBA; he would lead the league to new heights of popularity and profitability.

[Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan) became the league's most popular player during the 1990s, while leading the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/Chicago_Bulls) to six championships.

[Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan) entered the league in 1984 with the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/Chicago_Bulls), spurring more interest. In 1988 and 1989, the [Charlotte Hornets](/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets), [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat), [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic), and [Minnesota Timberwolves](/wiki/Minnesota_Timberwolves) made their NBA debuts, bringing the league to 27 teams. The [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons) won back-to-back NBA championships in 1989 and 1990, led by coach [Chuck Daly](/wiki/Chuck_Daly) and guard [Isiah Thomas](/wiki/Isiah_Thomas). Jordan and [Scottie Pippen](/wiki/Scottie_Pippen) led the Bulls to two three-peats in eight years during the 1991–1998 seasons. [Hakeem Olajuwon](/wiki/Hakeem_Olajuwon) won back-to-back titles with the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) in 1994 and 1995.

The [1992 Olympic](/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics) basketball [Dream Team](/wiki/1992_United_States_men%27s_Olympic_basketball_team), the first to use current NBA stars, featured Jordan as the anchor, along with Bird, Johnson, [David Robinson](/wiki/David_Robinson_(basketball)), [Patrick Ewing](/wiki/Patrick_Ewing), Scottie Pippen, [Clyde Drexler](/wiki/Clyde_Drexler), [Karl Malone](/wiki/Karl_Malone), [John Stockton](/wiki/John_Stockton), [Chris Mullin](/wiki/Chris_Mullin_(basketball)), [Charles Barkley](/wiki/Charles_Barkley), and star NCAA amateur [Christian Laettner](/wiki/Christian_Laettner). The team was elected to the [Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame), while 11 of the 12 players (along with three out of four coaches) have been inducted as individuals in their own right.

In 1995, the NBA expanded to Canada with the addition of the [Vancouver Grizzlies](/wiki/Vancouver_Grizzlies) and the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors). In 1996, the NBA created a women's league, the [Women's National Basketball Association](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) (WNBA).

### Lakers' and Spurs' dynasties (1998–2014)

Between 1998 and 2014, [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) of the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and [Tim Duncan](/wiki/Tim_Duncan) of the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs) led their teams to five championships each.

In 1998, the [NBA owners](/wiki/List_of_NBA_team_owners) began [a lockout that suspended all league business](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_NBA_lockout) until a new labor agreement could be reached, which led to the season being shortened to 50 games.

After the breakup of the [Chicago Bulls](/wiki/Chicago_Bulls) championship roster in the summer of 1998, the [Western Conference](/wiki/Western_Conference_(NBA)) dominated much of the next two decades. The [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers), coached by [Phil Jackson](/wiki/Phil_Jackson), and the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/San_Antonio_Spurs), coached by [Gregg Popovich](/wiki/Gregg_Popovich), combined to make 13 Finals in 16 seasons, with 10 titles. "[Twin Towers](/wiki/Twin_Towers_(San_Antonio_Spurs))" [Tim Duncan](/wiki/Tim_Duncan) and [David Robinson](/wiki/David_Robinson_(basketball)) won the [1999 championship](/wiki/1999_NBA_Finals) with the [Spurs](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_San_Antonio_Spurs_season), becoming the first former ABA team to win the NBA championship. [Shaquille O'Neal](/wiki/Shaquille_O%27Neal) and [Kobe Bryant](/wiki/Kobe_Bryant) started the 2000s with three consecutive championships for the Lakers. The Spurs [reclaimed the title in 2003](/wiki/2003_NBA_Finals) against the [Nets](/wiki/New_Jersey_Nets). In 2004, the Lakers [returned to the Finals](/wiki/2004_NBA_Finals), only to lose in five games to the [Detroit Pistons](/wiki/Detroit_Pistons).

After the Hornets moved to [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans) in 2002, the NBA returned to North Carolina, as the [Charlotte Bobcats](/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) were formed as an [expansion team](/wiki/Expansion_team) in 2004. New Orleans then [temporarily moved](/wiki/Effect_of_Hurricane_Katrina_on_the_New_Orleans_Hornets) to [Oklahoma City](/wiki/Oklahoma_City) in 2005 for two seasons due to damage caused to their arena by [Hurricane Katrina](/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina). The team returned to New Orleans in 2007.

The league's image was marred by a [violent incident between players and fans](/wiki/The_Malice_at_the_Palace) in a November 2004 game between the [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) and Detroit Pistons. In response, players were suspended for a total of 146 games with $11 million total lost in salary, and the league tightened security and limited the sale of alcohol.

On May 19, 2005, Commissioner Stern testified before the [U.S. House of Representatives](/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives)' Committee on Government Reform about the NBA's actions to combat the use of [steroids](/wiki/Anabolic_steroid) and other performance-enhancing drugs. The NBA started its drug-testing program in 1983 and substantially improved it in 1999. In the 1999–2000 season, all players were randomly tested during training camp, and all rookies were additionally tested three more times during the regular season. Of the nearly 4,200 tests for steroids and performance-enhancing drugs conducted over six seasons, only three players were confirmed positive for NBA's drug program, all were immediately suspended, and as of the time of the testimony, none were playing in the NBA.

After the Spurs [won the championship again in 2005](/wiki/2005_NBA_Finals), the [2006 Finals](/wiki/2006_NBA_Finals) featured two franchises making their inaugural Finals appearances. The [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat), led by their star shooting guard, [Dwyane Wade](/wiki/Dwyane_Wade), and Shaquille O'Neal, who had been traded from the Lakers during the summer of 2004, won the series over the [Dallas Mavericks](/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks). The Lakers/Spurs dominance [continued in 2007 with a four-game sweep](/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals) by the Spurs over the [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James)-led [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers). The [2008 Finals](/wiki/2008_NBA_Finals) saw a rematch of the league's [highest profile rivalry](/wiki/Celtics%E2%80%93Lakers_rivalry), the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) and Los Angeles Lakers, with the Celtics winning their 17th championship.

The NBA Board of Governors approved the request of the [Seattle SuperSonics](/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics) to [move to Oklahoma City](/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics_relocation_to_Oklahoma_City) on April 18, 2008. The team, however, could not move until it had settled a lawsuit filed by the city of [Seattle](/wiki/Seattle), which was intended to keep the SuperSonics in Seattle for the remaining two seasons of the team's lease at [KeyArena](/wiki/KeyArena). Following a court case, the city of Seattle settled with the [ownership group](/wiki/Professional_Basketball_Club) of the SuperSonics on July 2, 2008, allowing the team to move to Oklahoma City immediately in exchange for terminating the final two seasons of the team's lease at KeyArena. The [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) began playing in the 2008–09 season.

The Lakers won back-to-back championships in [2009](/wiki/2009_NBA_Finals) and [2010](/wiki/2010_NBA_Finals), against the [Orlando Magic](/wiki/Orlando_Magic) and the Celtics. The [2010 NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/2010_NBA_All-Star_Game) was held at [Cowboys Stadium](/wiki/Cowboys_Stadium) in front of the largest crowd ever: 108,713.

A referee lockout began on September 1, 2009, when the contract between the NBA and its referees expired. The first preseason games were played on October 1, 2009, and replacement referees from the [WNBA](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) and [NBA Development League](/wiki/NBA_Development_League) were used, the first time replacement referees had been used since the beginning of the 1995–96 season. The NBA and the regular referees reached a deal on October 23, 2009.

At the start of the 2010–11 season, free agents LeBron James and [Chris Bosh](/wiki/Chris_Bosh) signed with the Miami Heat, joining Dwyane Wade to form the "[Big Three](/wiki/Big_Three_(Miami_Heat))". The Heat dominated the league, reaching the Finals for four straight years. In [2011](/wiki/2011_NBA_Finals), they faced a re-match with the Dallas Mavericks but lost to the [Dirk Nowitzki](/wiki/Dirk_Nowitzki)-led team. They won back-to-back titles in [2012](/wiki/2012_NBA_Finals) and [2013](/wiki/2013_NBA_Finals) against the [Oklahoma City Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) and the Spurs, and lost in a re-match with the Spurs in the [2014 Finals](/wiki/2014_NBA_Finals).

The 2011–12 season began with [another lockout](/wiki/2011_NBA_lockout), the league's fourth. After the first few weeks of the season were canceled, the players and owners ratified a new collective bargaining agreement on December 8, 2011, setting up a shortened 66-game season.

After the 2012–13 season, the New Orleans Hornets were renamed the [Pelicans](/wiki/New_Orleans_Pelicans). By May 2014, the Bobcats officially reclaimed the Hornets name, and by agreement with the league and the Pelicans, also received sole ownership of all history, records, and statistics from the Pelicans' time in Charlotte. As a result, the Hornets are now officially considered to have been founded in 1988, suspended operations in 2002, and resumed in 2004 as the Bobcats, while the Pelicans are officially treated as a 2002 expansion team. (This is somewhat similar to the relationship between the [Cleveland Browns](/wiki/Cleveland_Browns) and [Baltimore Ravens](/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens) in the [NFL](/wiki/National_Football_League).)

On February 1, 2014, commissioner David Stern retired after 30 years in the position, and was succeeded by his deputy, [Adam Silver](/wiki/Adam_Silver).

### Warriors' dynasty (2014–2022)

[LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James) became an era-defining star during the 2010s, while leading the [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers) to a historic title in 2016.

After four seasons with the Miami Heat, [LeBron James](/wiki/LeBron_James) returned to the [Cleveland Cavaliers](/wiki/Cleveland_Cavaliers) for the 2014–15 season. He led the team to their second Finals appearance with the help of [Kyrie Irving](/wiki/Kyrie_Irving) and [Kevin Love](/wiki/Kevin_Love). The [Golden State Warriors](/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) defeated the Cavaliers in six games, led by the "[Splash Brothers](/wiki/Splash_Brothers)" [Stephen Curry](/wiki/Stephen_Curry) and [Klay Thompson](/wiki/Klay_Thompson). The Cavaliers and the Warriors faced each other in the Finals a record [four consecutive times](/wiki/Cavaliers%E2%80%93Warriors_rivalry). In the 2015–16 season, the Warriors finished the season 73–9, the [best season record in NBA history](/wiki/List_of_NBA_teams_by_single_season_win_percentage). However, the Cavaliers overcame a 3–1 deficit in the Finals to win [their first championship](/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals) that season, and end a [52-year professional sports championship drought](/wiki/Cleveland_sports_curse) for the city of [Cleveland](/wiki/Cleveland). In the 2016–17 season, the Warriors recruited free agent [Kevin Durant](/wiki/Kevin_Durant) and went on to win the [2017](/wiki/2017_NBA_Finals) and [2018](/wiki/2018_NBA_Finals) Finals against the Cavaliers.

After the departure of James in free agency in 2018, the Cavaliers' streak of playoff and Finals appearances ended. The Warriors returned for a [fifth consecutive Finals appearance in 2019](/wiki/2019_NBA_Finals) but lost to the Toronto Raptors, who won their first championship after acquiring [Kawhi Leonard](/wiki/Kawhi_Leonard) in a trade.

The 2019–20 season was [suspended indefinitely](/wiki/Suspension_of_the_2020_NBA_regular_season) on March 11, 2020, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), after Utah Jazz center [Rudy Gobert](/wiki/Rudy_Gobert) tested positive for the coronavirus. On June 4, 2020, the NBA Board of Governors voted to resume the season in a 22-team format with 8 seeding games per team and a regular playoffs format, with [all games played in a "bubble"](/wiki/2020_NBA_Bubble) in [Walt Disney World](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World) without any fans present.

[Stephen Curry](/wiki/Stephen_Curry) revolutionized the NBA during the 2010s, while leading the [Golden State Warriors](/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) to four championships between 2015 and 2022.

This era also saw the continuous near year-over-year decline in NBA viewership. Between 2012 and 2019, the league lost 40 to 45 percent of its viewership. While some of it can be attributed to "cable-cutting", other professional leagues, like the NFL and MLB have retained stable viewership demographics. The opening game of the 2020 Finals between the [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) and [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat) brought in only 7.41 million viewers to ABC, according to The Hollywood Reporter. That is reportedly the lowest [viewership seen for the Finals](/wiki/NBA_Finals_television_ratings) since at least 1994, when total viewers began to be regularly recorded and is a 45 percent decline from game one between the Golden State Warriors and [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors), which had 13.51 million viewers a year earlier. Some attribute this decline to the political stances the league and its players are taking, while others consider load management, the uneven talent distribution between the conferences and the cord-cutting of younger viewers as the main reason for the decline.

During the [2020–21](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NBA_season) and [2021–22](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NBA_season) seasons, the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) would defeat the [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/Phoenix_Suns) in the [2021 NBA Finals](/wiki/2021_NBA_Finals), securing their second NBA championship since 1971, and the Golden State Warriors made their sixth appearance in the finals defeating the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) in the [2022 NBA Finals](/wiki/2022_NBA_Finals), their fourth championship in eight years.

### Parity era (2023–present)

2023 saw the ratification of a new [collective bargaining agreement](/wiki/NBA_collective_bargaining_agreement), which will penalize teams who exceed the luxury tax above certain "apron" thresholds, making it significantly more difficult for teams to sign multiple superstars to maximum contracts. Seven different champions were crowned in seven years from 2019 to 2025, the longest such stretch in league history, leading numerous outlets to dub this the "[parity](/wiki/Parity_(sports)) era" in contrast to the dynasties which dominated previous decades.

> We set out to create a system that allowed for more competition around the league. The goal being to have 30 teams all in the position, if well managed, to compete for championships. And that's what we're seeing here.

— NBA Commissioner [Adam Silver](/wiki/Adam_Silver) on the NBA's new era of parity

The [2022–23 season](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NBA_season) saw the [Denver Nuggets](/wiki/Denver_Nuggets), led by center [Nikola Jokić](/wiki/Nikola_Joki%C4%87), make the [franchise's first NBA Finals appearance](/wiki/2023_NBA_Finals) and defeat the Miami Heat in five games to win their first NBA championship.

The [2023–24 NBA season](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NBA_season) saw the star-studded Boston Celtics, winning a championship over the Dallas Mavericks, after five [conference finals appearances](/wiki/NBA_conference_finals), and a finals appearance marking their 18th championship, their first since 2008.

The [2025 NBA playoffs](/wiki/2025_NBA_playoffs) featured four teams ([Knicks](/wiki/New_York_Knicks), [Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers), [Thunder](/wiki/Oklahoma_City_Thunder) and [Timberwolves](/wiki/Minnesota_Timberwolves)) who had championship droughts spanning several decades or who had never won a championship. The Thunder would ultimately defeat the Pacers in seven games in the [2025 NBA Finals](/wiki/2025_NBA_Finals) to win their second title in franchise history and their first in Oklahoma City.

### Affiliated leagues

In 1996, the NBA created a women's league, the [Women's National Basketball Association](/wiki/Women%27s_National_Basketball_Association) (WNBA). In 2001, an affiliated [minor league](/wiki/Minor_league), the National Basketball Development League, now called the [NBA G League](/wiki/NBA_G_League), was created. The NBA also operates the [NBA 2K League](/wiki/NBA_2K_League) and the [Basketball Africa League](/wiki/Basketball_Africa_League).

The NBA originated in 1946 with 11 teams, and through a sequence of team expansions, reductions, and relocations, consists of 30 teams – 29 in the United States and 1 in Canada.

The current league organization divides 30 teams into two 15-team conferences of three divisions with five teams each. The current divisional alignment was introduced in the [2004–05 season](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NBA_season). Reflecting the population distribution of the United States and Canada as a whole, most teams are in the eastern half of the country: 13 teams are in the [Eastern Time Zone](/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone), nine in the [Central](/wiki/Central_Time_Zone_(North_America)), three in the [Mountain](/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone), and five in the [Pacific](/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone).

## Regular season

Main article: [List of NBA seasons](/wiki/List_of_NBA_seasons)

Following the summer break, teams begin training camps in late September. Training camps allow the coaching staff to evaluate players (especially rookies), scout the team's strengths and weaknesses, prepare the players for the rigorous regular season and determine the 12-man active roster (and a 3-man inactive list) with which they will begin the regular season. Teams have the ability to assign players with less than two years of experience to the [NBA G League](/wiki/NBA_G_League). After training camp, a series of preseason exhibition games are held. Preseason matches are sometimes held in non-NBA cities, both in the United States and overseas. The NBA regular season begins in mid-October.

During the regular season, each team plays 82 games, 41 each home and away. A team faces opponents in its own division four times a year (16 games). Each team plays six of the teams from the other two divisions in its conference four times (24 games), and the remaining four teams three times (12 games). Finally, each team plays all the teams in the other conference twice apiece (30 games). This asymmetrical structure means the [strength of schedule](/wiki/Strength_of_schedule) will vary between teams (but not as significantly as the [NFL](/wiki/National_Football_League) or [MLB](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball)). Over five seasons, each team will have played 80 games against their division (20 games against each opponent, 10 at home, 10 on the road), 180 games against the rest of their conference (18 games against each opponent, 9 at home, 9 on the road), and 150 games against the other conference (10 games against each team, 5 at home, 5 on the road).

Starting with the [2023–24 season](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NBA_season), the regular season includes an [in-season tournament](/wiki/NBA_In-Season_Tournament), in which all games in the tournament (except for the final) count towards the regular season.

The NBA is also the only league that regularly schedules [games on Christmas Day](/wiki/NBA_Christmas_games). The league has been playing games regularly on the holiday since 1947, though the first Christmas Day games were not televised until [1983–84](/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_NBA_season). Games played on this day have featured some of the best teams and players. Christmas is also notable for NBA on television, as the holiday is when the first NBA games air on network television each season. Games played on this day have been some of the highest-rated games during a particular season.

The NBA has also played [games on Martin Luther King Jr. Day](/wiki/NBA_MLK_Day_games) (MLK Day) every year since the holiday was first observed in 1986.

In February, the regular season pauses to celebrate the annual [NBA All-Star Game](/wiki/NBA_All-Star_Game). Fans vote throughout the United States, Canada, and on the Internet, and the top vote-getters in each conference are named captains. Fan votes determine the rest of the All-Star starters. Coaches vote to choose the remaining 14 All-Stars. The player with the best performance during the game is rewarded with a [Game MVP award](/wiki/NBA_All-Star_Game_MVP). Other attractions of the All-Star break include the [Rising Stars Challenge](/wiki/Rising_Stars_Challenge) (originally Rookie Challenge), where the top rookies and second-year players in the NBA play in a 5-on-5 basketball game, with the current format pitting U.S. players against those from the rest of the world; the [Skills Challenge](/wiki/NBA_All-Star_Weekend_Skills_Challenge), where players compete to finish an obstacle course consisting of shooting, passing, and dribbling in the fastest time; the [Three Point Contest](/wiki/Three_Point_Contest), where players compete to score the highest number of three-point field goals in a given time; and the [NBA Slam Dunk Contest](/wiki/NBA_Slam_Dunk_Contest), where players compete to dunk the ball in the most entertaining way according to the judges. These other attractions have varying names which include the names of the various sponsors who have paid for naming rights.

Shortly after the All-Star break is the [trade deadline](/wiki/Trade_deadline), which is set to fall on the 16th Thursday of the season (usually in February) at 3 pm [Eastern Time](/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone). After this date, teams are not allowed to exchange players with each other for the remainder of the season, although they may still sign and release players. Major trades are often completed right before the trading deadline, making that day a hectic time for [general managers](/wiki/List_of_National_Basketball_Association_general_managers).

Around the middle of April, the regular season ends. It is during this time that voting begins for individual awards, as well as the selection of the honorary, league-wide, postseason teams. The [Sixth Man of the Year Award](/wiki/NBA_Sixth_Man_of_the_Year_Award) is given to the best player coming off the bench (must have more games coming off the bench than actual games started). The [Rookie of the Year Award](/wiki/NBA_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) is awarded to the most outstanding first-year player. The [Most Improved Player Award](/wiki/NBA_Most_Improved_Player_Award) is awarded to the player who is deemed to have shown the most improvement from the previous season. The [Defensive Player of the Year Award](/wiki/NBA_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year_Award) is awarded to the league's best defender. The [Coach of the Year Award](/wiki/NBA_Coach_of_the_Year_Award) is awarded to the coach that has made the most positive difference to a team. The [Most Valuable Player Award](/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) is given to the player deemed the most valuable for (his team) that season. Additionally, *[Sporting News](/wiki/Sporting_News)* awards an unofficial (but widely recognized) [Executive of the Year Award](/wiki/NBA_Executive_of_the_Year_Award) to the general manager who is adjudged to have performed the best job for the benefit of his franchise.

The postseason teams are the [All-NBA Team](/wiki/All-NBA_Team), the [All-Defensive Team](/wiki/NBA_All-Defensive_Team), and the [All-Rookie Team](/wiki/NBA_All-Rookie_Team); each consists of five players. There are three All-NBA teams, consisting of the top players at each position, with first-team status being the most desirable. There are two All-Defensive teams, consisting of the top defenders at each position. There are also two All-Rookie teams, consisting of the top first-year players regardless of position.

## Playoffs

Main article: [NBA playoffs](/wiki/NBA_playoffs)

The [Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy](/wiki/Larry_O%27Brien_Championship_Trophy) is awarded annually to the winning team of the [NBA Finals](/wiki/NBA_Finals), the league's championship series that concludes the playoffs.

The NBA playoffs begin in April after the conclusion of the regular season and [play-in tournament](/wiki/NBA_play-in_tournament) with the top eight teams in each conference, regardless of divisional alignment, competing for the league's championship title, the [Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy](/wiki/Larry_O%27Brien_Championship_Trophy). [Seeds](/wiki/Seeding_(sports)) are awarded in strict order of regular season record (with a tiebreaker system used as needed).

Having a higher seed offers several advantages. Since the first seed begins the playoffs playing against the eighth seed, the second seed plays the seventh seed, the third seed plays the sixth seed, and the fourth seed plays the fifth seed, having a higher seed typically means a team faces a weaker opponent in the first round. The team in each series with the better record has home-court advantage, including the First Round.

The league began using its current format, with the top eight teams in each conference advancing regardless of divisional alignment, in the 2015–16 season. Previously, the top three seeds went to the division winners.

The playoffs follow a tournament format. Each team plays an opponent in a best-of-seven series, with the first team to win four games advancing into the next round, while the other team is eliminated from the playoffs. In the next round, the successful team plays against another advancing team of the same conference. All but one team in each conference are eliminated from the playoffs. Since the NBA does not re-seed teams, the playoff bracket in each conference uses a traditional design, with the winner of the series matching the first- and eighth-seeded teams playing the winner of the series matching the fourth- and fifth-seeded teams, and the winner of the series matching the second- and seventh-seeded teams playing the winner of the series matching the third- and sixth-seeded teams. In every round, the best-of-7 series follows a 2–2–1–1–1 home-court pattern, meaning that one team will have home court in games 1, 2, 5, and 7, while the other plays at home in games 3, 4, and 6. From 1985 to 2013, the [NBA Finals](/wiki/NBA_Finals) followed a 2–3–2 pattern, meaning that one team had home court in games 1, 2, 6, and 7, while the other played at home in games 3, 4, and 5.

The final playoff round, a best-of-seven series between the victors of both conferences, is known as the [NBA Finals](/wiki/NBA_Finals) and is held annually in June (sometimes, the series will start in late May). The winner of the NBA Finals receives the [Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy](/wiki/Larry_O%27Brien_Championship_Trophy). Each player and major contributor—including coaches and the general manager—on the winning team receive a championship ring. In addition, the league awards the [Bill Russell NBA Finals Most Valuable Player Award](/wiki/Bill_Russell_NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) to the best performing player of the series.

## Media coverage

Main article: [NBA on television](/wiki/NBA_on_television)

See also: [List of current NBA broadcasters](/wiki/List_of_current_NBA_broadcasters)

As one of the major sports leagues in North America, the NBA has a long history of partnerships with television networks in the United States. The NBA signed a contract with [DuMont Television Network](/wiki/DuMont_Television_Network) in its eighth season, the [1953–54 season](/wiki/1953%E2%80%9354_NBA_season), marking the first year the NBA had a national television broadcaster. Similar to the [National Football League](/wiki/National_Football_League), the lack of television stations led to [NBC](/wiki/NBC) taking over the rights from the [1954–55 season](/wiki/1954-55_NBA_season) until April 1962–NBC's first tenure with the NBA. The [2025–26 season](/wiki/2025-26_NBA_season) marks the first year of 11-year agreements with broadcast networks [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) and NBC, pay television network [ESPN](/wiki/ESPN), and streaming services [Peacock](/wiki/Peacock_(streaming_service)) and [Amazon Prime Video](/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video) to nationally televise games in the United States. Games that are not broadcast nationally are usually aired over [regional sports networks](/wiki/Regional_sports_network) specific to the area where the teams are located.

In April 2026, [Reuters](/wiki/Reuters) and the National Basketball Association entered into a partnership to globally distribute game highlights and official league content, aiming to expand the NBA’s media reach and enhance its international revenue streams.

## Marketing and social responsibility

The NBA announced on April 15, 2016, that it would allow all 30 of its teams to sell corporate sponsor advertisement patches on official game uniforms, beginning with the 2017–18 season. The sponsorship advertisement patches would appear on the left front of jerseys, opposite [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.)'s logo, marking the first time a manufacturer's logo would appear on NBA jerseys, and would measure approximately 2.5 by 2.5 inches. The NBA would become the first [major North American professional sports league](/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) to allow corporate sponsorship logos on official team uniforms, and the last to have a uniform manufacturer logo appear on its team uniforms. The first team to announce a jersey sponsorship was the [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers), who agreed to a deal with [StubHub](/wiki/StubHub).

On July 6, 2017, the NBA unveiled an updated rendition of its logo; it was largely identical to the previous design, except with revised typography and a "richer" color scheme. The league began to phase in the updated logo across its properties during the [2017 NBA Summer League](/wiki/2017_NBA_Summer_League).

The NBA also officially released new [Nike](/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) uniforms for all 30 teams beginning with the 2017–18 season, replacing the previous supplier, [Adidas](/wiki/Adidas). All team jerseys included the Nike logo except for the [Charlotte Hornets](/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets), whose jerseys instead had the [Jumpman](/wiki/Jumpman_(logo)) logo associated with longtime Nike endorser [Michael Jordan](/wiki/Michael_Jordan), who owns the Hornets. In addition, the league eliminated "home" and "away" uniform designations. Instead, each team would have four or six uniforms: the "Association" edition, which is the team's white uniform, the "Icon" edition, which is the team's color uniform, and the "Statement" and "City" uniforms, which most teams use as an alternate uniform. In 2018, the NBA added the "Earned" uniform.

### NBA Cares

The league has a global social responsibility program, NBA Cares, that is responsible for the league's stated mission of addressing important social issues worldwide.

## International competitions

Main article: [List of games played between NBA and international teams](/wiki/List_of_games_played_between_NBA_and_international_teams)

The NBA has sporadically participated in international club competitions. The first international competition involving the NBA was a 1978 exhibition game in [Tel Aviv](/wiki/Tel_Aviv), Israel between the [Washington Bullets](/wiki/Washington_Bullets) and Israeli club [Maccabi Tel Aviv](/wiki/Maccabi_Tel_Aviv_B.C.). From 1987 to 1999 an NBA team played against championship club teams from Asia, Europe and South America in the [McDonald's Championship](/wiki/McDonald%27s_Championship). This tournament was won by the NBA invitee every year it was held.

The first regular season NBA league games in Europe took place in 2011. In two matchups, the [New Jersey Nets](/wiki/New_Jersey_Nets) faced the [Toronto Raptors](/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) at the [O2 Arena](/wiki/The_O2_Arena_(London)) in London in front of over 18,000 fans.

On March 10, 2025, NBA and Australia's [National Basketball League](/wiki/National_Basketball_League_(Australia)) (NBL) announced that in October 2025, the [New Orleans Pelicans](/wiki/New_Orleans_Pelicans) would play two preseason games at [Rod Laver Arena](/wiki/Rod_Laver_Arena) in [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne) as part of the [NBA x NBL: Melbourne Series](/wiki/NBA_x_NBL:_Melbourne_Series).

## Ticket prices and viewership demographics

In 2022, an average ticket cost $77.75. Depending on the market and stage of the season—preseason, regular season, postseason—a ticket can range from $10 to $100,000.

### Viewership demographics

According to Nielsen's survey, in 2013 the NBA had the youngest audience, with 45 percent of its viewers under 35. As of 2022, the league remains the least likely to be watched by women, who make up only 30% of the viewership. As of 2014, 45 percent of its viewers were [black](/wiki/African_Americans), while 40 percent of viewers were [white](/wiki/White_American), making it the only top North American sport that does not have a white majority audience.

As of 2017, the NBA's popularity further declined among white Americans, who during the 2016–17 season, made up only 34% of the viewership. At the same time, the black viewership increased to 47 percent, while Hispanic (of any race) stood at 11% and Asian viewership stood at 8%. According to the same poll, the NBA was favored more strongly by [Democrats](/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States)) than [Republicans](/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States)).

Outside the U.S., the NBA's biggest international market is in China, where an estimated 800 million viewers watched the [2017–18 season](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NBA_season). [NBA China](/wiki/Basketball_in_China) is worth approximately $4 billion.

## Criticisms and controversies

Main article: [NBA criticisms and controversies](/wiki/NBA_criticisms_and_controversies)

### Microfiber game ball

A [new official game ball](/wiki/NBA_criticisms_and_controversies#Microfiber_game_ball) was introduced on June 28, 2006, for the 2006–07 season, marking the first change to the ball in over 35 years and only the second ball in 60 seasons. Manufactured by [Spalding](/wiki/Spalding_(sports_equipment)), the new ball featured a new design and new synthetic material that Spalding claimed offered a better grip, feel, and consistency than the original ball. However, many players were vocal in their disdain for the new ball, saying that it was too sticky when dry, and too slippery when wet.

Commissioner [David Stern](/wiki/David_Stern) announced on December 11, 2006, that beginning January 1, 2007, the NBA would return to the traditional leather basketball in use prior to the 2006–07 season. The change was influenced by frequent player complaints and confirmed hand injuries (cuts) caused by the microfiber ball. The [Players' Association](/wiki/NBA_Players_Association) filed a suit on behalf of the players against the NBA over the new ball.

### Sports betting and illegal gambling

#### 2007 betting scandal

Main article: [2007 NBA betting scandal](/wiki/2007_NBA_betting_scandal)

The [Federal Bureau of Investigation](/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation) (FBI) began an investigation on July 19, 2007, over allegations that veteran NBA referee [Tim Donaghy](/wiki/Tim_Donaghy) bet on basketball games he officiated over the past two seasons and that he made calls affecting the [point spread](/wiki/Point_spread) in those games. On August 15, 2007, Donaghy pleaded guilty to two [federal charges related to the investigation](/wiki/2007_NBA_betting_scandal). Donaghy claimed in 2008 that certain referees were friendly with players and "company men" for the NBA, and he alleged that referees influenced the outcome of certain playoff and finals games in 2002 and 2005. Commissioner Stern denied the allegations and said Donaghy was a convicted felon and a "singing, cooperating witness". Donaghy served 15 months in prison and was released in November 2009.

According to an independent study by Ronald Beech of Game 6 of the [2002 Western Conference Finals](/wiki/2002_NBA_playoffs#Western_Conference_finals) between the Los Angeles Lakers and Sacramento Kings, although the refs increased the Lakers' chances of winning through foul calls during the game, there was no [collusion](/wiki/Collusion) to fix the game. On alleged "star treatment" during Game 6 by the referees toward certain players, Beech claimed, "there does seem to be issues with different standards and allowances for different players."

#### Sports betting

In 2018, Commissioner [Adam Silver](/wiki/Adam_Silver) showed support in the [Supreme Court's](/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States) decision to overturn a federal ban on [sports betting](/wiki/Sports_betting). Silver thought it would bring greater transparency and integrity as well as business opportunities. Before naming [DraftKings](/wiki/DraftKings) and [FanDuel](/wiki/FanDuel) co-official sports betting partners of the NBA in 2021, the NBA first named [MGM](/wiki/MGM_Resorts_International) as the exclusive official gaming partner of the NBA and WNBA—the first major American sports league to do so. With a deal between the 76ers and then-sportsbook FOX Bet as the first agreement between an NBA team and a sportsbook app, more teams partnered with operators thereafter. This early acceptance of sports betting translated to basketball being the most bet-on sport in the United States over football in 2023.

#### 2025 illegal gambling prosecution

Main article: [2025 NBA illegal gambling prosecution](/wiki/2025_NBA_illegal_gambling_prosecution)

On October 23, 2025, [Miami Heat](/wiki/Miami_Heat) player [Terry Rozier](/wiki/Terry_Rozier), [Portland Trail Blazers](/wiki/Portland_Trail_Blazers) head coach [Chauncey Billups](/wiki/Chauncey_Billups), and former player [Damon Jones](/wiki/Damon_Jones) were arrested by the FBI in connection with a wider federal investigation into [sports betting and gambling](/wiki/2025_NBA_illegal_gambling_prosecution) by various crime families of the [American Mafia](/wiki/American_Mafia).

### Tanking

The NBA has frequently struggled to mitigate [tanking](/wiki/Tanking), the practice of intentionally fielding non-competitive teams to secure a higher position in the upcoming [NBA draft](/wiki/NBA_draft), with many commentators describing the NBA's tanking problem as the worst out of all the [major North American professional sports leagues](/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada). In 2026, the [Utah Jazz](/wiki/Utah_Jazz) and [Indiana Pacers](/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) were fined $500,000 and $100,000, respectively, for deliberately benching healthy players with the intention of losing a game. Commissioner Silver has been especially critical of the practice, and the NBA plans to implement numerous changes starting with the [2026–27 season](/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_NBA_season) to discourage teams from deliberately losing games.

### Donald Sterling

[Donald Sterling](/wiki/Donald_Sterling), who was then-owner of the [Los Angeles Clippers](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Clippers), received a lifetime ban from the NBA on April 29, 2014, after racist remarks he made became public. Sterling was also fined US$2.5 million, the maximum allowed under the NBA Constitution.

### Partnerships

As part of its November 2021 multi-year partnership deal with the [United Arab Emirates](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates) (UAE), the NBA hosted two preseason games in Abu Dhabi on October 4 and 6, 2024, marking its third annual trip to the country. However, the [Human Rights Watch](/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch) (HRW) raised concerns, citing the UAE's pattern of using high-profile events to enhance its image. HRW accused the league of being complicit in "sportswashing" the UAE's poor human rights record, while the country seeks to display itself as open country, without addressing the abuses.

On September 30, HRW wrote a letter to the NBA, urging it to implement a human rights risk mitigation strategy, and to ensure that the preseason games were not used as a distraction from the UAE's human rights abuses. The rights organization also pointed out that the UAE hosted the games amidst the reports of the country being directly involved in fueling the [Sudanese civil war](/wiki/Sudanese_civil_war_(2023%E2%80%93present)). A coalition of human rights groups called upon the NBA to cancel the games in Abu Dhabi in solidarity with Sudanese.

#### Foreign players

Further information: [List of foreign NBA players](/wiki/List_of_foreign_NBA_players)

Following pioneers like [Vlade Divac](/wiki/Vlade_Divac) (Serbia) and [Dražen Petrović](/wiki/Dra%C5%BEen_Petrovi%C4%87) (Croatia), who joined the NBA in the late 1980s, an increasing number of international players have moved directly from playing elsewhere in the world to starring in the NBA. Since 2006, the NBA has faced [EuroLeague](/wiki/EuroLeague) teams [in exhibition matches](/wiki/NBA_vs._EuroLeague_games) in the [NBA Europe Live Tour](/wiki/NBA_Europe_Live_Tour), and since 2009, in the [EuroLeague American Tour](/wiki/EuroLeague_American_Tour). On November 9, 2007, when the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets) with [Yao Ming](/wiki/Yao_Ming) faced off against the [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) with [Yi Jianlian](/wiki/Yi_Jianlian), over 200 million people in China watched on 19 different networks, making it the most-viewed game in NBA history.

The [2013–14 season](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_NBA_season) opened with a record 92 international players on the opening night rosters, representing 39 countries and comprising over 20 percent of the league. The NBA defines "international" players as those born outside the 50 United States and Washington, D.C. This means that:

- Players born in U.S. territories such as Puerto Rico and the [U.S. Virgin Islands](/wiki/United_States_Virgin_Islands), most notably USVI native [Tim Duncan](/wiki/Tim_Duncan), are counted as "international" even though they are U.S. citizens by birth, and may even have represented the U.S. in international competition (like Duncan).
- U.S.-born players are not counted as "international" even if they were born with citizenship in another country and represent that country internationally, such as [Joakim Noah](/wiki/Joakim_Noah), and [Kosta Koufos](/wiki/Kosta_Koufos).

The beginning of the [2017–18 season](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NBA_season) saw a record 108 international players representing 42 countries marking 4 consecutive years of at least 100 international players and each team having at least one international player. In 2018, the [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/Phoenix_Suns) hired Serbian coach [Igor Kokoškov](/wiki/Igor_Koko%C5%A1kov) as their new head coach, replacing Canadian interim coach [Jay Triano](/wiki/Jay_Triano), making Kokoškov the first European coach to become a head coach for a team in the NBA.

In the [2023–24 season](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NBA_season), the Mavericks and the Thunder each had eight international players on their roster. For seven consecutive seasons from [2018–19](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_NBA_season) to [2024–25](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NBA_season), the league's [MVP award](/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) has been given to an international player.

- [Šarūnas Marčiulionis](/wiki/%C5%A0ar%C5%ABnas_Mar%C4%8Diulionis), Lithuania – 2014 inductee into the [Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame). - Vlade Divac, Serbia – 2019 inductee into the [Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame), two-time Olympic silver medalist, 2001 [NBA All-Star](/wiki/NBA_All-Star), two-time World champion, three-time European champion, 1989 Mr. Europa winner, 50 Greatest EuroLeague Contributors.
- [Toni Kukoč](/wiki/Toni_Kuko%C4%8D), Croatia – 2021 inductee into the [Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame), three-time NBA champion with the Chicago Bulls (1996, 1997, 1998), 1996 [Sixth Man Award](/wiki/NBA_Sixth_Man_of_the_Year_Award) winner, named in 2008 as one of the [50 Greatest EuroLeague Contributors](/wiki/50_Greatest_EuroLeague_Contributors).
- [Peja Stojaković](/wiki/Peja_Stojakovi%C4%87), Serbia – NBA champion with the Dallas Mavericks (2011), MVP of the [EuroBasket 2001](/wiki/EuroBasket_2001), member of the all-tournament team in the [2002 FIBA World Championship](/wiki/2002_FIBA_World_Championship), 2001 [Euroscar](/wiki/Euroscar) winner, two-time Mr. Europa winner, two-time [NBA Three-Point Shootout champion](/wiki/NBA_Three-Point_Shootout_champion), three-time NBA All-Star.
- [Dirk Nowitzki](/wiki/Dirk_Nowitzki), Germany – NBA champion with the Dallas Mavericks (2011), MVP of the [2002 FIBA World Championship](/wiki/2002_FIBA_World_Championship) and [EuroBasket 2005](/wiki/EuroBasket_2005), member of the all-tournament team in the [2002 FIBA World Championship](/wiki/2002_FIBA_World_Championship), six-time Euroscar winner, 2005 Mr. Europa, two-time [FIBA Europe Player of the Year](/wiki/FIBA_Europe_Player_of_the_Year), 2007 [NBA MVP](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association_Most_Valuable_Player_Award), 2011 [Bill Russell NBA Finals Most Valuable Player Award](/wiki/Bill_Russell_NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award), 2006 [NBA Three-Point Shootout champion](/wiki/NBA_Three-Point_Shootout_champion) and 14-time NBA All-Star.

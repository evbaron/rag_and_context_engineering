# List of Billboard Hot 100 number ones of 2002

Rapper [Eminem](/wiki/Eminem)'s "[Lose Yourself](/wiki/Lose_Yourself)" was the longest-running number-one single released in 2002.

The [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) is a chart that ranks the best-performing [singles](/wiki/Single_(music)) of the United States. Published by *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* magazine, the data are compiled by [Nielsen SoundScan](/wiki/Nielsen_SoundScan) based collectively on each single's weekly [physical](/wiki/CD_single) sales and [airplay](/wiki/Heavy_rotation). In [2002](/wiki/2002_in_music), there were seven singles that topped the chart, the lowest number of singles to top the chart in a single year ever (if the two songs which peaked in 2001 are included, 2002 would have the second lowest number of chart-topping singles in a year, behind [2005](/wiki/2005_in_music)). Although nine singles reached number one in fifty-two issues of the magazine in the calendar year, two songs began their peak position in 2001 and are thus excluded.

In 2002, five acts earned their first U.S. number one single, either as a lead artist or featured guest. These artists were [Ashanti](/wiki/Ashanti_(singer)), [Nelly](/wiki/Nelly), [Kelly Clarkson](/wiki/Kelly_Clarkson), and [Eminem](/wiki/Eminem). [Kelly Rowland](/wiki/Kelly_Rowland), despite having hit number one with [Destiny's Child](/wiki/Destiny%27s_Child), also earns her first number one song as a solo act. In 2002, [Ja Rule](/wiki/Ja_Rule), Ashanti, and Nelly had two number-one singles in the *Billboard* Hot 100.

Most of the number-one singles in 2002 were extended chart-toppers. "Lose Yourself" is the longest-running single, topping the *Billboard* Hot 100 for 12 consecutive weeks, eight of which were in this calendar year. "Foolish" and "Dilemma" both stayed at number one for 10 weeks, the latter of which was non-consecutive. "[Ain't It Funny](/wiki/Ain%27t_It_Funny_(Murder_Remix))" by [Jennifer Lopez](/wiki/Jennifer_Lopez), in its remix version with Ja Rule, peaked at number one for six weeks.

Rock band [Nickelback](/wiki/Nickelback)'s "[How You Remind Me](/wiki/How_You_Remind_Me)", which first peaked at number one in 2001, is the best-performing single of 2002. "Lose Yourself", which is the soundtrack to the 2002 film *[8 Mile](/wiki/8_Mile_(film))*, is the second most-successful [soundtrack](/wiki/Soundtrack) song in the entire rock era. It is behind [Whitney Houston](/wiki/Whitney_Houston)'s version of "[I Will Always Love You](/wiki/I_Will_Always_Love_You)", having topped the chart for 14 weeks. "Lose Yourself" is also the longest-running Oscar-winning number-one song since singer-actor [Bing Crosby](/wiki/Bing_Crosby)'s "[White Christmas](/wiki/White_Christmas_(song))" had 14 weeks on top in the 1940s. "A Moment Like This" is noted for its fifty-two-to-one leap in 2002, breaking the 38-year-old record set by [The Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles)' "[Can't Buy Me Love](/wiki/Can%27t_Buy_Me_Love)", which jumped from number twenty-seven to one.

Nelly became the first act to have consecutive number-one singles as the lead artist since [1994](/wiki/List_of_Hot_100_number-one_singles_of_1994_(U.S.)), when [Boyz II Men](/wiki/Boyz_II_Men) had consecutive number-ones.

## Chart history

Singer [Ashanti](/wiki/Ashanti_(entertainer))'s debut single "[Foolish](/wiki/Foolish_(Ashanti_song))" topped the chart for 10 straight weeks.

Rapper Nelly's "[Dilemma](/wiki/Dilemma_(Nelly_song))" stayed at the top spot for 10 weeks, tying with Ashanti's "Foolish".

Singer [Kelly Clarkson](/wiki/Kelly_Clarkson)'s debut single "[A Moment Like This](/wiki/A_Moment_Like_This)" is noted for its 52-to-one leap in 2002, breaking the 38-year-old record set by [The Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles)' "[Can't Buy Me Love](/wiki/Can%27t_Buy_Me_Love)", which jumped from number 27 to one.

**Roberto Martínez Felipe** (born 1 January 1970), better known as **Roberto Álamo**, is a Spanish actor who has appeared in more than fifty films since 1996. Álamo won the [Goya Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Goya_Award_for_Best_Actor) for his performance as Javier Alfaro in *[May God Save Us](/wiki/May_God_Save_Us)* (2016).

## Life and career

Roberto Martínez Felipe was born in [Madrid](/wiki/Madrid) in 1970. He developed an interest in cinema at a young age after watching [Jack Lemmon](/wiki/Jack_Lemmon) in *[Days of Wine and Roses](/wiki/Days_of_Wine_and_Roses_(film))*. He trained at the [Cristina Rota](/wiki/Cristina_Rota) acting school and was a member of theatre company Animalario. He made his feature film debut in *[The Ugliest Woman in the World](/wiki/The_Ugliest_Woman_in_the_World)* (1999).

The theatre play *Urtain* marked a turning point in his career. Álamo starred from 2008 to 2010 in the aforementioned play as the Basque boxer [José Manuel Urtain](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Urtain), winning a [Max Award](/wiki/Premios_Max) for Best Actor for his performance.

His portrayal of Alfaro, a violent police inspector under the spotlight of their superiors and colleagues chasing a rapist and murderer of elderly women, in [Rodrigo Sorogoyen](/wiki/Rodrigo_Sorogoyen)'s thriller film *[May God Save Us](/wiki/May_God_Save_Us)* (2016) won him the [Goya Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Goya_Award_for_Best_Actor).

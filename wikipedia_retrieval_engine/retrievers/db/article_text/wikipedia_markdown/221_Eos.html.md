**221 Eos** is a large [main-belt](/wiki/Asteroid_belt) [asteroid](/wiki/Asteroid) that was discovered by Austrian astronomer [Johann Palisa](/wiki/Johann_Palisa) on January 18, 1882, in [Vienna](/wiki/Vienna). In 1884, it was named after [Eos](/wiki/Eos), the [Greek](/wiki/Greek_mythology) [goddess of the dawn](/wiki/Dawn_deities), to honour the opening of a new [observatory](/wiki/Observatory) that was hoped to bring about a new dawn for Viennese astronomy.

The asteroid is orbiting the [Sun](/wiki/Sun) with a [semimajor axis](/wiki/Semimajor_axis) of 3.01 [AU](/wiki/Astronomical_unit), a [period](/wiki/Orbital_period) of 5.22 years, and an [eccentricity](/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity) of 0.1. The [orbital plane](/wiki/Orbital_plane) is inclined by 10.9° to the [plane of the ecliptic](/wiki/Plane_of_the_ecliptic). It has a mean cross-section of 104 km, and is spinning with a [rotation period](/wiki/Rotation_period) of 10.4 hours. Based upon its spectral characteristics, this object is classified as a [K-type asteroid](/wiki/K-type_asteroid). The orbital properties show it to be a member of the extensive [Eos asteroid family](/wiki/Eos_family), which is named after it. The spectral properties of the asteroid suggest it may have come from a [partially differentiated](/wiki/Planetary_differentiation) parent body.

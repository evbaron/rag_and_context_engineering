**Laurence Paul Fox** (born 26 May 1978) is an English actor, [right-wing](/wiki/Right-wing) political activist and leader of the [Reclaim Party](/wiki/Reclaim_Party). A member of the British entertainment industry's [Fox family](/wiki/Robin_Fox_family), he graduated from the [Royal Academy of Dramatic Art](/wiki/Royal_Academy_of_Dramatic_Art) and debuted in the film *[The Hole](/wiki/The_Hole_(2001_film))* (2001). His best-known role is [James Hathaway](/wiki/Sergeant_Hathaway) in the TV drama series *[Lewis](/wiki/Lewis_(TV_series))* (2006–2015).

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom), Fox was a frequent critic of the [British government's response](/wiki/British_government_response_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic). He was also publicly critical of the [George Floyd protests](/wiki/George_Floyd_protests) that began in 2020. After founding the [right-wing populist](/wiki/Right-wing_populism) political party [Reclaim](/wiki/Reclaim_Party), he stood unsuccessfully in the [2021 London mayoral election](/wiki/2021_London_mayoral_election) in opposition to what he deemed "extreme [political correctness](/wiki/Political_correctness)". He subsequently stood unsuccessfully in the [2024 London Assembly election](/wiki/2024_London_Assembly_election). Fox's political career has resulted in multiple controversies and legal disputes.

## Early life and education

Laurence Paul Fox was born in [Leeds](/wiki/Leeds) on 26 May 1978, the son of Mary Elizabeth Piper and actor [James Fox](/wiki/James_Fox). His paternal grandfather was talent agent [Robin Fox](/wiki/Robin_Fox_(theatrical_agent)) and his paternal grandmother was actress Angela Worthington, whose father was playwright [Frederick Lonsdale](/wiki/Frederick_Lonsdale). He has two older brothers named Tom and Robin, a younger sister named Lydia, and a younger brother named [Jack](/wiki/Jack_Fox_(actor)). Robin is a film producer, while Lydia and Jack are actors; Lydia is married to comedian and filmmaker [Richard Ayoade](/wiki/Richard_Ayoade). Fox's uncles are actors [Edward Fox](/wiki/Edward_Fox_(actor)) and producer [Robert Fox](/wiki/Robert_Fox_(producer)). He is also the cousin of actors [Emilia](/wiki/Emilia_Fox) and [Freddie Fox](/wiki/Freddie_Fox_(actor)), the children of his uncle Edward.

Fox was raised as an [evangelical Christian](/wiki/Evangelicalism). At the age of 13, he was enrolled at [Harrow School](/wiki/Harrow_School). He was expelled a few weeks before taking his [A-Level](/wiki/Advanced_Level_(UK)) exams, and was unable to obtain a place at any university due to a report about him from Harrow.

After working as a gardener and in an office, he decided to follow his family into acting, and successfully auditioned for the [Royal Academy of Dramatic Art](/wiki/Royal_Academy_of_Dramatic_Art) (RADA). During his time there, he appeared in numerous student productions, including the lead roles of Gregers Werle in [Ibsen](/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen)'s *[The Wild Duck](/wiki/The_Wild_Duck)*, Marcus Andronicus in *[Titus Andronicus](/wiki/Titus_Andronicus)*, and [Stephen Daedalus](/wiki/Stephen_Daedalus) in an adaptation of [James Joyce](/wiki/James_Joyce)'s novel *[Ulysses](/wiki/Ulysses_(novel))*. He graduated in 2001.

## Acting career

Fox made his acting debut in the horror-thriller film *[The Hole](/wiki/The_Hole_(2001_film))* (2001). He next appeared in [Robert Altman](/wiki/Robert_Altman)'s film *[Gosford Park](/wiki/Gosford_Park)* (2001). He then donned uniforms in a slew of film and television features, including roles as a German airman in *[Island at War](/wiki/Island_at_War)* (2004), an [SS](/wiki/Schutzstaffel) officer in *[The Last Drop](/wiki/The_Last_Drop)* (2005), and as British soldiers in the 2002 films *[Deathwatch](/wiki/Deathwatch_(2002_film))* and *[Ultimate Force](/wiki/Ultimate_Force)*, and in *[Colditz](/wiki/Colditz_(2005_TV_series))* (2005). In the last made-for-television film, Fox played Capt. Tom Willis who, after an unsuccessful attempt to break out of a [prisoner-of-war camp](/wiki/Prisoner-of-war_camp), is brought to [Oflag IV-C](/wiki/Oflag_IV-C) in [Colditz Castle](/wiki/Colditz_Castle), one of the most infamous [German](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) POW camps for officers in [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II). Actor [Kevin Whately](/wiki/Kevin_Whately) caught Fox's performance in the last ten minutes of the film. The next day, at a meeting regarding a new project, Whately mentioned that Fox "would be worth taking a look at".

As a result, Fox was cast in the [ITV](/wiki/ITV_Network) detective drama *[Lewis](/wiki/Lewis_(TV_series))* as [Detective Sergeant James Hathaway](/wiki/Detective_Sergeant_James_Hathaway), who becomes the partner of [Detective Inspector](/wiki/Inspector) [Robert Lewis](/wiki/Inspector_Lewis), played by Whately. The pilot of this spin-off from *[Inspector Morse](/wiki/Inspector_Morse_(TV_series))* (1987–2000) was ITV's highest-rated drama of 2006.

Fox has portrayed [Prince Charles](/wiki/Charles,_Prince_of_Wales), in *Whatever Love Means* (2005); Wisley, one of [Jane Austen](/wiki/Jane_Austen)'s suitors, in *[Becoming Jane](/wiki/Becoming_Jane)* (2007); and Sir [Christopher Hatton](/wiki/Christopher_Hatton), the [Lord Chancellor](/wiki/Lord_Chancellor) of England, in *[Elizabeth: The Golden Age](/wiki/Elizabeth:_The_Golden_Age)*, also released in 2007. In the same year Fox was seen on ITV as Cecil Vyse in [Andrew Davies](/wiki/Andrew_Davies_(writer))'s adaptation of *[A Room with a View](/wiki/A_Room_with_a_View_(2007_film))* based on [E. M. Forster](/wiki/E._M._Forster)'s [1908 novel](/wiki/A_Room_with_a_View).

On stage, Fox appeared in *[Mrs. Warren's Profession](/wiki/Mrs._Warren%27s_Profession)* by [George Bernard Shaw](/wiki/George_Bernard_Shaw) at the Strand Theatre (now the [Novello Theatre](/wiki/Novello_Theatre)) in London in 2002, and [John Ford](/wiki/John_Ford_(dramatist))'s 17th-century play *['Tis Pity She's a Whore](/wiki/%27Tis_Pity_She%27s_a_Whore)* in 2005. Between 2006 and 2007 he starred in *[Treats](/wiki/Treats_(play))* by [Christopher Hampton](/wiki/Christopher_Hampton) with his future wife, [Billie Piper](/wiki/Billie_Piper). In April 2007, Fox received a [police caution](/wiki/Police_caution) after he was arrested for assault when he punched a photographer outside the [Garrick Theatre](/wiki/Garrick_Theatre) in London where he was performing in *Treats*. The caution remained on his [record](/wiki/Criminal_record) for three years. In 2013, Fox played Guy Haines in *[Strangers on a Train](/wiki/Strangers_on_a_Train_(play))* at [London](/wiki/London)'s [Gielgud Theatre](/wiki/Gielgud_Theatre). On 9 May 2015, he read a letter written by a soldier three days before his death in the Second World War, as part of *[VE Day 70: A Party to Remember](/wiki/VE_Day_70:_A_Party_to_Remember)*, an anniversary concert for [VE Day](/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day).

In 2018, Fox joined the cast of the ITV series *[Victoria](/wiki/Victoria_(UK_TV_series))*, for its third season, playing [Lord Palmerston](/wiki/Henry_John_Temple,_3rd_Viscount_Palmerston).

In November 2020, Fox was dropped by his talent agency, Artists Rights Group, after stating on *[Question Time](/wiki/Question_Time_(TV_programme))* that an audience member's description of him as a "[white privileged](/wiki/White_privilege) male" was "racist". He had been dropped by his previous managers, Authentic Talent, earlier in the year.

In 2021, Fox portrayed [Hunter Biden](/wiki/Hunter_Biden) in filmmaker [Robert Davi](/wiki/Robert_Davi)'s biopic *[My Son Hunter](/wiki/My_Son_Hunter)*, starring alongside fellow conservatives [Gina Carano](/wiki/Gina_Carano) and [John James](/wiki/John_James_(actor)), the former of whom was also dropped by her agent after allegedly expressing some of the same opinions as Fox. The film was distributed by the American right-wing media company [Breitbart](/wiki/Breitbart_News).

## Other ventures

### Music

Fox has released two albums, *Holding Patterns* in 2016 and *A Grief Observed* in 2019. The former charted, at no. 89, but the latter did not.

### Broadcasting

From November 2022, Fox presented a regular Friday night slot on [GB News](/wiki/GB_News).

In September 2023, during an appearance on [Dan Wootton](/wiki/Dan_Wootton)'s GB News show, Fox said of journalist Ava Evans: "Show me a single self-respecting man that would like to climb into bed with that woman ever [...] That little woman has been fed, spoon-fed oppression day after day after day." He concluded by saying: "Who'd want to shag that?" Fox and Wootton, along with [Calvin Robinson](/wiki/Calvin_Robinson), were suspended by the channel. Fox released on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter) an apparent private message exchange with Wootton in which he had reacted approvingly to what Fox had said. In early October 2023, GB News confirmed that, following its investigation, it had terminated the contracts of Fox and Robinson. [Ofcom](/wiki/Ofcom), the government's broadcasting regulatory body, opened an investigation into the incident. Ofcom announced that the incident was the most complained-about broadcast on British television in 2023, with the regulator receiving 8,867 complaints. On 4 March 2024 Ofcom ruled that Fox's "misogynistic" comments broke broadcasting rules and "were degrading and demeaning both to [her] and women generally".

## Personal views

### Diversity in acting

In 2016, Fox criticised [Julie Walters](/wiki/Julie_Walters) and [Christopher Eccleston](/wiki/Christopher_Eccleston) for speaking about a lack of working-class voices in acting. He said: "I think people should keep pretty quiet about stuff like that especially given the money they earn. It's not completely true either. And didn't Julie Walters get paid 400 grand using taxpayers' money to do something recently? She's a really sweet woman but, please, shut up."

In 2020, Fox told [James Delingpole](/wiki/James_Delingpole) in a podcast: "The most annoying thing is the minute a black actor — it's the same with working-class actors — the minute they've got five million quid in the bank, every interview they do is about how racism is rampant and rife in the industry."

### Political correctness

In a November 2019 interview with *[The Times](/wiki/The_Times)* to promote his second album, Fox said [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) videos had "totally radicalised" him against "*[woke](/wiki/Woke)* culture" and "[political correctness](/wiki/Political_correctness)".

### COVID-19

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom), Fox criticised the [British government's response](/wiki/British_government_response_to_the_COVID-19_pandemic) to the pandemic and encouraged people to disregard the government's [social distancing](/wiki/Social_distancing) guidance and disobey other public health restrictions. During an interview on *[Good Morning Britain](/wiki/Good_Morning_Britain_(2014_TV_programme))*, he said that "if the [NHS](/wiki/National_Health_Service) can't cope, then the NHS isn't fit for purpose". Hosts [Piers Morgan](/wiki/Piers_Morgan) and [Susanna Reid](/wiki/Susanna_Reid) censured this. During a national [lockdown](/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns) in March 2021, Fox participated in an [anti-lockdown protest](/wiki/COVID-19_anti-lockdown_protests_in_the_United_Kingdom).

### Race and racism

Appearing as a panellist on the BBC's political debate programme *[Question Time](/wiki/Question_Time_(TV_programme))* in January 2020, Fox said that [Meghan Markle](/wiki/Meghan_Markle) was not a victim of racism and described an audience member who called him a "white privileged male" as racist. The British actors' union [Equity](/wiki/Equity_(British_trade_union)) called him "a disgrace to our industry" for his views, but withdrew its criticism and apologised two months later.

In January 2020, Fox attracted media attention for stating that the depiction of a [Sikh](/wiki/Sikhs) soldier in the film *[1917](/wiki/1917_(2019_film))* was "forced diversity" in spite of [Sikh soldiers](/wiki/Sikhs_in_the_British_Indian_Army) having fought on the Western Front in the [First World War](/wiki/First_World_War). When interviewed, he explained, "I suppose it would have been less incongruous to me if he'd got on the truck to a whole regiment of Sikh soldiers. [...] I mean, as you've noticed, I say quite a lot of unfortunate things, but I think it's really important that one is able to express one's opinion." He followed by apologising on Twitter to "fellow humans who are Sikhs" and wrote, "I am as moved by the sacrifices your relatives made as I am by the loss of all those who die in war, whatever creed or colour. Please accept my apology for being clumsy in the way I expressed myself."

In August 2021, Fox posted a tweet stating "get [kneeling](/wiki/Taking_the_knee), fuckers" about the recent arrest of black footballer [Benjamin Mendy](/wiki/Benjamin_Mendy) on charges of rape and sexual assault. The tweet was removed by Twitter and the account was temporarily locked for violating its rules against "hateful conduct".

In August 2023, Fox posted a photo of himself in [blackface](/wiki/Blackface), describing himself as having "racially transitioned".

### Progress pride flag

In June 2022, Fox [tweeted](/wiki/Twitter) an image of a [swastika](/wiki/Swastika) made from the [Progress Pride flag](/wiki/Rainbow_flag_(LGBT)) with the caption, "You can openly call the [[Union Flag](/wiki/Union_Jack)] a symbol of fascism and totalitarianism on Twatter [*[sic](/wiki/Sic)*]. You cannot criticise the holy flags." This led to his being suspended from Twitter for a day. His actions were publicly condemned by the [Holocaust Memorial Day Trust](/wiki/Holocaust_Memorial_Day_Trust) and the [Campaign Against Antisemitism](/wiki/Campaign_Against_Antisemitism).

## Political activity

### Reclaim Party

Main article: [Reclaim Party](/wiki/Reclaim_Party)

After becoming "a prominent right-wing commentator" around the time of the pandemic, in 2020, Fox attracted funding for a new political party, provisionally called Reclaim, and dubbed "[UKIP](/wiki/UK_Independence_Party) for culture". In October 2020, Fox became leader of the party, succeeding Jeremy Hosking.

It emerged in October 2020 that the party name had yet to be successfully registered with the [Electoral Commission](/wiki/Electoral_Commission_(United_Kingdom)) and that there was a naming conflict with the "Reclaim Project" of Manchester, an established charity in Manchester endeavouring to give opportunities to working-class children. The name Reclaim Party was approved in February 2021 as an identity mark for [Brexit Express](/wiki/Brexit_Express).

In May 2023, the Reclaim Party gained its first MP, [Andrew Bridgen](/wiki/Andrew_Bridgen), after he was expelled from the governing [Conservative Party](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)). Bridgen resigned from the party in December 2023 due to a "difference in the direction of the party".

In July 2023, Fox stood in the [2023 Uxbridge and South Ruislip by-election](/wiki/2023_Uxbridge_and_South_Ruislip_by-election) as a Reclaim Party candidate. He finished fourth with 2.3% of the vote, [losing his deposit](/wiki/Election_deposit).

### 2021 candidacy for London mayor

In March 2021, Fox announced he would stand in the [London mayoral election](/wiki/2021_London_mayoral_election), in order to "fight against extreme political correctness" and pledging to "end the [Met](/wiki/Metropolitan_Police)'s obsession with diversity and inclusivity."

The major source of Fox's campaign funds was Brexit backer [Jeremy Hosking](/wiki/Jeremy_Hosking), who, in the first quarter of 2021, gave the Reclaim Party more than £1,000,000 in cash and services. Fox finished in sixth place with 47,634 votes (1.9%) in the mayoral election losing his £10,000 [election deposit](/wiki/Election_deposit).

### 2024 London elections

Fox attempted to stand in [2024 London mayoral election](/wiki/2024_London_mayoral_election) but failed to fill in the nomination forms correctly. The election authorities returned Fox's deposit and other fees.

He was a candidate on the London-wide list in the [2024 London Assembly election](/wiki/2024_London_Assembly_election), though he stood under 'no description', rather than for Reclaim. He received 13,795 out of a total 2,476,687 votes (0.56%) and was not elected, losing his £5,000 deposit.

## Legal issues

### 2021 Blake, Seymour and Thorp v Fox defamation and libel lawsuit

In October 2020, Fox announced he would boycott the supermarket [Sainsbury's](/wiki/Sainsbury%27s) because they "support racial segregation and discrimination", making reference to the store establishing "[safe spaces](/wiki/Safe_space)" for black employees, while asking others to do the same. Sainsbury's later clarified that the safe spaces were online support groups established in response to [Black Lives Matter](/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) and were promoted as part of support for [Black History Month](/wiki/Black_History_Month). Feeling he was "falsely smeared as a racist", Fox replied to a number of tweets reacting to that announcement by calling their authors [paedophiles](/wiki/Pedophilia). Two of those people, *[RuPaul's Drag Race UK](/wiki/RuPaul%27s_Drag_Race_UK)* contestant [Crystal](/wiki/Crystal_(drag_queen)) (Colin Seymour) and Simon Blake, deputy chair of the LGBT rights charity [Stonewall](/wiki/Stonewall_(charity)), both gay men, later announced they would sue Fox for defamation. Fox deleted the tweets and explained in further tweets that he wanted to teach people a lesson in calling people something which they are not.

In April 2021, Crystal and Blake lodged a claim for [defamation](/wiki/English_defamation_law) in the [High Court](/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice) and were joined in the legal action by actress [Nicola Thorp](/wiki/Nicola_Thorp), whom Fox also called a paedophile. In response, Fox filed a countersuit over the accusations of racism. In December 2021, judge [Barbara Fontaine](/wiki/Barbara_Fontaine) urged the protagonists to reach a compromise; Fox's lawyers had estimated his legal costs would be between £360,000 and £500,000 for a full trial.

In April 2022, Fox requested a jury trial and said that "a judge could show involuntary bias", the first such request since the [Defamation Act 2013](/wiki/Defamation_Act_2013). It was refused. Court documents revealed that this request cost Fox legal fees of more than £116,000. Later that month the High Court ruled that Fox must pay more than £36,000 in legal fees to Blake, Seymour and Thorp.

The trial progressed at the High Court to decisions on preliminary issues such as the "natural and ordinary" meanings of the tweets, which included that they were a factual claim that "Ms Thorp was a paedophile". Fox took this decision to the [Court of Appeal](/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_(England_and_Wales)), who ruled in August 2023 that they were "satisfied that the ordinary reasonable reader of that tweet would not have taken the word [paedophile] literally", but dismissed Fox's other challenges.

The libel trial and trials for counterclaims were heard between 22 November and 1 December 2023 at the High Court by [Mrs Justice Collins Rice](/wiki/Rowena_Collins_Rice) as judge, with Blake and Seymour as claimants, against Fox as defendant and counterclaimant, and Thorp as defendant to the counterclaim.

On 29 January 2024 a judgment was given with Justice Collins Rice ruling in favour of the claimants. The judge ruled that Fox's labelling of Crystal and Blake as paedophiles was "seriously harmful, defamatory and baseless" and Fox's tweets "the very epitome of 'mere retaliation' – an escalatory and disproportionate response by way of entirely irrelevant statements." In ruling against Fox, the judge summarised that "the law affords few defences to defamation of this sort. Mr Fox did not attempt to show these allegations were true, and he was not able to bring himself on the facts within the terms of any other defence recognised in law."

Fox's counterclaims of defamation against Crystal, Blake and Thorp were also dismissed. Whilst the judge did not make a judgement on whether or not describing Fox as "a racist" was "substantially true", the judge ruled that the expression of such opinions were unlikely to significantly damage his reputation and that there were multiple other probable causes to any reputational damage. The judge summarised that "Mr Fox did not attempt to show these allegations were true, and he was not able to bring himself on the facts within the terms of any other defence recognised in law".

On 25 April 2024, it was announced that Fox would have to pay a total of £180,000 in compensatory damages to Crystal and Blake. Fox appealed the verdict, and in October 2025 the [Court of Appeal](/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_(England_and_Wales)) reduced the sum of damages to £90,000 and ordered the case to be [re-tried](/wiki/New_trial).

### 2023 ULEZ protest arrest

On 4 October 2023, it was reported that Fox had been arrested by police on suspicion of conspiring to commit criminal damage to [ULEZ](/wiki/Ultra_Low_Emission_Zone) cameras and encouraging or assisting offences to be committed. He was reported as having said on the previous day in an interview on [Rumble](/wiki/Rumble_(company)) that he declared support for a ULEZ vigilante group, saying: "I encourage them to tear down every single camera there is and I will be joining them [...] I am pretty close with several and I will be out there with my angle grinder."

In May 2025, it was reported that Fox had been charged with [intentionally encouraging or assisting the commission of an offence](/wiki/Encouraging_or_assisting_a_crime_in_English_law), and was expected to appear in court to answer the charge.

### 2024 Olympics defamation complaint

During the [opening ceremonies](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony) of the [2024 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in Paris on 26 July, Fox commented on a segment featuring French drag performer and host of *[Drag Race France](/wiki/Drag_Race_France)* [Nicky Doll](/wiki/Nicky_Doll), along with other French drag performers, calling them "deviant little [pedos](/wiki/Pedophilia)" and "child fuckers" on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter). Nicky Doll responded the following day on Twitter saying "Lawyer on the line, see you in court!" Doll later posted on 2 August that he had filed a [defamation complaint](/wiki/Defamation) against Fox and other Twitter users through lawyer Anne-Sophie Laguens and NGO STOP Homophobie.

### 2024 *Fox v Yassin* libel lawsuit

On 31 January 2024, a hearing was held at the High Court whereby Fox took legal action against a man who called him a "racist" on social media. Fox sued Mukhtar Ali Yassin for libel over a row on X (formerly known as [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter)) over four tweets sent during the exchange in May 2023. Ben Gallop, representing Fox, told the court that Yassin had made "seriously defamatory allegations of racism against my client that are bare comments". Judge Mrs Justice Collins Rice ruled that a hearing should take place to decide whether the posts were statements of fact or opinion, and what should be included in any future trial. Fox said in March 2024 that he had discontinued the case; Yassin said Fox had agreed to its dismissal, paying thousands of pounds of legal fees to do so.

### 2024 upskirt photo incident

On 3 May 2024, multiple news sources reported that Fox was under investigation by police for allegedly sharing an [upskirt](/wiki/Upskirt) photo of the broadcaster [Narinder Kaur](/wiki/Narinder_Kaur) on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter). Fox said he "would like to apologise" to Kaur, stating it was not his fault that Kaur was pictured in the compromising image more than 15 years ago. On 25 March 2025, Fox was charged with a sexual offence in relation to an alleged breach of section 66A of the [Sexual Offences Act 2003](/wiki/Sexual_Offences_Act_2003), with Fox alleged to have intentionally shared sexual images of someone without their consent, with the aim of causing alarm, distress, or humiliation, or for sexual gratification. Fox responded by describing the allegations as "untrue, ridiculous, vexatious and malicious nonsense", stating: "This is yet another deeply concerning example of the two-tier British justice system in all its Soviet glory."

## Personal life

Fox met actress [Billie Piper](/wiki/Billie_Piper) while they were performing together in the play *Treats* in 2006. They started dating soon after, and were married on 31 December 2007. Fox and Piper have two sons. They divorced in May 2016. In 2024, Piper commented on the divorce in *[British Vogue](/wiki/British_Vogue)*, where she expressed the desire for her children to have privacy and anonymity, and the difficulty of this given Fox's public profile. Fox disputed some of Piper's claims. He also said that the pair had been to court many times since their split and criticised the family court system as biased towards the mother.

In June 2025, Fox married his long-term girlfriend, Elizabeth Barker.

In an October 2012 *[Independent](/wiki/The_Independent)* interview, Fox described himself as a "vaguely lapsed [Christian](/wiki/Christianity)" who occasionally prays and thinks "the world is a better place for people who believe in God" despite not having "[squared that circle](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/square_the_circle)" himself.

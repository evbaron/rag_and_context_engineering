In this [Spanish name](/wiki/Spanish_name), the first or paternal [surname](/wiki/Surname) is  *Guardiola* and the second or maternal family name is  *Sala*.

**Josep** "**Pep**" **Guardiola Sala** (Catalan pronunciation: [[ˈpɛb ɡwəɾðiˈɔlə]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Catalan); born 18 January 1971) is a [Catalan](/wiki/Catalans) [football manager](/wiki/Manager_(association_football)) and former [player](/wiki/Football_player) from [Spain](/wiki/Spain) who is the manager of [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League) club [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.). Guardiola is one of two managers in history to win the [continental treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football)#Continental_trebles) twice and he holds the record for the most consecutive league games won in [La Liga](/wiki/La_Liga), [Bundesliga](/wiki/Bundesliga), and the Premier League. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest managers of all time.

Guardiola was a [defensive midfielder](/wiki/Defensive_midfielder) who usually played in a [deep-lying playmaker](/wiki/Deep-lying_playmaker)'s role. He spent the majority of his career with [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona), forming a part of [Johan Cruyff](/wiki/Johan_Cruyff)'s *[Dream Team](/wiki/FC_Barcelona#Dream_Team)* that won the club's first [European Cup](/wiki/European_Cup) in [1992](/wiki/1992_European_Cup_final), and four successive [Spanish league titles](/wiki/List_of_Spanish_football_champions) from 1991 to 1994. He captained the team from 1997 until his departure from Barcelona in 2001. Guardiola then had stints with [Brescia](/wiki/Brescia_Calcio) and [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma) in Italy, [Al-Ahli](/wiki/Al-Ahli_(Doha)) in Qatar and [Dorados](/wiki/Dorados_de_Sinaloa) in Mexico. He was [capped](/wiki/Cap_(association_football)) 47 times for the [Spanish national team](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) and appeared at the [1994 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), as well as at [UEFA Euro 2000](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000). He also played friendly matches for [Catalonia](/wiki/Catalonia_national_football_team).

After retiring as a player, Guardiola briefly managed [Barcelona B](/wiki/FC_Barcelona_B) and won the [Tercera División](/wiki/Tercera_Divisi%C3%B3n) title before taking charge of the first team in 2008. In his [first season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_FC_Barcelona_season), Guardiola led Barcelona to the [continental treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football)) of La Liga, the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/Copa_del_Rey) and the UEFA Champions League, becoming the youngest manager to win the latter competition. He was named the [FIFA World Coach of the Year](/wiki/FIFA_World_Coach_of_the_Year) in 2011 after leading the club to another La Liga and Champions League [double](/wiki/Double_(association_football)#European_double) in the [2010–11 season](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_FC_Barcelona_season). Guardiola ended his four-year Barcelona stint in 2012 with 14 honours, [a club record](/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_records_and_statistics#Managerial_records).

Guardiola joined [Bayern Munich](/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in 2013 and won the Bundesliga in each of the three seasons, including two [domestic doubles](/wiki/Double_(association_football)#Germany). He left the club for Manchester City in 2016 and has since won six Premier League titles, including four successive wins from 2019 to 2024, marking the first time in the history of the English top flight that a team has done so. His first title in his [second season](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Manchester_City_F.C._season) in charge broke numerous domestic records as the team became the first to [attain 100 points in a single season](/wiki/Premier_League_records_and_statistics#Points). He also led City to a [domestic treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football)#Domestic_trebles) in [2018–19](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Manchester_City_F.C._season). Guardiola guided City to their first [Champions League final](/wiki/2021_UEFA_Champions_League_final) in [2020–21](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Manchester_City_F.C._season), and their first [Champions League title](/wiki/2023_UEFA_Champions_League_final) as part of his second continental treble in [2022–23](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Manchester_City_F.C._season). He is the club's [second-longest serving manager](/wiki/List_of_Manchester_City_F.C._managers) and is the [longest-serving current manager in the top four divisions](/wiki/List_of_current_Premier_League_and_English_Football_League_managers).

## Club career

### 1988–2001: Barcelona

Born in [Santpedor](/wiki/Santpedor), Barcelona, Catalonia, Guardiola joined [La Masia](/wiki/La_Masia) at age 13 from [Gimnàstic Manresa](/wiki/Club_Gimn%C3%A0stic_de_Manresa) and rose through the ranks of Barcelona's youth academy for six years, making his debut in 1990 against [Cádiz](/wiki/C%C3%A1diz_CF). As [Phil Ball](/wiki/Phil_Ball_(writer)) writes in *Morbo*,

> In his first week at the club, [Johan Cruyff](/wiki/Johan_Cruyff) turned up unannounced at the [Mini Estadi](/wiki/Mini_Estadi), a venue just down the road from [Camp Nou](/wiki/Camp_Nou) used by [Barcelona B](/wiki/FC_Barcelona_B). Just before half-time he wandered into the dug-out and asked [Charly Rexach](/wiki/Carles_Rexach), the youth team manager at the time, the name of the young lad playing on the [right side of midfield](/wiki/Right_midfielder). "Guardiola – good lad," came the reply. Cruyff ignored the comment and told Rexach to move him into the middle for the second half, to play as [pivot](/wiki/Defensive_midfielder). It was a difficult position to adapt to and one not used by many teams in Spain at the time. Guardiola adjusted immediately, as Cruyff had suspected he would, and when he moved to the first-team in 1990, he became the pivot of the [Dream Team](/wiki/FC_Barcelona#Dream_Team).

21-year-old Guardiola (right), pictured with FC Barcelona teammates [Guillermo Amor](/wiki/Guillermo_Amor), [Albert Ferrer](/wiki/Albert_Ferrer), and club vice-president [Josep Mussons](/wiki/Josep_Mussons), in 1992

Guardiola became a first-team regular in the [1991–92 season](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_La_Liga), and at only 20 years old was a key component of a side that won [La Liga](/wiki/List_of_Spanish_football_champions) and the [European Cup](/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_European_Cup). The Italian magazine *Guerin Sportivo* heralded Guardiola as the finest player in the world under the age of 21. Cruyff's "Dream Team" went on to retain La Liga title in the [1992–93](/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_La_Liga) and [1993–94 seasons](/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_La_Liga). The side again reached the [1994 UEFA Champions League final](/wiki/1994_UEFA_Champions_League_final), but were beaten 4–0 by [Fabio Capello](/wiki/Fabio_Capello)'s [AC Milan](/wiki/A.C._Milan) side in [Athens](/wiki/Athens). Cruyff left in 1996, with Barcelona finishing fourth in the [1994–95 season](/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_La_Liga) and third in the [1995–96 season](/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_La_Liga), but Guardiola retained his position at the centre of Barcelona's midfield.

In the [1996–97 season](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_FC_Barcelona_season), Barcelona, this time led by [Bobby Robson](/wiki/Bobby_Robson), won three cups: the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_Copa_del_Rey), the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/1996_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), and the [European Cup Winners' Cup](/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_UEFA_Cup_Winners%27_Cup). In 1997, Guardiola was named as Barcelona [captain](/wiki/Captain_(association_football)) under new manager [Louis van Gaal](/wiki/Louis_van_Gaal), but a calf muscle injury ruled Guardiola out of most of the [1997–98 season](/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_FC_Barcelona_season), in which Barcelona won a [league](/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_La_Liga) and [cup](/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_Copa_del_Rey) [double](/wiki/Double_(association_football)). At the end of the season, Barcelona rejected offers from [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma) and [Parma](/wiki/Parma_F.C.) (of around 300 million [pesetas](/wiki/Spanish_peseta)) for Guardiola. After prolonged and complicated contract talks, he signed a new contract with Barcelona that extended his stay until 2001.

Guardiola returned to action [the following season](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_FC_Barcelona_season) and Barcelona once again won [La Liga](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_La_Liga). On 8 June 1998, Guardiola underwent surgery to try to resolve his lingering calf injury, which had caused him to miss the [1998 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) for Spain. A largely disappointing [1999–2000 season](/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_FC_Barcelona_season) again ended in surgery, with Guardiola missing the last three months of the season with a serious [ankle injury](/wiki/Sprained_ankle).

On 11 April 2001, Barcelona's captain announced his intention to leave the club after 17 years of service. He stated that it was a personal decision and, in part, a response to what he perceived as football heading in a new, more physical, direction. On 24 June 2001, Guardiola played his last match with Barcelona in the final game of the season against [Celta Vigo](/wiki/Celta_de_Vigo). Guardiola played 479 games in 12 seasons for the first team, winning 16 trophies. At the press conference after the Celta game, he said: "It's been a long journey. I'm happy, proud, happy with the way people treated me and I have made many friends. I cannot ask for more. I have had many years in the elite. I did not come to make history but to make my own history." A number of future Barcelona midfielders, including [Xavi](/wiki/Xavi_(footballer,_born_1980)), [Andrés Iniesta](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta) and [Cesc Fàbregas](/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas), have hailed Guardiola as their role model and hero.

### 2001–2006: Later career

After leaving Barcelona in 2001 at age 30, Guardiola joined [Serie A](/wiki/Serie_A) side [Brescia](/wiki/Brescia_Calcio) as [Andrea Pirlo](/wiki/Andrea_Pirlo)'s replacement in the [deep-lying playmaker](/wiki/Deep-lying_playmaker) role, where he played alongside [Roberto Baggio](/wiki/Roberto_Baggio) under manager [Carlo Mazzone](/wiki/Carlo_Mazzone). Following his stint at Brescia, Guardiola transferred to [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma). His time in Italy, however, was unsuccessful and included a four-month ban for testing positive for [nandrolone](/wiki/Nandrolone) (he was cleared of all charges in 2009).

After his career with Brescia and Roma, in 2003, Guardiola played in Qatar with [Al-Ahli](/wiki/Al-Ahli_(Doha)) from [Doha](/wiki/Doha) in the [Qatar Stars League](/wiki/Qatar_Stars_League). In 2005–06, he turned down offers from a number of European clubs, as he felt his playing career was coming to a close.

In 2006, [Juan Manuel Lillo](/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Lillo) was appointed the manager of Mexican club [Dorados](/wiki/Dorados_de_Sinaloa). Lillo recruited Guardiola to play for the club while he was in managing school in Axocopán, [Atlixco](/wiki/Atlixco), [Puebla](/wiki/Puebla). Guardiola played with Dorados for six months, but was limited to ten appearances due to injuries, before retiring. He scored one goal for the club.

## International career

### Spain

On 14 October 1992, Guardiola debuted for the [Spain national football team](/wiki/Spain_national_football_team) in a [friendly match](/wiki/Exhibition_game) against [Northern Ireland](/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team). The same year, he served as [Spain](/wiki/Spain_national_under-23_football_team) when they won a [gold medal](/wiki/Gold_medal) at the [Barcelona Olympic Games](/wiki/Football_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics). That same year he won the [Bravo Award](/wiki/Bravo_Award), which recognises the world's best player under the age of 21.

Guardiola was a member of the Spanish team during the [1994 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), where they reached the quarter-finals, losing 2–1 to [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team). Due to disagreements, he fell out of favour with Spain coach [Javier Clemente](/wiki/Javier_Clemente) and missed out on [UEFA Euro 1996](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996). Guardiola suffered a career-threatening injury in 1998 which kept him out of [the year's World Cup](/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), but later played at [Euro 2000](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), where Spain reached another quarter-final, this time losing to [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) by the same margin of 2–1. He continued to play in the Spanish midfield until his final appearance on 14 November 2001, in a 1–0 victory in a friendly match against [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_national_football_team). Guardiola scored his last international goal against [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_men%27s_national_football_team) in a 1–1 friendly draw during his 45th appearance.

### Catalonia

Guardiola has played for and advocated on behalf of the [Catalonia football team](/wiki/Catalonia_national_football_team). Between 1995 and 2005, he played seven [friendly matches](/wiki/Exhibition_game) for Catalonia.

## Player profile

### Style of play

Guardiola was a highly creative, hard-working, nimble, and elegant player, with good anticipation, tactical awareness, and an ability to read the game; throughout his career, he was usually deployed as either a [central](/wiki/Central_midfielder) or [defensive midfielder](/wiki/Defensive_midfielder) in front of his team's back-line, although he was also capable of playing in more [attacking midfield](/wiki/Attacking_midfield) roles. Although he was competent defensively and able to press opponents to break up play and win the ball effectively through his team-work and defensive positioning, he also had a tendency to give away many fouls; as such, and also in part due to his slender physical build, he usually functioned as a deep-lying playmaker in front of the defence, where he excelled courtesy of his technical ability and intelligent, efficient, precise passing game. He would also occasionally drop deeper to act as an additional [centre-back](/wiki/Centre-back) in Cruyff's fluid 3–4–3 formation at Barcelona. Despite his lack of notable pace, dribbling ability, aerial prowess, or strong physical or athletic attributes, Guardiola was highly regarded throughout his career for his vision, close control, passing range, positional sense, and calm composure on the ball, as well as his speed of thought, which enabled him to retain possession under pressure and either set the tempo of his team's play in midfield with quick and intricate short first-time exchanges, or switch the play or create chances with longer passes. His role has also been likened to that of a [metodista](/wiki/Metodista) ("centre-half", in Italian football jargon), due to his ability to dictate play in midfield as well as assist his team defensively.

Guardiola was capable of being an offensive threat, due to his ability to make attacking runs or strike accurately from distance; he was also effective at creating chances or shooting on goal from set-pieces. Having served as captain of both Barcelona and the Spanish national side, he also stood out for his leadership throughout his career. Despite his playing ability, he was also known to be injury prone throughout his career.

### Reception

Guardiola's playing style, which relied on creativity, technique and ball movement, rather than physicality and pace, inspired several future diminutive Spanish playmaking midfielders, such as [Xavi](/wiki/Xavi_(footballer,_born_1980)), [Andrés Iniesta](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Iniesta), and [Cesc Fàbregas](/wiki/Cesc_F%C3%A0bregas), with the latter describing him as his "idol". Pirlo instead described Guardiola as the "model" for the position which he himself occupied deep in midfield. Former Barcelona president Joan Laporta once described Guardiola as "the best central midfielder in our history." Johan Cruyff considered him to be one of the best [midfielders](/wiki/Midfielder) of his generation, a view echoed by Richard Jolly of *[FourFourTwo](/wiki/FourFourTwo)* and Marco Frattino, the latter of whom stated in 2018: "Twenty years ago, [...] Pep Guardiola was one of the best midfielders in the world." In 2001, his agent Josè Maria Orobitg described him as the best in the world at dictating the tempo and rhythm of his team's play.

Miguel Val of *Marca* considered Guardiola to be one of the greatest Spanish players of all time, describing him as the "brains of Barcelona's Dream Team under Johan Cruyff" in 2020. Federico Aquè described him as one of the best deep-lying playmakers in European football in his prime, while Lee Bushe of *[90min.com](/wiki/90min.com)* even included him in his list of "The Best Deep-Lying Playmakers of All Time" in 2020.

## Managerial career

### Barcelona

#### B team

Guardiola coaching [Barcelona B](/wiki/FC_Barcelona_Atl%C3%A8tic) in 2008

Guardiola was appointed manager of Barcelona B on 21 June 2007 with [Tito Vilanova](/wiki/Tito_Vilanova) as his assistant. Under his guidance, the team subsequently won their [Tercera División](/wiki/Tercera_Divisi%C3%B3n) group and qualified for the [2008 Segunda División B playoffs](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_B_Play-Off), which the team won, thereby achieving promotion. [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) President [Joan Laporta](/wiki/Joan_Laporta) announced in May 2008 that Guardiola would be appointed manager of the senior Barcelona squad to replace [Frank Rijkaard](/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard) at the end of the [2007–08 season](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_FC_Barcelona_season).

#### 2008–09: First season with first team and historic treble

Guardiola managing [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona) in 2009

Upon being appointed, Guardiola made headlines by announcing that stars such as [Ronaldinho](/wiki/Ronaldinho), [Deco](/wiki/Deco) and [Samuel Eto'o](/wiki/Samuel_Eto%27o) were not part of his plans for [the coming season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_La_Liga), though Eto'o was, in the end, allowed to remain.

In association with Barcelona Director of Sport [Txiki Begiristain](/wiki/Txiki_Begiristain), several new signings were made by Guardiola – [Dani Alves](/wiki/Dani_Alves) and [Seydou Keita](/wiki/Seydou_Keita_(footballer)) arrived from [Sevilla](/wiki/Sevilla_FC); [Martín Cáceres](/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_C%C3%A1ceres) from Villarreal by way of [Recreativo](/wiki/Recreativo_de_Huelva); [Gerard Piqué](/wiki/Gerard_Piqu%C3%A9) returned from [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.); and [Alexander Hleb](/wiki/Alexander_Hleb) was signed from [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.). Along with the new signings, Guardiola promoted *[canteranos](/wiki/Cantera)* [Sergio Busquets](/wiki/Sergio_Busquets), [Pedro](/wiki/Pedro_(footballer,_born_1987)) and [Jeffrén](/wiki/Jeffr%C3%A9n_Su%C3%A1rez) to the first-team squad. In interviews with the press, Guardiola stressed a harder work ethic than before, but also a more personal approach during training and a closer relationship with his players.

Guardiola's first competitive game as manager was in the [third qualifying round](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Champions_League_qualifying_rounds#Third_qualifying_round) of the [Champions League](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Champions_League), in which Barcelona comfortably beat Polish club [Wisła Kraków](/wiki/Wis%C5%82a_Krak%C3%B3w) 4–0 in the first leg at home. They then lost 1–0 in the second leg, but progressed with a 4–1 aggregate victory. Promoted [Numancia](/wiki/CD_Numancia) also defeated Barcelona in the opening matchday of the [2008–09 La Liga](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_La_Liga), but the team then went on an undefeated streak for over 20 matches to move to the top of the league. Barcelona maintained their spot atop La Liga's table, securing their first league title since 2006 when rivals [Real Madrid](/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF) lost at Villarreal on 16 May 2009. The most important match, however, was on 2 May when they defeated Real Madrid [6–2](/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico) at the [Santiago Bernabéu](/wiki/Santiago_Bernab%C3%A9u_Stadium). The league title was the second piece of silverware in Guardiola's first season at the club. Earlier, on 13 May, Barcelona won the [2008–09 Copa del Rey](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Copa_del_Rey), beating [Athletic Bilbao](/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao) 4–1 in the [2009 Copa del Rey final](/wiki/2009_Copa_del_Rey_final).

In the [final of the Champions League](/wiki/2009_UEFA_Champions_League_final), Barcelona beat Manchester United 2–0. In doing so, they became the [first Spanish club](/wiki/Football_records_in_Spain) to win the domestic cup, the league, and the European club titles ([the treble](/wiki/Treble_(association_football))) in the [same season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_FC_Barcelona_season). Guardiola became the [youngest man to manage a Champions League winning team](/wiki/List_of_European_Cup_and_UEFA_Champions_League_winning_managers), at age 37. The treble-winning season is regarded as one of the club's finest in its history.

#### 2009–10: Six trophies in a calendar year

Guardiola in 2010

Guardiola's [second season](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_FC_Barcelona_season) as manager began with defeats of Athletic Bilbao in the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2009_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) and [Shakhtar Donetsk](/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) in the [UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/2009_UEFA_Super_Cup). On 25 September 2009, Barcelona gave him his 50th professional victory, away against [Málaga](/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_CF) and on 19 December, they were crowned [FIFA Club World Cup champions](/wiki/2009_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) for the first time in [their history](/wiki/History_of_FC_Barcelona).

Guardiola finished the calendar year with a record [six trophies](/wiki/The_Sextuple), the [Spanish League](/wiki/La_Liga), [Copa del Rey](/wiki/Copa_del_Rey), [Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League), [Spanish Super Cup](/wiki/Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), [European Super Cup](/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup) and [Club World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup), becoming the [first manager](/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_managers) in [history](/wiki/History_of_FC_Barcelona) to do so. In January 2010, he became Barcelona's longest serving Spanish manager, overtaking the record previously held by [Josep Samitier](/wiki/Josep_Samitier). He agreed to a one-year contract extension to keep him with Barcelona until the end of the [2010–11 season](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_FC_Barcelona_season).

In February 2010, Guardiola managed his 100th match for Barcelona's first team. His record stood at 71 wins, 19 draws and 10 losses, with 242 goals for and 76 against. On 10 April 2010, he became the first manager in Barcelona's history to beat Real Madrid four times in a row in *El Clásico*. Barcelona reached the [semi-finals of the 2009–10 Champions League](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase), but lost 3–2 on aggregate to [José Mourinho](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mourinho)'s Inter Milan. Despite this, they managed to win their 20th La Liga title with 99 points by beating [Real Valladolid](/wiki/Real_Valladolid) 4–0 at home. At the time, this was the highest points total ever gained amongst any of [Europe's major leagues](/wiki/UEFA). The La Liga title was Guardiola's seventh trophy as manager of the club, tying [Ferdinand Daučík](/wiki/Ferdinand_Dau%C4%8D%C3%ADk) for second behind Johan Cruyff and his 11 trophies.

#### 2010–11: Second Champions League title

On 21 August, Barcelona beat Sevilla 5–3 on aggregate to win the [2010 Supercopa de España](/wiki/2010_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a), his second in a row. On 29 November 2010, Barcelona beat Real Madrid 5–0, giving Guardiola five straight wins in *El Clásico*. On 8 February 2011, Guardiola accepted the club's offer for a one-year deal extension, signing a contract until June 2012.

On 11 May 2011, Barcelona won the La Liga title and the club's third in a row after a 1–1 draw with [Levante](/wiki/Levante_UD). On 28 May, Barcelona beat Manchester United 3–1 at [Wembley](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium) in the [2011 Champions League final](/wiki/2011_UEFA_Champions_League_final).

#### 2011–12: Final season

The season started with a [5–4](/wiki/2011_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a) aggregate win over Real Madrid for the [Supercopa de España](/wiki/2011_Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a).

Barcelona won their second trophy of the season on 26 August, beating [Porto](/wiki/FC_Porto) 2–0 in the [2011 UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/2011_UEFA_Super_Cup). With the trophy won against Porto, he became all-time record holder of most titles won as a [manager at Barcelona](/wiki/List_of_FC_Barcelona_managers), with 12 trophies in only three years. November of the same year saw Guardiola manager his 200th match for Barcelona's first team. His record stood at 144 wins, 39 draws and 17 losses with 500 goals for and 143 against.

Barcelona ended the 2011 calendar year winning the [Club World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), beating Brazilian club [Santos](/wiki/Santos_FC) 4–0 in the [final](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final), the widest margin in an Intercontinental Cup/Club World Cup final since changing to a single match format. This was Guardiola's 13th title of only 16 tournaments played. On 9 January 2012, he was named [FIFA World Coach of the Year](/wiki/FIFA_World_Coach_of_the_Year). On his 41st birthday, he led his side to a 2–1 victory over arch-rivals Real Madrid in *El Clásico*, ensuring that he remained unbeaten against Real Madrid in regular time as a manager. On 21 April, Guardiola conceded the league title to leaders Real Madrid after they beat Barcelona 2–1 and extended their lead in the table to seven points with four matches remaining.

On 24 April, a 2–2 draw at home against Chelsea in the [second leg](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase) of the [Champions League semi-final](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase) knocked Barcelona out of the competition on a 2–3 aggregate score. That effectively left the team with only the [Copa del Rey](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Copa_del_Rey) to play for. Guardiola had faced criticism over his recent tactics and squad selections. On 27 April 2012, he announced he would step down as Barcelona's manager at the end of the [2011–12 season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season). He had been on a rolling contract that was renewed annually during his tenure as manager. Citing tiredness as the main reason for his decision, he also commented that four years at a club like Barcelona felt like an eternity.

Guardiola continued to lead Barcelona to wins in the remaining La Liga games of [the season](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_La_Liga), followed by a 3–0 win in the Copa del Rey [final](/wiki/2012_Copa_del_Rey_final). His record of 14 trophies in four seasons has made him the most successful manager in [Barcelona's history](/wiki/History_of_FC_Barcelona). Barcelona announced that he would be succeeded by [Tito Vilanova](/wiki/Tito_Vilanova), who would begin leading the first team at the start of the [2012–13 season](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_FC_Barcelona_season).

#### Sabbatical

After his time at Barcelona came to an end, Guardiola took a year's [sabbatical](/wiki/Sabbatical) in New York City. On 7 January 2013, he came in third place for the 2012 [FIFA World Coach of the Year](/wiki/2012_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or), behind the winner [Vicente del Bosque](/wiki/Vicente_del_Bosque) and runner-up José Mourinho. While at a news conference at the [2012 FIFA Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2012_FIFA_Ballon_d%27Or) gala in [Zürich](/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich), Guardiola said: "I have taken a decision to return to coaching but beyond that no decision has been taken. I don't have a team to go to but I would like to go back to coaching."

### Bayern Munich

#### 2013–2015: Consecutive Bundesliga titles

Guardiola managing [Bayern Munich](/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in 2013

On 16 January 2013, it was announced that Guardiola would take over as manager of [Bundesliga](/wiki/Bundesliga) club [Bayern Munich](/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) after the [2012–13 season](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_FC_Bayern_Munich_season), replacing [Jupp Heynckes](/wiki/Jupp_Heynckes) for the [following season](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_FC_Bayern_Munich_season). He addressed his first press conference at Bayern, on 24 June 2013, in German, and had his first training session two days later. His first official match was the [German Super Cup](/wiki/2013_DFL-Supercup) against [Borussia Dortmund](/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), with Bayern losing 4–2. His first trophy with Bayern was the [2013 UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/2013_UEFA_Super_Cup), defeating longtime adversary José Mourinho, who had just returned to manage at Chelsea. Bayern beat ten-man Chelsea in a [shoot-out](/wiki/Penalty_shoot-out_(association_football)) after [Manuel Neuer](/wiki/Manuel_Neuer) saved [Romelu Lukaku](/wiki/Romelu_Lukaku)'s kick.

In December 2013, Guardiola won his third [Club World Cup](/wiki/2013_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) after beating [Raja Casablanca](/wiki/Raja_Casablanca) in Morocco. On 25 March 2014, he led Bayern to their 23rd [Bundesliga](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Bundesliga) title by beating [Hertha Berlin](/wiki/Hertha_BSC) 3–1 at the [Olympiastadion](/wiki/Olympiastadion_(Berlin)) in Berlin. With seven matches remaining in the season, it was the earliest the championship had been won in Bundesliga history, breaking the record Heynckes' Bayern had set in the previous season. Guardiola broke [Karl-Heinz Feldkamp](/wiki/Karl-Heinz_Feldkamp)'s record for the longest winning streak to start his tenure at a Bundesliga club. The streak ended at 28 when [Augsburg](/wiki/FC_Augsburg) defeated Bayern 1–0 on matchday 29. The streak also ended Bayern's 53–match undefeated streak.

Bayern were drawn against Real Madrid in the [semi–finals](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase#Semi-finals) of [Champions League](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_UEFA_Champions_League). Bayern lost the first leg 1–0 and the second leg 4–0. The first leg was also Guardiola's first defeat at the Santiago Bernabéu. He finished the 2013–14 season by winning the [DFB-Pokal](/wiki/2014_DFB-Pokal_final) 2–0 in extra–time.

Guardiola in 2014

In the [2014–15 season](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_FC_Bayern_Munich_season), Bayern lost the [German Super Cup](/wiki/2014_DFL-Supercup) 2–0 to Borussia Dortmund. On 11 March 2015, Bayern defeated Shakhtar Donetsk 7–0, tying their largest win in Champions League history. In Guardiola's 100th match as manager, Bayern defeated Porto 6–1. With the win, Bayern reached their fourth-straight Champions League semi-final. On 28 April 2015, Bayern were knocked out of the German Cup in a penalty shoot-out. Bayern had missed all four of their shots. In his first competitive match against Barcelona, Bayern lost 3–0. Bayern failed to get a shot on target in the match. For the first time in his career, he lost four in a row (including the shoot-out loss).

#### 2015–16: Second domestic double and final season

Guardiola during a press conference in 2015

The [2015–16 season](/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_FC_Bayern_Munich_season) started on 1 August 2015 when Bayern lost in a shoot-out to Wolfsburg in the German Super Cup. In the league, Bayern won their first ten matches. The first time they dropped points in the league was on 30 October 2015 in a 0–0 scoreline against [Eintracht Frankfurt](/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt) and their first loss in the league was on 5 December 2015 in a 3–1 scoreline to Borussia Mönchengladbach. In the Champions League group stage, Bayern won Group F, winning five out of the six matches. Bayern's only loss in the Champions League group stage was against Arsenal on 20 October. This was Bayern's first loss in all competitions during the 2015–16 season.

On 20 December, Bayern confirmed that Guardiola was leaving the club after his contract expired at the end of the season, with [Carlo Ancelotti](/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti) his replacement for the 2016–17 season.

On 3 May 2016, Guardiola's Bayern Munich lost to Atlético Madrid in the Champions League semi-finals stage, thereby ending his final chance of winning a Champions League title with the Bavarian club. Guardiola's final match was on 21 May 2016, with Bayern defeating Borussia Dortmund in a shootout. He finished with a record of 82 wins, eleven draws and nine losses in the Bundesliga; a record of 14 wins, three draws and no losses in the [DFB-Pokal](/wiki/DFB-Pokal); a record of 23 wins, five draws and eight losses in the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League). He also went a combined two wins, two draws, and two losses in the [FIFA Club World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup), [UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup), and the [German Super Cup](/wiki/DFL-Supercup). In non–official competitions, he went a combined six wins, one draw, and one loss.

### Manchester City

#### 2016–17: Adjustment to England and first season

On 1 February 2016, Manchester City signed Guardiola to a three-year contract for the start of the [2016–17 season](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Manchester_City_F.C._season). Guardiola brought in several significant players in the summer, including midfielders [İlkay Gündoğan](/wiki/%C4%B0lkay_G%C3%BCndo%C4%9Fan) from [Borussia Dortmund](/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) and [Nolito](/wiki/Nolito) from [Celta Vigo](/wiki/RC_Celta_de_Vigo), winger [Leroy Sané](/wiki/Leroy_San%C3%A9) from [Schalke 04](/wiki/FC_Schalke_04) and defender [John Stones](/wiki/John_Stones) from [Everton](/wiki/Everton_F.C.). He also controversially replaced long-serving City starting goalkeeper [Joe Hart](/wiki/Joe_Hart) with [Claudio Bravo](/wiki/Claudio_Bravo) from his former club Barcelona; Hart would never make another appearance for the club.

On 13 August 2016, Guardiola earned victory in his first match of the [Premier League season](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Premier_League), as City defeated [Sunderland](/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.) 2–1. On 11 September, Guardiola won his first [Manchester derby](/wiki/Manchester_derby) as a manager in a 2–1 City victory at [Old Trafford](/wiki/Old_Trafford); this was also his sixth win against his "rival" manager José Mourinho.

City were leaders going into the international break, but their form declined thereafter. Manchester City lost to [Everton](/wiki/Everton_F.C.) 0–4 on 15 January 2017; this was Guardiola's biggest-ever managerial defeat in a domestic competition. In Europe, City were eliminated in the [Champions League](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_UEFA_Champions_League) round of 16 by [Monaco](/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) on [away goals](/wiki/Away_goals_rule) after a 6–6 aggregate draw. The second leg of the tie was Guardiola's 100th game as a manager in European competition, and he arrived at that mark with the best record of any manager, having earned 61 wins and 23 draws (one draw better than the previous record holder, Guardiola's former manager at Barcelona, Louis van Gaal). Following a loss to [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) in the [FA Cup](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_FA_Cup) semi-finals, Guardiola finished the season without a trophy for the first time in his managerial career.

#### 2017–18: "Centurions" and first Premier League title

Guardiola giving instructions to [Nicolás Otamendi](/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Otamendi) during a friendly match against [Tottenham Hotspur](/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) in the [2017 International Champions Cup](/wiki/2017_International_Champions_Cup)

Guardiola identified the defensive areas which required improvement for Manchester City in the summer transfer window to challenge for the league title, particularly in the goalkeeper and full-back positions. Due to Bravo's struggles the previous season, [Ederson](/wiki/Ederson_(footballer,_born_1993)) was brought in as the new first-choice goalkeeper. Wing-backs [Benjamin Mendy](/wiki/Benjamin_Mendy) and [Kyle Walker](/wiki/Kyle_Walker) were also signed, while dispensing of all the previous senior full-backs at the club in [Aleksandar Kolarov](/wiki/Aleksandar_Kolarov), [Gaël Clichy](/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl_Clichy), [Bacary Sagna](/wiki/Bacary_Sagna) and [Pablo Zabaleta](/wiki/Pablo_Zabaleta). Additionally, [Bernardo Silva](/wiki/Bernardo_Silva) and [Danilo](/wiki/Danilo_(footballer,_born_July_1991)) were also acquired from Monaco and Real Madrid, respectively.

On 25 February 2018, City won the [2017–18 EFL Cup](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_EFL_Cup) after beating Arsenal 3–0 in the [final](/wiki/2018_EFL_Cup_final), which was Guardiola's first trophy with the club. On 15 April, City were confirmed as [2017–18 Premier League](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Premier_League) champions following Manchester United's 1–0 home defeat to [West Bromwich Albion](/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.). After finishing the league season with a record-breaking 100 points, Guardiola signed a new contract with City until 2021.

#### 2018–2020: Domestic treble and Champions League disappointment

During Guardiola's third season as manager, Manchester City signed [Riyad Mahrez](/wiki/Riyad_Mahrez) from [Leicester City](/wiki/Leicester_City_F.C.) for a fee of £60 million. On 5 August 2018, City kicked off the season with a 2–0 victory over [FA Cup](/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_FA_Cup) holders Chelsea in the [2018 FA Community Shield](/wiki/2018_FA_Community_Shield).

On 24 February 2019, Guardiola's side played Chelsea in the [EFL Cup final](/wiki/2019_EFL_Cup_final) held at [Wembley](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium). The match ended 0–0 after extra time, and Manchester City won 4–3 on penalties to retain the trophy for the second year in a row. On 9 April, City faced Tottenham Hotspur in the first leg of their [Champions League](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Champions_League) quarter-finals, held at Tottenham's [new stadium](/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_Stadium). The game ended in a 0–1 defeat for City. The second leg was held at the [Etihad Stadium](/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium) on 17 April, where Guardiola's side beat Tottenham 4–3, with City's last-minute fifth goal having been controversially disallowed. Due to the [aggregate score](/wiki/Aggregate_score) being a 4–4 draw, Tottenham went through to the semi-finals on away goals. On 12 May, Guardiola secured a second consecutive [Premier League](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Premier_League) title. His side finished on 98 points, one point above [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), after a 4–1 victory at [Brighton & Hove Albion](/wiki/Brighton_%26_Hove_Albion_F.C.) in the final match of the season. On 18 May, City beat [Watford](/wiki/Watford_F.C.) 6–0 in the [final](/wiki/2019_FA_Cup_final) of the [FA Cup](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FA_Cup), becoming the first ever men's team in England to win a domestic treble.

Guardiola made two major acquisitions during the summer transfer window of 2019 in defender [João Cancelo](/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Cancelo) from [Juventus](/wiki/Juventus_FC) for £27.4m plus [Danilo](/wiki/Danilo_(footballer,_born_July_1991)) and midfielder [Rodri](/wiki/Rodri_(footballer,_born_1996)) from [Atlético Madrid](/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid) for a fee of £62.8 million, a club record. These signings meant that the value of the City squad had exceeded €1 billion, becoming the first football club in the world to assemble a squad with this value. On 4 August 2019, City began the season with a penalty shoot-out victory against Liverpool in the [Community Shield](/wiki/2019_FA_Community_Shield), claiming the trophy for the second straight year. During the match, Guardiola also became the first Premier League manager to receive a yellow card from the referee. On 1 March, Manchester City beat [Aston Villa](/wiki/Aston_Villa_F.C.) 2–1 in the [2020 EFL Cup final](/wiki/2020_EFL_Cup_final), winning the competition for a third successive season. City finished second in the [2019–20 Premier League](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Premier_League) after a spring hiatus due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_England). After defeating Real Madrid in the [2019–20 Champions League](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Champions_League) round of 16, Guardiola's side faced [Lyon](/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) in the single-elimination quarter-finals on 15 August 2020. City lost the match 1–3 and was eliminated at the quarter-final stage for the third season in a row.

#### 2020–2024: Premier League four-peat, Champions League win and continental treble

The [2020–21 season](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Manchester_City_F.C._season) saw City's defence greatly improve compared to the last campaign, conceding just one goal in twelve matches played. On 19 November 2020, Guardiola signed a new two-year contract with Manchester City until summer 2023. He won his 500th game as manager after City beat [Sheffield United](/wiki/Sheffield_United_F.C.) 1–0 at home in the [Premier League](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Premier_League) on 31 January 2021; it was the ninth game City had won in January, becoming the team with the most wins in a single month in the top four tiers of English football since the [Football League](/wiki/English_Football_League) began in 1888. Following a 3–1 victory over [Swansea City](/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.) in the [FA Cup](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FA_Cup) on 10 February, Guardiola's side broke the record for the longest winning run in English top-flight football history, with fifteen straight victories for City in all competitions.

Guardiola won his third Premier League title on 11 May after Manchester United's home defeat to Leicester City, two weeks after beating Tottenham Hotspur 1–0 in the [2021 EFL Cup final](/wiki/2021_EFL_Cup_final) to claim that trophy for the fourth successive time. On 29 May, Manchester City played in the [Champions League final](/wiki/2021_UEFA_Champions_League_final) for the first time in their history, losing to Chelsea 0–1. Following the match, Guardiola was criticised for his team selection and not starting a defensive midfielder. Chelsea manager [Thomas Tuchel](/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel) also admitted that he was surprised not to see midfielder [Fernandinho](/wiki/Fernandinho_(footballer,_born_May_1985)) in the City starting line-up.

Guardiola as manager of [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.) in 2021

During the summer transfer window of 2021, Manchester City broke the British transfer record by signing Aston Villa midfielder [Jack Grealish](/wiki/Jack_Grealish) for £100m. On 25 September, he surpassed [Les McDowall](/wiki/Les_McDowall) as the manager with the most wins in Manchester City history following their 1–0 away victory over Chelsea in the [Premier League](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Premier_League). On 22 May 2022, Manchester City won the Premier League with a 3–2 victory over Aston Villa. This was Guardiola's fourth title at the club, placing him second on the list of [managers with the most Premier League titles](/wiki/List_of_Premier_League_managers#Premier_League_title-winning_managers).

During the [2022–23 season](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Manchester_City_F.C._season), Manchester City won their third consecutive [Premier League](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Premier_League) title, their fifth under Guardiola. On 23 November 2022, Guardiola signed a new two-year contract with Manchester City until summer 2025. On 3 June 2023, the club won their second FA Cup under Guardiola after a 2–1 victory over rivals Manchester United in the [final](/wiki/2023_FA_Cup_final) to achieve another domestic double. On 10 June 2023, he led the club to their first Champions League title, and his personal third, after a 1–0 win against Inter Milan in the [final](/wiki/2023_UEFA_Champions_League_final), which completed their continental treble.

On 16 August 2023, Guardiola won a record-equaling [fourth](/wiki/2023_UEFA_Super_Cup) UEFA Super Cup title, also becoming the first-ever manager to win the trophy with three clubs, as the Mancunian side defeated [Sevilla](/wiki/Sevilla_FC) 5–4 on penalties following a 1–1 draw. On 22 December, Guardiola guided City to their first [FIFA Club World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) trophy, as they beat [Fluminense](/wiki/Fluminense_FC) 4–0 in the [final](/wiki/2023_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final), and became the first English club to win five titles in a calendar year. With this achievement, Guardiola became the first manager in history to win four FIFA Club World Cup titles. Following the match, Guardiola stated that he "had the feeling [...] would close the chapter, we won all the titles, there's nothing else to win. I had a feeling the job was done, it was over".

Man City's Champions League defence was ended in the quarter finals following a 4–3 loss on penalties after a 4—4 aggregate draw to [Real Madrid](/wiki/Real_Madrid_CF) who Man City beat 5–1 in the semi finals of the [previous Champions League](/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Champions_League) which ended Man City's hope of consecutive continental trebles.

On 19 May 2024, Man City beat [West Ham United](/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.) 3–1 on the final day of the season to win their fourth straight Premier League title with 91 points, two points ahead of Arsenal, becoming the first English club to win four top flight league titles in a row. A week later, Man City lost the [FA Cup Final](/wiki/2024_FA_Cup_final) to Manchester United 2–1 which ended their chance of a consecutive domestic double.

#### 2024–present: Decline and challenges

Under Pep Guardiola, Manchester City's 2024–25 season initially mirrored the success of the previous campaign, winning the [Community Shield](/wiki/2024_FA_Community_Shield) for the first time since [2019](/wiki/2019_FA_Community_Shield) and for the third time in the Guardiola era and beginning the league season with an unbeaten run of nine games. However, the team's fortunes took a downturn following a 1–2 defeat to [Tottenham Hotspur](/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) in the fourth round of the EFL Cup. This loss marked the beginning of a difficult period for the club, as they won only one of their next thirteen matches across all competitions.

On 9 November 2024, Manchester City were beaten 2–1 by Brighton & Hove Albion, meaning that for the first time in his managerial career, Guardiola had suffered four losses in a row in regulation time. On 22 November 2024, Guardiola signed a two-year contract extension with the club which will keep him at the Etihad until 2027, despite earlier widespread speculation that he might not renew his contract and could leave the club at the end of the [2024–25 Premier League](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Premier_League) season. However, a day later, Guardiola suffered his heaviest ever home defeat as Manchester City manager in a 4–0 defeat to Tottenham Hotspur.

On 19 February 2025, Manchester City was eliminated from the [2024–25 Champions League](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League) after a 3–6 aggregate loss to Real Madrid, marking their first failure to reach the round of sixteen since the 2012–13 season. Guardiola, reflecting on the team's struggles, openly criticized his own performance and suggested that he would not remain at the club if he were found to be the source of the problem.

With Manchester City only having the [FA Cup](/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_FA_Cup) to play for, they would make the [FA Cup final](/wiki/2025_FA_Cup_final) for the third season in a row after beating [Nottingham Forest](/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.) 2–0 in the semi finals but they lost the final 1–0 to [Crystal Palace](/wiki/Crystal_Palace_F.C.) marking Manchester City's second FA Cup final defeat in a row and the first time Guardiola had lost two finals in a row in the same competition in his managerial career. The loss also meant Manchester City would finish a season without a major trophy for the first time since the [2016–17 season](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Manchester_City_F.C._season). After a 2–0 win over [Fulham](/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) on the final day of the season, Manchester City finished third in the Premier League with 71 points ensuring Champions League qualification for the 15th consecutive season but the third place finish also marked Manchester City's worst finish in the league since the [2016–17 season](/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Premier_League).

On 5 October 2025, he secured his 250th Premier League victory with a 1–0 away win over [Brentford](/wiki/Brentford_F.C.), achieving the milestone in just 349 matches and surpassing [Alex Ferguson](/wiki/Alex_Ferguson)'s previous record of 404 matches. A month later, on 9 November, he took charge of his 1,000th match, leading his team to a 3–0 win over Liverpool and maintaining a 71.6% overall win rate. On 28 January 2026, he recorded his 400th win with the club in 569 matches in a 2–0 win over [Galatasaray](/wiki/Galatasaray_S.K._(football)) in the [Champions League](/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Champions_League), becoming the fastest manager in England to reach this milestone, breaking [Arsène Wenger](/wiki/Ars%C3%A8ne_Wenger)'s previous record of 696 matches.

## Manager profile

### Tactics

Although much emphasis is placed on retaining possession of the ball and dictating play, with the intent to have the opposing side's defence chase the ball for extended periods of play, Guardiola's teams are recognised for [pressing](/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#P) off the ball. Players press and harry the opposition collectively in an attempt to win back possession of the ball. This collective press is only conducted in the starting third of the opposition's pitch where less space exists and defenders and/or the goalkeeper may not be as good at dribbling or passing the ball as a midfielder.

As high pressing became prominent, Guardiola sought to counteract it with [goalkeepers](/wiki/Goalkeeper_(association_football)) and [defenders](/wiki/Defender_(association_football)) comfortable with controlling the ball and both long and short ball distribution, with goalkeepers such as [Victor Valdés](/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Vald%C3%A9s) and Manuel Neuer also acting as [sweeper-keepers](/wiki/Sweeper-keeper) at Barcelona and Bayern Munich, rushing off their line to prevent counter-attacks, and playing out from the back. At Manchester City, Ederson routinely played accurate long balls up-field when City were pressed high, at times taking the entire opposition out-of-play and creating 1-on-1 situations for City forwards. To avoid getting caught by long-range passing from City's defensive-third, the opposition defence would cautiously drop deep despite the forward line's high-press, hence creating space in the middle of the pitch.

Guardiola has stated he tries to constantly evolve his tactics. After learning the style analogous with [Total Football](/wiki/Total_Football) under Johan Cruyff, Guardiola was particularly influenced by his time as a player in Mexico under his friend and manager at [Dorados](/wiki/Dorados), [Juan Manuel Lillo](/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Lillo). Guardiola also sought help of [Marcelo Bielsa](/wiki/Marcelo_Bielsa) to learn from him. His editorials for *El Pais* during the [2006 World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in praise of [Luis Aragonés](/wiki/Luis_Aragon%C3%A9s)' Spanish side and [Ricardo La Volpe](/wiki/Ricardo_La_Volpe)'s Mexican side reveal the extent of his reverence for possession-based, attacking football, with defenders along with the goal-keeper playing it out from the back, which Guardiola later cited as a major inspiration on multiple occasions. In one of his editorials, he called [Zinedine Zidane](/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane) France's best defender, pointing out how recycling possession in itself is a key defensive tactic, something that Guardiola teams would later become synonymous with. [Philipp Lahm](/wiki/Philipp_Lahm), who played for Guardiola at Bayern Munich, pointed out that Guardiola's tactics were majorly "an offensive *Sacchi*", modelled after the [Arrigo Sacchi](/wiki/Arrigo_Sacchi)'s Milan team of the late 1980s, emphasising fluid movement, quick recoveries, and keeping possession of the ball, which was in sharp contrast to the strictly-defensive *[Catenaccio](/wiki/Catenaccio)*–inspired style employed effectively by José Mourinho and later by [Diego Simeone](/wiki/Diego_Simeone); and that Guardiola has evolved his approach that seemingly was now a mix of both those styles.

Tactics employed by Guardiola have been likened to *[Gegenpressing](/wiki/Gegenpressing)* invented by [Ralf Rangnick](/wiki/Ralf_Rangnick) and used to great effect by [Jürgen Klopp](/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klopp). His tactics have influenced approaches of managers such as [Maurizio Sarri](/wiki/Maurizio_Sarri), [Thomas Tuchel](/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel), [Graham Potter](/wiki/Graham_Potter), and [Luis Enrique](/wiki/Luis_Enrique); as well as those of other sports such as rugby. Guardiola admitted that he had to adapt his style to German and English leagues, but his "football education comes from [Catalunya]" which is possession-based, and distinct from *Gegenpressing*. While several pundits have often linked Guardiola's attacking–minded tactics at Barcelona – centred around quick passing, ball possession, movement, a high defensive line, and heavy pressing – to the *[tiki-taka](/wiki/Tiki-taka)* style employed by the Spanish national side under Aragonés at [Euro 2008](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), Guardiola himself has refuted this claim, and has even been critical of the system, commenting in 2014: "I loathe all that passing for the sake of it, all that tiki-taka. It's so much rubbish and has no purpose. You have to pass the ball with a clear intention, with the aim of making it into the opposition's goal. It's not about passing for the sake of it."

Guardiola has been praised by pundits for his flexibility as a coach, and has used several formations throughout his career. At Barcelona, he often used a [4–3–3 formation](/wiki/Formation_(association_football)#4–3–3) with inverted [wingers](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Winger) and attacking full-backs who would overlap and provide width to the team, as well as a [3–4–3 formation](/wiki/Formation_(association_football)#3–4–3) on occasion; he also later used these formations at Bayern Munich and Manchester City. In the 3–4–3 formation, defensive midfielders [Sergio Busquets](/wiki/Sergio_Busquets) at Barcelona and [Xabi Alonso](/wiki/Xabi_Alonso) at Bayern Münich would occasionally drop back into the back-line to act as an additional defender; this role was similar to the one Guardiola himself played under Cruyff at Barcelona. At Bayern Münich, he also used full-backs Phillip Lahm and [Joshua Kimmich](/wiki/Joshua_Kimmich) in midfield. Guardiola also began to use a [false 9](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#False_9) during his time at Barcelona, fielding [Lionel Messi](/wiki/Lionel_Messi) in the [centre](/wiki/Forward_(association_football)#Centre-forward) of the team's attacking line, who would drop deep into midfield to give the team a numerical advantage in the middle of the pitch. At Manchester City, after experimenting with several formations, he used a modern version of the [3–2–2–3 formation](/wiki/Formation_(association_football)#WM) during the 2022–23 treble–winning season, which was likened to the past *WM* formation. He deployed centre-back John Stones in a hybrid defensive and creative role in midfield, which [Jonathan Wilson](/wiki/Jonathan_Wilson_(writer)) of *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)* likened to both the *[libero](/wiki/Defender_(association_football)#Sweeper_(libero))* and [wing-half](/wiki/Midfielder#Wing-half) roles in 2023. Guardiola also used inverted [full-backs](/wiki/Full_back_(association_football)) who moved inside to occupy central areas of the pitch, while he also played in a more physical and direct style than in previous seasons, utilising [Erling Haaland](/wiki/Erling_Haaland) as a traditional [striker](/wiki/Striker_(association_football)).

### Reception

Considered by pundits to be one of the greatest managers of all time, Guardiola is often linked with the successes of the Spanish and German national teams in 2010s, both of whom had many first-team players that were coached by him.

[Jürgen Klopp](/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klopp) credits Guardiola with building the toughest teams he has ever faced, stating: "I could say City are the toughest opponent I've ever had but it wasn't much easier when I faced Pep's Bayern [..] We push each other to insane levels."

In 2017, Italian defender [Giorgio Chiellini](/wiki/Giorgio_Chiellini) was critical of Guardiola's philosophy, however, and expressed his belief that the popularisation of possession-based playing styles, associated with Barcelona under Guardiola, and the increasing focus on developing defenders who are comfortable with the ball at their feet from a young age in Italy, had in fact had a negative impact on their overall defensive quality. He commented: "*Guardiolismo* [a term he coined for "the Guardiola way"] has ruined a generation of Italian defenders a bit – now everyone is looking to push up, defenders know how to set the tone of play and they can spread the ball, but they don't know how to mark."

Several of Guardiola's former players, teammates, and coaching staff members, such as Xabi Alonso, Xavi, Luis Enrique, [Erik ten Hag](/wiki/Erik_ten_Hag), and [Mikel Arteta](/wiki/Mikel_Arteta), have pursued coaching careers, and have cited Guardiola as an inspiration.

## Personal life

Guardiola was born to Dolors and Valentí. He has two older sisters and a younger brother, [Pere Guardiola](/wiki/Pere_Guardiola), a football agent. He is an atheist. Guardiola met his wife Cristina Serra when he was 18. They married on 29 May 2014. They have three children named Maria, Màrius and Valentina. Serra and Guardiola split in 2025. He is a distant relative to Spanish politician [Anna Balletbò](/wiki/Anna_Balletb%C3%B2).

Following his tenure as Barcelona's manager, he stated that he would move to the United States to live in [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan), New York, for a year, until he had decided on his future. To prepare for his position as the manager of Bayern Munich, Guardiola studied German for four to five hours each day.

Guardiola supports the [political independence of Catalonia](/wiki/Catalan_independence). In 2015, he confirmed that he would participate in the pro-independence coalition, [Junts pel Sí](/wiki/Junts_pel_S%C3%AD), in [that year's regional parliamentary election](/wiki/2015_Catalan_regional_election). He has spoken in support of the people of [Palestine](/wiki/Palestine) and condemned the civilian loss of life in the [Gaza War](/wiki/Gaza_war), referring to the situation as a [genocide against Palestinians](/wiki/Gaza_genocide).

On 24 May 2023, Guardiola made a cameo appearance in *[Ted Lasso](/wiki/Ted_Lasso)*, in which Ted Lasso's (played by [Jason Sudeikis](/wiki/Jason_Sudeikis)) team, AFC Richmond, play against Manchester City and win. Guardiola gives Lasso a handshake following City's defeat and gives Lasso a word of advice, to which Lasso responds positively. Guardiola reportedly is a fan of the show and enjoys watching it with his wife and daughter.

Guardiola was one of the 13 sports personalities named in the [Pandora Papers](/wiki/Pandora_Papers) published by the [International Consortium of Investigative Journalists](/wiki/International_Consortium_of_Investigative_Journalists) (ICIJ). He had an account open in the [principality of Andorra](/wiki/Andorra) until 2012, exploiting the tax amnesty that [Mariano Rajoy](/wiki/Mariano_Rajoy)'s conservative government had enacted in Spain to regularise his fiscal situation. Until that point, he had not declared the funds held in that account to the [Spanish Tax Agency](/wiki/Spanish_Tax_Agency).

Old milestone in [Blandford Forum](/wiki/Blandford_Forum) showing use of the name Shaston for Shaftesbury

**Shaftesbury** ([/ˈʃɑːftsbəri, ˈʃæfts-/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English)) is a town and [civil parish](/wiki/Civil_parish) in [Dorset](/wiki/Dorset), England. It is on the [A30 road](/wiki/A30_road), 20 miles (32 kilometres) west of [Salisbury](/wiki/Salisbury,_Wiltshire) and 23 miles (37 kilometres) north-northeast of [Dorchester](/wiki/Dorchester,_Dorset), near the border with [Wiltshire](/wiki/Wiltshire). It is the only significant hilltop settlement in Dorset, being built about 215 metres (705 feet) [above sea level](/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) on a [greensand](/wiki/Greensand) hill on the edge of [Cranborne Chase](/wiki/Cranborne_Chase).

The town looks over the [Blackmore Vale](/wiki/Blackmore_Vale), part of the [River Stour](/wiki/River_Stour,_Dorset) basin.

Shaftesbury is the site of the former [Shaftesbury Abbey](/wiki/Shaftesbury_Abbey), which was founded in 888 by [King Alfred](/wiki/Alfred_the_Great) and became one of the richest religious establishments in the country, before being destroyed in the [dissolution](/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries) in 1539. Adjacent to the abbey site is [Gold Hill](/wiki/Gold_Hill,_Shaftesbury), a steep [cobbled](/wiki/Cobblestone) street used in the 1970s as the setting for [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott)'s television advertisement for [Hovis](/wiki/Hovis) bread.

In the [2021 census](/wiki/2021_United_Kingdom_census) the town's civil parish had a population of 9,162.

## Toponymy

Shaftesbury has acquired a number of names throughout its history. Writing in 1906, [Sir Frederick Treves](/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Treves,_1st_Baronet) referred to four of these names from Celtic, Latin and English traditions in his book *Highways & Byways in Dorset*:

> The city has had many names. It was, in the beginning, Caer Palladour. By the time of the Domesday Book it was Sceptesberie. It then, with all the affectation of a lady in an eighteenth-century lyric, called itself Sophonia. Lastly it became Shaston, and so the people call it to this day, while all the milestones around concern themselves only with recording the distances to "Shaston".

— Sir Frederick Treves, *Highways & Byways in Dorset* (1906)

The original [Celtic](/wiki/Common_Brittonic) name is first recorded in [Medieval Welsh literature](/wiki/Medieval_Welsh_literature) as *Caer Vynnydd y Paladr* (The Mountain Fort/City of the Spears) and [Thomas Gale](/wiki/Thomas_Gale_(classical_scholar)) records the name as *Caer Palladour* in his work of 1709. Though "Palladour" was described by one 19th-century directory as "mere invention", it has continued to be used as a poetic and alternative name for the town.

The Roman name was probably *Melezo*, mentioned in the [Ravenna Cosmography](/wiki/Ravenna_Cosmography) and interesting as containing an element M-vowel-L possibly left over from the speech of early European farmers.

The English name was recorded in the [Domesday Book](/wiki/Domesday_Book) of 1086 as *Sceptesberie*, and the use of "Shaston" ([/ˈʃæstən/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English)) was recorded in 1831 in Samuel Lewis's *A Topographical Dictionary of England* and in 1840 in *The Parliamentary Gazetteer of England and Wales*.

[Thomas Hardy](/wiki/Thomas_Hardy) used both "Shaston" and "Palladour" to refer to the town in the fictional [Wessex](/wiki/Thomas_Hardy%27s_Wessex) of his novels such as *[Jude the Obscure](/wiki/Jude_the_Obscure)*.

## History

There is no substantive evidence that Shaftesbury was the "Caer Palladur" (or "Caer Palladwr") of Celtic and Roman times, and instead the town's recorded history dates from [Anglo-Saxon times](/wiki/Anglo-Saxons). By the early eighth century there was an important minster church here, and in 880 [Alfred the Great](/wiki/Alfred_the_Great) founded a [burgh](/wiki/Burgh) (fortified settlement) here as a defence in the struggle with the [Danish](/wiki/Danes_(Germanic_tribe)) invaders. The burgh is recorded in the early-10th-century [Burghal Hidage](/wiki/Burghal_Hidage) as one of only three that existed in the county (the others being at [Wareham](/wiki/Wareham,_Dorset) and 'Bredy' – which is probably [Bridport](/wiki/Bridport)).

The ruins of [Shaftesbury Abbey](/wiki/Shaftesbury_Abbey)

In 888 Alfred founded [Shaftesbury Abbey](/wiki/Shaftesbury_Abbey), a [Benedictine](/wiki/Benedictine) [nunnery](/wiki/Nunnery) by the town's east gate, and appointed his daughter Ethelgifu as the first [abbess](/wiki/Abbess). [Æthelstan](/wiki/%C3%86thelstan) founded two royal mints, which struck pennies bearing the town's name, and the abbey became the wealthiest Benedictine nunnery in England. On 20 February 981 the relics of St [Edward the Martyr](/wiki/Edward_the_Martyr), the teenage King of England, were transferred from Wareham and received at the abbey with great ceremony, thereafter turning Shaftesbury into a major site of pilgrimage for miracles of healing.

[King Canute](/wiki/Cnut) died here in 1035, though he was buried at [Winchester](/wiki/Winchester). [Edward the Confessor](/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor) licensed a third mint for the town. By the time of the [Norman conquest](/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England) in 1066 Shaftesbury had 257 houses, though many were destroyed in the ensuing years of conflict, and by the time the [Domesday Book](/wiki/Domesday_Book) was compiled twenty years later, there were only 177 houses remaining, though this still meant that Shaftesbury was the largest town in Dorset at that time. In the [first English civil war](/wiki/The_Anarchy) (1135–1154) between [Empress Matilda](/wiki/Empress_Matilda) and [King Stephen](/wiki/Stephen,_King_of_England), an [adulterine castle](/wiki/Adulterine_castle) or fortified house was built on a small [promontory](/wiki/Promontory) at the western edge of the hill on which the old town was built. The site on Castle Hill, also known locally as Boltbury, is now under grass and is a [scheduled monument](/wiki/Scheduled_monument).

In 1240 Cardinal [Otto of Tonengo](/wiki/Otto_of_Tonengo), legate to the [Apostolic See](/wiki/Apostolic_See) of [Pope Gregory IX](/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX) visited the abbey and confirmed a charter of 1191, the first entered in the Glastonbury chartulary. During the [Middle Ages](/wiki/Middle_Ages) the abbey was the central focus of the town; the abbey's great wealth was acknowledged in a popular saying at the time, which stated that "If the abbot of Glastonbury could marry the abbess of Shaftesbury their heir would hold more land than the king of England". In 1260 a charter to hold a market was granted. By 1340 the mayor had become a recognised figure, sworn in by the steward of the abbess. In 1392 [Richard II](/wiki/Richard_II_of_England) confirmed a grant of two markets on different days. Edwardstowe, Shaftesbury's oldest surviving building, was built on Bimport at some time between 1400 and 1539. Also in this period a medieval farm owned by the Abbess of Shaftesbury was established, on a site now occupied by the Tesco supermarket car park.

Edwardstowe

In 1539, the last Abbess of Shaftesbury, [Elizabeth Zouche](/wiki/Elizabeth_Zouche), signed a deed of surrender, the (by then extremely wealthy) abbey was demolished, and its lands sold, leading to a temporary decline in the town. [Sir Thomas Arundell](/wiki/Thomas_Arundell_of_Wardour_Castle) purchased the abbey and much of the town in 1540, but when he was later exiled for treason his lands were forfeit, and the lands passed to Pembroke then [Anthony Ashley-Cooper, 7th Earl of Shaftesbury](/wiki/Anthony_Ashley-Cooper,_7th_Earl_of_Shaftesbury), and finally to the Grosvenors.

Shaftesbury was a parliamentary constituency returning two members from 1296 to the [Reform Act of 1832](/wiki/Reform_Act_1832), when it was reduced to one, and in 1884 the separate constituency was abolished.

In *Survey of Dorsetshire*, written in about 1630 by Thomas Gerard of the Dorset village of [Trent](/wiki/Trent,_Dorset), Shaftesbury is described as a "faire Thorough Faire, much frequented by Travellers to and from London".

The town was broadly Parliamentarian in the [Civil War](/wiki/English_Civil_War), but was in Royalist hands. [Wardour Castle](/wiki/Wardour_Castle) fell to Parliamentary forces in 1643; Parliamentary forces surrounded the town in August 1645, when it was a centre of local [clubmen](/wiki/Clubmen) activity. The clubmen were arrested and sent to trial in [Sherborne](/wiki/Sherborne). Shaftesbury took no part in the [Monmouth Rebellion](/wiki/Monmouth_Rebellion) of 1685.

In the 17th century the cloth industry formed part of Shaftesbury's economy, though much of the actual production took place as a [cottage industry](/wiki/Cottage_industry) in the surrounding area. In the 18th century the town produced a coarse white woollen cloth called 'swanskin', that was used by fishermen of [Newfoundland](/wiki/Newfoundland) and for uniforms. [Buttonmaking](/wiki/Dorset_button) also became important around this time, though with the later advent of [industrialisation](/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) this subsequently declined, resulting in unemployment, starvation and emigration, with 350 families leaving for Canada. [Malting](/wiki/Malting) and [brewing](/wiki/Brewing) were also significant in the 17th and 18th centuries, and like other Dorset towns such as [Dorchester](/wiki/Dorchester,_Dorset) and [Blandford Forum](/wiki/Blandford_Forum), Shaftesbury became known for its beer.

The railways however bypassed the town, which had consequences for Shaftesbury's economy; during the 19th century the town's brewing industry was reduced to serving only local markets, as towns elsewhere in the country could transport their produce more cheaply. During the 19th century the population of the town grew little. The population was 3,170 by the time of the 1841 census.

St Peter's Church and [Shaftesbury Town Hall](/wiki/Shaftesbury_Town_Hall)

The town hall was built in 1837 by [Earl Grosvenor](/wiki/Richard_Grosvenor,_2nd_Marquess_of_Westminster) after the guildhall was pulled down to widen High Street. It has been designated by [English Heritage](/wiki/English_Heritage) as a Grade II listed building. [Shaftesbury Town Hall](/wiki/Shaftesbury_Town_Hall) is next to the 15th-century St Peter's Church which is Grade II\* listed. The [Westminster Memorial Hospital](/wiki/Westminster_Memorial_Hospital) was constructed on Bimport in the mid-19th century with a legacy from the wife of the [Duke of Westminster](/wiki/Richard_Grosvenor,_2nd_Marquess_of_Westminster).

In 1918 Lord Stalbridge, 2nd Baron Stalbridge, Hugh Grosvenor, sold a large portion of the town, which was purchased by a syndicate and auctioned piece by piece over three days. The entire Stalbridge Estate of some 13,500 acres was sold and the title became extinct with the death of Hugh Grosvenor, 2nd Baron Stalbridge in 1949. His only son and heir had been killed in a flying accident in 1930.

Most of Shaftesbury's buildings date from no earlier than the 18th century, as the [Saxon](/wiki/Saxons) and most of the medieval buildings have not survived.

## Governance

In the [United Kingdom national parliament](/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom), Shaftesbury is in the [North Dorset parliamentary constituency](/wiki/North_Dorset_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), represented since 2015 by [Simon Hoare](/wiki/Simon_Hoare) of the [Conservative Party](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)). In local government, Shaftesbury is administered by [Dorset Council](/wiki/Dorset_Council_(UK)) (a [unitary authority](/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England)) and Shaftesbury Town Council, which has responsibilities that include open spaces and recreational facilities, allotments, litter, street markets, public conveniences, grants to voluntary organisations, cemetery provision, bus shelters, crime prevention initiatives, civic events and the town hall, planning (as a consultee) and the war memorial.

Shaftesbury is covered by an electoral ward called Shaftesbury Town, which elects two members to Dorset Council. Prior to [changes in 2019](/wiki/2019%E2%80%932023_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England), the town elected one member of [Dorset County Council](/wiki/Dorset_County_Council) and four members of [North Dorset District Council](/wiki/North_Dorset_District_Council).

## Geography

The old centre of Shaftesbury is sited on a westward-pointing [promontory](/wiki/Promontory) of high ground in northeast Dorset, on the [scarp edge](/wiki/Escarpment) of a range of hills that extend south and east into [Cranborne Chase](/wiki/Cranborne_Chase) and neighbouring [Wiltshire](/wiki/Wiltshire). The town's built-up area extends down the promontory slopes to lower ground at St James, Alcester and Enmore Green, and eastwards across the [watershed](/wiki/Drainage_divide) towards the hill's dip slope. Shaftesbury's altitude is between about 165 metres (541 feet) at the lowest streets below the promontory, to about 235 m (771 ft) at Wincombe Business Park on the hilltop in the north, with the promontory and town centre being at about 215 m (705 ft). Below the town to the west is the [Blackmore Vale](/wiki/Blackmore_Vale), which undulates between about 60 and 110 m (200 and 360 ft). About 2 mi (3 km) west of the town and within the Blackmore Vale is the conical mound of [Duncliffe Hill](/wiki/Duncliffe_Hill), visible for miles and home to Duncliffe Wood and a nature reserve. The countryside east of the town is part of the [Cranborne Chase and West Wiltshire Downs](/wiki/Cranborne_Chase_and_West_Wiltshire_Downs) [Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty](/wiki/Area_of_Outstanding_Natural_Beauty).

Geologically, Shaftesbury's hill mostly comprises [Upper Greensand](/wiki/Greensand), which is overlain by Lower [Chalk](/wiki/Chalk) in the east. These date from the [Cretaceous](/wiki/Cretaceous), with the greensand having been formed in the [Albian](/wiki/Albian) and early [Cenomanian](/wiki/Cenomanian), and the chalk also in the Cenomanian. The greensand is composed of three [beds](/wiki/Bed_(geology)): the oldest and lowest is a layer of Cann Sand, which is found in the lower parts of the town, such as St James and Alcester, that are below the promontory; above this is a layer of Shaftesbury Sandstone, which generally forms the steepest slopes around the promontory, and on top of this is a layer of Boyne Hollow Chert, which is found on top of the hill and on which most of the town is built. Below the Cann Sand, on the lower slopes of the hill to the north, west and south of the town, are extensive landslip deposits.

## Economy

In 2012 there were 3,400 people employed in Shaftesbury, 65% of whom were working full-time and 35% part-time. Excluding agriculture, the most important employment sectors were public administration, education and health (31% of non-agricultural employment), production and construction (29%), and distribution, accommodation and food (26%). Significant employers include [Dorset County Council](/wiki/Dorset_County_Council), [Pork Farms](/wiki/Pork_Farms), [Guys Marsh Prison](/wiki/Guys_Marsh_(HM_Prison)), [Royal Mail](/wiki/Royal_Mail), [Tesco](/wiki/Tesco), [Port Regis School](/wiki/Port_Regis_School), Wessex Electricals, Stalbridge Linen Services, Blackmore Press and [Dorset Chilled Foods](/wiki/Northern_Foods). There are two industrial estates in the town: Longmead Industrial Estate, covering 7.7 hectares (19 acres), and Wincombe Business Park, covering 6.5 hectares (16 acres).

In 2005 there were 75 shops in the town, with a total floorspace of 7,200 square metres (78,000 sq ft). The retail catchment area for major food shopping extends about 4+1⁄2 mi (7 km) in all directions. National [retail chains](/wiki/Chain_store) with a presence in the town include [Boots](/wiki/Boots_UK), [Superdrug](/wiki/Superdrug), Tesco and [TGJones](/wiki/TGJones).

A site has been identified for a projected parkway station on the [West of England](/wiki/West_of_England) main railway line. It would be situated to the north of the town, beneath the A350 road, and a bus service would connect it with the town. Currently the nearest railway station is located in neighbouring [Gillingham](/wiki/Gillingham,_Dorset).

## Demography

In the [2011 census](/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2011) Shaftesbury's civil parish had 3,493 dwellings, 3,235 households and a population of 7,314. The average age of inhabitants was 43, compared to 39.3 for England as a whole. 22.1% of inhabitants were age 65 or older, compared to 16.4% for England as a whole. 92% of Shaftesbury's residents were born in the United Kingdom, compared to 86.2% for England as a whole. ## Culture, art and media

Shaftesbury Snowdrops

Shaftesbury Arts Centre was established in 1957 and stages a variety of exhibitions, performances, workshops and training courses. It is based in the old covered market in the town centre and is a charitable company that is run wholly by its volunteer members.

Shaftesbury has two museums: Gold Hill Museum at the top of Gold Hill, and Shaftesbury Abbey Museum in the abbey grounds. Gold Hill Museum was founded in 1946 and displays many artefacts that relate to the history of Shaftesbury and the surrounding area, including Dorset's oldest fire engine, dating from 1744. Shaftesbury Abbey Museum tells the story of the abbey and also has a herb garden and medieval orchard.

Shaftesbury Snowdrops is a Diamond Jubilee Community Legacy with the aim of creating a series of free and accessible [snowdrop](/wiki/Galanthus_nivalis) walks by planting snowdrops within the publicly open spaces and along the pathways throughout the town. The project was started in the winter of 2012 with the planting of 60,000 bulbs. Since 2013 there has been an annual Snowdrop Festival to encourage tourists to see the snowdrops in flower. Highlights of the festival include the Snowdrop Art Exhibition and the Snowdrop Lantern Parade. In 2014 Shaftesbury Snowdrops started a heritage collection of rare and unusual snowdrops. These are held in trust for the people of Shaftesbury and displayed in Shaftesbury Abbey during the annual Snowdrop Festival. The collection is being built through sponsorship and donations.

Gold Hill Fair usually occurs in the first weekend of July and has food stalls, arts stalls and local music that can be found in the abbey ruins.

In 2016, Shaftesbury's first open arts fringe festival was organised and has since grown to become one of the key fringe festivals in the country. The fringe is always held on the first weekend in July and attracts an eccentric mix of performers from local singers to celebrity comics heading for Edinburgh.

Steep, [cobbled](/wiki/Cobblestones) Gold Hill, with a [buttressed](/wiki/Buttress) wall to the right, a [scheduled monument](/wiki/Scheduled_monument) on the edge of Shaftesbury Abbey [precinct](/wiki/Religious_precinct)

Local news and television programmes are provided by [BBC West](/wiki/BBC_West) and [ITV West Country](/wiki/ITV_West_Country). Television signals are received from [Mendip transmitting station](/wiki/Mendip_transmitting_station).

Shaftesbury's local radio stations are [BBC Radio Solent](/wiki/BBC_Radio_Solent), [Vale FM](/wiki/Vale_FM), and [Heart West](/wiki/Heart_West). The town is home to a volunteer-run [community radio](/wiki/Community_radio_in_the_United_Kingdom) station, Alfred, broadcasting local news, speech programming and music on 107.3 FM and online. It was awarded an [Ofcom](/wiki/Ofcom) licence in 2020, having grown from a weekly podcast which went daily during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic). The podcast won the gold award in the community radio awards in 2021.

### Representations

[Thomas Hardy](/wiki/Thomas_Hardy) used the names *Shaston* or *Palladour* to describe Shaftesbury in the fictional [Wessex](/wiki/Thomas_Hardy%27s_Wessex) of his novels. In *[Jude the Obscure](/wiki/Jude_the_Obscure)* he described the loss of the town's former architectural glories, principally the abbey:

> Vague imaginings of its castle, its three mints, its magnificent apsidal abbey, the chief glory of south Wessex, its twelve churches, its shrines, chantries, hospitals, its gabled freestone mansions—all now ruthlessly swept away—throw the visitor, even against his will, into a pensive melancholy, which the stimulating atmosphere and limitless landscape around him can scarcely dispel.

— Thomas Hardy, *Jude the Obscure*

In the 1970s [Ridley Scott](/wiki/Ridley_Scott) used [Gold Hill](/wiki/Gold_Hill,_Shaftesbury), a steep cobbled street in the town, as the setting for a television advertisement for [Hovis](/wiki/Hovis) bread, in which a bread delivery boy is seen pushing his bicycle up the street before freewheeling back down. The advertisement made the street nationally famous.

## Transport

Shaftesbury is served by the [A30](/wiki/A30_road), between [Salisbury](/wiki/Salisbury) and [Yeovil](/wiki/Yeovil), and the [A350](/wiki/A350_road), which connects [Poole](/wiki/Poole) and [Chippenham](/wiki/Chippenham). The town is 7 miles (11 km) south of the main [A303](/wiki/A303_road) trunk road between [London](/wiki/London) and [South West England](/wiki/South_West_England).

Shaftesbury has never had a direct railway connection. The challenging topography likely played a significant role in this, as constructing a railway up the steep slopes would have been difficult and costly. In 1859, the [Salisbury and Yeovil Railway](/wiki/Salisbury_and_Yeovil_Railway) established a station at [Semley](/wiki/Semley_railway_station), 2+1⁄2 miles (4.0 km) north of Shaftesbury. This station allowed residents and visitors to access the broader railway network.

Semley station closed in the [Beeching cuts](/wiki/Beeching_cuts) of 1966. The closest railway station to Shaftesbury is now [Gillingham railway station (Dorset)](/wiki/Gillingham_railway_station_(Dorset)), 4 miles (6.4 km) northwest of the town, on the [West of England line](/wiki/West_of_England_line) from [London Waterloo](/wiki/London_Waterloo_station) to Exeter.

Local bus services connect the town to surrounding villages and towns.

## Amenities

Shaftesbury has a [non-League football](/wiki/Non-League_football) club, [Shaftesbury F.C.](/wiki/Shaftesbury_F.C.), who play at Cockrams. The Shaftesbury Lido, an open air swimming pool at Barton Hill, is open during the summer months.

Shaftesbury has a community hospital, opened in 1874 and named Westminster Memorial Hospital in memory of the [Marquis of Westminster](/wiki/Richard_Grosvenor,_2nd_Marquess_of_Westminster), whose widow donated the site. The town has a public library operated by Dorset Council.

## Notable people

- [Cnut the Great](/wiki/Cnut), King of England, Denmark, and Norway, died in Shaftesbury on 12 November 1035; he was buried in [Winchester](/wiki/Winchester).

- [William Chamberlayne](/wiki/William_Chamberlayne_(poet)), poet, lived there his whole life, from 1619 to 1689.

- [Anthony Ashley-Cooper, 3rd Earl of Shaftesbury](/wiki/Anthony_Ashley-Cooper,_3rd_Earl_of_Shaftesbury) (1671–1713), an influential thinker on modern aesthetics.
- [John Haime](/wiki/John_Haime) (1708–1784), Methodist preacher.
- [Charles Fox Bennett](/wiki/Charles_Fox_Bennett), born in Shaftesbury in 1793, became premier of the [Colony of Newfoundland](/wiki/Newfoundland_Colony) from 1870 to 1874; he was one of the colony's wealthiest merchants.
- [Richard Upjohn](/wiki/Richard_Upjohn), architect, noted for his Gothic Revival churches in the United States, including [Trinity Church](/wiki/Trinity_Church_(Manhattan)) in New York, was born in Shaftesbury in 1802.

- [Robert Newton](/wiki/Robert_Newton), actor, best known for his portrayals of [Long John Silver](/wiki/Long_John_Silver) and [Bill Sikes](/wiki/Bill_Sikes) in the 1948 [David Lean](/wiki/David_Lean) film [*Oliver Twist*](/wiki/Oliver_Twist_(1948_film)), was born there.

- [Ernie Bourne](/wiki/Ernie_Bourne), actor, known for *Blue Heelers*, *Prisoner: Cell Block H* and *Neighbours* was born in 1926 in Shaftesbury.

- Seamus Malliagh, electronic music producer, known professionally as [Iglooghost](/wiki/Iglooghost), grew up in Shaftesbury.

- Electronic music producer and artist [Kai Whiston](/wiki/Kai_Whiston) grew up in Shaftesbury.

- [Lavinia Young](/wiki/Lavinia_Young) (1911–1986 ), born in Bimport, was elected Mayor of Shaftesbury (1970) and was [Matron](/wiki/Matron) of [Westminster Hospital](/wiki/Westminster_Hospital), London from 1951 to 1966.

- [Johnnie Walker](/wiki/Johnnie_Walker_(DJ)), BBC Radio and former [Radio Caroline](/wiki/Radio_Caroline) pirate radio DJ lived in St James.

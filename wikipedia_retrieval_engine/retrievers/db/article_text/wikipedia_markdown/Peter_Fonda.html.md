**Peter Henry Fonda** (February 23, 1940 – August 16, 2019) was an American actor, film director, and screenwriter. He was twice an [Academy Award](/wiki/Academy_Award) nominee, both for acting and screenwriting, and a two-time [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) winner for his acting. He was a member of the Fonda acting family, as the son of actor [Henry Fonda](/wiki/Henry_Fonda), the brother of actress and activist [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda), and the father of actress [Bridget Fonda](/wiki/Bridget_Fonda).

Fonda began his career on stage, winning a [New York Drama Critics' Circle Award](/wiki/New_York_Drama_Critics%27_Circle) and the [Theatre World Award](/wiki/Theatre_World_Award) for his performance in the play *[Blood, Sweat and Stanley Poole](/wiki/Blood,_Sweat_and_Stanley_Poole)*. He became a prominent figure in the [counterculture of the 1960s](/wiki/Counterculture_of_the_1960s), starring in and co-writing the film *[Easy Rider](/wiki/Easy_Rider)* (1969), which earned him an Oscar nomination for [Best Original Screenplay](/wiki/Best_Original_Screenplay). He then made his directorial debut with the [Revisionist Western](/wiki/Revisionist_Western) film *[The Hired Hand](/wiki/The_Hired_Hand)* (1971), in which he also starred. During the following decade, he established himself as an [action star](/wiki/Action_film), appearing in a variety of productions including *[Dirty Mary, Crazy Larry](/wiki/Dirty_Mary,_Crazy_Larry)* (1974) and *[Futureworld](/wiki/Futureworld)* (1976).

Fonda achieved a major critical comeback with his starring role in the drama film *[Ulee's Gold](/wiki/Ulee%27s_Gold)* (1997), receiving an Oscar nomination for Best Actor and a [Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Actor_%E2%80%93_Motion_Picture_Drama). He also won the [Golden Globe for Best Supporting Actor – Series, Miniseries or Television Film](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film) for *[The Passion of Ayn Rand](/wiki/The_Passion_of_Ayn_Rand_(film))* (1999). In 2003, Fonda received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) at 7018 Hollywood Blvd, for his contributions to the film industry.

## Early life

[Jane](/wiki/Jane_Fonda), [Henry](/wiki/Henry_Fonda) and Peter Fonda in July 1955

Fonda was born by [caesarean section](/wiki/Caesarean_section) (C-section) on February 23, 1940, at [LeRoy Hospital](/wiki/LeRoy_Hospital) in New York City, the only son of actor [Henry Fonda](/wiki/Henry_Fonda) and socialite [Frances Ford Seymour](/wiki/Frances_Ford_Seymour); his older sister is actress [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda). He and Jane had a half-sister, Frances de Villers Brokaw (1931–2008), from their mother's first marriage. Their mother committed suicide in a mental hospital when Peter, her youngest, was 10. He did not discover the circumstances or location of her death until he was 15.

One month prior to his 11th birthday, he accidentally shot himself in the abdomen and nearly died. He went to Ossining Hospital, in Ossining, New York and stayed for a few months for recovery. Years later, while taking [LSD](/wiki/LSD) with [John Lennon](/wiki/John_Lennon) and [George Harrison](/wiki/George_Harrison), he referred to this incident, saying, "I know what it's like to be dead." This inspired [the Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles)' song "[She Said She Said](/wiki/She_Said_She_Said)" from their seventh studio album *[Revolver](/wiki/Revolver_(Beatles_album))* (1966).

Peter attended the Fay School in [Southborough, Massachusetts](/wiki/Southborough,_Massachusetts), and was a member of the Class of 1954. He then matriculated in [Westminster School](/wiki/Westminster_School_(Connecticut)), a [Connecticut](/wiki/Connecticut) boarding school in [Simsbury](/wiki/Simsbury), where he graduated in 1958.

Once he graduated, Fonda studied acting in [Omaha, Nebraska](/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska), his father's hometown. While attending the [University of Nebraska Omaha](/wiki/University_of_Nebraska_Omaha), Fonda joined the [Omaha Community Playhouse](/wiki/Omaha_Community_Playhouse).

## Career

Main article: [Peter Fonda filmography](/wiki/Peter_Fonda_filmography)

### Early years and film work

Fonda guest starring with [Patty McCormack](/wiki/Patty_McCormack) in *[The New Breed](/wiki/The_New_Breed_(TV_series))* television series, 1962

Upon his return to New York, Fonda joined the Cecilwood Theatre in 1960. Afterwards, he found work on [Broadway](/wiki/Broadway_theatre) and gained notice in *[Blood, Sweat and Stanley Poole](/wiki/Blood,_Sweat_and_Stanley_Poole)*, written by [James](/wiki/James_Goldman) and [William Goldman](/wiki/William_Goldman), which ran for 84 performances in 1961. Fonda began guest starring on television shows like *[Naked City](/wiki/Naked_City_(TV_series))*, *[The New Breed](/wiki/The_New_Breed_(TV_series))*, *[Wagon Train](/wiki/Wagon_Train)*, and *[The Defenders](/wiki/The_Defenders_(1961_TV_series))*.

Fonda's first film came when producer [Ross Hunter](/wiki/Ross_Hunter) was looking for a new male actor to romance [Sandra Dee](/wiki/Sandra_Dee) in *[Tammy and the Doctor](/wiki/Tammy_and_the_Doctor)* (1963). He was cast in the role, in what was a minor hit. He followed this with a support part in *[The Victors](/wiki/The_Victors_(1963_film))* (1963), a bleak look at American soldiers in World War II, directed by [Carl Foreman](/wiki/Carl_Foreman). Fonda's performance won him a Golden Globe Award for most promising newcomer.

Fonda continued to work in television, guest starring in *[Channing](/wiki/Channing_(TV_series))*, *[Arrest and Trial](/wiki/Arrest_and_Trial)*, *[The Alfred Hitchcock Hour](/wiki/List_of_Alfred_Hitchcock_Presents_episodes)*, and *[12 O'Clock High](/wiki/Twelve_O%27Clock_High_(TV_series))*. He also tested for the role of [John F. Kennedy](/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) in *PT-109*. Fonda impressed [Robert Rossen](/wiki/Robert_Rossen) who cast him in what would be Rossen's last movie, *[Lilith](/wiki/Lilith_(film))* (1964), alongside [Warren Beatty](/wiki/Warren_Beatty), [Jean Seberg](/wiki/Jean_Seberg) and [Gene Hackman](/wiki/Gene_Hackman). Fonda's performance was well reviewed. Shortly before dying, Rossen signed him to a seven-film contract which was to start with an adaptation of *[Bang the Drum Slowly](/wiki/Bang_the_Drum_Slowly)*. Fonda graduated to a starring role in *[The Young Lovers](/wiki/The_Young_Lovers_(1964_film))* (1964), about out-of-wedlock pregnancy, the sole directorial effort of [Samuel Goldwyn Jr.](/wiki/Samuel_Goldwyn_Jr.)

### Counterculture figure and Roger Corman

Fonda in a 1962 promo photo

By the mid-1960s, Fonda was not a conventional "leading man" in Hollywood. As *[Playboy](/wiki/Playboy)* magazine reported, Fonda had established a "solid reputation as a dropout". He had become outwardly nonconformist and grew his hair long and took LSD regularly, alienating the "establishment" film industry. Desirable acting work became scarce. Through his friendships with members of the band [The Byrds](/wiki/The_Byrds), Fonda visited [The Beatles](/wiki/The_Beatles) in their rented house in [Benedict Canyon](/wiki/Benedict_Canyon) in Los Angeles in August 1965. While [John Lennon](/wiki/John_Lennon), [Ringo Starr](/wiki/Ringo_Starr), [George Harrison](/wiki/George_Harrison), and Fonda were under the influence of [LSD](/wiki/LSD), Lennon heard Fonda say, "I know what it's like to be dead." Lennon used the phrase in the lyrics for his song, "[She Said She Said](/wiki/She_Said_She_Said)", which was included on their 1966 album, *[Revolver](/wiki/Revolver_(Beatles_album))*.

In August 1966 Fonda was charged with possession of marijuana, and was later acquitted in December of that year. In November 1966 Fonda was arrested in the [Sunset Strip riot](/wiki/Sunset_Strip_curfew_riots), which the police ended forcefully. The band [Buffalo Springfield](/wiki/Buffalo_Springfield) protested the department's handling of the incident in their song "[For What It's Worth](/wiki/For_What_It%27s_Worth)". In 1967, Fonda recorded "November Night", a [45-rpm single](/wiki/Single_(music)) written by [Gram Parsons](/wiki/Gram_Parsons) for the Chisa label, backed with "[Catch the Wind](/wiki/Catch_the_Wind)" by [Donovan](/wiki/Donovan), produced by [Hugh Masekela](/wiki/Hugh_Masekela).

Fonda's first counterculture-oriented film role was as a biker in [Roger Corman](/wiki/Roger_Corman)'s [B movie](/wiki/B_movie) *[The Wild Angels](/wiki/The_Wild_Angels)* (1966). Fonda originally was to support [George Chakiris](/wiki/George_Chakiris), but graduated to the lead when Chakiris revealed he could not ride a motorcycle. In the film, Fonda delivered a "eulogy" at a fallen Angel's funeral service. The movie was a big hit at the box office, screened at the [Venice Film Festival](/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival), launched the biker movie genre, and established Peter Fonda as a movie name. Fonda made a television pilot, *High Noon: The Clock Strikes Noon Again*, filmed in December 1965. It was based on the film *[High Noon](/wiki/High_Noon)* (1952), starring [Gary Cooper](/wiki/Gary_Cooper), with Fonda in the Cooper role. It did not become a series.

Fonda next played the male lead in Corman's film *[The Trip](/wiki/The_Trip_(1967_film))* (1967), a take on the experience and "consequences" of consuming LSD, which was written by [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson). His co-stars included [Susan Strasberg](/wiki/Susan_Strasberg), [Bruce Dern](/wiki/Bruce_Dern), and [Dennis Hopper](/wiki/Dennis_Hopper). The movie was a hit. Fonda then traveled to France to appear in the [portmanteau](/wiki/Portmanteau_film) horror movie *[Spirits of the Dead](/wiki/Spirits_of_the_Dead)* (1968). His segment co-starred his sister Jane and was directed by her then-husband [Roger Vadim](/wiki/Roger_Vadim). For American television, he appeared in a movie, *[Certain Honorable Men](/wiki/Certain_Honorable_Men)* (1968), alongside [Van Heflin](/wiki/Van_Heflin), written by [Rod Serling](/wiki/Rod_Serling).

### *Easy Rider*

Replica of the "[Captain America](/wiki/Easy_Rider#Motorcycles)" [Harley-Davidson](/wiki/Harley-Davidson) [chopper](/wiki/Chopper_(motorcycle)) that Fonda rode in *Easy Rider* (1969), on display in a German museum.

Fonda produced, co-wrote and starred in *[Easy Rider](/wiki/Easy_Rider)* (1969), directed by [Dennis Hopper](/wiki/Dennis_Hopper). *Easy Rider* is about two long-haired bikers traveling through the [southwestern](/wiki/Southwestern_United_States) and southern United States where they encounter intolerance and violence. Fonda played "Wyatt", a charismatic, laconic man whose motorcycle jacket bore a large [American flag](/wiki/American_flag) across the back. [Dennis Hopper](/wiki/Dennis_Hopper) played the garrulous "Billy". [Jack Nicholson](/wiki/Jack_Nicholson) played George Hanson, an alcoholic [civil rights](/wiki/Civil_rights) lawyer who rides along with them. Fonda co-wrote the screenplay with [Terry Southern](/wiki/Terry_Southern) and Hopper.

Fonda tried to secure financing from Roger Corman and [American International Pictures](/wiki/American_International_Pictures) (AIP), with whom he had made *The Wild Angels* and *The Trip*, but they were reluctant to finance a film directed by Hopper. They succeeded in getting money from [Columbia Pictures](/wiki/Columbia_Pictures). Hopper filmed the cross-country road trip depicted almost entirely on location. Fonda had secured funding of around $360,000, largely based on the fact he knew that it was the budget [Roger Corman](/wiki/Roger_Corman) needed to make *[The Wild Angels](/wiki/The_Wild_Angels)*. The guitarist and composer [Robbie Robertson](/wiki/Robbie_Robertson), of [The Band](/wiki/The_Band), was so moved by an advance screening that he approached Fonda and tried to convince him to let him write a complete score, even though the film was nearly due for wide release. Fonda declined the offer, instead using [Steppenwolf](/wiki/Steppenwolf_(band))'s "[Born to Be Wild](/wiki/Born_to_Be_Wild)", [Bob Dylan](/wiki/Bob_Dylan)'s "[It's Alright, Ma (I'm Only Bleeding)](/wiki/It%27s_Alright,_Ma_(I%27m_Only_Bleeding))" sung by [The Byrds](/wiki/The_Byrds)' [Roger McGuinn](/wiki/Roger_McGuinn), and Robertson's own composition "[The Weight](/wiki/The_Weight)", performed by [The Band](/wiki/The_Band), among many other tracks.

The film was released to international success. Jack Nicholson was nominated for a Best Supporting Actor Oscar. Fonda, Hopper and Southern were nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. The film grossed over $40 million.

### Director and action star

After the success of *Easy Rider*, both Hopper and Fonda were sought for film projects. Hopper directed the film *[The Last Movie](/wiki/The_Last_Movie)* (1971), in which Fonda co-starred along with singer [Michelle Phillips](/wiki/Michelle_Phillips) of [The Mamas and the Papas](/wiki/The_Mamas_and_the_Papas). Fonda directed and starred in the [Western](/wiki/Western_(genre)) film *[The Hired Hand](/wiki/The_Hired_Hand)* (1971). He took the lead role in a cast that also featured [Warren Oates](/wiki/Warren_Oates), [Verna Bloom](/wiki/Verna_Bloom) and [Beat Generation](/wiki/Beat_Generation) poet [Michael McClure](/wiki/Michael_McClure). The film received mixed reviews and failed commercially upon its initial release, but many years later, in 2001, a fully restored version was shown at various film festivals and was re-released by the [Sundance Channel](/wiki/Sundance_TV) on DVD that same year in two separate editions. Fonda later directed the science fiction film *[Idaho Transfer](/wiki/Idaho_Transfer)* (1973). He did not appear in the film, and the film received mixed reviews upon its limited release. Around the same time, he co-starred with [Lindsay Wagner](/wiki/Lindsay_Wagner) in *[Two People](/wiki/Two_People_(1973_film))* (also 1973) for director [Robert Wise](/wiki/Robert_Wise), in which he portrayed a [Vietnam War](/wiki/Vietnam_War) deserter.

Fonda starred alongside [Susan George](/wiki/Susan_George_(actress)) and longtime-friend (and frequent co-star) [Adam Roarke](/wiki/Adam_Roarke) in the film *[Dirty Mary, Crazy Larry](/wiki/Dirty_Mary,_Crazy_Larry)* (1974), a film about two [NASCAR](/wiki/NASCAR) hopefuls who execute a supermarket heist to finance their jump into big-time auto racing. The film was a box-office hit that year. It led to Fonda's making a series of action movies: *[Open Season](/wiki/Open_Season_(1974_film))* (1974), with [William Holden](/wiki/William_Holden); *[Race with the Devil](/wiki/Race_with_the_Devil)* (1975), fleeing devil worshippers with Warren Oates (another hit); *[92 in the Shade](/wiki/92_in_the_Shade)* (1975), again with Oates, for writer-director [Thomas McGuane](/wiki/Thomas_McGuane); *[Killer Force](/wiki/Killer_Force)* (1976) for director [Val Guest](/wiki/Val_Guest); *[Futureworld](/wiki/Futureworld)* (1976), a sequel to *[Westworld](/wiki/Westworld_(film))* (1973), financed by AIP; *[Fighting Mad](/wiki/Fighting_Mad_(1976_film))* (1976), a reuniting with Roger Corman, directed by [Jonathan Demme](/wiki/Jonathan_Demme).

*[Outlaw Blues](/wiki/Outlaw_Blues)* (1977) was a drama, with Fonda playing a musician opposite [Susan Saint James](/wiki/Susan_Saint_James). After some more action with *[High-Ballin'](/wiki/High-Ballin%27)* (1978), Fonda returned to directing, with the controversial drama *[Wanda Nevada](/wiki/Wanda_Nevada)* (1979), wherein the 39-year-old Fonda starred as the "love" interest of the then 13-year-old [Brooke Shields](/wiki/Brooke_Shields). His father, [Henry Fonda](/wiki/Henry_Fonda), made a brief appearance as well, and it is the only film in which they performed together.

### 1980s and 1990s

Fonda was top billed in *[The Hostage Tower](/wiki/The_Hostage_Tower)* (1980), a television movie based on a story by [Alistair MacLean](/wiki/Alistair_MacLean). Fonda appeared in the hit film *[The Cannonball Run](/wiki/The_Cannonball_Run)* (1981) as the "chief biker", a tongue-in-cheek nod to his earlier motorcycle films. He also played a charismatic cult leader in *[Split Image](/wiki/Split_Image_(film))* (1982), a film that also starred [James Woods](/wiki/James_Woods), [Karen Allen](/wiki/Karen_Allen) and [Brian Dennehy](/wiki/Brian_Dennehy). Despite the strong cast and positive reviews, the film failed to find an audience.

Fonda later appeared in a series of films in the 1980s of varying genres — *[Daijōbu, My Friend](/wiki/Daij%C5%8Dbu,_My_Friend)* (1983), shot in Japan; *[Dance of the Dwarfs](/wiki/Dance_of_the_Dwarfs)* (1983); *Peppermint Peace* (1983), shot in Germany;  *[Spasms](/wiki/Spasms_(film))* (1983), a Canadian horror film with [Oliver Reed](/wiki/Oliver_Reed); *[A Reason to Live](/wiki/A_Reason_to_Live_(1985_film))* (1985), a TV movie; *[Certain Fury](/wiki/Certain_Fury)* (1985), with [Tatum O'Neal](/wiki/Tatum_O%27Neal); *Mercenary Fighters* (1988); *Hawken's Breed* (1988), a Western; *Sound* (1988); *Gli indifferenti* (1989) with [Liv Ullmann](/wiki/Liv_Ullmann); and *[The Rose Garden](/wiki/The_Rose_Garden_(film))* (1989).

In the early 1990s Fonda also contributed to the script of *[Enemy](/wiki/Enemy_(1990_film))* (1990), in which he starred. He had the lead in *Family Express* (1991) and *South Beach* (1993), but then drifted into supporting roles in many independent films: *[Deadfall](/wiki/Deadfall_(1993_film))* (1993), directed by [Christopher Coppola](/wiki/Christopher_Coppola); *[Bodies, Rest & Motion](/wiki/Bodies,_Rest_%26_Motion)* (1993), starring his daughter Bridget; *[Molly & Gina](/wiki/Molly_%26_Gina)* (1994) with [Frances Fisher](/wiki/Frances_Fisher) and [Natasha Gregson](/wiki/Natasha_Gregson); *[Love and a .45](/wiki/Love_and_a_.45)* (1994) with [Renée Zellweger](/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e_Zellweger); *[Nadja](/wiki/Nadja_(film))* (1994), produced by [David Lynch](/wiki/David_Lynch). He had a good supporting role in *[Escape from L.A.](/wiki/Escape_from_L.A.)* (1996) from [John Carpenter](/wiki/John_Carpenter) and was in *[Don't Look Back](/wiki/Don%27t_Look_Back_(1996_film))* (1996). He also guest starred on *[In the Heat of the Night](/wiki/In_the_Heat_of_the_Night_(TV_series))*.

After years of films of varying success, Fonda received high-profile critical recognition and universal praise for his performance in *[Ulee's Gold](/wiki/Ulee%27s_Gold)* (1997). He portrayed a taciturn North Florida beekeeper and Vietnam veteran who tries to save his son and granddaughter from a life of drug abuse. For his performance, he was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Actor](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actor). He had the lead in *Painted Hero* (1997). In 1998, Fonda starred in the TV movie *[The Tempest](/wiki/The_Tempest_(1998_film))*, based in part on [William Shakespeare](/wiki/William_Shakespeare)'s [play of the same name](/wiki/The_Tempest). It was directed by Jack Bender and starred Fonda, [John Glover](/wiki/John_Glover_(actor)), [Harold Perrineau](/wiki/Harold_Perrineau), and [Katherine Heigl](/wiki/Katherine_Heigl).

He played [Frank O'Connor](/wiki/Frank_O%27Connor_(actor,_born_1897)) in *[The Passion of Ayn Rand](/wiki/The_Passion_of_Ayn_Rand_(film))* (1998), a performance for which he received [a Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Supporting_Actor_%E2%80%93_Series,_Miniseries_or_Television_Film) in 2000, then appeared in the crime film *[The Limey](/wiki/The_Limey)* (1999) as Terry Valentine, an aging rock music producer who accidentally kills his younger girlfriend. The film was directed by [Steven Soderbergh](/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh).

Fonda wrote an autobiography, *Don't Tell Dad* (1998).

In the 1990s, Fonda appeared in an advertisement for [American Express](/wiki/American_Express).

### 2000s

Fonda's work in the 2000s included parts in *[South of Heaven, West of Hell](/wiki/South_of_Heaven,_West_of_Hell)* (2000), *Second Skin* (2000), *[Thomas and the Magic Railroad](/wiki/Thomas_and_the_Magic_Railroad)* (2000) *[Wooly Boys](/wiki/Wooly_Boys)* (2001), *[The Laramie Project](/wiki/The_Laramie_Project_(film))* (2001), *The Maldonado Miracle* (2003), *Capital City* (2004), *[The Heart Is Deceitful Above All Things](/wiki/The_Heart_Is_Deceitful_Above_All_Things)* (2004), *[A Thief of Time](/wiki/A_Thief_of_Time)* (2004), *[Back When We Were Grownups](/wiki/Back_When_We_Were_Grownups)* (2004), *[Supernova](/wiki/Supernova_(2005_film))* (2005), and *El cobrador: In God We Trust* (2006).

Fonda in 2009

In 2002, Fonda was inducted into the [AMA](/wiki/American_Motorcyclist_Association) [Motorcycle Hall of Fame](/wiki/Motorcycle_Hall_of_Fame).

In 2004, he provided the voice of aging [hippie](/wiki/Hippie) weed grower "The Truth" in *[Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas](/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto:_San_Andreas)*, which was one of the [best-selling](/wiki/List_of_best-selling_video_games) video games of all time.

In a 2007 interview, Fonda said that riding motorcycles helped him to focus, stating,

> I ride an [MV Agusta](/wiki/MV_Agusta). This is an Italian racing motorcycle. It forces focus. You have to be focused and in my life, in this business, focus is hard to find sometimes. So I need to force focus and that's great. The bike takes you on a free road. There's no fences on the roads I ride and I don't ride freeways. That's as much as I can tell you, because there are more lands waiting for this little Christian boy. That's not true. I'm an atheist, but what the heck.

Fonda made a return to the big screen as the [bounty hunter](/wiki/Bounty_hunter) Byron McElroy in *[3:10 to Yuma](/wiki/3:10_to_Yuma_(2007_film))* (2007), a remake of the [1957 Western](/wiki/3:10_to_Yuma_(1957_film)). He appeared with [Christian Bale](/wiki/Christian_Bale) and [Russell Crowe](/wiki/Russell_Crowe). The film received two Academy Award nominations and positive reviews from critics. He also appeared in the last scenes of the biker comedy *[Wild Hogs](/wiki/Wild_Hogs)* as Damien Blade, founder of the biker gang Del Fuegos and father of Jack, played by [Ray Liotta](/wiki/Ray_Liotta). Fonda also portrayed [Mephistopheles](/wiki/Mephisto_(Marvel_Comics)), one of two main villains in the film *[Ghost Rider](/wiki/Ghost_Rider_(2007_film))* (also 2007). Although he wanted to play the character in [the sequel](/wiki/Ghost_Rider:_Spirit_of_Vengeance), he was replaced by [Ciarán Hinds](/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_Hinds).

He appeared in *[Journey to the Center of the Earth](/wiki/Journey_to_the_Center_of_the_Earth_(2008_TV_film))* (2008), *[Japan](/wiki/Japan_(2008_film))* (2008), and *[The Perfect Age of Rock 'n' Roll](/wiki/The_Perfect_Age_of_Rock_%27n%27_Roll)* (2009) and as "The Roman", the main villain in *[The Boondock Saints II: All Saints Day](/wiki/The_Boondock_Saints_II:_All_Saints_Day)* (also 2009), the sequel to *[The Boondock Saints](/wiki/The_Boondock_Saints)*. Fonda also appeared on the television series *[Californication](/wiki/Californication_(TV_series))*.

### Later career

Fonda at the [2010 Toronto International Film Festival](/wiki/2010_Toronto_International_Film_Festival)

Fonda's later appearances include *American Bandits: Frank and Jesse James* (2010) for [Fred Olen Ray](/wiki/Fred_Olen_Ray); *[The Trouble with Bliss](/wiki/The_Trouble_with_Bliss)* (2011); episodes of *[CSI: NY](/wiki/CSI:_NY)*; *[Smitty](/wiki/Smitty_(film))* (2012); *[Harodim](/wiki/Harodim)* (2012); *[As Cool as I Am](/wiki/As_Cool_as_I_Am_(film))* (2013); *[Copperhead](/wiki/Copperhead_(2013_film))* (2013); *[The Ultimate Life](/wiki/The_Ultimate_Life)* (2013); *[The Harvest](/wiki/The_Harvest_(2013_film))* (2013); *HR* (2014); *[House of Bodies](/wiki/House_of_Bodies)* (2014); *Jesse James: Lawman* (2014) The blacklist ,(2015); *[The Runner](/wiki/The_Runner_(2015_film))* (2015) with [Nicolas Cage](/wiki/Nicolas_Cage); *[The Ballad of Lefty Brown](/wiki/The_Ballad_of_Lefty_Brown)* (2017); *[The Most Hated Woman in America](/wiki/The_Most_Hated_Woman_in_America)* (2017); *Borderland* (2017); *[You Can't Say No](/wiki/You_Can%27t_Say_No)* (2018); and *[Boundaries](/wiki/Boundaries_(2018_film))* (2018) with [Christopher Plummer](/wiki/Christopher_Plummer). His final portrayal was in the [Vietnam War](/wiki/Vietnam_War) movie *[The Last Full Measure](/wiki/The_Last_Full_Measure_(2019_film))*, whose director [Todd Robinson](/wiki/Todd_Robinson_(film_director)), has recounted that Peter Fonda was able to view that film in its entirety before his death, and got emotional upon viewing it.

## Honors

In 2000, a Golden Palm Star on the [Palm Springs, California](/wiki/Palm_Springs,_California), [Walk of Stars](/wiki/Palm_Springs_Walk_of_Stars) was dedicated to him.

## Personal life

Fonda was married three times. He married his first wife, Susan Brewer, in 1961; together they had three children: [Bridget](/wiki/Bridget_Fonda), Justin, and Thomas McGuane (stepson). They divorced in 1974 after 13 years of marriage. Fonda married his second wife, Portia Rebecca Crockett, in 1975. The marriage lasted for 36 years until they divorced in 2011. Fonda married his third wife, Margaret DeVogelaere, in 2011. The marriage lasted for eight years until Fonda's death in 2019.

### Political views

In 2011, Fonda and [Tim Robbins](/wiki/Tim_Robbins) produced *[The Big Fix](/wiki/The_Big_Fix_(2012_film))*, a documentary that examined the role of [BP](/wiki/BP) in the [*Deepwater Horizon* oil spill](/wiki/Deepwater_Horizon_oil_spill) and its effects on the [Gulf of Mexico](/wiki/Gulf_of_Mexico). At a press conference at the [Cannes Film Festival](/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival), Fonda stated that he had written to President [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) about the spill and criticized him as a "fucking traitor" for allowing "foreign boots on our soil telling our military—in this case the [Coast Guard](/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard)—what they can and could not do, and telling us, the citizens of the United States, what we could or could not do.'"

In June 2018, Fonda went on [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter) to criticize President [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump)'s administration's enforcement of U.S. immigration policy by [Jeff Sessions](/wiki/Jeff_Sessions) for separating children from their parents at the Mexican border, writing that "We should rip [Barron Trump](/wiki/Barron_Trump) from the arms of First Lady [Melania Trump](/wiki/Melania_Trump) and put him in a cage with [pedophiles](/wiki/Pedophiles)." He also suggested that Americans should seek out names of [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](/wiki/Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement) (ICE) agents in order to protest outside of their homes and the schools of their children. The [Secret Service](/wiki/United_States_Secret_Service) opened an investigation based on a report from the [Trump family](/wiki/Trump_family). White House Press Secretary [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](/wiki/Sarah_Huckabee_Sanders) (daughter of prominent Republican politician [Mike Huckabee](/wiki/Mike_Huckabee)), was also the object of Fonda's [tweets](/wiki/Tweet_(social_media)), in which he suggested that "Maybe we should take her (Sanders') children away..."

In another later deleted tweet, Fonda targeted [United States Department of Homeland Security](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Homeland_Security) Secretary [Kirstjen Nielsen](/wiki/Kirstjen_Nielsen) by calling her a "vulgar" name and calling for Nielsen to be "put in a cage and poked at by passersby ..."

Fonda stated that he deleted his tweet regarding Barron Trump, saying that he "immediately regretted it and sincerely apologize to the family for what I said and any hurt my words have caused." Backlash to Fonda's tweets resulted in a call for a boycott of his newest film, *[Boundaries](/wiki/Boundaries_(2018_film))*, and other Sony projects. Sony Pictures released *Boundaries* as planned on June 22, 2018, but released a statement stating that Fonda's comments "are abhorrent, reckless and dangerous, and we condemn them completely."

## Death

Fonda died from respiratory failure caused by [lung cancer](/wiki/Lung_cancer) at his home in [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) on August 16, 2019, at the age of 79.

Following Fonda's death, his older sister [Jane Fonda](/wiki/Jane_Fonda) made the following statement: "I am very sad. He was my sweet-hearted baby younger brother, the talker of the family. I have had beautiful alone time with him these last days. He went out laughing."

**Jill Valentine** is a character in *[Resident Evil](/wiki/Resident_Evil)* (*Biohazard* in Japan), a [survival horror](/wiki/Survival_horror) series created by the Japanese company [Capcom](/wiki/Capcom). She was introduced as one of two [player characters](/wiki/Player_characters) in the original *[Resident Evil](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(1996_video_game))* (1996) with her partner, [Chris Redfield](/wiki/Chris_Redfield), as a member of the [Raccoon City Police Department](/wiki/Raccoon_Police_Station)'s Special Tactics And Rescue Service (S.T.A.R.S.) unit. Jill and Chris fight the [Umbrella Corporation](/wiki/Umbrella_Corporation), a pharmaceutical company whose [bioterrorism](/wiki/Bioterrorism) creates [zombies](/wiki/Zombies_in_Resident_Evil) and other [bio-organic weapons](/wiki/Biological_agent), and later become founding members of the [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations)' Bioterrorism Security Assessment Alliance (BSAA).

Jill is the protagonist of several *Resident Evil* games, [novelizations](/wiki/Novelization) and films and has appeared in other game franchises, including *[Marvel vs. Capcom](/wiki/Marvel_vs._Capcom)*, *[Project X Zone](/wiki/Project_X_Zone)*, *[Teppen](/wiki/Teppen)*, and *[Dead by Daylight](/wiki/Dead_by_Daylight)*. In later games, such as the [2002 *Resident Evil* remake](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(2002_video_game)), *[The Umbrella Chronicles](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Umbrella_Chronicles)* (2007), *[Resident Evil 5](/wiki/Resident_Evil_5)* (2009), and *[The Mercenaries 3D](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Mercenaries_3D)* (2011), her features were based on Canadian model and actress [Julia Voth](/wiki/Julia_Voth). Several actresses have portrayed Jill, including [Sienna Guillory](/wiki/Sienna_Guillory) and [Hannah John-Kamen](/wiki/Hannah_John-Kamen) in the [live-action *Resident Evil* films](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series)).

[Video game publications](/wiki/Video_game_journalism) described Jill as among the most popular and iconic video game characters, and praised her as the most likable and consistent *Resident Evil* character. She has received acclaim and criticism with regard to [gender representation in video games](/wiki/Gender_representation_in_video_games). Several publications praised the series for its portrayal of women, and considered Jill significantly less [sexualized](/wiki/Sexual_objectification) than other female game characters; she was also cited as an example of a female character who was as competent as her male counterparts. Others said that she was weakened as a protagonist by attributes which undermined her role as a heroine – specifically, an unrealistic body shape which did not reflect her military background. Some of Jill's overtly-sexualized costumes have also been criticized.

## Concept and design

[Julia Voth](/wiki/Julia_Voth), pictured [cosplaying](/wiki/Cosplay) Jill's original design from the first *Resident Evil* and its remake, was the model for the character's appearance from 2002 until the [2020 remake of *Resident Evil 3*](/wiki/Resident_Evil_3_(2020_video_game)).

Jill was introduced as one of two [playable protagonists](/wiki/Player_character) with her partner, [Chris Redfield](/wiki/Chris_Redfield), in [Capcom](/wiki/Capcom)'s 1996 [survival horror](/wiki/Survival_horror) video game *[Resident Evil](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(1996_video_game)).* She was created by [director](/wiki/Video_game_creative_director) [Shinji Mikami](/wiki/Shinji_Mikami) and designer Isao Ohishi. To avoid the [sexual objectification](/wiki/Sexual_objectification) of [women in video games](/wiki/Gender_representation_in_video_games), Mikami refused to eroticize or depict women as submissive in *Resident Evil*; Jill was characterized as independent. In 2025, director [Hideki Kamiya](/wiki/Hideki_Kamiya) stated that he took the name "Jill" from one of his reference books, and the name "Valentine" was chosen randomly after he heard someone say [Valentine's Day](/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day).

Of Japanese and French descent, she excelled at [bomb disposal](/wiki/Bomb_disposal) during her [training](/wiki/Delta_Force#Training) with [Delta Force](/wiki/Delta_Force) and later joined the Special Tactics and Rescue Service (S.T.A.R.S.) [special operations](/wiki/Special_operations) unit. Experienced with weapons, explosives and [lock picking](/wiki/Lock_picking), she was intelligent, brave, and loyal. Although their [storylines](/wiki/Plot_(narrative)) progress in "the same general direction", the [gameplay](/wiki/Gameplay) differs for Jill and Chris; her [inventory](/wiki/Inventory_(videogames)) is larger and she has a lock-pick, giving her early access to more [health](/wiki/Health_pack) and ammunition. Based on these differences, Jill was recommended for first-time players of *Resident Evil*. Game designer [Jun Takeuchi](/wiki/Jun_Takeuchi) gave each character a unique clothing style to make them instantly recognisable, with Jill's character wearing a distinctive blue beret.

According to [digital media](/wiki/Digital_media) scholar [Esther MacCallum-Stewart](/wiki/Esther_MacCallum-Stewart), Mikami's female characters possess unique qualities (making them viable choices for players, instead of their male counterparts and their [combat attire](/wiki/Combat_uniform) helped them avoid criticism for catering to the [male gaze](/wiki/Male_gaze). Mikami said, "I don't know if I've put more emphasis on women characters, but when I do introduce them, it is never as objects. In [other] games, they will be peripheral characters with ridiculous breast physics. I avoid that sort of obvious eroticism." Despite this, subsequent games in the series not directed by Mikami depicted Jill in revealing costumes. Media scholars said that players have been encouraged to objectify and identify with the character, and several suggested that Mikami's initial portrayal of Jill as a military professional tempered the ability of subsequent game directors to overtly sexualize the character.

Jill was redesigned over the course of the series; in the [2002 remake](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(2002_video_game)) of the original game, her appearance was based on Canadian model and actress [Julia Voth](/wiki/Julia_Voth). Capcom producer [Hiroyuki Kobayashi](/wiki/Hiroyuki_Kobayashi_(producer)) said that they made her more *[kawaii](/wiki/Kawaii)* in the remake, but kept her tough and muscular. Voth was reused for *[Resident Evil: The Umbrella Chronicles](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Umbrella_Chronicles)* (2007) and *[Resident Evil 5](/wiki/Resident_Evil_5)* (2009). *Resident Evil 5* has Chris as a protagonist, and its designers opted to focus on how both characters had aged since the original game. Jill underwent another redesign for *Resident Evil 5* to reflect her status as a test subject in [biological research](/wiki/Biological_research) experiments. Depicted with blonde hair, pale skin, and a tight-fitting outfit – described as a [battle suit](/wiki/Catsuit) – her changes represent the effects of the experiments. Fans criticized the new appearance as an example of [whitewashing](/wiki/Whitewashing_(beauty)). MacCallum-Stewart suggested that the series' popularity was damaged by the unexplained reappearance of Jill as a "mind-controlled [BDSM](/wiki/BDSM) assassin". In the [2020 remake](/wiki/Resident_Evil_3_(2020_video_game)) of *Resident Evil 3*, the character is based on Russian model Sasha Zotova. Jill's redesign reflects the developers' vision behind her reimagination as an action hero; because the art direction for the remake is based on [photorealism](/wiki/Photorealism), Capcom wanted to ensure that Jill fit into the game-world environment.

Alternate costumes as rewards for players have been a staple of the *Resident Evil* series. Completing the 2002 remake unlocked the ability to dress Jill in her *[Resident Evil 3: Nemesis](/wiki/Resident_Evil_3:_Nemesis)* miniskirt costume and as [Sarah Connor](/wiki/Sarah_Connor_(Terminator)) in the 1991 film *[Terminator 2: Judgment Day](/wiki/Terminator_2:_Judgment_Day)*. The miniskirt was added during development at the staff's request; developers changed camera angles to reduce [upskirting](/wiki/Upskirt), matching the colors of the skirt and underwear to make any upskirting less obvious. The outfit reappeared in *[Resident Evil: The Mercenaries 3D](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Mercenaries_3D)* (2011). A prequel to the events of *Resident Evil 5*, the [downloadable content](/wiki/Downloadable_content) (DLC) episode *Lost in Nightmares* includes an alternative outfit for Jill which was based on military clothing and [sportswear](/wiki/Sportswear_(activewear)). Since *[Resident Evil: Revelations](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Revelations)* (2012) was set at sea, her costume resembled a [wetsuit](/wiki/Wetsuit). The initial design featured more tactical gear attached to her [buoyancy control device](/wiki/Buoyancy_compensator_(diving)), but it was removed to highlight her physical contours. Her alternate outfit in the game, a revealing pirate costume, was colorful to contrast with the storyline's dark themes.

### Voice-over and live-action actresses

The actresses who appeared in the original game's [live-action](/wiki/Live_action) [cutscenes](/wiki/Cutscene) and recorded the [voice work](/wiki/Voice-over) were credited only by their first names; Jill was played by "Inezh", a high school student. [Voice actresses](/wiki/Voice_acting) who played Jill included [Catherine Disher](/wiki/Catherine_Disher) in *Resident Evil 3*, Heidi Anderson in the 2002 remake, [Kathleen Barr](/wiki/Kathleen_Barr) in Capcom's 2004 action-adventure game *[Under the Skin](/wiki/Under_the_Skin_(video_game))*, and [Tara Platt](/wiki/Tara_Platt) in *[Pachislot](/wiki/Pachislot) Biohazard* (an [arcade game](/wiki/Arcade_game) released in Japan). [Patricia Ja Lee](/wiki/Patricia_Ja_Lee) provided the voice and [motion capture](/wiki/Motion_capture) for *The Umbrella Chronicles* and *Resident Evil 5*. [Kari Wahlgren](/wiki/Kari_Wahlgren) voiced the character in the 2011 game *[Marvel vs. Capcom 3: Fate of Two Worlds](/wiki/Marvel_vs._Capcom_3:_Fate_of_Two_Worlds)* and *[Ultimate Marvel vs. Capcom 3](/wiki/Ultimate_Marvel_vs._Capcom_3)*. Jill was voiced in *Revelations* by [Michelle Ruff](/wiki/Michelle_Ruff), who returned for the non-[canon](/wiki/Canon_(fiction)) game *[Resident Evil: Operation Raccoon City](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Operation_Raccoon_City)*. Nicole Tompkins voiced and provided [motion capture](/wiki/Motion_capture) for Jill in the 2020 *Resident Evil 3* remake, and reprised her role in the [CG](/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery) animated film *[Resident Evil: Death Island](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Death_Island)*. [Atsuko Yuya](/wiki/Atsuko_Yuya) voiced Jill in the Japanese versions of the games.

The character appeared in three entries of the original [*Resident Evil* film series](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film_series)), where she was played by English actress [Sienna Guillory](/wiki/Sienna_Guillory). [Hannah John-Kamen](/wiki/Hannah_John-Kamen) played the role in the 2021 film, *[Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Welcome_to_Raccoon_City)*.

Each game in the series is set in the fictional American metropolis of [Raccoon City](/wiki/Raccoon_City) until its destruction at the end of *Resident Evil 3: Nemesis*. Subsequent games with Jill are set in Russia Africa, and on the Mediterranean Sea. The original game is set in July 1998 in a mansion on the outskirts of Raccoon City, which Jill and her team enter while searching for [their colleagues](/wiki/Resident_Evil_Zero). Working with partner [Barry Burton](/wiki/Barry_Burton), she discovers that the mansion is a façade for a [biological warfare](/wiki/Biological_warfare) laboratory operated by the [Umbrella Corporation](/wiki/Umbrella_Corporation); its [undead](/wiki/Undead) occupants are scientists who developed the T-virus [mutagen](/wiki/Mutagen). Her commander, [Albert Wesker](/wiki/Albert_Wesker), is a [double agent](/wiki/Double_agent) for Umbrella. Jill and Chris are among five survivors of the incident, who form a strong friendship and become passionate opponents of [bioterrorism](/wiki/Bioterrorism).

Jill did not appear in *[Resident Evil 2](/wiki/Resident_Evil_2)* (1998), as the production team used new protagonists ([Leon S. Kennedy](/wiki/Leon_S._Kennedy) and [Claire Redfield](/wiki/Claire_Redfield)) to preserve the original game's horror elements; Mikami thought that Jill and Chris would be too experienced to be frightened by the sequel's events. She returned as the sole protagonist in *Resident Evil 3* (1999). Mikami – the lead producer of *Nemesis* and the concurrently-produced *[Resident Evil – Code: Veronica](/wiki/Resident_Evil_%E2%80%93_Code:_Veronica)* (2000) – wanted each game to highlight a female character who had previously appeared in the series; Jill is the protagonist in *Nemesis*, and Claire is the [lead character](/wiki/Lead_character) in *Code: Veronica*. Observers suggested that these decisions were made as a result of the success of the *[Tomb Raider](/wiki/Tomb_Raider)* series, with [Lara Croft](/wiki/Lara_Croft) as the protagonist.

*Nemesis* is set two months after the first game, when Jill joins the [Raccoon City Police Department](/wiki/Raccoon_Police_Station) (RCPD) to protect as many civilians as possible from the T-virus. The reason given by Capcom for her informal clothing in this entry was her resignation from the police immediately before the city was infected with the virus. Jill quit in protest over the failure of law enforcement to take action against Umbrella, but remained in the city to investigate the corporation. The police uniform she wore in the original game was replaced with a less-formal blue [tube top](/wiki/Tube_top), black [miniskirt](/wiki/Miniskirt), and [knee-high boots](/wiki/Knee-high_boots).

In *Nemesis*, Jill escapes Raccoon City before its destruction by a [nuclear strike](/wiki/Nuclear_warfare) as part of a [U.S. government](/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States) [cover-up](/wiki/Cover-up). She is pursued by [Nemesis](/wiki/Nemesis_(Resident_Evil)), a [supersoldier](/wiki/Supersoldier) tasked with killing the remaining S.T.A.R.S. team members. Instead of killing Jill, Nemesis infects her with the T-virus; her new partner, [Carlos Oliveira](/wiki/Carlos_Oliveira_(Resident_Evil)) – a former Umbrella [mercenary](/wiki/Mercenary) – cures her of the infection with an Umbrella-produced vaccine. *The Umbrella Chronicles* is set in 2003, when Jill and Chris join a private organization to expose Umbrella's biological-warfare activities (leading a group to destroy their only remaining research facility). After the fall of the corporation, Jill and Carlos become founding members of the [United Nations](/wiki/United_Nations)' Bioterrorism Security Assessment Alliance (BSAA). In *Revelations* – set two years later – Jill and new partner [Parker Luciani](/wiki/Parker_Luciani) are sent to rescue Chris, who is allegedly being held hostage on a [ghost ship](/wiki/Ghost_ship) in the Mediterranean. Once aboard, she discovers that the ship is infested with a new type of mutagen capable of infecting the [aquatic ecosystem](/wiki/Aquatic_ecosystem). Jill and Chris unravel a political conspiracy involving an earlier mutagenic outbreak and a botched investigation by a rival agency.

*Resident Evil 5* takes place in 2009 in the fictional African town of Kijuju, where [terrorists](/wiki/Terrorism) are turning local residents into [zombies](/wiki/Zombies_in_Resident_Evil). One of these terrorists is Jill, who was [missing in action](/wiki/Missing_in_action) for the previous two-and-a-half years. Set in 2006, the *Lost in Nightmares* [DLC](/wiki/Downloadable_content) has Jill and Chris searching a mansion for [Umbrella's founder](/wiki/Oswell_E._Spencer); to save Chris from Wesker, Jill tackles Wesker through a window. Neither body is recovered, and Jill is [declared dead](/wiki/Declared_death_in_absentia). She was actually injured by the fall and taken hostage by Wesker, who then uses her as a test subject in his biological experiments. The [antibodies](/wiki/Antibodies) produced by Jill's system as a result of her *Nemesis*-era T-virus infection are the basis for the Uroboros Virus, the catalyst for the events of *Resident Evil 5*.

During *Resident Evil 5*, Chris discovers that Jill is alive; Wesker attached a mind-control device to her which forces her to commit terrorist acts and fight Chris and his new partner, local BSAA agent [Sheva Alomar](/wiki/Sheva_Alomar). Jill has enough self-control to open her outfit so the device can be seen and removed. After its removal, she explains that she knew what she was doing but was unable to control her actions and urges Chris and Sheva to continue their mission. Another DLC episode, *Desperate Escape*, describes how Jill escaped safely with the help of local BSAA agent Josh Stone before they help Chris and Sheva kill Wesker.

### Other appearances

The character appears in several *Resident Evil* films. She was scheduled to be the protagonist of the first *[Resident Evil](/wiki/Resident_Evil_(film))* movie (2002), under the direction of [George A. Romero](/wiki/George_A._Romero). When [Paul W. S. Anderson](/wiki/Paul_W._S._Anderson) took over from Romero, he created a new character for the film series: [Alice](/wiki/Alice_(Resident_Evil)), played by [Milla Jovovich](/wiki/Milla_Jovovich). Jill appears in the 2004 sequel, *[Resident Evil: Apocalypse](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Apocalypse)*, as a disgraced police officer who escapes the ruins of Raccoon City with the help of Alice and other survivors. Her outfit in the film is based on her clothing from *Resident Evil 3*. Anderson considered describing the costume as an [undercover](/wiki/Undercover_operation) outfit, and Jovovich suggested a [heat wave](/wiki/Heat_wave) as the explanation. Anderson eventually decided to ignore the issue altogether, saying that people who criticized her attire "probably shouldn't be watching a *Resident Evil* movie [at all]."

Jill appears in a [post-credits scene](/wiki/Post-credits_scene) in *[Resident Evil: Afterlife](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Afterlife)* (2010), wearing the mind-controlling device from *Resident Evil 5* and leading an attack against Alice, Chris, Claire, and the survivors they rescued after a viral outbreak in Los Angeles. In *[Resident Evil: Retribution](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Retribution)* (2012), she is an antagonist programmed to capture Alice who regains control of herself after Alice removes Wesker's mind-control device. The film includes a fight between Jill and Alice with about 200 moves. She did not appear in the final film, *[Resident Evil: The Final Chapter](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_The_Final_Chapter)* (2016), apparently dying [offscreen](/wiki/Offscreen). According to Jovovich, Jill was excluded because there were too many other *Resident Evil* characters. The *Resident Evil* film series received consistently-negative reviews; *[Cinefantastique](/wiki/Cinefantastique)* praised Guillory's performance in *Apocalypse* as the film's only highlight. In the reboot film *Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City* (2021), Jill is played by Hannah John-Kamen. She has also appeared in the 2023 animated film, *Resident Evil: Death Island*. Jill's youthful appearance in the film, despite being 40 years old, was said to be a result of the T-virus slowing her aging.

She is a playable character in several non-canonical *Resident Evil* games. The character appears in a number of [*Resident Evil* mobile games](/wiki/Mobile_games_in_the_Resident_Evil_series) and is the protagonist of *Resident Evil: Genesis* (2008), an alternative-story version of the original game. Jill is a playable character in *[Marvel vs. Capcom 2](/wiki/Marvel_vs._Capcom_2)* (2000), *[Marvel vs. Capcom 3](/wiki/Marvel_vs._Capcom_3)* (2011), *[Project X Zone](/wiki/Project_X_Zone)* (2012), *[Project X Zone 2](/wiki/Project_X_Zone_2)* (2015), *[Dead by Daylight](/wiki/Dead_by_Daylight)* (2016), *[Puzzle Fighter](/wiki/Puzzle_Fighter_(2017_video_game))* (2017), the [digital collectible card game](/wiki/Digital_collectible_card_game) *[Teppen](/wiki/Teppen)* (2019), and has been announced as an additional character in *[Goddess of Victory: Nikke](/wiki/Goddess_of_Victory:_Nikke)* (2022). She is an [alternate skin](/wiki/Skin_(computing)) for [Chun-Li](/wiki/Chun-Li) in *[Super Gem Fighter Mini Mix](/wiki/Super_Gem_Fighter_Mini_Mix)* (1998), [Cammy](/wiki/Cammy) in *[Street Fighter V](/wiki/Street_Fighter_V)* (2016), and Zofia in *[Tom Clancy's The Division 2](/wiki/Tom_Clancy%27s_The_Division_2)* (2019), and can be selected as a playable skin in *[We Love Golf!](/wiki/We_Love_Golf!)* (2007), *[Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six Siege](/wiki/Tom_Clancy%27s_Rainbow_Six_Siege)* (2015), and *[Fortnite Battle Royale](/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale)* (2017). Jill has a [non-playable](/wiki/Non-player_character) cameo in 2004's *Under the Skin*, is one of the 'Spirit' [power-ups](/wiki/Power-up) in *[Super Smash Bros. Ultimate](/wiki/Super_Smash_Bros._Ultimate)* (2018), and a robot dressed as Jill makes a brief reference in *[Astro's Playroom](/wiki/Astro%27s_Playroom)* (2020) and *[Astro Bot](/wiki/Astro_Bot)* (2024).

Jill appears in [novelizations](/wiki/Novelizations) of the films and games and plays a supporting role in the first novel, *[Resident Evil: Caliban Cove](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Caliban_Cove)* (1998), of a series by [S. D. Perry](/wiki/S._D._Perry). In *Resident Evil: The Umbrella Conspiracy* (1998), Perry's novelization of the original game, Jill's Delta Force background is not mentioned; before her career in law enforcement, she is said to have acted as an accomplice for her father Dick Valentine, a professional thief. Several comic books based on the games were released, and she is a character in [Bandai](/wiki/Bandai)'s *Resident Evil Deck Building Card Game* (2011) and Steamforged Games' *Resident Evil 3: The Board Game* (2021). Merchandise featuring Jill includes action figures, figurines and a replica gun. The character was featured in *Resident Evil*-themed attractions at [Universal Studios Japan](/wiki/Universal_Studios_Japan) and [Universal Orlando](/wiki/Universal_Orlando)'s [Halloween Horror Nights](/wiki/Halloween_Horror_Nights). Capcom's [theme restaurant](/wiki/Theme_restaurant), Biohazard Cafe & Grill S.T.A.R.S., which opened in Tokyo's [Shibuya](/wiki/Shibuya) in 2012, served a noodle dish named for her. In 2026, to celebrate the release of *[Resident Evil Requiem](/wiki/Resident_Evil_Requiem)* (2026), [Alamo Drafthouse Cinema](/wiki/Alamo_Drafthouse_Cinema) introduced a *Resident Evil*–inspired menu featuring a dish called "Jill Sandwich".

## Reception and legacy

A cosplayer recreates Jill's redesigned appearance in *Resident Evil 5*, a brainwashed antagonist who was scrutinized by Esther MacCallum-Stewart.

Game publications, including the 2011 version of the *[Guinness World Records Gamer's Edition](/wiki/Guinness_World_Records_Gamer%27s_Edition)*, described Jill Valentine as among the most popular and iconic video-game characters. At the 2013 [Penny Arcade Expo](/wiki/Penny_Arcade_Expo), [journalists](/wiki/Video_game_journalism) and game developers named her one of the top three lead characters in Western and Japanese [role-playing video games](/wiki/Role-playing_video_game). Magazines praised her as the most likable *Resident Evil* character, with the series' most believable and consistent [story arc](/wiki/Story_arc). The character was cited as one of the [video game industry](/wiki/Video_game_industry)'s greatest [mascots](/wiki/Mascot), a tough, strong, attractive female character who could appeal to a broad [demographic](/wiki/Demography).

Critics noted that Jill was not oversexualized in her early appearances. She was highlighted as an example of the series' female characters who were not judged solely on [gender](/wiki/Gender), and for having "the most sensible design" for a female character of the mid-to-late 1990s. Her professional relationship with Chris was praised for its basis in loyalty rather than romance and its balance of personalities: Jill's intellect and Chris's brawn. Female digital critics felt that several of Jill's features undermine her role as a heroine and weaken her as a protagonist; her body shape is unrealistic and not reflective of her military background or physical training, and she was the only member of her team in the original game to not wear a [bulletproof vest](/wiki/Bulletproof_vest). The extent to which her appearance changed over the course of the game series has been criticized as excessive, with the *Resident Evil 3* outfit derided for deviating too much from Mikami's initial militaristic depiction of the character. In *[Tropes vs. Women in Video Games](/wiki/Tropes_vs._Women_in_Video_Games)*, feminist media critic [Anita Sarkeesian](/wiki/Anita_Sarkeesian) criticized Jill's alternate costumes as too revealing (particularly the pirate costume in *Resident Evil: Revelations*). Sarkeesian cited Jill's movement in *Revelations* as an example of female characters who walk in an overtly-sexualized manner.

MacCallum-Stewart said that the first *Resident Evil* game became famous for its "extremely clunky dialogue and voice acting, an element which lent the otherwise suspenseful game an element of charm that endeared it to players". Although weak dialogue might be attributed to poor translation of the original Japanese text, she said that this inadvertently helped to differentiate the series from its rivals. Several lines from the game achieved enduring popularity; "You were almost a Jill sandwich", a quip delivered in awkward voiceover by Barry Burton after a falling ceiling trap almost crushes Jill, was revived as an [Internet meme](/wiki/Internet_meme) a decade after the game's release and became the subject of [fan art](/wiki/Fan_art) depicting Jill in (or as) a sandwich. Capcom [used the line](/wiki/Easter_egg_(media)) in several of their later games, including *[Dead Rising](/wiki/Dead_Rising_(video_game))* (2006), *[Resident Evil: Uprising](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Uprising)* (2009), *[Resident Evil: Revelations 2](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Revelations_2)* (2015), and *[Resident Evil: Resistance](/wiki/Resident_Evil:_Resistance)* (2020). Another piece of dialogue by Burton – "And, Jill, here's a lock pick. It might be handy if you, the master of unlocking, take it with you" – also became popular. The quote, [parodied](/wiki/Parody) for its excessive [silence between words](/wiki/Pausa), was removed from later editions.

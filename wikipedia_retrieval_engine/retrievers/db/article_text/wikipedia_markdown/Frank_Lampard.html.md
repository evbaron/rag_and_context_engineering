**Frank James Lampard** (born 20 June 1978) is an English [football manager](/wiki/Manager_(association_football)) and former [midfielder](/wiki/Association_football) who is the manager of [EFL Championship](/wiki/EFL_Championship) club [Coventry City](/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.). Widely regarded as one of the greatest midfielders of all time and one of [Chelsea](/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.)'s and the [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League)’s greatest ever players, Lampard holds the record of the most Premier League goals (177) by a midfielder in its history.

A [box-to-box midfielder](/wiki/Midfielder#Box-to-box_midfielder), Lampard began his career in 1995 at [West Ham United](/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.), the club where his father [Frank Sr.](/wiki/Frank_Lampard_(footballer,_born_1948)) also played. In 2001, he was signed by London rivals Chelsea for £11 million. In his thirteen years with the club, Lampard established himself as a prolific scorer from midfield, becoming the club's [all-time leading goalscorer](/wiki/List_of_Chelsea_F.C._records_and_statistics#All-time_top_goalscorers), with 211 goals in all competitions.

At Chelsea, Lampard won three Premier League titles, the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League), the [UEFA Europa League](/wiki/UEFA_Europa_League), four [FA Cups](/wiki/FA_Cup), and two [League Cups](/wiki/EFL_Cup). In 2005, he was named [FWA Footballer of the Year](/wiki/FWA_Footballer_of_the_Year), and finished runner-up for both the [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/2005_Ballon_d%27Or) and [FIFA World Player of the Year](/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year). After leaving Chelsea, Lampard played for league rivals [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.), and [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) club [New York City FC](/wiki/New_York_City_FC) before retiring in 2017. He ranked highly on a number of statistics for Premier League players for ten years from 1 December 2000, including most games and most wins.

Lampard played [106 matches](/wiki/List_of_men%27s_footballers_with_100_or_more_international_caps) and scored 29 goals for the [England national team](/wiki/England_national_football_team), after making his debut in 1999. He represented his country at three [FIFA World Cups](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup) – in [2006](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2014](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup) – and in [UEFA Euro 2004](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004), where he was named in the Team of the Tournament. Lampard was voted [England Player of the Year](/wiki/England_Player_of_the_Year) in 2004 and 2005.

Lampard is one of 11 players, and the only midfielder, to have scored [150 or more goals](/wiki/List_of_Premier_League_players_with_100_or_more_goals) in the Premier League. He is fourth in the Premier League's all-time assists ranking, with 102 assists. Lampard holds a number of additional Chelsea and Premier League [records](#Records), and has won [PFA Fans' Player of the Year](/wiki/PFA_Fans%27_Player_of_the_Year) and the [FWA Tribute Award](/wiki/FWA_Tribute_Award). During his career, he was named in the [PFA Team of the Year](/wiki/PFA_Team_of_the_Year) three times, [Premier League Player of the Month](/wiki/Premier_League_Player_of_the_Month) four times, [Premier League Player of the Season](/wiki/Premier_League_Player_of_the_Season) once and finished as the Premier League's top assist provider three times, and was named in the [FIFPro World XI](/wiki/FIFPro_World_XI) and a [MLS All-Star](/wiki/MLS_All-Star). After departing, Lampard was named in the Chelsea Team of the Decade as voted for by Chelsea fans, and into the [Premier League Hall of Fame](/wiki/Premier_League_Hall_of_Fame).

After retiring from international football, Lampard was appointed an [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (OBE) in 2015 for services to football. Lampard served as a team captain on the [ITV](/wiki/ITV_(TV_network)) sport panel show *[Play to the Whistle](/wiki/Play_to_the_Whistle)* from 2015 until 2017. He has also written a number of children's books.

Lampard began his managerial career with [Derby County](/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.) in 2018, leading the club to the final of that season's [Championship](/wiki/EFL_Championship) [play-offs](/wiki/2019_EFL_Championship_play-off_Final). He returned to Chelsea as head coach a year later, guiding them to fourth place and the [FA Cup Final](/wiki/2020_FA_Cup_Final) in his first season, before being dismissed in 2021. Following a one-year spell with [Everton](/wiki/Everton_F.C.), and a brief return to Chelsea as caretaker, Lampard joined Coventry City as manager in 2024 and won the [2025–26 Championship](/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_EFL_Championship).

## Club career

### West Ham United

Lampard began his career at [West Ham United](/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.), [his father](/wiki/Frank_Lampard_(footballer,_born_1948))'s former club, joining the youth team in 1994, his schoolboy hero being West Ham striker [Frank McAvennie](/wiki/Frank_McAvennie). Lampard joined West Ham when his father was the assistant coach, entering as an apprentice in the youth team in 1994 and signing a professional contract the following year. He went on loan to [Second Division](/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) club [Swansea City](/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.) in October 1995, debuting in his team's victory 2–0 over [Bradford City](/wiki/Bradford_City_A.F.C.) and scoring his first career goal in a match against [Brighton & Hove Albion](/wiki/Brighton_%26_Hove_Albion_F.C.). Lampard played nine times for Swansea before returning to West Ham in January 1996.

#### 1995–97: Development

Lampard made his debut for West Ham on 31 January 1996 against [Coventry City](/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.) coming on as a [substitute](/wiki/Substitute_(association_football)) for [John Moncur](/wiki/John_Moncur). His only other game of the season was the season's last, on 5 May 1996, a 1–1 home draw with [Sheffield Wednesday](/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday_F.C.) when Lampard was used as a substitute for [Keith Rowland](/wiki/Keith_Rowland).

The following season Lampard made his first start for West Ham, on 17 August 1996, in a 2–0 away defeat to [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) before being substituted for [Robbie Slater](/wiki/Robbie_Slater). Lampard's season ended on 15 March 1997 when he sustained a broken leg during an away, 0–0 draw, against [Aston Villa](/wiki/Aston_Villa_F.C.). Carried from the pitch on a stretcher, his 31st-minute substitute was [Rio Ferdinand](/wiki/Rio_Ferdinand). The game also saw his first [booking](/wiki/Yellow_card_(sports)) as a West Ham player. Lampard claims to have been jeered from the pitch by [West Ham United supporters](/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C._supporters), an action which made him consider leaving football. He had made 16 appearances in all competitions for The Hammers.

#### 1997–99: First-team regular

From this season Lampard took the number 18 squad number having previously held the number 26 spot. On the first day of the [1997–98 season](/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_West_Ham_United_F.C._season), West Ham opened their fixtures with an away game against [Barnsley](/wiki/Barnsley_F.C.) who were playing in the top tier of English football for the first time in 110 years. Lampard came on as a 76th-minute substitute for [Eyal Berkovic](/wiki/Eyal_Berkovic). Barely a minute later he scored what was the winning goal in a 2–1 win for The Hammers having received the ball from [Michael Hughes](/wiki/Michael_Hughes_(footballer)) and flicking it past Barnsley goalkeeper [David Watson](/wiki/David_Watson_(footballer,_born_1973)). The season also saw his first [hat-trick](/wiki/Hat-trick_(association_football)). On 19 November 1997, West Ham played [Walsall](/wiki/Walsall_F.C.) in a [League Cup](/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_Football_League_Cup), fourth-round game. Lampard's three goals plus another from [John Hartson](/wiki/John_Hartson) were enough to beat Walsall who responded via a goal from [Andy Watson](/wiki/Andy_Watson_(footballer,_born_1967)). Lampard made 42 appearances for the 1997–98 season in all competitions scoring nine goals.

Lampard was an ever-present for West Ham in the [1998–99 season](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_West_Ham_United_F.C._season), helping his team to fifth place in the [1998–99 Premier League](/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Premier_League) and qualification for the [UEFA Intertoto Cup](/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup). This is the highest West Ham have ever come in the league.

#### 1999–2001: New contract and departure

Before the start of the [1999–2000 FA Premier League](/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_FA_Premier_League) season, Lampard signed an extension to his contract which would have kept him with the club until 2005. Having qualified for the [Intertoto Cup](/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup#1995–2005), West Ham's season started early, in July 1999. On 24 July 1999, Lampard scored his first goal in European football in a 1–1 away draw with Finnish side [Jokerit](/wiki/FC_Jokerit). Another goal in the semi-final against [Heerenveen](/wiki/SC_Heerenveen) put West Ham into the final against [Metz](/wiki/FC_Metz). Despite losing the first leg 1–0 in London, West Ham won 3–1 in France on 24 August 1999 with goals from [Trevor Sinclair](/wiki/Trevor_Sinclair), [Paulo Wanchope](/wiki/Paulo_Wanchope) and Lampard. Winning the competition saw West Ham in the [1999–2000 UEFA Cup](/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_UEFA_Cup). Lampard scored his first goal in this competition on 16 September 1999, in a 3–0 home win against [Osijek](/wiki/NK_Osijek), a tie which they won 6–1 on aggregate.

This season also saw West Ham beat [Bradford City](/wiki/Bradford_City_F.C.) 5–4 at [Upton Park](/wiki/Boleyn_Ground) in February 2000. The game received notoriety for Lampard's fight over the ball with [Paolo Di Canio](/wiki/Paolo_Di_Canio). With West Ham 4–2 down they gained a penalty. Lampard was West Ham's regular penalty taker. Di Canio, however, wanted to take the kick and the two tussled over the ball with Di Canio winning and scoring to make the game 4–3 to West Ham. In the [1999–2000](/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_West_Ham_United_F.C._season), season he finished as the club's third top scorer, behind Di Canio and Paulo Wanchope, with a total of 14 goals.

In the [2000–01 season](/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_West_Ham_United_F.C._season), West Ham's form suffered. They spent most of the season in the bottom half of the Premier League table. From fifth place the previous season they finished 15th. Despite this Lampard scored 9 goals in 37 games, behind only [Frédéric Kanouté](/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Kanout%C3%A9) and Di Canio. In May 2001, his uncle [Harry Redknapp](/wiki/Harry_Redknapp) left the club by mutual consent after seven years in the role. His father Frank also left the club and Lampard, under the pressure of being known as "Frank Lampard's son" and the poor treatment of his father by West Ham, decided to move to another club. He left West Ham for [Chelsea](/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.) for £11 million in June 2001, the Hammers having previously declined a £15 million joint bid from [Aston Villa](/wiki/Aston_Villa_F.C.) for Lampard and Frédéric Kanouté.

### Chelsea

#### 2001–04: Signing and first-team breakthrough

Lampard's Premier League debut with Chelsea came on 19 August 2001 in a 1–1 draw with [Newcastle United](/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) on the opening day of the [2001–02 season](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_FA_Premier_League), while his first red card came in a match against [Tottenham Hotspur](/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) on 16 September 2001. He appeared in all of Chelsea's league matches and scored eight goals in the [2001–02 season](/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Chelsea_F.C._season). He netted the match-winner in Chelsea's [2002–03](/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Chelsea_F.C._season) season-opener against [Charlton Athletic](/wiki/Charlton_Athletic_F.C.).

The [following season](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_FA_Premier_League), he was selected as the [Premier League Player of the Month](/wiki/Premier_League_Player_of_the_Month) in September 2003, and the PFA Fans' Player of the Month in October. Chelsea finished second in the 2003–04 [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League) behind unbeaten [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.) and he was named in the 2004 [PFA Team of the Year](/wiki/PFA_Team_of_the_Year) as he reached double figures in league goals (10) for the first time in his career, in addition to four goals in fourteen [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_UEFA_Champions_League) matches, as Chelsea advanced to the [semi-finals](/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_UEFA_Champions_League#Semi-finals). In the semi-final against [Monaco](/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) he scored, but Chelsea lost 5–3 on aggregate. At the end of the season, he came second behind [Thierry Henry](/wiki/Thierry_Henry) for the 2004 [FWA Footballer of the Year](/wiki/FWA_Footballer_of_the_Year) award.

#### 2004–06: Back-to-back Premier League wins

Lampard playing for [Chelsea](/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.) in 2004

Lampard played in all 38 Premier League matches for the third consecutive season in [2004–05](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Premier_League). He finished with 13 goals (19 in all competitions), in addition to leading the league in assists with 18.

In August 2004, he scored the winning goal against [Southampton](/wiki/Southampton_F.C.) in the Premier League, and continued scoring important goals as he scored both in a 2–0 win against Tottenham. In March 2005 he scored a long-range goal from 30 yards against [Crystal Palace](/wiki/Crystal_Palace_F.C.) in the Premier League which Chelsea won 4–1. Lampard continued his season strongly, and his brilliant performances for Chelsea in the Premier League and [Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League) further increased his reputation as one of the best midfielders in the world. In the [2004–05 Champions League](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_UEFA_Champions_League), he scored one of the goals in Chelsea's famous 4–2 win over [Barcelona](/wiki/FC_Barcelona), helping Chelsea to progress to the quarter-finals. In the quarter-finals, he scored three goals in two legs against [Bayern Munich](/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) as Chelsea won 6–5 on aggregate. His second goal in the first leg was a spectacular one; he controlled [Claude Makélélé](/wiki/Claude_Mak%C3%A9l%C3%A9l%C3%A9)'s cross with his chest then turned and swivelled and sent the ball inside the far post with a left-foot half volley.

In April 2005, Lampard scored both goals against [Bolton Wanderers](/wiki/Bolton_Wanderers_F.C.) in a 2–0 win which was the Premier League title-winning match for Chelsea, which also won the first major trophy of his career as Chelsea bagged their first top-flight title in 50 years, by a 12-point margin. He was named as Barclays Player of the Season for 2004–05. Though Chelsea were eliminated in the [Champions League](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_UEFA_Champions_League) [semi-finals](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League_2004-05#Semi-finals) by league rivals [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), they took home the [Football League Cup](/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_Football_League_Cup), in which Lampard scored twice in six matches, which included the opening goal against [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) in the League Cup semi-final, which Chelsea won 2–1. He landed his first personal award by being named the 2005 [Footballer of the Year](/wiki/FWA_Footballer_of_the_Year).

> "He is one of my best players ever, one of my best professionals ever, a Chelsea legend and a legend in the Premier League."

— [José Mourinho](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mourinho) on Lampard, 2015.

Lampard netted a career-high 16 league goals in [2005–06](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_FA_Premier_League). In September 2005 he was selected as a member of the inaugural [World XI](/wiki/FIFPro_World_XI). He finished as runner-up to [Ronaldinho](/wiki/Ronaldinho) for both the [Ballon d'Or](/wiki/Ballon_d%27Or) and [FIFA World Player of the Year](/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year) awards. His record of consecutive Premier League appearances ended at 164 (five better than previous record-holder [David James](/wiki/David_James_(footballer,_born_1970))) on 28 December 2005, when he sat out a match against [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.) due to illness. Lampard scored 12 goals in his first four months of the 2005–06 season. He scored twice in three separate matches from August to November, including both in a 2–1 win over Aston Villa, making Chelsea the first team to win their first seven matches in the Premier League. He scored twice again, this time against [Blackburn Rovers](/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.) in a 4–2 win, which included a free-kick from 25 yards. After the match, manager [José Mourinho](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mourinho) hailed Lampard as the "best player in the world". Chelsea eventually won the [Premier League](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_FA_Premier_League) for the second time, in which Lampard was Chelsea's topscorer with 16 league goals. In the [Champions League](/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Champions_League) group stage, he scored a free-kick against [Anderlecht](/wiki/R.S.C._Anderlecht). Chelsea progressed to the first knock-out round to face Barcelona, with Lampard scoring a goal in the second leg, but Chelsea were eliminated 3–2 on aggregate.

#### 2006–11: Domestic and individual success

Lampard playing for Chelsea in 2007

Due to a back injury sustained by [John Terry](/wiki/John_Terry), Lampard spent much of the [2006–07](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FA_Premier_League) campaign as team captain in his absence. He enjoyed a streak of seven goals in eight games. He scored both goals in a 2–0 win over [Fulham](/wiki/Fulham_F.C.) and scored his 77th goal for Chelsea from a long range strike in a 3–2 win over [Everton](/wiki/Everton_F.C.) on 17 December, overtaking [Dennis Wise](/wiki/Dennis_Wise) as Chelsea's highest scoring midfielder. Then in the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_UEFA_Champions_League) group stage he scored a goal from an extremely tight angle against Barcelona, at the [Camp Nou](/wiki/Camp_Nou), the match ended 2–2. Lampard finished with 21 goals in all competitions, including a career-high six [FA Cup](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FA_Cup) goals. He scored his first Chelsea hat-trick in the [third-round tie](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FA_Cup#Third_round_proper) against [Macclesfield Town](/wiki/Macclesfield_Town_F.C.) on 6 January 2007. He scored two goals to help Chelsea to a [quarter-final draw](/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FA_Cup#Sixth_round_proper) with Tottenham Hotspur after having trailed 3–1, and he was named the FA Cup player-of-the-round for his performance. He gave the assist to [Didier Drogba](/wiki/Didier_Drogba) in the [2007 FA Cup Final](/wiki/2007_FA_Cup_Final) which was the winning goal in extra-time, as Chelsea won it 1–0. In a post-match interview following Chelsea's [FA Cup Final](/wiki/2007_FA_Cup_Final) victory over Manchester United, Lampard said he wanted to stay at the club "forever".

Lampard's 2007–08 season was hampered by injury. He managed to play 40 matches, 24 of them in the Premier League. This was the fewest league games he had played in a season since 1996–97. On 16 February 2008, Lampard became the eighth Chelsea player to score 100 goals for the club in a 3–1 [FA Cup](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_FA_Cup) fifth-round win over [Huddersfield Town](/wiki/Huddersfield_Town_A.F.C.). After the final whistle, Lampard removed his jersey and flashed a T-shirt to the Chelsea fans with "100 Not Out, They Are All For You, Thanks" printed across the front. On 12 March 2008, Lampard scored four goals in a 6–1 rout of [Derby County](/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.). Then in the second leg of the [Champions League](/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Champions_League) quarter-final, he scored the winning goal against [Fenerbahçe](/wiki/Fenerbah%C3%A7e_S.K._(football)) in the 87th minute as Chelsea won 3–2 on aggregate.

Lampard playing for Chelsea in 2008

On 30 April, Lampard, grieving the loss of his mother a week earlier, decided to play in the second leg of Chelsea's [Champions League semi-final against Liverpool](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League_2007-08#Semi-finals), who were eliminated on 4–3 aggregate as he took an emotional penalty in the 98th minute of extra-time, which he scored confidently. In the [final against Manchester United](/wiki/2008_UEFA_Champions_League_Final), he scored an equalising goal in the 45th minute, as [Michael Essien](/wiki/Michael_Essien)'s deflected shot found him as he went to the box with his trade-mark late run, and he scored with a left foot finish. The match ended 1–1 after extra-time and Chelsea eventually lost 6–5 on penalties. He was later named [UEFA Club Midfielder of the Year](/wiki/UEFA_Club_Football_Awards).

On 13 August 2008, Lampard signed a new five-year contract with Chelsea worth £39.2 million, making him the highest-paid Premier League player. He started the [2008–09 season](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Premier_League) by scoring five goals in his first eleven league matches. He scored the 150th goal of his club career with a goal against Manchester City in the [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League). In October 2008, in the Premier League he scored a chipped goal against [Hull City](/wiki/Hull_City_A.F.C.) with his left foot; he unleashed a chip from 20 yards that curled and swerved and fooled the goalkeeper as it went into the net. [FIFA World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup)-winning coach [Luiz Felipe Scolari](/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari) said after the game, "It was the best goal I have seen, my vote for World Player of the year award will go to him, only a player with his intelligence could have done that." Lampard scored his hundredth career Premier League goal in a 5–0 victory over [Sunderland](/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.) on 2 November. Eighteen of Lampard's hundred goals were penalties. He was named Premier League Player of the Month for the third time in his career in [October](/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Premier_League#Monthly_awards).

After a streak of matches without scoring, Lampard scored three goals in two games, the first being against [West Bromwich Albion](/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.) and the latter two against Fulham. On 17 January 2009, he made his 400th Chelsea appearance against Stoke City, scoring a stoppage time winner. He again scored a stoppage time winner on 28 February, this time against [Wigan Athletic](/wiki/Wigan_Athletic_F.C.). Then in the fourth round of the FA Cup, he scored a free-kick from 35 yards against [Ipswich Town](/wiki/Ipswich_Town_F.C.). He scored twice against Liverpool in the second-leg quarter finals of the Champions League which ended 4–4, but Chelsea won 7–5 on aggregate. Then he provided two assists in the next game against Arsenal in FA Cup semi-finals which Chelsea won 2–1. Lampard finished the season with 20 goals and 19 assists. Lampard's 20th goal of the season was the winning goal in the [FA Cup Final](/wiki/2009_FA_Cup_Final) against Everton, with a left-foot shot from long-range. He repeated the corner flag celebration his father had done after scoring the winning goal in the [1979–80 FA Cup](/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_FA_Cup) semi-final second leg against Everton. It was the fourth consecutive season that he scored 20 or more goals. He was later named Chelsea's Player of the Year for the third time.

Lampard started the [2009–10 season](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Chelsea_F.C._season) by scoring against Manchester United, in the [2009 Community Shield](/wiki/2009_FA_Community_Shield), in the 72nd minute. The match ended 2–2, and Lampard scored in its penalty shootout which they won 4–1. He scored his 133rd goal for Chelsea in a Champions League match against [Atlético Madrid](/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid) on 21 October 2009, which moved him up to fifth among the club's all-time goalscorers. He had been struggling to score the number of goals he had in the past seasons, however this soon changed as he scored two goals in the 5–0 defeat of Blackburn on 24 October 2009. On 30 October, he was nominated for the [FIFA World Player of the Year](/wiki/FIFA_World_Player_of_the_Year) award for the sixth-straight year.

Lampard celebrating after winning the [2009–10 Premier League](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Premier_League) title with Chelsea

On 16 December, Lampard scored a crucial 79th minute winning goal against [Portsmouth](/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.) and on 20 December, Lampard scored a penalty against his former club West Ham in a 1–1 draw, however he had to take his spot-kick three times due to players running into the box too early, he nonetheless scored all three. In Chelsea's 7–2 thrashing of Sunderland in January 2010, Lampard scored twice to add to his league tally. On 27 January 2010, Lampard again scored two goals, in Chelsea's 3–0 win over [Birmingham City](/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C.) in the [Premier League](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Premier_League). On 27 February, Lampard yet again scored twice against Manchester City but despite his contribution Chelsea went on to lose the game 4–2 at home, for the first time in 38 games.

Lampard, for the second time in his career, hit four goals in one match against Aston Villa on 27 March 2010 to bring his goal tally past 20, for the fifth-consecutive season. This also brought him his 151st Chelsea goal, and it put him as the club's third highest scorer ever, overtaking [Peter Osgood](/wiki/Peter_Osgood)'s record of 150. Lampard scored in a 3–0 win, again over Aston Villa, in the 2010 FA Cup semi-final. Lampard reached 20 Premier League goals for the first time when he scored twice against [Stoke City](/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C.) in Chelsea's 7–0 win on 25 April 2010. This milestone also represented the first time he had hit 25 in all competitions in a season. On 2 May, Lampard scored against Liverpool in the second last game of the season to give Chelsea a vital three points that took them to the top of the league by a single point. He scored one and assisted two other goals in the final match of the season where Chelsea thrashed Wigan Athletic 8–0 at [Stamford Bridge](/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_(stadium)) to win the 2009–10 Premier League title and give him a remarkable 27 goals in the season. Lampard ended his season lifting the [2010 FA Cup](/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_FA_Cup) as Chelsea beat Portsmouth 1–0 in the final.

The [2010–11 season](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Chelsea_F.C._season) began brightly for Lampard, being an integral part of Chelsea's system as usual. After a match against Stoke in August 2010, which Chelsea won 2–0, it was revealed that Lampard was suffering from a [hernia](/wiki/Hernia), which was successfully operated on. However, he suffered fresh injuries during training in mid-November, and remained sidelined for a further three weeks. Manager [Carlo Ancelotti](/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti) said that he injured his abductor muscle in his leg in training on 11 November, and would not be fit until December. He finally made his comeback after four months out injured against Tottenham on 12 December, as a substitute in the 75th minute. Lampard scored a penalty in the 3–3 draw between Chelsea and Aston Villa on 2 January 2011, the first after returning from injury. On 9 January, Lampard scored twice and gave one assist in a 7–0 win against Ipswich Town in the FA Cup reaching the landmark of 201 career club goals.

On 1 February, Lampard scored a penalty against Sunderland, then produced an assist, with Chelsea ending up winning the game 4–2.
Lampard then assisted for [Nicolas Anelka](/wiki/Nicolas_Anelka) in Chelsea's 2–0 victory against [Copenhagen](/wiki/F.C._Copenhagen), in the Round of 16 match in the [Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League). On 1 March, Lampard scored the winning goal in a crucial 2–1 victory over Manchester United, keeping Chelsea's [Premier League](/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Premier_League) title hopes alive. Then in the next game, he scored two goals in Chelsea's 3–1 victory over [Blackpool](/wiki/Blackpool_F.C.), and also contributed with an assist for John Terry to score.

In April 2011, Lampard scored his 11th goal of the season in a 3–0 victory against former club West Ham. Lampard scored again against Manchester United, but it proved to be only a consolation, as Chelsea lost 2–1 at [Old Trafford](/wiki/Old_Trafford), thus ending Chelsea's title hopes.

#### 2011–13: European triumphs

Lampard and [Didier Drogba](/wiki/Didier_Drogba) in the [2012 UEFA Champions League Final](/wiki/2012_UEFA_Champions_League_Final) against [Bayern Munich](/wiki/Bayern_Munich)

In Chelsea's third [Premier League](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Premier_League) match of the season, Lampard scored his first goal of the season and also provided an assist for [José Bosingwa](/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bosingwa)'s opening goal, as Chelsea beat [Norwich City](/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.) 3–1.

After not featuring in Chelsea's 4–1 win against [Swansea City](/wiki/Swansea_City_A.F.C.) on 24 September, Lampard returned to the starting line-up for their [Champions League](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League) group stage game against [Valencia](/wiki/Valencia_CF) netting an important opening goal in their 1–1 draw. Lampard continued his fine form by netting his fifth Chelsea hat-trick on 2 October, in a 5–1 thrashing of Bolton Wanderers.

In the eleventh round of the Premier League, Lampard scored the only goal of the match against Blackburn Rovers with a diving header, giving Chelsea a win after two consecutive defeats. After starting the game against Manchester City on the bench, Lampard came on in the second half to score the winning goal from the penalty spot in the 82nd minute. Lampard again proved to be the match-winner as he scored in the 89th minute against [Wolverhampton Wanderers](/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.), with the match finishing 2–1 to Chelsea.

On 25 February 2012, Lampard scored his tenth Premier League goal of the season in Chelsea's 3–0 win against Bolton, becoming the only player to score at least ten goals in nine consecutive seasons in the Premier League and his 149th all-time league goal.

Eleven days after the dismissal of manager [André Villas-Boas](/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Villas-Boas), Chelsea hosted [Napoli](/wiki/S.S.C._Napoli) in the [2011–12 Champions League](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League) round of 16-second leg, trailing the Naples side 3–1 from the first leg. Lampard helped Chelsea make a remarkable comeback, assisting John Terry from a corner for the second goal and then equalising the aggregate score from the penalty spot with a powerful shot to the left of the goal. [Branislav Ivanović](/wiki/Branislav_Ivanovi%C4%87) sealed Chelsea's victory in extra-time. In the second leg of the quarter-finals of the [2011–12 UEFA Champions League](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Champions_League) against [Benfica](/wiki/S.L._Benfica), Lampard converted a crucial penalty to give Chelsea a 2–1 win at Stamford Bridge, and helping them advance with an aggregate score of 3–1. He scored the 150th Premier League goal of his career against Fulham in a 1–1 draw on 9 April 2012. In the semi-final of the [FA Cup](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FA_Cup) Chelsea defeated Tottenham Hotspur 5–1, in a match that would be remembered for an infamous "goal that never was" awarded to Chelsea's [Juan Mata](/wiki/Juan_Mata). Lampard assisted Didier Drogba for the first goal, and also scored the fourth goal for the Blues from a free-kick 35 yards from goal.

Chelsea players celebrate winning the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League)

Lampard was pivotal in the semi-finals of the 2011–12 Champions League against the heavily favoured defending champions Barcelona. Bookmakers had Barcelona on the shortest odds to win a second consecutive Champions League title, and various commentators opined that Chelsea would have to produce their best two games of the season – and hope that Barca played their worst two – to upset the Catalan giants. In the first leg at Stamford Bridge, Lampard stripped [Lionel Messi](/wiki/Lionel_Messi) off the ball then played a cross-field pass to [Ramires](/wiki/Ramires), who set up Drogba for the only goal of the game, giving Chelsea a 1–0 advantage. In the second leg at the Camp Nou, Lampard assumed the captaincy after Terry was sent off early in the game. Down 2–0 to Barça just before half-time, Lampard assisted Ramires' goal with a through-pass which levelled the aggregate score at 2–2 while putting Chelsea ahead on away goals. Chelsea teammate [Fernando Torres](/wiki/Fernando_Torres) added another goal in stoppage time to give Chelsea a 3–2 victory overall and produce one of the greatest upsets in the history of European football. This set up Chelsea's match with Bayern Munich at the [Allianz Arena](/wiki/Allianz_Arena) in the Champions League final.

In the [2012 FA Cup Final](/wiki/2012_FA_Cup_Final), Lampard assisted Didier Drogba's winning goal in 2–1 win over Liverpool, the seventh FA Cup in Chelsea's history as well as the fourth of his career. Lampard captained Chelsea in the [2012 Champions League final](/wiki/2012_UEFA_Champions_League_Final) against Bayern Munich due to Terry's suspension. It was a match where Chelsea were considered to be the underdogs. After finishing 1–1 in normal time, the game went to a penalty shootout with Lampard successfully converting his team's third spot-kick, helping Chelsea win 4–3 on penalties. As Chelsea's captain for the match, Lampard lifted the trophy with club captain Terry. Lampard ended the [2011–12](/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Chelsea_F.C._season) season as Chelsea's top scorer with 16 goals in all competitions, along with 10 assists.

Lampard playing for Chelsea in 2012

Lampard missed [Euro 2012](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012) with a thigh injury, but did play all of Chelsea's pre-season games, scoring against the MLS All-Stars and [Brighton & Hove Albion](/wiki/Brighton_%26_Hove_Albion_F.C.). In Chelsea's first game of the new [Premier League](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Premier_League) campaign, Lampard converted a penalty won by [Eden Hazard](/wiki/Eden_Hazard) as Chelsea brushed aside Wigan 2–0. In Chelsea's second game of the season, three days later against newly promoted [Reading](/wiki/Reading_F.C.), he again converted a penalty won by Hazard as Chelsea cruised to a 4–2 win. On 6 October, he scored his third league goal of the season in a 4–1 win against [Norwich City](/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.). After six weeks out injured, on 16 December Lampard appeared in the [final](/wiki/2012_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_Final) of the [2012 FIFA Club World Cup](/wiki/2012_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) with Chelsea losing 1–0 to Brazilian club [Corinthians](/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista). He marked his [500th appearance](/wiki/List_of_footballers_with_500_or_more_Premier_League_appearances) in the Premier League by scoring in an 8–0 win against Aston Villa on 23 December. On 30 December 2012, Lampard scored a brace as Chelsea came from a goal down in the game against Everton to win 2–1.

> "You talk about [the Zolas](/wiki/Gianfranco_Zola) and the best players to have played at the club, but for me he [Lampard] is the best. You can only single out two players to play consistently well and to score goals over the course of the last 10–15 years and that is him and [Ryan Giggs](/wiki/Ryan_Giggs). It's incredible, to see the number of goals he's scored at such a high level as well – Champions League goals, Premier League goals – and the hunger. He is an example to all the players here."

— [John Terry](/wiki/John_Terry) on Lampard scoring his 200th goal for Chelsea in March 2013.

In the third round of the [FA Cup](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_FA_Cup), Lampard entered in the second half and scored a penalty in Chelsea's victory over Southampton. On 6 January 2013, Lampard's agent Steve Kutner announced that Lampard would not be offered a new contract with Chelsea and would leave when his contract expired in June 2013. On 12 January 2013, Lampard scored a penalty in the 4–0 win over Stoke City at the [Britannia Stadium](/wiki/Britannia_Stadium). This goal made Lampard Chelsea's [second-highest goalscorer of all-time](/wiki/Chelsea_F.C._statistics#All-time_top_goalscorers) with 194 goals and moved him clear of [Kerry Dixon](/wiki/Kerry_Dixon) and putting him eight goals behind [Bobby Tambling](/wiki/Bobby_Tambling) in first. He scored his 199th goal for Chelsea in a 4–0 FA Cup victory over [Brentford](/wiki/Brentford_F.C.) on 17 February, becoming Chelsea's all-time leading FA Cup goalscorer with 26 goals. Lampard's 200th Chelsea goal came against his former club West Ham on 17 March 2013, heading home an Eden Hazard cross.

Lampard equalled Bobby Tambling's record with a long-range goal into the top left corner from just outside the box in the 61st minute in a league match against Aston Villa on 11 May. Then, later on in the 88th minute, he scored a second goal, breaking the record and securing a 2–1 victory for Chelsea. In the [UEFA Europa League final](/wiki/2013_UEFA_Europa_League_Final) on 15 May, Lampard captained the London club to a 2–1 victory over [Benfica](/wiki/S.L._Benfica), securing the 11th major trophy of the [Roman Abramovich](/wiki/Roman_Abramovich) era.

On 16 May 2013, Lampard signed a one-year extension to his contract with Chelsea, stating "I always maintained the dream was to stay at Chelsea."

#### 2013–14: Final season at Chelsea

Lampard on pre-season tour with [Chelsea](/wiki/Chelsea_F.C.) in 2013

On the opening day of the [2013–14 Premier League season](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Premier_League), Lampard scored a free-kick in a 2–0 win against newly promoted [Hull City](/wiki/Hull_City_A.F.C.). On 1 October, he scored his 205th goal for Chelsea in a 4–0 win away to [Steaua București](/wiki/FC_Steaua_Bucure%C8%99ti) in the [Champions League](/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_UEFA_Champions_League). On 23 November 2013, Lampard scored his 206th and 207th goals for Chelsea against his former club West Ham in a 3–0 win. Lampard moved to fourth on the all-time Premier League goal-scoring list on 2 December by beating [Robbie Fowler](/wiki/Robbie_Fowler)'s number of 164 goals. On 22 February 2014, Lampard scored a stoppage-time winner against Everton, initially credited to John Terry but awarded to Lampard by the [Dubious Goals Committee](/wiki/Dubious_Goals_Committee), keeping Chelsea in first place.

In March, in recognition of his contributions, Lampard was given share number 200 by the Chelsea Pitch Owners Association which owns the Stamford Bridge freehold. On 2 April, he played his 100th Champions League match, losing 3–1 to [Paris Saint-Germain](/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.). On 5 April 2014, Lampard scored the 250th goal of his club career in a 3–0 win against Stoke.

On 23 May 2014, Lampard was included on the list of players released by Chelsea. Lampard, however, said that any contract talks with Chelsea would be decided after the [2014 World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup), with a statement saying "he will meet with Chelsea before any consideration of a move elsewhere". On 3 June 2014, Chelsea confirmed Lampard would leave the club.

### Manchester City

#### Signing and New York City FC controversy

Lampard with [Manchester City](/wiki/Manchester_City_F.C.) in 2015

On 24 July 2014, it was claimed that Lampard, as a free agent, had signed a two-year contract with [New York City FC](/wiki/New_York_City_FC) (NYCFC) to commence ahead of the [2015 MLS season](/wiki/2015_MLS_season). It was announced at a live press conference in [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn) that he would be joining the club, which would be entering [Major League Soccer](/wiki/Major_League_Soccer) (MLS) for the 2015 season. Sporting director [Claudio Reyna](/wiki/Claudio_Reyna) hailed him as "one of the greatest players in world history". NYCFC's parent club, Manchester City, announced on 3 August 2014 that Lampard would join the club on a six-month deal ending in January 2015. Initially reported as a loan, reports emerged on 31 December 2014 that Lampard had signed a short-term contract with the club as a free agent with his MLS contract not to start until the end of his stay in [Manchester](/wiki/Manchester). The same day, it was announced Lampard would extend his contract with Manchester City until the end of the English season, missing the start of the [2015 MLS season](/wiki/2015_MLS_season) and it was confirmed that Lampard had never been an MLS player with his arrival date in MLS unclear.

Lampard extending his stay in Manchester prompted an angry response from fans in New York, with NYCFC being accused of dishonesty for using the player in their promotional material and MLS Commissioner [Don Garber](/wiki/Don_Garber) being accused of poor leadership and a lack of transparency in handling the affair. On 1 January 2015, Manchester City head coach [Manuel Pellegrini](/wiki/Manuel_Pellegrini) hinted that Lampard could extend his stay at Manchester City to a second season. On 9 January, Manchester City admitted that Lampard had never signed a two-year contract with NYCFC, having instead originally signed a non-binding "commitment" to join the team. The same day, Lampard confirmed his intention to join NYCFC at the end of the [2014–15 Premier League season](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Premier_League). Around 300 people asked for season ticket refunds in response to the affair, while the [2015 MLS SuperDraft](/wiki/2015_MLS_SuperDraft) started a chant of "Where's Frank Lampard?" from the crowd.

#### 2014–15: Only season in Manchester

Chelsea fans wave a "Super Frankie Lampard" banner during his appearance for Manchester City against Chelsea at [Stamford Bridge](/wiki/Stamford_Bridge_(stadium)) in 2015

Lampard made his Manchester City debut on 13 September in a 2–2 away draw against Arsenal; he received a yellow card and was substituted at half-time for [Samir Nasri](/wiki/Samir_Nasri). He scored his first goal for the club on 21 September with a volley from inside the box against former club Chelsea, having only been on the field for seven minutes as a substitute. Lampard's equalising goal ended Chelsea's perfect winning streak in the season, but it was a goal which he [did not celebrate](/wiki/Goal_celebration#Not_celebrating_a_goal), and he described it as a very emotional day. In Lampard's first [League Cup](/wiki/EFL_Cup) match for City three days later, he scored the first and last goals as the side defeated [Sheffield Wednesday](/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday_F.C.) 7–0 at home in the third round. On 27 September, Lampard came on in the 71st minute, only to score another goal to make it four goals in four matches and his fourth that week. He made his first [Champions League](/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_UEFA_Champions_League) appearance on 30 September in Manchester City's 1–1 draw with [Roma](/wiki/A.S._Roma).

> "For me, what makes Frank Lampard special is how professional he is in training. You see him at training: He is the first to arrive on the training ground and the last to go home. He won everything with Chelsea, he's a legend in football and he's still playing like he's 18 years old."

— [Pablo Zabaleta](/wiki/Pablo_Zabaleta) on Lampard, 2014.

On 1 January 2015, the day after extending his stay with the club, Lampard scored the winner for City in a Premier League encounter against Sunderland. On 14 March 2015, Lampard made his 600th Premier League appearance, becoming the second player to do so after [Ryan Giggs](/wiki/Ryan_Giggs), coming on as a substitute during City's 1–0 defeat to [Burnley](/wiki/Burnley_F.C.). On 24 May, Lampard captained Manchester City in their final match of the season and the last of his spell with the club. He scored his 177th Premier League goal to give City the lead in an eventual 2–0 win over Southampton at the [City of Manchester Stadium](/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium) and was substituted for [Jesús Navas](/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Navas) in the 77th minute.

### New York City FC

#### 2015–16: Final seasons and retirement

On 10 January 2015, Lampard signed a pre-contract to play for New York City FC in MLS, beginning on 1 July. Injury delayed his debut until 1 August, when he came on as a 69th-minute substitute for [Andrew Jacobson](/wiki/Andrew_Jacobson) in a 3–2 defeat to the [Montreal Impact](/wiki/Montreal_Impact_(2012%E2%80%932020)) at [Yankee Stadium](/wiki/Yankee_Stadium). In July, Lampard was one of the 22 players to be named to the [2015 MLS All-Star Game](/wiki/2015_MLS_All-Star_Game) roster. On 16 September, he scored his first MLS goal as his team beat [Toronto](/wiki/Toronto_FC) 2–0 to end a three-game losing streak. Eleven days later, Lampard scored the opening goal in a 2–1 victory over [Vancouver Whitecaps FC](/wiki/Vancouver_Whitecaps_FC) in the 29th minute of play; he was later also fouled in the area in the final minute of stoppage time, which allowed [David Villa](/wiki/David_Villa) to convert the match-winning goal from the resulting penalty. On 2 October, he scored New York City's fastest ever goal in MLS history in a 2–1 away defeat to [D.C. United](/wiki/D.C._United). The team finished [its debut season](/wiki/2015_Major_League_Soccer_season) outside a place for the [MLS Cup Playoffs](/wiki/2015_MLS_Cup_Playoffs), which drew criticism from some in the media, who expected more from the club's trio of designated players composed of former Champions League winners Lampard, [David Villa](/wiki/David_Villa), and [Andrea Pirlo](/wiki/Andrea_Pirlo).

Having missed the beginning of the season with a calf injury, Lampard made his first appearance in 2016 on 22 May in the New York derby, playing the final 15 minutes; New York City lost the game [7–0](/wiki/New_York_City_FC_0%E2%80%937_New_York_Red_Bulls), while Lampard was jeered by his side's own fans. However, he then scored in his first start of the season against [Philadelphia Union](/wiki/Philadelphia_Union) on 18 June and was cheered by the New York fans once again. In his second start, he managed to make it back-to-back goals against [Seattle Sounders FC](/wiki/Seattle_Sounders_FC) on 20 June. His fourth goal in five matches came against [Sporting Kansas City](/wiki/Sporting_Kansas_City) in a 3–1 defeat on 11 July. Lampard then took his goal tally for the season to five with the third goal in a 3–1 victory over the Montreal Impact on 17 July.

On 31 July 2016, Lampard became the first New York City player to score a hat-trick, achieving this feat in a 5–1 win over the [Colorado Rapids](/wiki/Colorado_Rapids). Lampard finished his second MLS season with the club with 13 goals and 3 assists in 19 appearances, as New York City finished in second place in the Eastern Conference, and qualified for the [MLS Cup Playoffs](/wiki/2016_MLS_Cup_Playoffs) for the first time ever, clinching a spot in the Eastern Conference semi-finals. He featured in both legs of the Conference semi-final against Toronto, as New York were eliminated from the Playoffs 7–0 on aggregate. On 14 November 2016, New York City announced that Lampard would leave as soon as his contract expired.

Lampard announced his retirement on 2 February 2017 after turning down several offers, and said that he would look to obtain his coaching qualifications after retiring.

## International career

### Youth and early career

Lampard was [capped](/wiki/Cap_(sport)) by England at youth level before making his [under-21](/wiki/England_national_under-21_football_team) debut on 13 November 1997 in a match against [Greece](/wiki/Greece_national_under-21_football_team). He played for the under-21 side from November 1997 to June 2000, and scored nine goals in 19 appearances, a mark bettered only by [Alan Shearer](/wiki/Alan_Shearer) and [Francis Jeffers](/wiki/Francis_Jeffers). He was capped once by [England B](/wiki/England_B_national_football_team), playing in a 2–1 home defeat against Chile on 10 February 1998.

On 10 October 1999, Lampard debuted for the [England senior squad](/wiki/England_national_football_team) in a 2–1 [friendly](/wiki/Friendly_(association_football)) win over [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team). He scored his first goal on 20 August 2003 in a 3–1 win over [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team). He was part of the team at the FA Summer Tournament (Manchester) 2004, which England won.

He was overlooked for [Euro 2000](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000) and the [2002 World Cup](/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), and had to wait until [Euro 2004](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) to take part in his first international competition. England reached the quarter-finals with Lampard scoring three goals in four matches. He scored against [France](/wiki/France_national_football_team) and [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) in the group stages. In the quarter-final, he equalised for England in the 112th minute against [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal_national_football_team), bringing the scoreline to 2–2 but England lost on penalties. He was named in the team of the tournament by [UEFA](/wiki/UEFA).

### 2004–10: UEFA Euro 2004 and 2006 FIFA World Cup

He became a regular in the squad following the retirement of [Paul Scholes](/wiki/Paul_Scholes), and was voted England Player of the Year by fans in 2004 and 2005. He was [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team)'s top-scorer in their [2006 World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) qualifying campaign, with five goals. He scored two crucial goals in the qualifiers, the first against [Austria](/wiki/Austria_national_football_team) in a 1–0 win, and the second being the winning goal against [Poland](/wiki/Poland_national_football_team).

Lampard *(wearing No.8)* lining up for [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) against [Paraguay](/wiki/Paraguay_national_football_team) at the [2006 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)

In [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team)'s first game of the tournament against Paraguay, Lampard was named [man of the match](/wiki/Man_of_the_match) as England won 1–0. Though Lampard played every minute of England's [2006 World Cup](/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) matches, he went scoreless as England were eliminated in the quarterfinals by [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal_national_football_team) on penalties, and he was one of the three [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) players whose penalty was saved alongside [Steven Gerrard](/wiki/Steven_Gerrard) and [Jamie Carragher](/wiki/Jamie_Carragher).

He scored in a 2–1 loss to Germany in a friendly at the [new Wembley](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium) in 2007. He was very disappointing during qualifying for [Euro 2008](/wiki/Euro_2008). He was booed by England supporters while coming on as a second-half substitute during England's [Euro 2008 qualifying](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_qualifying) match against [Estonia](/wiki/Estonia_national_football_team) on 13 October 2007. Lampard sent [Stipe Pletikosa](/wiki/Stipe_Pletikosa) the wrong way from the spot and finished with one goal (in a 3–2 loss to [Croatia](/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) on 21 November) as England failed to qualify for the tournament. He scored his first international goal in two years in a 4–0 win over Slovakia in March 2009, and also created another for [Wayne Rooney](/wiki/Wayne_Rooney). Lampard's goal was the 500th England goal scored at [Wembley](/wiki/Wembley_Stadium). On 9 September 2009, Lampard struck twice in England's 5–1 win against Croatia which secured their place at [2010 World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup).

### 2010–12: 2010 FIFA World Cup and UEFA Euro 2012 injury

Lampard *(wearing No.8)* enters the field with his England comrades before the [2010 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup) match against [the United States](/wiki/United_States_men%27s_national_soccer_team).

In the round-of-16 match of the [2010 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup) against rivals [Germany](/wiki/Germany_national_football_team), Lampard had a first-half shot at goal that bounced off the crossbar and from TV replays was clearly seen to cross the goal line. If counted, it would have tied the game 2–2. However, neither the referee nor the linesman saw it as a goal, and play was continued. In the second-half, Lampard hit the cross-bar again, with a 30-yard free-kick. The final score was a 4–1 win for Germany, eliminating England from the tournament.

As England exited the [2010 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup), Lampard had already achieved the record of having made 37 shots on goal without scoring in a World Cup tournament, more than any other player since [1966](/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup).

Lampard taking a [penalty](/wiki/Penalty_kick_(association_football)) in a [Euro 2012 qualifier](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying_Group_G) against [Wales](/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) in 2011

On 8 February 2011, it was announced that Lampard would [captain](/wiki/Captaincy_(association_football)) the national team against [Denmark](/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) in a friendly played the next day after both [Rio Ferdinand](/wiki/Rio_Ferdinand) and [Steven Gerrard](/wiki/Steven_Gerrard) were absent through injury. In the [Euro 2012 qualifiers](/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying), Lampard netted two goals for England, both penalties. The first against [Wales](/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) in a 2–0 win, and the latter vs [Switzerland](/wiki/Switzerland_national_football_team) in a 2–2 draw. In November 2011, Lampard captained England to a 1–0 friendly victory over current World Champions Spain, a game in which he scored the only goal.
On 31 May 2012, he was ruled out of the [Euro 2012](/wiki/Euro_2012) due to a thigh injury. He was subsequently replaced by [Jordan Henderson](/wiki/Jordan_Henderson).

### 2012–14: Final years with England

On 14 August 2012, manager [Roy Hodgson](/wiki/Roy_Hodgson) announced that Lampard would captain the Three Lions in their upcoming friendly against [Italy](/wiki/Italy_national_football_team) the following day, which England won 2–1. Lampard started England's first game in qualification and starred as [England](/wiki/England_national_football_team) thrashed [Moldova](/wiki/Moldova_national_football_team) 5–0, with Lampard scoring his 24th and 25th England goals. His first goal was England's 100th ever penalty and his second was a header from a cross from [Glen Johnson](/wiki/Glen_Johnson_(English_footballer)), after [Steven Gerrard](/wiki/Steven_Gerrard) was substituted he captained the side. On 22 March 2013, he surpassed [David Platt](/wiki/David_Platt_(footballer)) as [England's highest scoring midfielder](/wiki/England_national_football_team#Top_goalscorers) when he scored his 28th international goal in a 0–8 win over [San Marino](/wiki/San_Marino_national_football_team). Lampard became only the eighth English player to earn his 100th England cap, doing so in a World Cup qualifier against Ukraine, on 10 September 2013.

On 12 May 2014, Lampard was named in the 23-man England squad for the [2014 FIFA World Cup](/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup) and a week later he was named vice-captain. On 24 June, for the last game of the group stage, Lampard captained his England side to draw with [Costa Rica](/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) 0–0. On 26 August 2014, Lampard announced that he had retired from international football. He earned 106 caps, scoring 29 goals.

## Style of play

Lampard attempting to strike a volley

Regarded by some pundits, managers, and teammates as one of the greatest players of his generation, Lampard was an all-round and versatile [box-to-box](/wiki/Midfielder#Box-to-box_midfielder) player, who was capable of playing anywhere in midfield; throughout his career he was deployed as a [central midfielder](/wiki/Central_midfielder), as an [attacking midfielder](/wiki/Attacking_midfielder), as a [defensive midfielder](/wiki/Defensive_midfielder), and even as a [supporting striker](/wiki/Supporting_striker) on occasion. He was also deployed as a [deep-lying playmaker](/wiki/Deep-lying_playmaker) on occasion, in particular in his later career, due to his ability to build attacks with his passing.

A hard-working player, with notable stamina and an ability to read the game, Lampard was also capable of functioning creatively and starting attacking plays or providing [assists](/wiki/Assist_(association_football)) for teammates after winning back possession, due to his technique, vision, and passing range. In addition to his creative and defensive midfield duties, Lampard also possessed a keen eye for goal, due to his ability to get forward and make late attacking runs from behind into the penalty area, or produce accurate and powerful shots from distance. These attributes enabled him to maintain a prolific goalscoring record throughout his career, despite his deep playing position. Moreover, Lampard was also an accurate [set-piece](/wiki/Set_piece_(football)) and [penalty kick](/wiki/Penalty_kick_(association_football)) taker, who was known for scoring with powerful, low driving shots from [free kicks](/wiki/Free_kick_(association_football)).

Beyond his qualities as a footballer, he also stood out for his leadership throughout his career. In 2017, [Gareth Southgate](/wiki/Gareth_Southgate) labelled Lampard as one of the group of England's past players who were "very good", but below the likes of the nation's past "top players", such as [Gascoigne](/wiki/Paul_Gascoigne), [Scholes](/wiki/Paul_Scholes), and [Rooney](/wiki/Wayne_Rooney), commenting: "You've got very good players and then there are top players. In my time in the England setup, Paul Gascoigne, Paul Scholes and Rooney just had that little bit more than all the others. And we are talking high‑level people there, players like [Steven Gerrard](/wiki/Steven_Gerrard), Frank Lampard and [David Beckham](/wiki/David_Beckham)."

## Managerial career

### Derby County

On 31 May 2018, Lampard was appointed as [manager](/wiki/Manager_(association_football)) of [Championship](/wiki/EFL_Championship) club Derby County on a three-year contract. His first game in charge, on 3 August, resulted in a 2–1 away win for Derby at [Reading](/wiki/Reading_F.C.), with [Tom Lawrence](/wiki/Tom_Lawrence) scoring a last-minute winner. His first loss as a manager came on 11 August in a 4–1 loss against [Leeds United](/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.), in his second match in charge. On 25 September, in just his 12th game as a manager, Lampard's Derby County knocked [Premier League](/wiki/Premier_League) club [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) out of the [EFL Cup](/wiki/EFL_Cup) on penalties, following a 2–2 draw at [Old Trafford](/wiki/Old_Trafford).

After a 2–0 defeat in the return fixture against Leeds United at [Elland Road](/wiki/Elland_Road) on 11 January 2019 to further Leeds' lead at the top of the Championship table, Lampard was critical of Leeds head coach [Marcelo Bielsa](/wiki/Marcelo_Bielsa), as in the pre-match build-up Bielsa admitted he had sent a spy to the Derby training ground, after reports emerged in the press that a man was spotted the previous day outside the Derby training ground. On 12 January, Leeds United released a statement in response to the incident. [Tottenham Hotspur](/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) manager [Mauricio Pochettino](/wiki/Mauricio_Pochettino) described the incident as 'not a big deal' and commonplace in Argentina.

In his first season in charge of Derby, Lampard guided the club to the [Championship play-offs](/wiki/2019_English_Football_League_play-offs) after helping them finish 6th in the [2018–19 Championship season](/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_EFL_Championship). In the semi-finals of the play-offs, Derby overturned a 0–1 home loss to win 4–2 away against Leeds United and claim the tie 4–3 on aggregate, setting up an appearance against [Aston Villa](/wiki/Aston_Villa_F.C.) in the [final](/wiki/2019_EFL_Championship_play-off_Final). Derby lost the play-off final to Aston Villa 2–1.

Upon the conclusion of the season, Lampard was heavily linked with the vacant managerial spot at former club Chelsea. On 25 June 2019, Derby granted Lampard permission to hold talks with the Chelsea bosses over said position.

### Chelsea

Lampard managing Chelsea in 2019

On 4 July 2019, Lampard was appointed as the new head coach at former club Chelsea on a three-year contract, making him the first English manager to manage the side in over two decades. In Lampard's first competitive game with Chelsea, the club lost 4–0 to [Manchester United](/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) on the opening day of the [2019–20 Premier League](/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Premier_League). It was the biggest defeat for a Chelsea manager in their first game since [Danny Blanchflower](/wiki/Danny_Blanchflower)'s team was beaten 7–2 by [Middlesbrough](/wiki/Middlesbrough_F.C.) in December 1978. In Lampard's second competitive game, they lost to [Liverpool](/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) on penalties in the [2019 UEFA Super Cup](/wiki/2019_UEFA_Super_Cup).

On 24 August 2019, Lampard obtained his first Premier League victory as Chelsea manager, beating [Norwich City](/wiki/Norwich_City_F.C.) 2–3 away from home. On 25 September, Chelsea picked up their first home win under Lampard, beating [League Two](/wiki/EFL_League_Two) side [Grimsby Town](/wiki/Grimsby_Town_F.C.) 7–1 in the [EFL Cup](/wiki/EFL_Cup). Lampard's matchday squad against Grimsby featured ten [academy](/wiki/Chelsea_F.C._Under-23s_and_Academy) graduates, including [Reece James](/wiki/Reece_James_(footballer,_born_1999)) and [Billy Gilmour](/wiki/Billy_Gilmour_(footballer)), making their first-team debuts. Lampard won [Premier League Manager of the Month](/wiki/Premier_League_Manager_of_the_Month) for October 2019 after Chelsea recorded a perfect record in the league for the month. By doing so, he became the third manager to win [Premier League Player of the Month](/wiki/Premier_League_Player_of_the_Month) and Manager of the Month, after [Gareth Southgate](/wiki/Gareth_Southgate) and [Stuart Pearce](/wiki/Stuart_Pearce). Lampard eventually guided Chelsea to fourth in the Premier League and the [FA Cup Final](/wiki/2020_FA_Cup_Final), where they lost to [Arsenal](/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.).

In the [following season](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Chelsea_F.C._season), Chelsea made five major acquisitions in the summer transfer window in [Hakim Ziyech](/wiki/Hakim_Ziyech), [Timo Werner](/wiki/Timo_Werner), [Ben Chilwell](/wiki/Ben_Chilwell), [Kai Havertz](/wiki/Kai_Havertz) and [Édouard Mendy](/wiki/%C3%89douard_Mendy). Chelsea initially started strongly, topping their Champions League group and the Premier League in early December. However, after a run of two wins in eight Premier League matches, Chelsea dropped to ninth and Lampard was dismissed as manager on 25 January 2021. He remained on Chelsea's payroll to see out his contract, "pocketing £75,000" per week in compensation. His dismissal came just 24 hours after Chelsea's 3–1 win over [Luton Town](/wiki/Luton_Town_F.C.) in the [FA Cup](/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FA_Cup). On 26 January, German coach [Thomas Tuchel](/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel) was announced as his replacement.

It was reported Lampard's departure was also influenced by disagreements with the club's transfer policy; Lampard is said to have requested the acquisitions of [Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang](/wiki/Pierre-Emerick_Aubameyang), [Declan Rice](/wiki/Declan_Rice), and [James Tarkowski](/wiki/James_Tarkowski), with these moves being blocked by Chelsea's co-director [Marina Granovskaia](/wiki/Marina_Granovskaia). Lampard also reportedly fell out with or had limited communication with some players, causing former teammate and club advisor [Petr Čech](/wiki/Petr_%C4%8Cech) to have to serve as an intermediary.

### Everton

Having been out of work for a year, Lampard was appointed manager of Premier League club Everton on 31 January 2022, after the dismissal of [Rafael Benítez](/wiki/Rafael_Ben%C3%ADtez) due to poor results. At the time of his appointment, the club was positioned in 16th place, four points above the relegation zone. With one game remaining in the [2021–22 season](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Everton_F.C._season), on 19 May Lampard guided Everton to Premier League safety maintaining their 68-year top flight status. Having been 2–0 down at half-time to [Crystal Palace](/wiki/Crystal_Palace_F.C.) Lampard brought on [Dele Alli](/wiki/Dele_Alli) and played a more attacking formation resulting in a 3–2 win for the Merseyside team. Lampard described the night as "one of the greatest nights of my career".

The following season saw the club once again fall into a relegation battle, with a run of only 1 win in 11 matches leaving the club 19th in the league by January. Everton owner [Farhad Moshiri](/wiki/Farhad_Moshiri) in an interview with [Talksport](/wiki/Talksport) suggested that Lampard's position at the club remained secure despite this run of form. However, Lampard was dismissed a few days later on 23 January 2023, just eight days less than a year into the job, after a 2–0 loss to West Ham. Lampard was replaced by [Sean Dyche](/wiki/Sean_Dyche) on 30 January 2023.

### Return to Chelsea

On 6 April 2023, Lampard returned to Chelsea by being appointed as [caretaker manager](/wiki/Caretaker_manager) until the end of the [2022–23 season](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Chelsea_F.C._season), following the dismissal of [Graham Potter](/wiki/Graham_Potter). Chelsea's form dropped after Lampard took over and produced a first bottom-half finish since 1996 and a record low tally of points and goals scored in the Premier League era. In terms of winning percentage, Lampard also had the worst record (9%) among every [Chelsea manager](/wiki/List_of_Chelsea_F.C._managers) who had led 3 or more matches, achieving just one win in his 11 matches in charge.

### Coventry City

On 28 November 2024, Lampard was appointed as the new manager of [EFL Championship](/wiki/EFL_Championship) club [Coventry City](/wiki/Coventry_City_F.C.). He took the club to 5th in the [Championship](/wiki/EFL_Championship), however lost 3–2 on aggregate to [Sunderland](/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.) in the semi-finals of the [play-offs](/wiki/EFL_Championship_play-offs).

In the [2025–26 season](/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Coventry_City_F.C._season), Lampard led Coventry City to the top of the league in the early stages, getting named [EFL Championship Manager of the Month](/wiki/EFL_Championship_Manager_of_the_Month) for October 2025. Continued good form saw him win the award for a second consecutive month after a 100% record over five matches, scoring fourteen goals.

On 20 December 2025, after a 1–1 draw at [Southampton](/wiki/Southampton_F.C.), Lampard was instrumental in starting a melee involving players and staff from both teams after making provocative gestures at home supporters. His behaviour was criticised by a Regulatory Commission, whose report said: “It [the Commission] considered the behaviour of FL, both in goading the fans and in interacting with THB ([Taylor Harwood-Bellis](/wiki/Taylor_Harwood-Bellis)) to have been unacceptable and the catalyst for what followed …”. On 17 April 2026, Coventry City secured promotion to the Premier League after 25 years, finishing top of the Championship following a 1–1 away draw with [Blackburn Rovers](/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.).

## Media

From 2015 until 2017, Lampard served as a team captain on the [ITV](/wiki/ITV_(TV_network)) comedy panel show *[Play to the Whistle](/wiki/Play_to_the_Whistle)* alongside [Bradley Walsh](/wiki/Bradley_Walsh). He was a regular pundit for football on [BT Sport](/wiki/BT_Sport). For the [2018 World Cup](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup), Lampard was among the BBC's list of pundits as he insisted that it would not affect his managerial duties.

Lampard features in [EA Sports](/wiki/EA_Sports)' [*FIFA* video game series](/wiki/FIFA_(video_game_series)); he was on the cover for the International edition of *[FIFA 10](/wiki/FIFA_10)*, alongside [Wayne Rooney](/wiki/Wayne_Rooney) and [Theo Walcott](/wiki/Theo_Walcott). In 2018, Lampard was added as an icon to the Ultimate Team in *[FIFA 19](/wiki/FIFA_19)*.

On Sunday 9 June 2024, Lampard led England to a 6–3 victory as manager in the annual [Soccer Aid](/wiki/Soccer_Aid) game against the rest of the world. The match raised £15 million for charity.

## Personal life

Lampard in 2017

Lampard was born in [Romford](/wiki/Romford), [London](/wiki/London); he has two sisters, Natalie and Claire. He attended [Brentwood School](/wiki/Brentwood_School,_Essex) between 1989 and 1994, finishing with eleven [GCSEs](/wiki/General_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education), including an A\* in [Latin](/wiki/Latin).

Lampard belongs to a family of footballers; his father is [Frank Lampard, Sr.](/wiki/Frank_Lampard,_Sr.), the former [West Ham United](/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.) player and assistant manager who was still associated with the club when Lampard junior was playing for them. Lampard's uncle is [Harry Redknapp](/wiki/Harry_Redknapp) and his cousin is Redknapp's son [Jamie Redknapp](/wiki/Jamie_Redknapp). His mother Pat, a librarian, died in April 2008 aged 58 as a result of [pneumonia](/wiki/Pneumonia).

Lampard is a [Christian](/wiki/Christianity), and credits his faith for helping him cope with the death of his mother.

Lampard is a supporter of the [Conservative Party](/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)) and was rumoured to be running as a candidate for [Kensington](/wiki/Kensington_(UK_Parliament_constituency)) upon [Malcolm Rifkind](/wiki/Malcolm_Rifkind)'s retirement at the [2015 general election](/wiki/2015_United_Kingdom_general_election). He subsequently denied that he was running to be an MP.

He was appointed an [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (OBE) in the [2015 Birthday Honours](/wiki/2015_Birthday_Honours). Lampard published his autobiography, *Totally Frank*, in 2006. He has written a series of children's novels, inspired by his mother and children, related to football.

Lampard formerly owned The Pig's Ear, Chelsea's oldest pub, with his father. They sold the business in 2018, and his father then took over the [Grade II](/wiki/Grade_II) Nightingale pub in Wanstead in 2019. Lampard has retained the ownership of the freehold at the Old Church Street, Chelsea property.

Lampard has had a troubled relationship with his boyhood club West Ham United where he was seen by some fans as a product of [nepotism](/wiki/Nepotism). He claims that he often received negative taunts about his weight, and that during a match as an 18-year-old when he broke his leg in play, he heard cheers from fans. Following his transfer to Chelsea, Lampard turned against his former club and has since stated: "I remember when [Joe Cole](/wiki/Joe_Cole) first came to Chelsea he would turn away in disappointment if West Ham lost. I would smile. That's how deeply I felt. I wanted West Ham to lose. Now I don't even look for their results." Later in his career, Lampard adopted a more mature attitude towards his former club saying that he had complete respect for the club and thanking Harry Redknapp and [Tony Carr](/wiki/Tony_Carr) for the influence at the start of his career.

### Controversies

In 2000, Lampard, [Rio Ferdinand](/wiki/Rio_Ferdinand) and [Kieron Dyer](/wiki/Kieron_Dyer) appeared on a sex video that was filmed at the holiday resort of [Ayia Napa](/wiki/Ayia_Napa) in Cyprus. Channel 4 aired a brief edited clip as part of their 2004 documentary *Sex, Footballers and Videotape*, claiming it was used to "remind the viewer that this is based on real life". On 23 September 2001, Lampard, along with three other Chelsea players, was fined two weeks' wages by the club for his behaviour whilst on a drinking binge on 12 September. Lampard and the others had abused American tourists at a Heathrow hotel, just 24 hours after the [September 11 attacks](/wiki/September_11_attacks). A hotel manager stated: "they were utterly disgusting. They just didn't seem to care about what had happened". Lampard apologised over his behaviour when he signed for NYCFC in 2014, implying that he did not mean offence and that he was naive and young.

In 2021, Lampard was charged with using a mobile phone while driving after being filmed by [CyclingMikey](/wiki/CyclingMikey) holding a takeaway cup in one hand and a mobile phone in the other. Lampard hired [Nick Freeman](/wiki/Nick_Freeman) to defend him. The case was later dropped as the Crown Prosecution Service stated it had insufficient evidence.

### Relationships

Lampard lives in [Surrey](/wiki/Surrey) and London. He has two daughters with his former [fiancée](/wiki/Fianc%C3%A9e), Spanish model Elen Rivas.

On 24 April 2009, Lampard was involved in a radio confrontation with [James O'Brien](/wiki/James_O%27Brien_(radio_presenter)) on the London radio station [LBC 97.3](/wiki/LBC_97.3). Newspapers had reported that following Lampard's split from Rivas their children were living with her in a small flat while Lampard had converted their family home into a [bachelor pad](/wiki/Bachelor_pad). Lampard phoned in, objecting to criticism and stating that he had fought "tooth and nail" to keep his family together.

Lampard married [Christine Bleakley](/wiki/Christine_Lampard), a Northern Irish broadcaster, on 20 December 2015. They have a daughter born in 2018 and a son born in 2021.

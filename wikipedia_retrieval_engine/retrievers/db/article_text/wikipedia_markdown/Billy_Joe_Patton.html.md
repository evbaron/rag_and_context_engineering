**William Joseph Patton** (April 19, 1922 – January 1, 2011) was an American [golfer](/wiki/Golf) best known for almost winning the [1954 Masters Tournament](/wiki/1954_Masters_Tournament).

## Early life and amateur career

Patton was born in [Morganton, North Carolina](/wiki/Morganton,_North_Carolina) in 1922. He graduated from [Wake Forest University](/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) in 1943.

## Professional career

In the [1954 Masters Tournament](/wiki/1954_Masters_Tournament) at [Augusta National Golf Club](/wiki/Augusta_National_Golf_Club), Patton came within one stroke of being in a three-man playoff with [Ben Hogan](/wiki/Ben_Hogan) and [Sam Snead](/wiki/Sam_Snead). His final round 71 included a hole-in-one on the par-3 6th hole and a double bogey on the par-5 13th hole, when he tried to reach the green in two and put his ball into [Rae's Creek](/wiki/Augusta_National_Golf_Club#Natural_features).

Patton won several amateur tournaments including the [North and South Amateur](/wiki/North_and_South_Amateur) three times and the [Southern Amateur](/wiki/Southern_Amateur) twice. He also won the [Carolinas Open](/wiki/Carolinas_Open) twice.

Patton played on five [Walker Cup](/wiki/Walker_Cup) teams: in 1955, 1957, 1959, 1963, and 1965 and was captain of the 1969 team. He played on the [Eisenhower Trophy](/wiki/Eisenhower_Trophy) team in 1958 and 1962.

## Awards and honors

- In 1967, Patton was inducted into the [North Carolina Sports Hall of Fame](/wiki/North_Carolina_Sports_Hall_of_Fame).
- In 1974, he was inducted into the Wake Forest University's Sports Hall of Fame.
- In 1975, Patton earned entry into the Southern Golf Association Hall of Fame.
- In 1981, the Carolinas Golf Reporters' Association inducted him into the Carolinas Golf Hall of Fame.
- In 1982, Patton was awarded the [Bob Jones Award](/wiki/Bob_Jones_Award) by the [United States Golf Association](/wiki/United_States_Golf_Association).
- In 2007, he was inducted into the Burke County Sports Hall of Fame.

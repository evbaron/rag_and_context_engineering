# South Korea at the Olympics

## Infobox

- **IOC code:** KOR
- **NOC:** Korean Olympic Committee
- **Website:** www.sports.or.kr (in Korean and English)
- **Medals Ranked 15th:** Gold 145 Silver 134 Bronze 130 Total 409

The [Republic of Korea](/wiki/South_Korea) (commonly known as South Korea) first participated at the [Olympic Games](/wiki/Olympic_Games) in 1948, and has sent athletes to compete in every [Summer Olympic Games](/wiki/Summer_Olympic_Games) since then, except for [1980](/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics) which they [boycotted](/wiki/American-led_boycott_of_the_1980_Summer_Olympics). South Korea has also participated in every [Winter Olympic Games](/wiki/Winter_Olympic_Games) since 1948, except for the 1952 games.

The first ethnic Korean athletes to win medals did so at the [1936 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) in [Berlin](/wiki/Berlin), when [Sohn Kee-chung](/wiki/Sohn_Kee-chung) and [Nam Sung-yong](/wiki/Nam_Sung-yong) won gold and bronze respectively in the men's [marathon](/wiki/Marathon) as members of the Japanese team. As Korea was under [Japanese rule](/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule) at that time, making them both Japanese subjects, the IOC credits both medals to [Japan](/wiki/Japan_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics).

South Korea won its first medals as an independent nation at its first appearance in [1948](/wiki/South_Korea_at_the_1948_Summer_Olympics), and won its first gold medal in [1976](/wiki/South_Korea_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics). South Korean athletes have won a total of 287 medals at the Summer Games, with the most gold medals won in [archery](/wiki/Archery), and 79 medals at the Winter Games, a majority in [short track speed skating](/wiki/Short_track_speed_skating). The nation has won more medals in this winter sport than any other nation since it was introduced to the Olympic program in 1992.

The [National Olympic Committee](/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) for Korea is the [Korean Sport & Olympic Committee](/wiki/Korean_Sport_%26_Olympic_Committee), which was founded in 1946 and recognized in 1947.

During the [Sunshine Policy](/wiki/Sunshine_Policy) era, South Korea and [North Korea](/wiki/North_Korea_at_the_Olympics) symbolically marched as one team at the opening ceremonies of the 2000, 2004 and 2006 Olympics, but competed separately. At the [2018 Winter Olympics](/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics), North Korean and South Korean athletes [unified](/wiki/Unified_Korean_sporting_teams) to form the [Korea women's national ice hockey team](/wiki/Korea_women%27s_national_ice_hockey_team).

### Notes

On 11 February 2014, Lee Sang-hwa won the gold medal for the women's 500m longtrack speedskating race at the [2014 Sochi Winter Olympics](/wiki/2014_Sochi_Winter_Olympics), having previously won the one at the 2010 Games. She became the third woman and first Korean woman to win back-to-back golds at the 500m.

## Hosted Olympic logos and mottos

### 1988 Summer Olympics

The [1988 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics), held in the capital of [Seoul](/wiki/Seoul), marked the first time the Olympics were held in South Korea.

The logo consists of a rounded tricolor which represents the Olympic rings. The motto of the games was *Harmony and Progress* (화합과 전진).

### 2018 Winter Olympics

The [2018 Winter Olympics](/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics) saw the Olympics return to Korea when it was held in [Pyeongchang](/wiki/Pyeongchang_County).

The logo consists of five intertwined wings which represent the continents (Africa, America, Asia, Europe and Oceania). The motto of the games was *Passion. Connected.* (하나된 열정; Hanadoen, Yeoljeong).

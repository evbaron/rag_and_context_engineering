# 78th Academy Awards

The **78th Academy Awards**, presented by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) (AMPAS), took place on March 5, 2006, at the [Kodak Theatre](/wiki/Kodak_Theatre) in [Hollywood](/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles), Los Angeles beginning at 5:00 p.m. [PST](/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone) / 8:00 p.m. [EST](/wiki/Eastern_Time_Zone). The ceremony was scheduled one week later than usual to avoid a clash with the [2006 Winter Olympics](/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics). During the ceremony, AMPAS presented [Academy Awards](/wiki/Academy_Awards) (commonly referred to as Oscars) in 24 categories honoring [films released in 2005](/wiki/2005_in_film). The ceremony, televised in the United States by [ABC](/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company), was produced by [Gil Cates](/wiki/Gilbert_Cates) and directed by Louis J. Horvitz. Comedian [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart) hosted the show for the first time. Two weeks earlier in a ceremony at [The Beverly Hilton](/wiki/The_Beverly_Hilton) in [Beverly Hills, California](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California) held on February 18, the [Academy Awards for Technical Achievement](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Technical_Achievement) were presented by host [Rachel McAdams](/wiki/Rachel_McAdams).

*[Crash](/wiki/Crash_(2004_film))* won three awards, including [Best Picture](/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Picture). Other winners included *[Brokeback Mountain](/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain)*, *[King Kong](/wiki/King_Kong_(2005_film))*, and *[Memoirs of a Geisha](/wiki/Memoirs_of_a_Geisha_(film))* with three awards and *[Capote](/wiki/Capote_(film))*, *[The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe](/wiki/The_Chronicles_of_Narnia:_The_Lion,_the_Witch_and_the_Wardrobe)*, *[The Constant Gardener](/wiki/The_Constant_Gardener_(film))*, *[Hustle & Flow](/wiki/Hustle_%26_Flow)*, *[March of the Penguins](/wiki/March_of_the_Penguins)*, *[The Moon and the Son: An Imagined Conversation](/wiki/The_Moon_and_the_Son:_An_Imagined_Conversation)*, *[A Note of Triumph: The Golden Age of Norman Corwin](/wiki/A_Note_of_Triumph:_The_Golden_Age_of_Norman_Corwin)*, *[Six Shooter](/wiki/Six_Shooter_(film))*, *[Syriana](/wiki/Syriana)*, *[Tsotsi](/wiki/Tsotsi)*, *[Walk the Line](/wiki/Walk_the_Line)*, and *[Wallace & Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit](/wiki/Wallace_%26_Gromit:_The_Curse_of_the_Were-Rabbit)* with one. The telecast garnered nearly 39 million viewers in the United States.

## Winners and nominees

The nominees for the 78th Academy Awards were announced on January 31, 2006, at the [Samuel Goldwyn Theater](/wiki/Samuel_Goldwyn) in the academy's [Beverly Hills](/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_California) headquarters by [Sid Ganis](/wiki/Sid_Ganis), president of the academy, and actress [Mira Sorvino](/wiki/Mira_Sorvino). *[Brokeback Mountain](/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain)* earned the most nominations with eight total; *[Crash](/wiki/Crash_(2004_film))*, *[Good Night, and Good Luck](/wiki/Good_Night,_and_Good_Luck)*, and *[Memoirs of a Geisha](/wiki/Memoirs_of_a_Geisha_(film))* tied for second with six nominations each. All five Best Picture nominees received corresponding Best Director nominations (the fourth occurrence in Oscar history since the Best Picture nominees roster was limited to five films).

The winners were announced during the awards ceremony on March 5, 2006. *[Crash](/wiki/Crash_(2004_film))* was the first Best Picture winner since 1976's *[Rocky](/wiki/Rocky)* to win only three Oscars and the first Best Picture winner since 1981's *[Chariots of Fire](/wiki/Chariots_of_Fire)* to not win Best Director or any of the acting categories. Best Director winner [Ang Lee](/wiki/Ang_Lee) became the first non-Caucasian winner of that category. For this first time since the [34th ceremony](/wiki/34th_Academy_Awards) in 1962, all four acting winners were first-time nominees. At age 20, [Keira Knightley](/wiki/Keira_Knightley) was the [second-youngest](/wiki/List_of_oldest_and_youngest_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Best_Actress_in_a_Leading_Role) Best Actress nominee for her performance as [Elizabeth Bennet](/wiki/Elizabeth_Bennet) in *[Pride & Prejudice](/wiki/Pride_%26_Prejudice_(2005_film))*. Best Supporting Actor winner [George Clooney](/wiki/George_Clooney) was the fifth person to receive acting, directing, and screenwriting nominations in the same year and the first person to achieve this feat for two different films. By virtue of his nominations for both *[Memoirs of a Geisha](/wiki/Memoirs_of_a_Geisha_(film))* and *[Munich](/wiki/Munich_(2005_film))*, composer [John Williams](/wiki/John_Williams) earned a total of 45 nominations tying him with [Alfred Newman](/wiki/Alfred_Newman_(composer)) as the second most nominated individual in Oscar history.[[a]](#endnote_Williams) "[It's Hard out Here for a Pimp](/wiki/It%27s_Hard_out_Here_for_a_Pimp)" became the second rap song to win Best Original Song and the first such song to be performed at an Oscars ceremony.

## Ceremony information

[Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart) hosted the 78th Academy Awards.

Despite the negative reception from the [preceding year's ceremony](/wiki/77th_Academy_Awards), the academy rehired [Gilbert Cates](/wiki/Gilbert_Cates) to oversee production of the awards gala. However, in an article published in *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, it was stated that 2005 host [Chris Rock](/wiki/Chris_Rock) would not return to host the show. According to a statement released by his publicist, "He didn't want to do it in perpetuity, He'd like to do it again down the road." Furthermore, many media outlets speculated that several AMPAS members felt uncomfortable with Rock's disparaging comments about [Colin Farrell](/wiki/Colin_Farrell), [Jude Law](/wiki/Jude_Law), and [Tobey Maguire](/wiki/Tobey_Maguire). Initially, Cates sought actor and veteran Oscar host [Billy Crystal](/wiki/Billy_Crystal) to host the ceremony again. However, Crystal declined the offer citing his commitment to his one-man comedy show *[700 Sundays](/wiki/700_Sundays)*.

In January 2006, Cates announced that actor, comedian, and talk show host [Jon Stewart](/wiki/Jon_Stewart), who had previously hosted two consecutive [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Award) ceremonies in [2001](/wiki/43rd_Annual_Grammy_Awards) and [2002](/wiki/44th_Annual_Grammy_Awards), was chosen as host of the 2006 telecast. Cates explained the decision to hire him saying, "My wife and I watch him every night. Jon is the epitome of a perfect host — smart, engaging, irreverent and funny." In a statement, Stewart expressed that he was honored to be selected to emcee the program, jokingly adding, "Although, as an avid watcher of the Oscars, I can't help but be a little disappointed with the choice. It appears to be another sad attempt to smoke out Billy Crystal."

Several other people and companies participated in the production of the ceremony. [Bill Conti](/wiki/Bill_Conti) served as musical supervisor for the telecast. Media firm [The Ant Farm](/wiki/The_Ant_Farm) produced a thirty-second trailer promoting the broadcast featuring clips highlighting past Oscar winners to the tune of the song "[Our Lives](/wiki/Our_Lives_(song))" by [The Calling](/wiki/The_Calling_(band)). Previous Oscar hosts such as [Whoopi Goldberg](/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg) and [Steve Martin](/wiki/Steve_Martin), and actors [Mel Gibson](/wiki/Mel_Gibson), [George Clooney](/wiki/George_Clooney), [Halle Berry](/wiki/Halle_Berry) appeared in an opening comedic sketch. Actor [Tom Hanks](/wiki/Tom_Hanks) participated in a pre-taped comedic sketch lampooning Oscar speeches. [Stephen Colbert](/wiki/Stephen_Colbert) (host of *[The Colbert Report](/wiki/The_Colbert_Report)*, the sister program of Stewart's *[The Daily Show](/wiki/The_Daily_Show)*) narrated two different mock [attack ads](/wiki/Attack_ad) lampooning both the intense [campaigning and lobbying](/wiki/For_Your_Consideration_(advertising)) during Oscar season put forth by film studios and [political advertising](/wiki/Political_advertising) during elections. Violinist [Itzhak Perlman](/wiki/Itzhak_Perlman) performed excerpts from the five nominees for Best Original Score.

### Box office performance of nominated films

When the nominations were announced on January 31, the field of major nominees favored independent, low-budget films over blockbusters. The combined gross of the five Best Picture nominees when the Oscars were announced was $186 million with an average gross of $37.3 million per film. *Crash* was the highest earner among the Best Picture nominees with $53.4 million in domestic box office receipts. The film was followed by *Brokeback Mountain* ($51.7 million), *Munich* ($40.8 million), *Good Night and Good Luck* ($25.2 million), and finally *Capote* ($15.4 million).

Of the top 50 grossing movies of the year, 35 nominations went to 13 films on the list. Only  *Walk the Line* (19th), *Cinderella Man* (41st), *Wallace and Gromit: The Curse of the Were-Rabbit* (45th), and *Crash* (48th) were nominated for Best Picture, Best Animated Feature, or any of the directing, acting, or screenwriting. The other top 50 box office hits that earned nominations were *Star Wars Episode III: Revenge of the Sith* (1st), *Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire* (2nd), *The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, The Witch, and The Wardrobe* (3rd), *War of the Worlds* (4th), *King Kong* (5th), *Charlie and the Chocolate Factory* (7th), *Batman Begins* (8th), *March of the Penguins*, (26th), and *Memoirs of a Geisha* (47th).

### Critical reviews

Some media outlets received the broadcast positively. *[St. Louis Post-Dispatch](/wiki/St._Louis_Post-Dispatch)* television critic Gail Pennington praised Stewart's performance as host writing that he "did the Oscars proud Sunday night, turning in a four-star hosting performance that unfortunately made the rest of the show seem sluggish by comparison." Film critic [Roger Ebert](/wiki/Roger_Ebert) said that Stewart was "on target, topical and funny," and added, "He was as relaxed, amusing and at home as [Johnny Carson](/wiki/Johnny_Carson)." Columnist Ray Richmond of *[The Hollywood Reporter](/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* commented, "He seemed at times nervous and self-conscious, but on the whole, Stewart delivered with just the right balance of reverence and smugness."

Others media publications were more critical of the show. Television critic [Rob Owen](/wiki/Rob_Owen_(journalist)) of the *[Pittsburgh Post-Gazette](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Post-Gazette)* wrote that Stewart was more "amusing than funny". He added, "Many of his jokes fell flat with the stars in the Kodak Theatre, and his tendency to bow down before celebrities quickly grew tiresome." [Tom Shales](/wiki/Tom_Shales) from *[The Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* commented, "It's hard to believe that professional entertainers could have put together a show less entertaining than this year's Oscars, hosted with a smug humorlessness by comic Jon Stewart, a sad and pale shadow of great hosts gone by." Moreover, he derided the "piles and piles and miles and miles of clips from films present and past" writing that it "squandered the visual luster" of the ceremony. [Associated Press](/wiki/Associated_Press) television critic [Frazier Moore](/wiki/Frazier_Moore) remarked, "Stewart, usually a very funny guy, displayed a lack of beginner's luck as first-time host...His usually impeccable blend of puckishness and self-effacement fell flat in the service of Oscar." He also criticized the decision to play music over the winner's acceptance speeches calling it "distracting and obnoxious."

In retrospective commentary, *Crash*'s win for Best Picture over *Brokeback Mountain* has become "one of the most notorious [decisions] in modern Academy history" and a source of "earnest bafflement from critics". Rankings by several publications and critics have called *Crash* one of the worst or most controversial Best Picture winners.

### Ratings and reception

The American telecast on ABC drew in an average of 38.94 million people over its length, which was an 8% decrease from the [previous year's ceremony](/wiki/77th_Academy_Awards). Additionally, the show earned lower [Nielsen ratings](/wiki/Nielsen_ratings) compared to the previous ceremony with 23.0% of households watching over a 35 share. Furthermore, it garnered a lower 18–49 demo rating with a 13.9 rating among viewers in that demographic.

In July 2006, the ceremony presentation received nine nominations at the [58th Primetime Emmys](/wiki/58th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards). The following month, the ceremony won four of those nominations for [Outstanding Art Direction for a Variety, Music Program, or Special](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Production_Design_for_Variety,_Nonfiction,_Reality,_or_Reality-Competition_Programming) (Roy Christopher and Jeff Richman), [Outstanding Directing for a Variety, Music, or Comedy Program](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Directing_for_a_Variety_Series) (Louis J. Horvitz), [Outstanding Main Title Design](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Main_Title_Design) (Renato Grgic, Alen Petkovic, Kristijan Petrovic, and Jon Teschner), and [Outstanding Sound Mixing for a Variety, Music, or Animation Series or Special](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Sound_Mixing_for_a_Variety_Series_or_Special) (Patrick Baltzell, Robert Douglass, Edward J. Greene, Jamie Santos, and Tom Vicari).

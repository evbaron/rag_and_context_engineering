**David Michael Bell** (born September 14, 1972) is an American former professional [baseball](/wiki/Baseball) [infielder](/wiki/Infielder), [coach](/wiki/Coach_(baseball)), and [manager](/wiki/Manager_(baseball)) who most recently managed the [Cincinnati Reds](/wiki/Cincinnati_Reds) of [Major League Baseball](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball) (MLB). Over the course of his 12-year MLB playing career, Bell appeared at all four [infield](/wiki/Infield) positions while playing for the [Cleveland Indians](/wiki/Cleveland_Guardians), [St. Louis Cardinals](/wiki/St._Louis_Cardinals), [Seattle Mariners](/wiki/Seattle_Mariners), [San Francisco Giants](/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants), [Philadelphia Phillies](/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies), and [Milwaukee Brewers](/wiki/Milwaukee_Brewers), but played primarily at third and second. Bell made his MLB debut for the Indians in 1995.

After his retirement as an active player, Bell served as a [coach](/wiki/Coach_(baseball)) for the [Chicago Cubs](/wiki/Chicago_Cubs) and the [St. Louis Cardinals](/wiki/St._Louis_Cardinals). After managing the [Triple-A](/wiki/Triple-A_(baseball)) [Louisville Bats](/wiki/Louisville_Bats) and (former) [Double-A](/wiki/Double-A_(baseball)) [Carolina Mudcats](/wiki/Carolina_Mudcats_(1991%E2%80%932011)), both of which are or were in the Reds organization, Bell was chosen as the Reds manager in 2018. Bell spent six seasons with the team before he was fired just before the end of the 2024 season.

The grandson of [Gus Bell](/wiki/Gus_Bell), son of [Buddy Bell](/wiki/Buddy_Bell), and brother of [Mike Bell](/wiki/Mike_Bell_(third_baseman)), David Bell is a member of one of [five families to have three generations play in the Major Leagues](/wiki/List_of_second-generation_Major_League_Baseball_players#Three-generation_families). In addition, David and Buddy are the fifth father-son pair to serve as major league managers, joining [Connie](/wiki/Connie_Mack) and [Earle Mack](/wiki/Earle_Mack), [George](/wiki/George_Sisler) and [Dick Sisler](/wiki/Dick_Sisler), [Bob](/wiki/Bob_Skinner) and [Joel Skinner](/wiki/Joel_Skinner), and [Bob](/wiki/Bob_Boone) and [Aaron Boone](/wiki/Aaron_Boone).

## Amateur career

Bell attended [Moeller High School](/wiki/Moeller_High_School) in [Cincinnati](/wiki/Cincinnati), [Ohio](/wiki/Ohio). Playing for the school's [varsity](/wiki/Varsity_team) [baseball](/wiki/Baseball) team for three years, where he ranks in the Top Ten in five career categories including [doubles](/wiki/Double_(baseball)) and [plate appearances](/wiki/Plate_appearance). He ranks among the top in Single Season Doubles and Most Doubles in one game. He also played Mickey Mantle and Connie Mack Baseball National Championship teams in 1988 and 1989 respectively as well as leading Moeller to a state championship in 1989. Bell was also a member of the Moeller boys' basketball team. Bell committed to play baseball at the [University of Kentucky](/wiki/University_of_Kentucky) as a junior.

## Professional career

### Cleveland Indians

After the [Cleveland Indians](/wiki/Cleveland_Indians) selected Bell with their pick in the seventh round (190th overall), Bell decided to forgo his commitment to Kentucky.

Bell made his major league debut on May 3, [1995](/wiki/1995_Major_League_Baseball_season) as a [pinch hitter](/wiki/Pinch_hitter) for [Jim Thome](/wiki/Jim_Thome) and stayed in the game in a defensive replacement. He was optioned to the [Buffalo Bisons](/wiki/Buffalo_Bisons) on May 8, 1995. He hit .272 with eight home runs and 34 RBIs in 70 games.

### St. Louis Cardinals

On July 27, 1995, Bell was traded to the [St. Louis Cardinals](/wiki/St._Louis_Cardinals) along with Pepe McNeal and [Rick Heiserman](/wiki/Rick_Heiserman) for [Ken Hill](/wiki/Ken_Hill_(baseball)).

### Return to Cleveland

Bell with the Phillies in 2004

On April 14, [1998](/wiki/1998_Major_League_Baseball_season), Bell was claimed off waivers by the Indians. On April 15, he hit the first [inside-the-park home run](/wiki/Inside-the-park_home_run) in [Jacobs Field](/wiki/Jacobs_Field) history, and the first for the Indians since [1989](/wiki/1989_Major_League_Baseball_season).

### Seattle Mariners

On August 31, 1998, Bell was traded to the [Seattle Mariners](/wiki/Seattle_Mariners) for [Joey Cora](/wiki/Joey_Cora). Bell was re-signed on December 19, 2001.

### San Francisco Giants

On January 25, 2002, Bell was traded to the [San Francisco Giants](/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) for [Desi Relaford](/wiki/Desi_Relaford) so he could have a chance to play every day. Bell scored the [2002 NLCS](/wiki/2002_National_League_Championship_Series) winning run for the [San Francisco Giants](/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) from second on [Kenny Lofton](/wiki/Kenny_Lofton)'s [single](/wiki/Single_(baseball)). Bell was the runner bearing down on [home plate](/wiki/Home_plate) in Game 5 of the [2002 World Series](/wiki/2002_World_Series) when [J. T. Snow](/wiki/J._T._Snow) lifted 3 year old batboy [Darren Baker](/wiki/Darren_Baker_(baseball)) out of harm's way. Near the end of the season, he won the [2002](/wiki/2002_Major_League_Baseball_season) [Willie Mac Award](/wiki/Willie_Mac_Award) for his spirit and leadership – as voted on by his teammates and coaching staff.

### Philadelphia Phillies

On December 2, 2002, Bell signed a four-year $17 million deal with the [Philadelphia Phillies](/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies). He made Major League history on June 28, [2004](/wiki/2004_Major_League_Baseball_season), by joining his grandfather, [Gus Bell](/wiki/Gus_Bell), as the first grandfather-grandson combination to [hit for the cycle](/wiki/Hitting_for_the_cycle).

### Milwaukee Brewers

Bell was traded from the Philadelphia Phillies to the [Milwaukee Brewers](/wiki/Milwaukee_Brewers) on July 28, [2006](/wiki/2006_Major_League_Baseball_season), in a deal that swapped him for minor league pitcher Wilfrido Laureano. The Brewers chose not to re-sign Bell after the 2006 season, and he became a [free agent](/wiki/Free_agent).

## Coaching career

On October 31, [2008](/wiki/2008_Major_League_Baseball_season), the [Cincinnati Reds](/wiki/Cincinnati_Reds) named Bell the manager for their Double-A affiliate, the [Carolina Mudcats](/wiki/Carolina_Mudcats_(1991%E2%80%932011)). Bell spent three seasons as the Mudcats manager. In November 2011 he was named manager of the Reds' Triple-A affiliate [Louisville Bats](/wiki/Louisville_Bats).

On October 23, [2012](/wiki/2012_Major_League_Baseball_season), the [Chicago Cubs](/wiki/Chicago_Cubs) named Bell the third base coach for the Major League club.

On December 17, 2013, the [St. Louis Cardinals](/wiki/St._Louis_Cardinals) announced hiring Bell as their new assistant hitting coach.

From 2015 through 2017, Bell served as the Cardinals' bench coach. He left the team on October 20, 2017, to become the vice president of player development for the [San Francisco Giants](/wiki/2018_San_Francisco_Giants_season).

## Managing career

### Cincinnati Reds

Bell, as the Reds manager, argues with umpire Jim Reynolds during a game in the 2021 season

On October 21, 2018, the [Cincinnati Reds](/wiki/Cincinnati_Reds) announced Bell had been hired as the 63rd manager in franchise history. The contract spans three years with a club option for a fourth.

In an April 7, 2019, game against the [Pittsburgh Pirates](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates), Bell was ejected after his role in a bench clearing incident involving [Chris Archer](/wiki/Chris_Archer), [Derek Dietrich](/wiki/Derek_Dietrich), [Yasiel Puig](/wiki/Yasiel_Puig), [Amir Garrett](/wiki/Amir_Garrett), [Keone Kela](/wiki/Keone_Kela), and [Felipe Vázquez](/wiki/Felipe_V%C3%A1zquez). This was Bell's first career managerial ejection. Bell received a one-game suspension following the incident on April 9. On July 30, 2019, another bench-clearing mash-up occurred between the Reds and Pirates, with Bell (who had been ejected from the game in the previous half-inning) involved in it. On August 1, 2019, Bell received a 6-game suspension without eligibility to appeal.

On September 22, 2021, Bell agreed to a two-year contract extension with the Reds.

On July 28, 2023, Bell agreed to a contract extension with the Reds through 2026. However, on September 22, 2024, the Reds fired Bell with five games left in the season.

## Front office career

On November 15, 2024, the [Toronto Blue Jays](/wiki/Toronto_Blue_Jays) hired Bell to the concurrent roles of vice president of baseball operations and assistant general manager.

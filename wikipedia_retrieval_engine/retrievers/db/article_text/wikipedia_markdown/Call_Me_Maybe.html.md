"**Call Me Maybe**" is a song by Canadian singer-songwriter [Carly Rae Jepsen](/wiki/Carly_Rae_Jepsen) from her extended play *[Curiosity](/wiki/Curiosity_(EP))* (2012) and later appeared on her second studio album and international debut album *[Kiss](/wiki/Kiss_(Carly_Rae_Jepsen_album))* (2012). The song was written by Jepsen and Tavish Crowe as a [folk](/wiki/Folk_music) song, but its genre was modified to [pop](/wiki/Pop_music) following the production by [Josh Ramsay](/wiki/Josh_Ramsay). It was released as the lead single from the EP on September 20, 2011 in Canada through [604 Records](/wiki/604_Records). In 2012, Jepsen was signed to [Schoolboy Records](/wiki/Schoolboy_Records) and re-released "Call Me Maybe" worldwide through the label, as her debut international single. Musically, "Call Me Maybe" is a [teen pop](/wiki/Teen_pop), [dance-pop](/wiki/Dance-pop) and [bubblegum pop](/wiki/Bubblegum_pop) track that alludes to the inconvenience that [love at first sight](/wiki/Love_at_first_sight) brings to a girl who hopes for a call back from a new crush.

"Call Me Maybe" topped the [Canadian Hot 100](/wiki/Canadian_Hot_100). Outside of Canada, "Call Me Maybe" topped charts in over 15 countries including Australia, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, France, Hungary, Luxembourg, New Zealand, Poland, Ireland, Slovakia, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States. It peaked inside the top five of the charts in Austria, Belgium (Flanders & Wallonia), Germany, Israel, Japan, Netherlands, Norway, and Sweden. After peaking at the top position of the Canadian Hot 100, Jepsen became the fifth Canadian artist to do so in her home country since 2007. In the United States, the track reached number one on the [Mainstream Top 40](/wiki/Pop_Airplay) chart, and is the first number one by a Canadian female artist on the *Billboard* Hot 100 chart since 2007's "[Girlfriend](/wiki/Girlfriend_(Avril_Lavigne_song))" by [Avril Lavigne](/wiki/Avril_Lavigne).

An accompanying music video was directed by Ben Knechtel. In it, Jepsen seeks the attention of an attractive [boy next door](/wiki/Girl_next_door) who is revealed at the end of the story to be attracted to a male band member. As part of the promotion for the song, Jepsen performed the track on *[The Ellen DeGeneres Show](/wiki/The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show)*, where she made her US television debut, and at the [2012 *Billboard* Music Awards](/wiki/2012_Billboard_Music_Awards). "Call Me Maybe" has been covered by several artists, including [Ben Howard](/wiki/Ben_Howard), [Big Time Rush](/wiki/Big_Time_Rush_(group)), [Fun](/wiki/Fun_(band)), [Cimorelli](/wiki/Cimorelli), [JPEGMafia](/wiki/JPEGMafia), and [Cody Simpson](/wiki/Cody_Simpson), and parodied by [Cookie Monster](/wiki/Cookie_Monster) and some of the news staff of [NPR](/wiki/NPR). It was also covered on "[The New Rachel](/wiki/The_New_Rachel)", the season premiere episode of the fourth season of *[Glee](/wiki/Glee_(TV_series))*.

"Call Me Maybe" was nominated for two [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards), for [Song of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year) and [Best Pop Solo Performance](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Pop_Solo_Performance) at the [55th Annual ceremony](/wiki/55th_Annual_Grammy_Awards), but lost to "[We Are Young](/wiki/We_Are_Young)" by Fun and the live performance of "[Set Fire to the Rain](/wiki/Set_Fire_to_the_Rain)" by [Adele](/wiki/Adele), respectively. On December 11, 2012, "Call Me Maybe" was named *Song of the Year* for 2012 by MTV. In its 2012 [Year-End](/wiki/Billboard_Year_End) issue, *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* magazine ranked this song #2 in the [Hot 100 Songs](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100), [Digital Songs](/wiki/Digital_Songs), and Canadian Hot 100 charts. The song was also ranked number one by the Village Voice's annual Pazz and Jop poll, which compiles the votes of music critics from all over the United States.

The song was the best-selling single worldwide in 2012, selling over 12 million copies in that year alone, and the best-selling single on the iTunes Store worldwide in 2012. With worldwide sales of 18 million copies and over 1 [billion](/wiki/Billion) streams on Spotify, it became the best-selling single of the 21st century by a female artist and one of the most successful singles of all time. "Call Me Maybe" was the best-selling digital single of 2012 worldwide, and is the seventh best-selling digital single of all time. The song is ranked at #436 on *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*'s updated list of [The 500 Greatest Songs of All Time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Songs_of_All_Time), and eighth on *Billboard*'s list of the 500 Best Pop Songs of All Time.

## Background

[Marianas Trench](/wiki/Marianas_Trench_(band))'s vocalist [Josh Ramsay](/wiki/Josh_Ramsay) co-wrote "Call Me Maybe".

"Call Me Maybe" was initially written by Jepsen and Tavish Crowe as a [folk](/wiki/Folk_music) song, while they were on tour. Jepsen explained that the writing process was easy, and that she wasn't "over-thinking it. We brought in [Josh [Ramsay]](/wiki/Josh_Ramsay), and he helped us kind of [pop](/wiki/Pop_music)-ify it." The following days, she recorded the track at the Umbrella Studios in [Richmond](/wiki/Richmond,_British_Columbia), [British Columbia](/wiki/British_Columbia), Canada. Jepsen later explained that it is "basically a pick up. What person hasn't wanted to approach somebody before and stopped because it's scary? I know I have." "Call Me Maybe" had been first released in Canada only through [604 Records](/wiki/604_Records) on September 20, 2011.

In December 2011, singers [Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber) and [Selena Gomez](/wiki/Selena_Gomez) were in Canada and heard the track on the radio. After they spoke about the song on their [Twitter](/wiki/Twitter) accounts, Jepsen instantly gained international attention, and got signed by [Scooter Braun](/wiki/Scooter_Braun) to his [Schoolboy Records](/wiki/Schoolboy_Records). Bieber's tweet said the song "is possibly the catchiest song I've ever heard..." Braun revealed that Bieber has "never jumped out and promoted an artist like this before. He sends me different [YouTube](/wiki/YouTube) videos of unsigned artists that he'd like to work with, but never someone who already had a song out and is on the radio." Worldwide distribution of the single was done through [Interscope Records](/wiki/Interscope_Records).

## Composition

"Call Me Maybe" was written by Jepsen and Crowe, with additional writing and song production by Josh Ramsay. Lyrically, the song describes the "infatuation and inconvenience of a love at first sight," as described by Bill Lamb of [About.com](/wiki/Dotdash_Meredith). During the pre-chorus, Jepsen states how she suddenly becomes attracted to a person, singing, "Your stare was holding, ripped jeans, skin was showing/Hot night, wind was blowing/Where you think you're going, baby?" As the [chorus](/wiki/Refrain) begins, the background incorporates [synthesized](/wiki/Synthesizer) [string](/wiki/String_section) chords, and Jepsen explains that her feelings towards the guy are unexpected, "Hey, I just met you/And this is crazy/But here's my number/So call me maybe."

Melody Lau of *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* wrote that "Call Me Maybe" is a "[Taylor Swift](/wiki/Taylor_Swift) meets [Robyn](/wiki/Robyn)" song. Jon O'Brien of [AllMusic](/wiki/AllMusic) called it a [teen pop](/wiki/Teen_pop) song with "a chorus that just about straddles that fine line between sugary sweet and sickly." Tiffany Lee of [Yahoo! Music](/wiki/Yahoo!_Music) deemed it as an instant [summer hit](/wiki/Summer_hit), and added that "Call Me Maybe" has "a good beat, great melody and catchy lyrics; something you and your friends can belt out in the car while driving to the beach, a party, and pretty much anywhere." [Jon Caramanica](/wiki/Jon_Caramanica) of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* wrote that the song is "breezy and sweet, an eyelash-fluttering flirtation run hard through the [Disney](/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company)-[pop](/wiki/Pop_music) model of digitized feelings and brusque, chipper arrangements." [Kelsey McKinney](/wiki/Kelsey_McKinney) and Scott Kellum of *[Vox](/wiki/Vox_(website))* described it as "catchy [bubblegum pop](/wiki/Bubblegum_music)" that is given depth by "the absolute height of Jepsen's vocal range".

According to Musicnotes.com, "Call Me Maybe" is written in the key of [G major](/wiki/G_major) with a moderate [tempo](/wiki/Tempo) of 120 beats per minute (BPM). Jepsen's vocals span from G3 to C5, and the song follows a key progression of Cmaj7–G/D–Cmaj7–G/D.

## Critical reception

The song received critical acclaim. *Rolling Stone* journalist Melody Lau considered "Call Me Maybe" "a sugary [dance-pop](/wiki/Dance-pop) tune about hoping for a call back from a crush," while Kat George of [VH1](/wiki/VH1) described it as a "[guilty pop pleasure](/wiki/Guilty_pleasure)." Emma Carmichael of *[Gawker](/wiki/Gawker)* did a long review on the track, which she described as the "new perfect pop song." Carmichael further added that the song is "flawless" and that "we will be virtually incapable of escaping the song and its strident [disco](/wiki/Disco) strings and that horribly catchy [hook](/wiki/Hook_(music))." Nicole James of [MTV](/wiki/MTV) revealed that "Call Me Maybe" is probably the catchiest song she has ever heard, and added that "I don't even want to tell you what the play count is in my [iTunes](/wiki/ITunes) for that song, but the moment you press play you're sucked in."

*[The Village Voice](/wiki/The_Village_Voice)*'s [Maura Johnston](/wiki/Maura_Johnston) deemed it as an "utterly [earwormy](/wiki/Earworm)" song. *[RedEye](/wiki/RedEye)*'s Emily Van Zandt began her review of the track saying, "screw you, Internet. Thanks to a couple of posts on blogs that I refuse to own up to follow, my afternoon has been dedicated to Carly Rae Jepsen's 'Call Me Maybe'." Van Zandt continued to state that "all I know is that I have co-dependency issues when it comes to my music. When it's sad, I'm sad. When it's angry, I'm angry. And when it's ridiculously over-[produced](/wiki/Production_(music)), [up-tempo](/wiki/Up-tempo) bubblegum pop with terrible lyrics on a beautiful day in [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago) when I'm wearing pink pants, I just kind of want to start skipping around handing my number out to random bros, you know?" Jim Farber of the *[New York Daily News](/wiki/New_York_Daily_News)* said, "In lyrical construction, melodic flourish and instrumental arrangement, 'Maybe' has the urgency and sweep of the greatest teen pop songs ever recorded."

*[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_(website))* named "Call Me Maybe" the 29th best song of 2012, while *Rolling Stone* named it the 50th greatest single of that year. It was voted the best single of 2012 by *The Village Voice*'s 40th annual [Pazz & Jop](/wiki/Pazz_%26_Jop) critics' poll. As of April 2017, *[Billboard](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine))* ranked at number one on list "The Best Chorus of the 21st Century". In September 2021, *Rolling Stone* placed it at number 436 on their [500 Greatest Songs of All Time](/wiki/Rolling_Stone%27s_500_Greatest_Songs_of_All_Time) list. In October 2022, *[Variety](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))*'s Rachel Seo ranked it as Jepsen's 11th best song, writing: "The chronically catchy track evokes memories of 2012, which, among many things, was the year when [Marvel](/wiki/Marvel_Studios)'s *[The Avengers](/wiki/The_Avengers_(2012_film))* debuted in theaters and former [President](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama) was [reelected](/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election). Call it overplayed and outdated, but if there was a song that could be considered most likely to bring about world peace, 'Call Me Maybe' might rank second (after [John Lennon](/wiki/John_Lennon)'s '[Imagine](/wiki/Imagine_(John_Lennon_song))')."

## Commercial performance

### Canada

Jepsen became the sixth Canadian woman to reach the top of the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100).

"Call Me Maybe" achieved commercial success in Canada, and later in the United States and around the world. The song is Jepsen's third single to enter the [Canadian Hot 100](/wiki/Canadian_Hot_100), debuting at number 97 for the week of October 22, 2011. For the week of February 11, 2012, "Call Me Maybe" reached number one in its 17th week on the Canadian Hot 100. By doing so, Jepsen became the fifth Canadian artist ever to reach the top position on the new chart in her home country, after [Avril Lavigne](/wiki/Avril_Lavigne), [Nelly Furtado](/wiki/Nelly_Furtado), [Nikki Yanofsky](/wiki/Nikki_Yanofsky), and [Young Artists for Haiti](/wiki/Young_Artists_for_Haiti). Jepsen commented that she feels "ultimately honored to be mentioned among those names. These are all artists I look up to in a big way. I have their music, they've been on my records since I can remember. It's really hard to believe. It's cool because at the same time, it's all that I've all ever been working for." The song has since been certified Diamond by [Music Canada](/wiki/Music_Canada) (MC), for sales of 800,000 units of the track in the country. It spent a total of 74 weeks on the chart.

### United States of America

In the United States, the song debuted at number 38 on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100) on the week of March 10, 2012 with 80,000 [downloads](/wiki/Music_download). The song entered the top ten on the chart the week April 14, 2012 at number ten The song reached number one on the week ending June 23, 2012 and spent nine consecutive weeks at number one on the *Billboard* Hot 100. Jepsen is the first Canadian female artist to have a number one on *Billboard* Hot 100 since Avril Lavigne's "[Girlfriend](/wiki/Girlfriend_(Avril_Lavigne_song))", which reached number one the week of May 5, 2007, making her the first Canadian artist to have a number one in the [2010s](/wiki/2010s_in_music). The song is only the fourth song to log seven weeks at number one by an artist from Canada, tied with "[Sh-Boom](/wiki/Sh-Boom)" by [the Crew-Cuts](/wiki/The_Crew-Cuts), "[Informer](/wiki/Informer_(song))" by [Snow](/wiki/Snow_(musician)) featuring [MC Shan](/wiki/MC_Shan), and "[(Everything I Do) I Do It for You](/wiki/(Everything_I_Do)_I_Do_It_for_You)" by [Bryan Adams](/wiki/Bryan_Adams). Three weeks earlier, it had reached number one on the [Digital Songs](/wiki/Hot_Digital_Songs) component chart. The track also reached number one on the [Mainstream Top 40](/wiki/Mainstream_Top_40) chart. The song is Jepsen's first entry on the [*Billboard* charts](/wiki/Billboard_charts) in the country, which made her the first lead woman since [Kesha](/wiki/Kesha) with her single "[Tik Tok](/wiki/Tik_Tok_(song))" to have her debut single peak at such position. "Call Me Maybe" holds the record for the longest run at number one on the US Hot 100 among female Canadian artists and tied with [Percy Faith](/wiki/Percy_Faith)'s 1960 song, "Theme from a Summer Place", for the longest among all Canadian artists. "Call Me Maybe" was certified Diamond by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA), becoming third female artist to achieve with her own single and first Canadian female artist to achieve, and as of August 2015, it has sold 7.6 million copies in the US, making it the country's twelfth all-time best-selling digital single.

### Other countries

"Call Me Maybe" debuted at number 39 in Australia on the chart issue dated March 18, 2012, and four weeks later rose to number one. It remained at the top for five consecutive weeks, before falling to number two. The song has since been certified nine-times Platinum by the [Australian Recording Industry Association](/wiki/Australian_Recording_Industry_Association) (ARIA), denoting shipments of 630,000 copies. The song also made its debut on the [country's chart](/wiki/New_Zealand_Singles_Chart) for the week of March 5, 2012 at number 22, reaching the top position four weeks later. It remained at the top for five consecutive weeks, before falling to number two. By August 2012, "Call Me Maybe" had been certified three-times Platinum by the [Recording Industry Association of New Zealand](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_New_Zealand) (RIANZ), surpassing digital sales of 45,000 units. "Call Me Maybe" became New Zealand's best selling single in 2012, placed at the top spot by the Recording Industry Association of New Zealand.

"Call Me Maybe" performed well in Europe also, topping the charts in France, Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, Hungary, Luxembourg, Poland, Slovakia, Switzerland and the United Kingdom. The song debuted in the Republic of Ireland on March 15, 2012. The song went to number one the following week, March 22, 2012, and stayed consecutively at number one for four weeks. while attaining top three positions in Austria, Belgium ([Flanders](/wiki/Flanders) and [Wallonia](/wiki/Wallonia)), Germany, Netherlands, Norway, and Sweden. In the United Kingdom, the song debuted at number one on the [UK Singles Chart](/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart) on April 8, 2012 – for the week ending date April 14, 2012 – selling nearly 107,000 copies in the first week and remaining at the top for four weeks. The song became the third fastest-selling song of 2012 on the UK Singles Chart, behind [DJ Fresh](/wiki/DJ_Fresh) and [Rita Ora](/wiki/Rita_Ora)'s track "[Hot Right Now](/wiki/Hot_Right_Now)" and [Cheryl](/wiki/Cheryl_(singer))'s "[Call My Name](/wiki/Call_My_Name_(Cheryl_song))". "Call Me Maybe" remained at number one for a second week on April 15, 2012 – for the week ending date April 21, 2012 – keeping Justin Bieber's "[Boyfriend](/wiki/Boyfriend_(Justin_Bieber_song))" from reaching such position after outselling it by two to one. In its fourth week on the chart, the song stayed at number one with 99,569 copies sold after three consecutive weeks selling over 100,000 copies. The song became Britain's second best-selling single of 2012, with 1,143,000 copies sold. As of April 2017, "Call Me Maybe" has sold 1.35 million copies in the United Kingdom, making it the 11th biggest-selling song by a woman on the UK Singles Chart.

## Music video

### Background and release

The music video for "Call Me Maybe" was written and directed by Ben Knechtel and filmed at 19841 48a Ave, [Langley](/wiki/Langley,_British_Columbia_(city)), British Columbia on October 30, 2011. According to Knechtel, the main idea behind the concept was to have a "twist at the end", trying to get away from the idea of the classic "boy meets a girl" story. The music video was released on December 9, 2011, before being re-uploaded on March 1, 2012. In a 2018 interview with [iHeartRadio Canada](/wiki/Bell_Media_Radio), Holden Nowell said he had regrets about his participation in the video, claiming that he was paid [$](/wiki/Canadian_dollar)500 with a promise of additional [residuals](/wiki/Residual_(entertainment_industry)), but never received "a single penny in royalties". He also expressed misgivings about his role, saying, "I didn't like being known as the gay guy in the 'Call Me Maybe' video. It was just something I wasn't used to". He added that it was initially planned for the character's sexuality to be revealed when he kissed the guitarist, but he objected to this: "I was like, 'I'm going to be completely honest with you. I'm not going to kiss a guy, especially for $500.' I said, "I really don't think I'm comfortable kissing a guy for a music video". Nowell also claimed that he came up with actual ending for the video stating "You know what? What if instead of me kissing a guy at the end of the video, what if I just give a guy my number or something like that?"

### Synopsis and reception

The video begins with Jepsen spying on her attractive tattooed neighbour (Holden Nowell) as he is mowing his lawn. As he takes his shirt off and notices she is staring at him, Jepsen is embarrassed and falls below her window, out of sight. She is reading the books *Love at First Sight (Men in Uniform)* by [B. J. Daniels](/wiki/B._J._Daniels_(writer)) and *Skylar's Outlaw* by [Linda Warren](/wiki/Linda_Warren). The scene then cuts to her garage, where she is rehearsing the track with her band. Following the rehearsals, her bandmates push her to go and wash her vehicle, a [Ford Mustang](/wiki/Ford_Mustang_(first_generation)#1964½–1966), where she tries to divert her neighbor's attention from working on his vehicle, a [Chevrolet Camaro](/wiki/Chevrolet_Camaro_(second_generation)#1978) with forming various provocative poses only to fall off the vehicle hood. She is briefly knocked out from the fall, during which she dreams of a [romance novel](/wiki/Romance_novel)-type encounter with her crush against the backdrop of [Peggy's Cove](/wiki/Peggy%27s_Cove,_Nova_Scotia). As she comes to, the neighbor then helps her get up and watches the band rehearse the track again. After turning and writing down her telephone number, Jepsen sees her neighbor pass one of her male bandmates (Tavish Crowe) his own number, indicating he is [gay](/wiki/Gay), where the very end shows that Jepsen is taken aback by this. The video received three nominations on the 2012 [MuchMusic Video Awards](/wiki/IHeartRadio_MMVAs) in the categories of UR Fave Video, Pop Video of the Year, and Video of the Year.

## Cover versions and parodies

### Lip dubs

[Justin Bieber](/wiki/Justin_Bieber) and [Katy Perry](/wiki/Katy_Perry) were two of several celebrities who made a video lip syncing to "Call Me Maybe".

A number of parody and [lip dub](/wiki/Lip_dub) videos have been released throughout the internet since the song's release. [Big Time Rush](/wiki/Big_Time_Rush_(group)), [Ashley Tisdale](/wiki/Ashley_Tisdale), Justin Bieber, and Selena Gomez uploaded a parody video to YouTube on February 18, 2012; it instantly turned [viral](/wiki/Viral_video), having over 75 million views as of August 2019. Pop singer [Katy Perry](/wiki/Katy_Perry) also released a viral video with her friends on April 19, 2012, while hosts and members of the [E!](/wiki/E!) TV series *[Fashion Police](/wiki/Fashion_Police)* also released theirs on May 4, 2012. On May 23, 2012, a compilation from several fan videos was uploaded to Jepsen's [Vevo](/wiki/Vevo) page. The [Harvard University](/wiki/Harvard_University) [baseball team](/wiki/Harvard_Crimson_baseball#Baseball) uploaded a [lip-sync](/wiki/Lip_sync) video to the song on YouTube on May 6, 2012, which it had recorded on the way to a game over [spring break](/wiki/Spring_break). As of August 2019, it had been viewed over 19 million times. The [Miami Dolphins Cheerleaders](/wiki/Miami_Dolphins_Cheerleaders) have also made a cover of the song that has garnered over 25 million views as of August 2019. On July 11, 2012, [Crystal Palace F.C.](/wiki/Crystal_Palace_F.C.) released a cover version, in which the Crystal Palace cheerleaders squad, "the Crystals", sang and danced along to "Call Me Maybe". A promotional campaign to encourage the sale of [season](/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Crystal_Palace_F.C._season)'s tickets at the club, it was dubbed "Call Me Crystals".

In July 2012, members of the [United States Armed Forces](/wiki/United_States_Armed_Forces) stationed in [Kandahar International Airport](/wiki/Kandahar_International_Airport), Afghanistan had released another lip dub video with the idea to show troops in a more positive, light-hearted way. The *[Baracksdubs](/wiki/Baracksdubs)* YouTube channel used [Auto-Tune](/wiki/Auto-Tune) to produce a [satire](/wiki/Satire) version from clips of [Barack Obama](/wiki/Barack_Obama). [New York Mets](/wiki/New_York_Mets) infielder [Justin Turner](/wiki/Justin_Turner) has also used the song as his at-bat music at home games. On July 3, 2012, Mabson Enterprises released a digital-only compilation on [Bandcamp](/wiki/Bandcamp) comprising 43 versions of the track remixed or covered including tracks by [Dan Deacon](/wiki/Dan_Deacon), Ear Pwr, Poingly and [Sean Carnage](/wiki/Sean_Carnage). On July 20, 2012, the cast of *[Hollywood Heights](/wiki/Hollywood_Heights_(TV_series))*, [Cody Longo](/wiki/Cody_Longo), [Brittany Underwood](/wiki/Brittany_Underwood), [Carlos Ponce](/wiki/Carlos_Ponce), [Melissa Ordway](/wiki/Melissa_Ordway), [Jama Williamson](/wiki/Jama_Williamson), [Meredith Salenger](/wiki/Meredith_Salenger), among others, made a cover version of them lip singing and dancing along to the song on set of the show. On July 26, 2012, the [United States Olympic swimming team](/wiki/United_States_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics#Swimming) posted a video of them lip syncing to the song at practice and on the way to London for the [Olympics](/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics). On July 30, 2012, a mashup video featuring [NASA](/wiki/NASA) videos of the [Mars Science Laboratory](/wiki/Mars_Science_Laboratory) was posted on YouTube, just a week before the [Curiosity rover](/wiki/Curiosity_(rover)) landed on [Mars](/wiki/Mars). The video was then updated after the landing and played for the Mars Curiosity Rover team at the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) as the wake up video for Sol 18 on August 24, 2012. The cast of *[The Big Bang Theory](/wiki/The_Big_Bang_Theory)* made a [flashmob](/wiki/Flashmob) of the song on October 23, 2012, during the live taping of an episode.

### Cover versions, mashups, and remixes

A number of [covers](/wiki/Cover_version) also emerged since the song's release. On March 24, 2012, [Cimorelli](/wiki/Cimorelli) performed a dance routine version of the song. The group returned in a sequel titled "Don't Call Me Baby", this time featuring [MattyBRaps](/wiki/MattyBRaps) on May 9. On June 7, 2012, producer Chi Duly released "Call Me Calvin (Chi Duly Edit)", a [mashup](/wiki/Mashup_(music)) which replaced the original backing of "Call Me Maybe" with [Calvin Harris](/wiki/Calvin_Harris)' singles "[I'm Not Alone](/wiki/I%27m_Not_Alone)", "[Feel So Close](/wiki/Feel_So_Close)", and "[We Found Love](/wiki/We_Found_Love)".

On July 10, 2012, *[Sesame Street](/wiki/Sesame_Street)* released a parody of the song, called "Share it Maybe", which features "[Cookie Monster](/wiki/Cookie_Monster)-ified lyrics". A cover of the song released by Carly Rae Jepsen Tribute Team peaked at number 49 on the [UK Singles Chart](/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart) for one week and another version by [Hit Masters](/wiki/Hit_Masters) spent the same amount of time on the chart but peaked 23 places lower, at number 72. American [indie pop](/wiki/Indie_pop) band [Fun](/wiki/Fun_(band)) covered "Call Me Maybe" in an acoustic form at an in-studio session for Dutch radio [3FM](/wiki/3FM). On May 8, 2012, folk artist [Ben Howard](/wiki/Ben_Howard) covered the song for [BBC Radio](/wiki/BBC_Radio). Renditions from other notable people include [James Franco](/wiki/James_Franco) and [Colin Powell](/wiki/Colin_Powell). On July 30, 2012, [video game developer](/wiki/Video_game_developer) [HeR Interactive](/wiki/HeR_Interactive), well known for the [*Nancy Drew* computer games](/wiki/Nancy_Drew_Computer_Games), added a new video parody of the song about [Nancy Drew](/wiki/Nancy_Drew), titled "Call Me Nancy, Second Chance Me", a reference to the "Second Chance" feature in its games.

On the [fourth season](/wiki/Glee_(season_4)) of *Glee* premiere episode "[The New Rachel](/wiki/The_New_Rachel)", they covered "Call Me Maybe" as a way to decide who the "new [Rachel](/wiki/Rachel_Berry)" will be. During the [Australian fourth season](/wiki/The_X_Factor_(Australian_season_4)) of [*The X Factor*](/wiki/The_X_Factor_(Australian_TV_series)), the finalists recorded and performed a cover of "Call Me Maybe" as a [charity single](/wiki/Charity_record) in aid of Sony Foundation's You Can program. [Girls Aloud](/wiki/Girls_Aloud) covered "Call Me Maybe" live during their [Ten: The Hits Tour](/wiki/Ten:_The_Hits_Tour). [Los Angeles](/wiki/Los_Angeles) [comedy punk](/wiki/Comedy_punk) band [Radioactive Chicken Heads](/wiki/Radioactive_Chicken_Heads) recorded a [punk rock](/wiki/Punk_rock) version of the song in November 2012, also releasing a music video concurrently with their punk rock cover of Taylor Swift's "[We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together](/wiki/We_Are_Never_Ever_Getting_Back_Together)". During the first episode of the [eighth season](/wiki/America%27s_Got_Talent_(season_8)) of *[America's Got Talent](/wiki/America%27s_Got_Talent)*, aired on June 4, 2013, the 3 Penny Chorus and Orchestra directed by Arianne Abela did a cover of "Call Me Maybe", arranged by Colin Britt and Arianne Abela. ["Weird Al" Yankovic](/wiki/%22Weird_Al%22_Yankovic) briefly covered the song as part of his [polka](/wiki/Polka) [medley](/wiki/Medley_(music)) "[NOW That's What I Call Polka!](/wiki/List_of_%22Weird_Al%22_Yankovic_polka_medleys#"NOW_That's_What_I_Call_Polka!")" for his 2014 album *[Mandatory Fun](/wiki/Mandatory_Fun)*. For the thirtieth anniversary of [MathWorks](/wiki/MathWorks) in 2014, a group of employees created a flashmob-style parody version called "Call Me Nerdy". [Baltimore](/wiki/Baltimore) rapper [JPEGMafia](/wiki/JPEGMafia) released his own cover for the song as well. On September 25, 2020, a short for the [Disney Channel](/wiki/Disney_Channel) show *[Gravity Falls](/wiki/Gravity_Falls)* was released. In that short, one of the main characters, [Mabel](/wiki/Mabel_Pines), sings "Call Me Mabel", a parody of the song.

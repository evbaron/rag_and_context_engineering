**Hannah Einbinder** (born May 21, 1995) is an American actress and [stand-up comedian](/wiki/Stand-up_comedian). She is best known for her starring role as struggling comedy writer Ava Daniels in the [HBO Max](/wiki/Max_(streaming_service)) [dramedy](/wiki/Comedy-drama) series *[Hacks](/wiki/Hacks)*, for which she received a [Primetime Emmy Award](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) and a [Critics' Choice Television Award](/wiki/Critics%27_Choice_Television_Award), as well as four [Golden Globe Award](/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) nominations.

## Early life and education

Einbinder is the daughter of original *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* cast member [Laraine Newman](/wiki/Laraine_Newman) and writer/director Chad Einbinder. She grew up in Los Angeles in a [Reform Jewish](/wiki/Reform_Jewish) family.

As a teenager, Einbinder was a competitive cheerleader. She graduated from [Beverly Hills High School](/wiki/Beverly_Hills_High_School) and went on to study broadcast journalism at [Chapman University](/wiki/Chapman_University) before earning a Bachelor of Fine Arts degree in television writing and production. At Chapman, Einbinder joined the school's improv team and had her first experience with stand-up comedy when she opened for [Nicole Byer](/wiki/Nicole_Byer) for a campus show.

## Career

In 2019, Einbinder appeared in the [Just for Laughs](/wiki/Just_for_Laughs) festival's New Faces showcase and was named by [National Public Radio](/wiki/National_Public_Radio) as one of the 10 standout comedians to watch. She was also named one of [*Vulture*](/wiki/Vulture_(website))'s best new up-and-coming comedians to watch in 2019; she was recognized for "her refreshingly absurdist charm." The same year, she was among the "New Faces of Comedy" at Just For Laughs.

In March 2020, she made her national television debut on *[The Late Show with Stephen Colbert](/wiki/The_Late_Show_with_Stephen_Colbert)* and at the time was the youngest person to do a stand-up set on the show. It was the show's last on-stage stand-up performance for fifteen months, as the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic) began soon after the episode aired.

Starting in 2021, she co-starred as Ava Daniels in *[Hacks](/wiki/Hacks)* on [HBO Max](/wiki/HBO_Max), together with [Jean Smart](/wiki/Jean_Smart) and [Carl Clemons-Hopkins](/wiki/Carl_Clemons-Hopkins). The show received a total of 15 [Emmy](/wiki/Emmy) nominations, including acting nominations for Einbinder, Smart, and Clemons-Hopkins.

She was on [*Variety*](/wiki/Variety_(magazine))'s List of "10 Comics to Watch for 2021". She continued to perform stand-up comedy and toured nationally during the summer of 2022. [Max](/wiki/Max_(streaming_service)) released her debut stand-up special in June 2024, *Hannah Einbinder: Everything Must Go.*

In May 2025, it was announced that she would star alongside [Gillian Anderson](/wiki/Gillian_Anderson) in [Jane Schoenbrun](/wiki/Jane_Schoenbrun)'s upcoming [slasher film](/wiki/Slasher_film), *[Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma](/wiki/Teenage_Sex_and_Death_at_Camp_Miasma)*. Einbinder is set to play a lead role in the [Mubi](/wiki/Mubi_(streaming_service)) and [Plan B](/wiki/Plan_B_Entertainment) production.

In September 2025, she won her first [Emmy](/wiki/Emmy_Awards) for [Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series) for her role in *Hacks* Season 4.

## Influences

Einbinder has cited [Dana Gould](/wiki/Dana_Gould), [Janeane Garofalo](/wiki/Janeane_Garofalo), [Bo Burnham](/wiki/Bo_Burnham), [Maria Bamford](/wiki/Maria_Bamford), [Sklar Brothers](/wiki/Sklar_Brothers), [Marc Maron](/wiki/Marc_Maron), and [Steve Martin](/wiki/Steve_Martin) as being among her inspirations and influences.

## Personal life

Einbinder resides in Los Angeles, California. Einbinder is bisexual. She has [ADHD](/wiki/ADHD).

She is a longtime supporter of the [Philadelphia Eagles](/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles).

## Political views

In March 2025, Einbinder spoke about several issues while accepting the [HRC Visibility Award](/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign). In her speech, she voiced her concerns about [climate change](/wiki/Climate_change), condemning the "architects of humanity's slow extinction and the destruction of planet Earth." She also denounced the detention of [pro-Palestinian activist Mahmoud Khalil](/wiki/Detention_of_Mahmoud_Khalil), [the Israeli government's actions](/wiki/Israeli_war_crimes_in_the_Gaza_war) in the [Gaza war](/wiki/Gaza_war), and [the United States government's support for Israel](/wiki/United_States_support_for_Israel_in_the_Gaza_war).

In May 2025, Einbinder signed on to a letter criticizing the film industry's "passivity" during the [Gaza genocide](/wiki/Gaza_genocide). In September 2025, she signed the [Film Workers for Palestine](/wiki/Film_Workers_for_Palestine) pledge, a campaign in which film professionals commit not to work with Israeli film institutions implicated in genocide and [apartheid](/wiki/Israeli_apartheid) against Palestinians.

At the [Emmy Awards](/wiki/77th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards) in September 2025, after winning the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series](/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actress_in_a_Comedy_Series), Einbinder concluded her acceptance speech with the words, "Go [Birds](/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles), fuck [ICE](/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement), and free Palestine!" Afterwards Einbinder said she had made the speech because she felt she had an "obligation as a Jewish person to distinguish Jews from the State of Israel, because our religion and our culture is such an important and long-standing institution that is really separate to this sort of [ethno-nationalist](/wiki/Ethno-nationalist) state". The speech was criticized by some [pro-Israel](/wiki/Pro-Israel) figures, including [Creative Community for Peace](/wiki/Creative_Community_for_Peace), actor [Yuval David](/wiki/Yuval_David), and comedian [Elon Gold](/wiki/Elon_Gold). Other Jewish commentators have opined that the speech reflects an overall "decoupling from Israel" among young Jewish Americans.

In October 2025, Einbinder was interviewed for [Zeteo](/wiki/Zeteo) by [Simone Zimmerman](/wiki/Simone_Zimmerman), one of the subjects in the documentary *[Israelism](/wiki/Israelism_(film))*. She also signed a pledge to boycott *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* over its alleged "anti-Palestinian Bias" in its [coverage of the Gaza war](/wiki/List_of_The_New_York_Times_controversies#Gaza_war_coverage).

In January 2026, after the [killing of Renée Good](/wiki/Killing_of_Ren%C3%A9e_Good) in [Minneapolis](/wiki/Minneapolis), Einbinder posted an [Instagram](/wiki/Instagram) comment calling on artists and creatives to use their platforms to condemn [ICE](/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement). In the same post Einbender wrote, "The imperial boomerang has come back home. What we FUND and allow to happen to Palestinians by the hands of the [IDF](/wiki/Israel_Defense_Forces) who TRAIN ICE will happen to our people here in America".

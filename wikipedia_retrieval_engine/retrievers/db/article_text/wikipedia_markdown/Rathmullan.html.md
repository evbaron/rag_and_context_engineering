**Rathmullan** ([Irish](/wiki/Irish_language): *Ráth Maoláin*, meaning 'Maolán's ringfort') is a seaside village and [townland](/wiki/Townland) on the [Fanad](/wiki/Fanad) Peninsula in [County Donegal](/wiki/County_Donegal), [Ireland](/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland). It is situated on the western shore of [Lough Swilly](/wiki/Lough_Swilly), 11 kilometres (7 mi) north-east of [Ramelton](/wiki/Ramelton) and 12 km (7 mi) east of [Milford](/wiki/Milford,_County_Donegal). Rathmullan was the point of departure during the [Flight of the Earls](/wiki/Flight_of_the_Earls) in 1607, a major turning point in Irish history.

## History

Evidence of ancient settlement in the area includes [court tomb](/wiki/Court_tomb) and [ringfort](/wiki/Ringfort) sites in the neighbouring townlands of Crevary Upper and Rathmullan and Ballyboe.

Within Rathmullan village is a ruined [Carmelite](/wiki/Carmelite) [friary](/wiki/Friary), dating to 1516, which was built by Eoghan Rua MacSweeney. The friary was sacked by the English garrison from [Sligo](/wiki/Sligo) in 1595. In 1617, the friary was occupied by the Protestant [Bishop of Raphoe](/wiki/Bishop_of_Raphoe), [Andrew Knox](/wiki/Andrew_Knox_(bishop)). A subsequent Bishop of Raphoe turned it into a fortified house in anticipation of a possible French invasion during the [Napoleonic Wars](/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars).

On 14 September 1607, 99 aristocrats of the [Gaelic Order](/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland), including of [Clan Ó Néill](/wiki/O%27Neill_dynasty) and [Clan Ó Domhnaill](/wiki/O%27Donnell_Clan), left Rathmullan for the European continent, an episode known as the [Flight of the Earls](/wiki/Flight_of_the_Earls). On 14 September 2007, president [Mary McAleese](/wiki/Mary_McAleese) visited the village to mark the 400th anniversary of the event. She unveiled a sculpture by [John Behan](/wiki/John_Behan_(sculptor)) that represents the plight of the men who were led by the Gaelic aristocrats.

There are the remains of a [Martello tower](/wiki/Martello_tower) or battery in the village which serves as a heritage centre. The fortification was one of six originally built c. 1813 by the British as part of a defence along [Lough Swilly](/wiki/Lough_Swilly) against feared Napoleonic invasion. These batteries were manned up until the end of the [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I) to protect British warships that were moored in Lough Swilly.

## Amenities

There are three churches in Rathmullan: St. Joseph's Catholic Church, St. Columb's [Church of Ireland](/wiki/Church_of_Ireland) (Parish of Killygarvan), and the Rathmullan Presbyterian Church.

Other facilities in Rathmullan include shops, a resource centre, a spa, a wedding venue (Drumhalla House), and a hotel.

## Events

The annual *Lough Swilly Deep Sea Fishing Festival* is held locally in June. The 2007 festival took place on Saturday 2 June and Sunday 3 June.

## Literature

Rathmullan is the setting for Australian/British author Brand King's novel *An Irish Winter*, published in 2020. A number of features of the village are described in the novel, including the local beach. The traditional New Year's Day cold water swim also features, while a number of scenes are set in the Beachcomber Bar and An Bonnan Bui Cafe.

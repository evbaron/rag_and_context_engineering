**Mary Margaret Truman Daniel** (February 17, 1924 – January 29, 2008) was an American classical [soprano](/wiki/Soprano), actress, journalist, radio and television personality, writer, and New York socialite. She was the only child of President [Harry S. Truman](/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) and First Lady [Bess Truman](/wiki/Bess_Truman). While her father was president during the years 1945 to 1953, Margaret regularly accompanied him on campaign trips, such as the 1948 countrywide [whistle-stop](/wiki/Whistle-stop) campaign lasting several weeks. She also appeared at important White House and political events during those years and was a favorite with the media.

After graduating from [George Washington University](/wiki/George_Washington_University) in 1946, Truman embarked on a career as a [coloratura soprano](/wiki/Coloratura_soprano), beginning with a concert appearance with the [Detroit Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/Detroit_Symphony_Orchestra) in 1947. She appeared in the US in concerts with orchestras and in recitals through 1956. She made recordings for [RCA Victor](/wiki/RCA_Victor_Red_Seal) and made television appearances on programs such as *[What's My Line?](/wiki/What%27s_My_Line%3F)* and *[The Bell Telephone Hour](/wiki/The_Bell_Telephone_Hour)*.

In 1957, one year after her marriage, Truman abandoned her singing career to pursue a career as a journalist and radio personality when she became the cohost of the program *Weekday* with [Mike Wallace](/wiki/Mike_Wallace). She also wrote articles as an independent journalist for a variety of publications in the 1960s and 1970s. Truman later became the successful author of a series of murder mysteries as well as a number of works on first ladies and their families, including well-received biographies of her father.

Truman was married to journalist [Clifton Daniel](/wiki/Clifton_Daniel), managing editor of *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*. The couple had four sons and lived in a [Park Avenue](/wiki/Park_Avenue) apartment.

## Early life

Mary Margaret Truman was born at 219 North Delaware Street in [Independence, Missouri](/wiki/Independence,_Missouri), on February 17, 1924 and was named for her aunt Mary Jane Truman and maternal grandmother Margaret Gates Wallace, but she was called Margaret from early childhood. She took voice and piano lessons as a child (at the encouragement of her father, who famously played piano) and attended public school in Independence until her father's [1934 election](/wiki/1934_United_States_Senate_election_in_Missouri) to the [United States Senate](/wiki/United_States_Senate), after which her education was split between public schools in Independence and [Gunston Hall School](/wiki/Gunston_Hall_School), a private school for girls in Washington, D.C.

In 1942, Truman matriculated at [George Washington University](/wiki/George_Washington_University), where she was a member of [Pi Beta Phi](/wiki/Pi_Beta_Phi), and earned a [Bachelor of Arts](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in [history](/wiki/History) and [international relations](/wiki/International_relations) in 1946. In June 1944, she christened the battleship [USS *Missouri*](/wiki/USS_Missouri_(BB-63)) at [Brooklyn Navy Yard](/wiki/Brooklyn_Navy_Yard), and spoke again in 1986 at the ship's recommissioning. She studied singing with [Estelle Liebling](/wiki/Estelle_Liebling), the voice teacher of [Beverly Sills](/wiki/Beverly_Sills), in New York City.

On April 12, 1945, President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) died and his vice president Harry Truman assumed the presidency when Margaret was 21.

## Career

### Singing

Truman with her mother in Washington, D.C. in 1948

Truman portrait by [Greta Kempton](/wiki/Greta_Kempton), c. 1947

When Truman was 16 years old, she began taking voice lessons in Independence from Mrs. Thomas J. Strickler, a family friend. After classical vocal training, Truman's singing career began with a debut radio recital in March 1947, followed shortly thereafter with her professional concert debut with the [Detroit Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/Detroit_Symphony_Orchestra). She sang professionally for the next decade, appearing with major American orchestras and embarking on several national concert tours. Some of her credits include concert appearances with the [Los Angeles Philharmonic](/wiki/Los_Angeles_Philharmonic) at the [Hollywood Bowl](/wiki/Hollywood_Bowl), the [National Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/National_Symphony_Orchestra), the [NBC Symphony Orchestra](/wiki/NBC_Symphony_Orchestra), the [Pittsburgh Symphony](/wiki/Pittsburgh_Symphony), the [Philadelphia Orchestra](/wiki/Philadelphia_Orchestra), and the [Saint Louis Symphony](/wiki/Saint_Louis_Symphony). While she never performed in staged operas, she did perform opera arias in concert. Her performances were mainly of both sacred and secular art songs, *[Lieder](/wiki/Lieder)*, and works from the concert soprano repertoire. In 1951 and 1952, [RCA Victor](/wiki/RCA_Victor_Red_Seal) issued two albums by Truman, one of classical selections, the other of American art songs. She also made recordings of German *Lieder* for [NBC](/wiki/NBC). A 1951 *[Time Magazine](/wiki/Time_Magazine)* cover featured Truman with a single musical note floating by her head. She performed on stage, radio, and television through 1956.

At the beginning of her career, critical reviews of Truman's singing were positive, polite or diplomatic in tone, with some later reviewers speculating that negative opinions were withheld from deference to her father as a current president. This practice was broken in 1950 when *[Washington Post](/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* music critic [Paul Hume](/wiki/Paul_Hume_(music_critic)) wrote that Truman was "extremely attractive on the stage... [but] cannot sing very well. She is flat a good deal of the time. And still cannot sing with anything approaching professional finish." The review angered President Truman (who was dealing that same day with the sudden death of his childhood friend and White House press secretary [Charlie Ross](/wiki/Charlie_Ross_(journalist))), who wrote to Hume, "Some day I hope to meet you. When that happens you'll need a new nose, a lot of beefsteak for black eyes, and perhaps a supporter below!" Hume wanted to publish the letter, but *Washington Post* publisher [Philip Graham](/wiki/Philip_Graham) vetoed the idea. However, Hume showed the letter to a number of his colleagues, including Milton Berliner, music critic of the rival *Washington Times Herald*, which published a story. The *Washington* *Post* was then forced to acknowledge the letter, which drew international headlines, becoming a minor scandal for the Truman administration. Subsequent reviewers felt freer to be honest in their reviews of her performances, with mixed criticism for her singing thereafter.

### Acting, radio, and journalism

Truman's professional acting debut occurred on April 26, 1951 when she costarred with [James Stewart](/wiki/James_Stewart) in the "Jackpot" episode of *[Screen Directors Playhouse](/wiki/Screen_Directors_Playhouse)* on [NBC](/wiki/NBC) radio. On March 17, 1952, Truman was a guest soloist on *[The Railroad Hour](/wiki/The_Railroad_Hour)* in a presentation of the operetta *[Sari](/wiki/Der_Zigeunerprimas)*.

Truman also performed on the NBC Radio program *[The Big Show](/wiki/The_Big_Show_(NBC_Radio))* where she met writer [Goodman Ace](/wiki/Goodman_Ace), who offered her advice and pointers. Ace became a lifelong friend, advising Truman even after *The Big Show*. Truman became part of the team of [NBC Radio](/wiki/NBC_Radio)'s *Weekday* show that premiered in 1955, shortly after its *[Monitor](/wiki/Monitor_(NBC_Radio))* program made its debut. Paired with [Mike Wallace](/wiki/Mike_Wallace), she presented news and interviews aimed at a female listening audience.

She appeared several times as a panelist (and twice as a mystery guest) on the game show *[What's My Line?](/wiki/What%27s_My_Line%3F)* and guest-starred multiple times on [NBC](/wiki/NBC)'s *The [Martha Raye](/wiki/Martha_Raye) Show*.

In 1957, Truman sang and played piano on *[The Gisele MacKenzie Show](/wiki/The_Gisele_MacKenzie_Show).*

### Writing

Truman's full-length biography of her father, published shortly before his 1972 death, was critically acclaimed. She also wrote a personal biography of her mother and histories of the [White House](/wiki/White_House) and its inhabitants (including [first ladies](/wiki/First_Lady_of_the_United_States) and [pets](/wiki/United_States_presidential_pets)). Truman published regularly into her eighties.

#### Novels

From 1980 to 2011, 25 books in the Capital Crimes series of murder mysteries, most set in and around Washington, D.C., were published under Margaret Truman's name.

Professional [ghostwriter](/wiki/Ghostwriter) [Donald Bain](/wiki/Donald_Bain_(writer)) (1935–2017) acknowledged in the March 14, 2014, issue of *[Publishers Weekly](/wiki/Publishers_Weekly)* that he had written "27 novels in the Margaret Truman Capital Crimes series (mostly bylined by Truman, my close collaborator – my name is on only the most recent entries, released after her death)."

In 2000, another ghostwriter, William Harrington, had claimed in a self-written obituary before his apparent suicide that Margaret Truman and others were his clients.

### Institutions

She served on the board of directors for the [Harry S. Truman Presidential Library and Museum](/wiki/Harry_S._Truman_Presidential_Library_and_Museum) and the Board of Governors of the [Roosevelt Institute](/wiki/Roosevelt_Institute), and served as a Trustee for her alma mater.

## Personal life

On April 21, 1956, Truman married [Clifton Daniel](/wiki/Clifton_Daniel), a reporter for *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* and later its managing editor, at [Trinity Episcopal Church](/wiki/Trinity_Episcopal_Church_(Independence,_Missouri)) in Independence; he died in 2000. They had four sons:

- [Clifton Truman Daniel](/wiki/Clifton_Truman_Daniel) (born June 5, 1957), Director of Public Relations for [Harry S Truman College](/wiki/Harry_S_Truman_College).
- William Wallace Daniel (May 19, 1960 – September 4, 2000), a psychiatric social worker and researcher at [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University). He died after being struck by a taxicab in New York City.
- Harrison Gates Daniel (born 1963)
- Thomas Washington Daniel (born 1966)

## Popular culture

Italian dress designer [Micol Fontana](/wiki/Micol_Fontana), who designed Truman’s wedding gown, was invited to be a surprise guest on the TV show [What's My Line?](/wiki/What%27s_My_Line%3F) in New York City, just six days before the Truman/Daniel wedding on April 21, 1956, in Independence, Missouri.

## Later years and death

In later life, Truman lived in her [Park Avenue](/wiki/Park_Avenue) home. She died on January 29, 2008, in Chicago (to which she was relocating to be closer to her son Clifton). She was said to have been suffering from "a simple infection" and had been breathing with the assistance of a respirator. Her ashes and those of her husband are interred in Independence in her parents' burial plot on the grounds of the Truman Library.

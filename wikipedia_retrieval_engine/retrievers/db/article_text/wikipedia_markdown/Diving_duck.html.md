# Diving duck

The **diving ducks**, commonly called **pochards** or **scaups**, are a category of [duck](/wiki/Duck) which feed by diving beneath the surface of the water. They are part of [Anatidae](/wiki/Anatidae), the diverse and very large [family](/wiki/Family_(biology)) that includes ducks, [geese](/wiki/Goose), and [swans](/wiki/Swan).

The diving ducks are placed in a distinct tribe in the subfamily [Anatinae](/wiki/Anatinae), the **Aythyini**. While [morphologically](/wiki/Morphology_(biology)) close to the [dabbling ducks](/wiki/Dabbling_duck), there are nonetheless some pronounced differences such as in the structure of the [trachea](/wiki/Vertebrate_trachea). [mtDNA](/wiki/MtDNA) [cytochrome *b*](/wiki/Cytochrome_b) and [NADH dehydrogenase](/wiki/NADH_dehydrogenase) [subunit](/wiki/Protein_subunit) 2 [sequence](/wiki/DNA_sequence) data indicate that the dabbling and diving ducks are fairly distant from each other, the outward similarities being due to [convergent evolution](/wiki/Convergent_evolution).
Alternatively, the diving ducks are placed as a [subfamily](/wiki/Subfamily) **Aythyinae** in the family Anatidae which would encompass all duck-like birds except the [whistling-ducks](/wiki/Whistling-duck).
The [seaducks](/wiki/Seaduck) commonly found in coastal areas, such as the [long-tailed duck](/wiki/Long-tailed_duck) (formerly known in the U.S. as oldsquaw), [scoters](/wiki/Scoter), [goldeneyes](/wiki/Goldeneye_(duck)), [mergansers](/wiki/Merganser), [bufflehead](/wiki/Bufflehead) and [eiders](/wiki/Eider), are also sometimes colloquially referred to in North America as diving ducks because they also feed by diving; their subfamily (Merginae) is a very distinct one however.

Although the group is [cosmopolitan](/wiki/Cosmopolitan_distribution), most members are native to the [Northern Hemisphere](/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), and it includes several of the most familiar Northern Hemisphere ducks.

This group of ducks is so named because its members feed mainly by diving, although in fact the *Netta* species are reluctant to dive, and feed more like dabbling ducks.

These are gregarious ducks, mainly found on fresh water or on [estuaries](/wiki/Estuaries), though the [greater scaup](/wiki/Greater_scaup) becomes marine during the northern winter. They are strong fliers; their broad, blunt-tipped wings require faster wing-beats than those of many ducks and they take off with some difficulty. Northern species tend to be [migratory](/wiki/Bird_migration); southern species do not migrate though the [hardhead](/wiki/Hardhead) travels long distances on an irregular basis in response to rainfall. Diving ducks do not walk as well on land as the [dabbling ducks](/wiki/Dabbling_duck); their legs tend to be placed further back on their bodies to help propel them when underwater.

## Systematics

Three [genera](/wiki/Genus) are included in the Aythyini. The [marbled duck](/wiki/Marbled_duck) which makes up the [monotypic](/wiki/Monotypic) genus *Marmaronetta*, however, seems very distinct and might have diverged prior to the split of dabbling and diving ducks as indicated by morphological and molecular characteristics. The probably extinct [pink-headed duck](/wiki/Pink-headed_duck), previously treated separately in *Rhodonessa*, has been suggested to belong into *Netta*, but this approach has been questioned. [DNA sequence](/wiki/DNA_sequence) analyses have found it to be the earliest diverging member of the pochard group. The molecular analysis also suggests that the [white-winged duck](/wiki/White-winged_duck) should be placed into a [monotypic](/wiki/Monotypic) genus *Asarcornis* which is fairly close to *Aythya* and might belong into this subfamily.

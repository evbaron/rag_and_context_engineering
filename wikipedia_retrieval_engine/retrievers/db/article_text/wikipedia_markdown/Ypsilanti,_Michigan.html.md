**Ypsilanti** ([/ˌɪpsəˈlænti/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*IP-sə-LAN-tee*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), commonly shortened to **Ypsi** ([/ˈɪpsiː/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*IP-see*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), is a city and [college town](/wiki/College_town) located on the [Huron River](/wiki/Huron_River) in [Washtenaw County, Michigan](/wiki/Washtenaw_County,_Michigan), United States. As of the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the city's population was 20,648. The city is bounded to the north by [Superior Charter Township](/wiki/Superior_Township,_Washtenaw_County,_Michigan) and on the west, south, and east by [Ypsilanti Charter Township](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Township,_Michigan) (a separately governed municipality). Ypsilanti is a part of the [Ann Arbor–Ypsilanti metropolitan area](/wiki/Ann_Arbor,_Michigan), the [Huron River Valley](/wiki/Huron_River), and the [Detroit–Warren–Ann Arbor combined statistical area](/wiki/Metro_Detroit).

Ypsilanti is known for being the home of [Eastern Michigan University](/wiki/Eastern_Michigan_University) (formerly the Michigan State Normal College) since the university's founding as Michigan's first [normal school](/wiki/Normal_school) (teachers' college) in 1849, its location on the historic Detroit–Chicago Road (now [US Highway 12](/wiki/US_Highway_12)), its historic [Depot Town](/wiki/Depot_Town) commercial district, and for its distinctive [Ypsilanti Water Tower](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Water_Tower) constructed in 1890. The city is closely associated with [Ford Motor Company's](/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company) [Willow Run Bomber Plant](/wiki/Willow_Run_Airport) which manufactured over 8,500 [Consolidated B-24 Liberator](/wiki/Consolidated_B-24_Liberator) heavy bombers during [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II); Ypsilanti's [Ford Lake](/wiki/Ford_Lake) was created when Ford Motor Company dammed the Huron River to generate hydroelectric power. Ypsilanti is also home to the first [Domino's Pizza](/wiki/Domino%27s) location, located near EMU's [McKenny Student Union](/wiki/McKenny_Hall) in South Campus.

## History

Buildings in Depot Town

Originally a trading post established in 1809 by a [French-Canadian](/wiki/French-Canadian) fur trader from [Montreal](/wiki/Montreal), a permanent settlement was established on the east side of the [Huron River](/wiki/Huron_River_(Michigan)) in 1823 by Major Thomas Woodruff. It was incorporated into the [Territory of Michigan](/wiki/Territory_of_Michigan) as the village **Woodruff's Grove**. A separate community a short distance away on the west side of the river was established in 1825 under the name "Ypsilanti", after [Demetrios Ypsilantis](/wiki/Demetrios_Ypsilantis), a hero in the [Greek War of Independence](/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence). Woodruff's Grove changed its name to Ypsilanti in 1829, the year its namesake effectively won the war for Greek Independence at the [Battle of Petra](/wiki/Battle_of_Petra), with the two communities eventually merging. A bust of Demetrios Ypsilantis by Greek sculptor Christopher Nastos stands between a Greek and a US flag at the base of the landmark [Ypsilanti Water Tower](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Water_Tower).

[Elijah McCoy](/wiki/Elijah_McCoy) lived in Ypsilanti with his family after moving from Canada following the Civil War. In Ypsilanti, racial barriers prevented him from working immediately as an engineer, so he took a position as a fireman and oiler for the [Michigan Central Railroad](/wiki/Michigan_Central_Railroad) while experimenting with machinery in a home workshop. It was during this period in Ypsilanti that he developed his first automatic lubricator. Today, Ypsilanti honors McCoy’s connection to the city with a Michigan [historical marker](/wiki/Historical_marker) at or near his former home and workshop.

In the late 19th century, Ypsilanti became known for producing full-body [long underwear](/wiki/Long_underwear) called [union suits](/wiki/Union_suit), commonly referred to as “Ypsilanti suits.” The garments were made at the Hay & Todd woolen mill, later renamed the Ypsilanti Underwear Company, along the Huron River near Forest Avenue. The suits were displayed at the [1893](/wiki/1893) [The World's Columbian Exposition](/wiki/The_World%27s_Columbian_Exposition), (also known as the Chicago World's Fair) in [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago) and distributed internationally, earning the city a reputation for quality garment production. The factory employed many workers, particularly women, and featured a large mural of a union suit visible to passing trains. The company declined in the early 20th century, was sold to the Oak Knitting Company in 1907, and the mill buildings were demolished in 1933. Surviving examples of \*Ypsilanti\* underwear are preserved at the Ypsilanti Historical Museum.

Ypsilanti has played an important role in the automobile industry. From 1920 to 1922, Apex Motors produced the "ACE" car. The quarter mile stretch of land along [Michigan Avenue](/wiki/U.S._Route_12_in_Michigan) became a hub for car dealerships starting in 1912 when the first car dealership opened, and ending in the 1990s when the last dealership closed. This area has been referred to as "The Amazing Quarter Mile" and was eventually added to the [Ypsilanti Historic District](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Historic_District). This site is part of a collection of parcels known as the [Water Street Redevelopment Area](/wiki/Water_Street_Redevelopment_Area). It was in Ypsilanti that [Preston Tucker](/wiki/Preston_Tucker) (whose family owned the Ypsilanti Machine Tool Company) designed and built the prototypes for his [Tucker '48](/wiki/1948_Tucker_Sedan). Tucker's story was related in the film *[Tucker: The Man and His Dream](/wiki/Tucker:_The_Man_and_His_Dream)*, directed by [Francis Ford Coppola](/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola).

In 1945, [Henry J. Kaiser](/wiki/Henry_J._Kaiser) and [Joseph W. Frazer](/wiki/Joseph_W._Frazer) bought the nearby [Willow Run](/wiki/Willow_Run) [B-24 Liberator](/wiki/B-24_Liberator) bomber plant from [Ford Motor Company](/wiki/Ford_Motor_Company), and started to make [Kaiser and Frazer](/wiki/Kaiser-Frazer) model cars in 1947. The last [Kaiser](/wiki/Kaiser_Motors) car made in Ypsilanti rolled off the assembly line in 1953, when the company merged with [Willys–Overland](/wiki/Willys) and moved production to [Toledo, Ohio](/wiki/Toledo,_Ohio). [General Motors](/wiki/General_Motors_Corporation) purchased the Kaiser Frazer plant, and converted it into its [Hydramatic Division](/wiki/Hydramatic) (now called its Powertrain division), beginning production in November 1953. The GM Powertrain Division ceased production at this facility in 2010.

Ypsilanti is also the location of the last [Hudson](/wiki/Hudson_Motor_Car_Company) automobile dealership. Today, the former dealership is the site of the [Ypsilanti Automotive Heritage Collection](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Automotive_Heritage_Collection). The museum is the home to an original [Fabulous Hudson Hornet](/wiki/Fabulous_Hudson_Hornet) race car, which inspired the character Doc Hudson in the 2006 [Pixar](/wiki/Pixar) animated film *[Cars](/wiki/Cars_(film))*.

In the early 1970s, the citizens reduced the penalty for the use and sale of [marijuana](/wiki/Cannabis_(drug)) to $5 (the Ypsilanti Marijuana Initiative; see also the [Human Rights Party](/wiki/Human_Rights_Party_(United_States))).

In 1979, [Faz Husain](/wiki/Faz_Husain) was elected to the Ypsilanti city council, the first [Muslim](/wiki/Muslim) and the first native of India to win elected office in Michigan.

In the 1990s, Ypsilanti became the first city in Michigan to pass a [living wage](/wiki/Living_wage) ordinance.

On July 23, 2007, Governor [Jennifer Granholm](/wiki/Jennifer_Granholm) announced that Ypsilanti, along with the cities of [Caro](/wiki/Caro,_Michigan) and [Clio](/wiki/Clio,_Michigan), was chosen by the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) to take part in the Blueprints for Michigan's Downtowns program. The award provides for an economic development consultant to assist Ypsilanti in developing a growth and job creation strategy for the downtown area.

On June 23, 2020, Mayor [Beth Bashert](/wiki/Beth_Bashert) resigned after a controversial comment she made about race during a [Zoom](/wiki/Zoom_(software)) meeting.

## Geography

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 4.47 square miles (11.58 km2), of which 4.29 square miles (11.11 km2) is land and 0.18 square miles (0.47 km2) (4.02%) is water.

The [Huron River](/wiki/Huron_River_(Michigan)) flows through Ypsilanti with [Ford Lake](/wiki/Ford_Lake_(Michigan)) on the southern edge of the city. [Paint Creek](/wiki/Paint_Creek_(Washtenaw_County,_Michigan)) also runs through the city. The [Border-to-Border Trail](/wiki/Border-to-Border_Trail) runs through the city.

## Arts and culture

See also: [National Register of Historic Places listings in Washtenaw County, Michigan](/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Washtenaw_County,_Michigan)

Martha Washington Theatre

The [Martha Washington Theatre](/wiki/Martha_Washington_Theatre) opened in 1915. It was initially operated by Florence W. Signor, who was the only woman theatre operator in Michigan at the time. It was sold to [W. S. Butterfield Theatres](/wiki/W._S._Butterfield_Theatres) in 1925. It was converted to an adult theatre in 1971, then to a strip club in 1982. It closed in March 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and was damaged in a fire three months later.

[Domino's Pizza](/wiki/Domino%27s_Pizza) was founded in Ypsilanti in 1960 near the campus of Eastern Michigan University.

By 1963, Clara Owens established the Ypsilanti Greek Theater in Ypsilanti, Michigan for the performance of Greek theater productions.

Summer festivals that occur in [Depot Town](/wiki/Depot_Town) include the annual [Ypsilanti Heritage Festival](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Heritage_Festival), [Michigan ElvisFest](/wiki/Michigan_ElvisFest), the Orphan Car Festival, the [Michigan Brewers Guild](/wiki/Michigan_Brewers_Guild) Summer Beer Festival, a Latino festival.

Riverside Arts Center, established in 1994, features a 115-seat [black box theater](/wiki/Black_box_theater) and art gallery.

Since 2013, Ypsilanti has participated in [First Fridays](/wiki/First_Friday_(public_event)), an arts and culture-based monthly event that features a self-guided tour of participating businesses highlighting local artists, food and drink.

Ypsi Pride was established in 2017.

## Education

Estabrook Elementary School in Ypsilanti

### K–12 education

[Ypsilanti Community Schools](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Community_Schools) serve residents of the city, as well as parts of [Ypsilanti Township](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Township,_Michigan) and [Superior Township](/wiki/Superior_Township,_Washtenaw_County,_Michigan). [Ypsilanti Public Schools](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Public_Schools) and [Willow Run Community Schools](/wiki/Willow_Run_Community_Schools) merged to form a new, united district on July 1, 2013. [Charter schools](/wiki/Charter_school) in the city include [Arbor Preparatory High School](/wiki/Arbor_Preparatory_High_School).

The [High/Scope Perry Preschool Study](/wiki/HighScope#Effectiveness_of_the_program), which studied the effects of preschool on the later lives of low income children, occurred in Ypsilanti.

### Higher education

A college town, Ypsilanti is the location of [Eastern Michigan University](/wiki/Eastern_Michigan_University), founded in 1849 as Michigan State Normal School. Eastern Michigan University had over 10,000 undergraduate and more than 2000 graduate students in 2025.

[Washtenaw Community College](/wiki/Washtenaw_Community_College) sponsors an off-site extension center in Ypsilanti.

## Media

Ypsilanti is served by daily newspapers from Detroit. Ypsilanti once had its own daily newspaper, the *Ypsilanti Press*, but that paper closed June 28, 1994, after 90 years in business. Upon closing, the Press sold its masthead, archives and subscriber list to *[The Ann Arbor News](/wiki/The_Ann_Arbor_News)*, which then began publishing an Ypsilanti edition. *The Ann Arbor News* ceased publication on July 23, 2009; it was replaced by a new Internet-based news operation, [AnnArbor.com](/wiki/AnnArbor.com), which also produces print editions on Thursdays and Sundays. A weekly newspaper, the *[Ypsilanti Courier](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Courier)*, is published every Thursday by Heritage Media from their [Saline, Michigan](/wiki/Saline,_Michigan) offices. The only newspaper currently operating in Ypsilanti is [Eastern Michigan University](/wiki/Eastern_Michigan_University)'s independent newspaper *[The Eastern Echo](/wiki/The_Eastern_Echo)*.

Local radio stations include:

- [WEMU FM](/wiki/WEMU) - public radio

- [WDEO](/wiki/WDEO_(AM)) - religious radio
- [WSDS](/wiki/WSDS) - Spanish-language
- [WAAM](/wiki/WAAM) - talk and news

### Airport

Willow Run Airport, Ypsilanti's airport, located on the east side of the city, is a general aviation airport, operated by the Wayne County Airport Authority, which also operates Detroit's main airport.

### Bus

- [Ann Arbor Area Transportation Authority](/wiki/Ann_Arbor_Area_Transportation_Authority) operates bus service in the area, with routes serving the [Ypsilanti Transit Center](/wiki/Ypsilanti_Transit_Center).

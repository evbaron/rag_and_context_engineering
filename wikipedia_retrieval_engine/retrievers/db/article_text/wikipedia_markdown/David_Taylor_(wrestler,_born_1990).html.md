**David Morris Taylor III** (born December 5, 1990) is an American former [freestyle](/wiki/Freestyle_wrestler) and [folkstyle wrestler](/wiki/Collegiate_wrestling) who competed in the 74, 86, and 92-kilogram categories. He is the current head coach of the [Oklahoma State Cowboys](/wiki/Oklahoma_State_Cowboys_wrestling).

With his most prestigious accolades coming at 86 kilograms, Taylor was most notably the [2020 Summer Olympic gold medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg), a [World champion](/wiki/List_of_World_and_Olympic_Champions_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling) in [2018](/wiki/2018_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg), [2022](/wiki/2022_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) and [2023](/wiki/2023_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg), and held one of the biggest rivalries in the sport's history against [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani).

One of the most accomplished [Nittany Lions](/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_wrestling) in the history of the [Penn State](/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) program, Taylor was twice the [NCAA Division I National champion](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships#Individual_results) at 165 pounds, and earned the [Dan Hodge Trophy](/wiki/Dan_Hodge_Trophy) two times during his collegiate years.

## Early life and education

Taylor was born in [Reno, Nevada](/wiki/Reno,_Nevada), on December 5, 1990. His family moved to [Wyoming](/wiki/Wyoming) before he was five. His mother saw an ad in the newspaper for youth wrestling at Evanston High School in [Evanston, Wyoming](/wiki/Evanston,_Wyoming), where Taylor started wrestling. Although initially not having much success his first year, Taylor developed a passion to keep improving and eventually started winning local and national tournaments as a youth wrestler.

At age nine, he attended the Jeff Jordan State Champ Camp in [Ohio](/wiki/Ohio), and kept coming back to attend the camp each fall. By the time he was in sixth grade, his family moved to Ohio. Taylor attended [Graham High School](/wiki/Graham_High_School_(St._Paris,_Ohio)) in [St. Paris, Ohio](/wiki/St._Paris,_Ohio), where he wrestled for coach Jeff Jordan. As a [high schooler](/wiki/Scholastic_wrestling), Taylor became a four-time [OHSAA](/wiki/OHSAA) state champion with an outstanding 180–2 record and graduated with a 4.0 [GPA](/wiki/GPA). He received the Dave Schultz High School Excellence Award in 2009 as the nation's top high school wrestler.

During his time at [Penn State](/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) from 2009 to 2014, Taylor was a two-time [NCAA Division I national champion](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships#Individual_results) (four-time finalist), a four-time [Big Ten Conference](/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference) champion, and two-time [Dan Hodge Trophy](/wiki/Dan_Hodge_Trophy) winner. After a perfect [freshman](/wiki/Freshman) year to that point, Taylor was [pinned](/wiki/Pin_(amateur_wrestling)) by [Arizona State's](/wiki/Arizona_State_Sun_Devils_wrestling) [Bubba Jenkins](/wiki/Bubba_Jenkins) at the NCAA finals. As a [sophomore](/wiki/Sophomore), he moved up from 157 pounds (71 kg) to 165 lb (75 kg), winning the NCAA title and being named the best college wrestler in the US as the [Dan Hodge Trophy](/wiki/Dan_Hodge_Trophy) winner with a 70–1 overall record.

As a [junior](/wiki/Junior_(education_year)), he faced [Kyle Dake](/wiki/Kyle_Dake) from [Cornell](/wiki/Cornell_Big_Red_wrestling) in the NCAA finals, who moved up to attempt to become the third [four-time NCAA champion](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships#Four-time_NCAA_champions) and the first to win titles at four different weight classes in the history of the [NCAA](/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association). After a back-and-forth battle, Taylor was defeated 5–4. As a [senior](/wiki/Senior_(education)), he once again had an undefeated campaign, claimed his second NCAA title, helped clinch the team title for the [Nittany Lions](/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_wrestling), and became the third multiple-time [Dan Hodge Trophy](/wiki/Dan_Hodge_Trophy) winner in history. Taylor graduated with 134 wins and three losses, 50 [pins](/wiki/Pin_(amateur_wrestling)), 42 [technical falls](/wiki/Technical_fall), and 29 [major decisions](/wiki/Amateur_wrestling#Victory_conditions_in_collegiate_wrestling).

## Career

### 2010–2012

Taylor made his [freestyle](/wiki/Freestyle_wrestling) debut in April 2010, when he claimed a university U.S. national title. In 2012, he competed at the US Olympic Team Trials, where he went 3–2.

### 2013–2014

In 2013, Taylor claimed runner-up honors at the U.S. Open in April, defeating [Nick Marable](/wiki/Nick_Marable) to reach the finals and then being downed by [defending Olympic champion](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_74_kg) [Jordan Burroughs](/wiki/Jordan_Burroughs). After [pinning](/wiki/Pin_(amateur_wrestling)) 2012 junior world champion [Magomed Kurbanaliev](/wiki/Magomed_Kurbanaliev) from Russia at Beat the Streets, Taylor became a two-time
U.S. university national champion and made the US University World Team on May. At the 2013 US World Team Trials Challenge in June, Taylor went 4–1 with a lone loss to [four-time NCAA Division I national champion](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships#Four-time_NCAA_champions) [Kyle Dake](/wiki/Kyle_Dake) to claim third place. At the [2013 Summer Universiade](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2013_Summer_Universiade), he claimed a bronze medal for the United States.

In 2014, the prior year's U.S. Open result was repeated when Taylor fell to [Jordan Burroughs](/wiki/Jordan_Burroughs), this rematch being closer and forcing the now [defending world champion](/wiki/2013_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_74_kg) to [come from behind](/wiki/Come-from-behind) for a last-minute comeback. After dominating the eventual [2016 Olympic bronze medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_74_kg) and then-two-time [world](/wiki/World_Wrestling_Championships) bronze medalist and two-time [European champion](/wiki/European_Wrestling_Championships) [Jabrayil Hasanov](/wiki/Jabrayil_Hasanov) at Beat the Streets, Taylor made the U.S. World Team Trials final, but was defeated twice in a row by [Burroughs](/wiki/Jordan_Burroughs).

### 2015–2016

Now focusing on freestyle full time after graduating from college, Taylor opened 2015 with a fifth-place finish at the [Yasar Dogu](/wiki/2015_Yasar_Dogu_Tournament), where he went 3–2. He then claimed his first US Open National title in May, followed up by a [technical fall](/wiki/Technical_fall) over Cuba's [Liván López](/wiki/Liv%C3%A1n_L%C3%B3pez) at Beat the Streets. In his fourth attempt to make the US World Team, Taylor was once again overpowered by [Kyle Dake](/wiki/Kyle_Dake) in the challenge tournament, but he came back and claimed the third place when he beat veteran Andrew Howe. Taylor then claimed Grand Prix of Spain and Stepan Sargsyan Cup gold medals in July, before making the decision to bulk up to the 86 kilogram division on September. Taylor took fifth-place at the Golden Grand Prix of November, and seemed overpowered in his first tournament at a new weight class. In December, Taylor competed at the US Senior Nationals, and after running through '14 US World Team member and teammate [Ed Ruth](/wiki/Ed_Ruth), Taylor was defeated by long-time rival [Kyle Dake](/wiki/Kyle_Dake).

In 2016, Taylor was unable to make the US Olympic Team, as he was defeated by [Dake](/wiki/Kyle_Dake) for the fifth time in the senior level, forcing Taylor to battle for the bronze medal, which he comfortably earned. He then claimed his second Spain Grand Prix title on July, and competed again at the [World Clubs Cup](/wiki/2016_World_Wrestling_Clubs_Cup_-_Men%27s_freestyle) of December, where he went 3-1 and helped TMWC reach the first-place, while also defeating the accomplished [Alireza Karimi](/wiki/Alireza_Karimi).

### 2017

In his first competition of the year, Taylor dominated the Grand Prix of Paris in January, and despite being initially banned by the [Government of the Islamic Republic of Iran](/wiki/Government_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran), he competed at the [World Cup](/wiki/2017_Wrestling_World_Cup_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle) in February in [Iran](/wiki/Iran). At the World Cup, he defeated four top-level opponents: ['12 Olympic bronze medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_84_kg) and two-time [European Champion](/wiki/European_Wrestling_Championships) [Dato Marsagishvili](/wiki/Dato_Marsagishvili) in the first round (8-3), [that year's World Championship bronze medalist](/wiki/2017_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) [Vladislav Valiev](/wiki/Vladislav_Valiev) ([TF](/wiki/Technical_fall) 14–4), ['12 Olympic Gold medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_84_kg) [Sharif Sharifov](/wiki/Sharif_Sharifov) (TF 12–2), and ['16 Olympic Gold medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_74_kg) [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani) (fall).

Taylor claimed his second US national title in April, with an overall score of 40–4 against five opponents. After a quick win at Beat the Streets, Taylor made the World Team Trials finals when he ran through '16 US OTT [Greco-Roman](/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling) champion [Joe Rau](/wiki/Joe_Rau) and '12 Junior World Championship runner-up [Pat Downey](/wiki/Pat_Downey_(wrestler)), and defeated three-time [NCAA Division I](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships) All-American Nick Heflin to make the [best-of-three](/wiki/Best_of_three), where he faced returning [Olympic Bronze medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) [J'den Cox](/wiki/J%27den_Cox). In the first match, Taylor rallied comfortably with a 9–3 win, but was closely defeated in the second match 4–3, leading to a controversial third bout due to Cox's sweat and alleged passivity, which caused Taylor to kick the challenge cube and his cornerman [Cael Sanderson](/wiki/Cael_Sanderson) to throw items at the official and a chair onto the mat after losing 5–3.

He came back to competition at the World Clubs Cup on December, where he helped the TMWC to second place with notable victories over [Alireza Karimi](/wiki/Alireza_Karimi) and [Pawan Kumar](/wiki/Pawan_Kumar_(wrestler)).

### 2018

To start off the year, Taylor became only the 12th [American](/wiki/United_States_results_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling) to claim a gold medal at the [Ivan Yarygin Golden Grand Prix](/wiki/Golden_Grand_Prix_Ivan_Yarygin_2018), considered the toughest open tournament in the world. He opened with a win over two-time (and [defending](/wiki/2017_Pan_American_Wrestling_Championships)) [Pan American](/wiki/Pan_American_Wrestling_Championships) champion [Yurieski Torreblanca](/wiki/Yurieski_Torreblanca), followed by [Selim Yaşar](/wiki/Selim_Ya%C5%9Far) on points and a fall over '17 Junior World Champion [Artur Naifonov](/wiki/Artur_Naifonov) to make the finals. In the finals, he got another fall, this time over [Fatih Erdin](/wiki/Fatih_Erdin), to claim the [gold medal](/wiki/Golden_Grand_Prix_Ivan_Yarygin_2018_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg). On April, he claimed his second consecutive [World Cup championship](/wiki/2018_Wrestling_World_Cup_-_Men%27s_freestyle), recording four technical falls to help secure the gold medal for the United States. He also clinched his third US National title, with four victories over fellow Americans.

[On May](/wiki/2018_Pan_American_Wrestling_Championships), he claimed his first Pan American title, with notable wins over [Yurieski Torreblanca](/wiki/Yurieski_Torreblanca), [Pool Ambrocio](/wiki/Pool_Ambrocio) and [Eduardo Gajardo](/wiki/Eduardo_Gajardo). On his seventh attempt to make the US World/Olympic Team, Taylor finally and dominantly was able to punch tickets to the [World Championships](/wiki/World_Wrestling_Championships), when he defeated '18 Bill Farrell Memorial medalist Nick Reenan twice via technical fall on May, at Final X: State College. He then warmed up at the [Yasar Dogu](/wiki/2018_Yasar_Dogu_Tournament) of July, with four dominant pins over foreigners.

At the [World Championships](/wiki/2018_World_Wrestling_Championships), Taylor had a tough start, as he faced his biggest threat of the tournament in the first round, ['16 Olympic Gold medalist](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_74_kg) and [defending World champion](/wiki/2017_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani) from Iran. After being down two points to six at the end of the first period, Taylor was able to overcome adversity and put on nine points on his side, finishing the legendary match 11–6. In the next round, he tech'd '18 Alexander Medved champion Hajy Rajabau from Belarus, to advance to the quarterfinals. Next, he picked apart the accomplished Cuban [Yurieski Torreblanca](/wiki/Yurieski_Torreblanca), to pick up an 8–0 victory. In the semifinals, he had it harder, as he went to the distance against the ['17 European Champion](/wiki/2017_European_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) and Russia's best [Dauren Kurugliev](/wiki/Dauren_Kurugliev), but was able to score the comeback win 7–5. During the match, Taylor got briefly [knocked out](/wiki/Knockout) after Kurugliev accidentally [wheel kicked](/wiki/Kick#Reverse_roundhouse/wheel_kick) him on the chin when escaping a single leg attempt by Taylor. In the finals, he dismantled [Fatih Erdin](/wiki/Fatih_Erdin) from Turkey, overwhelming his opposition with 12 points to two, to claim the [World Championship](/wiki/2018_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) and help Team USA reach third place.

After the year was over, Taylor was named the [UWW](/wiki/United_World_Wrestling) International Freestyle wrestler of the Year, and was awarded the [John Smith](/wiki/John_Smith_(American_wrestler)) Award winner as USA's Freestyle wrestler of the Year.

### 2019–2020

After time off competition, the returning World Champion came back in April 2019, when he claimed his second straight [Pan American title](/wiki/2019_Pan_American_Wrestling_Championships#86_kg), tournament in where he scored 34 points to none against his four opponents and Team USA claimed all ten medals in freestyle. On May, he competed at the annual Beat the Streets for charity, against Drew Foster, where he suffered a severe knee injury which led him to forfeit out of the match, and ultimately, forced him to stay inactive during the whole year, missing the opportunity to make his second US World Team (forfeiting it to [Pat Downey](/wiki/Pat_Downey_(wrestler)) instead, whom he had tech'd twice), to represent the [United States at the 2019 Pan American Games](/wiki/United_States_at_the_2019_Pan_American_Games) or to defend his [title](/wiki/2018_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) at the [World Championships](/wiki/2019_World_Wrestling_Championships).

After almost a full year of no competition, Taylor came back to wrestle in March 2020, at the [Pan American Olympic Qualification Tournament](/wiki/2020_Pan_American_Wrestling_Olympic_Qualification_Tournament). He comfortably got three wins to win the [bracket](/wiki/2020_Pan_American_Wrestling_Olympic_Qualification_Tournament#86_kg) and [internationally qualify for the 2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Qualification). Taylor was then scheduled to compete at the [US Olympic Team Trials](/wiki/2020_U.S._Olympic_Team_Trials_(wrestling)) on April 4 at [State College, Pennsylvania](/wiki/State_College,_Pennsylvania), where he was a heavy favorite. However, the event was postponed for 2021 along with the [Summer Olympics](/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics) due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), leaving all the qualifiers unable to compete.

Taylor was unable to compete for a couple of months due to the [pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), but was scheduled to wrestle [Pat Downey](/wiki/Pat_Downey_(wrestler)) (whom he was unable to wrestle at the '19 US World Trials) on July 25, at [FloWrestling: Dake vs. Chamizo](/wiki/List_of_FloWrestling_events#Dake_vs._Chamizo). After Downey pulled out of the bout due to problems with the organization, Taylor wrestled and tech'd the accomplished [Myles Martin](/wiki/Myles_Martin). After more months of inactiveness, Taylor defeated two-time NCAA champion [Gabe Dean](/wiki/Gabe_Dean) by points on November 24, at the [NLWC III](/wiki/Nittany_Lion_Wrestling_Club#NLWC_III).

### 2021

To start the year, Taylor was scheduled to face his former rival and [five-time World and Olympic champion](/wiki/List_of_World_and_Olympic_Champions_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling#5_World_Level_Championships) (at 74 kg) [Jordan Burroughs](/wiki/Jordan_Burroughs), at 86 kilos, on January 9, while headlining [FloWrestling: Burroughs vs. Taylor](/wiki/List_of_FloWrestling_events#Mensah-Stock_vs._Gray). However, it was announced on January 8 that Taylor was unable to travel to [Austin, Texas](/wiki/Austin,_Texas) due to [COVID-19 restrictions](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_States) and the bout was subsequently postponed for four days later and changed its location for [Lincoln, Nebraska](/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska), thus moving to a different card also named [FloWrestling: Burroughs vs. Taylor](/wiki/List_of_FloWrestling_events#Burroughs_vs._Taylor). After a 4–0 lead for Taylor in the first period, Burroughs rallied late to score four points of his own, but was unable to secure the victory as Taylor had criteria, defeating Burroughs for the first time in five matches. Taylor was also scheduled to compete at the [Grand Prix de France Henri Deglane](/wiki/Grand_Prix_de_France_Henri_Deglane_2021) on January 16, but was not able to travel due to the postponement of his match against [Burroughs](/wiki/Jordan_Burroughs).

During April 2 to 3, Taylor competed at the rescheduled [US Olympic Team Trials](/wiki/2020_U.S._Olympic_Team_Trials_(wrestling)) as the heavy favorite and top–seed. After cleaning out All–American Brett Pfarr in the quarterfinal round, Taylor flawlessly knocked off reigning US National champion [Gabe Dean](/wiki/Gabe_Dean) in a rematch from their match at the [NLWC III](/wiki/Nittany_Lion_Wrestling_Club#NLWC_III), advancing to the best–of–three finals. In the finale, Taylor faced [reigning U23 World Champion](/wiki/2019_U23_World_Wrestling_Championships) and [Penn State](/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_wrestling) legend [Bo Nickal](/wiki/Bo_Nickal), whom he is close with as a friend and teammate at the [Nittany Lion Wrestling Club](/wiki/Nittany_Lion_Wrestling_Club). He defeated Nickal twice and did not allow him to score any points while scoring ten combined points of his own, becoming the US Olympic Team Member at 86 kilograms, and qualifying him to represent the [United States at the 2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/United_States_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics). In regards to his close relationship with Nickal, Taylor then stated:

> "It was a weird emotion," Taylor said. "We had a discussion leading up to it, and we have such a deep room in the [wrestling club](/wiki/Nittany_Lion_Wrestling_Club), especially at 86 kilos, so we're challenged every single day and none of us would be where we are without each other. Bo's amazing, and I wouldn't be where I am without Bo."

As a result, Taylor also competed at the [Pan American Continental Championships](/wiki/2021_Pan_American_Wrestling_Championships) on May 30. Taylor racked up 30 points against his three opponents while not getting scored on to claim the crown and help the USA reach all 10 freestyle medals.

On August 4, Taylor competed in the first date of [men's freestyle 86 kg](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) of the [2020 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2020_Summer_Olympics), where he looked dominant enough to score [technical falls](/wiki/Technical_fall) over all of his three opponents on his way to the finals; four-time [World Championship](/wiki/World_Wrestling_Championships) medalist [Ali Shabanau](/wiki/Ali_Shabanau) from [Belarus](/wiki/Belarus_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics), ['20 European Championship runner-up](/wiki/2020_European_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) [Myles Amine](/wiki/Myles_Amine) from [San Marino](/wiki/San_Marino_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) and [defending World silver medalist](/wiki/2019_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) [Deepak Punia](/wiki/Deepak_Punia_(wrestler)) from [India](/wiki/India_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics). On the other side of the finale awaited rival and [reigning Olympic and World Champion](/wiki/List_of_World_and_Olympic_Champions_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling#2018–2019) [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani) from [Iran](/wiki/Iran_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics), Taylor meeting him in a rubber match on August 5 after downing him twice earlier in his career. Down 0–2 to a step-out and a passivity point in the second period, Taylor scored the first takedown to tie it up, though Yazdani scored another step-out to make it 2–3 on his favor, before being blasted by Taylor for two points late in the match, resulting in the stunning upset comeback win, shocking the world as the underdog going into the match. This result gained Taylor the [2020 Summer Olympic Games gold medal](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) and extended his win-streak to 54 matches.

As an Olympic medalist, Taylor earned the right to automatically represent the United States at the [2021 World Championships](/wiki/2021_World_Wrestling_Championships) without having to compete domestically to make the [US World Team](/wiki/2021_United_States_World_Team_Trials_(wrestling)), and did do so in October 2–3. On the first date, Taylor once again ran through his competition, dominating the likes of World Championship runner-ups [Boris Makoev](/wiki/Boris_Makoev) and [Abubakr Abakarov](/wiki/Abubakr_Abakarov) to cruise to the finale. A fourth match with rival and [reigning World Champion](/wiki/List_of_World_and_Olympic_Champions_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling#2018–2019) [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani) took place in the finals, but this time the Iranian came up on top for the first time, nullifying Taylor and snapping his 57-match win streak, marking the series 3–1 in favor of Taylor and the latter leaving with a silver medal.

### 2022

In June, Taylor stopped [two-time NCAA champion](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships#1999–2024) [Zahid Valencia](/wiki/Zahid_Valencia) twice to make the US World Team once again, at Final X NYC. In September, after dominantly cruising to the finals, Taylor avenged his loss to [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani) and claimed his second [World championship](/wiki/2022_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg).

### 2023

Repeating as Final X champion to become the US World Team member at the weight, Taylor defeated US National champion and [teammate](/wiki/Nittany_Lion_Wrestling_Club) [Aaron Brooks](/wiki/Aaron_Brooks_(wrestler)) twice in a row in June. At the [World Championships](/wiki/2023_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg), Taylor dominated once again, defeating everyone on his way either by [fall](/wiki/Pin_(sport_wrestling)) or [technical fall](/wiki/Technical_fall), including [Hassan Yazdani](/wiki/Hassan_Yazdani), leading 5–1 in the series.

### 2024

Competing at the [US Olympic Trials](/wiki/2024_United_States_Olympic_trials_(wrestling)) in April, Taylor sat in the best-of-three finals as a [2023 World medalist](/wiki/2023_World_Wrestling_Championships), where he was upset twice in a row by now [U23 World](/wiki/2023_U23_World_Wrestling_Championships) and [four-time NCAA champion](/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships), as well as teammate, [Aaron Brooks](/wiki/Aaron_Brooks_(wrestler)), losing the series and staying out of the [2024 Summer Olympics](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics). In May, after becoming the head coach at [Oklahoma State](/wiki/Oklahoma_State_Cowboys_wrestling), Taylor expressed at a press conference that he had retired from the sport as a competitor, at the age of 33.

In September, Taylor, now the head coach of the [Cowboys](/wiki/Oklahoma_State_Cowboys_wrestling), announced he would come out of retirement to compete at the US World Team Trials up at 92 kilograms. After notorious wins on the first day over [NCAA champion](/wiki/2018_NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships) [Michael Macchiavello](/wiki/Michael_Macchiavello) and All-Americans Trent Hidlay and Marcus Coleman, Taylor made the best-of-three finals, where he faced [returning World medalist](/wiki/2023_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_92_kg) [Zahid Valencia](/wiki/Zahid_Valencia). Then, after consecutive victories over Valencia, Taylor was back on the US World Team.

In October, he competed at his last [World Championships](/wiki/2024_World_Wrestling_Championships), and in his first at 92 kilograms. In a battle of [Tokyo Olympic](/wiki/Wrestling_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) champions, Taylor fell to [seven–time World and Olympic champion](/wiki/List_of_World_and_Olympic_Champions_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling#5_World_Level_Championships) [Abdulrashid Sadulaev](/wiki/Abdulrashid_Sadulaev) from Russia, in the first round. After being pulled back into repechage, Taylor bounced back defeating [World medalist](/wiki/2021_World_Wrestling_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_freestyle_86_kg) [Abubakr Abakarov](/wiki/Abubakr_Abakarov) from Azerbaijan and Lars Schäfle from Germany to earn the right to contend for a bronze medal. In a battle of [2022 World champions](/wiki/2022_World_Wrestling_Championships), Taylor beat [two-time World champion](/wiki/List_of_World_and_Olympic_Champions_in_men%27s_freestyle_wrestling#5_World_Level_Championships) [Kamran Ghasempour](/wiki/Kamran_Ghasempour) from Iran to earn the bronze medal, and subsequently retired from the sport as an athlete.

## Coaching career

### Oklahoma State University

In May, a month after a runner-up finish at the [2024 U.S. Olympic Trials](/wiki/2024_United_States_Olympic_trials_(wrestling)), Taylor was announced as the head coach at [Oklahoma State University](/wiki/Oklahoma_State_University%E2%80%93Stillwater), following [John Smith](/wiki/John_Smith_(American_wrestler))'s retirement. During his first year, the Cowboys had a 13-1 dual-meet record; clinched the Big 12 Conference title with one individual champion; and finished third in the nation with six All-Americans and two individual national champions, [Dean Hamiti](/wiki/Dean_Hamiti) at 174 and [Wyatt Hendrickson](/wiki/Wyatt_Hendrickson) at 285.

## Endorsements

In 2015, Taylor and Adidas released a line of wrestling apparel labeled "M2" after Taylor's college nickname "Magic Man". Taylor received the nickname after a collegiate match in which he turned an unfavorable situation for himself into an advantage. The apparel line began with wrestling shoes and has since expanded to clothing, posters and Adidas sponsored events.

This article is about the former WHL team (1945–1970). For its successor NHL team (1970–present), see [Vancouver Canucks](/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks).

The **Vancouver Canucks** were a minor league professional [ice hockey](/wiki/Ice_hockey) team in the [Pacific Coast Hockey League](/wiki/Pacific_Coast_Hockey_League) and the [Western Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Hockey_League_(1952%E2%80%931974)), based in [Vancouver](/wiki/Vancouver), British Columbia, Canada. Inaugurated in 1945 with the PCHL, they became a WHL team with the merger of the PCHL with the [Western Canada Senior Hockey League](/wiki/Western_Canada_Senior_Hockey_League) in 1952. The Canucks played 25 seasons in the WHL between 1945 and 1970. They were replaced by an expansion [National Hockey League](/wiki/National_Hockey_League) franchise of the same name in 1970, joining that league with the expansion [Buffalo Sabres](/wiki/Buffalo_Sabres).

The Vancouver Canucks won six [President's/Lester Patrick Cups](/wiki/Lester_Patrick_Cup) (the trophy was renamed in 1960 after the death of [Lester Patrick](/wiki/Lester_Patrick)), two PCHL titles (1946 and 1948) and four WHL titles (1958, 1960, 1969 and 1970). They were also regular season champions four times. They played home games in the [PNE Forum](/wiki/PNE_Forum) arena at the [Pacific National Exhibition](/wiki/Pacific_National_Exhibition) in east Vancouver, before moving for their last two seasons into the [Pacific Coliseum](/wiki/Pacific_Coliseum) just to the north.

## Personnel

### Players

Five Canucks players have been inducted into the [Hockey Hall of Fame](/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame). [Andy Bathgate](/wiki/Andy_Bathgate) (inducted in 1978) played with Vancouver for four years (1952–54 and 1968–70) and was on the team for their final two championships. In 1969–70, he recorded 108 points in 72 games, earning the George Leader Cup as league [MVP](/wiki/Most_Valuable_Player). [Johnny Bower](/wiki/Johnny_Bower), [Tony Esposito](/wiki/Tony_Esposito), [Allan Stanley](/wiki/Allan_Stanley) and [Gump Worsley](/wiki/Gump_Worsley) – all Hall of Fame inductees – have all played one season with the Canucks.

# List of Nobel Peace Prize laureates

The Norwegian Nobel Institute assists the Norwegian Nobel Committee in selecting recipients of the Nobel Peace Prize and in organising the annual award in Oslo.

The [Norwegian Nobel Committee](/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee) awards the [Nobel Peace Prize](/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize) annually "to the person who shall have done the most or the best work for fraternity between nations, for the abolition or reduction of standing armies and for the holding and promotion of peace congresses." As dictated by [Alfred Nobel](/wiki/Alfred_Nobel)'s will, the award is administered by the [Norwegian Nobel Committee](/wiki/Norwegian_Nobel_Committee) and awarded by a committee of five people elected by the [Parliament of Norway](/wiki/Parliament_of_Norway).

Each recipient receives a medal, a [diploma](/wiki/Diploma), and a monetary award prize (that has varied throughout the years). It is one of the five prizes established by the 1895 [will](/wiki/Will_(law)) of [Alfred Nobel](/wiki/Alfred_Nobel) (who died in 1896), awarded for outstanding contributions in [chemistry](/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry), [physics](/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics), [literature](/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature), [physiology or medicine](/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physiology_or_Medicine).

## Overview

The Peace Prize is presented annually in [Oslo](/wiki/Oslo), in the presence of the [King of Norway](/wiki/King_of_Norway), on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death, and is the only Nobel Prize not presented in [Stockholm](/wiki/Stockholm). Unlike the other prizes, the Peace Prize is occasionally awarded to an organisation (such as the [International Committee of the Red Cross](/wiki/International_Committee_of_the_Red_Cross), a three-time recipient) rather than an individual.

The Nobel Peace Prize was first awarded in 1901 to [Frédéric Passy](/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Passy) and [Henry Dunant](/wiki/Henry_Dunant), who shared a prize of 150,782 [Swedish kronor](/wiki/Swedish_kronor) (equal to 7,731,004 kronor in 2008), and most recently in 2025 to [María Corina Machado](/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Corina_Machado).

- [Linus Pauling](/wiki/Linus_Pauling), the Nobel Peace Prize laureate in 1962, is the only person to have been awarded two unshared Nobel Prizes; he won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1954.
- At 17 years of age, [Malala Yousafzai](/wiki/Malala_Yousafzai), the 2014 recipient, is the youngest to be awarded the Peace Prize.
- The first woman to receive a Nobel Peace Prize was [Bertha von Suttner](/wiki/Bertha_von_Suttner) in 1905. Of the 113 individual Nobel Peace Prize Laureates, 20 have been women.
- The [International Committee of the Red Cross](/wiki/International_Committee_of_the_Red_Cross) has received the most Nobel Peace Prizes, having been awarded the Prize three times for its humanitarian work.
- Five Nobel Peace Prize Laureates were under arrest at the time of their awards: [Carl von Ossietzky](/wiki/Carl_von_Ossietzky) (in 1935), [Aung San Suu Kyi](/wiki/Aung_San_Suu_Kyi) (in 1991), [Liu Xiaobo](/wiki/Liu_Xiaobo) (in 2010), [Ales Bialiatski](/wiki/Ales_Bialiatski) (in 2022), and [Narges Mohammadi](/wiki/Narges_Mohammadi) (in 2023).

## Laureates

As of 2025, the Peace Prize has been awarded to 114 individuals and 28 organizations. Twenty women have won the Nobel Peace Prize, more than any other Nobel Prize. Only two recipients have won multiple Peace Prizes: the [International Committee of the Red Cross](/wiki/International_Committee_of_the_Red_Cross) has won three times (1917, 1944 and 1963) and the [Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](/wiki/United_Nations_High_Commissioner_for_Refugees) has won twice (1954 and 1981). There have been 19 years in which the Peace Prize was not awarded.

## Notes

:   [**A**](#ref_LateA) The following laureates were all awarded their respective Prizes one year late because the Committee decided that none of the nominations in the year in which they are listed as being awarded the Prize met the criteria in Nobel's will; per its rules the Committee delayed the awarding of the Prizes until the next year, although they were awarded as the previous year's Prize:

    :   Elihu Root (1912), Woodrow Wilson (1919), Austen Chamberlain (1925), Charles G. Dawes (1925), Frank B. Kellogg (1929), Norman Angell (1933), Carl von Ossietzky (1935), International Committee of the Red Cross (1944), Albert Schweitzer (1952), Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (1954), Albert Lutuli (1960), Linus Pauling (1962)
:   [**B**](#ref_Carl_von_OssietzkyB) Carl von Ossietzky's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because he was imprisoned and was refused a passport by the government of Germany.
:   [**C**](#ref_Dag_HammarskjöldC) Dag Hammarskjöld's Prize was awarded posthumously.
:   [**D**](#ref_Henry_KissingerD) Henry Kissinger's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because he did not want to become a target of anti-war protesters.
:   [**E**](#ref_Le_Duc_ThoE) Lê Đức Thọ declined to accept the Prize.
:   [**F**](#ref_Andrei_SakharovF) Andrei Sakharov's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because he was refused a passport by the government of the Soviet Union.
:   [**G**](#ref_Lech_WałęsaG) Lech Wałęsa's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because he was refused a passport by the government of Poland.
:   [**H**](#ref_Aung_San_Suu_KyiH) Aung San Suu Kyi's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because she was being held prisoner by the government of Myanmar. Following her release from house arrest and election to the Pyithu Hluttaw, Suu Kyi accepted her award in person on 16 June 2012.
:   [**I**](#ref_Liu_XiaoboI) Liu Xiaobo's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because he was imprisoned in China.
:   [**J**](#ref_Ales_BialiatskiJ) Ales Bialiatski's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because he was imprisoned in Belarus.
:   [**K**](#ref_Narges_MohammadiK) Narges Mohammadi's Prize was awarded *in absentia* because she was imprisoned in Iran.
:   [**L**](#ref_Maria_Corina_MachadoL) Machado gave her medal to U.S. President [Donald Trump](/wiki/Donald_Trump), though this is not recognized since "a Nobel Prize can neither be revoked, shared, nor transferred to others. Once the announcement has been made, the decision stands for all time."

# Coronet Records

[Louis Armstrong](/wiki/Louis_Armstrong)'s "[Mack The Knife](/wiki/Mack_The_Knife)", with octagonal label

**Coronet Records** is the name of at least three different record companies.

One was based in [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco) in the 1940s and was responsible for the first recordings of [Dave Brubeck](/wiki/Dave_Brubeck). Brubeck's Coronet Records disbanded when it couldn't pay its bills and its recordings were taken over by what became [Fantasy Records](/wiki/Fantasy_Records).

The second, a division of Premier Albums, issued a wide range of inexpensive [LP records](/wiki/LP_record) in the 1950s and 1960s in the United States. It re-released archival material from [The Four Lovers](/wiki/The_Four_Lovers), which eventually provoked a lawsuit in 1966 from [Bob Gaudio](/wiki/Bob_Gaudio) and [Nick Massi](/wiki/Nick_Massi) when the label used their images and likenesses to misportray the records as being from [The Four Seasons](/wiki/The_Four_Seasons_(band)).

The third was a [record label](/wiki/Record_label) in [Australia](/wiki/Australia), based in [Sydney](/wiki/Sydney). It operated from the early 1950s until around 1962 and was recognizable by its octagonal label. Until early 1960 Coronet was the principal house label of the [ARC](/wiki/Australian_Record_Company), releasing material licensed from international labels, primarily [CBS Records](/wiki/Columbia_Records) in the U.S., as well as material recorded by Australian artists.
In 1960 ARC was taken over by CBS Records (although the company continued to trade as ARC until the late 1970s) and the Coronet label was phased out in favour of the new [CBS Australia](/wiki/CBS_Records_International) label, which used the CBS/Columbia "walking eye" logo.

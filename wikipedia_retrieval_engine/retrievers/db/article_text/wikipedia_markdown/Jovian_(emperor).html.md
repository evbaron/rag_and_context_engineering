**Jovian** ([/ˈdʒoʊviən/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English); [Latin](/wiki/Latin_language): *Jovianus*; [Greek](/wiki/Ancient_Greek): Ἰοβιανός *Iobianós*; 331 – 17 February 364) was [Roman emperor](/wiki/Roman_emperor) from June 363 to February 364. As part of the imperial bodyguard, he accompanied [Julian](/wiki/Julian_(emperor)) on his [Persian expedition](/wiki/Julian%27s_Persian_expedition). Julian was killed in battle, and the exhausted and ill-provisioned [Roman army](/wiki/Roman_army) declared Jovian his successor. Unable to cross the [Tigris](/wiki/Tigris), Jovian made peace with the [Sasanian Empire](/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) on humiliating terms. He spent the rest of his seven-month reign traveling back towards [Constantinople](/wiki/Constantinople), but died at [Dadastana](/wiki/Dadastana) without reaching the capital city.

Jovian was a [Christian](/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Roman_Empire), whose accession ended the imperial endorsement of polytheism briefly revived under Julian; during a stop in [Edessa](/wiki/Edessa) he received petitions from bishops over doctrinal issues. He was also the third-last emperor to govern the empire as a single polity, and the last to do so for the whole of his reign. From his successor [Valentinian I](/wiki/Valentinian_I) to the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire), all subsequent administrations involved co-emperors governing a territory split into jurisdictions of [East and West](/wiki/Greek_East_and_Latin_West).

## Early life and accession

Jovian was born at [Singidunum](/wiki/Singidunum), [Moesia Superior](/wiki/Moesia_Superior) (today [Belgrade](/wiki/Belgrade) in [Serbia](/wiki/Serbia)), in 331, son of Varronianus, the commander of [Constantius II](/wiki/Constantius_II)'s imperial bodyguards (*[comes domesticorum](/wiki/Domesticus_(Roman_Empire))*). He also joined the guards and in this capacity in 361, escorted Constantius' remains to the [Church of the Holy Apostles](/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Apostles). Jovian was married to [Charito](/wiki/Charito) and they had two sons, Varronianus, and another whose name is unknown.

In 363 Jovian accompanied the Emperor [Julian](/wiki/Julian_(emperor)) on the [Mesopotamian](/wiki/Mesopotamia) campaign against [Shapur II](/wiki/Shapur_II), the Sassanid king. At the [Battle of Samarra](/wiki/Battle_of_Samarra), a small but decisive engagement, Julian was mortally wounded. [Ammianus Marcellinus](/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus) reports that the dying emperor declined to name his preferred successor, fearful that he either might overlook a worthy candidate, or put his desired candidate in danger of power-hungry nobles. Julian died on 26 June 363. The next day, the army offered the throne to the aged [Saturninius Secundus Salutius](/wiki/Salutius), [praetorian prefect](/wiki/Praetorian_prefect) of the Orient, who refused it. Instead, and in spite of Julian's reinstitution of paganism, the army chose the Christian Jovian, senior officer of the [Scholae](/wiki/Scholae), as Emperor.

## Reign

On the very morning of his accession, Jovian resumed the retreat begun by Julian. Though harassed by the Sasanians, the army succeeded in reaching the city of [Dura](/wiki/Dura-Europos) on the banks of the [Tigris](/wiki/Tigris). Unable to bridge the river and reach Roman lands on the western bank, Jovian was forced to sue for a [peace treaty](/wiki/Perso-Roman_Peace_Treaty_of_363) on humiliating terms. In exchange for an unhindered retreat to his own territory, he agreed to a thirty-year truce, a withdrawal from the five [Roman provinces](/wiki/Roman_province), Arzamena, [Moxoeona](/wiki/Moxoene), Azbdicena, Rehimena and [Corduena](/wiki/Gordyene), and to allow the Sasanians to occupy the fortresses of [Nisibis](/wiki/Nisibis), Castra Maurorum and [Singara](/wiki/Singara). The Romans also surrendered their interests in the [Kingdom of Armenia](/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_(antiquity)) to the Sasanians. The king of Armenia, [Arsaces II](/wiki/Arshak_II), was to receive no help from Rome. The treaty was widely seen as a disgrace.

After crossing the Tigris, Jovian sent an embassy to the [West](/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire) to announce his elevation. With the treaty signed, Jovian and his army marched to Nisibis, whose populace were given three days to leave before the city was handed over to the Sasanians.

In September 363 Jovian arrived at [Edessa](/wiki/Edessa) where he issued two edicts. The first, a limitation on the distance a soldier could be sent for straw, was to indicate an end to the war with [Sasanid Persia](/wiki/Sasanian_Empire). The second was the restoration of estates of the *res privata* to the Imperial finances following Julian's incorporating them to pagan temples.

Jovian's arrival at [Antioch](/wiki/Antioch) in October 363, was met with an enraged populace. Faced with offensive graffiti and insulting authorless bills (*famosi*) throughout the city, he ordered the [Library of Antioch](/wiki/Library_of_Antioch) to be burned down. Jovian left Antioch in November 363, headed for [Constantinople](/wiki/Constantinople). By December 363 he was at [Ancyra](/wiki/Ankara), where he proclaimed his infant son, [Varronianus](/wiki/Varronianus_(son_of_Jovian)), consul.

The journey ended prematurely on 17 February 364, when Jovian was found dead in his tent at Dadastana, halfway between Ancyra and [Nicaea](/wiki/Nicaea). His death, which went uninvestigated, was possibly the result of suffocating on poisonous fumes seeping from the newly painted bedchamber walls by a [brazier](/wiki/Brazier). Jovian died aged 33 and was buried in the [Church of the Holy Apostles](/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Apostles) in Constantinople, in a [porphyry sarcophagus](/wiki/Porphyry_(geology)#Imperial_sarcophagi). He was succeeded by the brothers [Valentinian I](/wiki/Valentinian_I) and [Valens](/wiki/Valens), who divided the empire between them. Seeking to remove any threats to their position, they blinded Varronianus; according to [John Chrysostom](/wiki/John_Chrysostom), Charito was spared but lived the rest of her life in fear.

## Restoration of Christianity

Jovian was met at Edessa by a group of [bishops](/wiki/Bishop), including [Athanasius](/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria), who was newly returned from exile. The [Semi-Arian](/wiki/Semi-Arianism) bishops received a poor greeting, while Athanasius delivered a letter to Jovian insisting on the [Nicene Creed](/wiki/Nicene_Creed) and the rejection of [Arianism](/wiki/Arianism). Athanasius was restored to his episcopal duties, and allowed to accompany Jovian to [Antioch](/wiki/Antioch).

Upon his arrival in the city, Jovian received a letter from the Synod of Antioch, imploring for [Meletius'](/wiki/Meletius_of_Antioch) restoration as bishop. By September 363, Jovian restored the [labarum](/wiki/Labarum) ("Chi-Rho") as the army's standard and revoked the [edicts of Julian](/wiki/Julian_(emperor)#Restoration_of_state_paganism) against Christians, but did not close any pagan temples. He issued an edict of toleration, granting his subjects full liberty of conscience, but also banned [magic](/wiki/Magic_in_the_Greco-Roman_world) and [divination](/wiki/Divination). Despite supporting the Nicene doctrines, he passed no edicts against Arians. [Philostorgius](/wiki/Philostorgius), an Arian church historian, stated, "The Emperor Jovian restored the churches to their original uses, and set them free from all the vexatious persecutions inflicted on them by the Apostate Julian."

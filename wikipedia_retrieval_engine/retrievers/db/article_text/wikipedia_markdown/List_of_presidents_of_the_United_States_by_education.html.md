# List of presidents of the United States by education

Most [presidents of the United States](/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_United_States) received a [college education](/wiki/Undergraduate_education), even most of the earliest. Of the first seven presidents, five were college graduates. College degrees have set the presidents apart from the general population, and presidents have held degrees even though it was quite rare and unnecessary for practicing most occupations, including [law](/wiki/Legal_profession). Of the 45 individuals to have been the president, 24 graduated from a private undergraduate college, 9 graduated from a public undergraduate college, and 12 held no degree. Every president since 1953 has had a [bachelor's degree](/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree), reflecting the increasing importance of [higher education in the United States](/wiki/Higher_education_in_the_United_States). 16 presidents received a Bachelor's or advanced degree from colleges in the [Ivy League](/wiki/Ivy_League).

## List by university attended

### Did not graduate from college

- [George Washington](/wiki/George_Washington) (The death of Washington's father ended his formal schooling. The [College of William & Mary](/wiki/College_of_William_%26_Mary) issued surveying licenses in Virginia, and Washington received his license from the College in 1749 — surveyors did not attend classes at the school. Washington believed strongly in formal education, and his will left money and/or stocks to support three educational institutions, including [George Washington University](/wiki/George_Washington_University) and [Washington and Lee University](/wiki/Washington_and_Lee_University))
- [James Monroe](/wiki/James_Monroe) (attended the College of William & Mary, but dropped out to fight in the [Revolutionary War](/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War))
- [Andrew Jackson](/wiki/Andrew_Jackson) (qualified as a lawyer in the age before law schools, where [a kind of apprenticeship system](/wiki/Reading_law) was used)
- [Martin Van Buren](/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren) (read for the law, like Jackson)
- [William Henry Harrison](/wiki/William_Henry_Harrison) (attended [Hampden–Sydney College](/wiki/Hampden%E2%80%93Sydney_College) for three years but did not graduate and then attended [University of Pennsylvania School of Medicine](/wiki/Perelman_School_of_Medicine) but never received a degree)
- [Zachary Taylor](/wiki/Zachary_Taylor) (received an Army commission without any formal training)
- [Millard Fillmore](/wiki/Millard_Fillmore) (read for the law; founded the [University at Buffalo](/wiki/University_at_Buffalo))
- [Abraham Lincoln](/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) (had only about a year of formal schooling of any kind)
- [Andrew Johnson](/wiki/Andrew_Johnson) (no formal schooling of any kind)
- [Grover Cleveland](/wiki/Grover_Cleveland) (read for the law)
- [William McKinley](/wiki/William_McKinley) (attended [Allegheny College](/wiki/Allegheny_College), but did not graduate; also attended [Albany Law School](/wiki/Albany_Law_School), but also did not graduate)
- [Harry S. Truman](/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) (went to business college and law school, but did not graduate)

#### Additional undergraduate information

Some presidents attended more than one institution. George Washington never attended college, though [the College of William & Mary](/wiki/College_of_William_%26_Mary) did issue him a surveyor's certificate. One president attended a foreign college at the undergraduate level: John Quincy Adams at [Leiden University](/wiki/Leiden_University) ([John F. Kennedy](/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) intended to study at the [London School of Economics](/wiki/London_School_of_Economics), but failed to attend as he fell ill before classes began.) Bill Clinton won a [Rhodes Scholarship](/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship), enrolling at the [University of Oxford](/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in Fall 1968, where he read for a [BPhil](/wiki/Bachelor_of_Philosophy) in politics. He left Oxford without earning a degree in order to enroll at [Yale Law School](/wiki/Yale_Law_School).

Three presidents have attended the [United States service academies](/wiki/United_States_service_academies): [Ulysses S. Grant](/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) and [Dwight D. Eisenhower](/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) graduated from the [United States Military Academy](/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) at [West Point](/wiki/West_Point,_New_York), while [Jimmy Carter](/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) graduated from the [United States Naval Academy](/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) at [Annapolis, Maryland](/wiki/Annapolis,_Maryland). No presidents have graduated from the [United States Coast Guard Academy](/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy) or the much newer [United States Air Force Academy](/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy). Eisenhower also graduated from the [Army Command and General Staff College](/wiki/United_States_Army_Command_and_General_Staff_College), [Army Industrial College](/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower_School_for_National_Security_and_Resource_Strategy), and [Army War College](/wiki/United_States_Army_War_College). These were not degree-granting institutions when Eisenhower attended, but were part of his professional education as a career soldier.

Several presidents who were lawyers did not attend law school, but became lawyers after [independent study](/wiki/Reading_law) under the tutelage of established attorneys. Some had attended college before beginning their legal studies, and several studied law without first having attended college. Presidents who were lawyers but did not attend law school include: [John Adams](/wiki/John_Adams); [Thomas Jefferson](/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson); [James Madison](/wiki/James_Madison); [James Monroe](/wiki/James_Monroe); [John Quincy Adams](/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams); [Andrew Jackson](/wiki/Andrew_Jackson); [Martin Van Buren](/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren); [John Tyler](/wiki/John_Tyler); [James K. Polk](/wiki/James_K._Polk); [Millard Fillmore](/wiki/Millard_Fillmore); [James Buchanan](/wiki/James_Buchanan); [Abraham Lincoln](/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln); [James A. Garfield](/wiki/James_A._Garfield); [Grover Cleveland](/wiki/Grover_Cleveland); [Benjamin Harrison](/wiki/Benjamin_Harrison); and [Calvin Coolidge](/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge).

Presidents who were admitted to the bar after a combination of law school and independent study include: [Franklin Pierce](/wiki/Franklin_Pierce); [Chester A. Arthur](/wiki/Chester_A._Arthur); [William McKinley](/wiki/William_McKinley); and [Woodrow Wilson](/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson).

**Ezra Michael Koenig** ([/ˌkeɪnɪɡ/](/wiki/Help:IPA/English) [*KAY-nig*](/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born April 8, 1984) is an American musician, [record producer](/wiki/Record_producer), and radio personality. He is the lead vocalist, guitarist, and primary songwriter of [indie rock](/wiki/Indie_rock) band [Vampire Weekend](/wiki/Vampire_Weekend). Additionally, Koenig is the creator of the [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) animated comedy series *[Neo Yokio](/wiki/Neo_Yokio)* and also hosts the [Apple Music](/wiki/Apple_Music) radio talk show *[Time Crisis with Ezra Koenig](/wiki/Time_Crisis_with_Ezra_Koenig). Time Crisis* is airing its tenth season, as of 2024.

Koenig's accolades include two [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Award) from five nominations; with Vampire Weekend, he won [Best Alternative Music Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album) in 2013 and 2019. He was also nominated for Album of the Year in [2016](/wiki/59th_Annual_Grammy_Awards) for his production work on [Beyoncé](/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9)'s album *[Lemonade](/wiki/Lemonade_(Beyonc%C3%A9_album))* (2016).

## Early life

Koenig was born in [New York City](/wiki/New_York_City) and is the son of Bobby Bass, a [psychotherapist](/wiki/Psychotherapist), and Robin Koenig, a [set dresser](/wiki/Set_dresser) on film and TV productions. He is [Jewish](/wiki/Jewish), and his family emigrated to the U.S. from [Romania](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Romania) and [Hungary](/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Hungary). His parents lived on the [Upper West Side](/wiki/Upper_West_Side) of [Manhattan](/wiki/Manhattan) before moving to [Glen Ridge, New Jersey](/wiki/Glen_Ridge,_New_Jersey) shortly after their son's birth. As a young teenager, Ezra attended [Buck's Rock Camp](/wiki/Buck%27s_Rock), a [Montessori](/wiki/Montessori)-style performing and creative arts camp in Connecticut. He graduated from [Glen Ridge High School](/wiki/Glen_Ridge_High_School). Koenig has a younger sister, Emma Koenig (born 1988), who is the author of the books *FUCK! I'm in my twenties* and *Moan*, and has written for television and magazines. Ezra began writing music around the age of ten, and his first song ever was titled "Bad Birthday Party".

While attending [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University), he ran a blog called *Internet Vibes* about fashion, [existentialism](/wiki/Existentialism), personal identity, and modern culture. After graduating, he taught English through [Teach for America](/wiki/Teach_for_America) at Junior High School 258 in [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn). Students recalled Koenig bringing his guitar with him to class, despite trying to hide it and his music career. He was described as a laid-back teacher who successfully made bonds with his students. In fall 2007, a deal with [XL Recordings](/wiki/XL_Recordings) cut short Koenig's teaching career.

During his schooling and college years, he was involved in numerous musical projects with [Wes Miles](/wiki/Wes_Miles), a childhood friend and now current frontman of [Ra Ra Riot](/wiki/Ra_Ra_Riot). Koenig and Miles formed an experimental band, The Sophisticuffs, which was described as "wildly inventive musical work". In 2004, Koenig formed the hip hop group L'Homme Run, which was notable for the comedic track "Pizza Party", with fellow Vampire Weekend band member [Chris Tomson](/wiki/Chris_Tomson), played saxophone for the [indie rock](/wiki/Indie_rock) band [Dirty Projectors](/wiki/Dirty_Projectors), and worked as an intern for [The Walkmen](/wiki/The_Walkmen).

## Career

### Vampire Weekend

Main article: [Vampire Weekend](/wiki/Vampire_Weekend)

In 2005, Ezra formed the indie rock band [Vampire Weekend](/wiki/Vampire_Weekend). The name of the group was in reference to an unreleased indie film of the same name that Ezra and his friends produced during a vacation. In the film, Ezra portrayed the protagonist, Walcott, a man hell-bent on escaping [Cape Cod](/wiki/Cape_Cod) as he believed vampires were coming. Many songs from the band's eponymous release made reference to the film.

Koenig performing with Vampire Weekend (2014)

Ezra met his bandmates at [Columbia University](/wiki/Columbia_University), prior to 2003. After already having met drummer Chris Tomson, Koenig became acquainted with [Rostam Batmanglij](/wiki/Rostam_Batmanglij). The two immediately bonded over [Radiohead](/wiki/Radiohead) at a party during freshman year and vowed to start a band one day. Rounding out the group was [Chris Baio](/wiki/Chris_Baio), Koenig's suite-mate in his sophomore year, with whom he shared a love of [Destiny's Child](/wiki/Destiny%27s_Child). The group immediately got to work, playing their first show in 2006 at a battle of the bands in a campus basement. They placed third out of four. Later that year, some of their demos appeared online, earning raves from sites like *[Stereogum](/wiki/Stereogum)* and *[Pitchfork](/wiki/Pitchfork_Media)*. Before they knew it, they were selling out shows and appearing on the cover of *[Spin](/wiki/Spin_(magazine))* without even having released an album.

Their [eponymous debut album](/wiki/Vampire_Weekend_(album)) arrived on January 29, 2008, and by the end of the year they had performed on *[Saturday Night Live](/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)*, played for 40,000 fans at England's Glastonbury festival, and sold nearly half a million albums. The album was self-produced while they were working full-time jobs. Since then, the band have released another four albums: *[Contra](/wiki/Contra_(album))* (2010), *[Modern Vampires of the City](/wiki/Modern_Vampires_of_the_City)* (2013), *[Father of the Bride](/wiki/Father_of_the_Bride_(album))* (2019), and *[Only God Was Above Us](/wiki/Only_God_Was_Above_Us)* (2024).

The band has been nominated for multiple [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards). In 2011, *Contra* was nominated for [Best Alternative Music Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Alternative_Music_Album), but *[Brothers](/wiki/Brothers_(The_Black_Keys_album))* by [The Black Keys](/wiki/The_Black_Keys) won the award. In 2014, *Modern Vampires of the City* was nominated in the category and, this time, the band won the award. Following the win, Koenig exclaimed, "I'm the pre-eminent Ezra of my time, and when I die, then we can talk about who comes next." In 2020, *Father of the Bride* earned the band a second win for Best Alternative Music Album and a first nomination for [Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year). "[Harmony Hall](/wiki/Harmony_Hall_(song))" was nominated for [Best Rock Song](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rock_Song).

### *Time Crisis*

Main article: [Time Crisis with Ezra Koenig](/wiki/Time_Crisis_with_Ezra_Koenig)

In 2015 Koenig began hosting his own podcast on [Apple Music](/wiki/Apple_Music).

The show, *Time Crisis*, features Koenig with co-host [Jake Longstreth](/wiki/Jake_Longstreth), an American painter, musician, and internet radio personality. Each episode lasts two hours, during which Koenig and company discuss corporate snack food history, the tasteful palette of ’70s rock (with a focus on the Grateful Dead), [portmanteaus](/wiki/Portmanteaus), and respond to emails from listeners. Often, the show ends with The Top Five where Koenig and Longstreth compare the top five songs on Apple Music this week to that of a week from the past relevant to the discourse of the episode.

[Jonah Hill](/wiki/Jonah_Hill), [Rashida Jones](/wiki/Rashida_Jones), [Mark Ronson](/wiki/Mark_Ronson), [Florence Welch](/wiki/Florence_Welch), [Jamie Foxx](/wiki/Jamie_Foxx), [Tim Heidecker](/wiki/Tim_Heidecker), [James Corden](/wiki/James_Corden), and Koenig's sister Emma Koenig have appeared as guests on the internet radio show.

The show debuted on July 12, 2015, and new episodes air fortnightly. The show is in its eighth season.

### *Neo Yokio*

Main article: [Neo Yokio](/wiki/Neo_Yokio)

Following a hiatus from Vampire Weekend, Koenig wrote and produced an animated comedy-adventure series about a depressive, demon-slaying playboy voiced by [Jaden Smith](/wiki/Jaden_Smith). The series, *[Neo Yokio](/wiki/Neo_Yokio)*, debuted on [Netflix](/wiki/Netflix) in September 2017. The show was written and produced in 2015, with the final sound mix being completed in January 2016; however, due to issues with [Fox](/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company), the show was postponed until finally being picked up by Netflix.

*Neo Yokio* is presented in the style of an [anime](/wiki/Anime) series. However, in an interview with *[Rolling Stone](/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* magazine, Koenig stated that he believes the show is not a traditional anime: "First of all, out of respect for true anime, I've always called Neo Yokio "anime-inspired" – it's a hybrid. But I've always been a fan of anime, and I always wanted to do something that was kind of an homage to it. Maybe a loving parody. Initially the people I was working with thought I should be the voice of the main character, but I was, like, 'I just spent seven years being the frontman of something, using my voice all the time. What I need right now is to slip into the background of something.'"

Upon release, the show's first season received mixed reviews from critics, earning a 54% score on [Rotten Tomatoes](/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes). Shannon Liao of [The Verge](/wiki/The_Verge) criticised the show's story and voice-acting, writing that "the initial glamor of the backdrops and talent involved wears off fast. It has awful voice acting, and a pointless, predictable story that’s only surprising because it’s so willing to hit the bottom of the barrel." *[The New York Times](/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* gave the show a more optimistic review, stating that "if a defensive reading of the line, “Yes, my girlfriend broke up with me to take a finance job in San Francisco,” makes you chuckle, “Neo Yokio” may be for you."

On an episode of *Time Crisis* in early 2018, Koenig spoke about the future of *Neo Yokio*, hinting that "Neo's not dead." However, Netflix canceled the show the following month after one season and a Christmas special.

## Other projects and appearances

In 2009, Koenig provided vocals on the song "Carby" on *[LP](/wiki/LP_(Discovery_album))*, the debut album from [Discovery](/wiki/Discovery_(band)), a group which features Vampire Weekend keyboardist [Rostam Batmanglij](/wiki/Rostam_Batmanglij) and [Ra Ra Riot](/wiki/Ra_Ra_Riot) vocalist Wes Miles. In the same year, he appeared on "[Warm Heart of Africa](/wiki/Warm_Heart_of_Africa)" by [the Very Best](/wiki/The_Very_Best_(band)), "Pyromiltia" by [Theophilus London](/wiki/Theophilus_London), "I Could Be Wrong" by [Chromeo](/wiki/Chromeo) and "Dynamo" by [Abd al Malik](/wiki/Abd_al_Malik_(rapper)).

In 2012, Koenig performed "I Think Ur a Contra" with [Angelique Kidjo](/wiki/Angelique_Kidjo) in her PBS special. He appeared and provided vocals in the music video for [Duck Sauce](/wiki/Duck_Sauce)'s "[Barbra Streisand](/wiki/Barbra_Streisand_(song))". His recording of the song "Papa Hobo", by [Paul Simon](/wiki/Paul_Simon), was included as part of the soundtrack for [Max Winkler](/wiki/Max_Winkler_(director))'s film *[Ceremony](/wiki/Ceremony_(2010_film))*.

In 2013, Koenig was featured in the song "Jessica" by [Major Lazer](/wiki/Major_Lazer).

In 2014, Koenig was featured on "Ezra's Interlude" by Chromeo, as well as "New Dorp, New York" by [SBTRKT](/wiki/SBTRKT). He also appeared in the [HBO](/wiki/HBO) drama series *[Girls](/wiki/Girls_(TV_series))* and [Haim](/wiki/Haim_(band))'s music video for "[My Song 5](/wiki/My_Song_5)" featuring [A$AP Ferg](/wiki/A$AP_Ferg), and performed the voice of Ryland on the animated series *[Major Lazer](/wiki/Major_Lazer_(TV_series))*. At the [86th Academy Awards](/wiki/86th_Academy_Awards), Koenig accompanied [Karen O](/wiki/Karen_O) on backing vocals and acoustic guitar for her performance of the Oscar-nominated song "[The Moon Song](/wiki/The_Moon_Song)" from the film *[Her](/wiki/Her_(2013_film))*.

In 2016, Koenig was credited as a writer and producer on the song "[Hold Up](/wiki/Hold_Up_(Beyonc%C3%A9_song))" by [Beyoncé](/wiki/Beyonc%C3%A9). Due to these contributions, Koenig was nominated for a [Grammy](/wiki/Grammy) Award for the album *[Lemonade](/wiki/Lemonade_(Beyonc%C3%A9_album))*. In January and April 2016, Koenig vocally supported political candidate [Bernie Sanders](/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) by performing at a number of his rallies.

In 2017, Koenig made an appearance in [Charli XCX](/wiki/Charli_XCX)'s music video for "[Boys](/wiki/Boys_(Charli_XCX_song))".

In 2018, Koenig was credited as a songwriter and producer on the single "I Promise You" by [James Corden](/wiki/James_Corden), from the film [*Peter Rabbit*](/wiki/Peter_Rabbit_(film)), which also prominently featured Vampire Weekend's songs. His demo of the song was also released.

In February 2020, Vampire Weekend performed at a rally for U.S Presidential Candidate Bernie Sanders.

In 2022, Koenig was credited as a co-writer and producer of [Liam Gallagher](/wiki/Liam_Gallagher)'s song "Moscow Rules" from his album *[C'mon You Know](/wiki/C%27mon_You_Know)*. He also plays multiple instruments across the album.

Also in 2022, Koenig was featured as a vocalist in [Phoenix](/wiki/Phoenix_(French_band))'s song “[Tonight](/wiki/Tonight_(Phoenix_song)),” which is the third single from the band's seventh album, *[Alpha Zulu](/wiki/Alpha_Zulu)*.

On August 11, 2024, he appeared in the [2024 Summer Olympics closing ceremony](/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics_closing_ceremony) with Phoenix, [Angèle](/wiki/Ang%C3%A8le_(singer)), [Vannda](/wiki/Vannda) and [Kavinsky](/wiki/Kavinsky).

## Influences

Since the age of 9, Koenig has been an admirer of music. In a 2014 interview with [Vulture](/wiki/Vulture.com), he stated, "My dad is a massive music fan, so even just growing up in the house, he was buying new cool music, up through when I was born. So I was very familiar with the [Ramones](/wiki/Ramones), [Run DMC](/wiki/Run_DMC), [Blondie](/wiki/Blondie_(band))—core New York music. My parents would often play the Ramones song, "We’re a Happy Family", so I remember when I was 9 or 10, learning the lyrics and trying to understand." By the time he got to high school, Koenig's taste had slightly changed: "I had some older friends who were really obsessed with hip-hop, [De La Soul](/wiki/De_La_Soul), [Tribe Called Quest](/wiki/Tribe_Called_Quest). There’s something about *[Midnight Marauders](/wiki/Midnight_Marauders)*; it feels like a late night in the city album."

Koenig is also a self-professed fan of bands such as the [Grateful Dead](/wiki/Grateful_Dead) and [Sublime](/wiki/Sublime_(band)), who he believes largely influenced his musical taste.

## Personal life

In August 2018, Koenig's girlfriend, actress [Rashida Jones](/wiki/Rashida_Jones), gave birth to their son. In a 2024 interview published in *[The Guardian](/wiki/The_Guardian)*, Koenig referred to Jones as his wife. In a 2024 interview published in *[People](/wiki/People_(magazine))*, Koenig said that the couple are “married in the eyes of God.”

**Bessemer** is a city in [Jefferson County](/wiki/Jefferson_County,_Alabama), Alabama, United States and a southwestern suburb of [Birmingham](/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama). The population was 26,019 at the [2020 census](/wiki/2020_United_States_census), and was estimated to be 25,079 in 2024. It is within the Birmingham–[Hoover](/wiki/Hoover,_Alabama), AL [Metropolitan statistical area](/wiki/Birmingham_metropolitan_area,_Alabama), of which Jefferson County is the center. It developed rapidly as an industrial city in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## History

The town was founded in the [Reconstruction Era](/wiki/Reconstruction_Era) by the Bessemer Land and Improvement Company, named after [Henry Bessemer](/wiki/Henry_Bessemer) and owned by coal magnate [Henry F. DeBardeleben](/wiki/Henry_F._DeBardeleben). He had inherited [Daniel Pratt](/wiki/Daniel_Pratt_(industrialist))'s investments. The mayor and councilmen voted to incorporate the city of Bessemer on September 9, 1887. Located 15.3 miles southwest of Birmingham, Bessemer grew rapidly and its promoters believed that it might overtake the other city in economic power.

Given the iron ore, coal and limestone deposits in the area, the city became a center of steelmaking from about 1890 through the 20th century. It attracted rural migrants from across the South, as well as European immigrants. By the 1950s, the city was majority African American in population.

The steel industry went through considerable restructuring in the late 20th century, and jobs moved out of the area. Steel is no longer made there. By 2019, Bessemer was named Alabama’s “Worst City to Live in.”

In 2019, it was named Alabama's "Worst City to Live in" by 24/7 Wall Street.

## Geography

Bessemer is located approximately 15.3 miles (24.6 km) southwest of Birmingham.

According to the [United States Census Bureau](/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 40.851 square miles (105.80 km2), of which 40.639 square miles (105.25 km2) is land and 0.212 square miles (0.55 km2) (0.52%) is water.

Bessemer is situated in the midst of the [iron ore](/wiki/Iron_ore) and [limestone](/wiki/Limestone) district of Alabama, in the southern part of Jones Valley (about 3 miles (4.8 km) wide). Iron ore was mined on the hills on the city's southeast side, [coal](/wiki/Coal) was, and still is mined to the north and west, and limestone deposits were also nearby. All three ingredients were necessary for steelmaking, which led to the area becoming a major steel center from about 1890 through the twentieth century. Steel is no longer made within the city limits but is still manufactured in the neighboring city of [Fairfield](/wiki/Fairfield,_Alabama).

Bessemer has a [humid subtropical climate](/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate), abbreviated "Cfa" on climate maps, within the [Köppen Climate Classification](/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_Climate_Classification) system.

## Economy

The Bright Star in Bessemer is Alabama's oldest restaurant

The groundbreaking of the Amazon fulfillment center in Bessemer in 2018.

In 1900, Bessemer ranked eighth in the population in Alabama, second in the amount of capital invested in manufacturing, and fourth in the value of its manufactured product for the year. By 1911, ore mining, iron smelting, and the manufacture of [iron](/wiki/Iron) and [coke](/wiki/Coke_(fuel)) were the chief industries of Bessemer. Truck farming was an important industry, dating from the area's agricultural past.

Both blacks and whites from rural areas were attracted to the city for its new work opportunities. Gradually African Americans moved into industrial jobs and became part of integrated unions. Such jobs enabled many working-class families to enjoy middle-class incomes.

Today, ore mining has ended, as supplies were exhausted. Manufacturing remains a factor, with the U.S. Pipe and Foundry ductile pipe plant on the city's north side. In May 2007, U.S. Pipe announced that it would be building a new $45-million foundry near the current plant. The site was selected, among other reasons, for having available space for potential future expansions. U.S. Pipe is the largest domestic producer of [ductile iron pipe](/wiki/Ductile_iron_pipe) in sizes 4 inch through 64 inch.

Bessemer was once home to a large railroad car manufacturing factory, operated by [Pullman Standard](/wiki/Pullman_Standard) for many decades and later by Trinity Industries. With railroad restructuring in the late 20th century and other manufacturing moving offshore, this plant ceased most production in the 1990s. In 2012, BLOX LLC (bloxbuilt.com) a manufacturer of modular components for healthcare facilities moved into this facility.

The decline of mining and exodus of the steelmaking and railcar manufacturing industries resulted in extensive loss of jobs. Bessemer has lost population since a peak population in 1970. It faced an economic crisis in the early to mid-1980s, as unemployed workers constituted more than one-third of the workforce. Since that time the city has been successful in diversifying its economy, through the efforts of the Bessemer Area Chamber of Commerce and the Bessemer Industrial Development Board. It is recognized for its business growth. In June 2018, [Amazon](/wiki/Amazon_(company)) announced that it would build a new 800,000 square feet (74,000 m2), $325 million fulfillment center in Bessemer, which will initially create 1,500 new jobs.

## Government

Bessemer uses the [mayor](/wiki/Mayor)–[city council](/wiki/City_council) form of government. The council has seven members, elected from [single-member districts](/wiki/Single-member_districts). As of 2016, Kenneth Gulley is mayor, a position elected [at-large](/wiki/At-large). He was first elected in 2010 and reelected to a second term in 2014.

A satellite [Jefferson County](/wiki/Jefferson_County,_Alabama) [Courthouse](/wiki/Courthouse) is located in downtown Bessemer. There is a special county government district, known as the "Bessemer Cutoff", which was established in the middle of the 20th century when Bessemer was a major city in its own right. A separate county government was considered a possibility, but there was not sufficient land area to meet legislative requirements for a county. The "Cutoff" had a separate series of Alabama [license plates](/wiki/License_plate), with a different numeric prefix than the rest of the county.

Bessemer has since been surpassed in size by Birmingham suburbs such as [Hoover](/wiki/Hoover,_Alabama), [Vestavia Hills](/wiki/Vestavia_Hills,_Alabama), and [Homewood](/wiki/Homewood,_Alabama). But Bessemer retains the branch county courthouse to this day. The term "Bessemer Cutoff" continues to be used regularly by area residents.

The [United States Postal Service](/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) operates the Bessemer Post Office.

The state [Alabama Department of Corrections](/wiki/Alabama_Department_of_Corrections) operates the [William E. Donaldson Correctional Facility](/wiki/William_E._Donaldson_Correctional_Facility), a prison for men, in [unincorporated](/wiki/Unincorporated_area) [Jefferson County](/wiki/Jefferson_County,_Alabama), [Alabama](/wiki/Alabama), near Bessemer. The prison includes one of the two Alabama death rows for men.

## Media

*The Western Star* is a weekly newspaper which covers Bessemer and nearby communities.

*[The Birmingham News](/wiki/The_Birmingham_News)* is published three days per week, and also publishes a weekly section devoted to news from Bessemer and nearby communities.

One [radio station](/wiki/Radio_station), [WZGX](/wiki/WZGX) (1450 AM), operates within the city; it broadcasts some Spanish-language programming and music to appeal to the growing Mexican-American population of Jefferson County. All of metro Birmingham's stations are heard in Bessemer, as well as several stations broadcasting from [Tuscaloosa](/wiki/Tuscaloosa,_Alabama).

### Transportation

In 1911, the town was served by five [railroad](/wiki/Railroad) lines: [Alabama Great Southern](/wiki/Alabama_Great_Southern) (Queen & Crescent route), the [Louisville & Nashville Railroad](/wiki/Louisville_%26_Nashville_Railroad), the Kansas City, Memphis & Birmingham ([St. Louis & San Francisco Railroad](/wiki/St._Louis_%26_San_Francisco_Railroad) system), the [Birmingham Southern Railroad](/wiki/Birmingham_Southern_Railroad), and the [Atlanta, Birmingham and Atlantic Railways](/wiki/Atlanta,_Birmingham_and_Atlantic_Railway). Passenger service decreased after people started choosing to travel by automobiles, increasingly so after World War II. In addition, there was widespread restructuring in the railroad industry that also applied to freight lines.

By 2006, the companies listed above had consolidated into [CSX Transportation](/wiki/CSX_Transportation), which has lines to Birmingham and [Brookwood](/wiki/Brookwood,_Alabama), and the [Norfolk Southern Railway](/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Railway), with lines to Birmingham, [Mobile](/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama) and [New Orleans](/wiki/New_Orleans). Birmingham Southern continues in service. A major railroad feature is the "High Line", constructed by Tennessee Coal & Iron (predecessor to [U.S. Steel](/wiki/U.S._Steel)) to ship iron ore from the mines on the city's south side to the steel works in nearby [Fairfield](/wiki/Fairfield,_Alabama). This elevated line traverses the eastern side of the city. Though tracks were removed over much of the High Line when the mines closed, part of the line is still used by the Birmingham Southern. All of the roadbed and bridges remain in place.

Transit service in Bessemer is provided by [Birmingham-Jefferson County Transit Authority](/wiki/Birmingham-Jefferson_County_Transit_Authority), which operates Max Transit bus service.

Bessemer is served by the small [Bessemer Airport](/wiki/Bessemer_Airport) to the southeast of the city. Commercial service in the region is provided by the much larger [Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International Airport](/wiki/Birmingham-Shuttlesworth_International_Airport), located 5 miles (8.0 km) north of downtown Birmingham, about 21 miles away in total.

Major highways in Bessemer include [I-20](/wiki/Interstate_20_(Alabama))/[59](/wiki/Interstate_59_(Alabama)), [I-459](/wiki/Interstate_459), [U.S. Route 11](/wiki/U.S._Route_11_in_Alabama), and [State Route 150](/wiki/State_Route_150_(Alabama)), which connects Bessemer with [Hoover](/wiki/Hoover,_Alabama).

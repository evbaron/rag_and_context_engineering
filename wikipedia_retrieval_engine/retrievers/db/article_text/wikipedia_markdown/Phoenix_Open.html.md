The **Phoenix Open** (branded as the **WM Phoenix Open** for sponsorship reasons) is a professional [golf](/wiki/Golf) tournament on the [PGA Tour](/wiki/PGA_Tour), held in early February at [TPC Scottsdale](/wiki/TPC_Scottsdale) in [Scottsdale, Arizona](/wiki/Scottsdale,_Arizona), United States.

The tournament was originally the **Arizona Open** but was known for most of its history as the Phoenix Open until the investment bank [Friedman Billings Ramsey](/wiki/Friedman_Billings_Ramsey) became the title sponsor in October 2003. For the next six editions, it was known as the **FBR Open**. [Waste Management, Inc.](/wiki/Waste_Management_(corporation)) began its sponsorship in 2010.

The event's relaxed atmosphere, raucous by professional golf standards, has earned it the nickname "The Greatest Show on Grass" and made it one of the most popular events on the PGA Tour calendar.

## History

The Phoenix Open began 94 years ago in 1932 but was discontinued after the 1935 tournament. The rebirth of the Phoenix Open came in 1939 when Bob Goldwater Sr. convinced fellow Thunderbirds to help run the event. The Thunderbirds, a prominent civic organization in [Phoenix](/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona), were less enthusiastic about running the event than he was, leaving Goldwater Sr. to do most of the work in getting a golf open started.

The event was played at the [Phoenix Country Club](/wiki/Phoenix_Country_Club) in Phoenix ([33°29′N 112°04′W / 33.48°N 112.06°W / 33.48; -112.06](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Phoenix_Open&params=33.48_N_112.06_W_)), both in its earlier incarnations and after Goldwater resuscitated it. Beginning in 1955, the Arizona Country Club (also in Phoenix) ([33°29′N 111°58′W / 33.49°N 111.96°W / 33.49; -111.96](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Phoenix_Open&params=33.49_N_111.96_W_)), alternated as event host with Phoenix Country Club; this arrangement lasted until Phoenix Country Club took The Arizona Country Club's turn in 1975 and became the event's permanent home again.

The tournament moved 39 years ago in [1987](/wiki/1987_PGA_Tour) to its current home, the Stadium Course at [TPC Scottsdale](/wiki/TPC_Scottsdale), northeast of downtown Phoenix. The approximate average [elevation](/wiki/Elevation) of the course is 1,530 feet (465 m) above [sea level](/wiki/Sea_level).

The purse was [$](/wiki/United_States_dollar)8.2 million in [2022](/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_PGA_Tour), then increased over 140% to $20 million for [2023](/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_PGA_Tour), with a winner's share of $3.6 million.

## Popularity

The five-day attendance of the tournament is usually around a half million, the best-attended event in golf. In 2016, it set a PGA Tour and Phoenix Open single-day attendance record with 201,003 fans on Saturday, February 6, and a tournament week attendance record of 618,365 fans.

The most popular location for spectators is the par-3 16th hole, nicknamed "The Coliseum." ([33°38′N 111°55′W / 33.64°N 111.91°W / 33.64; -111.91](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Phoenix_Open&params=33.64_N_111.91_W_)) One of the shortest holes on tour at 162 yards (148 m), it is enclosed by a temporary 20,000-seat grandstand. The hole could be described as "one big party," with many students from the nearby [Arizona State University](/wiki/Arizona_State_University) in [Tempe](/wiki/Tempe,_Arizona) in attendance. Poor shots at the 16th hole receive boos because the hole is straightforward by the PGA's standards. Good shots, however, are cheered loudly. Players who make [holes in one](/wiki/Hole_in_one) at the 16th will cause the gallery to erupt, leading to beverages and other objects being tossed in celebrations; [Tiger Woods](/wiki/Tiger_Woods) (1997), [Jarrod Lyle](/wiki/Jarrod_Lyle) (2011), and [Sam Ryder](/wiki/Sam_Ryder_(golfer)) (2022) have each aced the hole on Saturday, creating raucous celebrations at the hole. The anger of a poor shot can lead to tempers flaring, as [Justin Leonard](/wiki/Justin_Leonard) gave obscene gestures to the gallery after a poor shot one year. After 2013, the PGA Tour banned the practice of caddies racing the 150 yards (140 m) from the tee box to the green, citing injury concerns.

Former [Arizona State](/wiki/Arizona_State_Sun_Devils#Golf) players are very popular at the Phoenix Open, with many often wearing a [Pat Tillman](/wiki/Pat_Tillman) jersey when entering the 16th-hole stadium. [Phil Mickelson](/wiki/Phil_Mickelson) and [Jon Rahm](/wiki/Jon_Rahm) are popular there for that reason. In addition to golf, there is a concert/party held in the Scottsdale area called the Birds Nest, at which music artists like [Huey Lewis and the News](/wiki/Huey_Lewis_and_the_News) play.

The Thunderbirds are still highly active in organizing the tournament. The Thunderbirds use portions of the proceeds to fund [Special Olympics](/wiki/Special_Olympics) activities in Phoenix.

## Conflicts with the Super Bowl

Since [1973](/wiki/1973_PGA_Tour), the Phoenix Open has been played on the weekend of the [Super Bowl](/wiki/Super_Bowl). In 1976, coverage of the tournament's final round was joined in progress [immediately after](/wiki/List_of_Super_Bowl_lead-out_programs) CBS's coverage of [Super Bowl X](/wiki/Super_Bowl_X). In [1996](/wiki/1996_PGA_Tour), it was played Wednesday through Saturday, as [Super Bowl XXX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXX) was held at [Sun Devil Stadium](/wiki/Sun_Devil_Stadium) in nearby Tempe. In [2009](/wiki/2009_PGA_Tour), the tournament overlapped with [Super Bowl XLIII](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIII) in [Tampa](/wiki/Raymond_James_Stadium), [Florida](/wiki/Florida), when [Kenny Perry](/wiki/Kenny_Perry) and [Charley Hoffman](/wiki/Charley_Hoffman) went to a playoff. That denied the spectators a chance to watch the beginning of the game on [NBC](/wiki/NBC_Sunday_Night_Football), which featured the local [Arizona Cardinals](/wiki/2008_Arizona_Cardinals_season).

Because of the Super Bowl weekend status, the PGA Tour's television contracts with [CBS](/wiki/PGA_Tour_on_CBS) and [NBC](/wiki/PGA_Tour_on_NBC) include an alternating tournament. Usually, a CBS-broadcast tournament occurs when ABC/ESPN, NBC or Fox televises the Super Bowl; on the other hand, the Phoenix Open airs on NBC when CBS has the Super Bowl, and NBC's [Honda Classic](/wiki/Honda_Classic) aired on CBS during the [2018 Winter Olympics](/wiki/2018_Winter_Olympics).

In 2015, [Super Bowl XLIX](/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIX) was held in Glendale at [University of Phoenix Stadium](/wiki/State_Farm_Stadium) and broadcast by NBC; the network would use sister network [Golf Channel](/wiki/Golf_Channel) (which broadcasts early-round coverage for PGA Tour events) to provide cross-promotional tie-ins for the Super Bowl, including live tapings of [*Feherty*](/wiki/Feherty_(TV_program)) from the [Orpheum Theater](/wiki/Orpheum_Theatre_(Phoenix,_Arizona)).

## Highlights

- 1949: [Ben Hogan](/wiki/Ben_Hogan) loses in a playoff; driving home with his wife; he is involved in a near-fatal accident.
- 1987: [Paul Azinger](/wiki/Paul_Azinger) wins the first edition of the Phoenix Open held at TPC Scottsdale.
- 1990: [Tom Pernice Jr.](/wiki/Tom_Pernice_Jr.) makes the first double eagle in tournament history on the par-5 15th hole.
- 1996: [Grant Waite](/wiki/Grant_Waite) sets the course record of 60 (−11). [Phil Mickelson](/wiki/Phil_Mickelson), an alumnus of nearby Arizona State University, wins the tournament for the first time.
- 1997: [Tiger Woods](/wiki/Tiger_Woods) aces the par-3 16th hole in the third round on Saturday.
- 2001: [Andrew Magee](/wiki/Andrew_Magee) makes the first ace on a par-4 in PGA Tour history on the par-4 17th hole. [Mark Calcavecchia](/wiki/Mark_Calcavecchia) sets the tournament record for lowest aggregate score with 256 (−28), including a course record-tying 60 (−11) in the second round.
- 2011: [Jarrod Lyle](/wiki/Jarrod_Lyle) aces the par-3 16th hole in the first round.
- 2019: [Amy Bockerstette](/wiki/Amy_Bockerstette), a golfer with [Down syndrome](/wiki/Down_syndrome), pars the par-3 16th hole during the pro-am in front of [Gary Woodland](/wiki/Gary_Woodland), a moment that went viral on social media.
- 2020: Woodland and Bockerstette reunite one year after their viral moment with a $25,000 contribution to the I Got This! Foundation, launched the previous year.
- 2022: [Sam Ryder](/wiki/Sam_Ryder_(golfer)) aces on the par-3 16th hole during the third round on Saturday, then in the final round, [Carlos Ortiz](/wiki/Carlos_Ortiz_(golfer)) would have a hole in one at the same hole.
- 2025: [Thomas Detry](/wiki/Thomas_Detry) becomes the first Belgian PGA Tour winner.

## Records

The 17th hole during the 2020 Waste Management Phoenix Open.

The tournament's lowest 72-hole score was set by [Mark Calcavecchia](/wiki/Mark_Calcavecchia) in [2001](/wiki/2001_PGA_Tour) with 256 (–28), which was matched by Mickelson in [2013](/wiki/2013_PGA_Tour). In the second round Calcavecchia scored a 60 (–11), which equalled the lowest score at the Phoenix Open (by [Grant Waite](/wiki/Grant_Waite) in 1996) and subsequently matched by Mickelson in 2005 and 2013. Calcavecchia had 32 [birdies](/wiki/Birdie_(golf)) in the tournament, which was also an all-time record.

There have been only two [double eagles](/wiki/Par_(score)#Albatross) in the history of the Phoenix Open. [Tom Pernice Jr.](/wiki/Tom_Pernice_Jr.) made the first one on the 558-yard (510 m) par-5 15th hole in 1990. [Andrew Magee](/wiki/Andrew_Magee) scored the second on the 332-yard (304 m) par-4 17th hole in 2001, and was the first-ever ace on a par-4 in PGA Tour history.

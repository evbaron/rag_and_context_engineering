**Archibald Henry Macdonald Sinclair, 1st Viscount Thurso**, [KT](/wiki/Knight_Companion_of_the_Order_of_the_Thistle), [CMG](/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George), [PC](/wiki/Privy_Council_(United_Kingdom)) (22 October 1890 – 15 June 1970), known as **Sir Archibald Sinclair** between 1912 and 1952, and often as **Archie Sinclair**, was a British politician and leader of the [Liberal Party](/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK)).

## Background and education

Sinclair was born in 1890 in [Caithness](/wiki/Caithness), Scotland. Sinclair was the son Clarence Granville Sinclair, and his American wife Mabel Sands, daughter of [Mahlon Day Sands](/wiki/Mahlon_Day_Sands), and half-sister of [Ethel Sands](/wiki/Ethel_Sands). His mother died shortly after his birth, and his father in 1895. He was brought up in families including those of his paternal grandfather [Sir Tollemache Sinclair, 3rd Baronet](/wiki/Sir_John_Sinclair,_3rd_Baronet), his uncle [William Macdonald Sinclair](/wiki/William_Sinclair_(archdeacon_of_London)), and [Owen Williams](/wiki/Owen_Williams_(British_Army_officer)), married to his aunt Nina.

Educated at [Eton College](/wiki/Eton_College) and the [Royal Military College, Sandhurst](/wiki/Royal_Military_College,_Sandhurst), Sinclair was commissioned into the [Life Guards](/wiki/Life_Guards_(United_Kingdom)) in 1910. In 1912, he succeeded his grandfather as the fourth Baronet of [Ulbster](/wiki/Ulbster). He became one of the largest landowners in the United Kingdom, owning an estate of about 100,000 acres (40,000 ha) in Caithness. His recreations included [polo](/wiki/Polo) and flying: he was a keen aviator. At this period he made a friend of [Winston Churchill](/wiki/Winston_Churchill).

[Colin Coote](/wiki/Colin_Coote) in his memoirs wrote of Sinclair's "irresistible charm, allied to the face and figure of an Adonis". The handsome Sinclair was at this period thought of as a possible husband for Nellie Hozier, younger sister of [Clementine Churchill](/wiki/Clementine_Churchill).

## Military career

Major Archibald Sinclair, seated to the left, with Commanding Officer Winston Churchill 6th Battalion, [Royal Scots Fusiliers](/wiki/Royal_Scots_Fusiliers) officers in 1916

Shield of Arms of Archibald Henry Macdonald Sinclair, 1st Viscount Thurso, KT, CMG, PC

Sinclair served on the [Western Front](/wiki/Western_Front_(World_War_I)) during [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I), in 1915 as aide de camp to [J. E. B. Seely](/wiki/J._E._B._Seely,_1st_Baron_Mottistone) who commanded the [Canadian Cavalry Brigade](/wiki/Canadian_Cavalry_Brigade). He rose to the rank of [Major](/wiki/Major_(rank)) in the [Guards Machine Gun Regiment](/wiki/Guards_Machine_Gun_Regiment).

After Winston Churchill resigned as [First Lord of the Admiralty](/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty), Sinclair served as his [second-in-command](/wiki/Second-in-command) when Churchill took up command at the beginning of 1916 of the 6th Battalion of the Royal Scots Fusiliers. Churchill arranged the transfer with [Douglas Haig](/wiki/Douglas_Haig,_1st_Earl_Haig), who turned down the request that Seely should be moved too, and also refused him [Edward Spears](/wiki/Edward_Spears). They were stationed in the [Ploegsteert Wood](/wiki/Ploegsteert_Wood) sector of the Western Front.

## Working with Churchill

From 1919 to 1921 Sinclair served as Personal Military Secretary to Churchill, when he returned to the Cabinet as [Secretary of State for War](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War), and then accompanied him to the [Colonial Office](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Colonies) as [Private Secretary](/wiki/Private_Secretary).

Sinclair's duties included acting as liaison for Churchill with the [Secret Intelligence Service](/wiki/Secret_Intelligence_Service). He had dealings with [George Alexander Hill](/wiki/George_Alexander_Hill) and Malcolm Wollcombe, agent in the [Soviet Union](/wiki/Soviet_Union) for [Mansfield Smith-Cumming](/wiki/Mansfield_Smith-Cumming). [Stewart Menzies](/wiki/Stewart_Menzies) who had the official liaison role at the [War Office](/wiki/War_Office) was a personal friend. Sinclair collated [humint](/wiki/Humint) and [technical intelligence](/wiki/Technical_intelligence) for Churchill, for example on [Leonid Krasin](/wiki/Leonid_Krasin). He also assisted in the delicate handling of [Boris Savinkov](/wiki/Boris_Savinkov), who was brought to London. It was Sinclair who introduced the prominent British agent [Sidney Reilly](/wiki/Sidney_Reilly) to Nikolai Alekseyev, intelligence chief of the [White Russian](/wiki/White_movement) leader [Alexander Guchkov](/wiki/Alexander_Guchkov).

## Political career 1922–1939

In 1922, Sinclair entered the [House of Commons](/wiki/British_House_of_Commons) as a Liberal Member of Parliament (MP) for [Caithness and Sutherland](/wiki/Caithness_and_Sutherland_(UK_Parliament_constituency)), supporting [David Lloyd George](/wiki/David_Lloyd_George) and defeating the incumbent Liberal supporter of [H. H. Asquith](/wiki/H._H._Asquith). He rose through the Liberal ranks as the party shrank in Parliament, becoming [Chief Whip](/wiki/Chief_Whip) by 1930. At this period he worked on land policy with Lloyd George, including the "Tartan Book" that addressed [Scottish devolution](/wiki/Scottish_devolution).

In July 1931, a meeting took place at Sinclair's house, where [Oswald Mosley](/wiki/Oswald_Mosley) and [Harold Nicolson](/wiki/Harold_Nicolson) met Churchill, Lloyd George and [Brendan Bracken](/wiki/Brendan_Bracken), to discuss a political alliance. About a month later, the Liberal Party joined the [National Government](/wiki/National_Government_(1931)) of [Ramsay MacDonald](/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald), with Sinclair appointed [Secretary of State for Scotland](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Scotland). He was sworn of the [Privy Council](/wiki/Her_Majesty%27s_Most_Honourable_Privy_Council) at the same time. In 1932, he, together with other Liberal ministers led by [Herbert Samuel](/wiki/Herbert_Samuel), resigned from the government in protest at the [Ottawa Conference](/wiki/Ottawa_Conference) introducing [Imperial Preference](/wiki/Imperial_Preference).

In the [1935 general election](/wiki/1935_United_Kingdom_general_election), Herbert Samuel lost his seat, and Sinclair became the Liberal Party's leader at the head of 20 MPs. During the [Abdication Crisis](/wiki/Abdication_Crisis) of 1936, his name was put forward as a possible leader of a government that might be formed if [Edward VIII](/wiki/Edward_VIII) held onto the throne against the wishes of the [Baldwin administration](/wiki/National_Government_(1935%E2%80%931937)). Churchill came to support the King's position, and [Lord Beaverbrook](/wiki/Lord_Beaverbrook) entertained the idea of Sinclair as Prime Minister. Both [Clement Attlee](/wiki/Clement_Attlee) for Labour and Sinclair for the Liberals, however, in late November ruled out forming a government under those circumstances.

Sinclair consistently opposed the [continental dictatorships](/wiki/European_interwar_dictatorships) and kept the [National Liberals](/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_(UK,_1931)) at arms length. He supported the [League of Nations](/wiki/League_of_Nations) and [collective security](/wiki/Collective_security). He backed, as did Attlee, Churchill's book *[Arms and the Covenant](/wiki/Arms_and_the_Covenant)*. He joined the [Anti-Nazi Council](/wiki/Anti-Nazi_Council) of Eugen Spier, with Churchill and [Violet Bonham Carter](/wiki/Violet_Bonham_Carter), [Margaret Bondfield](/wiki/Margaret_Bondfield) and [Hugh Dalton](/wiki/Hugh_Dalton). Public opinion at this point of the later 1930s by no means agreed, and [John Alfred Spender](/wiki/John_Alfred_Spender) attacked Sinclair in *[The Times](/wiki/The_Times)* on foreign policy, claiming that he, like the [League of Nations Union](/wiki/League_of_Nations_Union), wished for war with the [Axis powers](/wiki/Axis_powers).

At the time of the [Munich Crisis](/wiki/Munich_Crisis) in September 1938, Sinclair was one of the anti-appeasement group who gathered around Churchill, with [Leo Amery](/wiki/Leo_Amery), [Robert Boothby](/wiki/Robert_Boothby), [Robert Cecil](/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_1st_Viscount_Cecil_of_Chelwood), [Harold Macmillan](/wiki/Harold_Macmillan) and Harold Nicolson. During parliamentary debate over the [Munich Agreement](/wiki/Munich_Agreement) he attacked Prime Minister [Neville Chamberlain](/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain) for "wilting" to [Nazi Germany](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) and tossing "justice and respect for treaties... to the winds." On a personal level, Violet Bonham Carter was a frequent guest of the Sinclairs at Dalnawillan Lodge in the [Flow Country](/wiki/Flow_Country), as were [Harcourt Johnstone](/wiki/Harcourt_Johnstone) and Lady Gwendoline Churchill, wife of [Jack Churchill](/wiki/Jack_Churchill_(1880%E2%80%931947)) and Winston's sister-in-law. Bonham Carter was a Liberal activist, close follower of Churchill, anti-appeaser and [League of Nations Union](/wiki/League_of_Nations_Union) member.

## Second World War

When Churchill formed an [all-party coalition government](/wiki/Churchill_war_ministry) in 1940, Sinclair entered the cabinet as [Secretary of State for Air](/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Air). During the [May 1940 British war cabinet crisis](/wiki/War_cabinet_crisis,_May_1940) after the [fall of France](/wiki/Battle_of_France) he sided with Churchill against [Lord Halifax](/wiki/Edward_Wood,_1st_Earl_of_Halifax)'s plan to seek a negotiated settlement with Nazi Germany mediated by [Fascist Italy](/wiki/Fascist_Italy_(1922%E2%80%931943)).

Sinclair's first task was to work with the [Royal Air Force](/wiki/Royal_Air_Force) in planning the [Battle of Britain](/wiki/Battle_of_Britain). Towards the end of the war, he found himself at odds with Churchill, arguing against [Bomber Harris](/wiki/Arthur_Harris)' strategy for the [Bombing of Dresden](/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden) and other German cities. He was not a strong political personality: [Max Hastings](/wiki/Max_Hastings) reports that he was often regarded as the "[Head of School](/wiki/Head_girl_and_head_boy)'s [fag](/wiki/Fagging)" to Churchill, and as the political mouthpiece rather than the master of [Air Chief Marshals](/wiki/Air_Chief_Marshals) [Portal](/wiki/Charles_Portal,_1st_Viscount_Portal_of_Hungerford) and [Harris](/wiki/Arthur_Harris). However, in 1942 he did convince Churchill and his cabinet not to carry out reprisals on German villages for [war atrocities](/wiki/German_war_crimes) such as the [Lidice massacre](/wiki/Lidice_massacre).

Sinclair remained a minister until May 1945, when the coalition ended. In the [1945 general election](/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election), he lost his seat. His margin of defeat was narrow: he came in third place, with the victor [Eric Gandar Dower](/wiki/Eric_Gandar_Dower) having 61 votes more.

## Last years

At the [1950 general election](/wiki/1950_United_Kingdom_general_election), Sinclair again stood for his old seat, coming second. In 1952, the year of his first stroke, he accepted elevation to the [House of Lords](/wiki/House_of_Lords) as **Viscount Thurso** of Ulbster in the [County of Caithness](/wiki/Caithness). A more serious stroke in 1959 left him largely bedridden and in a state of precarious health, until he died at his home in [Twickenham](/wiki/Twickenham) in 1970.

## Family

In 1918 Sinclair married Marigold Forbes (1897–1975), daughter of Lieutenant-Colonel James Stewart Forbes and [Lady Angela Forbes](/wiki/Lady_Angela_Forbes). They had four children:

1. Catherine (1919–2007), married 1957 [Kazimierz Zielenkiewicz](/wiki/Kazimierz_Zielenkiewicz).
2. Elizabeth (1921–1994), married in 1942 Archibald Michael Lyle, son of [Sir Archibald Lyle, 2nd Baronet](/wiki/Sir_Archibald_Lyle,_2nd_Baronet), and was mother of [Veronica Linklater](/wiki/Veronica_Linklater).
3. [Robin](/wiki/Robin_Sinclair,_2nd_Viscount_Thurso) (1922–1995), married 1952 Margaret Beaumont Robertson, and was father of [John Sinclair, 3rd Viscount Thurso](/wiki/John_Sinclair,_3rd_Viscount_Thurso)
4. Angus John (1925–2003), married firstly in 1955 Pamela Karen Bower, daughter of [Dallas Bower](/wiki/Dallas_Bower) (dissolved 1967), secondly in 1968 Judith Anne Percy (dissolved 1992), thirdly in 1992 Kate Fry.

## Legacy

Nameplate from SR Battle of Britain locomotive 34059 "Sir Archibald Sinclair"

The [Southern Railway](/wiki/Southern_Railway_(UK)) named a [Battle of Britain Class Light Pacific steam locomotive](/wiki/SR_West_Country_and_Battle_of_Britain_classes) "Sir Archibald Sinclair". The ceremonial naming of the locomotive was performed by Sir Archibald himself at [Waterloo station](/wiki/London_Waterloo_station) on 24 February 1948. The locomotive is preserved on the Bluebell Railway.

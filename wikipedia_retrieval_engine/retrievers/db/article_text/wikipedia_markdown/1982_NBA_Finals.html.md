# 1982 NBA Finals

## Infobox

- **Team:** Team Coach Wins Los Angeles Lakers Pat Riley 4 Philadelphia 76ers Billy Cunningham 2
- **Dates:** May 27–June 8
- **MVP:** Magic Johnson (Los Angeles Lakers)
- **Hall of Famers:** Lakers: Kareem Abdul-Jabbar (1995) Michael Cooper (2024) Magic Johnson (2002) Bob McAdoo (2000) Jamaal Wilkes (2012) 76ers: Maurice Cheeks (2018) Julius Erving (1993) Bobby Jones (2019) Coaches: Billy Cunningham (1986, player) Pat Riley (2008) Officials: Hugh Evans (2022) Darell Garretson (2016) Earl Strom (1995)

- **Western finals:** Lakers defeated Spurs, 4–0

The **1982 NBA World Championship Series** was the championship round of the [National Basketball Association](/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) (NBA)'s [1981–82 season](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_NBA_season), the top level of competition in men's professional [basketball](/wiki/Basketball) in North America. The series saw the [Western Conference](/wiki/Western_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Los Angeles Lakers](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) face the [Eastern Conference](/wiki/Eastern_Conference_(NBA)) champion [Philadelphia 76ers](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Philadelphia_76ers_season). It was a rematch of the [1980 NBA Finals](/wiki/1980_NBA_Finals). The Lakers won in six games, clinching their eighth championship.

The 1982 NBA Finals documentary "Something to Prove" recaps all the action of this series. It was the last NBA video documentary to exclusively use film in all on-court action. [Dick Stockton](/wiki/Dick_Stockton) narrated the documentary, with the condensed [USA Network](/wiki/NBA_on_USA) version narrated by [Al Albert](/wiki/Al_Albert_(sportscaster)).

This was the first Finals to be claimed on the winning team's home court since [1977](/wiki/1977_NBA_Finals). The series ended June 8, later than any previous NBA Finals. The previous record was June 7, 1978. This record was eclipsed two years later when the finals ended on June 12, 1984.

## Background

### Los Angeles Lakers

Main article: [1981–82 Los Angeles Lakers season](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season)

The Lakers were stunned in the [1981 NBA playoffs](/wiki/1981_NBA_playoffs) by the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_Houston_Rockets_season) in a 3-game mini-series. The previous season saw the Lakers in a state of uncertainty, after [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) missed 45 games due to a knee injury. Their problems continued early in the new season, and with the team at 7–4 the Lakers decided to fire head coach [Paul Westhead](/wiki/Paul_Westhead). Taking over as head coach was [Pat Riley](/wiki/Pat_Riley), and his promotion to the job led to the birth of the [Showtime](/wiki/Showtime_(basketball)) offense.

With a healthy Johnson and the additions of [Kurt Rambis](/wiki/Kurt_Rambis) and [Bob McAdoo](/wiki/Bob_McAdoo), the Lakers rallied to finish with a 57–25 record, best in the Western Conference. They were even more flawless in the playoffs as they both swept the [Phoenix Suns](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Phoenix_Suns_season) in the Western Conference semifinals, and the [San Antonio Spurs](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_San_Antonio_Spurs_season) in the Western Conference finals.

### Philadelphia 76ers

Main article: [1981–82 Philadelphia 76ers season](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Philadelphia_76ers_season)

Like the Lakers, the 76ers were coming off a heartbreaking playoff defeat, as they blew a 3–1 lead and lost to the eventual champion [Boston Celtics](/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_Boston_Celtics_season) in the Eastern Conference finals. Not much was changed for the 76ers roster-wise and record-wise in the new season, however, as the team finished second behind the Celtics in the Atlantic Division for the third straight year.

Due to a then-existing rule where division winners would earn a first-round bye, the 76ers were forced to play a best-of-three miniseries, even though their 58–24 record was three games better than the Central Division champion [Milwaukee Bucks](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Milwaukee_Bucks_season). Nevertheless, Philadelphia easily swept the [Atlanta Hawks](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Atlanta_Hawks_season) 2–0 in the first round, then ousted the Bucks in the next round 4–2. In the Eastern Conference finals, the 76ers blew out the [Celtics](/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_Boston_Celtics_season) twice at [The Spectrum](/wiki/Spectrum_(arena)) to take a 3–1 lead, only to lose the next two games in a harrowing replay of the 1981 playoffs. But led by [Andrew Toney](/wiki/Andrew_Toney)'s 34 points, the 76ers exorcised the demons of 1981 by blowing out the Celtics 120–106 in Game 7. As time wound down, the Boston Garden crowd began to chant "Beat L.A.!", encouraging the 76ers to defeat the hated Lakers in the championship round.

Fresh from holding off the Celtics in the conference finals, the Sixers worked their offense to precision and held a 15-point lead midway through the third quarter 83–68. But, then, the Lakers began to turn it up on defense and the result was many fast breaks. The Lakers went on a 40–9 run over the game's next 11 minutes. Wilkes scored 10, Kareem and McAdoo scored 8 each, Nixon and Cooper scored 7 each, and Nixon had 4 assists during the run, on the way to a 124-117 Game 1 win, thereby stealing the home-court advantage.

After the game, Sixers coach [Billy Cunningham](/wiki/Billy_Cunningham) commented that the Sixers weren't affected that much by the trapping Laker defense, just cold shooting and sloppy play. However, he also questioned whether or not it was a "zone defense", which was illegal at the time.

In this game, Laker coach [Pat Riley](/wiki/Pat_Riley) took a different defensive approach, assigning [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) to cover [Julius Erving](/wiki/Julius_Erving) straight-up on defense. While Magic couldn't match the Doctor's athleticism, the move did keep Erving from the offensive boards.

In Game 2 that wasn't quite enough, as Erving brought the Sixers back with 24 points and 16 rebounds, mostly defensive. [Billy Cunningham](/wiki/Billy_Cunningham) used all his centers, [Caldwell Jones](/wiki/Caldwell_Jones), [Darryl Dawkins](/wiki/Darryl_Dawkins) and [Earl Cureton](/wiki/Earl_Cureton) at different points to guard [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar). The Sixers got 38 offensive rebounds for 50 second-chance points, while the Lakers only had six offensive boards.

The Sixers used that advantage to take a 110–94 win that evened the series. In a balanced scoring attack, [Maurice Cheeks](/wiki/Maurice_Cheeks) had 19 points and eight assists, Jones added 12 points and 11 rebounds, and [Bobby Jones](/wiki/Bobby_Jones_(basketball,_born_1951)) and [Clint Richardson](/wiki/Clint_Richardson) each scored 10. This was the Lakers first loss in the 1982 post season.

The Lakers controlled the tempo in Game 4 by going to their half-court game, passing down low to [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar). On the defensive end, they kept up the pressure with their zone trap. The Lakers went up, three games to one, with a 111–101 win. [Jamaal Wilkes](/wiki/Jamaal_Wilkes) and [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson) had 24 points each, while Abdul-Jabbar added 22 and [Bob McAdoo](/wiki/Bob_McAdoo) 19 off the bench. Hard-charging bruiser [Kurt Rambis](/wiki/Kurt_Rambis) pulled down 11 rebounds, matching Abdul-Jabbar's output.

Back in Philadelphia, the Sixers took out their frustrations and destroyed the Lakers, 135–102. [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) was held to just six points, a career playoff low, thanks to the spirited defense of [Darryl Dawkins](/wiki/Darryl_Dawkins). In the midst of the offensive explosion, Dawkins also contributed 20 points and seven rebounds to the effort.

This was the Lakers' most lopsided playoff defeat up to this time. It was eclipsed when they were routed 148–114 by the [Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) in Game 1 of the [1985 Finals](/wiki/1985_NBA_Finals). Coincidentally, the Lakers also won that series in six games despite the blowout loss.

| [The Forum](/wiki/The_Forum_(Inglewood)), [Inglewood](/wiki/Inglewood,_California), [California](/wiki/California) **Attendance:** 17,505 **Referees:**    - No. 10 Darell Garretson - No. 14 Jack Madden |

The Sixers' strong showing in Game 5 gave them hope for Game 6 in the Forum, but the Lakers got the early lead and were up, 66–57, at the half.

In the third period, the Sixers' defense turned it up a notch. They held Los Angeles to 20 points for the quarter and several times cut the lead to one point. Super-sub [Bob McAdoo](/wiki/Bob_McAdoo), known more for his offense, made a key defensive play late in the third when he blocked a [Julius Erving](/wiki/Julius_Erving) layup on a breakaway that would have given the Sixers the lead.

The Lakers came back and surged early in the fourth period to boost their lead to 11. Erving, who led all scorers with 30 points, and [Andrew Toney](/wiki/Andrew_Toney), who had 29, responded by trimming the lead to 103–100 with about four minutes left, but then [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) scored and was fouled and made the free throw to put Los Angeles up by six. Moments later, Wilkes got a breakaway layup to close it out, 114–104.

[Jamaal Wilkes](/wiki/Jamaal_Wilkes) led the Lakers with 27 points, and [Magic Johnson](/wiki/Magic_Johnson), with 13 points, 13 rebounds and 13 assists, was named the series MVP. McAdoo, who had 16 points, nine rebounds and three blocks, was pretty much reborn as a player in this series after being cast off by several teams as a selfish, non-team player.

Aside from the Doctor's and Toney's efforts, no one else stepped up for the Sixers. [Darryl Dawkins](/wiki/Darryl_Dawkins) fouled out and only had 10 points and one rebound in 20 minutes played. Dawkins would soon be shipped to the [New Jersey Nets](/wiki/New_Jersey_Nets), and the 76ers acquired the final piece of their championship puzzle: [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone), an MVP center from the [Houston Rockets](/wiki/Houston_Rockets).

## Television coverage

Unlike previous years, where weeknight games were shown on tape delay, all games in the Finals were televised live by [CBS](/wiki/NBA_on_CBS). As a compromise to CBS to allow the live telecasts, the NBA returned the start of its season to late October after starting it earlier in October the previous two seasons, meaning the finals would start after the conclusion of the mid-May [sweeps period](/wiki/Sweeps_period). The later date also eliminated the back-to-back games on [Mother's Day](/wiki/Mother%27s_Day) weekend, which was used in 1980 and 1981 to avert another tape delay broadcast.

This was also the first of nine straight NBA finals (1982–1990) that Dick Stockton would call for CBS Sports. Stockton also announced the starting lineups of the 1982 NBA Finals in lieu of P.A. announcers [Dave Zinkoff](/wiki/Dave_Zinkoff) (for the 76ers) and Larry McKay (for the Lakers; McKay would be replaced the next season by Lawrence Tanter).

## Aftermath

Both teams would meet in the Finals again in [the next year](/wiki/1983_NBA_Finals). The Sixers, bolstered by the addition of league MVP [Moses Malone](/wiki/Moses_Malone), won 65 games, and steamrolled through the playoffs, in which they lost only once (completing Malone's famous "Fo, Fo, Fo" prediction, stating that the Sixers needed to win 4 games in each of the three series) in route to their third NBA title overall (they won in [1955](/wiki/1955_NBA_Finals) as the [Syracuse Nationals](/wiki/Syracuse_Nationals), and in [1967](/wiki/1967_NBA_Finals)). The Lakers finished the regular season with 58 wins, but were overmatched by the hungrier Sixers in the Finals, and swept. Then-rookie and future [Hall of Famer](/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame) [James Worthy](/wiki/James_Worthy) did not play in the series because of a late-season leg injury.

The Lakers would win their next championship [three years later](/wiki/1985_NBA_Finals) over their archrival in the [Boston Celtics](/wiki/Boston_Celtics) in six games.

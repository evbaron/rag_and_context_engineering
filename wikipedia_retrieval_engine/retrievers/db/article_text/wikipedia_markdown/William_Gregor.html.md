# William Gregor

## Infobox

- **Born:** ( 1761-12-25 ) 25 December 1761 Trewarthenick, Cornwall
- **Died:** 11 June 1817 (1817-06-11) (aged 55) Creed, Cornwall, UK
- **Alma mater:** Bristol Grammar School then St John's College, Cambridge
- **Known for:** Titanium
- **Fields:** Mineralogy

**William Gregor** (25 December 1761 – 11 June 1817) was a British [clergyman](/wiki/Clergyman) and [mineralogist](/wiki/Mineralogist) who discovered the elemental metal [Titanium](/wiki/Titanium).

## Early years

He was born at the [Trewarthenick Estate](/wiki/Trewarthenick_Estate) in [Cornwall](/wiki/Cornwall), the son of Francis Gregor and Mary Copley and the brother of [Francis Gregor](/wiki/Francis_Gregor_(MP)), MP for Cornwall. He was educated at [Bristol Grammar School](/wiki/Bristol_Grammar_School), where he became interested in [chemistry](/wiki/Chemistry), then after two years with a private tutor entered [St John's College, Cambridge](/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Cambridge), graduating BA in 1784 and MA in 1787. He was ordained in the [Church of England](/wiki/Church_of_England). He became [vicar](/wiki/Vicar) of St Mary's Church [Diptford](/wiki/Diptford) near [Totnes](/wiki/Totnes), [Devon](/wiki/Devon). He married Charlotte Anne Gwatkin in 1790 and they had one daughter, Charlotte-Anne Gregor.

## Discovery of titanium

After a brief interval at [Bratton Clovelly](/wiki/Bratton_Clovelly), in 1793 William and his family moved permanently to the [rectory](/wiki/Rectory) of [Creed](/wiki/Creed,_Cornwall) in [Cornwall](/wiki/Cornwall). Here he continued his remarkably accurate chemical analysis of [minerals](/wiki/Mineral), most of which came from Cornwall, such as the [zeolites](/wiki/Zeolites) found in [gabbro](/wiki/Gabbro) on [The Lizard](/wiki/The_Lizard). He also analysed [wavellite](/wiki/Wavellite), [tourmaline](/wiki/Tourmaline), and the [uranium](/wiki/Uranium) minerals [torbernite](/wiki/Torbernite) and [autunite](/wiki/Autunite), the arsenate [scorodite](/wiki/Scorodite), the [lead](/wiki/Lead) mineral [mimetite](/wiki/Mimetite) and the [nickel](/wiki/Nickel) mineral [niccolite](/wiki/Niccolite), and others. But he is best known for one of his earliest discoveries: in 1791, while analysing the minerals in a [black sand](/wiki/Black_sand) he had discovered in the [Manaccan](/wiki/Manaccan) valley, he isolated the [calx](/wiki/Calx) of an unknown metal which he named *manaccanite*. Later in 1791, [Martin Heinrich Klaproth](/wiki/Martin_Heinrich_Klaproth) discovered what is now known as the [transition metal](/wiki/Transition_metal), [titanium](/wiki/Titanium) in the mineral [rutile](/wiki/Rutile). Believing this to be a new discovery, Klaproth named it *titanium* after the [Titans](/wiki/Titan_(mythology)) of [Greek Mythology](/wiki/Greek_Mythology), but eventually it was clarified that Gregor made the discovery first. Gregor was credited with the discovery, but the element kept the name chosen by Klaproth. Gregor later found titanium in [corundum](/wiki/Corundum) from [Tibet](/wiki/Tibet), and in a [tourmaline](/wiki/Tourmaline) from a local tin mine.

## Death and legacy

Gregor was made an honorary member of the [Geological Society of London](/wiki/Geological_Society_of_London) on its inception in 1807, and was a founding member of the [Royal Geological Society of Cornwall](/wiki/Royal_Geological_Society_of_Cornwall) in 1814. His friends and correspondents included [John Hawkins](/wiki/John_Hawkins_(geologist)), [Philip Rashleigh](/wiki/Philip_Rashleigh_(1729-1811)) and [John Ayrton Paris](/wiki/John_Ayrton_Paris). Never letting his scientific work interfere with his pastoral duties, he was also a distinguished landscape painter, etcher and musician. He died of [tuberculosis](/wiki/Tuberculosis) on 11 June 1817 and was buried at nearby Cornelly church.

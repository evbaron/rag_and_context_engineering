**Molde** (Norwegian pronunciation: [[ˈmɔ̂ɫdə]](/wiki/Help:IPA/Norwegian) [ⓘ](/wiki/File:Molde.ogg)) is a [town](/wiki/List_of_towns_and_cities_in_Norway), an [Urban Area](/wiki/Urban_Area), and the seat of [Molde Municipality](/wiki/Molde_Municipality) in [Møre og Romsdal](/wiki/M%C3%B8re_og_Romsdal) county, [Norway](/wiki/Norway). It is located in the [traditional district](/wiki/Traditional_districts_of_Norway) of [Romsdal](/wiki/Romsdal). It is located on the [Romsdal Peninsula](/wiki/Romsdal_Peninsula), surrounding the [Fannefjord](/wiki/Fannefjord) and [Moldefjord](/wiki/Moldefjord).

Molde has a [maritime](/wiki/Oceanic_climate), [temperate climate](/wiki/Temperate_climate), with cool-to-warm summers, and relatively mild winters. It is nicknamed *The Town of Roses*.

The settlement emerged as a shipping port for lumber to the Netherlands in the late 1500s. Formal trading rights were granted at some point before 1604, and the town was incorporated through a [royal charter](/wiki/Royal_charter) in 1742. [Bolsøy Municipality](/wiki/Bols%C3%B8y_Municipality), which later merged with Molde town, was established on 1 January 1838 (see [formannskapsdistrikt](/wiki/Formannskapsdistrikt) law).

The town continued to grow throughout the 17th to 19th centuries, becoming a centre for the Norwegian textile and garment industry, as well as the administrative centre for the region, and was a major tourist destination until [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I). After [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), Molde experienced accelerated growth, merging with [Bolsøy Municipality](/wiki/Bols%C3%B8y_Municipality) and parts of [Veøy Municipality](/wiki/Ve%C3%B8y_Municipality) and [Nord-Aukra Municipality](/wiki/Nord-Aukra_Municipality) on 1 January 1964, making the municipality much larger than the urban area of the town of Molde. On 1 January 2020, the municipality was expanded again through the incorporation of the neighboring [Midsund Municipality](/wiki/Midsund_Municipality) and [Nesset Municipality](/wiki/Nesset_Municipality), making the area of the municipality quite large in comparison to the town.

The town has a population of 22,410 (2024) and a population density of 2,364 inhabitants per square kilometre (6,120/sq mi).

## History

Main article: [History of Molde](/wiki/History_of_Molde)

Illustration of Molde, painting by [Nico Wilhelm Jungmann](/wiki/Nico_Wilhelm_Jungmann), 1904

The city's current location dates from the late Medieval times but is preceded by an earlier [Medieval](/wiki/Medieval) township on [Veøya](/wiki/Ve%C3%B8ya), an island to the south of present-day Molde. The settlement at Veøya probably dates from the [Migration Period](/wiki/Migration_Period), but is first mentioned in the sagas by [Snorri Sturluson](/wiki/Snorri_Sturluson) as the location of the *Battle of [Sekken](/wiki/Sekken)* in 1162, where [king Håkon](/wiki/Haakon_II_of_Norway) the Broad-shouldered was killed fighting the aristocrat [Erling Skakke](/wiki/Erling_Skakke), during the [Norwegian civil wars](/wiki/Civil_war_era_in_Norway).
However, settlement in the area can be traced much further back in time—evidence given by two rock slabs carved with [petroglyphs](/wiki/Petroglyphs) found at Bjørset, west of the city center.

At the eve of the 15th century, the influence of Veøya waned, and the island was eventually deserted.

Originating from the two farms Reknes and Molde (later Moldegård), a minor port called *Moldefjæra* (*Molde Landing*) emerged around 1600, based on trade with timber and herring.

Molde's main street and commercial center. Molde Cathedral (orange roof on the far right) with its freestanding bell tower replaced the church that was destroyed during World War II

The town gained formal trading rights before 1604 under the supervision of [Trondheim](/wiki/Trondheim_(city)). After the [Treaty of Copenhagen](/wiki/Treaty_of_Copenhagen_(1660)) in 1660, Molde became the [administrative center](/wiki/Administrative_center) of [Romsdal amt](/wiki/M%C3%B8re_og_Romsdal), and was incorporated as a [city](/wiki/Kj%C3%B8pstad) through a [royal charter](/wiki/Royal_charter) in 1742. Molde continued to grow throughout the 18th and 19th centuries, becoming a center for the Norwegian textile and garment industry. Tourism later became a major industry, and Molde saw notabilities such as the German emperor [Wilhelm II](/wiki/William_II,_German_Emperor) of Germany and the [Prince of Wales](/wiki/Prince_of_Wales) as regular summer visitors. Molde consisted of luxurious hotels surrounding an idyllic township with quaint, wooden houses, lush gardens, and parks, esplanades, and pavilions, earning it the nickname *the Town of Roses*. This was interrupted when one-third of the city was destroyed in a fire on 21 January 1916. However, Molde recovered and continued to grow in the economically difficult interbellum period.

A second fire, or series of fires, struck from the [German](/wiki/Nazi_Germany) air-raids in [April and May 1940](/wiki/Operation_Weser%C3%BCbung), which destroyed about two-thirds of the town. Molde was in effect the capital of Norway for a week after King Haakon, Crown Prince Olav, and members of the government and parliament arrived at Molde on April 23, after a dramatic flight from Oslo. They were put up at Glomstua, then at the western outskirts of the town, and experienced the bombing raids personally. The Norwegian gold reserve was also conveyed to Molde, and was hidden in a clothing factory.

However, German intelligence was well aware of this, and on April 25 the [Luftwaffe](/wiki/Luftwaffe) initiated a series of air-raids. For a week the air-raid siren on the chimney of the dairy building announced the repeated attacks. April 29 turned out to be the worst day in the history of Molde, as the city was transformed into a sea of flames by incendiary bombs. Until then the church had escaped undamaged, but in the final sortie a firebomb became stuck high up in the tower, and the wooden church was obliterated by fire.

After World War II, Molde experienced tremendous growth. As the modernization of the Norwegian society accelerated in the post-reconstruction years, Molde became a center for not only administrative and public services, but also academic resources and industrial output. After the consolidation of the town itself and its adjacent communities in 1964, Molde became a modern city, encompassing most branches of employment, from farming and fisheries to industrial production, banking, higher education, tourism, commerce, health care, and civil administration.

### Municipality

The town of Molde was established as an urban municipality on 1 January 1838 (see [formannskapsdistrikt](/wiki/Formannskapsdistrikt) law). It was surrounded by the rural [Bolsøy Municipality](/wiki/Bols%C3%B8y_Municipality). On 1 July 1915, a part of Bolsøy Municipality (population: 183) was transferred into the city of Molde. On 1 January 1952, another part of Bolsøy Municipality (population: 1,913) was transferred into the city of Molde.

During the 1960s, there were many municipal mergers across Norway due to the work of the [Schei Committee](/wiki/Schei_Committee). On 1 January 1964, the [Molde (town)](/wiki/Molde_(town)) (population: 8,289) merged with the [Sekken](/wiki/Sekken), [Veøya](/wiki/Ve%C3%B8ya), and [Nesjestranda](/wiki/Nesjestranda) parts of [Veøy Municipality](/wiki/Ve%C3%B8y_Municipality) (population: 756), all of [Bolsøy Municipality](/wiki/Bols%C3%B8y_Municipality) (population: 7,996), and the Mordal area of [Nord-Aukra Municipality](/wiki/Nord-Aukra_Municipality) (population: 77) to form a new, larger Molde Municipality.

On 1 January 2020, the neighbouring [Midsund Municipality](/wiki/Midsund_Municipality) and [Nesset Municipality](/wiki/Nesset_Municipality) merged with Molde to form a much larger Molde Municipality.

## Name

The municipality (originally the town and [parish](/wiki/Prestegjeld)) is named after the old *Molde* farmstead ([Old Norse](/wiki/Old_Norse_language): *Moldar*) since this was where the town was built. The name's origin is somewhat uncertain. It could be the plural form is *[mold](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mold#Old_Norse)* which means "fertile soil" or "earth". The other possibility is that it comes from the word *[moldr](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/moldr#Old_Norse)* which means "skull" or "[mold](/wiki/Molding_(process))" (referring to the rounded peaks in [Moldemarka](/wiki/Moldemarka)). Pronunciation varies between the standard *Molde* and the rural *Molle* (pronounced with thick "l"). A person from Molde will refer to themself as a *Moldenser*.

## Geography

Molde Municipality includes part of the [Romsdal peninsula](/wiki/Romsdal_peninsula) as well as many islands including the islands of [Otrøya](/wiki/Otr%C3%B8ya) and [Midøya](/wiki/Mid%C3%B8ya). To the southeast, Molde Municipality stretches about 75 kilometres (47 mi) inland. The [town of Molde](/wiki/Molde_(town)) consists of a 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) long and 1 to 2 kilometres (0.62 to 1.24 mi) wide strip of urban land running east–west along the north shore of the [Moldefjord](/wiki/Moldefjord), an arm of the [Romsdalsfjord](/wiki/Romsdalsfjord), on the [Romsdal peninsula](/wiki/Romsdal_peninsula). The city is sheltered by [Bolsøya](/wiki/Bols%C3%B8ya) and the [Molde archipelago](/wiki/Molde_archipelago), a chain of low-lying islands and islets, to the south, and the wood-clad hills of [Moldemarka](/wiki/Moldemarka) to the north. The city centre is located just west of the river [Moldeelva](/wiki/Moldeelva), which runs into the city from the north, originating in the [Moldevatnet](/wiki/Moldevatnet) lake, through the valley Moldedalen. Despite the river being minor and seasonal, it supported several [sawmills](/wiki/Sawmill) in the 16th and 17th centuries. This gave rise to the original town itself through a combination of a good harbour, proximity to the sea routes, vast timber resources, and a river capable of supporting mills. In 1909, the river housed the first [hydro electric](/wiki/Hydro_electric) [power plant](/wiki/Power_plant) capable of providing sufficient electricity for the city, and the upper reaches of the river still provide drinking water for most of the city.

The highest point in the municipality is the 1,964.92-metre (6,446.6 ft) tall mountain [Kleneggen](/wiki/Kleneggen). Its panoramic view of some 222 partly snow-clad peaks, usually referred to as the [Molde panorama](/wiki/Molde_panorama), is one of Molde's main attractions, and has drawn tourists to the city since the 19th century. Molde is nicknamed the *Town of Roses*, a name which originated during Molde's era as a tourist destination of international fame in the late 19th century.

Neighbouring municipalities are [Aukra](/wiki/Aukra_Municipality), [Gjemnes](/wiki/Gjemnes_Municipality), and [Hustadvika](/wiki/Hustadvika_Municipality) (to the north); [Ålesund](/wiki/%C3%85lesund_Municipality) (to the southwest); [Vestnes](/wiki/Vestnes_Municipality) and [Rauma](/wiki/Rauma_Municipality) (to the south); and [Tingvoll](/wiki/Tingvoll_Municipality) and [Sunndal](/wiki/Sunndal_Municipality) (to the east).

### Points of interest

Some points of interest in and near Molde Municipality:

- [Salmon](/wiki/Salmon), [sea trout](/wiki/Sea_trout) and [sea char](/wiki/Salvelinus) are found in the rivers throughout the area, especially the [Rauma](/wiki/River_Rauma), [Driva](/wiki/Driva), and [Eira](/wiki/River_Eira), already legendary among the British [gentry](/wiki/Gentry) in the mid-19th century. [Trout](/wiki/Trout) is abundant in most lakes. [Cod](/wiki/Cod), [pollock](/wiki/Pollock), [saithe](/wiki/Saithe), [mackerel](/wiki/Mackerel) and other species of saltwater fish are commonly caught in the Romsdalsfjord, both from land and from boat. [Skiing](/wiki/Skiing) is a common activity among the inhabitants of Molde in the winter, on groomed tracks, in resorts or by own trail. There are several [rock climbing](/wiki/Rock_climbing), [ice climbing](/wiki/Ice_climbing), [bouldering](/wiki/Bouldering), [glacier](/wiki/Glacier) and [basejumping](/wiki/Basejumping) areas in the immediate vicinity of Molde.

- The *[Atlantic road](/wiki/Atlantic_road)* was voted the Norwegian Construction of the Century in 2005. It is built on bridges and landfills across small islands and skerries, and spans from the small communities of Vikan and Vevang to [Averøya](/wiki/Aver%C3%B8ya), an island with several historic landmarks, such as the Bremsnes cave with [Mesolithic](/wiki/Mesolithic) findings from the [Fosna](/wiki/Fosna) culture, the [mediaeval](/wiki/Mediaeval) [Kvernes stave church](/wiki/Kvernes_stave_church), and Langøysund, now a remote fishing community, but once a bustling port along the main coastal route. Langøysund was the site of the compromise between King [Magnus I](/wiki/Magnus_I_of_Norway) and the farmers along the coast in 1040. The compromise is regarded as Norway's [Magna Carta](/wiki/Magna_Carta), and is commemorated though the *Pilespisser* (English: Arrowheads) monument.

- *Trollkirka* (lit. 'Troll Church') is a marble grotto leading up to an underground waterfall. The grotto is situated 30 minutes outside Molde, followed by a 1-hour hike up a steep trail. *[Trollveggen](/wiki/Trollveggen)* is Europe's tallest vertical, overhanging mountain face, with several very difficult climbing routes. *[Trollstigen](/wiki/Trollstigen)* is the most visited tourist road in Norway. The road twists and turns its way up an almost vertical mountainside through 11 hairpin bends to an altitude of 858 m (2,814.96 ft). *Mardalsfossen* is the highest [waterfall](/wiki/Waterfall) in Northern Europe and the fourth highest waterfall in the world, cascading 297 metres down into the valley. The total height of the waterfall is 655 m (2,148.95 ft).

- [Bud](/wiki/Bud,_Norway) is a fishing village on the very tip of the [Romsdal peninsula](/wiki/Romsdal_peninsula). It gained importance during the [Middle Ages](/wiki/Middle_Ages) as a trading post, and hosted the last free [Privy Council](/wiki/Privy_Council) of Norway in 1533, a desperate attempt to save the country's independence and stave off the [Protestant Reformation](/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), led by [Olav Engelbrektsson](/wiki/Olav_Engelbrektsson), [archbishop of Nidaros](/wiki/Archbishop_of_Nidaros) (today *Trondheim*). The massive Ergan coastal defences, a restored German coastal fort from [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II), and a part of the [Atlantic Wall](/wiki/Atlantic_Wall), is situated in Bud. The fishing communities of Ona, Bjørnsund and Håholmen are located on remote islands off the coast, only accessible by boat or ferry.

### Moldemarka

View from the top of Varden

Main article: [Moldemarka](/wiki/Moldemarka)

Moldemarka, the hilly woodland area north of the city, is [public land](/wiki/Public_land). The area has an extensive network of paths, walking trails and skiing tracks. Forest roads enter the area from several directions. Bulletin boards and maps provide information regarding local plants and wildlife, as well as signposts along the trails. Marked trails lead to a number of peaks, sites and fishing lakes and rivers. A national fishing licence is required to fish in the lakes and streams.

Varden, 407 metres (1,335 ft) [above sea level](/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) is a viewpoint directly above Molde, with a good view of the city, the [fjord](/wiki/Romsdalsfjord) with the [Molde archipelago](/wiki/Molde_archipelago) and the [Molde panorama](/wiki/Molde_panorama).

### Climate

Molde has a temperate [oceanic climate](/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (*Cfb*) also known as marine west coast climate. Molde holds the national high for the month of October, with 25.6 °C or 78.1 °F recorded on 11 October 2005. Due to its geographic location, Molde experiences frequent snowfalls in winter, but this snow is usually wet as the winters tend to be mild. The record high 32.2 °C or 90.0 °F was recorded 13 July 2025. The record low −17 °C or 1 °F was recorded in both January and February 2010.

A [natural phenomenon](/wiki/Natural_phenomenon) occurring in Molde and the adjacent district, are frequent winter days with temperatures above 10 °C (50 °F), sometimes even above 14 °C (57 °F). This is due to the [foehn wind](/wiki/Foehn_wind) from south and south-east. The sheltered location of the city, facing south with hills to the north, mountains to the east and mountainous islands to the west, contributes to Molde's climate and rich plant life, especially among species naturally growing on far lower latitudes, like [chestnut](/wiki/Chestnut), [oak](/wiki/Oak), [tilia](/wiki/Tilia) (*lime* or *linden*), [beech](/wiki/Beech), [yew](/wiki/Taxus), and others.

## Government

Molde town hall in 2018

Molde Municipality is responsible for primary education (through 10th grade), outpatient [health services](/wiki/Health_care), [senior citizen](/wiki/Old_age) services, [welfare](/wiki/Welfare_spending) and other [social services](/wiki/Social_work), [zoning](/wiki/Zoning), [economic development](/wiki/Economic_development), and municipal roads and utilities. The municipality is governed by a [municipal council](/wiki/Municipal_council_(Norway)) of [directly elected](/wiki/Direct_election) representatives. The mayor is [indirectly elected](/wiki/Indirect_election) by a vote of the municipal council. The municipality is under the jurisdiction of the [Møre og Romsdal District Court](/wiki/M%C3%B8re_og_Romsdal_District_Court) and the [Frostating Court of Appeal](/wiki/Frostating_Court_of_Appeal).

## Culture

Cultural building, Plassen

Three of the *[four great](/wiki/Norwegian_literature#National_Romantic_Period)* Norwegian authors are connected to Molde. [Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson](/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rnstjerne_Bj%C3%B8rnson) spent his childhood years at [Nesset Municipality](/wiki/Nesset_Municipality) outside Molde, and attended school in the city. [Henrik Ibsen](/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen) frequently spent his vacations at the mansion *Moldegård* visiting the family Møller; and [Alexander Kielland](/wiki/Alexander_Kielland) resided in the city as the governor of [Romsdals amt](/wiki/M%C3%B8re_og_Romsdal). Ibsen's play *[Rosmersholm](/wiki/Rosmersholm)* is generally thought to be inspired by life at the mansion Moldegård, and *[The Lady from the Sea](/wiki/The_Lady_from_the_Sea)* is also believed to be set in the city of Molde, although never actually mentioned. Other authors from or with ties to Molde include [Edvard Hoem](/wiki/Edvard_Hoem), [Jo Nesbø](/wiki/Jo_Nesb%C3%B8), [Knut Ødegård](/wiki/Knut_%C3%98deg%C3%A5rd), and [Nini Roll Anker](/wiki/Nini_Roll_Anker), a friend of [Sigrid Undset](/wiki/Sigrid_Undset).

The [Romsdal Museum](/wiki/Romsdal_Museum), one of Norway's largest folk museums, was established in 1912. Buildings originating from all over the region have been moved here to form a typical cluster of farm buildings including "open [hearth](/wiki/Hearth)" houses, sheds, outhouses, smokehouses and a small chapel. The "town street" with Mali's Café shows typical Molde town houses from the pre-World War I period. The Museum of the Fisheries is an open-air museum located on the island of Hjertøya, 10 minutes from the centre of Molde. A small fishing village with authentic buildings, boats and fishing equipment, the museum shows local coastal culture from 1850 onwards.

The local newspaper is *[Romsdals Budstikke](/wiki/Romsdals_Budstikke)*.

### Festivals

The [Moldejazz](/wiki/Moldejazz) [jazz](/wiki/Jazz) festival is held in Molde every July. Moldejazz is one of the largest and oldest jazz festivals in Europe, and one of the most important. An estimated 40,000 tickets are sold for the more than a hundred events during the festival. Between 80,000 and 100,000 visitors visit the city during the one-week-long festival.

Every August, Molde Municipality hosts to the [Bjørnson Festival](/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rnson_Festival), an international literature festival (Prior to 2020, it was hosted by both Molde and [Nesset Municipality](/wiki/Nesset_Municipality) – Nesset has since become part of Molde). Established by the poet Knut Ødegård in connection with the 250-year anniversary of Molde, the festival is named in honour of the [Nobel Prize in Literature](/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Literature) laureate [Bjørnstjerne Bjørnson](/wiki/Bj%C3%B8rnstjerne_Bj%C3%B8rnson) (1832–1910). It is the oldest and the most internationally acclaimed literature festival in Norway.

In addition to the two major events, a number of minor festivals are held annually. Byfest, the city's celebration of incorporation, is an arrangement by local artists, coinciding with the anniversary of the royal charter of 29 June 1742.

## Education

Molde University College in 2014

[Molde University College](/wiki/Molde_University_College) offers a wide range of academic opportunities, from nursing and health-related studies, to economics and administrative courses. The school is Norway's leading college in logistics, and well established as a centre for research and academic programmes in information technology, with degrees up to and including PhD.

## Transportation

[Hurtigruta](/wiki/Hurtigruta) calls on Molde every day, on its journey between [Bergen](/wiki/Bergen_(city)) and [Kirkenes](/wiki/Kirkenes). The nearest railway station is [Åndalsnes](/wiki/%C3%85ndalsnes), the terminus for the [Rauma Line](/wiki/Rauma_Line).

The local airport is [Molde Airport](/wiki/Molde_Airport,_%C3%85r%C3%B8) which has several daily flights to Oslo, Bergen, and Trondheim, as well as weekly flights to other domestic and international destinations.

The [European route E39](/wiki/European_route_E39) and [Norwegian County Road 64](/wiki/Norwegian_County_Road_64) both pass through the municipality. The city of Molde is connected to Fræna Municipality (to the north) by the [Tussen Tunnel](/wiki/Tussen_Tunnel). The city is connected to the [Røvika](/wiki/R%C3%B8vika) and [Nesjestranda](/wiki/Nesjestranda) part of the municipality by the [Fannefjord Tunnel](/wiki/Fannefjord_Tunnel) and [Bolsøy Bridge](/wiki/Bols%C3%B8y_Bridge), significantly shortening the drive by avoiding driving all the way around the [Fannefjorden](/wiki/Fannefjorden). The proposed [Langfjord Tunnel](/wiki/Langfjord_Tunnel) would connect Molde Municipality to Rauma Municipality via a tunnel under the [Langfjorden](/wiki/Langfjorden_(M%C3%B8re_og_Romsdal)).

## Sports

The stadium to [Molde FK](/wiki/Molde_FK)

Molde hosts a variety of sports teams, most notably the [football](/wiki/Association_football) team, [Molde FK](/wiki/Molde_FK), which plays in the [Eliteserien](/wiki/Eliteserien), the top division in the [Norwegian football league system](/wiki/Norwegian_football_league_system). Their home matches are played at [Aker stadion](/wiki/Aker_stadion), inaugurated in 1998, which holds a record attendance of 13,308. The team is five-time league champions ([2011](/wiki/2011_Molde_FK_season), [2012](/wiki/2012_Molde_FK_season), [2014](/wiki/2014_Molde_FK_season), [2019](/wiki/2019_Molde_FK_season) and [2022](/wiki/2022_Molde_FK_season)), five-time Norwegian Cup winners ([1994](/wiki/1994_Norwegian_Football_Cup), [2005](/wiki/2005_Norwegian_Football_Cup), [2013](/wiki/2013_Norwegian_Football_Cup), [2014](/wiki/2014_Norwegian_Football_Cup) and [2021–22](/wiki/2021-22_Norwegian_Football_Cup)), and has made numerous appearances in European tournaments, including the [UEFA Champions League](/wiki/UEFA_Champions_League). The club was founded in 1911, during Molde's period of great British and Continental influx, and was first named "International", since it predominantly played teams made up from crews of foreign vessels visiting the city.

In addition to a number of international players, the city has also produced several [ski jumpers](/wiki/Ski_jumping), [cross-country skiers](/wiki/Cross-country_skiing_(sport)) and [alpine skiers](/wiki/Alpine_skiing) of international merit.

Other sports include the accomplished [team handball](/wiki/Team_handball) clubs ([Molde Elite](/wiki/Molde_Elite), [SK Træff](/wiki/SK_Tr%C3%A6ff), SK Rival), athletics teams ([IL Molde-Olymp](/wiki/IL_Molde-Olymp)), skiing clubs, basketball and volleyball teams.

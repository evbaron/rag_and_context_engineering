# List of Billboard 200 number-one albums of 1999

In 1999, the [Backstreet Boys](/wiki/Backstreet_Boys)' album *[Millennium](/wiki/Millennium_(Backstreet_Boys_album))* established a new record for the largest sales in a single week since [Nielsen SoundScan](/wiki/Nielsen_SoundScan) began tracking sales in 1991.

The [*Billboard* 200](/wiki/Billboard_200), published in [*Billboard*](/wiki/Billboard_(magazine)) magazine, is a weekly chart that ranks the highest-selling [music albums](/wiki/Music_albums) and [EPs](/wiki/Extended_play) in the United States. These data are compiled by [Nielsen SoundScan](/wiki/Nielsen_SoundScan) from a database of merchants that represents more than 90% of the U.S. music retail market. The sample includes not only music stores and the music departments at electronics and [department stores](/wiki/Department_stores), but also [direct-to-consumer](/wiki/Direct-to-consumer) transactions and [internet](/wiki/Internet) sales (both physical [albums](/wiki/Albums) and the ones bought via [digital downloads](/wiki/Music_download)).

There were 23 number-one albums on this chart in 1999, including [Garth Brook's](/wiki/Garth_Brooks) *[Double Live](/wiki/Double_Live_(Garth_Brooks_album))* which, starting in early December 1998, spent a consecutive run of five weeks at the top of the chart. Rapper [DMX](/wiki/DMX_(rapper))' *[Flesh of My Flesh, Blood of My Blood](/wiki/Flesh_of_My_Flesh,_Blood_of_My_Blood)* debuted at number one, making it his second album that debuted at the top of the chart. [Pop](/wiki/Pop_music) singer [Britney Spears](/wiki/Britney_Spears)'s first album *[...Baby One More Time](/wiki/...Baby_One_More_Time_(album))* peaked at number one for six non-consecutive weeks. Certified as [diamond](/wiki/RIAA_certification) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA), it made her the youngest artist that reach the ten million sales mark and the top selling female act of 1999. [TLC](/wiki/TLC_(music))'s third album, *[Fanmail](/wiki/Fanmail_(album))*, the year's top selling hip-hop album, remained at number one for five weeks and won the [Grammy Award for Best R&B Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_R%26B_Album) at the [42nd Grammy Awards](/wiki/42nd_Grammy_Awards).

*[I Am…](/wiki/I_Am%E2%80%A6_(Nas_album))*, the third studio album by rapper [Nas](/wiki/Nas_(rapper)), stayed at the top of the chart for two weeks and has been certified double platinum by the RIAA. [Puerto Rican](/wiki/Puerto_Rico) pop singer [Ricky Martin](/wiki/Ricky_Martin)'s eponymous [set](/wiki/Ricky_Martin_(1999_album)) achieved sales of six million copies, making it the year's top-selling album by a solo male artist. *[Millennium](/wiki/Millennium_(Backstreet_Boys_album))* by [vocal group](/wiki/Vocal_group) [Backstreet Boys](/wiki/Backstreet_Boys) spent ten weeks at number one on the chart, selling 1,130,000 copies in its first week of release, establishing a new record for the largest sales in a single week since Nielsen SoundScan began tracking sales in 1991. *Millennium* became the best-selling album of 1999, with sales of over 11 million copies. The album also received an [Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year) and a [Best Pop Vocal Album](/wiki/Best_Pop_Vocal_Album) nomination at the [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Awards). [Nu metal](/wiki/Nu_metal) band [Limp Bizkit](/wiki/Limp_Bizkit) released their second album *[Significant Other](/wiki/Significant_Other_(album))*, which debuted at number one and sold over five million copies by the end of the year; this helped push Limp Bizkit's first album past the platinum mark. Pop/R&B singer [Christina Aguilera](/wiki/Christina_Aguilera)'s [self-titled debut album](/wiki/Christina_Aguilera_(album)) was released in August 1999. Its lead single "[Genie in a Bottle](/wiki/Genie_in_a_Bottle)" reached the top of the singles chart for five weeks, which helped the album reach the number one position in September, selling five million copies by January 2000.

*[Fly](/wiki/Fly_(Dixie_Chicks_album))* by the [country](/wiki/Country_music) group [Dixie Chicks](/wiki/The_Chicks) peaked at number one for two weeks, making them the first country group to reach the top of the *Billboard* 200; the album received the [Grammy Award for Best Country Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Country_Album). *[Human Clay](/wiki/Human_Clay)*, the second album by [post-grunge](/wiki/Post-grunge) band [Creed](/wiki/Creed_(band)), became a hit, entering the charts at number one and selling ten million copies over the next two years. [Latin rock](/wiki/Rock_en_Espa%C3%B1ol) band [Santana](/wiki/Santana_(band))'s *[Supernatural](/wiki/Supernatural_(Santana_album))*, was the longest-running number-one album of the year, topping the chart for twelve non-consecutive weeks, three of which were in 1999, making it his first number-one album in 28 years. The album sold five million copies in 1999 and won two [Grammy Awards](/wiki/Grammy_Award) for [Best Rock Album](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rock_Album) and for [Album of the Year](/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Album_of_the_Year).
*[Breathe](/wiki/Breathe_(Faith_Hill_album))*, the fourth album by country artist [Faith Hill](/wiki/Faith_Hill), entered the charts at number one upon its release in late 1999. Its [title track](/wiki/Breathe_(Faith_Hill_song)) became Hill's biggest hit, topping the [country](/wiki/Hot_Country_Songs) and the [adult contemporary](/wiki/Hot_Adult_Contemporary_Tracks) singles charts; the album sold five million copies by the end of 2000 and won the Grammy Award for Best Country Album at the [43rd Grammy Awards](/wiki/43rd_Grammy_Awards). *[Born Again](/wiki/Born_Again_(The_Notorious_B.I.G.))* by [The Notorious B.I.G.](/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.) was the second posthumous album by the rapper to reach number-one on the albums chart, and the seventh posthumous title to reach the top during the 1990s.
